# Cosine Similarity

Also called: Cosine Similarity on Unit Hypersphere

## Overview
Cosine similarity measures the angular agreement between two vectors by computing the inner product of their L2-normalized forms, effectively factoring out magnitude variations to focus on directional information [S1, unofficial]. In volume rendering classification, this allows transfer functions to distinguish semantic content based on the orientation of feature vectors (e.g., gradients or latent codes) rather than their scalar intensity or norm, which may be affected by scale or activation statistics [S1, unofficial]. It is distinct from magnitude-dependent methods and correlation-based methods like Pearson or Spearman, which assess linear or monotonic agreement across individual feature dimensions rather than global direction [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Two feature vectors $\mathbf{z}_{\text{render}}, \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ associated with a sample point $\mathbf{x}$ in the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A similarity score $sim \in [-1, 1]$, representing the cosine of the angle between the vectors [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   Vectors $\mathbf{z}$ are dense arrays of floating-point numbers of dimension $d$ [G].
*   No additional lookup tables or textures are required; the computation is algebraic [G].

**Steps:**
1. Compute the squared L2 norm for each vector by summing the squares of its components [S1, unofficial].
2. Take the square root of the sum to obtain the L2 norm [S1, unofficial].
3. Divide each component of the original vectors by their respective L2 norm plus a stability constant $\epsilon$ to obtain normalized vectors on the unit hypersphere [S1, unofficial].
4. Compute the dot product of the two normalized vectors [S1, unofficial].
5. Return the dot product as the similarity score [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   L2 norm: $\|\mathbf{z}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^d z_k^2}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Normalization map: $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \phi(\mathbf{z}) = \frac{\mathbf{z}}{\|\mathbf{z}\|_2 + \epsilon}$, where $\phi: \mathbb{R}^d \to S^{d-1}$ maps to the unit hypersphere [S1, unofficial].
*   Similarity: $sim = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{render}}^\top \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{target}} = \cos \theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the original vectors [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\epsilon$: A small positive constant added for numerical stability in the denominator [S1, unofficial]. Typical values are not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Zero Vectors:** If a feature vector is zero, the L2 norm is zero [G]. The addition of $\epsilon$ ensures the denominator is non-zero, resulting in a normalized vector of zeros, which yields a similarity of $0$ against any other vector [S1, unofficial].
*   **Dimensionality:** The method assumes vectors are in the same space $\mathbb{R}^d$. Mismatched dimensions are undefined [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def cosine_similarity(z_render, z_target, eps):
    # Compute L2 norms
    norm_render = sqrt(sum(z**2 for z in z_render))
    norm_target = sqrt(sum(z**2 for z in z_target))
    # Normalize vectors
    z_hat_render = [z / (norm_render + eps) for z in z_render]
    z_hat_target = [z / (norm_target + eps) for z in z_target]
    # Compute dot product
    sim = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(z_hat_render, z_hat_target))
    return sim
```

## Subtopics

### Class-Conditional Affinity Metric
A pairwise cosine similarity metric normalized per query to remove encoder-specific global structure, averaged by class pair to summarize within-class and between-class affinity [S2, unofficial]. It computes a query-wise normalized affinity $a_{ij}$ for every non-self pair using the mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ of the non-self gallery for each query, then averages $a_{ij}$ by class pair [S2, unofficial]. The normalization formula is $a_{ij} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{s_{ij} - \mu_j}{\sigma_i} + \frac{s_{ij} - \mu_i}{\sigma_j} \right)$ [S2, unofficial]. This normalization ensures that zero denotes the query-specific gallery average, not absolute similarity, revealing stronger class-relative organization in the feature space [S2, unofficial]. Parameters include an embedding dimension of $768$ and query-wise normalization using gallery statistics [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(d)$ per pair, where $d$ is the vector dimension [G]. The normalization and dot product are linear in dimensionality [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(d)$ to store the vectors [G]. No auxiliary structures like LUTs are needed for the core calculation [G].
*   **Strengths:** Scale-invariant; robust to variations in vector magnitude caused by activation statistics or training dynamics [S1, unofficial]. Focuses on directional semantic content [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Ignores magnitude information, which may be relevant in some physical contexts [G]. Sensitive to the dimensionality $d$; high-dimensional spaces may suffer from the "curse of dimensionality" where distances become less discriminative [G].
*   **When to use:** When feature vectors encode semantic content primarily through orientation (e.g., deep latent representations) rather than norm [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When magnitude carries critical information (e.g., density magnitude in some physical simulations) or when linear correlation across dimensions is more important than global direction (use Pearson/Spearman instead) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method is applied in probing vision models for 3D scene understanding using differentiable rendering [S1, unofficial]. It is used to handle latent representations from deep networks where semantic content is encoded by vector orientation rather than norm, which can be affected by scale or training dynamics [S1, unofficial]. The method maps vectors to $S^{d-1}$ via L2-normalization and computes the inner product, which corresponds to $\cos \theta$ [S1, unofficial]. It is contrasted with Pearson and Spearman correlations, which assess agreement across individual feature dimensions rather than global direction [S1, unofficial].
