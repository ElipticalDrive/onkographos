# Silhouette Enhancement

Also called: View-Vector Silhouette, Oriented Feature Enhancement, Orthogonal gradient opacity modulation

## Overview
Silhouette Enhancement is a feature-based classification technique that emphasizes object boundaries in volume rendering by increasing the opacity of voxels where the local gradient vector is orthogonal to the view direction [S1, S2][S3, S6, unofficial]. It solves the problem of ambiguous surface perception in translucent volumes by strengthening silhouette cues, which are strongest when the view vector is perpendicular to the surface normal [S1][S6, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Boundary-Enhanced Opacity](795-boundary-enhanced-opacity.md), which scales opacity based solely on gradient magnitude regardless of view angle, Silhouette Enhancement is view-dependent, modulating opacity based on the geometric relationship between the gradient and the camera [S3, S6, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Volume dataset $V$, transfer function parameters (base opacity $\alpha_g$ or $o_v$), view direction vector $V$, and enhancement parameters $k_{sc}, k_{ss}, k_{se}$ (or $k_{f1}, k_{f2}$).
*   **Output:** Enhanced opacity $\alpha_{gf}$ (or $o_s$) for each sample point along the ray.

**Data Structures**
*   **Gradient Field:** Precomputed or on-the-fly gradient $\nabla f$ at each voxel.
*   **View Vector:** Normalized vector from the sample point to the camera eye position.

**Steps**
1.  **Compute Gradient:** Calculate the volume gradient $\nabla f(r_i)$ at the sample point $r_i$ [S1, S2].
2.  **Normalize Gradient:** Normalize the gradient to obtain the surface normal approximation $\nabla f_n = \frac{\nabla f(r_i)}{\|\nabla f(r_i)\|}$ [S5][S6, S7, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Silhouette Factor:** Calculate the dot product between the normalized gradient $\nabla f_n$ and the view direction $V$ [S3, S6, unofficial]. The silhouette factor is derived from the perpendicularity of these vectors. A dot product near zero indicates orthogonality (silhouette), while a dot product near $\pm 1$ indicates parallelism (facing the viewer) [S6, S7, unofficial].
4.  **Modulate Opacity:** Apply the silhouette enhancement formula to the base opacity [S5][S6, S7, unofficial].
5.  **Composite:** Use the enhanced opacity in the standard volume rendering integration loop [S2].

**Formulas**
Two primary formulations are cited in the literature.

*Formulation 1 (Ebert et al.):*
$$ \alpha_{gf}(r_i) = \alpha_g(r_i) [1 + k_{f1} (1 - |\nabla f(r_i) \cdot V|)^{k_{f2}}] $$
Where:
*   $\alpha_{gf}(r_i)$ is the enhanced opacity.
*   $\alpha_g(r_i)$ is the base opacity from the transfer function.
*   $\nabla f(r_i)$ is the gradient at sample point $r_i$.
*   $V$ is the view direction vector.
*   $k_{f1}$ determines the scaling of the opacity increase [S2].
*   $k_{f2}$ determines the non-linear tuning of the transfer function [S2].

*Formulation 2 (Rheingans et al.):*
$$ o_s = o_v (k_{sc} + k_{ss} (1 - |\nabla f_n \cdot V|)^{k_{se}}) $$
Where:
*   $o_s$ is the silhouette-enhanced opacity.
*   $o_v$ is the original/base opacity.
*   $\nabla f_n$ is the normalized value gradient.
*   $V$ is the view direction vector.
*   $k_{sc}$ controls the scaling of non-silhouette regions (baseline opacity factor) [S5][S6, S7, unofficial].
*   $k_{ss}$ controls the amount of silhouette enhancement [S5][S6, S7, unofficial].
*   $k_{se}$ controls the sharpness of the silhouette curve [S5][S6, S7, unofficial].

*Note on Formula Discrepancy:* Source [S7, unofficial] displays a formula with a cross product notation in the text description but uses the dot product in the equation block: $(1 - |\nabla f_n \cdot V|)$. Source [S4] describes the logic as "normal perpendicular to the view" and suggests using a paletted texture. The dot product formulation $(1 - |\nabla f_n \cdot V|)$ yields 1 when orthogonal (silhouette) and 0 when parallel, which aligns with the textual description of increasing opacity at silhouettes [S6, S7, unofficial]. The cross product magnitude $|\nabla f_n \times V|$ is mathematically equivalent to $\sqrt{1 - (\nabla f_n \cdot V)^2}$ for unit vectors, but the cited formulas explicitly use the dot product term inside the absolute value [S2, S5][S6, S7, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   $k_{sc}$: Typical value 0.9 [S5][S6, S7, unofficial]. Controls the baseline opacity level for non-silhouette regions.
*   $k_{ss}$: Typical value 50 [S5][S6, S7, unofficial]. Controls the intensity of the silhouette enhancement.
*   $k_{se}$: Typical value 0.25 [S5][S6, S7, unofficial]. Controls the sharpness/thickness of the silhouette lines.
*   $k_{f1}$: Typical value 0.5 (for retinal imaging) [S2].
*   $k_{f2}$: Typical value 0.4 (for retinal imaging) [S2].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases**
*   **Darkening Effect:** Increasing the opacity of edge voxels causes them to reach the opacity threshold faster during compositing, resulting in less accumulated emission and making the edges appear darker [S2]. This is often desirable for sketch-like effects but reduces brightness.
*   **Transparency Requirements:** In applications requiring visualization of deep structures (e.g., anterior segment eye imaging), the silhouette enhancement contribution ($k_{f1}$ or $k_{ss}$) must be significantly reduced (e.g., by a factor of 10) to prevent the silhouettes from occluding internal features [S2].
*   **Degenerate Gradients:** At flat regions or noise, the gradient magnitude may be near zero. Normalization requires a guard against division by zero [G].
*   **View Dependency:** The effect changes dynamically with camera rotation. Features facing the camera become transparent, while edges become opaque.

**Pseudocode**
```python
def silhouette_enhance(base_opacity, gradient, view_direction, k_sc, k_ss, k_se):
    """
    Computes silhouette-enhanced opacity.
    
    Args:
        base_opacity (float): Original opacity from transfer function.
        gradient (Vector3): Unnormalized gradient at sample point.
        view_direction (Vector3): Normalized view direction from sample to camera.
        k_sc (float): Scaling of non-silhouette regions.
        k_ss (float): Amount of silhouette enhancement.
        k_se (float): Sharpness of the silhouette curve.
        
    Returns:
        float: Enhanced opacity.
    """
    # Guard against zero gradient magnitude
    grad_mag = length(gradient)
    if grad_mag < 1e-6:
        return base_opacity
        
    # Normalize gradient to approximate surface normal
    grad_normal = gradient / grad_mag
    
    # Ensure view_direction is normalized
    # view_direction = normalize(view_direction) 
    
    # Compute dot product between normal and view
    # Dot product is 1 when parallel (facing viewer), -1 when anti-parallel, 0 when orthogonal
    dot_product = dot(grad_normal, view_direction)
    
    # Silhouette factor: 1 when orthogonal (silhouette), 0 when parallel
    silhouette_factor = 1.0 - abs(dot_product)
    
    # Apply enhancement formula
    # o_s = o_v * (k_sc + k_ss * silhouette_factor^k_se)
    enhanced_opacity = base_opacity * (k_sc + k_ss * (silhouette_factor ** k_se))
    
    return enhanced_opacity
```

## Subtopics

### Sketching
An extreme application where non-silhouette regions are made fully transparent and silhouette regions are rendered with high-contrast colors (e.g., white or black lines) to create a pen-and-ink appearance [S1][S6, S7, unofficial]. Shadows can be calculated based on the original volume opacity to maintain depth cues [S6, S7, unofficial].

### Edge Coloring
Modifying the color of samples based on the silhouette term rather than just opacity, often used to darken or saturate features oriented away from the viewer [S1][S6, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, requiring one dot product and a few arithmetic operations. No additional passes are strictly required if computed in the ray-casting shader, though texture-based approaches may require a second pass [S4, S8].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per sample. If using the texture-based approach from [S4], a paletted texture mapping gradient directions to opacities is required.
*   **Performance:** Hardware-acceleratable and suitable for real-time interactive exploration [S8].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively conveys surface shape and relative depth in translucent volumes; creates illustrative "sketch" styles; enhances fine internal structures (e.g., liver honeycomb) when combined with boundary enhancement [S6, S7, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can obscure internal details if enhancement is too strong; view-dependent nature may cause flickering or instability if the camera moves rapidly; edges appear darker due to early opacity thresholding [S2].
*   **When to use:** When the goal is illustrative visualization, medical sketching, or emphasizing surface boundaries in complex, overlapping translucent structures.
*   **When not to use:** When quantitative visualization of internal intensity values is required, or when deep structures must remain visible without occlusion by surface outlines [S2].

## Background
Silhouette enhancement in volume rendering was introduced by Ebert, Rheingans, and others in the early 2000s as part of the "Volume Illustration" framework [S1, S5][S6, unofficial]. It draws inspiration from traditional artistic silhouettes (named after Étienne de Silhouette) and surface illustration techniques [S1][S6, unofficial]. The method extends the concept of using gradient magnitude for boundary detection by incorporating view-vector orientation to distinguish between surfaces facing the viewer and those forming the outline [S1]. It is widely used in medical illustration to emphasize organ boundaries and in scientific visualization to clarify complex volumetric structures [S3, S6, unofficial].
