# Near-Far Linear Ray Generation

Also called: Coarse Sampling, NeRF Near-Far Linear Ray Generation, Near-Far Disparity Linear Ray Generation, Ray Marching Sampling, Ray Marching with Linear/Disparity Sampling

## Overview
Near-Far Linear Ray Generation generates a list of 3D sampling points along a ray segment defined by a near plane ($t_{min}$) and a far plane ($t_{max}$) for a specified number of points [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. It serves as the foundational sampling strategy for coarse volume rendering passes, building on the ray origins and directions provided by the [Grid Ray Sampler](3930-grid-ray-sampler.md). It differs from its siblings by offering two distinct spacing modes—linear depth and linear disparity—allowing the renderer to control sample density relative to geometric depth or inverse depth, which is critical for handling scenes with varying depth scales [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `rays_o`: Ray origins, shape $(N, 3)$, where $N$ is the number of rays.
*   `rays_d`: Ray directions, shape $(N, 3)$.
*   `near`: Near plane distance $t_{min}$, scalar or shape $(N,)$.
*   `far`: Far plane distance $t_{max}$, scalar or shape $(N,)$.
*   `num_samples` ($N_s$): Number of sampling points per ray.
*   `lindisp` (boolean): If `True`, use linear disparity sampling; if `False`, use linear depth sampling [S3, unofficial].
*   `perturb` (float): Jitter magnitude. $0.0$ for no jitter, $1.0$ for full jitter [S3, unofficial].
*   `bbox_3D` (optional): Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) defined by min/max corners, shape $(2, 3)$. If provided, ray intervals are clipped to the box intersection [S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `z_vals`: Sample depths along the ray, shape $(N, N_s)$.
*   `xyz_samples`: 3D coordinates of samples, shape $(N, N_s, 3)$.

**Data Structures:**
*   `z_vals`: Tensor of depth values $t$ along the ray parameterization.
*   `xyz_samples`: Tensor of Cartesian coordinates derived from $z\_vals$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Decompose Rays:** Extract origins `rays_o` and directions `rays_d`. Normalize `rays_d` if necessary to ensure unit length, as segment length calculations depend on direction norm [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Bounding Box Intersections (Optional):** If `bbox_3D` is provided, compute the intersection interval $[t_{min}, t_{max}]$ for each ray using the Slab Method (DDA) [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ t_{min} = \max_i \left( \frac{bbox_{min}[i] - rays\_o[i]}{rays\_d[i]} \right) $$
    $$ t_{max} = \min_i \left( \frac{bbox_{max}[i] - rays\_o[i]}{rays\_d[i]} \right) $$
    Clamp $t_{min}$ and $t_{max}$ such that $t_{min} < t_{max}$ and $t_{min} \ge 0$. If $t_{min} \ge t_{max}$, the ray misses the box. Replace the input `near` and `far` with these computed values [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Generate Base Parameters:** Create a linear space vector `tvals` of shape $(N_s + 1)$ or $(N_s)$ depending on the variant. For standard linear generation, create `tvals` from $0$ to $1$ with $N_s + 1$ steps [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Map to Depth/Disparity:** Interpolate between `near` and `far` based on the `lindisp` flag [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]:
    *   **Linear Depth (`lindisp=False`):**
        $$ z\_vals = near \cdot (1 - tvals) + far \cdot tvals $$
    *   **Linear Disparity (`lindisp=True`):**
        $$ z\_vals = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{near} \cdot (1 - tvals) + \frac{1}{far} \cdot tvals} $$
    This results in `z_vals` of shape $(N_s + 1)$ representing the boundaries of the segments [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
5.  **Compute Segment Midpoints:** Calculate the center of each segment to place samples. For $N_s$ samples, compute midpoints from the $N_s + 1$ boundaries:
    $$ z\_vals\_mid = \frac{z\_vals[:, :-1] + z\_vals[:, 1:]}{2} $$
    Shape $(N, N_s)$ [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
6.  **Apply Jitter (Optional):** If `perturb > 0`, perturb the sample positions within their segments to break symmetry and reduce aliasing [S3, unofficial]:
    *   Compute segment bounds: $lower = z\_vals[:, :-1]$, $upper = z\_vals[:, 1:]$.
    *   Generate random noise $r \sim U(0, 1)$ of shape $(N, N_s)$.
    *   Update midpoints:
        $$ z\_vals\_perturbed = lower + (upper - lower) \cdot perturb \cdot r $$
    *   Replace $z\_vals\_mid$ with $z\_vals\_perturbed$ [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
7.  **Compute 3D Coordinates:** Transform depth values to Cartesian coordinates:
    $$ xyz\_samples = rays\_o + rays\_d \cdot z\_vals\_perturbed $$
    Broadcasting handles the dimensions: $(N, 1, 3) + (N, 1, 3) \cdot (N, N_s, 1)$ results in shape $(N, N_s, 3)$ [S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch

def near_far_linear_ray_generation(rays_o, rays_d, near, far, num_samples, lindisp=False, perturb=1.0, bbox_3d=None):
    """
    Generate samples along rays using near-far linear or disparity spacing.
    
    Args:
        rays_o: Tensor (N, 3), ray origins.
        rays_d: Tensor (N, 3), ray directions.
        near: Tensor (N,) or scalar, near plane distance.
        far: Tensor (N,) or scalar, far plane distance.
        num_samples: int, number of samples per ray.
        lindisp: bool, if True use disparity sampling.
        perturb: float, jitter magnitude (0.0 to 1.0).
        bbox_3d: Tensor (2, 3), [min_corner, max_corner]. Optional.
        
    Returns:
        z_vals: Tensor (N, num_samples), sampled depths.
        xyz_samples: Tensor (N, num_samples, 3), sampled 3D coordinates.
    """
    # 1. Normalize directions
    rays_d = rays_d / torch.norm(rays_d, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    
    # 2. Handle Bounding Box Clipping
    if bbox_3d is not None:
        inv_ray_d = 1.0 / (rays_d + 1e-6)
        t1 = (bbox_3d[0] - rays_o) * inv_ray_d
        t2 = (bbox_3d[1] - rays_o) * inv_ray_d
        t_min = torch.max(torch.min(t1, t2), dim=-1).values
        t_max = torch.min(torch.max(t1, t2), dim=-1).values
        
        # Clamp to valid range
        t_min = torch.clamp(t_min, min=0.0)
        valid_mask = t_min < t_max
        near = torch.where(valid_mask, t_min, near)
        far = torch.where(valid_mask, t_max, far)
        
        # If ray misses box, set near=far to avoid division by zero or invalid ranges
        near = torch.where(valid_mask, near, torch.zeros_like(near))
        far = torch.where(valid_mask, far, torch.zeros_like(far))

    # 3. Generate linear tvals from 0 to 1
    # Using num_samples + 1 to define segment boundaries
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, num_samples + 1, device=rays_o.device)
    t_vals = t_vals.unsqueeze(0) # Shape (1, num_samples + 1)
    
    # Broadcast near/far to (N, 1)
    near = near.unsqueeze(-1)
    far = far.unsqueeze(-1)
    
    # 4. Map to Depth or Disparity
    if lindisp:
        # Linear Disparity: 1/z is linear
        # z = 1 / ( (1/near)*(1-t) + (1/far)*t )
        inv_near = 1.0 / (near + 1e-6)
        inv_far = 1.0 / (far + 1e-6)
        inv_z_vals = inv_near * (1 - t_vals) + inv_far * t_vals
        z_vals = 1.0 / inv_z_vals
    else:
        # Linear Depth: z is linear
        z_vals = near * (1 - t_vals) + far * t_vals
        
    # z_vals shape: (N, num_samples + 1)
    
    # 5. Compute Midpoints
    z_vals_mid = (z_vals[:, :-1] + z_vals[:, 1:]) / 2.0 # Shape (N, num_samples)
    
    # 6. Apply Jitter
    if perturb > 0:
        # Compute segment bounds
        lower = z_vals[:, :-1]
        upper = z_vals[:, 1:]
        
        # Random noise
        rand_noise = torch.rand_like(z_vals_mid)
        
        # Perturb midpoints
        z_vals_mid = lower + (upper - lower) * perturb * rand_noise
        
    # 7. Compute 3D Coordinates
    # rays_o: (N, 3), rays_d: (N, 3), z_vals_mid: (N, num_samples)
    # Expand dims for broadcasting
    rays_o = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) # (N, 1, 3)
    rays_d = rays_d.unsqueeze(1) # (N, 1, 3)
    z_vals_mid = z_vals_mid.unsqueeze(-1) # (N, num_samples, 1)
    
    xyz_samples = rays_o + rays_d * z_vals_mid
    
    return z_vals_mid, xyz_samples
```

**Parameters:**
*   `num_samples`: Number of points per ray. Typical values range from 48 to 192 for coarse passes [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   `perturb`: Controls jitter. $0.0$ disables jitter; $1.0$ applies maximum jitter within segments [S3, unofficial].
*   `lindisp`: Boolean flag. `False` for linear depth (uniform spacing in world space); `True` for linear disparity (uniform spacing in inverse depth, denser near the camera) [S3, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Division by Zero:** When computing disparity space, if `near` is $0$, add a small epsilon ($1e-6$) to prevent division by zero [S3, unofficial].
*   **Ray-Box Intersection:** If a ray does not intersect the bounding box, the slab method may yield $t_{min} \ge t_{max}$. These rays must be handled by clamping or masking to prevent invalid sampling intervals [S3, unofficial].
*   **Direction Normalization:** Segment length calculations and jitter scaling assume normalized ray directions. Failure to normalize `rays_d` leads to incorrect sample spacing [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Near-Far Disparity Linear Ray Generation with Jitter
A variant that explicitly combines disparity spacing with segment-based jitter. It generates `tvals` from $0$ to $1$ with `point_count` steps, converts them to disparity space using $tvals = 1. / (1. / near \cdot (1 - tvals) + 1. / far \cdot tvals)$, and applies jitter by computing midpoints, upper/lower bounds, and random offsets within segments. It returns the jittered `tvals` as the depth output. This method is often used in NeRF-style pipelines to ensure denser sampling near the camera while breaking grid artifacts [S2, unofficial].

### Linear Seed Line Generation
A simpler method that generates seed points along a line segment between a start point `m_startPt` and end point `m_endPt`. It calculates a delta vector $\Delta = (m\_endPt - m\_startPt) / count$ and iterates $i$ from $0$ to $count - 1$, setting $m\_h\_seeds[i] = m\_startPt + i \cdot \Delta$. This is used for basic line visualization or uniform sampling along a fixed vector, lacking the ray-specific near/far parameterization of the main method [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot N_s)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $N_s$ is the number of samples. The operations are vectorizable and highly parallel on GPUs.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot N_s)$ for storing `z_vals` and `xyz_samples`.
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; provides explicit control over depth distribution via `lindisp`; jitter reduces aliasing artifacts in neural rendering [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Uniform spacing in depth/disparity may be inefficient for scenes with large empty regions or complex geometry far from the camera. It does not adapt to scene content (unlike [Refined Ray Generation via PDF Sampling](6837-refined-ray-generation-via-pdf-sampling.md)) [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Coarse sampling passes in neural radiance fields (NeRF) or volume rendering where computational efficiency is prioritized over adaptive density.
*   **When Not to Use:** Final rendering passes where adaptive sampling based on variance or importance is required.

## Background
The method originates from the Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) paper by Mildenhall et al. (2020), which introduced stratified sampling along rays with linear disparity spacing to improve reconstruction quality for scenes with varying depth scales [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. It has since become a standard component in many neural rendering pipelines, including TensoRF [S3, unofficial] and PointNeRF [S2, unofficial]. The "linear depth" variant is common in traditional volume rendering, while "linear disparity" is specific to neural rendering approaches that benefit from denser sampling near the camera.
