# Viewing Plane Distance Adjustment
Also called: Divergence minimization, Ray Divergence Reduction via Viewing Plane Distance

## Overview
Viewing Plane Distance Adjustment minimizes aliasing artifacts caused by ray divergence in volume rendering by increasing the distance between the camera eye and the viewing plane [S1, unofficial]. By reducing the angle between neighboring rays, it ensures that adjacent pixels sample adjacent regions of the data volume, preventing gaps in coverage [S1, unofficial]. This method is distinct from lens-based optical corrections or complex camera models, relying solely on geometric scaling of the virtual camera setup [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Input**: Camera eye position $\mathbf{o}$, viewing plane normal $\mathbf{n}$, initial viewing plane distance $d_{initial}$, and data volume bounds.
- **Output**: Adjusted viewing plane distance $d_{adjusted}$ and updated ray directions.

**Data Structures**
No specific complex data structures are required beyond standard camera parameters. The core variable is the scalar distance $d$.

**Steps**
1. **Calculate Ray Divergence**: Determine the angular separation between rays passing through adjacent pixels. For a pixel grid of width $W$ and height $H$ at distance $d$, the maximum divergence angle $\alpha$ between neighboring rays (e.g., horizontally) can be approximated by:
   $$ \alpha \approx \frac{p}{d} $$
   where $p$ is the pixel pitch (physical size of a pixel on the viewing plane) [S1, unofficial].
2. **Adjust Distance**: Increase the distance $d$ between the viewer's eye and the viewing plane. As $d$ increases, the angle of divergence $\alpha$ decreases [S1, unofficial].
3. **Convergence Check**: Continue increasing $d$ until the divergence angle is sufficiently small to prevent sampling gaps. As $d \to \infty$, ray divergence $\to 0$ [S1, unofficial].
4. **Ray Generation**: Generate rays using the adjusted distance. The ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ for a pixel at position $(u, v)$ on the viewing plane is:
   $$ \mathbf{d} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{o} + \mathbf{p}_{uv} - \mathbf{o}) $$
   where $\mathbf{p}_{uv}$ is the 3D position of the pixel on the viewing plane at distance $d$ [G].

**Parameters**
- **Typical Distance**: In the source example, a $512 \times 512$ pixel image plane was set at a distance of 5000 units from the viewer's eye [S1, unofficial].
- **Resulting Divergence**: With the above configuration, the maximum divergence between neighboring rays was $0.011^\circ$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Precision**: Floating-point precision may limit the maximum usable distance. Extremely large distances may cause ray origins and directions to lose precision relative to the data volume bounds [G].
- **Performance**: Increasing distance reduces divergence but may increase the number of samples required if the field of view is fixed, or require larger data volumes to maintain coverage [G].
- **Degenerate Input**: If the viewing plane distance is zero or negative, ray generation is undefined.

**Pseudocode**
```python
def adjust_viewing_plane_distance(eye_pos, pixel_pitch, target_divergence_angle_rad):
    """
    Adjusts the viewing plane distance to minimize ray divergence.
    
    Args:
        eye_pos: 3D vector, camera eye position.
        pixel_pitch: float, physical size of a pixel on the viewing plane.
        target_divergence_angle_rad: float, maximum acceptable divergence angle in radians.
        
    Returns:
        float: Adjusted viewing plane distance.
    """
    # Approximate formula: divergence_angle ≈ pixel_pitch / distance
    # distance ≈ pixel_pitch / divergence_angle
    if target_divergence_angle_rad <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Target divergence angle must be positive.")
    
    adjusted_distance = pixel_pitch / target_divergence_angle_rad
    return adjusted_distance

def generate_ray(eye_pos, pixel_pos_3d):
    """
    Generates a ray from the eye to a pixel position.
    
    Args:
        eye_pos: 3D vector, camera eye position.
        pixel_pos_3d: 3D vector, position of the pixel on the viewing plane.
        
    Returns:
        Ray: A ray with origin eye_pos and direction normalized(pixel_pos_3d - eye_pos).
    """
    direction = pixel_pos_3d - eye_pos
    direction = normalize(direction)
    return Ray(origin=eye_pos, direction=direction)
```

## Subtopics

### Geometric Phase Lens Viewing Distance Reduction
A flat field lens exploiting spatial anisotropy to gather diffracted light and reduce the minimum viewing distance required for pixel overlap in holographic displays [S2]. It uses a Geometric Phase Lens (GPL) with 98% diffraction efficiency across the visible spectrum [S2]. The minimum viewing distance is calculated as $D = 0.5 \times L / \tan(\theta/2)$, where $L$ is the horizontal display length and $\theta$ is the viewing angle [S2]. For a 10-inch display ($L \approx 22$ cm) and $\theta = 0.25^\circ$, the distance is reduced from ~25 m to ~1 m (25× reduction) using a 1 m focal length GPL [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: $O(1)$ for distance adjustment calculation. Ray generation remains $O(N)$ for $N$ pixels.
- **Memory Complexity**: $O(1)$ additional memory for distance parameter.
- **Performance**: Reduces aliasing artifacts effectively without increasing computational cost per sample [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Simple to implement; no additional hardware or complex optics required in software-only rendering [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: Requires increasing the virtual distance, which may not be physically meaningful in all contexts; does not address divergence in other dimensions (e.g., depth) [G].
- **When to Use**: When ray divergence causes visible aliasing in volume rendering, especially for large datasets or high-resolution displays [S1, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use**: When hardware constraints limit the maximum representable distance or when other camera models (e.g., perspective with fixed FOV) are required [G].

## Background
Originating from volumetric rendering techniques for aerodynamic flow field data, this method was described in a DTIC report (ADA202661) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of rays diverging and failing to sample adjacent pixels, which causes aliasing [S1, unofficial]. The technique is particularly relevant for subvolume isolation via cutting plane methods [S1, unofficial].
