# General Perspective Transformation

Also called: Eye-to-screen transformation, Perspective Transformation with Eye and Projection Plane Off-Origin

## Overview
General Perspective Transformation is a camera model where both the eye position and the projection plane are displaced from the world origin, unlike standard canonical perspective projections which typically fix the eye at the origin or the projection plane at $z=0$ [S1]. This method solves the problem of numerical instability in screen-space depth values (screen-z) when rendering scenes with large bounding boxes or when the visibility bounding box (Vbb) is far from the origin [S1]. By shifting the eye and projection plane together, it ensures the Jacobian of the transformation is the identity at the center of the visibility bounding box, minimizing floating-point errors in depth ordering during compositing [S1]. It differs from [Average Pose Calculation](1645-average-pose-calculation.md) by focusing on the geometric projection matrix construction rather than pose averaging, and from [Camera Look-at Basis Construction](3990-camera-look-at-basis-construction.md) by explicitly managing the off-origin projection plane geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Eye offset parameter $e_o$: The distance from the projection plane to the eye along the optical axis in the canonical frame before translation.
- Translation parameter $z_t$: The user-specified translation in the z-direction that moves both the eye and the projection plane away from the origin [S1].
- Visibility Bounding Box (Vbb): The 3D region containing the geometry to be rendered, centered at $(0, 0, -e_o)$ before the user translation [S1].

**Outputs:**
- A perspective projection matrix (or transformation logic) that maps 3D world coordinates to 2D screen coordinates with corrected depth values [S1].
- The effective eye position in the transformed coordinate system.
- The effective projection plane equation.

**Data Structures:**
- No specific complex data structures are required beyond standard 4x4 matrix representations for the final projection matrix. The core logic relies on scalar parameters $e_o$ and $z_t$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1. **Define Canonical Geometry:** Establish the initial canonical perspective setup where the eye is at $(0, 0, e_o)$ and the projection plane is at $z=0$ [S1]. The Vbb is centered at $(0, 0, -e_o)$ in this canonical frame [S1].
2. **Apply User Translation:** Apply a translation in the z-direction by $z_t$. This translation moves both the eye and the projection plane simultaneously [S1].
3. **Calculate New Eye Position:** The new eye position $E$ is calculated as:
   $$ E = (0, 0, e_o - z_t) $$
   [S1]
4. **Calculate New Projection Plane:** The new projection plane equation is:
   $$ z = -z_t $$
   [S1]
5. **Verify Jacobian Identity:** Ensure that at the center of the Vbb (which is now at $(0, 0, -e_o - z_t)$ relative to the new eye? No, the text states the Jacobian is identity at the center of the Vbb. The Vbb center in the *original* canonical frame was $(0,0,-e_o)$. After translation by $-z_t$ (implied by moving eye/projection plane), the geometry shifts. The key property is that this specific shift ensures the Jacobian determinant of the projection mapping is 1 at the center of the Vbb, preventing depth sorting artifacts [S1].

**Formulas:**
- Eye Position: $E = (0, 0, e_o - z_t)$ [S1]
- Projection Plane: $z = -z_t$ [S1]
- Jacobian Property: $|J| = 1$ at the center of the Vbb [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $e_o$: Typical value depends on the scene scale and desired field of view. Not given in the sources.
- $z_t$: Chosen such that the center of the Vbb aligns with the point where the Jacobian is identity. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision Loss:** If $z_t$ is not chosen appropriately relative to the Vbb size, standard perspective transformations suffer from floating-point errors in screen-z values, leading to incorrect depth ordering of closely spaced triangles [S1].
- **Vbb Centering:** The method assumes the Vbb is known and can be centered relative to the transformed projection plane. If the Vbb is not centered, the Jacobian identity property may not hold at the visual center [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def general_perspective_transformation(e_o, z_t, vbb_center):
    """
    Computes the effective eye and projection plane for a general perspective transformation.
    
    Args:
        e_o (float): Canonical eye distance from projection plane.
        z_t (float): User-specified translation in z.
        vbb_center (tuple): Center of the visibility bounding box (x, y, z).
        
    Returns:
        tuple: (eye_position, projection_plane_z, is_jacobian_identity_at_center)
    """
    # Step 1 & 2: Apply user translation
    # The eye moves from (0, 0, e_o) to (0, 0, e_o - z_t)
    eye_position = (0.0, 0.0, e_o - z_t)
    
    # The projection plane moves from z=0 to z = -z_t
    projection_plane_z = -z_t
    
    # Step 3: Check Jacobian property
    # The method ensures Jacobian is identity at the center of the Vbb.
    # This is a geometric property of the transformation defined by e_o and z_t.
    # We assume the implementation correctly sets up the projection matrix
    # to satisfy this condition.
    is_jacobian_identity_at_center = True  # [G] Assumed by the method's design [S1]
    
    return eye_position, projection_plane_z, is_jacobian_identity_at_center
```

## Subtopics

### [Numerically Stable Perspective Transformation](2008-numerically-stable-perspective-transformation.md)
This method prevents floating-point errors in screen-z values that occur when translating vertices far from the origin in standard perspective projections, which otherwise cause ordering inconsistencies during the compositing of thousands of closely spaced triangles. It differs from standard perspective transformations by positioning the eye away from the world origin, thereby establishing a geometric basis where relative errors are minimized without requiring double-precision arithmetic. Choose this when rendering large scenes with high-precision depth requirements where single-precision floating-point depth buffers are insufficient for correct z-sorting. See [Numerically Stable Perspective Transformation](2008-numerically-stable-perspective-transformation.md).

### Film Shift
Film shift constrains the film position by the refraction of the ray connecting the lens to the pivot. The shifted film interaction position $x'_{\text{film}}$ is evaluated using $x'_{\text{lens}}$ and $x'_{\text{pivot}}$. The Jacobian for this shift is given by:
$$ |S'_{i \rightarrow j}(x_{\text{film}})| = \frac{g(x_{\text{lens}}, x_{\text{pivot}})}{g(x_{\text{lens}}, x_{\text{film}})} \times \frac{g(x'_{\text{lens}}, x'_{\text{pivot}})}{g(x'_{\text{lens}}, x'_{\text{film}})} $$
where $g(a,b) = D(a,b) / |b-a|^2$ and $D(a,b) = |\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{ab} \times \tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}_b|$ if $b$ is on a surface, else $D(a,b) = 1$ [S3]. This is used in volumetric multi-view rendering to account for changes in measure when shifting interactions [S3].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The transformation itself is a simple matrix multiplication or coordinate shift, $O(1)$ per vertex. The benefit is reduced need for double-precision arithmetic or complex depth sorting corrections, potentially improving overall rendering performance in large scenes [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory for parameters $e_o$ and $z_t$.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling:** Scales well with scene size because it mitigates depth precision issues that worsen with distance from the origin [S1].
- **Strengths:** Eliminates screen-z ordering artifacts for large bounding boxes; maintains single-precision accuracy [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires knowledge of the Vbb center to set $z_t$ optimally; less common in standard graphics pipelines which assume origin-centered cameras [S1].
- **When to use:** When rendering large volumes or scenes with significant depth range where standard perspective projection causes z-fighting or incorrect compositing order [S1].
- **When not to use:** For small scenes near the origin where standard perspective projection is sufficient and simpler to implement [S1].

## Background
The General Perspective Transformation was introduced in the context of hierarchical and parallelizable direct volume rendering for irregular and multiple grids [S1]. It addresses the specific challenges of volume rendering where correct depth ordering of many small primitives (like isosurface triangles) is critical for correct compositing [S1]. The method was developed to overcome the limitations of standard OpenGL-style perspective projections which assume the eye is at the origin or the projection plane is at $z=0$, leading to precision issues when the scene is far from the origin [S1].
