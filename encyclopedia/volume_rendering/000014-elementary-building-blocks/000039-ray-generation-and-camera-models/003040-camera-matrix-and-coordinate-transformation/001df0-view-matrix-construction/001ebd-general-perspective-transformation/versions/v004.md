# General Perspective Transformation

Also called: Eye-to-screen transformation, Perspective Transformation with Eye and Projection Plane Off-Origin

## Overview
General Perspective Transformation is a camera model technique where both the eye position and the projection plane are displaced from the world origin to optimize numerical stability during volume rendering [S1]. Unlike standard fixed-function pipelines that often assume an eye at the origin or a projection plane at $z=0$, this method allows arbitrary placement of the eye and plane, ensuring the Jacobian of the transformation is the identity at the center of the visibility bounding box (Vbb) [S1]. This separation prevents floating-point errors in screen-z values that occur when translating vertices far from the origin, which otherwise cause ordering inconsistencies during the compositing of thousands of closely spaced triangles [S1]. It differs from standard perspective transformations by establishing a geometric basis where relative errors are minimized without requiring double-precision arithmetic [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Eye offset parameter $e_o$, translation parameter $z_t$, and the center of the visibility bounding box (Vbb).
*   **Outputs:** A 4x4 transformation matrix mapping world coordinates to clip/screen space, and the transformed vertex positions.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Eye Position:** Defined as $(0, 0, e_o - z_t)$ in the camera coordinate system.
*   **Projection Plane:** Defined as the plane $z = -z_t$.
*   **Transformation Matrix:** A 4x4 matrix combining translation and perspective projection. The specific matrix elements depend on the clipping planes ($f_{left}, f_{right}, f_{down}, f_{up}, f_{near}, f_{far}$) [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Define Visibility Bounding Box (Vbb):** Determine the center of the Vbb in world space. Let this center be $C_{Vbb}$.
2.  **Calculate Translation Parameters:**
    *   The user specifies a translation in $z$, denoted as $z_t$.
    *   The eye point is positioned at $(0, 0, e_o - z_t)$. Here, $e_o$ is a base eye distance parameter.
    *   The projection plane is positioned at $z = -z_t$.
    *   This configuration ensures that the Jacobian of the transformation is the identity matrix at the center of the Vbb [S1].
3.  **Construct Projection Matrix:**
    *   Define the six clipping planes bounding the view frustum: $f_{left}, f_{right}, f_{down}, f_{up}, f_{near}, f_{far}$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Construct the perspective projection matrix $P$ using these parameters. In OpenGL-style conventions, this is often achieved via `glFrustum` [S2, unofficial]. The matrix maps the frustum to clip space.
    *   The general form of a perspective projection matrix $P$ for a frustum defined by $l, r, b, t, n, f$ is:
        $$
        P = \begin{bmatrix}
        \frac{2n}{r-l} & 0 & \frac{r+l}{r-l} & 0 \\
        0 & \frac{2n}{t-b} & \frac{t+b}{t-b} & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & -\frac{f+n}{f-n} & -\frac{2fn}{f-n} \\
        0 & 0 & -1 & 0
        \end{bmatrix}
        $$
        Where $n = f_{near}$ and $f = f_{far}$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Apply Transformation:**
    *   For each vertex $v$ in world space, transform it to clip space $v_c$ using the combined view-projection matrix.
    *   Perform perspective division to obtain normalized device coordinates (NDC).

**Parameters:**
*   $e_o$: Base eye distance. Typical value depends on scene scale.
*   $z_t$: Translation parameter. Chosen such that the projection plane is optimally located relative to the Vbb center.
*   $f_{left}, f_{right}, f_{down}, f_{up}, f_{near}, f_{far}$: Clipping plane distances. $f_{near}$ and $f_{far}$ must be positive and $f_{far} > f_{near}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision Loss:** If the eye and projection plane are not adjusted relative to the Vbb center, significant floating-point errors can occur in screen-z values for vertices far from the origin [S1].
*   **Degenerate Frustum:** If $f_{near} = f_{far}$ or if the clipping planes are invalid (e.g., $f_{left} \ge f_{right}$), the projection is undefined.
*   **Jacobian Identity:** The condition that the Jacobian is identity at the Vbb center is critical for maintaining correct depth ordering in volume rendering compositing [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def general_perspective_transformation(vbb_center, e_o, z_t, f_left, f_right, f_down, f_up, f_near, f_far):
    """
    Constructs a general perspective transformation matrix.
    
    Args:
        vbb_center: (x, y, z) coordinates of the visibility bounding box center.
        e_o: Base eye distance parameter.
        z_t: Translation parameter for eye and projection plane.
        f_left, f_right, f_down, f_up, f_near, f_far: Clipping plane parameters.
        
    Returns:
        A 4x4 transformation matrix (list of lists or numpy array).
    """
    # Step 1: Define Eye and Projection Plane
    # Eye is at (0, 0, e_o - z_t) in camera space
    # Projection plane is at z = -z_t in camera space
    # Note: The actual world-to-camera transformation depends on the camera orientation,
    # which is handled by the parent View Matrix Construction.
    # This function focuses on the perspective projection component.
    
    # Step 2: Construct Perspective Projection Matrix
    # Using the standard glFrustum-like formulation
    if f_right == f_left or f_up == f_down or f_far == f_near:
        raise ValueError("Invalid clipping planes: dimensions must be non-zero and f_far > f_near")
        
    # Calculate matrix elements
    # Note: The exact matrix structure depends on the coordinate system convention (OpenGL vs DirectX).
    # Assuming OpenGL convention (right-handed, clip space z in [-1, 1] or [0, 1] depending on version).
    # The formula below assumes OpenGL clip space z in [-1, 1] for the projection matrix part.
    
    # Perspective projection matrix P
    P = [
        [2 * f_near / (f_right - f_left), 0, (f_right + f_left) / (f_right - f_left), 0],
        [0, 2 * f_near / (f_up - f_down), (f_up + f_down) / (f_up - f_down), 0],
        [0, 0, -(f_far + f_near) / (f_far - f_near), -2 * f_far * f_near / (f_far - f_near)],
        [0, 0, -1, 0]
    ]
    
    # Step 3: Combine with View Matrix
    # The full transformation is View * P.
    # The View matrix is constructed by the parent method [View Matrix Construction](1644-view-matrix-construction.md).

    return P
```

## Subtopics

### [Numerically Stable Perspective Transformation](2008-numerically-stable-perspective-transformation.md)
This method prevents floating-point errors in screen-z values that occur when translating vertices far from the origin in standard perspective projections, which otherwise cause ordering inconsistencies during the compositing of thousands of closely spaced triangles. It differs from standard perspective transformations by positioning the eye away from the world origin, thereby establishing a geometric basis where relative errors are minimized without requiring double-precision arithmetic.

### [Custom Perspective Projection Matrix Construction](2175-custom-perspective-projection-matrix-construction.md)
This method constructs the explicit mathematical matrices required to transform 3D scene coordinates into clip space for rendering, specifically tailored for volume rendering techniques like Gaussian Splatting. It solves the problem of establishing the correct camera coordinate system and perspective distortion by combining view and projection matrices, often handling coordinate system conversions (e.g., OpenGL to WGPU) via explicit matrix multiplication or axis flipping. It differs from standard fixed-function pipeline setups by explicitly managing the computation of tangent-based Field of View (FOV) parameters for shader uniforms and allowing for custom focal lengths and aspect ratios rather than relying solely on standard API calls.

### Film Shift
Constrain the film position by the refraction of the ray connecting the lens to the pivot. Evaluate the shifted film interaction position $x'_{\text{film}}$ using $x'_{\text{lens}}$ and $x'_{\text{pivot}}$. The Jacobian for this shift is given by:
$$
|S'_{i \rightarrow j}(x_{\text{film}})| = \frac{g(x_{\text{lens}}, x_{\text{pivot}})}{g(x_{\text{lens}}, x_{\text{film}})} \times \frac{g(x'_{\text{lens}}, x'_{\text{pivot}})}{g(x'_{\text{lens}}, x'_{\text{film}})}
$$
Where $g(a,b) = D(a,b) / |b-a|^2$ and $D(a,b) = |\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{ab} \times \tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}_b|$ if $b$ is on a surface, else $D(a,b) = 1$ [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per vertex for matrix multiplication and perspective division.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for the transformation matrix.
*   **Performance:** Improves rendering accuracy for large scenes by reducing z-fighting and depth sorting errors [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Numerical stability for large coordinate values; flexibility in camera placement.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful calculation of clipping planes and translation parameters to maintain the identity Jacobian at the Vbb center [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When rendering scenes with large coordinate ranges or when high precision in depth sorting is critical.
*   **When Not to Use:** For simple scenes with small coordinate ranges where standard perspective projection suffices.

## Background
The General Perspective Transformation was introduced in the context of hierarchical and parallelizable direct volume rendering for irregular and multiple grids [S1]. It addresses the limitations of standard perspective projections in maintaining numerical stability when dealing with large bounding boxes. The method is particularly relevant in volume rendering applications where depth ordering of many small primitives is essential.
