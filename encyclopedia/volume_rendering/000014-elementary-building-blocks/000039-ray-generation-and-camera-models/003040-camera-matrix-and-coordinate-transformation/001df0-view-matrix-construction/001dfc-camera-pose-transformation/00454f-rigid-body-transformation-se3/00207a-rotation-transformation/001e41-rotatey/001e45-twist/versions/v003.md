# Twist
Also called: Twist Transformation

## Overview
Twist is a geometric transformation that twists a shape along an axis [S1, unofficial]. Specifically, it twists space around the Y-axis proportional to height, where $k$ controls the rate of twist [S2, unofficial]. Unlike [RotateY](1720-rotatey.md), which applies a uniform rotation to the entire object, Twist applies a spatially varying rotation where the angle depends on the Y-coordinate [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A point $p = (p_x, p_y, p_z)$ and a twist factor $k$ (float) [S1, S2, unofficial].
- Output: A transformed point $p'$ [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No persistent data structures. The transformation is computed per-point during SDF evaluation [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Calculate the twist angle $\theta$ based on the Y-coordinate and the twist factor:
   $$ \theta = k \cdot p_y $$
   [S2, unofficial]
2. Compute the sine and cosine of the angle:
   $$ c = \cos(\theta) $$
   $$ s = \sin(\theta) $$
   [S2, unofficial]
3. Apply a 2D rotation matrix to the X and Z components of the point, leaving the Y component unchanged:
   $$ p'_x = c \cdot p_x - s \cdot p_z $$
   $$ p'_y = p_y $$
   $$ p'_z = s \cdot p_x + c \cdot p_z $$
   [S2, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def twist(p: tuple, k: float) -> tuple:
    """Twists space around the Y-axis proportional to height."""
    px, py, pz = p
    angle = k * py
    cos_a = math.cos(angle)
    sin_a = math.sin(angle)
    new_x = cos_a * px - sin_a * pz
    new_y = py
    new_z = sin_a * px + cos_a * pz
    return (new_x, new_y, new_z)
```

**Parameters:**
- $k$: The twist factor controlling the rate of twist [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** For very large values of $k \cdot p_y$, standard floating-point arithmetic may lose precision in trigonometric evaluations [G].
- **Boundaries:** The twist is continuous across space. During raymarching, high twist rates relative to step sizes may cause visible discretization artifacts if the step size is not adaptive [G].

## Subtopics

### [Bend](2044-bend.md)
Bend is a geometric deformation operation for Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) that curves a shape around the X-axis. It sets itself apart from Twist by applying a spatially varying rotation around the X-axis to create a smooth arc, rather than rotating around the Y-axis. Choose it when a simple arc or bend along the X-axis is desired.

### [Bend Transformation](2729-bend-transformation.md)
The Bend Transformation bends 3D space along the X-axis around the Y-axis. It sets itself apart from its sibling [Bend](2044-bend.md) and parent [Twist](1722-twist.md) by its specific axis of rotation (Y) and the linear dependency of the rotation angle on the X-coordinate, rather than the Z-coordinate or a global twist. Choose it when a specific X-axis curvature effect is needed.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per point evaluation. The transformation involves a constant number of arithmetic operations and two trigonometric function calls [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$. The operation requires only a few temporary scalar variables for intermediate calculations [G].
- **Strengths:** Computationally inexpensive and simple to implement [G].
- **Limitations:** The transformation is non-linear, which complicates analytical normal calculation. Normals must be transformed using the inverse transpose of the Jacobian of the transformation to remain perpendicular to the deformed surface [G].

## Background
Twist is used in Signed Distance Field (SDF) raymarching to deform primitives [S3, unofficial].
