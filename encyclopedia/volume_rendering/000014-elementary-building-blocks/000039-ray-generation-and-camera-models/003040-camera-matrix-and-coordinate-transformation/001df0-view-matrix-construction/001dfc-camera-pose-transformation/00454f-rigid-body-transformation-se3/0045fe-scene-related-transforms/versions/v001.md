# Scene-Related Transforms
## Overview
Scene-related transforms are a class of imaging transforms that operate primarily on 3D scenes rather than on specific objects extracted from them [S1]. They facilitate the transformation of information between scene, object, and view coordinate spaces [S1]. This family is distinct from structure-related transforms, which focus on individual objects [S1].

## Shared principles
The core principle of scene-related transforms is their domain of operation: the entire scene volume or space [S1]. They are applicable to both grey-level and binary scene representations, although their utility for binary scenes may be trivial in some contexts [S1]. These transforms map data between different coordinate systems, specifically among scene, object, and view spaces [S1].

## Background
The concept was introduced by J. Udupa and P. Goncalves in their work on imaging transforms for visualizing surfaces and volumes [S1]. The classification into scene-related and structure-related transforms provides a framework for organizing basic imaging operations based on whether they act on the global scene or local object structures [S1].
