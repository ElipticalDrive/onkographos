# Camera Pose Transformation

Also called: Camera Offset and Rotation, Pose Transformation, RotateX

## Overview
Camera Pose Transformation applies geometric operations—specifically translation and rotation—to camera-to-world (c2w) pose matrices or 3D points to align the virtual camera with a desired viewing configuration or to normalize a set of poses for volume rendering [S1][S3, S6, unofficial]. It solves the problem of mapping coordinates between different reference frames (e.g., converting COLMAP's world-to-camera outputs to camera-to-world formats, or centering a scene at the origin) while preserving the rigid body structure of the camera [S1, S5][S6, unofficial]. It differs from [View Matrix Construction](1644-view-matrix-construction.md) by focusing on the explicit manipulation of pose parameters (rotation angles, translation vectors) rather than constructing the final view matrix from basis vectors, and from [Average Pose Calculation](1645-average-pose-calculation.md) by operating on individual poses or specific transformations rather than computing statistical averages [S1][S3, unofficial][S5].

## How it works
The method applies rotation matrices to orientation components and adjusts translation vectors to account for coordinate system inversions or scene centering [S1][S3, unofficial][S5][S6, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A camera pose representation, typically a $3 \times 4$ matrix $P = [R_p \ t_p]$ where $R_p \in SO(3)$ is the rotation matrix and $t_p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the translation vector [S1]. Alternatively, inputs may be raw rotation angles and a translation distance [S6, unofficial], or continuous velocity vectors [S4].
*   **Output:** A transformed camera pose $P'$ in the target coordinate system (e.g., camera-to-world, recentered, or continuous motion parameters) [S1, S5][S6, unofficial].

### Data Structures
1.  **Pose Matrix ($P$):** A $3 \times 4$ matrix [S1].
    *   Fields: $R_p$ (3x3 rotation submatrix), $t_p$ (3x1 translation column vector).
    *   Layout: Mathematically defined as $P = [R_p \ t_p]$ [S1].
2.  **Pose Vector ($\eta$):** A vector containing rotation angles and translation distance [S6, unofficial].
    *   Fields: $R_x, R_y$ (rotation angles in degrees), $D_z$ (translation distance along z-axis) [S6, unofficial].
3.  **Velocity Vectors:** $\omega(t), v(t) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ representing angular and linear velocities [S4].

### Steps and Formulas

#### 1. Standard Rotation and Translation (Point-wise)
Used for simple scene alignment or shader-based transformations [S3, unofficial].

1.  **Translation:** Offset the point $p$ by a vector $t_{offset}$ [S3, unofficial].
    $$ p' = p + t_{offset} $$
    *   Typical value: $t_{offset} = [0, -1, -2.2]$ for specific scene origins [S3, unofficial].
2.  **Rotation:** Rotate the point $p'$ around an axis (e.g., Y-axis) by angle $\theta$ [S3, unofficial].
    For Y-axis rotation:
    $$ p'_{y} = \cos(\theta) p_{y} + \sin(\theta) p_{z} $$
    $$ p'_{z} = -\sin(\theta) p_{y} + \cos(\theta) p_{z} $$
    *   Parameter: $\theta$ (angle in radians, e.g., 0.3) [S3, unofficial].

#### 2. Coordinate System Conversion (World-to-Camera to Camera-to-World)
Used when converting outputs from structure-from-motion tools like COLMAP to formats required by volume rendering pipelines (e.g., LLFF) [S5].

1.  **Input:** World-to-camera pose with rotation $R$ and translation $t$ [S5].
2.  **Compute Camera-to-World Translation:** Since the inverse of a rotation matrix $R$ is its transpose $R^T$, the camera-to-world translation $t'$ is computed as [S5]:
    $$ t' = -R^T \cdot t $$
3.  **Construct Output Pose:** The camera-to-world matrix $M$ is:
    $$ M = \begin{bmatrix} R^T & t' \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    *   Note: The rotation component $R$ in the world-to-camera matrix becomes $R^T$ in the camera-to-world matrix [S5].

#### 3. Pose Representation for Arcball Control
Used for interactive volume rendering where the camera view is centered on the volume [S6, unofficial].

1.  **Input:** Rotation angles $R_x, R_y$ (degrees) and zoom distance $D_z$ [S6, unofficial].
2.  **Centering:** Ensure the camera view is centered on the volume origin [S6, unofficial].
3.  **Application:** The pose vector $\eta = (R_x, R_y, D_z)$ is used to update the camera position and orientation incrementally [S6, unofficial].

#### 4. Continuous Camera Motion Modeling
Used for video-based NeRF where discrete poses are insufficient [S4].

1.  **Input:** Time $t$ [S4].
2.  **Predict Velocities:** Use an MLP $\phi_v$ to predict angular velocity $\omega(t)$ and linear velocity $v(t)$ [S4]:
    $$ \omega(t), v(t) = \phi_v(t) $$
3.  **Integrate Motion:** Solve the differential equations to obtain rotation $R$ and translation $\mathbf{t}$ over time interval $\Delta t$ [S4]:
    $$ \frac{dR}{dt} = [\omega(t)]_\times R $$
    $$ \frac{d\mathbf{t}}{dt} = v(t) $$
    where $[\omega(t)]_\times$ is the skew-symmetric matrix of $\omega(t)$ [S4].
4.  **Discrete Update (Euler Method):**
    $$ R_{t \to t+l} = \prod_{u=0}^{U-1} \psi(\omega(t + u\Delta t)\Delta t) $$
    $$ \mathbf{t}_{t \to t+l} = \sum_{u=0}^{U-1} v(t + u\Delta t)\Delta t $$
    where $\psi(\cdot)$ converts rotation angles to a rotation matrix and $\Delta t = l/U$ [S4].

### Pseudocode

```python
import math
import numpy as np

def rotate_point_y_axis(point: list, angle_rad: float) -> list:
    """
    Rotates a 3D point around the Y-axis by a given angle.
    Based on [S3, unofficial].
    """
    x, y, z = point
    cos_a = math.cos(angle_rad)
    sin_a = math.sin(angle_rad)
    
    # Apply 2D rotation to y and z components using temporary variables to avoid source bug
    new_y = cos_a * y + sin_a * z
    new_z = -sin_a * y + cos_a * z
    
    return [x, new_y, new_z]

def convert_w2c_to_c2w(rotation_w2c: np.ndarray, translation_w2c: np.ndarray) -> tuple:
    """
    Converts a world-to-camera pose (R, t) to a camera-to-world pose (R', t').
    Based on [S5].
    
    Args:
        rotation_w2c: 3x3 rotation matrix for world-to-camera.
        translation_w2c: 3x1 translation vector for world-to-camera.
        
    Returns:
        rotation_c2w: 3x3 rotation matrix for camera-to-world.
        translation_c2w: 3x1 translation vector for camera-to-world.
    """
    # Inverse of rotation is its transpose
    rotation_c2w = rotation_w2c.T
    
    # Translation conversion: t' = -R^T * t
    translation_c2w = -rotation_c2w @ translation_w2c
    
    return rotation_c2w, translation_c2w

def apply_pose_offset(point: list, offset: list) -> list:
    """
    Offsets a point by a translation vector.
    Based on [S3, unofficial].
    """
    return [p + o for p, o in zip(point, offset)]

def integrate_continuous_motion(omega: np.ndarray, v: np.ndarray, dt: float) -> tuple:
    """
    Integrates continuous camera motion using Euler method for one step.
    Based on [S4].
    
    Args:
        omega: Angular velocity vector (3,).
        v: Linear velocity vector (3,).
        dt: Time step.
        
    Returns:
        delta_R: Rotation matrix representing the change.
        delta_t: Translation vector representing the change.
    """
    # Skew-symmetric matrix of omega
    wx, wy, wz = omega
    skew_omega = np.array([
        [0, -wz, wy],
        [wz, 0, -wx],
        [-wy, wx, 0]
    ])
    
    # dR/dt = [omega]_x * R
    # For small dt, delta_R approx I + [omega]_x * dt
    delta_R = np.eye(3) + skew_omega * dt
    
    # d_t/dt = v
    delta_t = v * dt
    
    return delta_R, delta_t
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Rotation Angle ($\theta$):** Radians. Example: 0.3 [S3, unofficial].
*   **Translation Offset:** Vector. Example: $[0, -1, -2.2]$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Noise Coefficient:** For simulating imperfect poses, a coefficient of 0.35 generates noise $n \in se(3)$ corresponding to ~30.4° rotation deviation and 0.56 translation deviation [S1].
*   **Time Step ($\Delta t$):** Depends on video frame rate and integration window $l$ [S4].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** Floating-point errors can accumulate during iterative integration of continuous motion [S4].
*   **Coordinate System Mismatch:** Failing to transpose the rotation matrix when converting between world-to-camera and camera-to-world leads to incorrect orientations [S5].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Rotation matrices must remain orthogonal; numerical drift during integration may require re-orthogonalization (e.g., via SVD or Gram-Schmidt) [G].

## Subtopics

### [Pose Recentering](1786-pose-recentering.md)
Transforms a set of c2w poses so their average pose becomes the identity, centering the scene at the origin. Essential for NeRF numerical stability. Simultaneously handles positional centering and rotational alignment, unlike [Rotation Transformation](2279-rotation-transformation.md) or [Translation Transformation](2281-translation-transformation.md) which handle only one aspect [G].

### [Rotation Transformation](2279-rotation-transformation.md)
Rotates points/vectors around a specified axis (X, Y, Z) by an angle. Constructs the rotational component of view matrices. Operates directly on Cartesian coordinates using trigonometry, simpler for single-axis rotations than [Quaternion Rotation](2302-quaternion-rotation.md) but lacks singularity-free interpolation.

### [Translation Transformation](2281-translation-transformation.md)
Moves points by a vector without altering orientation. Used for shifting spatial coordinates. Excludes rotational components, unlike [Rigid Body Transformation (SE3)](11475-rigid-body-transformation-se3.md).

### [C-Arm to Camera Parameters Conversion](2589-c-arm-to-camera-parameters-conversion.md)
Converts C-arm geometric parameters (SID, AP angle, lateral angle, center point, table translation) into a camera pose (position, look-at, up vector) in RAS coordinates.

### [Camera Matrix Decomposition](3164-camera-matrix-decomposition.md)
Decomposes a 3x4 projection matrix into intrinsic $K$, rotation $R$, and translation $t$. Separates optical properties from physical pose.

### [CameraMover](8734-cameramover.md)
A utility class for incrementally updating a 4x4 extrinsic matrix using sequential translations and rotations. Supports axis-aligned moves and aiming at world positions, encapsulating state management better than individual [RotateY](1720-rotatey.md) or [Translation Transformation](2281-translation-transformation.md) calls.

### [Rigid Body Transformation (SE3)](11475-rigid-body-transformation-se3.md)
Applies coupled rotations and translations in the Special Euclidean group $SE(3)$ [G]. General applicability to any rigid mapping, unlike [Pose Recentering](1786-pose-recentering.md) which is specific to centering [G].

### [Camera-to-Object Space Transformation & MVP Computation](12391-camera-to-object-space-transformation-mvp-computation.md)
Precomputes the MVP matrix and transforms camera parameters into object space for shader-based rendering (e.g., box overlays). Handles synchronization of projection types and provides inverse-transformed camera direction/position. Builds on [Camera Pose Transformation](1672-camera-pose-transformation.md) by consuming the pose data to construct the final transformation chain.

### Continuous Camera Motion Modeling
Models camera motion as continuous time-dependent angular velocity $\omega(t)$ and linear velocity $v(t)$ instead of discrete poses [S4]. Enables handling of large motions by integrating relative motions over time [S4]. Uses an MLP to predict velocities and solves differential equations $dR/dt = [\omega(t)]_\times R$ and $d\mathbf{t}/dt = v(t)$ [S4].

### LLFF Camera Pose Conversion
Converts COLMAP's world-to-camera pose parameters into the camera-to-world transformation matrix required by the LLFF dataset format [S5]. Specifically converts the translation vector $t$ to $t'$ using $t' = -R^T \cdot t$ [S5].

### Camera Pose Representation
Represents camera position and orientation using a vector of rotation angles ($R_x, R_y$) and a single translation distance ($D_z$) along the z-axis [S6, unofficial]. Assumes the camera view is always centered on the volume [S6, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ for standard rotation/translation of a single point or pose matrix. $O(N)$ for processing $N$ poses in a dataset. Continuous motion modeling requires $O(T)$ for $T$ time steps, with overhead from MLP inference [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for single pose transformations. $O(N)$ for storing a sequence of poses or continuous motion parameters [G].
*   **Performance:** Standard matrix operations are highly optimized on GPUs. Continuous motion modeling adds computational cost due to neural network evaluation and integration steps [S4].
*   **Strengths:** Flexible; supports various input formats (matrices, vectors, continuous velocities). Essential for preprocessing data from different sources (e.g., COLMAP) into formats compatible with volume rendering engines [S5].
*   **Limitations:** Discrete transformations may not capture smooth motion as well as continuous modeling [S4]. Coordinate system conversions require careful handling of transposes and signs to avoid orientation errors [S5].
*   **When to Use:** When aligning camera poses to a specific coordinate system, converting between pose representations, or centering a scene for NeRF training [G].
*   **When Not to Use:** When high-fidelity smooth motion interpolation is required without discrete pose assumptions; consider continuous motion modeling instead [S4].

## Background
Camera pose transformation is a fundamental operation in computer vision and graphics, originating from the mathematical formulation of rigid body transformations in the Special Euclidean group $SE(3)$ [S1]. The use of Lie groups to describe camera poses has been widely adopted in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) research to handle noisy or imperfect camera poses, as seen in methods like CBARF [S1]. The conversion between world-to-camera and camera-to-world representations is standard practice in Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines, particularly when adapting outputs from tools like COLMAP to specific NeRF dataset formats like LLFF [S5]. Continuous camera motion modeling extends these concepts to video-based NeRF, allowing for the joint optimization of scene geometry and camera motion from monocular videos [S4].
