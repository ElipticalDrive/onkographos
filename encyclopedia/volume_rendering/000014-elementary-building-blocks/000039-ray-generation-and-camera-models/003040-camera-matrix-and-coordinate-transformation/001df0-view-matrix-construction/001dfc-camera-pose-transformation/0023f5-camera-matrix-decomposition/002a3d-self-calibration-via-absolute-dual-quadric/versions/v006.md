# Self-Calibration via Absolute Dual Quadric

Also called: Linear Self-Calibration, Projective Self-Calibration, Projective to Metric Upgrade, Self-Calibration via Absolute Quadric, Self-Calibration via Plane at Infinity, Upgrade to Metric Structure

## Overview
Self-calibration via the absolute dual quadric is a method to recover metric (Euclidean) camera intrinsics and 3D structure from a projective reconstruction obtained from uncalibrated images [S1, unofficial]. It solves the ambiguity of projective reconstruction by imposing constraints on the absolute dual quadric $\Omega^*$, which is invariant under Euclidean transformations [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods requiring known calibration targets, this approach derives intrinsic parameters solely from the geometry of the scene across multiple views [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from direct linear methods by leveraging the global constraint of the absolute dual quadric to upgrade a projective structure to a metric one, building on [Camera Matrix Decomposition](3164-camera-matrix-decomposition.md) [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of $n$ projective camera matrices $\hat{P}_i$ for $i=1 \dots n$, derived from a projective factorization of the point correspondence matrix [S1, unofficial]. Each $\hat{P}_i$ is a $3 \times 4$ matrix [S1, unofficial].
- Assumptions on intrinsic parameters (see Parameters) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Metric camera matrices $P_i^M = K_i [R_i | t_i]$ [S1, unofficial].
- Metric 3D points $X^M$ [S1, unofficial].
- The rectifying transformation $H$ (Projective Distortion Matrix) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- $\hat{P}_i$: $3 \times 4$ matrix. Partitioned into $\hat{P}_i = [S_i | s_i]$, where $S_i$ is $3 \times 3$ and $s_i$ is $3 \times 1$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\Omega^*$: $4 \times 4$ symmetric matrix representing the absolute dual quadric in projective space [S1, unofficial].
- $H$: $4 \times 4$ rectifying transformation matrix [S1, unofficial].
- $K_i$: $3 \times 3$ intrinsic calibration matrix [S1, unofficial].
- $\varpi_i^*$: $3 \times 3$ dual image of the absolute conic (DIAC) for view $i$ [S1, unofficial].

**Definitions:**
- $\varpi = (KK^T)^{-1}$: Image of the absolute conic (IAC) [S1, unofficial].
- $\varpi^* = KK^T$: Dual image of the absolute conic (DIAC) [S1, unofficial].
- $\Omega^*$: Absolute dual quadric. In a metric frame, $\Omega^* = \tilde{I} = \begin{pmatrix} I_{3 \times 3} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\omega^* = P \Omega^* P^T$: Projection of the absolute dual quadric into the image plane [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1. **Partition Projective Cameras:**
   For each view $i$, partition the projective camera matrix $\hat{P}_i$ into $S_i$ ($3 \times 3$) and $s_i$ ($3 \times 1$):
   $$ \hat{P}_i = [S_i | s_i] $$
   [S1, unofficial]

2. **Formulate Self-Calibration Equations:**
   The relationship between the metric and projective camera matrices is $P_i^M = \hat{P}_i H$ [S1, unofficial]. Using the structure of $H$ and the orthogonality of rotation matrices ($R_i R_i^T = I$), derive the constraint relating the DIACs:
   $$ \varpi_i^* = (S_i - s_i p^T) \varpi_1^* (S_i - s_i p^T)^T, \quad i = 2, \dots, m $$
   where $p$ is a 3-vector defining the plane at infinity $\pi_\infty = (p^T, 1)^T$ in projective space, and $\varpi_1^*$ is the DIAC of the first camera [S1, unofficial].

3. **Apply Intrinsic Constraints:**
   To linearize the problem, assume the following constraints on the intrinsic parameters for all views $i$:
   - Principal point at image center: $\varpi_{13}^* = \varpi_{23}^* = 0$ [S1, unofficial].
   - Zero skew: $\varpi_{12}^* = 0$ [S1, unofficial].
   - Aspect ratio equal to 1: $\varpi_{11}^* = \varpi_{22}^*$ [S1, unofficial].
   
   These assumptions leave only the focal length as a variable [S1, unofficial]. Each view provides 4 linear constraints on the elements of $\Omega^*$ [S1, unofficial].

4. **Solve for Absolute Dual Quadric:**
   Collect the linear constraints from all $m$ views into an overdetermined linear system $A \mathbf{x} = 0$, where $\mathbf{x}$ contains the independent elements of $\Omega^*$ [S1, unofficial]. The system has dimensions $4m \times 10$ (since $\Omega^*$ is symmetric $4 \times 4$ with 10 unique elements) [S1, unofficial].
   Solve for $\mathbf{x}$ using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) [S1, unofficial]. A unique solution exists for $m \ge 3$ [S1, unofficial].
   Reconstruct the $4 \times 4$ matrix $\Omega^*$ from $\mathbf{x}$ [S1, unofficial].

5. **Compute Rectifying Transformation $H$:**
   Decompose $\Omega^*$ to find $H$. Since $\Omega^* = H \tilde{I} H^T$, and $\tilde{I} = \text{diag}(1,1,1,0)$, $H$ can be found via Cholesky-like decomposition or by solving for the parameters directly [S1, unofficial].
   The structure of $H$ is:
   $$ H = \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & 0 \\ -p^T K_1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
   where $K_1$ is the calibration matrix of the first camera and $p$ is the vector from step 2/4 [S1, unofficial].
   Specifically, from $\Omega^* = \begin{bmatrix} \varpi_1^* & -\varpi_1^* p \\ -p^T \varpi_1^* & p^T \varpi_1^* p \end{bmatrix}$, extract $\varpi_1^*$ and $p$ [S1, unofficial]. Then compute $K_1$ by Cholesky factorization of $\varpi_1^* = K_1 K_1^T$ [S1, unofficial].

6. **Upgrade to Metric Reconstruction:**
   Compute the metric camera matrices:
   $$ P_i^M = \hat{P}_i H $$
   Decompose $P_i^M$ into $K_i [R_i | t_i]$ using standard decomposition (e.g., [Camera Matrix Decomposition](3164-camera-matrix-decomposition.md)) [S1, unofficial].
   Compute the metric 3D points:
   $$ X^M = H^{-1} \hat{X} $$
   where $\hat{X}$ are the projective 3D points [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def self_calibration_absolute_dual_quadric(projective_cameras, num_views):
    """
    projective_cameras: list of n 3x4 matrices P_hat_i
    num_views: number of views m
    Returns: metric_cameras (list of 3x4), H (4x4)
    """
    m = num_views
    S_list = []
    s_list = []
    for i in range(m):
        P_hat = projective_cameras[i]
        S_list.append(P_hat[:, :3])  # 3x3
        s_list.append(P_hat[:, 3:])  # 3x1

    # x contains the 10 independent elements of Omega_star:
    # x = [O00, O01, O02, O03, O11, O12, O13, O22, O23, O33]
    # Mapping from (k, l) with k <= l to x index:
    # (0,0)->0, (0,1)->1, (0,2)->2, (0,3)->3, (1,1)->4, (1,2)->5, (1,3)->6, (2,2)->7, (2,3)->8, (3,3)->9
    def get_x_idx(k, l):
        if k == l:
            return [0, 4, 7, 9][k]
        else:
            pairs = [(0,1), (0,2), (0,3), (1,2), (1,3), (2,3)]
            return pairs.index((min(k,l), max(k,l)))

    A = []
    for i in range(m):
        P = np.hstack([S_list[i], s_list[i]])  # 3x4
        
        def constraint_row(target_i, target_j, sign=1):
            row = np.zeros(10)
            for k in range(4):
                for l in range(k, 4):
                    idx = get_x_idx(k, l)
                    if k == l:
                        coeff = P[target_i, k] * P[target_j, k]
                    else:
                        coeff = P[target_i, k] * P[target_j, l] + P[target_i, l] * P[target_j, k]
                    row[idx] += coeff * sign
            return row

        # Constraints on omega_i_star (3x3 submatrix of P * Omega * P^T)
        # Principal point at center: omega_02=0, omega_12=0
        A.append(constraint_row(0, 2))
        A.append(constraint_row(1, 2))
        # Zero skew: omega_01=0
        A.append(constraint_row(0, 1))
        # Aspect ratio 1: omega_00 - omega_11 = 0
        A.append(constraint_row(0, 0) - constraint_row(1, 1))

    A = np.array(A)
    _, _, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A)
    x = Vt[-1, :]

    # Reconstruct Omega_star
    O00, O01, O02, O03, O11, O12, O13, O22, O23, O33 = x
    Omega_star = np.array([
        [O00, O01, O02, O03],
        [O01, O11, O12, O13],
        [O02, O12, O22, O23],
        [O03, O13, O23, O33]
    ])

    # Extract varpi_1_star and p from Omega_star
    # Omega_star = [[varpi_1*, -varpi_1* p], [-p^T varpi_1*, p^T varpi_1* p]]
    varpi_1_star = Omega_star[:3, :3]
    varpi_1_star_p = Omega_star[:3, 3]
    p = -np.linalg.inv(varpi_1_star) @ varpi_1_star_p
    K_1 = np.linalg.cholesky(varpi_1_star).T
    H = np.eye(4)
    H[:3, :3] = K_1
    H[3, :3] = -p.T @ K_1

    metric_cameras = [P @ H for P in projective_cameras]
    return metric_cameras, H
```

**Parameters:**
- $K_1$: $3 \times 3$ calibration matrix of the first camera. Encodes focal length $f_1$, principal point $(u_{01}, v_{01})$, and aspect ratio $\alpha_1$. $K_1 = \begin{bmatrix} f_1 & 0 & u_{01} \\ 0 & \alpha_1 f_1 & v_{01} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ [S1, unofficial].
- $p$: 3-vector defining the plane at infinity. Total unknowns: 3 for $p$ and 5 for $K_1$ [S1, unofficial].
- $m$: Number of views. Minimum $m=3$ for a unique solution [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Original attempts at self-calibration yielded successful examples only for special cases; for general cases, new algorithms are needed to output explicit Euclidean structures from uncalibrated images [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Linear solution; no initial guess required; robust for small numbers of views ($m \ge 3$) [G].
- **Limitations:** Relies on strong assumptions about intrinsics (zero skew, square pixels). Does not handle varying intrinsics [G].
- **When to use:** When a projective reconstruction is available and the camera has fixed, simple intrinsics (e.g., standard digital cameras with known principal point) [G].
- **When not to use:** When intrinsics are unknown and vary, or when high precision is required without subsequent non-linear refinement [G].

## Background
The method was developed in the context of structure from motion and camera calibration, specifically as part of the stratified reconstruction approach [S1, unofficial]. It relies on the geometric invariance of the absolute dual quadric under Euclidean transformations [S1, unofficial]. The key paper is "Accelerated volumetric reconstruction from uncalibrated camera views" [S1, unofficial], which details the derivation and application of this method for upgrading projective reconstructions to metric ones. The concept of the absolute dual quadric and its role in self-calibration is rooted in the work of Triggs [Triggs97] and Hartley and Zisserman [HarZis00], as cited in [S1, unofficial].
