# C-Arm to Camera Parameters Conversion

Also called: C-Arm View Matrix Construction, C-Arm Geometry to Camera Pose

## Overview
This method converts C-arm X-ray geometric parameters (Source-to-Image Distance, AP angle, lateral angle, center, table translation) into camera position, look-at point, and up vector in RAS coordinates [S1, unofficial]. It differs from generic rotation transformations by explicitly modeling the C-arm's mechanical degrees of freedom (AP and lateral tilts) relative to a patient-centered coordinate system, enabling the generation of synthetic Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) or Digital Radiography (DRR) views [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `sid`: Source-to-Image Distance in mm.
*   `ap_angle`: Anteroposterior angle in degrees.
*   `lat_angle`: Lateral angle in degrees.
*   `center_ras`: 3D vector representing the center of the CT scan in RAS coordinates (mm).
*   `table_si`: Table translation in the Superior-Inferior direction in mm (default 0.0).

**Outputs:**
*   `cam_pos`: Camera (X-ray source) position in RAS coordinates.
*   `look_at`: Look-at point (isocenter) in RAS coordinates.
*   `look_up`: Up vector for the camera orientation in RAS coordinates.

**Data Structures:**
*   Vectors are 3-element arrays/tensors.
*   Rotation matrices are $3 \times 3$ matrices.

**Steps:**
1.  **Angle Conversion:** Convert angles from degrees to radians.
    $$ \text{ap\_rad} = \text{deg2rad}(\text{ap\_angle}) $$
    $$ \text{lat\_rad} = \text{deg2rad}(\text{lat\_angle}) $$
2.  **Center Calculation:** Adjust the center point for table translation. The table moves along the Superior-Inferior axis (Z-axis in RAS).
    $$ \text{center} = \text{center\_ras} + [0, 0, \text{table\_si}]^T $$
3.  **Local Source Position:** Define the initial source position at distance `sid` along the negative Y-axis (assuming standard C-arm orientation where Y is anterior-posterior axis in local frame, but source is "in front").
    $$ \text{source\_local} = [0, -\text{sid}, 0]^T $$
4.  **Rotation Matrices:** Construct rotation matrices for lateral and AP tilts.
    *   Lateral rotation ($R_{lat}$) around the Z-axis:
        $$ R_{lat} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\text{lat\_rad}) & -\sin(\text{lat\_rad}) & 0 \\ \sin(\text{lat\_rad}) & \cos(\text{lat\_rad}) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    *   AP rotation ($R_{ap}$) around the X-axis:
        $$ R_{ap} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \cos(\text{ap\_rad}) & -\sin(\text{ap\_rad}) \\ 0 & \sin(\text{ap\_rad}) & \cos(\text{ap\_rad}) \end{bmatrix} $$
5.  **Combined Rotation:** Combine rotations. The source is first rotated laterally, then by the AP angle.
    $$ R_{total} = R_{ap} \times R_{lat} $$
    $$ \text{source\_rotated} = R_{total} \times \text{source\_local} $$
6.  **Camera Position:** Translate the rotated source vector to the adjusted center.
    $$ \text{cam\_pos} = \text{source\_rotated} + \text{center} $$
7.  **Look-At Point:** The look-at point is the isocenter (adjusted center).
    $$ \text{look\_at} = \text{center} $$
8.  **Up Vector:** Rotate the local up vector (Z-axis) by the combined rotation.
    $$ \text{up\_local} = [0, 0, 1]^T $$
    $$ \text{look\_up} = R_{total} \times \text{up\_local} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def c_arm_to_camera_params(sid, ap_angle, lat_angle, center_ras, table_si=0.0):
    """
    Convert C-arm geometric parameters to camera parameters in RAS coordinates.
    
    Args:
        sid (float): Source-to-Image Distance in mm.
        ap_angle (float): Anteroposterior angle in degrees.
        lat_angle (float): Lateral angle in degrees.
        center_ras (array-like): Center point of CT scan in RAS coordinates (mm).
        table_si (float): Table translation in SI direction in mm.
        
    Returns:
        tuple: (cam_pos, look_at, look_up) as numpy arrays.
    """
    # 1. Convert angles to radians
    ap_rad = np.deg2rad(ap_angle)
    lat_rad = np.deg2rad(lat_angle)
    
    # 2. Calculate center point with table translation
    # Table moves along Z (Superior-Inferior)
    center = np.array(center_ras) + np.array([0.0, 0.0, table_si])
    
    # 3. Define initial source position at SID distance along negative Y axis
    source_local = np.array([0.0, -sid, 0.0])
    
    # 4. Construct lateral rotation matrix R_lat around Z-axis
    cos_lat = np.cos(lat_rad)
    sin_lat = np.sin(lat_rad)
    R_lat = np.array([
        [cos_lat, -sin_lat, 0.0],
        [sin_lat,  cos_lat, 0.0],
        [0.0,      0.0,     1.0]
    ])
    
    # 5. Construct AP rotation matrix R_ap around X-axis
    cos_ap = np.cos(ap_rad)
    sin_ap = np.sin(ap_rad)
    R_ap = np.array([
        [1.0, 0.0,         0.0],
        [0.0, cos_ap, -sin_ap],
        [0.0, sin_ap,  cos_ap]
    ])
    
    # 6. Combine rotations: R_total = R_ap @ R_lat
    R_total = R_ap @ R_lat
    
    # 7. Rotate source
    source_rotated = R_total @ source_local
    
    # 8. Calculate camera position
    cam_pos = source_rotated + center
    
    # 9. Set look-at point to center
    look_at = center
    
    # 10. Define local up vector and rotate it
    up_local = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
    look_up = R_total @ up_local
    
    return cam_pos, look_at, look_up
```

**Parameters:**
*   `sid`: Source-to-Image Distance in mm. Typical values range from 800 mm to 1200 mm for C-arms [S1, unofficial].
*   `ap_angle`: Anteroposterior angle in degrees. 0° = AP view. Positive = caudal (source tilts inferiorly). Negative = cranial (source tilts superiorly) [S1, unofficial].
*   `lat_angle`: Lateral angle in degrees. 0° = AP view. Positive = RAO (source moves to patient's right). Negative = LAO (source moves to patient's left) [S1, unofficial].
*   `center_ras`: Center point of the CT scan in RAS coordinates (mm). RAS = Right, Anterior, Superior [S1, unofficial].
*   `table_si`: Table translation in superior-inferior direction in mm (default: 0.0). Positive = table moves superior (head up). Negative = table moves inferior (head down) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Use float64 for intermediate calculations to avoid precision loss in rotation matrices, especially for small angles [S1, unofficial].
*   **Coordinate System:** Ensure `center_ras` is in the same coordinate system (RAS) as the output. If the volume is in LPS or another system, convert `center_ras` first [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If `sid` is 0, the source coincides with the center, leading to undefined view direction. Guard against `sid <= 0` [G].

### [Random C-Arm View Sampling](2596-random-c-arm-view-sampling.md)
A method to generate random camera-to-world view matrices for C-arm X-ray projections by uniformly sampling geometric parameters (Source-to-Image Distance, AP angle, lateral angle, table SI translation) and converting them to camera coordinates.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ as it involves a fixed number of arithmetic operations and matrix multiplications.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, storing only a few vectors and matrices.
*   **Strengths:** Simple, fast, and directly maps mechanical C-arm parameters to camera pose. Suitable for real-time view generation.
*   **Limitations:** Assumes ideal C-arm geometry (perfect rotations around fixed axes). Does not account for mechanical imperfections, focal spot blur, or detector distortion [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When generating synthetic X-ray projections from CT volumes where the C-arm geometry is known or simulated.
*   **When not to use:** When high-fidelity simulation of X-ray physics (scatter, beam hardening) is required, or when the C-arm geometry is unknown or non-standard.

## Background
This method is part of the `flashdrr` library, which provides differentiable volume rendering for X-ray imaging [S1, unofficial]. It enables the generation of Digital Radiography (DRR) and Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) images from CT volumes by simulating the C-arm projection geometry. The approach is based on the standard geometric model of C-arm X-ray systems, where the X-ray source rotates around the isocenter (center of the CT scan) [S1, unofficial].
