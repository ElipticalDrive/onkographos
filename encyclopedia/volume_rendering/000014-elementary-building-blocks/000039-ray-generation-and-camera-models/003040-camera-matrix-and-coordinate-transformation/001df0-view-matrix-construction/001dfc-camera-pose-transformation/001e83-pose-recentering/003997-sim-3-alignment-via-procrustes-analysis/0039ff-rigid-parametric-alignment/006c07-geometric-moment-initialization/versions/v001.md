# Geometric Moment Initialization

Also called: Moment-based Initialization

## Overview
Geometric Moment Initialization estimates the initial rigid transformation parameters (translation and rotation) for 3D volume registration by computing the center of gravity and principal axes of the source and target volumes. It solves the problem of poor convergence in mutual information (MI) optimization, where zero-initialization leads to a rugged error surface with many local extrema [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [Homography Parametric Alignment](8618-homography-parametric-alignment.md) and [Iterative Closest Point (ICP)](10563-iterative-closest-point-icp.md) by relying on global volume statistics rather than point correspondences or 2D projections, making it suitable for monomodal intra-subject registration where anatomical structures are rigid but displaced [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Reference volume $V_{ref}$: 3D discrete image data $f(i,j,k)$.
- Floating volume $V_{float}$: 3D discrete image data $g(i,j,k)$.
- Dimensions: $N_x, N_y, N_z$ (number of voxels along each axis).
- Coordinate indices: $i \in [0, N_x-1], j \in [0, N_y-1], k \in [0, N_z-1]$.

**Outputs:**
- Initial translation vector $\mathbf{t}_0 = (tx_0, ty_0, tz_0)$.
- Initial rotation parameters $\mathbf{r}_0 = (rx_0, ry_0, rz_0)$, typically derived from a rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_0$ via Euler angles or axis-angle representation.

**Data Structures:**
- Moments arrays: `M1_ref`, `M1_float` (3-element vectors for first-order moments).
- Covariance matrices: `C_ref`, `C_float` (3x3 matrices for second-order central moments).
- Eigenvectors: `E_ref`, `E_float` (3x3 matrices, columns are principal axes).

**Steps:**

1. **Calculate First-Order Geometric Moments (Center of Gravity):**
   Compute the sum of voxel coordinates weighted by intensity to find the center of gravity for each volume.
   $$ M_{1,u} = \sum_{i,j,k} i^u j^v k^w \cdot f(i,j,k) $$
   Specifically, for the center of gravity $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})$:
   $$ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i,j,k} i \cdot f(i,j,k)}{\sum_{i,j,k} f(i,j,k)}, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{\sum_{i,j,k} j \cdot f(i,j,k)}{\sum_{i,j,k} f(i,j,k)}, \quad \bar{z} = \frac{\sum_{i,j,k} k \cdot f(i,j,k)}{\sum_{i,j,k} f(i,j,k)} $$
   Let $\mathbf{c}_{ref} = (\bar{x}_{ref}, \bar{y}_{ref}, \bar{z}_{ref})$ and $\mathbf{c}_{float} = (\bar{x}_{float}, \bar{y}_{float}, \bar{z}_{float})$.

2. **Initialize Translation:**
   Set the initial translation as the vector difference between the centers of gravity:
   $$ tx_0 = \bar{x}_{float} - \bar{x}_{ref} $$
   $$ ty_0 = \bar{y}_{float} - \bar{y}_{ref} $$
   $$ tz_0 = \bar{z}_{float} - \bar{z}_{ref} $$

3. **Calculate Second-Order Central Moments (Covariance):**
   Compute the covariance matrix to determine the main direction axes. The central moments are defined as:
   $$ \mu_{pq} = \sum_{i,j,k} (i-\bar{x})^p (j-\bar{y})^q (k-\bar{z})^w \cdot f(i,j,k) $$
   Construct the 3x3 covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ for each volume:
   $$ \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} \mu_{200} & \mu_{110} & \mu_{101} \\ \mu_{110} & \mu_{020} & \mu_{011} \\ \mu_{101} & \mu_{011} & \mu_{002} \end{bmatrix} $$
   Note: The sources define geometric moments of order $u+v+w$ generally, but specify that second-order moments determine the axes. In practice, this implies the covariance matrix of the voxel coordinates.

4. **Estimate Rotation Matrix:**
   Perform eigendecomposition on $\mathbf{C}_{ref}$ and $\mathbf{C}_{float}$ to obtain the principal axes (eigenvectors corresponding to the largest eigenvalues). Let $\mathbf{E}_{ref}$ and $\mathbf{E}_{float}$ be the matrices where columns are the sorted eigenvectors (by eigenvalue magnitude).
   The rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_0$ aligning the reference to the floating volume is:
   $$ \mathbf{R}_0 = \mathbf{E}_{float} \mathbf{E}_{ref}^T $$
   [G] Ensure $\det(\mathbf{R}_0) = 1$ to maintain a proper rotation; if $\det(\mathbf{R}_0) = -1$, negate the column of $\mathbf{E}_{float}$ corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue before multiplication.

5. **Derive Initial Rotation Parameters:**
   Convert $\mathbf{R}_0$ to the specific rotation parameterization required by the downstream solver (e.g., Euler angles $rx_0, ry_0, rz_0$).
   $$ rx_0, ry_0, rz_0 = \text{RotationMatrixToEuler}(\mathbf{R}_0) $$

**Pitfalls:**
- Initializing parameters to zero when real values are non-zero leads to many local extrema and an inconspicuous global extremum in the MI surface [S1, unofficial].
- The method assumes the volume intensity distribution correlates with geometric structure. It may fail if the volume has low contrast or uniform intensity regions.
- Precision issues may arise if the center of gravity calculation involves division by a near-zero total intensity sum.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def geometric_moment_initialization(V_ref, V_float):
    # V_ref, V_float are 3D numpy arrays of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz)
    
    # 1. Calculate Centers of Gravity
    # Sum of intensities
    sum_ref = np.sum(V_ref)
    sum_float = np.sum(V_float)
    
    if sum_ref == 0 or sum_float == 0:
        raise ValueError("Volume intensity sum is zero")

    # Create coordinate grids
    x, y, z = np.meshgrid(np.arange(V_ref.shape[0]), 
                          np.arange(V_ref.shape[1]), 
                          np.arange(V_ref.shape[2]), indexing='ij')
    
    # First order moments
    cx_ref = np.sum(x * V_ref) / sum_ref
    cy_ref = np.sum(y * V_ref) / sum_ref
    cz_ref = np.sum(z * V_ref) / sum_ref
    c_ref = np.array([cx_ref, cy_ref, cz_ref])

    cx_float = np.sum(x * V_float) / sum_float
    cy_float = np.sum(y * V_float) / sum_float
    cz_float = np.sum(z * V_float) / sum_float
    c_float = np.array([cx_float, cy_float, cz_float])

    # 2. Initialize Translation
    t0 = c_float - c_ref

    # 3. Calculate Covariance Matrices
    # Central coordinates
    x_c_ref = x - cx_ref
    y_c_ref = y - cy_ref
    z_c_ref = z - cz_ref
    
    x_c_float = x - cx_float
    y_c_float = y - cy_float
    z_c_float = z - cz_float

    # Helper to compute covariance matrix from central coords and volume
    def compute_covariance(x_c, y_c, z_c, V):
        # Flatten for easier matrix operations
        coords = np.vstack([x_c.ravel(), y_c.ravel(), z_c.ravel()])
        weights = V.ravel()
        # Weighted covariance: E[xx^T] - E[x]E[x]^T, but E[x] is 0 here
        # So just sum of outer products weighted
        cov = np.zeros((3, 3))
        for i in range(3):
            for j in range(3):
                # Element-wise multiplication of coordinate vectors and weights
                term = coords[i] * coords[j] * weights
                cov[i, j] = np.sum(term)
        return cov

    C_ref = compute_covariance(x_c_ref, y_c_ref, z_c_ref, V_ref)
    C_float = compute_covariance(x_c_float, y_c_float, z_c_float, V_float)

    # 4. Eigendecomposition
    eigvals_ref, eigvecs_ref = np.linalg.eigh(C_ref)
    eigvals_float, eigvecs_float = np.linalg.eigh(C_float)

    # Sort eigenvectors by eigenvalue (descending)
    idx_ref = np.argsort(eigvals_ref)[::-1]
    idx_float = np.argsort(eigvals_float)[::-1]
    
    E_ref = eigvecs_ref[:, idx_ref]
    E_float = eigvecs_float[:, idx_float]

    # 5. Rotation Matrix
    R0 = E_float @ E_ref.T
    
    # Ensure proper rotation (det = 1)
    if np.linalg.det(R0) < 0:
        E_float[:, -1] *= -1
        R0 = E_float @ E_ref.T

    # 6. Convert to Euler Angles (ZYX convention as common default, adjust if specific solver requires)
    # [G] Standard conversion from rotation matrix to Euler angles
    sy = np.sqrt(R0[0,0]**2 + R0[1,0]**2)
    singular = sy < 1e-6
    if not singular:
        rx0 = np.arctan2(R0[2,1], R0[2,2])
        ry0 = np.arctan2(-R0[2,0], sy)
        rz0 = np.arctan2(R0[1,0], R0[0,0])
    else:
        rx0 = np.arctan2(-R0[1,2], R0[1,1])
        ry0 = np.arctan2(-R0[2,0], sy)
        rz0 = 0

    return t0, (rx0, ry0, rz0)
```

## Subtopics

### [Rigid Registration via Mutual Information Maximization](21006-rigid-registration-via-mutual-information-maximization.md)
This method performs 3D/3D monomodal intra-subject rigid registration by maximizing the Mutual Information (MI) between a reference volume and a transformed target volume. It distinguishes itself from intensity-based linear measures (like Correlation Coefficient) by being robust to non-linear intensity variations, and it relies on Geometric Moment Initialization to avoid local minima inherent in MI optimization.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of voxels, as it requires a single pass to compute sums and moments.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the volume data and coordinate grids.
- **Strengths:** Provides a robust, closed-form initial guess that significantly smooths the MI optimization surface, preventing convergence to local minima [S1, unofficial]. It is fast and does not require feature extraction.
- **Limitations:** Assumes the volume has a distinct geometric structure and intensity distribution. It may be inaccurate for volumes with low contrast, noise, or non-rigid deformations. It only provides a rigid transformation estimate.
- **When to use:** When performing rigid registration of medical volumes (e.g., kidneys) where anatomical structures are rigid but displaced, and where zero-initialization causes optimization failure [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** For non-rigid registration, or when volumes have very low signal-to-noise ratio or lack distinct geometric features.

## Background
The method originates from research into medical volume visualization and preoperative planning systems, specifically for kidney registration [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address the issue of local extrema in mutual information optimization surfaces when parameters are initialized to zero. The approach leverages the properties of image geometric moments to estimate the center of gravity and principal axes, providing a more accurate initial guess than random or zero initialization [S1, unofficial].
