# Pose Recentering

Also called: LLFF Pose Normalization for NDC

## Overview
Pose Recentering transforms a set of camera-to-world (c2w) poses so that their average pose becomes the identity transformation, effectively centering the scene at the origin and aligning the principal axes [S1, S3, unofficial]. It simultaneously computes a unified transformation from mean camera centers and axes, distinguishing it from [Rotation Transformation](2279-rotation-transformation.md) and [Translation Transformation](2281-translation-transformation.md) which handle only one aspect [G].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of $N$ camera poses, where each pose is a $3 \times 4$ matrix $P_i$ representing the transformation from camera space to world space [S1, unofficial]. The last column is the translation vector (camera center), and the first three columns are the orthonormal basis vectors (x, y, z axes) of the camera frame [G].
**Outputs:** A set of $N$ recentered $3 \times 4$ poses $P'_i$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- `poses`: Array of shape $(N, 3, 4)$, dtype float32.
- `pose_avg`: $3 \times 4$ matrix.
- `pose_avg_homo`: $4 \times 4$ matrix.

**Steps:**
1. **Pad Poses:** Convert all $3 \times 4$ poses to $4 \times 4$ homogeneous matrices by appending a row $[0, 0, 0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial]. Let the padded poses be $P_{homo}$.
2. **Compute Scene Center:** Calculate the mean of all camera centers (the translation vectors, i.e., the 4th column of the original $3 \times 4$ poses) [S2, unofficial].
   $$ \text{center} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} P_i[:, 3] $$
3. **Compute Z-Axis:** Calculate the mean of all pose z-axes (the 3rd column of the original $3 \times 4$ poses) and normalize it [S2, unofficial].
   $$ z = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} P_i[:, 2]}{\|\sum_{i=1}^{N} P_i[:, 2]\|} $$
4. **Compute Intermediate Y-Axis:** Calculate the mean of all pose y-axes (the 2nd column of the original $3 \times 4$ poses) [S2, unofficial].
   $$ y_{temp} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} P_i[:, 1] $$
5. **Compute X-Axis:** Compute the cross product of $y_{temp}$ and $z$, then normalize [S2, unofficial].
   $$ x = \frac{y_{temp} \times z}{\|y_{temp} \times z\|} $$
   *Note:* $y_{temp}$ is not used directly as the y-axis because it is not guaranteed to be orthogonal to $z$ [S2, unofficial].
6. **Compute Final Y-Axis:** Compute the cross product of $z$ and $x$ to ensure orthonormality [S2, unofficial].
   $$ y = z \times x $$
7. **Construct Average Pose Matrix:** Stack the computed axes and center into a $3 \times 4$ matrix [S2, unofficial].
   $$ \text{pose\_avg} = [x, y, z, \text{center}] $$
8. **Construct Homogeneous Average Pose:** Create a $4 \times 4$ matrix from `pose_avg` by appending $[0, 0, 0, 1]$ as the last row [S2, unofficial].
   $$ \text{pose\_avg\_homo} = \begin{bmatrix} \text{pose\_avg} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
9. **Compute Inverse Transformation:** Calculate the inverse of `pose_avg_homo` [S2, unofficial].
   $$ T_{inv} = \text{pose\_avg\_homo}^{-1} $$
10. **Transform Poses:** Left-multiply the inverse transformation with all padded poses [S1, unofficial].
    $$ P'_{homo} = T_{inv} @ P_{homo} $$
11. **Unpad and Return:** Truncate $P'_{homo}$ to the first 3 rows to obtain the final $3 \times 4$ recentered poses [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def recenter_poses(poses: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Recenter a set of camera poses.
    
    Args:
        poses: np.ndarray of shape (N, 3, 4), camera-to-world poses.
        
    Returns:
        np.ndarray of shape (N, 3, 4), recentered camera-to-world poses.
    """
    # Step 1: Pad poses to 4x4
    ones = np.ones((poses.shape[0], 1, 4))
    poses_homo = np.concatenate([poses, ones], axis=1)  # Shape (N, 4, 4)
    
    # Step 2: Compute scene center (mean of translation vectors)
    center = np.mean(poses[:, :, 3], axis=0)  # Shape (3,)
    
    # Step 3: Compute z-axis (mean of z-axes, normalized)
    z_raw = np.mean(poses[:, :, 2], axis=0)  # Shape (3,)
    z = z_raw / np.linalg.norm(z_raw)
    
    # Step 4: Compute intermediate y-axis (mean of y-axes)
    y_temp = np.mean(poses[:, :, 1], axis=0)  # Shape (3,)
    
    # Step 5: Compute x-axis (cross product of y_temp and z, normalized)
    x_raw = np.cross(y_temp, z)
    x = x_raw / np.linalg.norm(x_raw)
    
    # Step 6: Compute final y-axis (cross product of z and x)
    y = np.cross(z, x)
    
    # Step 7: Construct average pose matrix (3x4)
    pose_avg = np.stack([x, y, z, center], axis=1)  # Shape (3, 4)
    
    # Step 8: Construct homogeneous average pose matrix (4x4)
    pose_avg_homo = np.vstack([pose_avg, np.array([0, 0, 0, 1])])  # Shape (4, 4)
    
    # Step 9: Compute inverse transformation
    pose_avg_homo_inv = np.linalg.inv(pose_avg_homo)
    
    # Step 10: Transform all poses
    poses_centered_homo = pose_avg_homo_inv @ poses_homo  # Shape (N, 4, 4)
    
    # Step 11: Unpad and return
    poses_centered = poses_centered_homo[:, :3, :]  # Shape (N, 3, 4)
    
    return poses_centered
```

**Parameters:**
- None.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Degenerate Z-axis:** If the mean z-axis vector is zero (e.g., cameras are distributed symmetrically around the origin with no preferred viewing direction), normalization fails [G]. In practice, this is rare in real-world captures but should be guarded against by checking the norm of `z_raw` before normalization.
- **Non-orthonormality:** Using the mean y-axis directly as the y-axis is incorrect because it is not orthogonal to the mean z-axis [S2, unofficial]. The algorithm explicitly derives y from z and x to maintain orthonormality.
- **Precision:** Use float32 or float64 consistently. Inverting a poorly conditioned matrix (if poses are nearly singular) can lead to numerical instability [G].

## Subtopics

### [PCA Pose Alignment](7626-pca-pose-alignment.md)
PCA Pose Alignment transforms a set of camera-to-world poses so that the principal components of their translation vectors lie on the XYZ axes. It re-centers the poses, rotates them based on the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, ensures a consistent coordinate system orientation, and scales them to fit within the $[-1, 1]^3$ cube. It is distinct from [Sim(3) Alignment via Procrustes Analysis](8526-sim-3-alignment-via-procrustes-analysis.md) which aligns poses using Procrustes analysis.

### [Sim(3) Alignment via Procrustes Analysis](8526-sim-3-alignment-via-procrustes-analysis.md)
Sim(3) Alignment via Procrustes Analysis computes a 3D similarity transformation (rotation, translation, and uniform scale) to align estimated camera poses to ground truth poses by analyzing camera location coordinates. It is primarily used for pose quality assessment in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines. Unlike [PCA Pose Alignment](7626-pca-pose-alignment.md), which aligns based on principal component directions, this method explicitly solves for the similarity transform that minimizes the distance between corresponding camera centers.
