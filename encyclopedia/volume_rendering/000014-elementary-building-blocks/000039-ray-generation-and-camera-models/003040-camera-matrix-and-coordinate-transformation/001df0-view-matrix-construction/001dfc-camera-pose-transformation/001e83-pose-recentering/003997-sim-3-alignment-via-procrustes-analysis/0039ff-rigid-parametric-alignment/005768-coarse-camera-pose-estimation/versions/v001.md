# Coarse Camera Pose Estimation

Also called: Coarse Pose Initialization, 2D-3D PnP Registration

## Overview
Coarse Camera Pose Estimation is an incremental initialization method that estimates the initial 3D camera pose for unregistered target images by leveraging a pre-trained scene regressor. It solves the problem of establishing a global coordinate frame for unposed images without requiring external structure-from-motion pipelines. It differs from its sibling [Iterative Closest Point (ICP)](10563-iterative-closest-point-icp.md) by relying on deep learning-based 3D point prediction rather than geometric surface matching, and from its child [Structure-from-Motion (SfM)](16341-structure-from-motion-sfm.md) by using a neural scene regressor instead of traditional feature matching and triangulation.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Registered reference image $I_{ref}$ (or $I_i$).
- Target image $I_k$ to be registered.
- Camera intrinsic matrix $K$.
- Inlier threshold $s_{inlier} = 5,000$ [S1, unofficial].
- Reprojection error threshold: 6 pixels [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Coarse camera pose $T_{coarse}$ for $I_k$.
- Boolean flag indicating whether $I_k$ is added to the training buffer.

**Data Structures:**
- Scene Regressor $f_{SCR}$: A neural network (specifically a transformer-based regressor in the source) that takes two images and outputs 3D points.
- 3D Points $\{X_k\}$: Predicted coordinates in the global frame.
- 2D Pixels $\{u_k\}$: Pixel coordinates from $I_k$.
- Correspondences $\{(u_k, X_k)\}$: Pairs of 2D-3D matches.

**Steps:**
1. **Scene Regression:** Pass $I_{ref}$ and $I_k$ to the scene regressor $f_{SCR}$ to obtain the set of 3D points $\{X_k\}$ in the global coordinate frame [S1, unofficial].
   $$ \{X_k\} = f_{SCR}(I_{ref}, I_k) $$
2. **Correspondence Generation:** Extract pixel coordinates $\{u_k\}$ from $I_k$. Form 2D-3D correspondences $\{(u_k, X_k)\}$ [S1, unofficial].
3. **Pose Estimation:** Solve for the camera pose $T_{coarse}$ using a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) solver with RANSAC (specifically DSAC, modified for asymmetric focal lengths) [S1, unofficial].
   $$ T_{coarse}, S_k = \text{PnP}(K, \{(u_k, X_k)\}) $$
   Where $S_k$ is the number of inliers [S1, unofficial].
4. **Validation:** Check if the number of inliers $S_k$ exceeds the threshold $s_{inlier}$ [S1, unofficial].
   $$ \text{if } S_k > s_{inlier} \text{ then add } I_k \text{ to training buffer} $$
5. **Refinement (Optional):** The source notes that while MLP pose refiners exist (e.g., ACE0), they were found not to improve accuracy with the transformer-based scene regressor used here [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $s_{inlier}$: Inlier threshold, set to 5,000 [S1, unofficial].
- Reprojection threshold: 6 pixels [S1, unofficial].
- DSAC Modification: Supports different focal lengths for x and y axes [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Gauge Ambiguity:** The camera pose of the seed image is fixed during optimization [S1, unofficial].
- **Scale Ambiguity:** The scaling factor between the seed image and its most similar adjacent image is fixed [S1, unofficial].
- **Regressor Accuracy:** If the scene regressor has not seen the target image type, the initial pose may be inaccurate, necessitating refinement or careful selection of reference images [S1, unofficial].
- **Reference Selection:** The reference image is selected from registered images that connect to the most unregistered images to ensure robust initialization [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the inference time of the scene regressor $f_{SCR}$ and the PnP solver. The source does not provide specific FLOPs or timing metrics.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the 3D points $\{X_k\}$ and the training buffer. Specific memory footprint is not given.
- **Performance:** The method is effective for incremental registration in neural rendering pipelines. It avoids the need for external SfM tools.
- **Strengths:** Integrates seamlessly with neural scene representations; handles unposed images directly.
- **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the scene regressor; may fail if the regressor has poor generalization to new views; requires a sufficient number of inliers (>5,000) for robust registration.
- **When to use:** When initializing camera poses for neural rendering (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) from unposed image collections where external SfM is undesirable or unavailable.
- **When not to use:** When high-precision initial poses are required without subsequent refinement, or when the scene regressor is not available/trained.

## Background
The method is described in the paper "ZeroGS: Training 3D Gaussian Splatting from Unposed Images" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of using neural networks to predict 3D structure from images, enabling pose estimation without traditional feature matching. The approach addresses the gauge and scale ambiguities by fixing the seed image pose and scale factor [S1, unofficial].
