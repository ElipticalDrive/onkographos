# Point to Camera Transformation

Also called: ptstocam

## Overview
The Point to Camera Transformation converts 3D points from world coordinates into the camera's local coordinate system. It serves as the foundational step for ray generation and visibility checks by establishing the relative position of scene geometry with respect to the viewer. Unlike [NDC Ray Transformation](905-ndc-ray-transformation.md), which maps pixels to rays, or [View Matrix Construction](1644-view-matrix-construction.md), which builds the full affine transform matrix, this method explicitly computes the rotated and translated position of individual points to facilitate downstream operations like depth sampling or projection.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $\mathbf{P}_{world}$: A 3D point in world coordinates ($\mathbb{R}^3$).
- $\mathbf{C}_{c2w}$: The camera-to-world transformation matrix (4x4). This matrix defines the camera's orientation and position in the world.

**Outputs:**
- $\mathbf{P}_{cam}$: The 3D point in camera coordinates ($\mathbb{R}^3$).

**Data Structures:**
- The camera-to-world matrix $\mathbf{C}_{c2w}$ is typically stored as a 4x4 column-major or row-major array. It consists of a 3x3 rotation submatrix $\mathbf{R}$ and a 3x1 translation vector $\mathbf{t}$ (the camera origin in world space).
  $$
  \mathbf{C}_{c2w} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R} & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix}
  $$
  Where $\mathbf{R}$ contains the basis vectors (right, up, forward) of the camera in world coordinates.

**Steps:**
1. **Extract Camera Origin:** Identify the translation component $\mathbf{t}$ from $\mathbf{C}_{c2w}$. This represents the camera's eye position $\mathbf{e}$ in world coordinates.
2. **Compute Relative Vector:** Calculate the vector from the camera origin to the world point:
   $$
   \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{P}_{world} - \mathbf{e}
   $$
3. **Rotate to Camera Space:** Transform the relative vector $\mathbf{v}$ into the camera's local frame. Since $\mathbf{R}$ transforms vectors from camera space to world space, its inverse is required to go from world to camera space. For a rotation matrix, the inverse is the transpose:
   $$
   \mathbf{P}_{cam} = \mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{v}
   $$
   Alternatively, using dot products with the camera's basis vectors $\mathbf{r}$ (right), $\mathbf{u}$ (up), and $\mathbf{f}$ (forward) from $\mathbf{R}$:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   P_{cam,x} &= \mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{v} \\
   P_{cam,y} &= \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} \\
   P_{cam,z} &= \mathbf{f} \cdot \mathbf{v}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def point_to_camera(P_world: vec3, c2w_matrix: mat4x4) -> vec3:
    """
    Transforms a 3D point from world coordinates to camera coordinates.
    
    Args:
        P_world: The 3D point in world space.
        c2w_matrix: The 4x4 camera-to-world matrix.
        
    Returns:
        The 3D point in camera space.
    """
    # Extract camera origin (translation part of c2w matrix)
    # Assuming column-major storage where the last column is the translation
    cam_origin = c2w_matrix[3] 
    
    # Compute vector from camera to point
    relative_vec = P_world - cam_origin
    
    # Extract rotation matrix (top-left 3x3 of c2w)
    # R = [r_right, r_up, r_forward] as columns
    R = c2w_matrix[0:3, 0:3]
    
    # Inverse rotation is transpose for orthonormal basis
    R_inv = transpose(R)
    
    # Transform relative vector to camera space
    P_cam = matmul(R_inv, relative_vec)
    
    return P_cam
```

**Parameters:**
- Not applicable (pure geometric transformation).

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Non-orthonormal Basis:** If $\mathbf{C}_{c2w}$ contains scaling or shearing, $\mathbf{R}^T$ is not the exact inverse. In standard camera models, $\mathbf{R}$ is orthonormal, so this is safe [S1, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Subtracting large world coordinates from the camera origin can lead to floating-point precision loss if the scene is very large. Using double precision or centering the scene around the camera origin is recommended [G].

## Subtopics

### World to Clip Space Conversion
Converts a world position to camera clip space coordinates (2D), used for sampling textures or determining pixel locations in camera space [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Basis Construction:** Calculate camera basis vectors. Let $\mathbf{f}$ be the forward vector (usually $-\mathbf{z}$ in view space or derived from target). Calculate $\mathbf{r} = \text{normalize}(\text{cross}(\mathbf{f}, \text{vec3}(0.0, 1.0, 0.0)))$ and $\mathbf{u} = \text{normalize}(\text{cross}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{f}))$ [S2, unofficial].
2. **View Space Position:** Compute the position relative to the camera: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{P}_{world} - \mathbf{e}$. Then compute view coordinates:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \text{viewX} &= \mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{v} \\
   \text{viewY} &= \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} \\
   \text{viewZ} &= \mathbf{f} \cdot \mathbf{v}
   \end{aligned}
   $$
3. **Perspective Projection:** Apply perspective division factors. Let $\text{scale} = \tan(\text{fov}/2)$.
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \text{clipX} &= \frac{\text{viewX}}{\text{viewZ} \cdot \text{scale} \cdot \text{aspectRatio}} \\
   \text{clipY} &= \frac{\text{viewY}}{\text{viewZ} \cdot \text{scale}}
   \end{aligned}
   $$
4. **Offset and Scale:** Adjust for pixel coordinates.
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \text{clipX} &= \text{clipX} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{1}{\text{screenWidth}}\right) \\
   \text{clipY} &= \text{clipY} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{1}{\text{screenHeight}}\right) \\
   \text{clipX} &= \frac{\text{clipX}}{2.0} + 0.5 \\
   \text{clipY} &= \frac{\text{clipY}}{2.0} + 0.5
   \end{aligned}
   $$
5. **Pixel Coordinates:**
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \text{pixelX} &= \text{clipX} \cdot (\text{screenWidth} - 1) \\
   \text{pixelY} &= \text{clipY} \cdot (\text{screenHeight} - 1)
   \end{aligned}
   $$
   Return $\text{vec2}(\text{pixelX}, \text{pixelY})$ [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per point. Requires a few vector subtractions, dot products, and matrix multiplications.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary space.
- **Strengths:** Direct access to camera-relative coordinates is essential for depth-based sampling and occlusion queries.
- **Limitations:** Does not handle perspective projection or clipping directly; requires additional steps for screen-space mapping.
- **When to use:** When you need the exact 3D position relative to the camera for volumetric traversal or texture lookups.
- **When not to use:** When you need screen-space coordinates directly; use [World to Clip Space Conversion](3148-3d-point-projection-to-2d.md) or [NDC Ray Transformation](905-ndc-ray-transformation.md) instead.

## Background
The method is a standard linear algebra operation in computer graphics, derived from the definition of coordinate frames. It is implicitly used in all ray marching and rasterization pipelines. The specific implementation details, such as the use of transpose for rotation inversion, are standard practices in graphics programming [S1, unofficial]. The variant for clip space conversion is often found in custom rendering engines or WebGPU demos [S2, unofficial].
