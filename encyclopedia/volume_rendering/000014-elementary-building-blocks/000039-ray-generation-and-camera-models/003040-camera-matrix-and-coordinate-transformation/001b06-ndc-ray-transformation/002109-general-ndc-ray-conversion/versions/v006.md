# General NDC Ray Conversion

Also called: General NDC, NDC Ray Conversion, NDC Rays, NDC projection, Normalized Device Coordinate rays

## Overview
General NDC Ray Conversion transforms camera-space rays into Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) space, mapping the viewing frustum to a canonical cube (typically $[-1, 1]^3$) [S1, S4, S6, unofficial]. This technique fixes the near and far clipping planes to constant values (e.g., 0 and 1 or -1 and 1), removing the need for scene-dependent depth bounds and enabling uniform sampling strategies for forward-facing scenes [S2, S4, unofficial]. It differs from [Pinhole Camera Projection](3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md) by producing a full ray for traversal rather than a single point, and from [Ray Generation from Pixel Coordinates](2514-ray-generation-from-pixel-coordinates.md) by specifically handling the normalization of depth bounds rather than just generating world-space rays [S4, S7, unofficial]. The resulting `directions_ndc` are not unit length, and the projection of the original ray does not equal the NDC ray (`projection(origins + t * directions) \neq origins_ndc + t * directions_ndc`) [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `rays_o`: Ray origins in camera space, shape `(batch_size, 3)` or `(H, W, 3)` [S5, S6, unofficial].
- `rays_d`: Ray directions in camera space, shape `(batch_size, 3)` or `(H, W, 3)` [S5, S6, unofficial].
- `focal`: Focal length of the pinhole camera (float) [S6, unofficial].
- `W`: Image width in pixels (int) [S6, unofficial].
- `H`: Image height in pixels (int) [S6, unofficial].
- `near`: Near plane distance along the negative z-axis (float). Default is 1.0 [S1, S4, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `rays_o_ndc`: Transformed origins in NDC space, shape `(batch_size, 3)` or `(H, W, 3)` [S6, unofficial].
- `rays_d_ndc`: Transformed directions in NDC space, shape `(batch_size, 3)` or `(H, W, 3)` [S6, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The inputs and outputs are dense floating-point arrays [G]. The z-component of the origin in NDC space maps the near plane to a constant value (typically 1 or 0 depending on convention) and infinity to another constant (typically 3 or 1) [S2, S6, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Shift Origins to Near Plane:**
    Calculate the shift factor $t$ to move the ray origin to the near plane ($z = -near$ in standard camera coordinates) [S1, S5, S9, unofficial].
    $$ t = -\frac{near + rays\_o[..., 2]}{rays\_d[..., 2]} $$
    Update origins:
    $$ rays\_o = rays\_o + t[..., \text{None}] \cdot rays\_d $$
    Some implementations use $t = \frac{near - rays\_o[..., 2]}{rays\_d[..., 2]}$ due to different sign conventions (e.g., Blender vs. OpenGL) [S7, S8, unofficial].

2.  **Project Origins to NDC:**
    Compute the NDC coordinates for the shifted origins [S1, S3, unofficial]. The x and y components are scaled by the focal length and image dimensions [S6, unofficial]. The z component maps the depth to the NDC range [S6, unofficial].
    $$ o_0 = -\frac{1}{W / (2 \cdot focal)} \cdot \frac{rays\_o[..., 0]}{rays\_o[..., 2]} $$
    $$ o_1 = -\frac{1}{H / (2 \cdot focal)} \cdot \frac{rays\_o[..., 1]}{rays\_o[..., 2]} $$
    $$ o_2 = 1 + 2 \cdot \frac{near}{rays\_o[..., 2]} $$
    At the near plane ($rays\_o[..., 2] = -near$), $o_2 = -1$ [S6, unofficial]. As $rays\_o[..., 2] \to -\infty$, $o_2 \to 1$, mapping the depth range to $[-1, 1]$ [S4, S6, unofficial].

3.  **Project Directions to NDC:**
    Compute the NDC direction components, which accounts for the perspective division difference between the origin and the point at infinity [S6, unofficial].
    $$ d_0 = -\frac{1}{W / (2 \cdot focal)} \cdot \left( \frac{rays\_d[..., 0]}{rays\_d[..., 2]} - \frac{rays\_o[..., 0]}{rays\_o[..., 2]} \right) $$
    $$ d_1 = -\frac{1}{H / (2 \cdot focal)} \cdot \left( \frac{rays\_d[..., 1]}{rays\_d[..., 2]} - \frac{rays\_o[..., 1]}{rays\_o[..., 2]} \right) $$
    $$ d_2 = -2 \cdot \frac{near}{rays\_o[..., 2]} $$
    The direction z-component $d_2$ ensures that the ray in NDC space points towards the correct depth range [S6, unofficial]. At the near plane, $d_2 = 2$. As $z \to -\infty$, $d_2 \to 0$ [S6, unofficial].

4.  **Stack Results:**
    Concatenate the components to form the final NDC rays [S4, S6, unofficial].
    $$ rays\_o\_ndc = [o_0, o_1, o_2] $$
    $$ rays\_d\_ndc = [d_0, d_1, d_2] $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def convert_to_ndc(rays_o, rays_d, focal, W, H, near=1.0):
    """
    Convert camera-space rays to NDC space.
    
    Args:
        rays_o: (..., 3) ray origins in camera space.
        rays_d: (..., 3) ray directions in camera space.
        focal: float, focal length.
        W: int, image width.
        H: int, image height.
        near: float, near plane distance.
        
    Returns:
        rays_o_ndc: (..., 3) NDC origins.
        rays_d_ndc: (..., 3) NDC directions.
    """
    # Step 1: Shift origins to the near plane
    # t = -(near + rays_o_z) / rays_d_z
    t = -(near + rays_o[..., 2:3]) / rays_d[..., 2:3]
    rays_o_shifted = rays_o + t * rays_d
    
    # Step 2: Project origins to NDC
    # o0 = -1 / (W / (2*focal)) * (ox / oz)
    # o1 = -1 / (H / (2*focal)) * (oy / oz)
    # o2 = 1 + 2 * near / oz
    inv_hw = np.array([W / (2.0 * focal), H / (2.0 * focal)])
    
    o0 = -1.0 / inv_hw[0] * (rays_o_shifted[..., 0:1] / rays_o_shifted[..., 2:3])
    o1 = -1.0 / inv_hw[1] * (rays_o_shifted[..., 1:2] / rays_o_shifted[..., 2:3])
    o2 = 1.0 + 2.0 * near / rays_o_shifted[..., 2:3]
    
    rays_o_ndc = np.concatenate([o0, o1, o2], axis=-1)
    
    # Step 3: Project directions to NDC
    # d0 = -1 / (W / (2*focal)) * (dx/dz - ox/oz)
    # d1 = -1 / (H / (2*focal)) * (dy/dz - oy/oz)
    # d2 = -2 * near / oz
    ox_oz = rays_o_shifted[..., 0:1] / rays_o_shifted[..., 2:3]
    oy_oz = rays_o_shifted[..., 1:2] / rays_o_shifted[..., 2:3]
    
    dx_dz = rays_d[..., 0:1] / rays_d[..., 2:3]
    dy_dz = rays_d[..., 1:2] / rays_d[..., 2:3]
    
    d0 = -1.0 / inv_hw[0] * (dx_dz - ox_oz)
    d1 = -1.0 / inv_hw[1] * (dy_dz - oy_oz)
    d2 = -2.0 * near / rays_o_shifted[..., 2:3]
    
    rays_d_ndc = np.concatenate([d0, d1, d2], axis=-1)
    
    return rays_o_ndc, rays_d_ndc
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Zero Direction Z:** If `rays_d[..., 2]` is zero, the shift factor $t$ becomes infinite [G]. This occurs for rays parallel to the near plane [G]. In practice, this should be handled by clamping or checking for zero denominators [G].
- **Sign Conventions:** Different libraries (e.g., Blender vs. OpenGL) use different camera coordinate systems [S7, unofficial]. Blender uses a right-handed system where the camera looks down +z, while OpenGL uses a left-handed system where the camera looks down -z [G]. The formula for $t$ and the signs in the projection must be adjusted accordingly [S7, S8, unofficial].
- **Spherical Poses:** NDC assumes a forward-facing scene [S2, unofficial]. For spherical or omnidirectional scenes, NDC can clip geometry or distort depth significantly [S2, unofficial]. A `spheric_poses` flag is often used to bypass NDC conversion and use scene bounds instead [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Ray Generation from Pixel Coordinates](2514-ray-generation-from-pixel-coordinates.md)
Generates world-space rays from pixel indices. It typically precedes NDC conversion if the scene requires NDC sampling.

### [Pinhole Camera Projection (Pixel to Camera)](3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md)
Projects pixels to camera-space rays. This is the immediate predecessor to NDC conversion.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of rays [G]. The operations are element-wise arithmetic [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing the transformed rays [G].
- **Performance:** The transformation is computationally inexpensive compared to ray marching or volume rendering. It is typically performed once per ray batch [G].
- **Strengths:** Simplifies sampling strategies by fixing the depth range to $[0, 1]$ or $[-1, 1]$. Enables uniform sampling in NDC space to correspond to inverse depth sampling in world space, which is beneficial for forward-facing scenes with large depth variations [S2, S4, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Not suitable for non-forward-facing or spherical scenes, as it distorts depth and can clip geometry [S2, S6, unofficial]. Requires accurate focal length and image dimensions [S6, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** Forward-facing scenes (e.g., LLFF dataset) where depth range is large and uniform sampling is desired [S4, S6, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** Spherical scenes, scenes with significant camera rotation around the object, or when exact world-space depth is required for other processing steps [S2, S6, unofficial].

## Background
General NDC Ray Conversion was introduced in the context of NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) to handle forward-facing scenes efficiently [S4, unofficial]. The technique maps the perspective frustum to a canonical cube, allowing for simpler and more robust sampling strategies [S4, unofficial]. It is particularly useful for datasets like LLFF (Look at Light From the Front) where the camera moves primarily in a forward-facing manner [S4, S6, unofficial].
