# General NDC Ray Conversion

Also called: General NDC, NDC Ray Conversion, Normalized Device Coordinates ray transformation

## Overview
General NDC Ray Conversion maps camera-space rays into a normalized device coordinate (NDC) cube $[-1, 1]^3$ for general scenes, enabling uniform sampling in NDC space to correspond to inverse depth (disparity) sampling in the original world space [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike Blender-specific NDC conventions which assume a fixed focal length and aspect ratio, this method handles arbitrary camera intrinsics and extrinsics by explicitly shifting ray origins to the near plane and projecting them based on the camera's focal length and image dimensions [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is primarily used in forward-facing scene reconstruction to handle large depth ranges efficiently [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `rays_o`: Ray origins in world/camera space, shape $(..., 3)$.
- `rays_d`: Ray directions in world/camera space, shape $(..., 3)$.
- `near`: Distance to the near plane (scalar). Typically hardcoded to $1.0$ in implementations [S1, unofficial].
- `focal`: Focal length of the camera (scalar).
- `W`: Image width (scalar).
- `H`: Image height (scalar).

**Outputs:**
- `rays_o_ndc`: Transformed ray origins in NDC space, shape $(..., 3)$.
- `rays_d_ndc`: Transformed ray directions in NDC space, shape $(..., 3)$.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on batched tensors. The last dimension represents the 3D vector components $(x, y, z)$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Shift Ray Origins to Near Plane:**
    The ray origins are shifted along the ray direction so that they lie exactly on the near plane. This ensures that the NDC transformation maps the near plane to a fixed coordinate (typically $z' = -1$ or $1$ depending on convention, here mapped to $1$ in the formula below with specific sign conventions) and handles the singularity at the camera center.
    
    Compute the shift parameter $t$:
    $$ t = \frac{\text{near} - \text{rays\_o}_z}{\text{rays\_d}_z} $$
    
    Update the origin:
    $$ \text{rays\_o} \leftarrow \text{rays\_o} + t \cdot \text{rays\_d} $$
    
    *Note: Source [S1, unofficial] uses a slightly different formulation involving negative near plane logic or specific axis conventions ($-(near + rays\_o[..., 2:3]) / rays\_d[..., 2:3]$). Source [S2, unofficial] uses the standard projection formula. We follow the explicit component-wise projection formulas provided in [S1, unofficial] which assume the origin has been shifted or handles the shift implicitly in the projection logic for forward-facing scenes.*
    
    *Correction based on [S1, unofficial] specific code logic:* The source [S1, unofficial] explicitly calculates $t$ as `-(near + rays_o[..., 2:3]) / rays_d[..., 2:3]` and updates `rays_o`. This implies a coordinate system where the camera looks along the negative Z axis or similar, and `near` is positive. However, the subsequent projection formulas in [S1, unofficial] use `rays_o[..., 2:3]` directly. To be implementable and consistent with the provided formulas in [S1, unofficial], we assume the inputs are pre-processed or the formula accounts for the shift. The most robust interpretation from [S1, unofficial] is:
    $$ t = \frac{-(\text{near} + \text{rays\_o}_z)}{\text{rays\_d}_z} $$
    $$ \text{rays\_o} \leftarrow \text{rays\_o} + t \cdot \text{rays\_d} $$
    *(Note: If the camera looks along +Z, `near` might be negative or the sign flips. We stick to the exact arithmetic from [S1, unofficial])*

2.  **Compute Transformed Origin Components:**
    Project the shifted origins into NDC space. The x and y components are scaled by the inverse of the horizontal and vertical fields of view (derived from focal length and image size). The z component is mapped to the range $[1, \infty)$ or similar, bounded by the near plane.
    
    $$ o'_x = -\frac{f}{W/2} \cdot \frac{\text{rays\_o}_x}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
    $$ o'_y = -\frac{f}{H/2} \cdot \frac{\text{rays\_o}_y}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
    $$ o'_z = 1 + \frac{2 \cdot \text{near}}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
    
    Where $f$ is `focal`, $W$ is `W`, $H$ is `H`.

3.  **Compute Transformed Direction Components:**
    Project the directions. The direction in NDC space is the difference between the projected direction endpoint and the projected origin endpoint.
    
    $$ d'_x = -\frac{f}{W/2} \cdot \left( \frac{\text{rays\_d}_x}{\text{rays\_d}_z} - \frac{\text{rays\_o}_x}{\text{rays\_o}_z} \right) $$
    $$ d'_y = -\frac{f}{H/2} \cdot \left( \frac{\text{rays\_d}_y}{\text{rays\_o}_z} \dots \text{Wait, check source} \right) $$
    
    *Re-reading [S1, unofficial] for direction:*
    $$ d_0 = -\frac{1}{W / (2.0 \cdot \text{focal})} \cdot \left( \frac{\text{rays\_d}_x}{\text{rays\_d}_z} - \frac{\text{rays\_o}_x}{\text{rays\_o}_z} \right) $$
    $$ d_1 = -\frac{1}{H / (2.0 \cdot \text{focal})} \cdot \left( \frac{\text{rays\_d}_y}{\text{rays\_d}_z} - \frac{\text{rays\_o}_y}{\text{rays\_o}_z} \right) $$
    $$ d_2 = -\frac{2 \cdot \text{near}}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
    
    *Note on $d_2$:* Source [S1, unofficial] lists $d_2 = -2.0 \cdot \text{near} / \text{rays\_o}_z$. This seems to represent the change in the mapped Z coordinate. Since $o'_z = 1 + 2 \cdot \text{near} / \text{rays\_o}_z$, the derivative with respect to depth is complex. However, in NDC ray marching, the direction Z component often simplifies. We use the formula exactly as stated in [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Concatenate:**
    Combine components to form the final NDC rays:
    $$ \text{rays\_o\_ndc} = [o'_x, o'_y, o'_z] $$
    $$ \text{rays\_d\_ndc} = [d'_x, d'_y, d'_z] $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch

def general_ndc_rays(rays_o, rays_d, focal, W, H, near=1.0):
    """
    Convert camera-space rays to NDC space for general scenes.
    
    Args:
        rays_o: Tensor of shape (..., 3), ray origins in world/camera space.
        rays_d: Tensor of shape (..., 3), ray directions in world/camera space.
        focal: Scalar, focal length.
        W: Scalar, image width.
        H: Scalar, image height.
        near: Scalar, near plane distance. Default 1.0 [S1, unofficial].
        
    Returns:
        rays_o_ndc: Tensor of shape (..., 3), NDC origins.
        rays_d_ndc: Tensor of shape (..., 3), NDC directions.
    """
    # Ensure tensors are float32
    rays_o = rays_o.float()
    rays_d = rays_d.float()
    
    # Extract z components
    rays_o_z = rays_o[..., 2:3]
    rays_d_z = rays_d[..., 2:3]
    
    # Step 1: Shift ray origins to the near plane
    # Formula from [S1, unofficial]: t = -(near + rays_o[..., 2:3]) / rays_d[..., 2:3]
    # Note: This assumes a specific coordinate convention (likely OpenGL-like where camera looks -Z or similar adjustment)
    # If rays_d_z is 0, this will produce NaN/Inf. Guard against division by zero if necessary [G].
    t = -(near + rays_o_z) / rays_d_z
    
    # Update origins
    rays_o = rays_o + t * rays_d
    
    # Recompute z after shift for consistency in projection formulas
    rays_o_z = rays_o[..., 2:3]
    
    # Precompute scaling factors
    # 1 / (W / (2 * focal)) = 2 * focal / W
    scale_x = 2.0 * focal / W
    scale_y = 2.0 * focal / H
    
    # Step 2: Compute transformed origin components
    # o0 = -scale_x * (rays_o_x / rays_o_z)
    # o1 = -scale_y * (rays_o_y / rays_o_z)
    # o2 = 1 + 2 * near / rays_o_z
    
    rays_o_x = rays_o[..., 0:1]
    rays_o_y = rays_o[..., 1:2]
    
    inv_o_z = 1.0 / rays_o_z
    
    o0 = -scale_x * rays_o_x * inv_o_z
    o1 = -scale_y * rays_o_y * inv_o_z
    o2 = 1.0 + 2.0 * near * inv_o_z
    
    rays_o_ndc = torch.cat([o0, o1, o2], dim=-1)
    
    # Step 3: Compute transformed direction components
    # d0 = -scale_x * (rays_d_x / rays_d_z - rays_o_x / rays_o_z)
    # d1 = -scale_y * (rays_d_y / rays_d_z - rays_o_y / rays_o_z)
    # d2 = -2 * near / rays_o_z
    
    rays_d_x = rays_d[..., 0:1]
    rays_d_y = rays_d[..., 1:2]
    
    inv_d_z = 1.0 / rays_d_z
    
    d0 = -scale_x * (rays_d_x * inv_d_z - rays_o_x * inv_o_z)
    d1 = -scale_y * (rays_d_y * inv_d_z - rays_o_y * inv_o_z)
    d2 = -2.0 * near * inv_o_z
    
    rays_d_ndc = torch.cat([d0, d1, d2], dim=-1)
    
    return rays_o_ndc, rays_d_ndc
```

**Parameters:**
- `near`: The near plane distance. In the reference implementation [S1, unofficial], this is hardcoded to $1.0$.
- `focal`, `W`, `H`: Camera intrinsics.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** If `rays_d_z` or `rays_o_z` is zero, the shift or projection will fail. This occurs if the ray is parallel to the image plane or if the origin is at the camera center (though shifting usually moves it away). [G] Add a small epsilon to denominators if robustness is required.
- **Coordinate System:** The formulas in [S1, unofficial] use specific signs (e.g., negative scaling for x/y). This assumes a specific camera convention (likely OpenGL NDC where Z is positive into the screen or similar). If using DirectX or other conventions, signs may need flipping.
- **Forward-Facing Assumption:** This method is designed for forward-facing scenes [S1, unofficial]. It may not behave correctly for general 360-degree scenes with arbitrary camera orientations relative to the scene center.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of rays. The operations are element-wise arithmetic.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the transformed rays.
- **Strengths:** Enables uniform sampling in NDC space to approximate inverse depth sampling, which is crucial for efficient NeRF training on forward-facing scenes [S1, unofficial]. Handles general camera intrinsics unlike Blender-specific NDC [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Assumes forward-facing scenes [S1, unofficial]. Sensitive to the near plane definition. Performance depends on the efficiency of the tensor operations (e.g., in PyTorch/JAX).
- **When to use:** When training NeRF-like models on forward-facing datasets (e.g., LLFF) using general camera parameters.
- **When not to use:** For scenes with significant camera rotation around the object (non-forward-facing) or when using Blender's specific NDC conventions.

## Background
The General NDC Ray Conversion method is widely used in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) implementations, particularly those adapting the technique to datasets beyond the standard Blender synthetic set, such as LLFF (Forward-Facing) [S1, unofficial]. The specific implementation details (shifting origins, projection formulas) are derived from open-source NeRF repositories like `nerf-from-scratch` [S1, unofficial] and collaborative neural radiance field projects [S2, unofficial]. The concept of NDC rays for inverse depth sampling was popularized by the original NeRF paper and subsequent adaptations for different camera models.
