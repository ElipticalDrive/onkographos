# General NDC Ray Conversion

Also called: NDC Ray Conversion, NDC Rays, General NDC

## Overview
General NDC Ray Conversion maps camera-space rays into a normalized device coordinate (NDC) space to fix the near and far clipping planes at constant values, removing the need for scene-dependent depth bounds in non-spherical datasets [S1, S3, S4, S5, unofficial]. Unlike Blender-specific NDC conventions that assume fixed intrinsics, this method handles arbitrary camera parameters by explicitly shifting ray origins to the near plane and projecting them based on focal length and image dimensions [S1, S3, S4, S5, unofficial]. It is primarily used for forward-facing scenes where depth ranges are large, enabling uniform sampling in NDC space to correspond to inverse depth (disparity) sampling in the original world space [S3, S4, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `rays_o`: Ray origins in world/camera space, shape `(..., 3)`.
- `rays_d`: Ray directions in world/camera space, shape `(..., 3)`.
- `focal`: Focal length of the camera (scalar).
- `W`: Image width (scalar).
- `H`: Image height (scalar).
- `near`: Distance to the near plane (scalar). Typically hardcoded to $1.0$ in implementations [S1, S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `rays_o_ndc`: Transformed ray origins in NDC space, shape `(..., 3)`.
- `rays_d_ndc`: Transformed ray directions in NDC space, shape `(..., 3)`.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on batched tensors where the last dimension holds the 3D vector components $(x, y, z)$ [S3, S4, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Shift Ray Origins to Near Plane:**
   Compute the shift parameter $t$ along the ray direction to move the origin to the near plane:
   $$ t = \frac{\text{near} - \text{rays\_o}_z}{\text{rays\_d}_z} $$
   Update the origin:
   $$ \text{rays\_o} \leftarrow \text{rays\_o} + t \cdot \text{rays\_d} $$
   (Note: Source [S3, unofficial] uses $t = -(\text{near} + \text{rays\_o}_z) / \text{rays\_d}_z$, which is algebraically equivalent when the camera looks along the negative $z$ axis and `near` is positive [S3, S4, unofficial].)

2. **Project Origins to NDC Space:**
   Compute the transformed origin components by scaling the $x$ and $y$ coordinates by the inverse field of view, and mapping $z$ to a fixed range:
   $$ o'_x = -\frac{1}{W / (2 \cdot \text{focal})} \cdot \frac{\text{rays\_o}_x}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
   $$ o'_y = -\frac{1}{H / (2 \cdot \text{focal})} \cdot \frac{\text{rays\_o}_y}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
   $$ o'_z = 1 + \frac{2 \cdot \text{near}}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
   (Source [S4, unofficial] uses positive signs for $o'_x, o'_y$ and $o'_z = 1 - 2 \cdot \text{near} / \text{rays\_o}_z$, reflecting a different sign convention [S4, unofficial].)

3. **Project Directions to NDC Space:**
   Compute the transformed direction components as the difference between the projected direction endpoint and the projected origin endpoint:
   $$ d'_x = -\frac{1}{W / (2 \cdot \text{focal})} \cdot \left( \frac{\text{rays\_d}_x}{\text{rays\_d}_z} - \frac{\text{rays\_o}_x}{\text{rays\_o}_z} \right) $$
   $$ d'_y = -\frac{1}{H / (2 \cdot \text{focal})} \cdot \left( \frac{\text{rays\_d}_y}{\text{rays\_d}_z} - \frac{\text{rays\_o}_y}{\text{rays\_o}_z} \right) $$
   $$ d'_z = -\frac{2 \cdot \text{near}}{\text{rays\_o}_z} $$
   (Source [S4, unofficial] uses positive signs for these components [S4, unofficial].)

4. **Concatenate Components:**
   Stack the computed components along the last dimension to form the final NDC rays:
   $$ \text{rays\_o\_ndc} = [o'_x, o'_y, o'_z] $$
   $$ \text{rays\_d\_ndc} = [d'_x, d'_y, d'_z] $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import torch

def general_ndc_rays(rays_o, rays_d, focal, W, H, near=1.0):
    """
    Convert camera-space rays to NDC space for general scenes.
    
    Args:
        rays_o: Tensor of shape (..., 3), ray origins in world/camera space.
        rays_d: Tensor of shape (..., 3), ray directions in world/camera space.
        focal: Scalar, focal length.
        W: Scalar, image width.
        H: Scalar, image height.
        near: Scalar, near plane distance. Default 1.0 [S1, S3, unofficial].
        
    Returns:
        rays_o_ndc: Tensor of shape (..., 3), NDC origins.
        rays_d_ndc: Tensor of shape (..., 3), NDC directions.
    """
    # Extract z components for broadcasting
    rays_o_z = rays_o[..., 2:3]
    rays_d_z = rays_d[..., 2:3]
    
    # Step 1: Shift ray origins to the near plane
    t = (near - rays_o_z) / rays_d_z
    rays_o = rays_o + t * rays_d
    
    # Recompute z after shift for projection formulas
    rays_o_z = rays_o[..., 2:3]
    
    # Precompute scaling factors
    scale_x = 2.0 * focal / W
    scale_y = 2.0 * focal / H
    
    # Step 2: Compute transformed origin components
    inv_o_z = 1.0 / rays_o_z
    o0 = -scale_x * rays_o[..., 0:1] * inv_o_z
    o1 = -scale_y * rays_o[..., 1:2] * inv_o_z
    o2 = 1.0 + 2.0 * near * inv_o_z
    rays_o_ndc = torch.cat([o0, o1, o2], dim=-1)
    
    # Step 3: Compute transformed direction components
    inv_d_z = 1.0 / rays_d_z
    d0 = -scale_x * (rays_d[..., 0:1] * inv_d_z - rays_o[..., 0:1] * inv_o_z)
    d1 = -scale_y * (rays_d[..., 1:2] * inv_d_z - rays_o[..., 1:2] * inv_o_z)
    d2 = -2.0 * near * inv_o_z
    rays_d_ndc = torch.cat([d0, d1, d2], dim=-1)
    
    return rays_o_ndc, rays_d_ndc
```

**Parameters:**
- `near`: The near plane distance. In reference implementations, this is typically hardcoded to $1.0$ [S1, S3, unofficial].
- `focal`, `W`, `H`: Camera intrinsic parameters.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** If `rays_d_z` or `rays_o_z` is zero, the shift or projection will fail. This occurs if a ray is parallel to the image plane or if the origin lies exactly at the camera center before shifting. Adding a small epsilon to denominators is recommended for robustness [G].
- **Coordinate System Conventions:** The formulas above follow the sign convention of Source [S3, unofficial]. Source [S4, unofficial] uses the opposite signs for $x, y, z$ components, reflecting a different camera projection convention. Implementers must match the signs to their camera model [S3, S4, unofficial].
- **Forward-Facing Assumption:** The method assumes forward-facing scenes where depth variation is bounded along the viewing direction. For spherical or 360-degree scenes, the code typically checks a `spheric_poses` flag and falls back to scene-dependent bounds instead of NDC conversion [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Ray Generation from Pixel Coordinates](2514-ray-generation-from-pixel-coordinates.md)
Converts 2D screen pixel coordinates into 3D ray origins and directions in world space, enabling volume rendering or ray marching to begin from the camera's perspective. It differs from [Pinhole Camera Projection (Pixel to Camera)](3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md) by producing a full ray (origin and direction) for traversal rather than a single 3D point at a specific depth, and from General NDC Ray Conversion by typically handling the full transformation from pixel indices to world-space rays, often assuming the origin is the camera center.

### [NDC Ray Transformation](905-ndc-ray-transformation.md)
The parent technique describing the general concept of mapping rays to NDC for inverse depth sampling.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of rays, as the operations are element-wise arithmetic [S3, S4, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the transformed rays [S3, S4, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Enables uniform sampling in NDC space to approximate inverse depth sampling, which is crucial for efficient NeRF training on forward-facing scenes [S1, S3, unofficial]. Handles general camera intrinsics unlike Blender-specific NDC conventions [S2, S4, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Assumes forward-facing scenes and bounded depth ranges [S1, S3, unofficial]. Sensitive to the near plane definition and camera coordinate conventions [S3, S4, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When training NeRF-like models on forward-facing datasets (e.g., LLFF) using general camera parameters [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** For scenes with significant camera rotation around the object (non-forward-facing) or when using Blender's specific NDC conventions [S1, S4, unofficial].

## Background
The General NDC Ray Conversion method is widely used in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) implementations, particularly those adapting the technique to datasets beyond the standard Blender synthetic set, such as LLFF (Forward-Facing) [S1, S3, unofficial]. The specific implementation details (shifting origins, projection formulas) are derived from open-source NeRF repositories like `nerf-from-scratch` [S3, unofficial] and collaborative neural radiance field projects [S5, unofficial]. The concept of NDC rays for inverse depth sampling was popularized by the original NeRF paper and subsequent adaptations for different camera models [S1, S3, unofficial].
