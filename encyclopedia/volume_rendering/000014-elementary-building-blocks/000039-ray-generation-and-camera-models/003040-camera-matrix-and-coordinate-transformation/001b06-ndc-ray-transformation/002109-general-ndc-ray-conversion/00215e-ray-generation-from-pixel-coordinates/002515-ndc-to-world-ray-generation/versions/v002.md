# NDC-to-World Ray Generation

Also called: Camera Raycasting, View Space Ray Origin

## Overview
NDC-to-World Ray Generation computes the world-space position of a ray origin for a given Normalized Device Coordinate (NDC) pixel, assuming a pinhole camera model where the camera is at the origin in view space, facing the positive Z axis, with a near plane at $Z=1$ [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Ray Generation from Pixel Coordinates](2514-ray-generation-from-pixel-coordinates.md) by explicitly constructing the ray origin in world space based on the near-plane geometry defined by the field of view and aspect ratio, rather than just transforming pixel coordinates to camera space [S1, unofficial]. It differs from the sibling method [Pinhole Camera Projection (Pixel to Camera)](3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md) by outputting the world-space origin directly instead of returning camera-space coordinates [S1, unofficial]. A common implementation pitfall is incorrectly offsetting the ray origin by screen-space coordinates, which causes field-of-view distortion and curvature artifacts [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `ndc_x`, `ndc_y`: Normalized Device Coordinates of the pixel in the range $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
- `half_fov_rad_x`: Horizontal half-field of view in radians [S1, unofficial].
- `aspect`: Screen aspect ratio (width divided by height) [S1, unofficial].
- `camera_pos`: World-space position of the camera [S1, unofficial].
- `camera_forward`, `camera_up`, `camera_right`: Camera orientation basis vectors [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `world_pos`: The world-space origin of the ray [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- `Vector3d`: A 3D vector structure with fields `x`, `y`, `z` [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Initialize camera basis vectors in view space: `forward = (0, 0, 1)`, `up = (0, 1, 0)`, and `right = cross(up, forward)` [S1, unofficial].
2. Rotate the camera basis vectors around a specified axis (x, y, or z) by a given angle in degrees, updating each vector independently [S1, unofficial].
3. Calculate the near-plane dimensions using the horizontal half-field of view and aspect ratio [S1, unofficial].
4. Calculate the view-space position by offsetting the forward vector along the right and up axes based on the NDC coordinates [S1, unofficial].
5. Transform the view-space position to world space by adding the camera position [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
- $near\_plane\_width = 2.0 \cdot \tan(\text{half\_fov\_rad\_x})$ [S1, unofficial].
- $near\_plane\_height = \frac{near\_plane\_width}{\text{aspect}}$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\text{view\_pos} = \text{forward} + \text{right} \cdot (\text{ndc\_x} - 0.5) \cdot near\_plane\_width + \text{up} \cdot (\text{ndc\_y} - 0.5) \cdot near\_plane\_height$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\text{world\_pos} = \text{view\_pos} + \text{camera\_pos}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- `half_fov_rad_x`: Horizontal half-field of view in radians, derived from degrees via $(\text{deg} \cdot \pi) / 180.0$ [S1, unofficial].
- `aspect`: Screen aspect ratio used to scale the near-plane height [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The formula assumes NDC coordinates are in the range $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
- Adding screen-space coordinates (e.g., `vec3(x, y, 0)`) to the ray origin while also adding the direction vector results in an incorrect effective field of view that varies with pixel position, leading to distortion and curvature artifacts, especially when the camera is close to objects [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def ndc_to_world_ray(ndc_x, ndc_y, half_fov_rad_x, aspect, camera_pos, camera_forward, camera_up, camera_right):
    """Computes the world-space ray origin for a given NDC pixel."""
    near_plane_width = 2.0 * math.tan(half_fov_rad_x)
    near_plane_height = near_plane_width / aspect
    view_pos = camera_forward + camera_right * (ndc_x - 0.5) * near_plane_width + camera_up * (ndc_y - 0.5) * near_plane_height
    world_pos = view_pos + camera_pos
    return world_pos
```

## Subtopics

### Correct Camera Ray Origin
A correction for raymarching where the ray origin is incorrectly initialized with screen-space pixel coordinates, causing field-of-view distortion and curvature artifacts when the camera is close to objects [S2, unofficial]. The correct method initializes the ray origin `ray_pos` to the camera position `cam_pos` only, without adding screen-space coordinates [S2, unofficial]. Specifically, one should not add the screen-space vector `vec3(x, y, 0)` to the ray origin before adding the ray direction `r` [S2, unofficial]. Adding `vec3(x, y, 0)` to the ray origin while also adding the direction vector `r` (which already contains screen-space information) results in an incorrect effective field of view that varies with pixel position, leading to distortion [S2, unofficial]. Standard raymarching leaves the ray origin at the camera position (or world origin relative to camera) and varies only the direction based on fragment coordinates, whereas the flawed approach adds pixel offsets to the origin [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method is derived from the implementation of a terminal-based signed distance field renderer [S1, unofficial]. The specific pitfall of adding screen-space coordinates to the ray origin was identified in a discussion on avoiding curvature artifacts in raytracers [S2, unofficial]. The source [S1, unofficial] provides the core formula for calculating the ray origin based on NDC coordinates, field of view, and aspect ratio. The source [S2, unofficial] highlights the common mistake of offsetting the ray origin, which leads to distortion.
