# Ray Generation from Pixel Coordinates

Also called: Camera Ray Generation, Camera Raycasting, Pixel to Ray Unprojection, Ray Direction Calculation, Ray Generation from Pixels, Ray Generation from Screen Coordinates

## Overview
This method converts 2D screen pixel coordinates into 3D ray origins and directions in world space, serving as the foundational step for ray marching and volume rendering [S1, S4, S6, unofficial]. It solves the problem of mapping discrete image samples to continuous 3D geometry by inverting the camera projection pipeline [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Pinhole Camera Projection (Pixel to Camera)](3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md), which outputs camera-space coordinates, this method typically targets world-space rays or handles the full transformation chain including the inverse Projection-View matrix [S1, S6, unofficial]. It differs from [General NDC Ray Conversion](2429-general-ndc-ray-conversion.md) by often assuming specific camera configurations (like Blender's or standard OpenGL/NDC conventions) and explicitly handling the generation of both origin and direction vectors for rasterization or marching [S1, S4, S6, unofficial].

## How it works
The method generally follows two primary approaches: matrix-based unprojection (common in OpenGL/NDC pipelines) and intrinsic-based geometric calculation (common in PyTorch3D/Metal implementations). Both approaches require pixel coordinates $(u, v)$, image resolution $(W, H)$, and camera parameters (intrinsics or projection matrices).

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**:
  - Pixel coordinates: $(u, v)$ or a grid of indices.
  - Image resolution: Width $W$, Height $H$.
  - Camera parameters: Either an inverse Projection-View matrix ($M_{inv}$) or intrinsic parameters (Field of View $FOV$, aspect ratio, or focal length).
  - Near plane depth $z_{near}$ (often 0 or 1 depending on coordinate system).
- **Outputs**:
  - Ray origins: $\mathbf{r}_o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (usually the camera center).
  - Ray directions: $\mathbf{r}_d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (normalized vectors pointing from origin through the pixel).

### Data Structures
- **Pixel Grid**: A tensor or array of shape $(H, W, 2)$ or $(N, 2)$ containing $(u, v)$ coordinates.
- **NDC Points**: Homogeneous coordinates in Normalized Device Coordinates, shape $(N, 4)$ or $(N, 3)$ with appended $w=1$.
- **RayBundle**: A structure containing:
  - `rays_o`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$ for origins.
  - `rays_d`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$ for directions.
  - `sample_points`: Zero-initialized tensor for marching results.

### Method 1: Matrix Unprojection (NDC to World)
This approach uses the inverse of the combined Projection and View matrices. It is standard in OpenGL-style pipelines where NDC ranges from $[-1, 1]$.

1.  **Convert to NDC**: Map pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ to NDC space $(x_{ndc}, y_{ndc})$.
    $$ x_{ndc} = \frac{2u}{W} - 1 $$
    $$ y_{ndc} = \frac{2v}{H} - 1 $$
    The $z$ component is set to the near plane depth in NDC, typically $0$ for OpenGL (where near is 0) or $-1$ for others. Let $z_{ndc} = 0$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{ndc} = [x_{ndc}, y_{ndc}, z_{ndc}, 1]^T $$

2.  **Transform to World**: Multiply by the inverse Projection-View matrix $M_{inv}$.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{clip} = M_{inv} \times \mathbf{p}_{ndc} $$
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{world\_homo} = [x_w, y_w, z_w, w_w]^T $$

3.  **Perspective Division**: Convert to Cartesian coordinates.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{world} = \left[ \frac{x_w}{w_w}, \frac{y_w}{w_w}, \frac{z_w}{w_w} \right]^T $$

4.  **Generate Origin and Direction**:
    - Calculate $\mathbf{p}_{world}$ for $z_{ndc}=0$ (near plane) to get the ray origin $\mathbf{r}_o$. Note: In many camera models, the origin is simply the camera center, but unprojecting the near plane provides the intersection with the view frustum's front face [S1, unofficial].
    - Calculate $\mathbf{p}_{world}$ for $z_{ndc}=1$ (or another depth) to get a second point $\mathbf{p}_{far}$.
    - Compute direction: $\mathbf{r}_d = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{p}_{far} - \mathbf{r}_o)$ [S1, unofficial].

### Method 2: Intrinsic-Based Geometric Calculation
This approach is common in deep learning frameworks (e.g., PyTorch3D) and Metal shaders, using Field of View (FOV) and resolution.

1.  **Calculate Centered Coordinates**:
    $$ x_{center} = u - \frac{W}{2} $$
    $$ y_{center} = v - \frac{H}{2} $$
    [S4, unofficial]

2.  **Calculate Z-component (Depth)**:
    Using the vertical field of view $FOV$ (in degrees):
    $$ z = \frac{H}{\tan\left(\frac{FOV \cdot \pi}{360}\right)} $$
    [S4, unofficial]

3.  **Construct Ray Direction**:
    The direction vector in camera space (assuming camera looks down negative Z) is:
    $$ \mathbf{d}_{cam} = [x_{center}, y_{center}, -z] $$
    $$ \mathbf{r}_d = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{d}_{cam}) $$
    [S4, unofficial]

4.  **Set Origin**:
    The ray origin is the camera center $\mathbf{C}$ [S2, S5, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{r}_o = \mathbf{C} $$

### Pseudocode (Matrix Unprojection)
```python
import numpy as np

def generate_rays_matrix(pixel_u, pixel_v, width, height, inv_proj_view):
    """
    Generate ray origin and direction using inverse Projection-View matrix.
    
    Args:
        pixel_u: float, x-coordinate of pixel (0 to width-1)
        pixel_v: float, y-coordinate of pixel (0 to height-1)
        width: int, image width
        height: int, image height
        inv_proj_view: np.array, shape (4, 4), inverse of Projection * View matrix
        
    Returns:
        origin: np.array, shape (3,)
        direction: np.array, shape (3,)
    """
    # 1. Convert to NDC
    # OpenGL NDC: x, y in [-1, 1], z in [0, 1] (or -1 to 1 depending on API)
    # Here we assume z_ndc = 0 for near plane as per [S1, unofficial]
    x_ndc = (2.0 * pixel_u) / width - 1.0
    y_ndc = (2.0 * pixel_v) / height - 1.0
    z_ndc = 0.0
    
    # 2. Transform NDC to World Space (Near Plane)
    p_ndc = np.array([x_ndc, y_ndc, z_ndc, 1.0])
    p_world_homo = inv_proj_view @ p_ndc
    
    # Perspective division
    p_world_near = p_world_homo[:3] / p_world_homo[3]
    origin = p_world_near
    
    # 3. Transform NDC to World Space (Far Point for Direction)
    # Using z_ndc = 1.0 to get a point further along the ray [S1, unofficial]
    z_ndc_far = 1.0
    p_ndc_far = np.array([x_ndc, y_ndc, z_ndc_far, 1.0])
    p_world_far_homo = inv_proj_view @ p_ndc_far
    p_world_far = p_world_far_homo[:3] / p_world_far_homo[3]
    
    # 4. Compute Direction
    direction = p_world_far - origin
    direction = direction / np.linalg.norm(direction)
    
    return origin, direction
```

### Pseudocode (Intrinsic-Based)
```python
import numpy as np

def generate_rays_intrinsic(pixel_u, pixel_v, width, height, fov_deg):
    """
    Generate ray direction using FOV and resolution. Origin is assumed to be (0,0,0) in camera space.
    
    Args:
        pixel_u: float, x-coordinate
        pixel_v: float, y-coordinate
        width: int
        height: int
        fov_deg: float, vertical field of view in degrees
        
    Returns:
        origin: np.array, shape (3,), typically [0,0,0]
        direction: np.array, shape (3,)
    """
    # 1. Centered coordinates
    x_center = pixel_u - width / 2.0
    y_center = pixel_v - height / 2.0
    
    # 2. Z component
    # tan(fov/2) where fov is in radians
    fov_rad = np.radians(fov_deg)
    z = height / np.tan(fov_rad / 2.0)
    
    # 3. Direction in camera space (looking down -Z)
    direction = np.array([x_center, y_center, -z])
    direction = direction / np.linalg.norm(direction)
    
    # 4. Origin
    origin = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0])
    
    return origin, direction
```

### Parameters
- **FOV**: Vertical Field of View in degrees. Typical value: $45.0^\circ$ [S4, unofficial].
- **Resolution**: $(W, H)$. Example: $1280 \times 720$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Near Plane Z**: Often $0$ or $1$ in NDC space [S1, S7, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Coordinate System Origin**: Metal and some graphics APIs place $(0,0)$ at the top-left, while mathematical conventions may place it at the center. Adjustments like `fragCoord - size / 2.0` are necessary to center the coordinate system [S4, unofficial].
- **Aspect Ratio**: If using horizontal FOV, the vertical component must be scaled by the aspect ratio $H/W$ [S7, unofficial].
- **Perspective Division**: If $w_w$ (the w-component after matrix multiplication) is zero or very close to zero, the ray is at infinity or the matrix is singular. Guard against division by zero [S1, unofficial].
- **NDC Convention**: OpenGL uses $z \in [0, 1]$ (or $[-1, 1]$ in newer versions), while Vulkan/DirectX use $z \in [0, 1]$ or $[-1, 1]$. Ensure the $z_{ndc}$ value matches the projection matrix's expectation [S1, S7, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Pinhole Camera Projection (Pixel to Camera)](3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md)
Converts pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ and depth $z$ into camera-space homogeneous coordinates using intrinsic parameters (focal length, principal point). It outputs points in the camera's local coordinate system, unlike this method which often outputs world-space rays. Choose this when working in camera space or when depth is known and needed for 3D reconstruction [3162-pinhole-camera-projection-pixel-to-camera.md].

### NDC-to-World Ray Generation
Computes the world-space position of a ray origin for a given NDC pixel, assuming the camera is at the origin in view space, facing positive Z, with a near plane at $Z=1$. It explicitly constructs the view frustum geometry using rotation matrices and basis vectors. Choose this for custom ray marchers that operate in view space or require explicit near-plane intersection calculations [S7, unofficial].

### NDC to Screen Camera Conversion
Transforms a camera from normalized device coordinates (NDC) space to screen pixel coordinates for rasterization. It converts focal lengths and principal points to screen-space units. This is the inverse operation of ray generation, used for rendering or visualization rather than ray marching [S8, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: $O(1)$ per pixel for intrinsic-based; $O(1)$ per pixel for matrix-based (assuming matrix multiplication is constant size $4 \times 4$). For $N$ pixels, $O(N)$.
- **Memory Complexity**: $O(N)$ to store ray origins and directions for all pixels.
- **Performance**: Matrix unprojection is slightly more expensive due to matrix-vector multiplication but handles arbitrary camera orientations and projections. Intrinsic-based is faster and simpler but assumes a standard pinhole model.
- **Strengths**: Robust handling of perspective projection; works with any camera pose if the inverse matrix is available.
- **Limitations**: Requires accurate camera calibration or matrix inversion. Sensitive to precision errors in perspective division.

## Background
Ray generation from pixel coordinates is a fundamental technique in computer graphics, originating from the development of ray tracing and rasterization pipelines in the 1970s and 1980s. It is essential for volume rendering, where rays must be cast from the camera through each pixel to sample the volume [S1, S4, unofficial]. Modern implementations are prevalent in neural rendering frameworks like NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting, where efficient ray generation is critical for training and inference speed [S2, S3, S8, unofficial].
