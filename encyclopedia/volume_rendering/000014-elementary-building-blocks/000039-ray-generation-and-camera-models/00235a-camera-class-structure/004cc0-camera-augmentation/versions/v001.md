# Camera Augmentation
Also called: Intrinsic/Extrinsic perturbation, Perspective Augmentation

## Overview
Camera augmentation perturbs camera parameters during training to improve robustness against small image misalignments and promote diversity in generated views [S1, S2]. It deviates from the fixed camera assumptions of EG3D by introducing stochastic variations in intrinsic and extrinsic properties [S1]. While EG3D assumes a fixed camera radius of 2.7, focal length of 18.83, zero roll, and a central principal point, this method samples these values from specific distributions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Base camera parameters (focal length $f$, radius $r$, principal point $(c_x, c_y)$, roll angle $\theta_{roll}$), dataset type (FFHQ or AFHQ), current training epoch $e$, and maximum epoch $E_{max}$ (typically 100 for the augmentation schedule) [S1, S2].
**Outputs:** Perturbed camera parameters used for ray generation [S1, S2].

**Data Structures:**
The method modifies the following scalar parameters:
- $f$: Focal length (float)
- $r$: Camera radius (float)
- $c_x, c_y$: Principal point coordinates (int/float)
- $\theta_{roll}$: Camera roll angle in degrees (float)

**Steps:**

1. **Determine Augmentation Schedule (Perspective Augmentation):**
   If using the perspective augmentation technique [S2], calculate the current maximum angle range based on the epoch. The range expands linearly from $[0^\circ, 0^\circ]$ to $[-15^\circ, 15^\circ]$ over 100 epochs [S2].
   $$ \theta_{max} = 15^\circ \times \min\left(1, \frac{e}{E_{max}}\right) $$
   Sample an angle $\theta_{persp}$ uniformly from $[-\theta_{max}, \theta_{max}]$ [S2]. This angle is applied as a perspective distortion along the height axis (rotation around the z-axis) [S2]. The rotation matrix $R_z$ is defined as:
   $$ R_z = \begin{pmatrix} \cos \theta_z & -\sin \theta_z & 0 \\ \sin \theta_z & \cos \theta_z & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$
   where $\theta_z = \theta_{persp}$ [S2]. Note: Direct optimization of Euler angles is avoided due to discontinuities at $\theta=0$; instead, cosine and sine values are carried [S2].

2. **Sample Intrinsic and Extrinsic Perturbations:**
   For each training step, sample the following parameters from normal distributions [S1]. The parameters depend on the dataset:

   **For FFHQ:**
   - Focal length $f \sim \mathcal{N}(18.83, 1.0^2)$ [S1]
   - Camera radius $r \sim \mathcal{N}(2.7, 0.1^2)$ [S1]
   - Principal point $c_x, c_y \sim \mathcal{N}(256, 14^2)$ [S1]
   - Camera roll $\theta_{roll} \sim \mathcal{N}(0^\circ, 2^\circ)$ [S1]

   **For AFHQ:**
   - Focal length $f \sim \mathcal{N}(18.83, 1.5^2)$ [S1]
   - Camera radius $r \sim \mathcal{N}(2.7, 0.1^2)$ [S1]
   - Principal point $c_x, c_y \sim \mathcal{N}(256, 25^2)$ [S1]
   - Camera roll $\theta_{roll} \sim \mathcal{N}(0^\circ, 6^\circ)$ [S1]

3. **Apply Perturbations:**
   Combine the sampled parameters to form the augmented camera matrix. If perspective augmentation is active, apply the rotation $R_z$ to the camera orientation [S2]. The final camera pose and intrinsics are then used for ray generation [S1, S2].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def sample_camera_augmentation(dataset_type, epoch, max_epochs=100):
    """
    Samples augmented camera parameters based on dataset and training progress.
    
    Args:
        dataset_type: 'FFHQ' or 'AFHQ'
        epoch: Current training epoch
        max_epochs: Total epochs for linear schedule (default 100)
        
    Returns:
        dict with keys: 'focal_length', 'radius', 'principal_point', 'roll', 'perspective_angle'
    """
    # Base parameters (EG3D defaults)
    base_focal = 18.83
    base_radius = 2.7
    base_principal = 256
    
    # Standard deviations for FFHQ and AFHQ
    if dataset_type == 'FFHQ':
        std_focal = 1.0
        std_radius = 0.1
        std_principal = 14
        std_roll = 2.0
    elif dataset_type == 'AFHQ':
        std_focal = 1.5
        std_radius = 0.1
        std_principal = 25
        std_roll = 6.0
    else:
        raise ValueError("Unknown dataset type")

    # Sample intrinsic and extrinsic perturbations
    focal_length = np.random.normal(base_focal, std_focal)
    radius = np.random.normal(base_radius, std_radius)
    principal_point = np.random.normal(base_principal, std_principal)
    roll = np.random.normal(0.0, std_roll)

    # Sample perspective augmentation angle (linear schedule)
    # Range expands from [0, 0] to [-15, 15] over max_epochs
    current_max_angle = 15.0 * min(1.0, epoch / max_epochs)
    perspective_angle = np.random.uniform(-current_max_angle, current_max_angle)

    return {
        'focal_length': focal_length,
        'radius': radius,
        'principal_point': principal_point,
        'roll': roll,
        'perspective_angle': perspective_angle
    }
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The linear schedule for perspective augmentation assumes a fixed `max_epochs` of 100 [S2]. If training exceeds this, the angle remains at $15^\circ$ [S2].
- Principal point sampling is centered at 256, implying a 512x512 image resolution [S1]. For different resolutions, the center and standard deviation may need adjustment [G].
- Direct optimization of Euler angles is avoided in SinGRAF due to discontinuities at $\theta=0$; however, for sampling-based augmentation, direct sampling from normal distributions is used [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, as it involves simple random number generation and arithmetic operations [S1, S2].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, storing only the current sampled parameters [S1, S2].
- **Strengths:** Improves robustness to misalignment and increases view diversity without requiring additional data [S1, S2].
- **Limitations:** Introduces variability that must be learned by the model, potentially slowing convergence if perturbations are too large [S1]. The linear schedule is fixed and may not adapt to different convergence rates [S2].

## Background
Camera augmentation was introduced to address the limitations of fixed camera models in generative radiance fields, specifically EG3D [S1]. It was adopted in Real-Time Radiance Fields for Single-Image Portrait View Synthesis to handle real image misalignments [S1]. SinGRAF also employs a similar perspective augmentation technique to promote diversity in 3D generative radiance fields [S2]. The technique is particularly relevant for single-image view synthesis where ground-truth multi-view data is unavailable [S1, S2].
