# Ray-Confidence Modeling

Also called: Ray-confidence modeling with target degree-of-freedom regularization

## Overview
Ray-confidence modeling estimates the reliability of volumetric primitives based on ray coverage to adaptively control their shape and regularization strength [S1]. It solves the problem of over-smoothing or artifacts in sparse-view reconstruction by assigning higher confidence (and thus sharper, Gaussian-like shapes) to well-observed regions and lower confidence (heavier tails) to occluded or sparsely sampled areas [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Angle Feature Generation](7126-angle-feature-generation.md) by directly influencing the primitive's statistical distribution parameters rather than just providing angular features for downstream networks.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Set of learnable 3D Student’s $t$ primitives $M_T = \{T_i\}_{i=1}^M$ [S1].
- For each primitive $i$: center $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$, degrees of freedom $\nu_i$, density coefficient $\rho_i$ [S1].
- Ray data: normalized direction vectors $\mathbf{u}_{ij}$ from primitive center to camera $j$ in local coordinates [S1].
- Hyperparameters: temperature $T$, scaling factor $\alpha$, min/max degrees of freedom $\nu_{\min}, \nu_{\max}$, regularization weight $\lambda_\nu$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Target degrees of freedom $\nu_i^{\text{target}}$ for each primitive [S1].
- Regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_\nu$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- Primitive $i$ stores: $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, $\nu_i \in \mathbb{R}$, $\rho_i \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
- Ray coverage metric $c_i \in [0, 1]$ representing the fraction of the primitive's support sphere covered by rays [S1].

**Steps:**

1. **Compute Effective Directional Ratio:**
   For each primitive $i$ and camera $j$, compute the effective directional ratio $e_{ij}$ [S1]:
   $$
   e_{ij} = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{(a_i u_{ij}^x)^2 + (b_i u_{ij}^y)^2 + (c_i u_{ij}^z)^2}}
   $$
   where $u_{ij}^x, u_{ij}^y, u_{ij}^z$ are the components of the normalized direction vector $\mathbf{u}_{ij}$, and $a_i, b_i, c_i$ are scaling factors derived from the primitive's covariance or anisotropy [S1]. Note: The source defines $e_{ij}$ but does not explicitly detail how $a_i, b_i, c_i$ are extracted from $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ in the provided excerpt; this is a gap [G]. Typically, these correspond to the inverse square roots of the eigenvalues or diagonal elements of the covariance matrix if axis-aligned.

2. **Compute Ray Coverage Confidence $c_i$:**
   Calculate $c_i$, the fraction of the primitive's sphere covered by rays [S1]. The exact geometric integration method is not specified in the provided text [G]. A standard implementation would involve sampling directions on the sphere and checking if they correspond to valid ray origins, or integrating the solid angle of visible rays.

3. **Compute Target Degrees of Freedom $\nu_i^{\text{target}}$:**
   Map the confidence $c_i$ to a target degree of freedom using a sigmoid function controlled by temperature $T$ [S1]:
   $$
   \text{logit}(c_i) = \log\left(\frac{c_i}{1 - c_i}\right)
   $$
   $$
   \nu_i^{\text{target}} = \nu_{\min} + (\nu_{\max} - \nu_{\min}) \cdot \sigma\left(\frac{\text{logit}(c_i)}{T}\right)
   $$
   where $\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}$ is the sigmoid function [S1].
   - High confidence ($c_i \to 1$) yields $\nu_i^{\text{target}} \to \nu_{\max}$ (Gaussian-like, sharp).
   - Low confidence ($c_i \to 0$) yields $\nu_i^{\text{target}} \to \nu_{\min}$ (heavy-tailed, robust to outliers).

4. **Compute Regularization Loss:**
   Apply a soft log-space Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss to regularize the current $\nu_i$ toward $\nu_i^{\text{target}}$ [S1]:
   $$
   \mathcal{L}_\nu = \frac{\lambda_\nu}{M} \sum_{i=1}^M \omega(c_i) \left( \log \nu_i - \log \nu_i^{\text{target}} \right)^2
   $$
   where $\omega(c_i)$ is a weighting function that emphasizes low-confidence primitives [S1]. The exact form of $\omega(c_i)$ is not specified in the provided text [G]. A common choice is $\omega(c_i) = 1 - c_i$ or similar inverse confidence weighting.

## Subtopics

### Uncertainty-aware sensor model (beams)
A sensor model that incorporates angular uncertainty, improving reconstruction of thin objects and surfaces compared to a model that disregards it [S2]. On the Newer College dataset, it significantly improves the ability to reconstruct surfaces and thin objects at higher resolutions [S2]. Compared to 'ours (rays)' (which disregards angular uncertainty), 'ours (beams)' produces larger maps (30-70% increase) and higher runtime (50% increase) but offers better quality for thin objects and surfaces, especially in noisy real-world data [S2].

### Uncertainty-agnostic sensor model (rays)
A sensor model that disregards angular uncertainty [S2]. On the Panoptic Flat dataset (no noise), there is no difference in performance between 'ours (rays)' and 'ours (beams)' [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given, but the uncertainty-aware model ('ours (beams)') has a 50% increase in runtime compared to the uncertainty-agnostic model ('ours (rays)') [S2].
- **Memory Complexity:** The uncertainty-aware model produces larger maps by 30-70% depending on resolution [S2].
- **Performance:** On the Newer College dataset, the uncertainty-aware model performs on par with TSDF-based methods (voxblox) in surface reconstruction and superior in thin object reconstruction [S2]. On the Panoptic Flat dataset (no noise), performance is identical to the uncertainty-agnostic model [S2].
- **Strengths:** Better reconstruction of thin objects and surfaces in noisy data; good general performance in memory and runtime with potential for multi-threading improvements [S2].
- **Limitations:** Higher memory and runtime costs for the uncertainty-aware variant; performance gain is negligible in noise-free scenarios [S2].

## Background
The ray-confidence modeling method is part of the TR-GS framework, which uses Student’s $t$ primitives for volumetric representation [S1]. The framework was introduced in "TR-GS: High-Fidelity Sparse-View CT Volumetric Rendering via t-Distribution Gaussian Splatting and Ray-Confidence Modeling" [S1]. The uncertainty-aware sensor model ('ours (beams)') is described in "Efficient volumetric mapping of multi-scale environments using wavelet-based compression" [S2].
