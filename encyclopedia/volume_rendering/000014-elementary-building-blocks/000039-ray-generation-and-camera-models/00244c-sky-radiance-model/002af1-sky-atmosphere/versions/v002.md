# Sky Atmosphere
Also called: Rayleigh + Mie + ozone, physically based sky

## Overview
An Earth-like atmosphere model that bakes single-scattered sunlight (Rayleigh, Mie, and ozone scattering) into an equirectangular map sampled by `environment_light`, with sun irradiance attenuated by air along its direction [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its parent [Sky Radiance Model](3256-sky-radiance-model.md) by explicitly modeling the spectral attenuation and scattering physics of an Earth-like atmosphere rather than relying solely on a precomputed view texture and simple sun disk addition [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Sun direction vector, atmospheric parameters (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone coefficients), and the geometry of the atmosphere (implied Earth-like spherical shell) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** An equirectangular environment map containing the baked sky radiance [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Equirectangular Map:** A 2D texture mapping longitude to the x-axis and latitude to the y-axis, requiring one byte per cell [S1, unofficial].
*   **Atmospheric Parameters:** Coefficients for Rayleigh scattering, Mie scattering, and ozone absorption [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Single-Scattered Sunlight:** Compute the contributions from Rayleigh, Mie, and ozone scattering for sunlight interacting with an Earth-like atmosphere [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Bake Contributions:** Store these computed radiance values into an equirectangular map [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Sample Map:** Use an `environment_light` sampler to retrieve sky radiance based on the ray direction [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Attenuate Sun Irradiance:** Modulate the sun's direct irradiance by the air along its direction to redden low suns [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas and Parameters:**
*   The sources describe the baking process conceptually but do not provide explicit mathematical formulas for the scattering coefficients or atmospheric integration [S1, unofficial].
*   The attenuation of the sun's irradiance is described qualitatively as being "attenuated by the air along its direction" [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Low Sun Angles:** The method specifically handles the reddening effect of low suns by attenuating irradiance based on the air path [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** No specific precision requirements are given for the baking process [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def bake_sky_atmosphere(sun_direction, atmosphere_params):
    """
    Bakes single-scattered sunlight into an equirectangular map.
    Args:
        sun_direction: tuple(float, float, float), direction of the sun.
        atmosphere_params: dict containing Rayleigh, Mie, and ozone coefficients.
    Returns:
        list[list[list[float]]]: Equirectangular map of sky radiance (height x width x 3).
    """
    width = 2048
    height = 1024
    sky_map = [[[0.0, 0.0, 0.0] for _ in range(width)] for _ in range(height)]

    for y in range(height):
        for x in range(width):
            # Convert pixel coordinates to spherical coordinates
            longitude = (x / width) * 2.0 * math.pi
            latitude = (y / height) * math.pi - math.pi / 2.0

            # Convert to Cartesian direction vector
            dir_vec = (
                math.cos(latitude) * math.cos(longitude),
                math.sin(latitude),
                math.cos(latitude) * math.sin(longitude)
            )

            # Calculate single-scattered sunlight contributions [G]
            radiance = calculate_single_scattering(dir_vec, sun_direction, atmosphere_params)

            # Store in the equirectangular map
            sky_map[y][x] = radiance

    return sky_map

def sample_sky_atmosphere(ray_direction, sky_map, sun_direction, atmosphere_params):
    """
    Samples the baked sky atmosphere for a given ray direction.
    Args:
        ray_direction: tuple(float, float, float), direction of the ray.
        sky_map: list[list[list[float]]], the baked equirectangular map.
        sun_direction: tuple(float, float, float), direction of the sun.
        atmosphere_params: dict containing atmospheric coefficients.
    Returns:
        list[float]: The sky radiance for the given direction.
    """
    # Convert ray direction to equirectangular coordinates
    longitude = math.atan2(ray_direction[2], ray_direction[0])
    latitude = math.asin(ray_direction[1])

    # Map to texture coordinates
    x = int(((longitude + math.pi) / (2.0 * math.pi)) * len(sky_map[0]))
    y = int(((latitude + math.pi / 2.0) / math.pi) * len(sky_map))

    # Clamp coordinates to valid range
    x = max(0, min(x, len(sky_map[0]) - 1))
    y = max(0, min(y, len(sky_map) - 1))

    # Sample the baked sky radiance
    sky_radiance = sky_map[y][x]

    # Attenuate sun irradiance by air along its direction [G]
    sun_attenuation = calculate_sun_attenuation(sun_direction, atmosphere_params)

    # Combine sky radiance and attenuated sun contribution
    final_radiance = [
        sky_radiance[i] + sun_attenuation[i] for i in range(3)
    ]

    return final_radiance

def calculate_single_scattering(dir_vec, sun_direction, params):
    """Standard single-scattering approximation for Rayleigh, Mie, and ozone [G]."""
    cos_theta = dir_vec[0]*sun_direction[0] + dir_vec[1]*sun_direction[1] + dir_vec[2]*sun_direction[2]
    rayleigh_phase = 3.0 / (16.0 * math.pi) * (1.0 + cos_theta**2)
    mie_g = params.get('mie_g', 0.8)
    mie_phase = 3.0 / (8.0 * math.pi) * ((1.0 + cos_theta**2) / (1.0 + mie_g**2 - 2.0 * mie_g * cos_theta))
    ozone_factor = params.get('ozone_factor', 0.5)
    return [
        rayleigh_phase * params.get('rayleigh', 0.0025) * ozone_factor,
        mie_phase * params.get('mie', 0.0010) * ozone_factor,
        rayleigh_phase * params.get('rayleigh', 0.0025) * ozone_factor
    ]

def calculate_sun_attenuation(sun_direction, params):
    """Calculates the attenuation of the sun's irradiance due to atmospheric scattering [G]."""
    path_length = 1.0 / max(math.sin(sun_direction[1]), 0.01)
    attenuation = math.exp(-path_length * params.get('total_extinction', 0.003))
    return [
        attenuation * 1.0,
        attenuation * 0.8,
        attenuation * 0.6
    ]
```

## Subtopics

### A-cube Model
A general illumination model for atmospheric effects (blue skies, sunsets, rainbows, shafts of light) based on the A-cube, modeling the atmosphere by particles of different sizes and using volume sampling techniques [S2]. The A-cube or atmospheric cube limits the modeling space to a finite volume [S2]. The atmospheric model handles absorption, scattering (Rayleigh and Mie) and geometric optics (refractive dispersion) but operating in the RGB colour model [S2]. Density and phase functions of the scattering particles inside the cube can vary from point to point [S2]. For complex atmospheric descriptions, the A-cube can be subdivided into a number of voxels to store pre-computed data [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The baking step is performed offline, and the sampling step is O(1) per ray if the map is precomputed [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the resolution of the equirectangular map, with the sources specifying one byte per cell [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Physically based representation of Earth-like sky colors and sun attenuation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to single scattering and Earth-like atmospheres [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When a physically based, Earth-like sky is required with minimal runtime cost [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When complex atmospheric effects like rainbows or non-Earth-like atmospheres are needed [S2].

## Background
The method is associated with the implementation of MagicaVoxel, specifically in the context of adding a physically based sky with Rayleigh, Mie, and ozone scattering [S1, unofficial]. The A-cube model was introduced by Inakage as a general illumination model for atmospheric effects [S2].
