# Camera Motion Trajectory Approximation
Also called: Lie Algebra SE(3) Interpolation, SE(3) Camera Trajectory Approximation

## Overview
This method approximates the continuous camera trajectory during a finite shutter exposure time by interpolating between known discrete poses within the Lie algebra of the special Euclidean group SE(3) [S1]. It solves the problem of modeling motion blur in differentiable rendering by providing a smooth, differentiable parameterization of camera motion that respects the non-commutative nature of 3D rigid body transformations [S1]. It distinguishes itself from linear interpolation in Euclidean space by ensuring interpolated poses remain valid rigid transformations, and from B-spline methods by offering explicit control over the interpolation order via Bézier curves [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Start pose $T_0 \in SE(3)$, end pose $T_1 \in SE(3)$, shutter duration $\tau$, and current time $t \in [0, \tau]$ [S1]. For higher-order methods, additional control points $T_j$ and interpolation order $K$ are required [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Interpolated camera pose $T_t \in SE(3)$ at time $t$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Pose ($T$):** A $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix representing rotation and translation in SE(3) [S1].
*   **Lie Algebra Element ($\xi$):** A 6-dimensional vector representing the logarithm of a rotation matrix, packed as $[\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z, v_x, v_y, v_z]^T$ [G].

**Mathematical Primitives**
*   **Logarithm ($\log$):** Maps $T \in SE(3)$ to its Lie algebra element $\xi \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$ [S1].
*   **Exponential ($\exp$):** Maps $\xi \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$ back to $T \in SE(3)$ [S1].
*   **Matrix Multiplication ($\cdot$):** Standard $4 \times 4$ matrix multiplication [S1].

**Interpolation Methods**

1.  **Linear Interpolation**
    Suitable for short shutter times and small motion amplitudes [S1]. Given start $T_0$ and end $T_1$:
    $$ T_t = T_0 \cdot \exp\left( \log(T_0^{-1} \cdot T_1) \frac{t}{\tau} \right) $$
    where $t \in [0, \tau]$ and $\tau$ is the shutter time [S1].

2.  **Cubic Spline Interpolation**
    Requires four control points $T_0, T_1, T_2, T_3$ [S1]. Let $u = t/\tau \in [0, 1]$. The basis function matrix $\tilde{B}(u)$ is:
    $$ \tilde{B}(u) = C \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ u \\ u^2 \\ u^3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad C = \frac{1}{6} \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 5 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    The interpolated pose is computed as:
    $$ T(u) = T_0 \cdot \prod_{j=0}^{2} \exp\left( \tilde{B}_{j+1}(u) \cdot \Omega_j \right) $$
    where $\Omega_j$ are the Lie algebra elements corresponding to the control points $T_j$ [S1]. The notation $\tilde{B}_{j+1}$ refers to the $(j+1)$-th entry of the basis vector result [S1].

3.  **Bézier Curve Interpolation**
    Uses $K+1$ control points $T_0, \dots, T_K$ and order $K$ [S1]. The source implementation uses $K=7$ [S1]. Let $u = t/\tau \in [0, 1]$. The interpolated pose is:
    $$ T_t = \prod_{j=0}^{K} \exp\left( \binom{K}{j} (1-u)^{K-j} u^j \log(T_j) \right) $$
    This formulation applies De Casteljau’s algorithm in the Lie algebra representation, ensuring differentiability with respect to control points $T_j$ [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import numpy as np

def se3_log(T):
    """Computes the 6-vector Lie algebra element from a 4x4 SE(3) matrix [G]."""
    R = T[:3, :3]
    t_vec = T[:3, 3]
    theta = np.arccos(np.clip((np.trace(R) - 1) / 2, -1.0, 1.0))
    if theta < 1e-6:
        omega = np.zeros(3)
        V_inv = np.eye(3)
    else:
        k = np.array([R[2,1]-R[1,2], R[0,2]-R[2,0], R[1,0]-R[0,1]]) / (2 * np.sin(theta))
        omega = theta * k
        K_skew = np.array([[0, -k[2], k[1]], [k[2], 0, -k[0]], [-k[1], k[0], 0]])
        V = np.eye(3) + ((1 - np.cos(theta)) / (theta**2)) * K_skew + ((theta - np.sin(theta)) / (theta**3)) * (K_skew @ K_skew)
        V_inv = np.linalg.inv(V)
    v = V_inv @ t_vec
    return np.concatenate([omega, v])

def se3_exp(xi):
    """Computes the 4x4 SE(3) matrix from a 6-vector Lie algebra element [G]."""
    omega = xi[:3]
    v = xi[3:]
    theta = np.linalg.norm(omega)
    if theta < 1e-6:
        R = np.eye(3)
        V = np.eye(3)
    else:
        k = omega / theta
        K_skew = np.array([[0, -k[2], k[1]], [k[2], 0, -k[0]], [-k[1], k[0], 0]])
        R = np.eye(3) + np.sin(theta) * K_skew + (1 - np.cos(theta)) * (K_skew @ K_skew)
        V = np.eye(3) + ((1 - np.cos(theta)) / (theta**2)) * K_skew + ((theta - np.sin(theta)) / (theta**3)) * (K_skew @ K_skew)
    t_vec = V @ v
    T = np.eye(4)
    T[:3, :3] = R
    T[:3, 3] = t_vec
    return T

def linear_se3_interpolation(T0, T1, t, tau):
    """Linear interpolation in SE(3) Lie algebra [S1]."""
    delta_T = np.linalg.inv(T0) @ T1
    xi = se3_log(delta_T)
    xi_scaled = xi * (t / tau)
    exp_delta = se3_exp(xi_scaled)
    return T0 @ exp_delta

def bezier_se3_interpolation(control_points, K, t, tau):
    """Bézier interpolation in SE(3) Lie algebra [S1]."""
    u = t / tau
    result = np.eye(4)
    for j in range(K + 1):
        binom = np.math.comb(K, j)
        weight = binom * ((1 - u)**(K - j)) * (u**j)
        xi_j = se3_log(control_points[j])
        xi_scaled = xi_j * weight
        exp_term = se3_exp(xi_scaled)
        result = result @ exp_term
    return result
```

**Parameters**
*   **Order $K$:** The degree of the Bézier curve. The source uses $K=7$ [S1].
*   **Shutter Time $\tau$:** The duration of the exposure [S1].
*   **Time $t$:** The current timestamp within the exposure, $t \in [0, \tau]$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Identity Rotation:** When $\theta \approx 0$, the logarithm and exponential functions must handle the singularity gracefully to avoid division by zero [G].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If control points are identical, the trajectory remains static. If $T_0 = T_1$, linear interpolation yields $T_0$ [S1].
*   **Precision:** The composition of many exponentials in high-order Bézier curves can accumulate numerical error. Guard clauses for small angles are required [G].

## Subtopics

### [Camera Trajectory Representations](18006-camera-trajectory-representations.md)
Defines the mathematical parameterization of camera motion trajectories. Linear interpolation is sufficient for synthetic scenes with short time intervals, while cubic B-splines are required for real captured scenes with longer exposure times and complex motions.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Standard evaluation is $O(1)$ for linear and $O(K)$ for Bézier [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Standard storage is $O(1)$ for linear and $O(K)$ for Bézier control points [G].
*   **Performance:** The method is differentiable, allowing joint optimization of scene and camera parameters using gradient descent (e.g., Adam optimizer) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Ensures valid SE(3) poses; differentiable; handles arbitrary smooth motion [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires initialization of control points (e.g., via COLMAP); higher-order curves increase computational cost [S1].
*   **When to use:** When motion blur needs to be modeled in differentiable rendering pipelines, particularly for deblurring or novel view synthesis from blurred images [S1].

## Background
The method originates from "Deblur-GS: 3D Gaussian Splatting from Camera Motion Blurred Images" [S1]. It builds on the principle that camera motion during shutter time can be parameterized in SE(3) and approximated using interpolation in its Lie algebra [S1]. The approach allows for the optimization of motion blurring by optimizing the control points of the trajectory [S1].
