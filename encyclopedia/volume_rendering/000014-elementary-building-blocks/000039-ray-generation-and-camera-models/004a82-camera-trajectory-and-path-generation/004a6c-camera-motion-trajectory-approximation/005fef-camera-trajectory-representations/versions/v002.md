# Camera Trajectory Representations

Also called: Camera trajectory interpolation, SE(3) trajectory parameterization, Trajectory Representations for Camera Motion

## Overview
This family defines the mathematical parameterization of camera motion trajectories used to approximate camera movement during shutter exposure time for motion blur synthesis [S1]. It solves the problem of how to interpolate between known camera poses to generate intermediate virtual views [S1]. The choice of representation is primarily driven by the complexity of the motion and the duration of the exposure: linear interpolation is sufficient for synthetic scenes with short time intervals, while cubic B-splines are required for real captured scenes with longer exposure times and complex motions [S1]. Builds on [Camera Motion Trajectory Approximation](12740-camera-motion-trajectory-approximation.md).

## Shared principles
All representations in this family operate within the Lie group SE(3) to ensure valid rigid body transformations [G]. The core mechanism involves optimizing a set of parameters to define the path between camera poses [S1]. The trajectory is typically discretized into $n$ intermediate virtual images to balance rendering performance and training efficiency, with $n=10$ being a common empirical choice [S1]. The optimization targets the camera poses themselves, rather than just the trajectory shape, allowing the model to adapt to the specific blur characteristics of the input data [S1].

## Subtopics

### Linear Interpolation
Linear interpolation parameterizes the trajectory by optimizing only the start pose $\mathbf{T}_{start}$ and the end pose $\mathbf{T}_{end}$ [S1]. The intermediate poses are generated by linearly interpolating between these two endpoints [G]. This method is computationally efficient and sufficient for synthetic datasets where camera motions are simpler and exposure times are effectively shorter [S1]. It is employed for synthetic datasets such as those from Deblur-NeRF-S and MBA-VO [S1].

### Cubic B-Spline Interpolation
Cubic B-spline interpolation parameterizes the trajectory by jointly optimizing four control knots: $\mathbf{T}_1, \mathbf{T}_2, \mathbf{T}_3,$ and $\mathbf{T}_4$ [S1]. This higher-order spline allows for the modeling of more complex camera motions and nuances over longer time intervals [S1]. It outperforms linear interpolation in real captured scenes, particularly due to the longer exposure times inherent in real-world image capture [S1]. It is employed for real datasets, such as the 10 real captured scenes from Deblur-NeRF [S1].

### Differentially Flat Trajectory Generation
This variant uses a differentially flat dynamical model of a quadrotor for camera trajectory generation [S2]. The flat outputs consist of 3D Euclidean position and yaw, with pitch treated as an additional independent state, resulting in 5-dimensional waypoints [S2]. Between successive waypoints $\mathbf{x}_t \to \mathbf{x}_{t+\Delta t}$, trajectories are calculated using Dijkstra's algorithm combined with rotorpy to solve a quadratic optimization problem [S2]. The flat outputs are parameterized using a 7th-order polynomial to minimize the integral of the squared snap [S2]. Camera pitch is linearly interpolated along the trajectory since it is independent of the quadrotor dynamics [S2]. An additional $2\pi$ yaw rotation with pitch modulation is performed at the end of the trajectory to provide extra information for NeRF training and target spotting [S2].

## Background
The distinction between linear and higher-order spline representations for camera trajectories in the context of neural rendering and deblurring was formalized in the BAD-Gaussians paper [S1]. The authors conducted experiments on both synthetic datasets (Deblur-NeRF-S, MBA-VO) and real datasets (Deblur-NeRF) to assess the impact of trajectory representations [S1]. They found that while linear interpolation performed comparably to cubic B-splines on synthetic data, the latter was necessary for real data to handle complex motions and longer exposure times effectively [S1]. The specific mathematical details of the cubic B-spline implementation are referenced from external literature [S1].
