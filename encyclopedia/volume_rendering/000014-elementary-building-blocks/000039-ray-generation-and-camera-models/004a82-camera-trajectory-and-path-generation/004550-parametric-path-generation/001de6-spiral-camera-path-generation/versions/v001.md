# Spiral Camera Path Generation

Also called: Novel View Synthesis Path, load_tankstemple_data spiral generation

## Overview
Spiral Camera Path Generation creates a sequence of camera poses along a helical trajectory around a scene centroid, designed for novel view synthesis (NVS) and fly-through rendering [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. Unlike static camera models or simple circular orbits, this method introduces vertical oscillation to cover 3D volume more effectively, making it suitable for forward-facing scenes and 360° video generation [S2, S3, unofficial]. It differs from [Circular Camera Path Generation](2734-circular-camera-path-generation.md) by adding a sinusoidal vertical component to the radius, preventing the camera from staying in a single horizontal plane [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method generates a list of $4 \times 4$ camera-to-world transformation matrices. It relies on an initial set of training poses to estimate the scene's spatial extent, center, and scale.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   `poses`: Array of training camera poses, shape $(N, 4, 4)$, where each pose is a camera-to-world matrix [S2, S5, unofficial].
*   `n_frames` ($N$): Number of output poses to generate [S2, unofficial].
*   `n_rots` ($R$): Number of full rotations around the scene [S2, unofficial].
*   `z_rate` ($Z_r$): Vertical oscillation rate [S2, unofficial].
*   `rads_scale` ($S_r$): Scale factor for the spiral radius [S2, unofficial].
*   `c2w`: Optional specific center pose to use as the origin reference; if not provided, the average of `poses` is computed [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   `up`: Optional up vector; if not provided, the mean of training pose up vectors is used [S2, unofficial].
*   `shift`: Optional $(x, y, z)$ shift applied to the centroid [S5, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `render_poses`: List of $N$ camera-to-world matrices, shape $(N, 4, 4)$ [S1, S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Pose Matrix**: A $4 \times 4$ matrix. The upper-left $3 \times 3$ is the rotation basis $[\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{z}]$ (right, up, forward). The rightmost column is the translation $\mathbf{t}$ (camera center in world space) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Radii Vector**: $\mathbf{rads} = [r_x, r_y, r_z]$. If input has fewer than 3 elements, it is padded with $1.0$ [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps
1.  **Estimate Scene Properties**:
    *   Compute the centroid $\mathbf{c}_{avg}$ as the mean of all training camera positions: $\mathbf{c}_{avg} = \frac{1}{N_{train}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{train}} \mathbf{poses}_i[:3, 3]$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Apply optional shifts: $\mathbf{c}_{avg} \leftarrow \mathbf{c}_{avg} + \mathbf{shift}$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Compute the average up vector $\mathbf{up}_{avg}$ from training poses: $\mathbf{up}_{avg} = \frac{1}{N_{train}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{train}} \mathbf{poses}_i[:3, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute the focal length $f$. A common heuristic is the mean norm of camera positions relative to the centroid, or derived from intrinsics if available [S2, S3, unofficial].
    *   Compute the base radius scale. This is often the 90th percentile of the absolute distances of training cameras from the centroid, scaled by `rads_scale` [S2, unofficial]. Let this base radius be $r_{base}$.

2.  **Define Spiral Parameters**:
    *   Calculate the radii vector $\mathbf{rads}$. In many implementations (e.g., NeRF), $\mathbf{rads}$ is derived from the scene extent. If not explicitly provided, it may be assumed isotropic or derived from the bounding box of training cameras [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   Ensure $\mathbf{rads}$ has 3 components $[r_x, r_y, r_z]$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Generate Poses**:
    *   Initialize empty list `render_poses`.
    *   For $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
        *   Calculate normalized time $t = i / N$.
        *   Calculate angle $\theta = 2 \pi R t$.
        *   Calculate vertical offset $z_{offset} = \sin(2 \pi R t \cdot Z_r)$ [S4, unofficial].
        *   Calculate the offset vector in the local spiral frame:
            $$ \mathbf{c}_{offset} = [r_x \cos(\theta), -r_y \sin(\theta), -r_z \sin(\theta \cdot Z_r)] $$
            *(Note: Signs and axis assignments vary by implementation; S2 uses this specific mapping)* [S2, unofficial].
        *   Determine the camera center $\mathbf{c}$:
            $$ \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{c}_{avg} + \mathbf{c}_{offset} $$
            *(If a specific `c2w` center is used, $\mathbf{c}$ is computed relative to that center's basis)* [S2, unofficial].
        *   Determine the forward vector $\mathbf{z}$. This is typically the normalized vector from the camera center $\mathbf{c}$ towards the scene center (or a focal point). A common definition is:
            $$ \mathbf{z} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{c}_{avg} - \mathbf{c}) $$
            *(Some implementations use a fixed focal point or the average pose's look-at direction)* [S2, S3, unofficial].
        *   Construct the right vector $\mathbf{r}$:
            $$ \mathbf{r} = \text{normalize}(\text{cross}(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{up}_{avg})) $$
        *   Construct the up vector $\mathbf{u}$:
            $$ \mathbf{u} = \text{cross}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{z}) $$
        *   Assemble the pose matrix $P$:
            $$ P = \begin{bmatrix} r_x & r_y & r_z & t_x \\ u_x & u_y & u_z & t_y \\ z_x & z_y & z_z & t_z \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
            where $\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{c}$ [S1, S3, unofficial].
        *   Append $P$ to `render_poses`.

4.  **Return** `render_poses`.

### Pseudocode
```python
import numpy as np

def generate_spiral_path(poses, n_frames=120, n_rots=2, z_rate=0.5, rads_scale=1.0, shift=None):
    """
    Generates a spiral camera path for novel view synthesis.
    
    Args:
        poses: (N, 4, 4) array of training camera-to-world matrices.
        n_frames: Number of poses to generate.
        n_rots: Number of full rotations.
        z_rate: Vertical oscillation rate.
        rads_scale: Scale factor for radius.
        shift: Optional (3,) array to shift the centroid.
        
    Returns:
        render_poses: (n_frames, 4, 4) array of generated poses.
    """
    # 1. Estimate scene properties
    # Centroid: mean of camera positions
    centroids = poses[:, :3, 3]
    centroid = np.mean(centroids, axis=0)
    
    if shift is not None:
        centroid += shift
        
    # Average up vector
    up_vectors = poses[:, :3, 1]
    up = np.mean(up_vectors, axis=0)
    up = up / np.linalg.norm(up)
    
    # Focal length estimation (mean distance from centroid)
    # This is a heuristic; some implementations use intrinsic K if available
    focal = np.mean(np.linalg.norm(centroids - centroid, axis=1))
    
    # Radius estimation: 90th percentile of distances from centroid
    dists = np.linalg.norm(centroids - centroid, axis=1)
    base_radius = np.percentile(dists, 90) * rads_scale
    
    # Radii vector [rx, ry, rz]
    # Often assumed isotropic in simple spirals, but S2 implies distinct scaling
    # If not provided, we use base_radius for all axes
    rads = np.array([base_radius, base_radius, base_radius])
    
    # 2. Generate poses
    render_poses = []
    
    for i in range(n_frames):
        t = i / n_frames
        theta = 2 * np.pi * n_rots * t
        
        # Calculate offset vector
        # S2: c_offset = [cos(theta)*rads[0], -sin(theta)*rads[1], -sin(theta*z_rate)*rads[2]]
        c_offset = np.array([
            np.cos(theta) * rads[0],
            -np.sin(theta) * rads[1],
            -np.sin(theta * z_rate) * rads[2]
        ])
        
        # Camera center
        c = centroid + c_offset
        
        # Forward vector (look at centroid)
        z = centroid - c
        z = z / np.linalg.norm(z)
        
        # Right vector
        r = np.cross(z, up)
        r = r / np.linalg.norm(r)
        
        # Up vector
        u = np.cross(r, z)
        
        # Construct pose matrix
        pose = np.eye(4)
        pose[:3, 0] = r
        pose[:3, 1] = u
        pose[:3, 2] = z
        pose[:3, 3] = c
        
        render_poses.append(pose)
        
    return np.array(render_poses)
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   `n_frames`: 120–200 [S2, S5, unofficial].
*   `n_rots`: 2–4 rotations [S2, unofficial].
*   `z_rate`: 0.5 (vertical oscillation frequency relative to horizontal rotation) [S2, unofficial].
*   `rads_scale`: 1.0 (multiplier for the estimated scene radius) [S2, unofficial].
*   `shift`: $(0, 0, 0)$ by default [S5, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degenerate Up Vector**: If the training cameras are all aligned such that their up vectors are parallel or zero, the cross product for $\mathbf{r}$ may fail. A default up vector (e.g., $[0, 1, 0]$) should be used as a fallback [S3, unofficial].
*   **Precision**: Using single-precision floats for large coordinate systems can cause jitter in the camera path. Double precision is recommended for path generation if the scene coordinates are large [G].
*   **Boundary Conditions**: The spiral should not start or end at a discontinuity. Using `linspace` excluding the last element ensures the path does not repeat the start pose exactly, avoiding a visible jump if looped [S1, S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Circular Camera Path Generation](2734-circular-camera-path-generation.md)
Circular Camera Path Generation creates a sequence of camera poses arranged in a 360-degree circular trajectory around a central origin at a fixed height and radius. It solves the problem of generating consistent, orbiting viewpoints for novel view synthesis or 360-degree visualization. It is a degenerate case of Spiral Camera Path Generation where the vertical offset rate is zero, resulting in a planar orbit rather than a helical one. Choose this when the scene is flat or when vertical parallax is not required.

### Spiral Camera Pose Generation
This variant (often found in PyTorch-based NVS pipelines like [S6, unofficial]) generates poses using a circular path with optional pitch rotation. It computes the eye position based on a radius and angle, then constructs a `PerspectiveCameras` object using a `look_at_view_transform` function. It differs from the standard NeRF spiral by explicitly handling the camera's intrinsic parameters (focal length, principal point) within the pose generation step and supporting an optional pitch angle to tilt the camera up or down [S6, unofficial]. Choose this when integrating with differentiable renderers that require explicit camera intrinsics in the pose object.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of frames. The computation involves basic trigonometric functions and vector operations per frame.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(N)$ to store the resulting pose matrices.
*   **Performance**: Generation is instantaneous for typical $N < 1000$. The cost is negligible compared to the rendering cost.
*   **Strengths**: Provides smooth, continuous motion; covers 3D volume better than circular orbits; easy to implement.
*   **Limitations**: The path is fixed and does not adapt to scene content; may pass through empty space or occluded regions if the scene is not convex or well-distributed [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use**: Novel view synthesis for forward-facing scenes (e.g., LLFF, Tanks and Temples) where a smooth fly-through is desired [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When not to use**: Scenes with significant vertical structure where a fixed spiral might miss details; interactive applications requiring user-defined paths.

## Background
Spiral camera paths were popularized by the NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) paper and its accompanying codebase for rendering novel views of forward-facing scenes [S1, S3, unofficial]. They are particularly effective for datasets like LLFF and Tanks & Temples, where the camera distribution is roughly circular or spiral-like [S2, unofficial]. The method has become a standard baseline for evaluating novel view synthesis algorithms [S2, S3, unofficial].
