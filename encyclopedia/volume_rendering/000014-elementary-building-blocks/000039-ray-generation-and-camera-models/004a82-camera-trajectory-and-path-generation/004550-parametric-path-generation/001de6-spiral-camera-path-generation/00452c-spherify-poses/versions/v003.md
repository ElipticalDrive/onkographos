# Spherify Poses

Also called: Pose Spherification, Spherical Path Centering, Spherical Pose Reorganization

## Overview
Spherify Poses reorients a set of input camera poses into a uniform spherical arrangement centered around the scene's centroid, ensuring consistent scale and distribution for novel view synthesis [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [Circular Camera Path Generation](2734-circular-camera-path-generation.md) by computing a 3D center from ray intersections rather than assuming a 2D circular path, and from [Spiral Camera Path Generation](1635-spiral-camera-path-generation.md) by enforcing a strict spherical radius rather than a spiral trajectory with vertical offset [G].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `poses`: Array of shape $[N_{\text{orig}}, 4, 4]$ representing input camera poses in world coordinates, where `poses[:,:3,2:3]` stores ray directions and `poses[:,:3,3]` stores camera positions [S1, unofficial].
- `bds`: Bounding box data (optional, used for scaling reference) [S1, unofficial].
- `N`: Integer, the number of new poses to generate [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `poses_spherical`: Array of shape $[N, 4, 4]$ containing the new camera poses arranged on a sphere [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- `rays_d`: Array of shape $[N_{\text{orig}}, 3, 1]$ containing unit ray directions extracted from poses [S1, unofficial].
- `rays_o`: Array of shape $[N_{\text{orig}}, 3, 1]$ containing ray origins (camera positions) extracted from poses [S1, unofficial].
- `A_i`: Matrix of shape $[3, 3]$ for each ray $i$, defined as $I - \mathbf{d}_i \mathbf{d}_i^T$ [S1, unofficial].
- `b_i`: Vector of shape $[3, 1]$ for each ray $i$, defined as $-A_i \mathbf{o}_i$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Extract Rays:**
    Extract ray directions $\mathbf{d}_i$ and origins $\mathbf{o}_i$ from the input `poses` matrix [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{d}_i = \text{poses}[i, :3, 2:3] $$
    $$ \mathbf{o}_i = \text{poses}[i, :3, 3:4] $$
    [S1, unofficial]

2.  **Compute Sphere Center:**
    Calculate the point $\mathbf{p}_{\text{mindist}}$ that minimizes the distance to all camera rays by solving a least-squares problem [S1, unofficial].
    $$ A_i = I - \mathbf{d}_i \mathbf{d}_i^T $$
    $$ b_i = -A_i \mathbf{o}_i $$
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{\text{mindist}} = -\left( \frac{1}{N_{\text{orig}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{orig}}} A_i^T A_i \right)^{-1} \left( \frac{1}{N_{\text{orig}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{orig}}} b_i \right) $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Set $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{mindist}}$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Up Vector:**
    Calculate the mean vector from the center to the camera positions to define the initial "up" direction [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{orig}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{orig}}} (\text{poses}[i, :3, 3] - \mathbf{c}) $$
    [S1, unofficial]

4.  **Construct Local Coordinate System:**
    Normalize $\mathbf{u}$ to get $\mathbf{vec}_0$ [S1, unofficial]. Compute $\mathbf{vec}_1$ by crossing $\mathbf{vec}_0$ with a fixed arbitrary vector $\mathbf{v}_{\text{fix}} = [0.1, 0.2, 0.3]^T$ to avoid degeneracy if $\mathbf{u}$ is parallel to the world up [S1, unofficial]. Compute $\mathbf{vec}_2$ by crossing $\mathbf{vec}_0$ and $\mathbf{vec}_1$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{vec}_0 = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|} $$
    $$ \mathbf{vec}_1 = \frac{\mathbf{v}_{\text{fix}} \times \mathbf{vec}_0}{\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{fix}} \times \mathbf{vec}_0\|} $$
    $$ \mathbf{vec}_2 = \frac{\mathbf{vec}_0 \times \mathbf{vec}_1}{\|\mathbf{vec}_0 \times \mathbf{vec}_1\|} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

5.  **Reset Poses:**
    Construct a transformation matrix $T$ using the basis vectors and the center [S1, unofficial].
    $$ T = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{vec}_1 & \mathbf{vec}_2 & \mathbf{vec}_0 & \mathbf{c} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Apply this transformation to all input poses to get `poses_reset` [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Compute Radius and Scale:**
    Calculate the radius $r$ of the centered poses [S1, unofficial].
    $$ r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_{\text{orig}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{orig}}} \|\text{poses\_reset}[i, :3, 3]\|^2} $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Compute scale factor $s = 1/r$. Apply this scale to `poses_reset` and `bds` [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Compute Spherical Parameters:**
    Calculate the centroid of the scaled poses [S1, unofficial]. Extract the z-coordinate of the centroid, $z_h$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ z_h = \text{centroid}[2] $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Compute the radius of the circle in the xy-plane, $r_{\text{circle}}$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ r_{\text{circle}} = \sqrt{r^2 - z_h^2} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

8.  **Generate New Poses:**
    Iterate $\theta$ from $0$ to $2\pi$ with $N$ steps [S1, unofficial]. For each $\theta$:
    $$ \mathbf{camorigin} = [r_{\text{circle}} \cos(\theta), r_{\text{circle}} \sin(\theta), z_h] $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Construct a new local basis for the camera orientation:
    $$ \mathbf{vec}_2' = \frac{\mathbf{camorigin}}{\|\mathbf{camorigin}\|} $$
    $$ \mathbf{vec}_0' = \frac{\mathbf{vec}_2' \times [0, 0, -1]^T}{\|\mathbf{vec}_2' \times [0, 0, -1]^T\|} $$
    $$ \mathbf{vec}_1' = \frac{\mathbf{vec}_2' \times \mathbf{vec}_0'}{\|\mathbf{vec}_2' \times \mathbf{vec}_0'\|} $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Set the pose matrix $P$ with columns $\mathbf{vec}_0', \mathbf{vec}_1', \mathbf{vec}_2'$ and translation $\mathbf{camorigin}$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ P = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{vec}_0' & \mathbf{vec}_1' & \mathbf{vec}_2' & \mathbf{camorigin} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

9.  **Append Homogeneous Row:**
    Append the original pose's w-row (typically `poses[0,:3,-1:]`) to the new poses to maintain homogeneous coordinate structure [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def spherify_poses(poses, N):
    """
    Reorients camera poses to a spherical arrangement.
    
    Args:
        poses: np.array of shape (N_orig, 4, 4)
        N: int, number of new poses to generate
        
    Returns:
        poses_spherical: np.array of shape (N, 4, 4)
    """
    # 1. Extract rays
    rays_d = poses[:, :3, 2:3]  # Shape (N_orig, 3, 1)
    rays_o = poses[:, :3, 3:4]  # Shape (N_orig, 3, 1)
    
    # 2. Compute sphere center
    I = np.eye(3)
    # A_i = I - d_i d_i^T
    A_i = I - (rays_d @ rays_d.transpose(0, 2, 1))
    # b_i = -A_i o_i
    b_i = -A_i @ rays_o
    
    # pt_mindist = -inv(mean(A_i^T @ A_i)) @ mean(b_i)
    mean_A = np.mean(A_i.transpose(0, 2, 1) @ A_i, axis=0)
    mean_b = np.mean(b_i, axis=0)
    pt_mindist = -np.linalg.inv(mean_A) @ mean_b.flatten()
    center = pt_mindist
    
    # 3. Compute up vector
    cam_positions = poses[:, :3, 3]
    up = np.mean(cam_positions - center, axis=0)
    
    # 4. Construct local coordinate system
    vec0 = up / np.linalg.norm(up)
    v_fix = np.array([0.1, 0.2, 0.3])
    vec1 = np.cross(v_fix, vec0)
    vec1 = vec1 / np.linalg.norm(vec1)
    vec2 = np.cross(vec0, vec1)
    vec2 = vec2 / np.linalg.norm(vec2)
    
    # 5. Reset poses
    # c2w = [vec1, vec2, vec0, pos=center]
    R = np.column_stack((vec1, vec2, vec0))
    T = np.eye(4)
    T[:3, :3] = R
    T[:3, 3] = center
    
    # Apply transformation to all input poses
    poses_reset = np.zeros_like(poses)
    for i in range(poses.shape[0]):
        poses_reset[i] = T @ poses[i]
        
    # 6. Compute radius and scale
    positions = poses_reset[:, :3, 3]
    rad = np.sqrt(np.mean(np.sum(positions**2, axis=-1)))
    sc = 1.0 / rad
    poses_reset[:, :3, 3] *= sc  # Apply scale to poses_reset
    
    # 7. Compute spherical parameters
    centroid = np.mean(poses_reset[:, :3, 3], axis=0)
    zh = centroid[2]
    radcircle = np.sqrt(rad**2 - zh**2)
    
    # 8. Generate new poses
    thetas = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, N, endpoint=False)
    poses_spherical = np.zeros((N, 4, 4))
    
    for i, th in enumerate(thetas):
        camorigin = np.array([radcircle * np.cos(th), radcircle * np.sin(th), zh])
        
        vec2_prime = camorigin / np.linalg.norm(camorigin)
        vec0_prime = np.cross(vec2_prime, np.array([0, 0, -1]))
        vec0_prime = vec0_prime / np.linalg.norm(vec0_prime)
        vec1_prime = np.cross(vec2_prime, vec0_prime)
        vec1_prime = vec1_prime / np.linalg.norm(vec1_prime)
        
        poses_spherical[i, :3, 0] = vec0_prime
        poses_spherical[i, :3, 1] = vec1_prime
        poses_spherical[i, :3, 2] = vec2_prime
        poses_spherical[i, :3, 3] = camorigin
        poses_spherical[i, 3, :] = poses[0, 3, :]  # Append original w-row
        
    return poses_spherical
```

**Parameters:**
- `N`: Number of poses to generate [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Degenerate Up Vector:** If the mean up vector is zero (cameras are symmetric around the center), normalization fails [G]. Guard against this by checking `np.linalg.norm(up) < epsilon`.
- **Parallel Ray Directions:** If all rays are parallel, $A_i$ becomes singular, and the least-squares solution for the center is unstable [G]. Check for collinearity of ray directions.
- **Complexity of Cross Product:** If $\mathbf{vec}_0$ is parallel to $\mathbf{v}_{\text{fix}}$, the cross product for $\mathbf{vec}_1$ is zero [G]. The fixed vector $[0.1, 0.2, 0.3]$ is chosen to be unlikely to be parallel to typical scene up vectors, but a fallback is recommended [G].
- **Radius Calculation:** The formula for $r_{\text{circle}}$ assumes $r \ge |z_h|$ [G]. If the scene is not roughly spherical or the centering is poor, this may result in a NaN [G]. Check for $r^2 < z_h^2$.

## Subtopics

### [Circular Camera Path Generation](2734-circular-camera-path-generation.md)
Generates poses on a 2D circle. Choose when the scene is planar or when a simple orbit is sufficient. Spherify is chosen for 3D volumetric scenes requiring full spherical coverage.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ for extracting rays and computing the center. $O(N)$ for generating new poses [S1, unofficial]. Overall linear in the number of input poses and output poses [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store rays, intermediate matrices, and output poses [G].
- **Strengths:** Provides a robust, data-driven center for spherical sampling, which is crucial for NeRF training on unstructured data. Ensures uniform distribution of views [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Assumes the scene is roughly spherical. If the scene is elongated or has a specific orientation, the spherical path may not cover all parts of the scene uniformly [G]. The fixed vector for cross product is arbitrary [G].
- **When to Use:** When preparing unstructured image collections for NeRF training where a spherical camera path is desired [S1, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** When the scene is known to be planar or when a specific trajectory (e.g., spiral, linear) is required for artistic or physical reasons [G].

## Background
The method originates from the NerfingMVS project [S1, unofficial]. It is used in the context of novel view synthesis, specifically for reorganizing camera poses from arbitrary viewpoints into a structured spherical path to facilitate training and rendering [S1, unofficial].
