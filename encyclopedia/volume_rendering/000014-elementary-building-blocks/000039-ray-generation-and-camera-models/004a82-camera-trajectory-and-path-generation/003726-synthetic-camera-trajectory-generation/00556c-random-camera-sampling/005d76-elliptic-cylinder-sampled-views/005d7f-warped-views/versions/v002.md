# Warped Views

## Overview
Warped Views is a view sampling strategy that generates novel viewpoints by rotating training cameras around an estimated center axis to provide large view overlapping, unlike SDS sampled views [S1]. It is primarily used in conjunction with Depth Warping Loss to enforce geometric consistency between slightly different perspectives of the same scene [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of training camera poses, where each pose consists of a position and a rotation matrix [S1].
**Outputs:** A set of warped camera poses [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- Camera Position $P$: A 3D vector representing the camera center [S1].
- Rotation Matrix $R$: A $3 \times 3$ matrix [S1]. The up-vector is extracted from its first column: $Y = [R_{11}, R_{21}, R_{31}]^T$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1. **Extract Up-Vectors:** For each training camera $i$, extract the up-vector $Y_i$ from the first column of its rotation matrix $R_i$ [S1].
2. **Compute Average Up-Vector:** Calculate the mean of all $N$ up-vectors and normalize it to obtain the estimated center axis $\bar{Y}$ [S1].
3. **Define Rotation Angles:** Select a set of small angles $\theta$ [S1].
4. **Rotate Camera Poses:** For each training camera position $P$ and each angle $\theta$, compute the new position $P'$ using Rodrigues' rotation formula around the axis $\bar{Y}$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
$$ \bar{Y} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} Y_i $$
$$ \bar{Y} \leftarrow \frac{\bar{Y}}{\|\bar{Y}\|} $$
$$ P' = P \cos \theta + (P \times \bar{Y}) \sin \theta + \bar{Y} (\bar{Y} \cdot P) (1 - \cos \theta) $$
where $P$ is the original camera position vector, $\bar{Y}$ is the normalized average up-vector serving as the rotation axis, $\theta$ is the rotation angle, and $P'$ is the warped camera position [S1].

**Parameters:**
- Rotation angles $\theta$: $-3^\circ, -1.5^\circ, 1.5^\circ, 3^\circ$ [S1].
- Number of warped viewpoints per training camera: 4 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Degenerate Axis:** If the average up-vector $\bar{Y}$ has a norm close to zero, the rotation axis is ill-defined, which may occur if training cameras are distributed symmetrically around the vertical axis [G].
- **Units:** Angles must be converted to radians before evaluating trigonometric functions in the rotation formula [G].

## Subtopics

### Extrapolated View Generation
Extrapolated View Generation synthesizes novel rasterized views by perturbing the original camera center to maintain scene overlap while providing a novel perspective [S2]. It perturbs the camera center by $\pm 0.25$ units along the x and y axes and translates it backward by $0.5$ units along the z-axis [S2]. This backward-only perturbation ensures high visibility overlap with the original training view, keeping most scene content within shared frustums for reliable correspondence estimation [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Strengths:** Provides large view overlapping compared to SDS sampled views, which is crucial for depth warping and consistency losses [S1].
- **Usage:** Applied to sparse input views in conjunction with Depth Warping Loss [S1].
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Limitations:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
Warped Views were introduced in SparseGS (Sparse View Synthesis Using 3D Gaussian Splatting) to address the lack of view overlap when using standard SDS sampling for depth consistency losses [S1]. The method leverages Rodrigues' rotation formula to create small perturbations around the scene center, estimated from the average up-vector of the training cameras [S1].
