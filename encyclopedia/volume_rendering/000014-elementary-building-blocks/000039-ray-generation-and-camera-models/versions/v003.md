# Ray generation and camera models
## Overview
Ray generation and camera models define the mapping from 2D image coordinates to 3D rays, establishing the geometric foundation for volume rendering by determining which parts of the scene are observed [S2]. This family solves the problem of translating pixel locations into valid ray origins and directions for subsequent intersection and integration steps [S3, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family generate a set of rays, typically represented as a `RayBundle` containing origins $\mathbf{o}$ and directions $\mathbf{d}$, for every pixel in the output image [S2]. The core mechanism involves:
1. **Coordinate Mapping**: Converting discrete pixel indices $(u, v)$ or normalized device coordinates (NDC) into continuous 2D positions on a virtual image plane [S2].
2. **Camera Basis Construction**: Defining a local coordinate system for the camera using an eye position $\mathbf{e}$, a target point $\mathbf{t}$, and an up vector $\mathbf{up}$. This yields the basis vectors: forward $\mathbf{f}$, right $\mathbf{r}$, and camera-up $\mathbf{u}$ [S3, unofficial].
3. **Ray Assembly**: Computing the origin $\mathbf{o}$ (often the eye position or a point on the image plane) and the direction $\mathbf{d}$ (normalized vector from origin to image plane point) [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NDC Ray Transformation](905-ndc-ray-transformation.md)
Maps camera-space rays into NDC space, converting perspective rays into parallel rays and using disparity as the sampling parameter for linear sampling from near plane to infinity.

### [Stochastic Jittering](924-stochastic-jittering.md)
Reduces banding artifacts in volume rendering by adding stochastic jittering controlled by a noise texture and maximum noise factor.

### [Beam Optimization](932-beam-optimization.md)
Improves primary ray performance by grouping rays into beams.

### [Orthographic (Parallel) Camera Ray Generation](1319-orthographic-parallel-camera-ray-generation.md)
Generates ray origins distributed on a grid perpendicular to the viewing direction and identical normalized directions for an orthographic camera.

### [Sample Streaming Payload Ray Computation](1509-sample-streaming-payload-ray-computation.md)
Regenerates ray geometry from discrete pixel indices and stored camera parameters for streaming or deferred rendering pipelines, handling payload context such as shadow vs. main view.

### [Spiral Camera Path Generation](1635-spiral-camera-path-generation.md)
Generates camera poses along a spiral trajectory for novel view synthesis using center, radius, focal length, vertical offset rate, rotation count, and number of views.

### [Arcball Camera](1639-arcball-camera.md)
Maps 2D screen coordinates to a 3D arcball sphere to compute rotation quaternions for smooth orbiting around a center point.

### [View Matrix Construction](1644-view-matrix-construction.md)
Constructs a 4x4 camera-to-world transformation matrix from a viewing direction, up vector, and position.

### [Camera Pose Transformation](1672-camera-pose-transformation.md)
Not given in the sources.

### [Custom Frustum Ray Generation via Vertex Z-Index](1753-custom-frustum-ray-generation-via-vertex-z-index.md)
Generates per-pixel ray directions for raymarching by encoding pre-computed frustum corner vectors into the Z-coordinate of a full-screen quad's vertices, allowing the vertex shader to look up the correct ray direction from a uniform array.

### Multi-Sample Anti-Aliasing via Ray Distribution
An anti-aliasing technique that casts multiple rays per pixel, distributed within the pixel area, and averages the results to reduce jagged edges [S1, unofficial]. Distribute multiple ray origins/directions within a single pixel, sum the color results of all rays, and calculate the average color. This technique significantly reduces framerate due to increased ray count. Optimization: Only perform AA if the iteration count is above average, as this likely indicates an object edge [S1, unofficial].

### Sky Gradient
Interpolates between a horizon color and a zenith color based on the ray's vertical component to simulate a sky dome [S6, unofficial]. Normalize the view direction $\mathbf{d}$, compute interpolation factor $t = \text{smoothstep}(0.0, 0.7, \mathbf{d}.y)$, and return $\text{mix}(\text{horizon}, \text{zenith}, t)$. Parameters: $\text{horizon} = (0.80, 0.90, 1.0)$ (RGB color at the horizon, $\mathbf{d}.y \approx 0$), $\text{zenith} = (0.35, 0.60, 0.95)$ (RGB color at the zenith, $\mathbf{d}.y \approx 1$), and $\text{smoothstep}$ bounds $(0.0, 0.7)$ (transition range for $\mathbf{d}.y$) [S6, unofficial].

### OptiX Ray Generation & Traversal Setup
The ray generation program that computes pixel rays, initializes the RNG, and dispatches volume tracing via OptiX `optixTrace` [S7, unofficial]. Computes pixel index, initializes RNG state, computes normalized screen position, generates ray origin and direction, packs payload for OptiX trace, and traces volume rays with specific visibility masks and SBT offsets [S7, unofficial]. Parameters: `VISIBILITY_MASK_VOLUME` (bitmask to restrict traversal to volume geometry), `MethodOptiX::RADIANCE_RAY_TYPE` (SBT offset and miss index for radiance shading), `MethodOptiX::RAY_TYPE_COUNT` (stride between SBT entries) [S7, unofficial].

### Object Space Ray Setup
A method for constructing ray origin and direction in object/local space rather than world space, making the raytraced object move, scale, and rotate with the mesh transform [S8, unofficial]. In the vertex shader, compute local position and transform camera world position to object space (w-component must be 1 to include translation). Compute view direction vector. In the fragment shader, normalize the view direction. Normalization must happen in the fragment shader, not vertex shader, because interpolation between vertices distorts the vector length, especially near low-poly objects or close camera distances [S8, unofficial]. Layout: Vertex Shader Output Struct `v2f` contains `position` (SV_POSITION, clip space), `localPosition` (TEXCOORD0, float4, local space origin), `viewDirection` (TEXCOORD1, float4, unnormalized local space view vector) [S8, unofficial].

### Average Pose Calculation
Computes an average camera pose from a set of poses by averaging origins, summing viewing directions and up vectors [S9, unofficial]. Extract intrinsic scaling from the first pose, compute center as mean of pose origins, compute viewing direction as normalized sum of pose viewing directions, compute up as sum of pose up vectors, and construct c2w matrix using viewmatrix and concatenate intrinsic scaling [S9, unofficial].

### Point to Camera Transformation
Transforms 3D points from world coordinates to camera coordinates given a c2w matrix [S9, unofficial]. Computes the difference between points and camera origin, then multiplies by the transpose of the rotation part of c2w [S9, unofficial].

### Uniform Sphere Sampling
Generates pseudo-random unit vectors distributed over a sphere using a hash-based random function and spherical coordinate conversion [S10, unofficial]. Compute $\theta = 2\pi \times \text{rand}$, $\phi = \arccos(1 - 2 \times \text{rand})$, convert to Cartesian coordinates ($x = \cos(\theta)\sin(\phi)$, $y = \sin(\theta)\sin(\phi)$, $z = \cos(\phi)$), and return the vector [S10, unofficial].

## Background
The pinhole camera model, which simulates depth by positioning the eye behind the image plane at a distance $f$ (focal length), is the standard reference for perspective ray generation [S1, unofficial]. This approach creates an illusion of depth by casting rays through the image plane, where the ray direction is the normalized difference between the image plane point and the eye position [S1, unofficial].
In parallel beam projections, rays are generated with identical directions, often calculated using projection angles and detector offsets [S5, unofficial].
Given a bounding box, direction, and distance, the system computes the eye position, target, and field of view (FOV) to generate rays for both perspective and parallel projections [S3, unofficial]. The FOV is typically defined vertically in degrees, with defaults around 60 degrees [S3, unofficial].
A common pitfall in camera basis construction is the singularity when the forward vector is parallel to the up vector, causing the cross product for the 'right' vector to become zero. This is handled by switching to an alternative up vector (e.g., $[1, 0, 0]$) and recomputing the basis [S3, unofficial].
