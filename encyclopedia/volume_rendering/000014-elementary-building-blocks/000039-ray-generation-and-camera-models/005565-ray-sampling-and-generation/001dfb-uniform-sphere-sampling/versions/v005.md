# Uniform Sphere Sampling

Also called: UniformSphereSampler, Uniform Solid Angle Sampling, sampleUniformSphere, SphereSampler, getSampleUniformSolidAngle

## Overview
Uniform Sphere Sampling generates a random direction vector uniformly distributed over the surface of a unit sphere [S2, S4, unofficial]. It solves the problem of casting rays or sampling scattering directions in volume rendering where no preferred orientation exists, ensuring that every point on the sphere has an equal probability of being selected [S2, S4, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Random Hemisphere Sampling](2034-random-hemisphere-sampling.md) by covering the full $4\pi$ steradians rather than restricting samples to a half-space, and from [Rejection Sampling for Uniform Sphere Sampling](3286-rejection-sampling-for-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by using direct spherical coordinate transformation which avoids the computational cost and variance of rejection loops [G].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Two independent uniform random numbers $u, v \in [0, 1)$, typically derived from a pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) seeded by pixel coordinates or a global seed [S1, S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A 3D unit vector $\mathbf{d} = (x, y, z)$ representing the sampled direction [S2, S4, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
The method operates on scalar random values and produces a 3D vector [S2, S4, unofficial].

**Steps**
1.  **Generate Random Inputs:** Obtain two independent uniform random numbers $u$ and $v$ in the range $[0, 1)$ [S1, S2, S4, unofficial].
    *   In shader code, this is often done using a hash function on the fragment/pixel coordinate or a seed vector [S1, S4, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Azimuthal Angle ($\theta$):** Calculate the angle in the $xy$-plane [S2, S4, unofficial].
    $$ \theta = 2\pi u $$
3.  **Compute Polar Angle Cosine ($\cos \phi$):** Calculate the cosine of the polar angle [S2, S4, unofficial].
    $$ \cos \phi = 2v - 1 $$
    *   This maps the uniform input $v \in [0, 1)$ to $\cos \phi \in [-1, 1]$, ensuring uniform distribution over the sphere's surface area [S2, S4, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Polar Angle Sine ($\sin \phi$):** Calculate the sine of the polar angle [S2, S4, unofficial].
    $$ \sin \phi = \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 \phi} $$
5.  **Convert to Cartesian Coordinates:** Compute the components of the direction vector $\mathbf{d} = (x, y, z)$ [S2, S4, unofficial].
    $$ x = \sin \phi \cos \theta $$
    $$ y = \sin \phi \sin \theta $$
    $$ z = \cos \phi $$
6.  **Return Vector:** Return the normalized vector $(x, y, z)$ [S2, S4, unofficial].

**Formulas**
The complete transformation from random inputs $(u, v)$ to direction $\mathbf{d}$ is:
$$ \mathbf{d} = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{1 - (2v - 1)^2} \cos(2\pi u) \\ \sqrt{1 - (2v - 1)^2} \sin(2\pi u) \\ 2v - 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
where $u, v \sim \text{Uniform}[0, 1)$ [S2, S4, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   **Random Source:** The quality of the PRNG affects the uniformity [G].
*   **Precision:** Standard `float` (32-bit) is typically sufficient [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Poles:** At the poles ($\cos \phi = \pm 1$), $\sin \phi = 0$, so $x$ and $y$ become 0 [G].
*   **Random Number Correlation:** Using correlated random numbers for $u$ and $v$ will result in non-uniform sampling patterns (e.g., stripes or clumps) [G].
*   **Normalization:** The method described above produces a unit vector by construction [G].
*   **Volume Sampling:** If the goal is to sample points *within* a sphere (not just on the surface), the radius $r$ must be sampled as $r = \sqrt[3]{w}$ where $w \sim \text{Uniform}[0, 1)$ to account for the volume element $r^2 dr$ [S5, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
import math

def uniform_sphere_sample(u: float, v: float) -> tuple:
    """
    Generates a uniformly distributed random direction on a unit sphere.
    
    Args:
        u: Uniform random number in [0, 1) for azimuthal angle.
        v: Uniform random number in [0, 1) for polar angle cosine.
        
    Returns:
        (x, y, z): Cartesian coordinates of the unit vector.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute azimuthal angle theta
    theta = 2.0 * math.pi * u
    
    # Step 2: Compute cosine of polar angle phi
    cos_phi = 2.0 * v - 1.0
    
    # Step 3: Compute sine of polar angle phi
    sin_phi = math.sqrt(1.0 - cos_phi * cos_phi)
    
    # Step 4: Convert to Cartesian coordinates
    x = sin_phi * math.cos(theta)
    y = sin_phi * math.sin(theta)
    z = cos_phi
    
    return (x, y, z)
```

## Subtopics

### [Random Hemisphere Sampling](2034-random-hemisphere-sampling.md)
Generates directions uniformly over a hemisphere. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by restricting the domain to a hemisphere, and from [Cosine-Weighted Hemisphere Sampling](2283-cosine-weighted-hemisphere-sampling.md) by maintaining a uniform probability density function. Choose it when sampling scattering from a surface where only one side is visible.

### [Importance Phong Sampling](2224-importance-phong-sampling.md)
Samples directions biased towards a specular lobe. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by using a non-uniform distribution defined by a shininess exponent, and from [Anisotropic Scattering via Importance Sampling](4770-anisotropic-scattering-via-importance-sampling.md) by assuming isotropic lobes around the reflection vector. Choose it for glossy materials to reduce variance.

### [Rejection Sampling for Uniform Sphere Sampling](3286-rejection-sampling-for-uniform-sphere-sampling.md)
Samples by generating points in a cube and rejecting those outside the sphere. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by avoiding trigonometric functions, relying instead on simple arithmetic and normalization. Choose it when trigonometric functions are expensive or unavailable.

### [Spherical Background Sampling](3991-spherical-background-sampling.md)
Maps a direction to environment map coordinates. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by converting a 3D ray direction into 2D UV coordinates for environment maps rather than generating random directions. Choose it for skybox or environment lighting.

### [Anisotropic Scattering via Importance Sampling](4770-anisotropic-scattering-via-importance-sampling.md)
Samples according to a phase function. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by replacing the uniform distribution with one weighted by the phase function to avoid artifacts in anisotropic media. Choose it for anisotropic media like clouds or skin.

### [Spherical Coordinate Test Data Generation](12368-spherical-coordinate-test-data-generation.md)
Generates deterministic spherical shells for validation. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by providing deterministic, structured geometric primitives rather than stochastic samples. Choose it for testing coordinate transformations.

### Random Camera Batch Generation
Generates a batch of random camera positions on a sphere for rendering [S6, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by sampling spherical coordinates (azimuth: $0$ to $2\pi$, elevation: $-\pi/2$ to $\pi/2$) for a given `batch_size`, converting them to Cartesian coordinates at a specified `cam_radius`, and adding a random offset to the camera positions [S6, unofficial]. Choose it when generating multiple camera viewpoints for batch rendering.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample [S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only storage for the input random numbers and output vector [S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement, unbiased, covers the full sphere [S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires trigonometric functions, which can be slower than simple arithmetic on some hardware [G].
*   **When to Use:** When no prior information about the scene or material is available, or when sampling background radiation [S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When sampling glossy surfaces (use [Importance Phong Sampling](2224-importance-phong-sampling.md)) or anisotropic media (use [Anisotropic Scattering via Importance Sampling](4770-anisotropic-scattering-via-importance-sampling.md)) [G].

## Background
Uniform Sphere Sampling is a fundamental technique in computer graphics and volume rendering, originating from the need to sample directions uniformly for Monte Carlo integration [S2, S4, unofficial]. The method of using spherical coordinates with $\cos \phi$ uniformly distributed is a standard result in probability theory for generating points on a sphere [S2, S4, unofficial]. It is widely used in global illumination, sky lighting, and volume rendering for scattering [S1, S2, S4, S5, unofficial].
