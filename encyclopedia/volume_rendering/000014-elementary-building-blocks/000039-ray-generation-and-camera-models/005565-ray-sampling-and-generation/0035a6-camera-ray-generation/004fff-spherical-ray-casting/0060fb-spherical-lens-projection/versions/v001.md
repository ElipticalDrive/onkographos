# Spherical Lens Projection

Also called: Cylindrical-Spherical Projection, SphericalLens

## Overview
Spherical Lens Projection generates a cylindrical-spherical projection of a volume dataset, mapping 3D spatial data onto a 2D flat image plane suitable for equirectangular display. It solves the problem of visualizing omnidirectional or spherical data (such as 360-degree videos) by projecting rays from a central viewpoint through a spherical lens model onto a rectangular pixel grid. Unlike standard perspective cameras, this method wraps the horizontal angle around the full 360 degrees and the vertical angle across the full 180 degrees, creating an equirectangular map. It is distinct from its siblings, [Spherical Coordinate GPU Ray Casting](19744-spherical-coordinate-gpu-ray-casting-with-3d-textures.md) which focuses on texture sampling efficiency in spherical coordinates, and [Spherical Light Grid for Shadows](19872-spherical-light-grid-for-shadows.md) which focuses on shadow computation rather than image projection.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Volume dataset (scalar or vector field).
- Camera position (center of the sphere).
- Image resolution: width $W$ (pixels) and height $H$ (pixels).
- Field of view parameters: typically full spherical coverage ($\theta_{min} = -\pi/2$, $\theta_{max} = \pi/2$, $\phi_{min} = -\pi$, $\phi_{max} = \pi$).

**Outputs:**
- A 2D image buffer of size $W \times H$.

**Data Structures:**
- Ray direction vector $\mathbf{d} = (d_x, d_y, d_z)$, normalized.
- Pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ where $u \in [0, W-1]$ and $v \in [0, H-1]$.

**Steps:**
1. For each pixel $(u, v)$ in the output image:
2. Calculate the horizontal angle $\phi$ (azimuth) and vertical angle $\theta$ (elevation) corresponding to the pixel's position.
   - $\phi = \frac{u}{W} \cdot 2\pi - \pi$
   - $\theta = \frac{v}{H} \cdot \pi - \frac{\pi}{2}$
3. Convert spherical angles to Cartesian ray direction $\mathbf{d}$:
   - $d_x = \cos(\theta) \cos(\phi)$
   - $d_y = \cos(\theta) \sin(\phi)$
   - $d_z = \sin(\theta)$
   [G] Ensure the direction vector is normalized. Since $\cos^2(\theta)\cos^2(\phi) + \cos^2(\theta)\sin^2(\phi) + \sin^2(\theta) = \cos^2(\theta) + \sin^2(\theta) = 1$, the vector is inherently normalized.
4. Cast a ray from the camera center $C$ along direction $\mathbf{d}$.
5. Perform volume rendering integration along the ray (e.g., using ray marching) to compute the color $C_{pixel}$ for this pixel.
6. Store $C_{pixel}$ in the image buffer at $(u, v)$.

**Parameters:**
- Resolution $W, H$: Determines the granularity of the spherical sampling.
- Field of View: Fixed to full sphere for standard 360-degree video output.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Pole Singularities:** At $\theta = \pm \pi/2$, the horizontal resolution effectively compresses to a point. Standard equirectangular projection handles this by mapping all horizontal pixels at the top and bottom rows to the same pole, which is acceptable for display but may cause aliasing if not antialiased.
- **Aspect Ratio:** The projection assumes a 2:1 aspect ratio for standard equirectangular videos. If $W \neq 2H$, the mapping distorts the spherical geometry unless the angle calculations are adjusted.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(W \cdot H \cdot N)$, where $N$ is the number of steps per ray for volume integration.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(W \cdot H)$ for the output image buffer.
- **Strengths:** Produces standard equirectangular images compatible with 360-degree video platforms (e.g., YouTube VR). Simple mathematical mapping.
- **Limitations:** Non-uniform sampling density on the sphere (higher density at equator, lower at poles). Not ideal for VR headsets without stereo processing (see StereoSphericalLens).
- **When to use:** When the goal is to generate 360-degree video content or omnidirectional visualizations.
- **When not to use:** When uniform angular sampling is required (consider [Spherical Coordinate GPU Ray Casting](19744-spherical-coordinate-gpu-ray-casting-with-3d-textures.md) with adaptive sampling) or when stereoscopic VR output is needed directly (use StereoSphericalLens).

## Background
The Spherical Lens Projection is part of the volume rendering components in the yt project, a tool for analyzing and visualizing volumetric data. It was introduced to support the creation of 360-degree videos and virtual reality content from scientific simulations. The method produces a cylindrical-spherical projection, which is the standard format for equirectangular mapping. A variant, StereoSphericalLens, extends this by rendering two images from slightly offset camera positions for stereoscopic VR displays.
