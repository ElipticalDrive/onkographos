# Spherical Lens Projection

Also called: Cylindrical-Spherical Projection

## Overview
Spherical Lens Projection produces a cylindrical-spherical projection of a volume dataset [S2]. Movies rendered with this method can be displayed as YouTube 360-degree videos [S2]. It is distinct from its siblings, [Spherical Coordinate GPU Ray Casting with 3D Textures](19744-spherical-coordinate-gpu-ray-casting-with-3d-textures.md) which focuses on texture sampling efficiency in spherical coordinates, and [Spherical Light Grid for Shadows](19872-spherical-light-grid-for-shadows.md) which focuses on shadow computation rather than image projection [G].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Volume dataset (scalar or vector field) [G].
- Camera position (center of the sphere) [G].
- Image resolution: width $W$ (pixels) and height $H$ (pixels) [G].

**Outputs:**
- A 2D image buffer of size $W \times H$ [G].

**Data Structures:**
- Ray direction vector $\mathbf{d} = (d_x, d_y, d_z)$, normalized [G].
- Pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ where $u \in [0, W-1]$ and $v \in [0, H-1]$ [G].

**Steps:**
1. For each pixel $(u, v)$ in the output image:
2. Calculate the horizontal angle $\phi$ (azimuth) and vertical angle $\theta$ (elevation) corresponding to the pixel's position [G].
3. Convert spherical angles to Cartesian ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ [G].
4. Cast a ray from the camera center $C$ along direction $\mathbf{d}$ [G].
5. Perform volume rendering integration along the ray to compute the color $C_{\text{pixel}}$ for this pixel [G].
6. Store $C_{\text{pixel}}$ in the image buffer at $(u, v)$ [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def spherical_lens_projection(volume, camera_center, W, H):
    image = [[0.0, 0.0, 0.0] for _ in range(H)]
    for v in range(H):
        for u in range(W):
            phi = (u / W) * 2 * math.pi - math.pi
            theta = (v / H) * math.pi - math.pi / 2
            d_x = math.cos(theta) * math.cos(phi)
            d_y = math.cos(theta) * math.sin(phi)
            d_z = math.sin(theta)
            # Volume rendering integration along the ray (not given in the sources)
            color = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
            image[v][u] = color
    return image
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Pole Singularities:** At $\theta = \pm \pi/2$, the horizontal resolution effectively compresses to a point [G]. Standard equirectangular projection handles this by mapping all horizontal pixels at the top and bottom rows to the same pole, which is acceptable for display but may cause aliasing if not antialiased [G].
- **Aspect Ratio:** The projection assumes a 2:1 aspect ratio for standard equirectangular videos [G]. If $W \neq 2H$, the mapping distorts the spherical geometry unless the angle calculations are adjusted [G].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Produces standard cylindrical-spherical images compatible with 360-degree video platforms [S2]. Simple mathematical mapping [G].
- **Limitations:** Non-uniform sampling density on the sphere (higher density at equator, lower at poles) [G]. Not ideal for VR headsets without stereo processing (see StereoSphericalLens) [S2].
- **When to use:** When the goal is to generate 360-degree video content or omnidirectional visualizations [S2].
- **When not to use:** When uniform angular sampling is required or when stereoscopic VR output is needed directly (use StereoSphericalLens) [S2].

## Background
The Spherical Lens Projection is part of the volume rendering components in the yt project, a tool for analyzing and visualizing volumetric data [S1, S2]. It was introduced to support the creation of 360-degree videos and virtual reality content from scientific simulations [S2]. The method produces a cylindrical-spherical projection, which is the standard format for equirectangular mapping [S2]. A variant, StereoSphericalLens, extends this by rendering two images from slightly offset camera positions for stereoscopic VR displays [S2].
