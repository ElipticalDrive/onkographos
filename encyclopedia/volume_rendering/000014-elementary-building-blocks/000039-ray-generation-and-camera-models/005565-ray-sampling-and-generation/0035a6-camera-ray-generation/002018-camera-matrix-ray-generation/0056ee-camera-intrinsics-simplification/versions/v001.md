# Camera Intrinsics Simplification

Also called: FOV from Intrinsics, Field of View Calculation, Simplified Camera Intrinsics, Square Pixel Camera Model

## Overview
This method computes the horizontal and vertical field of view (FOV) angles, focal lengths, and principal point offsets from a camera's intrinsic calibration matrix and sensor parameters [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of deriving geometric camera parameters required for ray generation when only the intrinsic matrix $K$ and physical sensor/lens specifications are available, bypassing the need for explicit FOV inputs [S3, unofficial]. It differs from [Pinhole Camera Ray Generation](5058-pinhole-camera-ray-generation.md) by explicitly handling sensor fit modes and pixel aspect ratios to resolve ambiguities in focal length scaling, and from [Camera Matrix Ray Generation](2172-camera-matrix-ray-generation.md) by deriving the intrinsic parameters from raw sensor data rather than assuming a pre-computed matrix [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Intrinsic matrix $K$ (typically $3 \times 3$) [S1, S2, unofficial].
- Image width $W$ and height $H$ in pixels [S3, S4, unofficial].
- Camera lens focal length $f_{lens}$ (in mm) [S3, unofficial].
- Sensor width $S_w$ and sensor height $S_h$ (in mm) [S3, unofficial].
- Pixel aspect ratio $p_{aspect} = p_x / p_y$ [S3, unofficial].
- Sensor fit mode: "AUTO", "HORIZONTAL", or "VERTICAL" [S3, unofficial].
- Principal point shifts $c_x^{shift}, c_y^{shift}$ (normalized coordinates, typically -1 to 1 or 0 to 1 depending on convention; here assumed normalized relative to image dimensions) [S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Dictionary containing:
  - `camera_angle_x`: Horizontal FOV in radians [S3, unofficial].
  - `camera_angle_y`: Vertical FOV in radians [S3, unofficial].
  - `fl_x`: Focal length in pixels (X) [S3, unofficial].
  - `fl_y`: Focal length in pixels (Y) [S3, unofficial].
  - `cx`: Principal point X coordinate in pixels [S3, unofficial].
  - `cy`: Principal point Y coordinate in pixels [S3, unofficial].
  - `w`: Effective image width [S3, unofficial].
  - `h`: Effective image height [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- $K$: $3 \times 3$ matrix. Standard layout:
  $$
  K = \begin{bmatrix}
  f_x & s & c_x \\
  0 & f_y & c_y \\
  0 & 0 & 1
  \end{bmatrix}
  $$
  where $f_x, f_y$ are focal lengths in pixels, $s$ is skew (usually 0), and $c_x, c_y$ are principal points [S4, unofficial].
- `intrinsics_inv`: $3 \times 3$ matrix, inverse of $K$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Effective Resolution:**
    Calculate the effective width $w$ and height $h$ based on the scene resolution and percentage scaling [S3, unofficial]:
    $$
    w = \left\lfloor \text{scene.render.resolution\_x} \times \frac{\text{scene.render.resolution\_percentage}}{100} \right\rfloor
    $$
    $$
    h = \left\lfloor \text{scene.render.resolution\_y} \times \frac{\text{scene.render.resolution\_percentage}}{100} \right\rfloor
    $$
    [S3, unofficial]

2.  **Determine Sensor Fit Mode:**
    If `sensor_fit` is "AUTO", determine the fit direction based on the aspect ratio [S3, unofficial]:
    - If $w \times p_{aspect} \ge h$, set `sensor_fit` = "HORIZONTAL".
    - Else, set `sensor_fit` = "VERTICAL".
    [S3, unofficial]

3.  **Compute Focal Lengths in Pixels:**
    Calculate the focal length in pixels ($f_{px}$) based on the lens focal length $f_{lens}$, sensor dimensions, and fit mode [S3, unofficial]:
    
    If `sensor_fit` == "HORIZONTAL":
    $$
    fl_x = \frac{f_{lens}}{S_h} \times h \times p_{aspect}
    $$
    $$
    fl_y = \frac{f_{lens}}{S_h} \times h
    $$
    [S3, unofficial]
    
    If `sensor_fit` == "VERTICAL":
    $$
    fl_x = \frac{f_{lens}}{S_w} \times w
    $$
    $$
    fl_y = \frac{f_{lens}}{S_w} \times \frac{w}{p_{aspect}}
    $$
    [S3, unofficial]
    
    *Note: Some implementations derive $fl_x, fl_y$ directly from the inverse intrinsic matrix corner vectors (see Pseudocode).*

4.  **Compute Principal Point:**
    Calculate the principal point coordinates in pixels [S3, unofficial]:
    $$
    cx = \frac{w}{2.0} - (c_x^{shift} \times w)
    $$
    $$
    cy = \frac{h}{2.0} + (c_y^{shift} \times h)
    $$
    [S3, unofficial]

5.  **Compute Field of View (FOV):**
    Two methods are cited. Method A uses the derived focal lengths [S3, unofficial]. Method B uses the inverse intrinsic matrix and corner vectors [S1, S2, unofficial].
    
    **Method A (Direct):**
    $$
    \text{camera\_angle\_x} = 2.0 \times \arctan\left(\frac{w}{2.0 \times fl_x}\right)
    $$
    $$
    \text{camera\_angle\_y} = 2.0 \times \arctan\left(\frac{h}{2.0 \times fl_y}\right)
    $$
    [S3, unofficial]
    
    **Method B (Vector-based):**
    Define corner vectors in homogeneous coordinates [S1, S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    v_{left} = [0, 0.5, 1]^T
    $$
    $$
    v_{right} = [1, 0.5, 1]^T
    $$
    $$
    v_{top} = [0.5, 0, 1]^T
    $$
    $$
    v_{bottom} = [0.5, 1, 1]^T
    $$
    [S1, S2, unofficial]
    
    Compute the inverse intrinsic matrix $K^{-1}$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
    
    Transform and normalize the vectors to camera space [S1, S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    \hat{v} = \text{normalize}(K^{-1} v)
    $$
    [S1, S2, unofficial]
    
    Compute FOV as the angle between the transformed left/right and top/bottom vectors [S1, S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    \text{fov\_x} = \arccos(\hat{v}_{left} \cdot \hat{v}_{right})
    $$
    $$
    \text{fov\_y} = \arccos(\hat{v}_{top} \cdot \hat{v}_{bottom})
    $$
    [S1, S2, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math
import numpy as np

def compute_camera_intrinsics_simplification(
    K: np.ndarray,
    resolution_x: float,
    resolution_y: float,
    resolution_percentage: float,
    lens_focal_length_mm: float,
    sensor_width_mm: float,
    sensor_height_mm: float,
    pixel_aspect_x: float,
    pixel_aspect_y: float,
    sensor_fit: str,
    shift_x: float,
    shift_y: float,
    camera_type: str
) -> dict:
    """
    Computes camera intrinsics (FOV, focal lengths, principal point) from intrinsic matrix and sensor data.
    
    Args:
        K: 3x3 intrinsic matrix.
        resolution_x, resolution_y: Base resolution.
        resolution_percentage: Scaling factor (0-100).
        lens_focal_length_mm: Lens focal length in mm.
        sensor_width_mm, sensor_height_mm: Sensor physical dimensions.
        pixel_aspect_x, pixel_aspect_y: Pixel aspect ratio components.
        sensor_fit: "AUTO", "HORIZONTAL", or "VERTICAL".
        shift_x, shift_y: Normalized principal point shifts.
        camera_type: "PERSP" or other.
        
    Returns:
        Dictionary with camera_angle_x, camera_angle_y, fl_x, fl_y, cx, cy, w, h.
    """
    # Pitfall: Check camera type
    if camera_type != "PERSP":
        raise ValueError("3DGS export requires a perspective camera.")
    
    # Step 1: Effective Resolution
    w = int(resolution_x * resolution_percentage / 100)
    h = int(resolution_y * resolution_percentage / 100)
    
    # Step 2: Pixel Aspect Ratio
    pixel_aspect = pixel_aspect_x / pixel_aspect_y
    
    # Step 3: Sensor Fit Mode
    if sensor_fit == "AUTO":
        if w * pixel_aspect >= h:
            sensor_fit = "HORIZONTAL"
        else:
            sensor_fit = "VERTICAL"
            
    # Step 4: Focal Lengths in Pixels (Method A from [S3, unofficial])
    if sensor_fit == "HORIZONTAL":
        fl_x = (lens_focal_length_mm / sensor_height_mm) * h * pixel_aspect
        fl_y = (lens_focal_length_mm / sensor_height_mm) * h
    else: # VERTICAL
        fl_x = (lens_focal_length_mm / sensor_width_mm) * w
        fl_y = (lens_focal_length_mm / sensor_width_mm) * (w / pixel_aspect)
        
    # Step 5: Principal Point
    cx = w / 2.0 - (shift_x * w)
    cy = h / 2.0 + (shift_y * h)
    
    # Step 6: FOV (Method A from [S3, unofficial])
    camera_angle_x = 2.0 * math.atan(w / (2.0 * fl_x))
    camera_angle_y = 2.0 * math.atan(h / (2.0 * fl_y))
    
    return {
        "camera_angle_x": camera_angle_x,
        "camera_angle_y": camera_angle_y,
        "fl_x": fl_x,
        "fl_y": fl_y,
        "cx": cx,
        "cy": cy,
        "w": w,
        "h": h
    }

def compute_fov_from_intrinsic_matrix(K: np.ndarray) -> tuple:
    """
    Alternative method using inverse intrinsic matrix and corner vectors [S1, S2, unofficial].
    Returns (fov_x, fov_y) in radians.
    """
    # Inverse intrinsic matrix
    K_inv = np.linalg.inv(K)
    
    # Corner vectors in homogeneous coordinates [S1, S2, unofficial]
    left = np.array([0, 0.5, 1])
    right = np.array([1, 0.5, 1])
    top = np.array([0.5, 0, 1])
    bottom = np.array([0.5, 1, 1])
    
    def process_vector(v):
        # Transform to camera space and normalize [S1, S2, unofficial]
        vec = K_inv @ v
        return vec / np.linalg.norm(vec)
    
    left_cam = process_vector(left)
    right_cam = process_vector(right)
    top_cam = process_vector(top)
    bottom_cam = process_vector(bottom)
    
    # Compute angles using dot product [S1, S2, unofficial]
    fov_x = math.acos(np.dot(left_cam, right_cam))
    fov_y = math.acos(np.dot(top_cam, bottom_cam))
    
    return fov_x, fov_y
```

**Parameters:**
- `sensor_fit`: Determines which sensor dimension scales the lens focal length. Values: "AUTO", "HORIZONTAL", "VERTICAL" [S3, unofficial].
- `lens_focal_length_mm`: Physical focal length of the lens [S3, unofficial].
- `sensor_width_mm`, `sensor_height_mm`: Physical dimensions of the camera sensor [S3, unofficial].
- `pixel_aspect_x`, `pixel_aspect_y`: Pixel aspect ratio components [S3, unofficial].
- `shift_x`, `shift_y`: Normalized principal point offsets [S3, unofficial].
- `degree`: Degree of spherical harmonics for intrinsic embedding (variant) [S2, unofficial].
- `downsample`: Factor to downsample image grid dimensions (variant) [S2, unofficial].
- `merge_hw`: If True, merge height and width dimensions into a single sequence dimension (variant) [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Non-perspective cameras: If `camera.type != "PERSP"`, the method raises an error and aborts [S3, unofficial].
- Precision: The `acos` function in the vector-based FOV calculation is sensitive to normalization errors; ensure vectors are strictly normalized [S1, S2, unofficial].
- Sensor Fit Ambiguity: In "AUTO" mode, the decision boundary is $w \times p_{aspect} == h$ [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Optimized 3D to 2D Projection](15857-optimized-3d-to-2d-projection.md)
This method transforms 3D world-space vertices of geometric primitives (specifically cube faces) into 2D screen-space coordinates using a simplified perspective projection model [15857-optimized-3d-to-2d-projection.md]. It solves the problem of determining which faces are visible on the screen by combining coordinate transformation, perspective division, and visibility culling (backface, near-plane, and screen-boundary checks) [15857-optimized-3d-to-2d-projection.md]. It differs from general-purpose graphics pipelines by operating on raw vertex arrays with explicit yaw/pitch rotations and a custom visibility mask calculation, rather than relying on a full view-projection matrix multiplication [15857-optimized-3d-to-2d-projection.md].

### Intrinsic Embedding via Spherical Harmonics
This variant generates an embedding of camera intrinsic parameters by converting ray directions to spherical harmonics coefficients [S2, unofficial]. It extracts batch, views, and image dimensions, calculates target dimensions after downsampling, and samples image grid coordinates [S2, unofficial]. It converts local ray directions to spherical harmonics coefficients based on a specified degree (0, 2, 4, or 8) [S2, unofficial]. The output shape depends on the `merge_hw` parameter: if True, shape is `[b, v, (h w), d]`; otherwise `[b, v, d, h, w]` [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ for the direct calculation of FOV from focal lengths [S3, unofficial]. $O(1)$ for the vector-based calculation, involving a fixed number of matrix-vector multiplications and normalizations [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only temporary storage for a few vectors and the inverse intrinsic matrix [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Extremely fast, suitable for per-pixel or per-camera initialization in real-time applications [S3, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Handles sensor fit modes and pixel aspect ratios explicitly, providing robust focal length calculation from physical sensor data [S3, unofficial]. The vector-based method is robust to skew and non-square pixels if the intrinsic matrix is accurate [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires accurate knowledge of sensor dimensions and lens focal length for the physical method [S3, unofficial]. The vector method assumes the intrinsic matrix correctly models the camera [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When initializing a camera model from physical sensor specifications or when deriving FOV from a known calibration matrix [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When the camera is not perspective [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from implementations in 3D Gaussian Splatting dataset builders and novel view synthesis frameworks [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. The physical sensor-based calculation is detailed in the `3dgs-dataset-builder` repository [S3, unofficial]. The vector-based FOV calculation from intrinsic matrices is used in depth estimation and splatting frameworks like `depthsplat` and `NoPoSplat` [S1, S2, unofficial]. The spherical harmonics embedding variant is used in `NoPoSplat` for intrinsic parameter representation [S2, unofficial].
