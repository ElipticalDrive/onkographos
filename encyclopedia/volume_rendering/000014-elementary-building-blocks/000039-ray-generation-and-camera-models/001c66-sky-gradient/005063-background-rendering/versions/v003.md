# Background Rendering


## Overview
Background Rendering composites a distant environment field behind a volume, handling rays that have low transmittance (high opacity) after traversing the foreground volume [S4, unofficial]. It differs from standard equirectangular sampling by representing the background as a 3D grid sampled along concentric spheres, which mitigates aliasing and provides structured traversal for infinite-distance or skybox-like backgrounds [S1, S4, unofficial]. It builds on [Sky Gradient](1262-sky-gradient.md) for procedural sky components and complements [SkyboxRendering](2619-skyboxrendering.md) by supporting volumetric background layers rather than static cubemaps.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$; background grid resolution `background_reso` (width/height) and `background_nlayers` (depth); background data grid containing 4 channels (3 color + 1 density); log-transmittance of the ray through the foreground volume (`log_transmit`); optimization options including `stop_thresh` and `background_brightness`; sun direction (optional, for procedural effects).
*   **Outputs:** Background color contribution $\mathbf{C}_{bg}$ and updated log-transmittance.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Background Grid:** A 3D texture or tensor of shape `(background_reso * 2, background_reso, background_nlayers)`. The first dimension corresponds to the longitude (equirectangular wrap), the second to latitude, and the third to the radial depth layer. Each voxel stores 4 floats: $[R, G, B, \sigma]$.
*   **Ray Spec:** Contains origin, direction, and current sampling state.

**Parameters:**
*   `background_reso`: Resolution of the equirectangular texture. Typical value: 512 [S4, unofficial].
*   `background_nlayers`: Number of radial layers. Typical value: 32 [S4, unofficial].
*   `step_size`: Step size for background sampling. Used to calculate `n_steps` [S4, unofficial].
*   `stop_thresh`: Threshold for terminating early. Typical value: $\exp(-25)$ [S4, unofficial].
*   `background_brightness`: Constant ambient term multiplier [S4, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Calculate the distance from the ray origin to the world origin: $d_{origin} = \|\mathbf{o}\|$.
    *   Set the minimum sampling radius: $r_{min} = \max(d_{origin} + 10^{-3}, 1.0)$. This ensures sampling starts at least 1 unit away from the origin to avoid numerical instability [S4, unofficial].
    *   Calculate the number of sampling steps: $N_{steps} = \lfloor \frac{\text{background\_nlayers}}{\text{step\_size}} \rfloor + 2$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   Initialize output color accumulator $\mathbf{C}_{out} = [0, 0, 0]$ and update `log_transmit` if necessary [S4, unofficial].

2.  **Sampling Loop:**
    *   Iterate $i$ from $0$ to $N_{steps} - 1$:
        *   Calculate the sampling radius $r_i$ using non-linear spacing:
            $$ r_i = \frac{N_{steps}}{N_{steps} - i - 0.5} $$
            This formula creates denser samples near the origin and sparser samples at infinity [S4, unofficial].
        *   If $r_i < r_{min}$, continue to the next iteration [S4, unofficial].
        *   Intersect the ray with a sphere of radius $r_i$ centered at the world origin. If no intersection or intersection distance $t < 0$, continue [S4, unofficial].
        *   Calculate the intersection point: $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} + t \mathbf{d}$.
        *   Convert $\mathbf{p}$ to normalized spherical coordinates or unit vector $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{p} / \|\mathbf{p}\|$ [S4, unofficial].
        *   Convert $\mathbf{u}$ to equirectangular grid indices $(l_x, l_y)$ and fractional offsets $(f_x, f_y)$:
            *   $l_x = \lfloor \text{background\_reso} \times 2 \times \frac{\text{atan2}(u_y, u_x)}{2\pi} + \text{background\_reso} \rfloor$ (clamped to $[0, \text{background\_reso} \times 2 - 1]$) [S4, unofficial].
            *   $l_y = \lfloor \text{background\_reso} \times \frac{\text{acos}(u_z)}{\pi} \rfloor$ (clamped to $[0, \text{background\_reso} - 1]$) [S4, unofficial].
            *   $f_x, f_y$ are the fractional parts of the coordinates [S4, unofficial].
        *   Calculate the depth index $l_z$ based on the inverse radius to map radial distance to layers:
            $$ l_z = \text{clamp}\left( (1 - \frac{1}{r_i}) \times \text{background\_nlayers} - 0.5, 0, \text{background\_nlayers} - 1 \right) $$
            (Note: Exact formula may vary slightly based on implementation, but relies on mapping $1/r$ to layer index) [S4, unofficial].
        *   Perform trilinear interpolation on the background grid at $(l_x, l_y, l_z)$ with fractional offsets $(f_x, f_y, f_z)$ to obtain color $\mathbf{C}_{voxel}$ and density $\sigma$ [S4, unofficial].
        *   If $\sigma > 0$:
            *   Calculate the optical depth increment: $\Delta \tau = (r_{last} - r_i) \times \text{world\_step} \times \sigma$, where $r_{last}$ is the previous radius (initially $r_{min}$ or infinity) and `world_step` is the scaling factor for the radial step [S4, unofficial].
            *   Calculate the weight: $w = \exp(\text{log\_transmit}) \times (1 - \exp(-\Delta \tau))$ [S4, unofficial].
            *   Update log-transmittance: $\text{log\_transmit} \leftarrow \text{log\_transmit} - \Delta \tau$ [S4, unofficial].
            *   Accumulate color: $\mathbf{C}_{out} \leftarrow \mathbf{C}_{out} + w \times \text{clamp}(\mathbf{C}_{voxel} + 0.5, 0, \infty)$ [S4, unofficial].
            *   If $\exp(\text{log\_transmit}) < \text{stop\_thresh}$, break the loop [S4, unofficial].
        *   Update $r_{last} = r_i$ [S4, unofficial].

3.  **Final Compositing:**
    *   Add the ambient background term: $\mathbf{C}_{out} \leftarrow \mathbf{C}_{out} + \exp(\text{log\_transmit}) \times \text{background\_brightness}$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   Merge with foreground: $\mathbf{C}_{final} = \mathbf{C}_{fg} + \exp(\text{log\_transmit}) \times \mathbf{C}_{out}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def render_background_forward(ray_origin, ray_dir, grid_data, grid_reso, grid_nlayers, 
                              log_transmit, step_size, stop_thresh, bg_brightness):
    """
    Renders the background color for a single ray using concentric sphere sampling.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: (3,) float array, ray origin.
        ray_dir: (3,) float array, ray direction (normalized).
        grid_data: (W*2, H, D, 4) float array, background grid [R, G, B, sigma].
        grid_reso: int, resolution of the equirectangular texture.
        grid_nlayers: int, number of radial layers.
        log_transmit: float, log of transmittance through foreground volume.
        step_size: float, step size for background sampling.
        stop_thresh: float, threshold for early termination.
        bg_brightness: float, ambient background brightness.
        
    Returns:
        bg_color: (3,) float array, background color contribution.
        new_log_transmit: float, updated log transmittance.
    """
    # 1. Initialization
    dist_to_origin = np.linalg.norm(ray_origin)
    r_min = max(dist_to_origin + 1e-3, 1.0)
    n_steps = int(grid_nlayers / step_size) + 2
    
    bg_color = np.zeros(3)
    r_last = float('inf') # Or r_min depending on implementation logic for first step
    
    # 2. Sampling Loop
    for i in range(n_steps):
        # Non-linear spacing: denser near origin, sparser at infinity
        r_curr = n_steps / (n_steps - i - 0.5)
        
        if r_curr < r_min:
            continue
            
        # Intersect ray with sphere of radius r_curr
        # Ray: o + t*d. Sphere: ||x||^2 = r^2
        # ||o + t*d||^2 = r^2 => t^2 + 2*dot(o,d)*t + ||o||^2 - r^2 = 0
        a = 1.0
        b = 2.0 * np.dot(ray_origin, ray_dir)
        c = np.dot(ray_origin, ray_origin) - r_curr**2
        discriminant = b**2 - 4*a*c
        
        if discriminant < 0:
            continue
            
        sqrt_disc = np.sqrt(discriminant)
        t1 = (-b - sqrt_disc) / (2*a)
        t2 = (-b + sqrt_disc) / (2*a)
        
        # Choose the positive intersection closest to the ray origin
        t = t2 if t2 > 0 else t1
        if t < 0:
            continue
            
        # Intersection point
        p = ray_origin + t * ray_dir
        u = p / np.linalg.norm(p) # Unit vector
        
        # Convert to equirectangular coordinates
        # Longitude: atan2(y, x) -> [0, 2*pi]
        lon = np.arctan2(u[1], u[0])
        if lon < 0:
            lon += 2 * np.pi
        lon_idx = int(lon / (2 * np.pi) * grid_reso * 2)
        lon_frac = (lon / (2 * np.pi) * grid_reso * 2) - lon_idx
        
        # Latitude: acos(z) -> [0, pi]
        lat = np.arccos(np.clip(u[2], -1.0, 1.0))
        lat_idx = int(lat / np.pi * grid_reso)
        lat_frac = (lat / np.pi * grid_reso) - lat_idx
        
        # Depth index based on inverse radius
        # Mapping 1/r to [0, grid_nlayers]
        inv_r = 1.0 / r_curr
        # Formula from notes: (1 - invr_mid) * grid_nlayers - 0.5
        # Note: invr_mid likely refers to 1/r_curr or similar normalized inverse radius
        # Assuming inv_r is the inverse radius at current step
        depth_val = (1.0 - inv_r) * grid_nlayers - 0.5
        depth_idx = int(depth_val)
        depth_frac = depth_val - depth_idx
        
        # Clamp indices
        lon_idx = np.clip(lon_idx, 0, grid_reso * 2 - 1)
        lat_idx = np.clip(lat_idx, 0, grid_reso - 1)
        depth_idx = np.clip(depth_idx, 0, grid_nlayers - 1)
        
        # Trilinear interpolation
        # grid_data shape: (W*2, H, D, 4)

        def trilerp(x0, y0, z0, x1, y1, z1, data):
            # Helper for trilinear interpolation

            pass
            
        sigma = trilerp(lon_idx, lat_idx, depth_idx, lon_idx+1, lat_idx+1, depth_idx+1, grid_data)[:, 3] # Placeholder for actual trilerp
        color = trilerp(lon_idx, lat_idx, depth_idx, lon_idx+1, lat_idx+1, depth_idx+1, grid_data)[:, :3]
        
        # If sigma > 0, compute weight and accumulate
        if sigma > 0:
            # Calculate optical depth increment
            # world_step is a scaling factor for the radial step size
            # Assuming world_step = 1.0 for normalized coordinates or derived from grid scale
            world_step = 1.0 
            delta_tau = (r_last - r_curr) * world_step * sigma
            
            weight = np.exp(log_transmit) * (1.0 - np.exp(-delta_tau))
            log_transmit -= delta_tau
            
            # Accumulate color
            # Clamp color + 0.5 to >= 0
            color_clamped = np.maximum(color + 0.5, 0.0)
            bg_color += weight * color_clamped
            
            # Early termination
            if np.exp(log_transmit) < stop_thresh:
                break
                
        r_last = r_curr
        
    # 3. Final Compositing
    bg_color += np.exp(log_transmit) * bg_brightness
    
    return bg_color, log_transmit
```

## Subtopics

### [Background Rendering Kernel (Forward)](14913-background-rendering-kernel-forward.md)
A CUDA kernel that renders the background color for rays that have transmitted through the volume. It serves as the final compositing stage in the background rendering kernel, accumulating color from the background layers and adding a constant ambient term based on remaining transmission. It differs from standard equirectangular sampling by using concentric spheres to distribute samples, which helps mitigate aliasing and provides a structured traversal of the background layers.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{steps})$ per ray, where $N_{steps}$ is proportional to `background_nlayers` / `step_size`. Typically small (e.g., 32-64 steps) [S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(\text{background\_reso}^2 \times \text{background\_nlayers})$ for the background grid. For `background_reso`=512 and `background_nlayers`=32, this is $512 \times 512 \times 32 \times 4$ bytes $\approx 32$ MB [S4, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Efficient on GPU due to structured memory access and early termination for opaque rays. Skips rays with `log_transmit < -25` to avoid numerical instability [S4, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles infinite backgrounds naturally; reduces aliasing compared to simple equirectangular sampling; supports volumetric background effects (e.g., fog, light shafts) [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires pre-computation or training of the background grid; less flexible than procedural sky for dynamic environments unless updated frequently [S4, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When a volumetric or high-resolution environment map is needed behind a volume, especially in neural radiance field (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting contexts [S1, S4, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** For simple sky domes where a procedural gradient suffices [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Background Rendering in the context of volume rendering and neural radiance fields was introduced as part of the Svox2 framework [S4, unofficial]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRFs in rendering distant environments by treating the background as a learnable 3D grid sampled along concentric spheres. This approach allows for the integration of volumetric effects (like fog or light shafts) and high-resolution environment maps into the volume rendering pipeline [S1, S4, unofficial]. The method has been adopted in various neural rendering frameworks, including Plenoxels [S2, unofficial] and SDFStudio [S1, unofficial], to improve the visual quality of rendered scenes with complex backgrounds.
