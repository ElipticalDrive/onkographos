# Uniform Sphere Sampling

Also called: UniformSphereSampler, Uniform Solid Angle Sampling, sampleUniformSphere, SphereSampler, getSampleUniformSolidAngle

## Overview
Uniform Sphere Sampling generates a random direction vector uniformly distributed over the surface of a unit sphere [S2, S4, S5, unofficial]. It solves the problem of creating isotropic ray directions for global illumination, sky sampling, or volume scattering where no preferred direction exists [S2, S5, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Random Hemisphere Sampling](2034-random-hemisphere-sampling.md) by covering the full $4\pi$ steradians rather than restricting samples to a half-sphere, and from [Rejection Sampling for Uniform Sphere Sampling](3286-rejection-sampling-for-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by using a direct transformation method that avoids discarding samples [S2, S4, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Two independent uniform random numbers $u, v \in [0, 1)$ [S1, S2, S4, unofficial]. These are typically derived from a seed, pixel coordinates, or a random number generator [S1, S4, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- A unit direction vector $\mathbf{d} = (x, y, z)$ such that $|\mathbf{d}| = 1$ and the distribution is uniform over the sphere surface [S2, S5, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No persistent data structures are required for the core sampling function [G]. The output is a 3D vector [S2, S4, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Generate two independent random values $u$ and $v$ in the range $[0, 1)$ [S1, S2, S4, unofficial].
2. Compute the azimuthal angle $\phi$ (longitude) and the polar angle $\theta$ (colatitude) or its cosine [S1, S2, S4, unofficial].
   - The azimuthal angle $\phi$ is sampled uniformly from $[0, 2\pi)$ [S2, S4, unofficial]:
     $$ \phi = 2\pi u $$
   - The cosine of the polar angle $\cos\theta$ is sampled uniformly from $[-1, 1]$ to ensure uniform area distribution on the sphere [S2, S4, S5, unofficial]:
     $$ \cos\theta = 2v - 1 $$
   - The sine of the polar angle is derived from the identity $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$:
     $$ \sin\theta = \sqrt{1 - \cos^2\theta} $$
3. Convert spherical coordinates to Cartesian coordinates [S1, S2, S4, unofficial]:
   $$ x = \sin\theta \cos\phi $$
   $$ y = \sin\theta \sin\phi $$
   $$ z = \cos\theta $$
4. Return the vector $\mathbf{d} = (x, y, z)$ [S2, S4, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def sample_uniform_sphere(rand_2d: tuple) -> tuple:
    """
    Generates a uniformly distributed random direction on a unit sphere.
    
    Args:
        rand_2d: A tuple (u, v) of two independent uniform random numbers in [0, 1).
        
    Returns:
        A tuple (x, y, z) representing the unit direction vector.
    """
    u, v = rand_2d
    
    # Calculate azimuthal angle phi in [0, 2*pi)
    phi = 2.0 * math.pi * u
    
    # Calculate cosine of polar angle theta in [-1, 1]
    cos_theta = 2.0 * v - 1.0
    
    # Calculate sine of polar angle theta
    sin_theta = math.sqrt(1.0 - cos_theta * cos_theta)
    
    # Convert to Cartesian coordinates
    x = sin_theta * math.cos(phi)
    y = sin_theta * math.sin(phi)
    z = cos_theta
    
    return (x, y, z)
```

**Parameters:**
- `rand_2d`: The source of randomness. In GPU implementations, this is often derived from fragment coordinates or a hash function [S1, S4, unofficial]. In CPU implementations, it comes from a PRNG [S5, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** When $v$ is very close to 0 or 1, $\cos\theta$ approaches $\pm 1$ and $\sin\theta$ approaches 0. Floating-point inaccuracies in `sqrt(1 - cos_theta * cos_theta)` can lead to vectors slightly outside the unit sphere [G]. Normalization is not strictly necessary if the math is exact, but can be used as a safeguard [G].
- **Uniformity:** Sampling $\theta$ uniformly from $[0, \pi]$ instead of $\cos\theta$ uniformly from $[-1, 1]$ results in clustering of samples near the poles (the "ice cream cone" effect) [S2, S4, unofficial]. The sources explicitly use the cosine method [S2, S5, unofficial], though some implementations compute the polar angle directly via `acos` [S4, unofficial].
- **Random Number Quality:** The quality of the output depends on the independence and uniformity of the input random numbers [S1, S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Random Hemisphere Sampling](2034-random-hemisphere-sampling.md)
Random Hemisphere Sampling generates a random unit vector uniformly distributed within a hemisphere oriented around a given normal vector. It is primarily used to compute indirect lighting contributions at a surface point by sampling the hemisphere above the surface normal and recursively tracing rays. It sets itself apart from its parent [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by restricting the sample space to a half-sphere, which is essential for surface-based shading models where light only interacts with one side of a surface [G]. Unlike [Rejection Sampling for Uniform Sphere Sampling](3286-rejection-sampling-for-uniform-sphere-sampling.md), which discards samples falling outside the hemisphere, this method directly constructs valid hemisphere directions, ensuring 100% efficiency per sample generation attempt [G].

### [Rejection Sampling for Uniform Sphere Sampling](3286-rejection-sampling-for-uniform-sphere-sampling.md)
Rejection Sampling for Uniform Sphere Sampling generates uniformly distributed points within a unit ball (or on its surface) by sampling from a bounding cube and discarding outliers [G]. It differs from spherical coordinate conversion [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md) by avoiding trigonometric functions, relying instead on simple arithmetic and normalization, which makes it computationally cheaper per sample but potentially slower in wall-clock time due to rejected iterations [G]. It sets itself apart from the direct method by potentially discarding samples, requiring a loop until a valid sample is found.

### [Spherical Background Sampling](3991-spherical-background-sampling.md)
Spherical Background Sampling converts a 3D ray direction vector into 2D UV coordinates suitable for sampling a background environment map. It assumes the ray origin lies inside the background sphere, mapping the direction directly to spherical coordinates. Unlike [Uniform Sphere Sampling](1669-uniform-sphere-sampling.md), which generates random directions, this method deterministically maps existing directions to texture space. It sets itself apart by focusing on the background geometry rather than general direction generation, often integrating with camera models to determine the ray direction towards the background.

### Importance Phong Sampling
Importance Phong Sampling generates samples based on a Phong reflection model, concentrating samples around the specular highlight direction [S3, unofficial].
- **Definition:** Samples directions according to a Phong lobe, useful for glossy reflections [S3, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
  1. Generate two uniform random numbers $\eta_1, \eta_2 \in [0, 1)$ [S3, unofficial].
  2. Retrieve the Phong exponent $n$ from the material [S3, unofficial].
  3. Compute the polar angle $\Theta$ and azimuthal angle $\Phi$:
     $$ \Theta = \arccos((1 - \eta_1)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}) $$
     $$ \Phi = 2\pi \eta_2 $$
  4. Construct the sample vector in local spherical coordinates:
     $$ x = \cos\Phi \sin\Theta $$
     $$ y = \sin\Phi \sin\Theta $$
     $$ z = \cos\Theta $$
  5. Transform the sample vector into the local coordinate system defined by the normal $\mathbf{n}$, and tangent vectors $\mathbf{V}$ and $\mathbf{W}$:
     $$ \text{sample} = \mathbf{V} \cdot x + \mathbf{W} \cdot y + \mathbf{n} \cdot z $$
  6. Normalize and return the direction [S3, unofficial].
- **Formula:** The PDF for this sampling is proportional to $\cos^n\Theta$ [S3, unofficial].
- **When to choose:** Use when rendering glossy or specular materials where energy should be concentrated around the reflection lobe.

### Random Camera Batch Generation
Random Camera Batch Generation creates a batch of random camera positions on a sphere for rendering [S6, unofficial].
- **Definition:** Generates camera poses for batch rendering by sampling positions on a sphere [S6, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
  1. Sample spherical coordinates (azimuth: $0$ to $2\pi$, elevation: $-\pi/2$ to $\pi/2$) for `batch_size` points [S6, unofficial].
  2. Convert spherical coordinates to Cartesian coordinates at `cam_radius` [S6, unofficial].
  3. Add a random offset to camera positions: `eye = camera_pos + torch.rand((batch_size, 1), device=device) * 0.5 - 0.25` [S6, unofficial].
  4. Set camera look-at direction to origin: `at = torch.zeros(batch_size, 3)` [S6, unofficial].
  5. Set camera up vector to Y-axis: `up = torch.tensor([[0., 1., 0.]])` [S6, unofficial].
- **Parameters:**
  - `fovy`: Field of view in radians, default 45 degrees [S6, unofficial].
  - `iter_res`: Image resolution, default [2048, 2048] [S6, unofficial].
  - `cam_near_far`: Near and far clipping planes, default [0.1, 1000.0] [S6, unofficial].
  - `cam_radius`: Radius of the sphere for camera positions, default 3.0 [S6, unofficial].
- **When to choose:** Use when generating multiple viewpoints around a scene for batch rendering or multi-view reconstruction.

### Anisotropic Scattering via Importance Sampling
Anisotropic Scattering via Importance Sampling samples scattering directions according to the volume's phase function to avoid artifacts associated with uniform sampling [S7, unofficial].
- **Definition:** A technique to sample scattering directions based on the phase function, such as the Henyey-Greenstein function [S7, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
  1. Initially, sample over a uniform sphere and weight resulting directions by the phase function for given incident and new outgoing directions [S7, unofficial].
  2. Modify the ray marcher to importance sample the scattering direction based on the phase function instead of uniform sampling [S7, unofficial].
- **Parameters:**
  - Henyey-Greenstein asymmetry parameter $g$: Used to define anisotropic scattering. Tested values include $g=0.8$, $g=0$ (isotropic), and $g=1$ [S7, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** Uniform sphere sampling with phase function weighting creates artifacts [S7, unofficial].
- **When to choose:** Use when rendering participating media with strong forward or backward scattering, such as fog, smoke, or underwater scenes.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample [G]. The method involves a constant number of arithmetic operations and trigonometric functions [S2, S4, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$. No additional memory is required beyond the input random numbers and output vector [S2, S4, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:** Performance depends on the efficiency of the random number generator and trigonometric functions [G]. In GPU shaders, `sin`, `cos`, and `sqrt` are typically hardware-accelerated [S1, S4, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** Scales linearly with the number of samples. Suitable for high-sample counts in path tracing or volume rendering [S2, S5, unofficial].
- **Strengths:**
  - Unbiased and uniform distribution over the sphere surface [S2, S4, S5, unofficial].
  - Efficient with no rejection overhead [S2, S4, unofficial].
  - Simple to implement [S2, S4, unofficial].
- **Limitations:**
  - Requires high-quality random numbers to avoid patterns [S1, S4, unofficial].
  - Not suitable for anisotropic scattering without modification (importance sampling required) [S7, unofficial].
- **When to use:** General purpose ray direction generation, sky sampling, isotropic volume scattering [S2, S5, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** Surface shading requiring hemisphere sampling (use [Random Hemisphere Sampling](2034-random-hemisphere-sampling.md)) [S2, S4, unofficial], or anisotropic scattering (use [Importance Phong Sampling](#importance-phong-sampling) [S3, unofficial] or Henyey-Greenstein sampling [S7, unofficial]).

## Background
Uniform Sphere Sampling is a fundamental technique in computer graphics, originating from the need to simulate isotropic light sources and sky illumination [S2, S4, unofficial]. The method of sampling $\cos\theta$ uniformly is a standard result in Monte Carlo integration for spherical domains [S2, S4, unofficial]. It is widely used in path tracers, volume renderers, and sky models [S2, S5, S7, unofficial]. The implementation details vary slightly between CPU and GPU contexts, with GPU implementations often using hash functions for random number generation [S1, S4, unofficial].
