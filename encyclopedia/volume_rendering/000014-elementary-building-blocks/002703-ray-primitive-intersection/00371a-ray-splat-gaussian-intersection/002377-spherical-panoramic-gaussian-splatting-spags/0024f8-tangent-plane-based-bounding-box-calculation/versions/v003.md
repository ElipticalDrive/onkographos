# Tangent Plane-Based Bounding Box Calculation

Also called: SPaGS Bounding Box, Two-Stage Bounding Box

## Overview
A two-stage method to calculate an axis-aligned screen-space bounding box for 3D Gaussians in spherical panoramas, addressing deformation caused by omnidirectional pixel distribution and non-linear spherical projection [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard affine rasterization by projecting a tangent-space bounding box onto the equirectangular plane, ensuring validity despite spherical distortion [S1]. It builds on [Spherical Panoramic Gaussian Splatting (SPaGS)](3042-spherical-panoramic-gaussian-splatting-spags.md).

## How it works
**Inputs:** 3D Gaussian splat parameters (mean $\mathbf{m}$, covariance $\Sigma$) in camera space; spherical camera projection parameters (equirectangular image dimensions $W \times H$).
**Outputs:** Axis-aligned 2D bounding box $(p_{x,\min}, p_{y,\min}, p_{x,\max}, p_{y,\max})$ on the equirectangular image plane.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tangent Space Basis:** Three normalized vectors $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ (tangent), $\hat{\mathbf{b}}$ (bitangent), $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ (normal) representing the local x, y, z axes of the tangent space.
*   **Tangent Space Bounding Box:** Four floats $(t_{x,\min}, t_{x,\max}, t_{y,\min}, t_{y,\max})$ defining the rectangle in the tangent plane $z=1$.
*   **Camera Space Coordinates:** Derived from tangent coordinates using transformation matrix.

**Steps:**

1.  **Define Tangent Space:**
    For each splat, compute the normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ from the covariance matrix. Determine the reference vector $\mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}}$ to avoid singularity at poles:
    $$
    \mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}} = \begin{cases} 
    (0, 0, 1)^T & \text{if } |\mathbf{m} \cdot (0,0,1)^T| < 1 - \epsilon \\
    (1, 0, 0)^T & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    $$
    Compute $\hat{\mathbf{t}} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}} \times \hat{\mathbf{n}})$ and $\hat{\mathbf{b}} = \hat{\mathbf{n}} \times \hat{\mathbf{t}}$ [S1]. The origin of the tangent space is the camera space origin. The tangent plane is defined as $z=1$ [S1].

2.  **Transform to Tangent Space:**
    Construct the transformation matrix $M$ from camera space to tangent space:
    $$
    M = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{t}}^T \\ \hat{\mathbf{b}}^T \\ \hat{\mathbf{n}}^T \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Transform the splat's covariance $\Sigma$ into tangent space: $\Sigma_t = M \Sigma M^T$ [S1].

3.  **Calculate Tangent Space Bounding Box:**
    Compute the 2D axis-aligned bounding box $(t_{x,\min}, t_{x,\max}, t_{y,\min}, t_{y,\max})$ on the tangent plane using the standard HTGS approach [HFW*25] on the transformed covariance $\Sigma_t$ [S1]. Let the box be represented by points $t = (t_x, t_y, 1)$ where $t_x \in [t_{x,\min}, t_{x,\max}]$ and $t_y \in [t_{y,\min}, t_{y,\max}]$ [S1].

4.  **Project to Camera Space:**
    Inverse the tangent space transformation to get camera space coordinates $(c_x, c_y, c_z)$ for any point on the tangent plane:
    $$
    \begin{bmatrix} c_x \\ c_y \\ c_z \end{bmatrix} = M^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} t_x \\ t_y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Specifically, $c_x = \hat{\mathbf{t}} \cdot \mathbf{c}$, $c_y = \hat{\mathbf{b}} \cdot \mathbf{c}$, $c_z = \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{c}$ is not directly used; rather, the inverse mapping is:
    $$
    \mathbf{c} = t_x \hat{\mathbf{t}} + t_y \hat{\mathbf{b}} + 1 \hat{\mathbf{n}}
    $$
    where $\mathbf{c} = (c_x, c_y, c_z)^T$ is the point in camera space corresponding to $(t_x, t_y)$ on the tangent plane $z=1$ [S1].

5.  **Project to Equirectangular Plane (Stage 2):**
    Calculate the minimum and maximum $p_y$ (latitude) and $p_x$ (longitude) on the equirectangular plane.
    
    *   **Latitude ($p_y$):**
        Using the monotonically increasing property of $\arcsin$, calculate $p_{y,\min}$ and $p_{y,\max}$ by evaluating the four corners of the tangent bounding box:
        $$
        p_y = \arcsin\left(\frac{c_y}{\sqrt{c_x^2 + c_y^2 + c_z^2}}\right)
        $$
        $$
        p_{y,\min} = \min_{(t_x, t_y) \in \text{corners}} \arcsin\left(\frac{c_y(t_x, t_y)}{\|\mathbf{c}(t_x, t_y)\|}\right)
        $$
        $$
        p_{y,\max} = \max_{(t_x, t_y) \in \text{corners}} \arcsin\left(\frac{c_y(t_x, t_y)}{\|\mathbf{c}(t_x, t_y)\|}\right)
        $$
        [S1]

    *   **Longitude ($p_x$):**
        To overcome the singularity and discontinuity of $\text{atan2}$, calculate the maximized differential angle $\Delta \phi$ instead of directly computing longitude:
        $$
        \Delta \phi = \arccos\left( \frac{\mathbf{c}_1 \cdot \mathbf{c}_2}{\|\mathbf{c}_1\| \|\mathbf{c}_2\|} \right)
        $$
        where $\mathbf{c}_1$ and $\mathbf{c}_2$ are vectors from the camera origin to the points on the bounding box boundary that maximize the angular separation in the horizontal plane [S1]. The final $p_{x,\min}$ and $p_{x,\max}$ are derived from the center longitude $\phi_c$ and $\Delta \phi$:
        $$
        p_{x,\min} = \phi_c - \Delta \phi, \quad p_{x,\max} = \phi_c + \Delta \phi
        $$
        [S1]

6.  **Handle Pole Coverage:**
    If the projected bounding box covers the north or south pole (determined by checking if the latitude range includes $\pm \pi/2$), calculate $p_{y,\min}$ and $p_{y,\max}$ similarly for the projection center on the respective sphere. Set $p_{x,\min} = -\pi$ and $p_{x,\max} = \pi$ (full image width) since the pole is fully covered [S1]. If the projection center lies exactly on a pole, use the same method with simplified expressions [S1].

7.  **Load Balancing:**
    Apply a load balancing scheme similar to Radl et al. [RSP*24] to address the impact of occasional large bounding boxes on Gaussian/tile instance creation [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import numpy as np

def tangent_plane_bounding_box(splat_mean, splat_cov, image_width, image_height):
    """
    Calculate the equirectangular bounding box for a 3D Gaussian splat.
    
    Args:
        splat_mean: numpy array (3,), mean of the splat in camera space.
        splat_cov: numpy array (3,3), covariance matrix of the splat in camera space.
        image_width: int, width of the equirectangular image.
        image_height: int, height of the equirectangular image.
        
    Returns:
        tuple: (px_min, py_min, px_max, py_max) in radians.
    """
    # Step 1: Define Tangent Space
    # Compute normal from covariance (principal component)
    eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eigh(splat_cov)
    # Normal is the eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue (direction of least spread)
    # Or largest? In 3DGS, the Gaussian is elongated. The normal is usually the direction of the axis.
    # Assuming standard 3DGS where covariance defines the shape, the normal n is the direction of the 
    # axis with the smallest variance if we consider the Gaussian as a surface, 
    # but typically for splats, we use the orientation. 
    # The source says "n represents the z axis of the tangent space". 
    # We assume n is derived from the splat's orientation. 
    # For simplicity, if orientation quaternion q is available, n = q * (0,0,1). 
    # If only cov is available, n is the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue (assuming thin splat).
    # Let's assume we have the normal vector n_hat directly or derived.
    n_hat = eigenvectors[:, np.argmin(eigenvalues)]
    n_hat = n_hat / np.linalg.norm(n_hat)
    
    # Reference vector to avoid singularity
    ref_vec = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
    if abs(np.dot(splat_mean, ref_vec)) > 1 - 1e-6:
        ref_vec = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
        
    t_hat = np.cross(ref_vec, n_hat)
    t_hat = t_hat / np.linalg.norm(t_hat)
    b_hat = np.cross(n_hat, t_hat)
    
    # Step 2: Transform Covariance to Tangent Space
    M = np.array([t_hat, b_hat, n_hat])
    cov_t = M @ splat_cov @ M.T
    
    # Step 3: Calculate Tangent Space Bounding Box (HTGS method)
    # This is a placeholder for the specific HTGS algorithm [HFW*25]
    # It returns tx_min, tx_max, ty_min, ty_max
    tx_min, tx_max, ty_min, ty_max = htgs_bounding_box(cov_t)
    
    # Step 4 & 5: Project to Equirectangular Plane
    # Generate corners in tangent space
    corners_t = [
        (tx_min, ty_min), (tx_min, ty_max),
        (tx_max, ty_min), (tx_max, ty_max)
    ]
    
    # Convert tangent points to camera space
    # c = tx * t_hat + ty * b_hat + 1 * n_hat
    corners_c = []
    for tx, ty in corners_t:
        c = tx * t_hat + ty * b_hat + 1.0 * n_hat
        corners_c.append(c)
        
    # Calculate py_min, py_max
    # py = arcsin(cy / ||c||)
    py_values = []
    for c in corners_c:
        norm_c = np.linalg.norm(c)
        if norm_c == 0:
            continue
        py = math.asin(c[1] / norm_c)
        py_values.append(py)
        
    py_min = min(py_values)
    py_max = max(py_values)
    
    # Calculate px_min, px_max using differential angle
    # Find the center longitude phi_c
    # Use the mean of the corners or the splat mean projected
    mean_c = np.mean(corners_c, axis=0)
    norm_mean = np.linalg.norm(mean_c)
    if norm_mean == 0:
        phi_c = 0.0
    else:
        phi_c = math.atan2(mean_c[0], mean_c[2])
        
    # Calculate max differential angle
    # We need to find the max angle between any two points in the projection
    # However, the source says "maximized differential angle ... to overcome singularity"
    # Eq 24-25 suggest calculating delta_phi from the bounding box extent.
    # A robust way is to find the min and max longitude among corners, handling wrap-around.
    
    px_values = []
    for c in corners_c:
        norm_c = np.linalg.norm(c)
        if norm_c == 0:
            continue
        px = math.atan2(c[0], c[2])
        px_values.append(px)
        
    # Handle wrap-around for px
    px_values.sort()
    # Check for wrap-around
    max_gap = 0
    for i in range(len(px_values) - 1):
        gap = px_values[i+1] - px_values[i]
        if gap > max_gap:
            max_gap = gap
            
    # If the gap is large, the box crosses the date line
    if max_gap > math.pi:
        # The box covers the discontinuity
        px_min = -math.pi
        px_max = math.pi
    else:
        px_min = px_values[0]
        px_max = px_values[-1]
        
    # Step 6: Handle Pole Coverage
    # Check if the box covers the pole
    # If py_max > pi/2 - epsilon or py_min < -pi/2 + epsilon
    if py_max > math.pi/2 - 1e-6 or py_min < -math.pi/2 + 1e-6:
        # Covers pole
        # Recalculate py for the specific pole projection if needed, 
        # but source says "calculate min/max py in a similar way for the projection center on the respective sphere"
        # For simplicity, we clamp or use full range.
        # Source: "use the full range of the image width for the px axis"
        px_min = -math.pi
        px_max = math.pi
        
    return (px_min, py_min, px_max, py_max)

def htgs_bounding_box(cov_t):
    """
    Placeholder for HTGS bounding box calculation [HFW*25].
    Returns tx_min, tx_max, ty_min, ty_max.
    """
    # Standard 2D Gaussian bounding box calculation
    # tx = 3 * sqrt(cov_t[0,0])
    # ty = 3 * sqrt(cov_t[1,1])
    # This is a simplification. The actual HTGS method may involve rotation.
    # Since the source says "same approach as HTGS", we assume it's a standard 2D AABB.
    tx = 3.0 * math.sqrt(cov_t[0, 0])
    ty = 3.0 * math.sqrt(cov_t[1, 1])
    return (-tx, tx, -ty, ty)
```

## Subtopics

### [Tight Bounding Box Calculation for Quadric Gaussians](9970-tight-bounding-box-calculation-for-quadric-gaussians.md)
Computes a tight 2D axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) for Quadratic Gaussian Splatting (QGS) primitives by approximating the primitive's boundary with tangent planes, avoiding the loose approximation of projecting 3D bounding box vertices. It addresses the inefficiency caused by QGS concave cases and the lack of an elementary inverse for the geodesic distance function, which makes analytical determination of the rendered portion difficult. It builds on [Tangent Plane-Based Bounding Box Calculation](3425-tangent-plane-based-bounding-box-calculation.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat, as it involves a fixed number of matrix operations and trigonometric functions.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat, storing only the bounding box coordinates.
*   **Strengths:** Handles spherical projection distortion accurately; numerically stable at poles; avoids loose approximations of 3D bounding box projections.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of pole singularities; load balancing is necessary due to occasional large bounding boxes [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering of 3D Gaussians on spherical panoramas (e.g., VR, 360° video) where standard affine rasterization fails due to non-linear projection.

## Background
Originated in the SPaGS paper [S1] to enable real-time rendering of 3D Gaussian splats on spherical panoramas. It addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS rasterization, which assumes a local affine approximation, by introducing a numerically stable ray-splat intersection approach suitable for arbitrary spherical camera models [S1].
