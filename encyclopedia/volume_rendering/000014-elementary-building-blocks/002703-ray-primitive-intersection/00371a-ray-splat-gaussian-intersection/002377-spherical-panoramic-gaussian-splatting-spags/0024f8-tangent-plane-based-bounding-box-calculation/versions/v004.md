# Tangent Plane-Based Bounding Box Calculation

Also called: SPaGS Bounding Box, Two-Stage Bounding Box

## Overview
A two-stage method to calculate an axis-aligned screen-space bounding box for 3D Gaussians in spherical panoramas, addressing deformation caused by omnidirectional pixel distribution and non-linear spherical projection [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard affine rasterization by projecting a tangent-space bounding box onto the equirectangular plane, ensuring validity despite spherical distortion [S1]. It builds on [Spherical Panoramic Gaussian Splatting (SPaGS)](3042-spherical-panoramic-gaussian-splatting-spags.md) [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** 3D Gaussian splat parameters (mean $\mathbf{m}$ in camera space); spherical camera projection parameters (equirectangular image dimensions $W \times H$) [S1].
**Outputs:** Axis-aligned 2D bounding box $(p_{x,\min}, p_{y,\min}, p_{x,\max}, p_{y,\max})$ on the equirectangular image plane [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tangent Space Basis:** Three normalized vectors $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ (tangent), $\hat{\mathbf{b}}$ (bitangent), $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ (normal) representing the local x, y, z axes of the tangent space [S1].
*   **Tangent Space Bounding Box:** Four floats $(t_{x,\min}, t_{x,\max}, t_{y,\min}, t_{y,\max})$ defining the rectangle in the tangent plane $z=1$ [S1].
*   **Camera Space Coordinates:** Derived from tangent coordinates using transformation matrix [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Define Tangent Space:**
    For each splat, compute the normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ from the covariance matrix [S1]. Determine the reference vector $\mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}}$ to avoid singularity at poles: if the mean $\mathbf{m}$ lies on the vector of the two poles, use $\mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}} = (1, 0, 0)^T$, otherwise use $\mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}} = (0, 0, 1)^T$ [S1]. Compute $\hat{\mathbf{t}} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{r}_{\text{ref}} \times \hat{\mathbf{n}})$ and $\hat{\mathbf{b}} = \hat{\mathbf{n}} \times \hat{\mathbf{t}}$ [S1]. The origin of the tangent space is the camera space origin. The tangent plane is defined as $z=1$ [S1].

2.  **Transform to Tangent Space:**
    Construct the transformation matrix $M$ from camera space to tangent space:
    $$
    M = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{t}}^T \\ \hat{\mathbf{b}}^T \\ \hat{\mathbf{n}}^T \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Transform the splat into tangent space [S1].

3.  **Calculate Tangent Space Bounding Box:**
    Compute the 2D axis-aligned bounding box $(t_{x,\min}, t_{x,\max}, t_{y,\min}, t_{y,\max})$ on the tangent plane using the standard HTGS approach [HFW*25] on the transformed splat parameters [S1]. Let the box be represented by points $t = (t_x, t_y, 1)$ where $t_x \in [t_{x,\min}, t_{x,\max}]$ and $t_y \in [t_{y,\min}, t_{y,\max}]$ [S1].

4.  **Project to Camera Space:**
    Inverse the tangent space transformation to get camera space coordinates $(c_x, c_y, c_z)$ for any point on the tangent plane:
    $$
    \begin{bmatrix} c_x \\ c_y \\ c_z \end{bmatrix} = M^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} t_x \\ t_y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Specifically, $\mathbf{c} = t_x \hat{\mathbf{t}} + t_y \hat{\mathbf{b}} + 1 \hat{\mathbf{n}}$, where $\mathbf{c} = (c_x, c_y, c_z)^T$ is the point in camera space corresponding to $(t_x, t_y)$ on the tangent plane $z=1$ [S1].

5.  **Project to Equirectangular Plane (Stage 2):**
    Calculate the minimum and maximum $p_y$ (latitude) and $p_x$ (longitude) on the equirectangular plane [S1].
    
    *   **Latitude ($p_y$):**
        Using the monotonically increasing property of $\arcsin$, calculate $p_{y,\min}$ and $p_{y,\max}$ by evaluating the four corners of the tangent bounding box:
        $$
        p_y = \arcsin\left(\frac{c_y}{\sqrt{c_x^2 + c_y^2 + c_z^2}}\right)
        $$
        $$
        p_{y,\min} = \min_{(t_x, t_y) \in \text{corners}} \arcsin\left(\frac{c_y(t_x, t_y)}{\|\mathbf{c}(t_x, t_y)\|}\right)
        $$
        $$
        p_{y,\max} = \max_{(t_x, t_y) \in \text{corners}} \arcsin\left(\frac{c_y(t_x, t_y)}{\|\mathbf{c}(t_x, t_y)\|}\right)
        $$
        [S1]

    *   **Longitude ($p_x$):**
        To overcome the singularity and discontinuity of $\text{atan2}$, calculate the maximized differential angle $\Delta \phi$ instead of directly computing longitude [S1]. The final $p_{x,\min}$ and $p_{x,\max}$ are derived from the center longitude $\phi_c$ and $\Delta \phi$:
        $$
        p_{x,\min} = \phi_c - \Delta \phi, \quad p_{x,\max} = \phi_c + \Delta \phi
        $$
        [S1]

6.  **Handle Pole Coverage:**
    If the projected bounding box covers the north or south pole (determined by checking if the latitude range includes $\pm \pi/2$), calculate $p_{y,\min}$ and $p_{y,\max}$ similarly for the projection center on the respective sphere. Set $p_{x,\min} = -\pi$ and $p_{x,\max} = \pi$ (full image width) since the pole is fully covered [S1]. If the projection center lies exactly on a pole, use the same method with simplified expressions [S1].

7.  **Load Balancing:**
    Apply a load balancing scheme similar to Radl et al. [RSP*24] to address the impact of occasional large bounding boxes on Gaussian/tile instance creation [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import numpy as np

def tangent_plane_bounding_box(splat_mean, splat_cov, image_width, image_height):
    # Step 1: Define Tangent Space
    # Compute normal from covariance (principal component) [G]
    eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eigh(splat_cov)
    n_hat = eigenvectors[:, np.argmin(eigenvalues)]
    n_hat = n_hat / np.linalg.norm(n_hat)
    
    # Reference vector to avoid singularity [S1]
    ref_vec = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
    if abs(np.dot(splat_mean, ref_vec)) > 1 - 1e-6:
        ref_vec = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
        
    t_hat = np.cross(ref_vec, n_hat)
    t_hat = t_hat / np.linalg.norm(t_hat)
    b_hat = np.cross(n_hat, t_hat)
    
    # Step 2: Transform to Tangent Space [S1]
    M = np.array([t_hat, b_hat, n_hat])
    cov_t = M @ splat_cov @ M.T
    
    # Step 3: Calculate Tangent Space Bounding Box [S1]
    # Standard 2D Gaussian bounding box calculation (e.g., 3-sigma) [G]
    tx_min, tx_max = -3.0 * math.sqrt(cov_t[0, 0]), 3.0 * math.sqrt(cov_t[0, 0])
    ty_min, ty_max = -3.0 * math.sqrt(cov_t[1, 1]), 3.0 * math.sqrt(cov_t[1, 1])
    
    # Step 4 & 5: Project to Equirectangular Plane
    corners_t = [
        (tx_min, ty_min), (tx_min, ty_max),
        (tx_max, ty_min), (tx_max, ty_max)
    ]
    
    corners_c = []
    for tx, ty in corners_t:
        c = tx * t_hat + ty * b_hat + 1.0 * n_hat
        corners_c.append(c)
        
    # Calculate py_min, py_max [S1]
    py_values = []
    for c in corners_c:
        norm_c = np.linalg.norm(c)
        if norm_c == 0:
            continue
        py = math.asin(c[1] / norm_c)
        py_values.append(py)
        
    py_min = min(py_values)
    py_max = max(py_values)
    
    # Calculate px_min, px_max using differential angle [S1]
    mean_c = np.mean(corners_c, axis=0)
    norm_mean = np.linalg.norm(mean_c)
    if norm_mean == 0:
        phi_c = 0.0
    else:
        phi_c = math.atan2(mean_c[0], mean_c[2])
        
    # Compute max differential angle from corners [S1]
    px_values = []
    for c in corners_c:
        norm_c = np.linalg.norm(c)
        if norm_c == 0:
            continue
        px = math.atan2(c[0], c[2])
        px_values.append(px)
        
    # Handle wrap-around for px [G]
    px_values.sort()
    max_gap = 0
    for i in range(len(px_values) - 1):
        gap = px_values[i+1] - px_values[i]
        if gap > max_gap:
            max_gap = gap
            
    if max_gap > math.pi:
        px_min = -math.pi
        px_max = math.pi
    else:
        px_min = px_values[0]
        px_max = px_values[-1]
        
    # Step 6: Handle Pole Coverage [S1]
    if py_max > math.pi/2 - 1e-6 or py_min < -math.pi/2 + 1e-6:
        px_min = -math.pi
        px_max = math.pi
        
    return (px_min, py_min, px_max, py_max)
```

## Subtopics

### [Tight Bounding Box Calculation for Quadric Gaussians](9970-tight-bounding-box-calculation-for-quadric-gaussians.md)
Computes a tight 2D axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) for Quadratic Gaussian Splatting (QGS) primitives by approximating the primitive's boundary with tangent planes, avoiding the loose approximation of projecting 3D bounding box vertices. It addresses the inefficiency caused by QGS concave cases and the lack of an elementary inverse for the geodesic distance function, which makes analytical determination of the rendered portion difficult. It builds on [Tangent Plane-Based Bounding Box Calculation](3425-tangent-plane-based-bounding-box-calculation.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat, as it involves a fixed number of matrix operations and trigonometric functions [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat, storing only the bounding box coordinates [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles spherical projection distortion accurately; numerically stable at poles; avoids loose approximations of 3D bounding box projections [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of pole singularities; load balancing is necessary due to occasional large bounding boxes [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering of 3D Gaussians on spherical panoramas (e.g., VR, 360° video) where standard affine rasterization fails due to non-linear projection [S1].

## Background
Originated in the SPaGS paper [S1] to enable real-time rendering of 3D Gaussian splats on spherical panoramas [S1]. It addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS rasterization, which assumes a local affine approximation, by introducing a numerically stable ray-splat intersection approach suitable for arbitrary spherical camera models [S1].
