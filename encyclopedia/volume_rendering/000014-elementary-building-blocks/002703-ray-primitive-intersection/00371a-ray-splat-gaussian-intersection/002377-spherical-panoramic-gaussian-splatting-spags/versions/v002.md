# Spherical Panoramic Gaussian Splatting (SPaGS)

Also called: Fast and Accurate 3D Gaussian Splatting for Spherical Panoramas, HTGS extended to spherical camera model, SPaGS (Spherical Panoramic Gaussian Splatting), SPaGS Ray-Plane Intersection, omnidirectional Ray-Splat Intersection

## Overview
SPaGS is a method for real-time volume rendering of 3D Gaussian splats on spherical panoramas, specifically equirectangular images, supporting arbitrary spherical camera models and distorted panoramic inputs [S1]. It solves the problem of rendering artifacts and numerical instability inherent in standard 3DGS when applied to wide-field-of-view (FoV) spherical projections, where the first-order affine approximation fails [S1]. Unlike standard 3DGS which relies on local affine approximations, SPaGS replaces rasterization with a numerically stable ray-splat intersection approach using perpendicular planes, avoiding matrix inversion of the covariance [S1]. It differs from ODGS by providing better rendering quality and speed, and from 3DGUT by using ray-splat intersection instead of the Unscented Transform [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of $M$ 3D Gaussian splats representing the scene, and an equirectangular image of width $W$ and height $H$ [S1].
**Outputs:** A rendered equirectangular image with per-pixel color $C$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian splat $i$ is defined by: position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion), scale $s_{u,i}, s_{v,i}, s_{w,i} \in \mathbb{R}$, opacity $o_i \in \mathbb{R}$, and Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients of degree 3 for view-dependent color [S1]. The transformation from world to camera space is $M \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$, and the transformation from local splat coordinates to world space is $T \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$, defined by scaling, rotation, and position parameters [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Ray Direction Calculation:** For each pixel $(x, y)$ in the equirectangular image, compute the ray direction $d$ in camera space [S1]. The image plane is defined as the surface of a unit sphere in camera space, mapped to the equirectangular plane [S1]. The direction components are:
    $$ d_x = \cos\left(\frac{2\pi x}{W}\right) \cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{H}\right) $$
    $$ d_y = \sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{H}\right) $$
    $$ d_z = \sin\left(\frac{2\pi x}{W}\right) \cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{H}\right) $$
    [S1]

2.  **Plane Normal Calculation:** Compute the normals $n_x$ and $n_y$ of two perpendicular planes $\pi_x$ and $\pi_y$ that contain the ray direction $d$ [S1]. These planes pass through the camera origin $(0,0,0)^\top$ [S1]. The normals are computed directly in camera space [S1]:
    $$ n_y = \begin{pmatrix} -d_y \\ d_x \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} $$
    $$ n_x = \begin{pmatrix} -d_x d_z \\ -d_y d_z \\ d_x^2 + d_y^2 \end{pmatrix} $$
    [G] (Standard orthogonal basis construction for planes containing a vector and the origin)

3.  **Plane Representation:** Since the planes contain the camera origin, they are represented in homogeneous coordinates as $\pi_x = (n_x, 0)^\top$ and $\pi_y = (n_y, 0)^\top$ [S1].

4.  **Transformation to Splat Local Space:** Transform the planes from camera space into the local coordinate system of each 3D Gaussian using the world-to-camera matrix $M$ and the local-to-world matrix $T$ [S1]. The transformed planes are computed as $\pi_{local} = T^{-1} M^{-1} \pi_{camera}$ [G] (Standard homogeneous coordinate transformation for planes).

5.  **Ray-Splat Intersection:** Perform the intersection test in the splat-local coordinate system to avoid matrix inversion of the covariance $\Sigma_i$ and handle degenerate Gaussians numerically stably [S1]. Compute the squared distance $\rho_i(x)^2$ from the ray origin to the Gaussian center, and the intersection point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1]. The alpha value $\alpha_i$ is evaluated at $x$ using the Gaussian's covariance $\Sigma_i$ and opacity $o_i$ [S1]:
    $$ \alpha_i = o_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \rho_i(x)^2\right) $$
    [S1]

6.  **Hybrid Transparency Blending:** Split splats contributing to a pixel into a 'core' (first $K$ depth-sorted splats) and a 'tail' (remaining splats) [S1].
    - **Core:** Apply standard $\alpha$-blending for the first $K$ splats [S1].
    - **Tail:** Combine remaining splats' colors $c_i$ and opacities $\alpha_i$ without sorting to compute combined color $c_{tail}$ and remaining opacity $T_{tail}$ [S1].
    - **Final Color:** Compute the final pixel color as $C = c_{tail} + (1 - T_{tail}) c_{background}$, where $c_{background}$ is the background color [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $K$: Number of depth-sorted splats in the core [S1].
- SH Degree: 3 [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
- Degenerate 3D Gaussians cause numerical instability in standard covariance-based rasterization [S1]. SPaGS avoids this by using ray-splat intersection via perpendicular planes, which avoids matrix inversion [S1].
- Standard 3DGS uses a first-order affine approximation, leading to residual errors and artifacts in wide-FoV views [S1]. SPaGS avoids this by using exact ray-splat intersection [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Tangent Plane-Based Bounding Box Calculation](3425-tangent-plane-based-bounding-box-calculation.md)
A two-stage method to calculate an axis-aligned screen-space bounding box for 3D Gaussians in spherical panoramas, addressing deformation caused by omnidirectional pixel distribution and non-linear spherical projection. It sets itself apart from standard affine approximations by projecting a tangent-space bounding box onto the equirectangular plane, ensuring validity despite spherical distortion.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The ray-splat intersection approach is more computationally expensive than the affine approximation of standard 3DGS but is optimized for real-time rendering on spherical panoramas [S1]. The use of hybrid blending and bounding box culling accelerates the process [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians $M$ and the SH coefficients [S1]. Not given in the sources.
- **Performance:** Achieves real-time rates for spherical panoramas [S1]. It is faster and provides better quality than ODGS [S1].
- **Strengths:** Numerically stable for degenerate Gaussians [S1]. Handles arbitrary spherical camera models (e.g., equirectangular) [S1]. Avoids artifacts from affine approximations in wide-FoV views [S1]. Supports hybrid transparency blending for efficient rendering [S1].
- **Limitations:** More complex intersection logic than standard rasterization [S1]. Requires transformation of planes for each splat [S1].
- **When to use:** Real-time rendering of 3D Gaussian splats on spherical panoramas, specifically equirectangular images, supporting distorted panoramic inputs [S1].
- **When not to use:** Standard perspective cameras without distortion, where standard 3DGS affine approximation is sufficient and faster [S1].

## Background
SPaGS extends the approach of Hahlbohm et al. (HTGS) to a spherical camera model [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of standard 3DGS which only supports undistorted perspective camera models, restricting its application to 360° images [S1]. It is used for real-time rendering of 3D Gaussian splats on spherical panoramas [S1].
