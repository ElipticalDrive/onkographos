# Tetrahedron Circum-Center Calculation

Also called: Circum-center relative to vertex

## Overview
This method computes the center and radius of the unique sphere passing through the four vertices of a tetrahedron, known as the circum-sphere. It is primarily used in volume rendering for constructing bounding volumes (such as in TetSplat) or for quality metrics in isosurface extraction, where the circum-sphere approximates the local geometry. It differs from triangle circum-center calculation by operating in 3D space with four points rather than three, and provides a robust analytic solution based on coordinate differences to minimize numerical error.

## How it works
**Inputs:** Four vertices of a tetrahedron in $\mathbb{R}^3$, denoted as $a, b, c, d$.
**Outputs:** The circum-center relative to vertex $a$ ($m_{tet}$), the absolute circum-center ($C$), and the circum-radius ($R$).

**Data Structures:**
- Vertices $a, b, c, d$: 3D vectors $(x, y, z)$.
- Intermediate vectors: $u = b-a$, $v = c-a$, $w = d-a$.

**Steps:**
1. Compute vectors relative to vertex $a$:
   $$ u = b - a $$
   $$ v = c - a $$
   $$ w = d - a $$

2. Compute cross products required for the numerator terms. Define the helper function $A(u, v) = u \times v$:
   $$ term_1 = |w|^2 A(u, v) $$
   $$ term_2 = |v|^2 A(w, u) $$
   $$ term_3 = |u|^2 A(v, w) $$
   *Note: The source formula uses specific pairings. Based on [S2, unofficial], the terms are:*
   $$ T_1 = |d - a|^2 A(b, c) $$
   $$ T_2 = |c - a|^2 A(d, b) $$
   $$ T_3 = |b - a|^2 A(c, d) $$
   *Correction on notation from [S2, unofficial]:* The source defines $A(u, v) = (u - a) \times (v - a)$. Thus:
   $$ A(b, c) = (b - a) \times (c - a) = u \times v $$
   $$ A(d, b) = (d - a) \times (b - a) = w \times u $$
   $$ A(c, d) = (c - a) \times (d - a) = v \times w $$
   So the numerator vector $N$ is:
   $$ N = |w|^2 (u \times v) + |v|^2 (w \times u) + |u|^2 (v \times w) $$

3. Compute the denominator scalar $D$. The source [S2, unofficial] presents the denominator as a determinant-like structure involving components. Specifically, it is $2$ times the scalar triple product of the relative vectors, which corresponds to the determinant of the matrix formed by $u, v, w$.
   $$ D = 2 \det(u, v, w) = 2 (u \cdot (v \times w)) $$
   *Note on Source [S2, unofficial] Formula:* The source writes the denominator as a determinant of components:
   $$ \det \begin{bmatrix} [u]_x & [u]_y & [u]_z \\ [v]_x & [v]_y & [v]_z \\ [w]_x & [w]_y & [w]_z \end{bmatrix} $$
   This is equivalent to the scalar triple product $u \cdot (v \times w)$.

4. Calculate the relative circum-center $m_{tet}$:
   $$ m_{tet} = \frac{N}{D} $$

5. Calculate the absolute circum-center $C$ and radius $R$:
   $$ C = a + m_{tet} $$
   $$ R = |m_{tet}| $$

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
- **Degeneracy:** The method is unstable if the denominator $D$ is close to zero. This occurs when the tetrahedron is nearly degenerate (flat or needle-shaped), meaning the four vertices are coplanar or nearly so. [S2, unofficial]
- **Numerical Precision:** The formula relies on differences between coordinates ($b-a$, etc.) rather than absolute coordinates. This reduces relative error because vertices are typically closer to each other than to the origin. [S2, unofficial]
- **Slivers:** Tetrahedra with two large dihedral angles ("slivers") can cause instability and may require subdivision. [S2, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def tetrahedron_circum_center(a, b, c, d):
    """
    Computes the circum-center and radius of a tetrahedron defined by vertices a, b, c, d.
    Returns (center, radius).
    """
    # Relative vectors
    u = b - a
    v = c - a
    w = d - a
    
    # Squared lengths
    len_u_sq = dot(u, u)
    len_v_sq = dot(v, v)
    len_w_sq = dot(w, w)
    
    # Cross products A(u,v) = u x v, etc.
    # Note: Source [S2, unofficial] defines A(u,v) = (u-a)x(v-a). Here u,v,w are already relative to a.
    cross_uv = cross(u, v)
    cross_wu = cross(w, u)
    cross_vw = cross(v, w)
    
    # Numerator vector N
    # Formula: |d-a|^2 A(b,c) + |c-a|^2 A(d,b) + |b-a|^2 A(c,d)
    # Mapping to u,v,w:
    # A(b,c) -> u x v
    # A(d,b) -> w x u
    # A(c,d) -> v x w
    N = (len_w_sq * cross_uv) + (len_v_sq * cross_wu) + (len_u_sq * cross_vw)
    
    # Denominator D = 2 * det(u, v, w) = 2 * (u . (v x w))
    # We can compute v x w directly or use the scalar triple product
    cross_vw_direct = cross(v, w)
    D = 2.0 * dot(u, cross_vw_direct)
    
    # Check for degeneracy
    if abs(D) < 1e-12: # [G] Threshold for numerical stability
        raise ValueError("Tetrahedron is degenerate or near-degenerate")
        
    # Relative circum-center
    m_tet = N / D
    
    # Absolute circum-center and radius
    center = a + m_tet
    radius = length(m_tet)
    
    return center, radius
```

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ constant time operations involving basic vector arithmetic (dot products, cross products).
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary space.
- **Strengths:** Analytic solution avoids iterative approximation. Using coordinate differences minimizes floating-point relative error for vertices far from the origin. [S2, unofficial]
- **Limitations:** Numerically unstable for degenerate tetrahedra (coplanar vertices). [S2, unofficial]

## Background
The method is described in the context of "TetSplat," a technique for real-time rendering and volume clipping of large volumetric datasets. The circum-sphere of a tetrahedron is used to define bounding volumes for the tetrahedral elements. [S2, unofficial] The quality of such approximations is often measured by the radius error, defined as the deviation of vertex distances from the computed center relative to the radius. [S1, unofficial]
