# Bounding Volume {B}
Also called: AO Bounding Polyhedron, Polyhedral Bounding Volume for AO

## Overview
The Bounding Volume {B} defines the spatial extent of the ambient occlusion volume (AOV) generated by a convex polygon $P$ using the Convex Polygon Falloff Function. It solves the problem of efficiently rasterizing the region where occlusion is non-zero by constructing a closed polyhedron $V$ bounded by $k+2$ planes. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by explicitly computing the displacement vectors required to extrude the polygon's vertices into a 3D volume suitable for depth-buffer-based occlusion queries.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Convex polygon $P$ with $k$ vertices $\{\vec{p}_0, \dots, \vec{p}_{k-1}\}$, no three collinear.
- Maximum occlusion distance $\delta$.
- Edge normals $\hat{m}_i$ for $i \in [0, k-1]$, pointing inward relative to the polygon projection.
- Face normal $\hat{m}_k$ (normal to the plane containing $P$).

**Outputs:**
- A polyhedron $V$ defined by $2k$ vertices $\{\vec{v}_0, \dots, \vec{v}_{2k-1}\}$.
- The bounding planes $\{B_0, \dots, B_{k+1}\}$.

**Data Structures:**
- **Vertices:** Array of $2k$ 3D vectors. Indices $0$ to $k-1$ lie in the plane of $P$. Indices $k$ to $2k-1$ are displaced along $\hat{m}_k$.
- **Normals:** Array of $k+2$ 3D unit vectors. $\hat{m}_0 \dots \hat{m}_{k-1}$ are edge normals; $\hat{m}_k$ is the face normal; $\hat{m}_{k+1}$ is the normal for the "above" plane.

**Steps:**

1.  **Define Plane Normals and Reference Points:**
    - Set $\hat{m}_0, \dots, \hat{m}_{k-1}$ as the inward-facing normals of the polygon edges.
    - Set $\hat{m}_k$ as the unit normal to the plane containing $P$.
    - Define $\vec{p}_k = \vec{p}_0$.
    - Define $\vec{p}_{k+1} = \vec{p}_0 + \delta \hat{m}_{k+1}$, where $\hat{m}_{k+1}$ is the normal of the plane $B_{k+1}$ containing $P$ (typically $\hat{m}_{k+1} = \hat{m}_k$ or $-\hat{m}_k$ depending on orientation; the source defines $B_{k+1}$ as containing $P$). Note: The source text implies $B_{k+1}$ is the plane *containing* $P$, so its normal is $\hat{m}_k$. The plane $B_k$ is "above" $P$.

2.  **Compute Extension Vectors $\vec{e}_i$ for Base Vertices ($0 \le i < k$):**
    The extension vector $\vec{e}_i$ displaces polygon vertex $\vec{p}_i$ to volume vertex $\vec{v}_i$. It is constrained by the geometry of the bounding planes:
    $$ \vec{e}_i \cdot -\hat{m}_i = \delta \quad (9) $$
    $$ \vec{e}_i \cdot -\hat{m}_{(i-1+k) \bmod k} = \delta \quad (10) $$
    $$ \vec{e}_i \cdot \hat{m}_k = 0 \quad (11) $$
    
    Solve for $\vec{e}_i$ using the linear system:
    $$ \vec{e}_i = \begin{bmatrix} -\hat{m}_i \\ -\hat{m}_{(i-1+k) \bmod k} \\ \hat{m}_k \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \delta \\ \delta \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad (12) $$
    *Note: The source formula (12) writes the matrix with positive normals and negative RHS. Mathematically equivalent to solving $M \vec{e} = \vec{b}$ where rows are $-\hat{m}_i$, etc.*

3.  **Clamp Extension Vectors:**
    To prevent sliver polygons from creating excessively long volumes with negligible occlusion, clamp the magnitude of each extension vector:
    $$ \|\vec{e}_i\| = \min(\|\vec{e}_i\|, 2\delta) \quad \text{for all } i < k $$
    Normalize $\vec{e}_i$ if clamped, or simply scale it to length $2\delta$ while preserving direction. [G] Ensure the direction is preserved by $\vec{e}_i \leftarrow \vec{e}_i \cdot \min(1, \frac{2\delta}{\|\vec{e}_i\|})$.

4.  **Compute Top Extension Vectors ($k \le i < 2k$):**
    The vertices in the "top" plane are displaced from the base vertices along the face normal:
    $$ \vec{e}_i = \vec{e}_{i-k} - \delta \hat{m}_k \quad (13) $$
    *Note: The source uses $-\delta \hat{m}_k$. This assumes $\hat{m}_k$ points "up" relative to the viewer or light direction such that the volume extends in the negative normal direction for the top cap. Verify orientation consistency with the specific rendering pipeline.*

5.  **Construct Volume Vertices:**
    - For $0 \le i < k$: $\vec{v}_i = \vec{p}_i + \vec{e}_i$.
    - For $k \le i < 2k$: $\vec{v}_i = \vec{p}_{i-k} + \vec{e}_i$.

6.  **Define Bounding Planes:**
    - Planes $B_0, \dots, B_{k-1}$: Correspond to polygon edges. Normal $\hat{m}_i$. Offset determined by $\delta$.
    - Plane $B_k$: Above $P$. Normal $\hat{m}_k$ (or $-\hat{m}_k$).
    - Plane $B_{k+1}$: Contains $P$. Normal $\hat{m}_k$.

**Simplified Falloff Evaluation:**
When evaluating the falloff function $g$ for a point at the origin (as derived in the parent article), the formula simplifies to:
$$ g(\vec{0}) = \bar{\alpha} \prod_{i=0}^k \max(0, \min(1, 1 - \vec{p}_{i \bmod k} \cdot \hat{m}_i / \delta)) \quad (8) $$
Here, $\bar{\alpha}$ is the average alpha of the polygon (for masked polygons).

**Pitfalls:**
- **Sliver Polygons:** Thin polygons produce long, thin volumes that are computationally expensive to rasterize but contribute little occlusion. The clamp $\|\vec{e}_i\| \le 2\delta$ is critical.
- **Orientation:** Ensure $\hat{m}_i$ are inward-facing. Incorrect normals invert the volume or create empty intersections.
- **Precision:** Solving the $3 \times 3$ linear system for $\vec{e}_i$ may suffer from numerical instability if the polygon is nearly degenerate (collinear vertices). The "no three collinear" constraint helps, but near-collinearity should be checked.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(k)$ to compute the $2k$ vertices and normals, where $k$ is the number of polygon vertices. The linear system solve is constant time per vertex.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(k)$ to store the vertices and normals.
- **Strengths:** Provides a tight, convex bounding volume suitable for efficient rasterization-based occlusion culling. Handles arbitrary convex polygons.
- **Limitations:** Performance degrades for high-$k$ polygons. Sliver polygons require clamping, which introduces approximation errors. Not suitable for non-convex polygons without decomposition.
- **When to use:** When using rasterization to compute ambient occlusion volumes for convex polygonal geometry.
- **When not to use:** For non-convex shapes, or when ray-tracing is preferred over rasterization for occlusion queries.

## Background
The Bounding Volume {B} method was introduced by Matt McGuire in the 2010 paper "Ambient Occlusion Volumes" [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to enable efficient rasterization-based ambient occlusion for complex geometry, particularly foliage and fences, by converting polygonal occluders into volumetric primitives that can be processed by standard graphics pipelines. The method relies on the Convex Polygon Falloff Function to define the occlusion intensity within the volume.
