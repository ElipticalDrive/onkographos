# Analytical AABB Fog Feathering via Case Classification

Also called: Analytical AABB Fog Feathering, Case-Based Fog Integration

## Overview
This method computes the optical density (fog amount) for a ray intersecting an Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) with edge feathering, where fog density increases linearly from zero at the boundary to a constant value in the interior. It solves the problem of analytically integrating this variable density profile by classifying the ray's geometric relationship to the box into specific cases (miss, parallel pass, corner entry, etc.) and summing their contributions. It differs from standard ray-box intersection by returning a scalar fog value rather than just hit distances, and from ray marching by attempting to provide a closed-form solution, though it is noted to be complex due to numerous corner cases.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origin $O$ and direction $D$ (normalized).
- AABB defined by min corner $B_{min}$ and max corner $B_{max}$.
- Feather parameter $f$: the distance from the AABB boundary into the volume over which fog density ramps from 0 to 1.

**Outputs:**
- Fog amount (optical density) $F$.

**Data Structures:**
- The AABB is treated as a 3D box.
- The ray is parameterized as $R(t) = O + tD$.

**Steps:**
1. **Compute Ray-Box Intersection:**
   Calculate the intersection of the ray with the AABB. This involves finding the entry and exit distances $t_{entry}$ and $t_{exit}$. If the ray misses the box, compute the closest distance $d_{miss}$ from the ray to the box and the parameter $t_{closest}$ where this minimum occurs [S1, unofficial].

2. **Classify Case:**
   Use the intersection results ($t_{entry}, t_{exit}$) or the miss parameters ($d_{miss}, t_{closest}$) to determine which geometric case applies. The source identifies the following primary cases [S1, unofficial]:
   - **Miss:** Ray does not intersect the box.
   - **Parallel Pass:** Ray runs parallel to the constant fog region, passing only through the non-constant (feathered) region.
   - **Corner Intersection:** Ray passes through the non-constant density region at a corner of the bounding region.
   - **Partial Entry:** Fragment is partially inside the non-constant fog.
   - **Full Entry:** Ray goes completely from zero to constant fog density.
   - **Start Inside:** Ray starts inside the non-constant region.

3. **Compute Fog Contribution:**
   Calculate the fog value $F$ based on the classified case. The total fog is the sum of contributions from the relevant geometric segments [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The fog density $\rho(t)$ is assumed to increase linearly from 0 to $\rho_{max}$ over the feather distance $f$. The optical density is the integral of $\rho(t)$ along the ray path.

- **Case: Full Entry (Zero to Constant):**
  If the view ray goes completely from zero density to constant density, the integrated fog value is directly related to the constant fog value by a factor of $1/2$ [S1, unofficial].
  $$ F = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{ConstantFogValue} $$
  This holds because the area under the linear ramp is half the area of the corresponding rectangle. The angle of incidence does not change this ratio, as the rate of change remains constant relative to the path length [S1, unofficial].

- **Case: Partial Entry:**
  If a fragment is partially inside the non-constant fog, the value is related by a ratio of the distance between the pixel and the edge of the constant region. For example, if the pixel is halfway through the non-constant fog, the value is $1/2$ of $1/2$, or $1/4$ of the full constant fog value [S1, unofficial].
  $$ F = \text{Ratio} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{ConstantFogValue} $$
  Where $\text{Ratio}$ is the fraction of the feather distance traversed.

- **Case: Parallel Pass (Camera Outside):**
  If the camera is outside the fog and the view vector is parallel to the constant fog region, passing only through the non-constant fog, the non-constant fog is essentially constant but at a lowered density. This lowered density is related to the ratio of the distance from the constant density fog [S1, unofficial]. The region has a changing section that increases until the lowered density, then remains constant until it saturates or exits through another changing region.

- **Case: Corner Intersection (Zero Density Region):**
  If the view ray passes through the non-constant density region at a corner of the bounding region and the fragment is in a zero density region, the fog calculation equals the constant density region value. The constant density is related to the distance of the constant bounding region from the view ray (line-to-box distance). It equals the constant calculation because it passes through an increasing section and a decreasing section of equal size. Thus, it is $2 \times 1/2$ of the constant value, effectively equaling the constant value [S1, unofficial].

- **Case: Miss:**
  For misses, the method returns the distance of the ray from the box and a time $T$ along the ray where the ray is closest to the box or intersects it. The fog value is derived from these parameters, likely involving the distance $d_{miss}$ relative to the feather distance $f$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The method involves numerous corner cases (parallel passes, corner intersections, starting inside, etc.), making it complex and error-prone [S1, unofficial].
- Precision issues may arise when the ray is nearly parallel to the box faces or when the feather distance is very small relative to the box size.
- The source notes that due to the high number of corner cases, switching to a ray-marched implementation is recommended as a more attractive solution [S1, unofficial].

The foundational geometric primitive used to determine entry/exit points or miss distances. [Ray-box intersection](5308-ray-box-intersection.md)

### Ray Marching
An alternative method for computing fog in volumes, recommended by the source due to the complexity of the analytical case classification. [Ray Marching](5300-ray-marching.md)

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ for the analytical calculation, assuming constant-time geometric primitives. However, the constant factor is high due to the numerous case checks.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only a few scalar variables for intermediate calculations.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling:** Does not scale with resolution or sample count, as it is a single evaluation per ray.
- **Strengths:** Provides an exact analytical solution without sampling noise.
- **Limitations:** Extremely complex to implement correctly due to the many corner cases. The source explicitly states that a ray-marched implementation is often more attractive [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When a noise-free, analytical fog solution is required and the implementation complexity can be managed.
- **When not to use:** When performance is critical and the overhead of case classification is prohibitive, or when the volume shape is complex and ray marching is already in use.

## Background
This method originates from a discussion on Computer Graphics Stack Exchange regarding numerically integratable fog volume feathering along a ray. The author, pmw1234, sought an analytical approach to compute fog optical length with edge feathering on an AABB, similar to Inigo Quilez's height fog but for a box volume. The method was proposed as a theoretical solution but was ultimately deemed too complex for practical implementation compared to ray marching [S1, unofficial].
