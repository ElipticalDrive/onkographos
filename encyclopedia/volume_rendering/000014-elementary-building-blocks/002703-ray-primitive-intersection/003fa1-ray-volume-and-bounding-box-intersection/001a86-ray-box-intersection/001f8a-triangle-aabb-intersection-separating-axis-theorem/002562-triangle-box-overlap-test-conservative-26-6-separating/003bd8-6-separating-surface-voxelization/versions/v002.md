# 6-Separating Surface Voxelization

Also called: Thinner Connected Voxelization, Thinner Surface Voxelization

## Overview
6-Separating Surface Voxelization is a surface voxelization criterion that produces a "thinner" set of voxels compared to conservative voxelization, while remaining gap-free and 26-connected [S1, S2]. Unlike conservative voxelization, which is 26-separating (and thus 6-connected), this method produces a voxel set that is 6-separating (and hence 26-connected) [S1]. It is derived by adapting the triangle/box overlap test used in conservative voxelization [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Triangle vertices $\mathbf{v}_0, \mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$ and voxel grid spacing $\Delta \mathbf{p}$ [G].
**Outputs:** Set of voxels intersected by the triangle satisfying the 6-separating criterion [G].

**Data Structures:** Standard triangle representation (3 vertices) and voxel grid metadata (spacing, bounds) [G].

**Steps:**
1. **Compute Normal & Dominant Axis:** Calculate the triangle normal $\mathbf{n} = (\mathbf{v}_1 - \mathbf{v}_0) \times (\mathbf{v}_2 - \mathbf{v}_0)$ and normalize it. Identify the dominant axis $k \in \{x, y, z\}$ that maximizes the absolute normal component: $k = \arg \max_{c \in \{x,y,z\}} |n_c|$ [S1]. This optimization reduces the number of projection tests required [S1].
2. **Plane Test (6-Separating):** Determine if the triangle intersects the voxel slab along the dominant axis. The offsets for the plane test are modified from the conservative case [S1]:
   $$d_1 = \langle \mathbf{n}, \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{v}_0 \rangle + \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{p}_{\perp} |\mathbf{n}_{\perp}|$$
   $$d_2 = \langle \mathbf{n}, \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{v}_0 \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{p}_{\perp} |\mathbf{n}_{\perp}|$$
   where $\Delta \mathbf{p}_{\perp}$ and $\mathbf{n}_{\perp}$ denote the components of $\Delta \mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{n}$ orthogonal to the dominant axis $k$ [G]. The sign selection depends on the axis that maximizes the offset magnitude [S1].
3. **2D Projection Tests:** For the two axes perpendicular to the dominant axis, perform 2D projection overlap tests. Adopt Huang et al.'s criterion for 4-separating line pixelization [S1]. For an edge $e_i$ projected onto the perpendicular plane, the offsets are:
   $$de^{\perp}_i = \langle \mathbf{n}_{e_i}^{\perp}, \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{p}_{\perp} - \mathbf{v}_{i,\perp} \rangle + \frac{1}{2} \Delta \mathbf{p}_{e_i}^{\perp} |\mathbf{n}_{e_i}^{\perp}|$$
   where the selection is based on $\arg \max |\mathbf{n}_{e_i}^{\perp}|$ within the projection plane [S1].
4. **Voxel Selection:** Mark voxels that satisfy the 6-separating plane test and the 2D projection tests. This results in a thinner set than conservative voxelization while maintaining connectivity [S2].

    k = argmax(abs(n))  # dominant axis index (0, 1, or 2)

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Triangles:** If the triangle area is zero, the normal is undefined. Handle by skipping or treating as a line/point [G].
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision issues may arise in the dot products and comparisons. Use epsilon values for comparisons if necessary [G].
*   **Dominant Axis Selection:** If two components of the normal are equal in magnitude, the choice of dominant axis may affect the result slightly, but the method should remain valid [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The use of the dominant axis optimization reduces the number of projection tests [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The method produces fewer voxels than conservative voxelization [S2].
*   **Performance:** Faster than previous conservative surface voxelization solutions, up to one order of magnitude [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Produces a gap-free, connected surface voxelization that is thinner than conservative voxelization, reducing memory usage and potentially improving rendering quality for thin surfaces [S2].
*   **Limitations:** May not be suitable for applications requiring strict 6-connectivity (e.g., certain morphological operations) as it produces 26-connected voxels [S1].

## Background
6-Separating Surface Voxelization was introduced by Michael Schwarz et al. in their 2010 paper "Fast Parallel Surface and Solid Voxelization on GPUs" [S1, S2]. It was developed to address the high memory consumption of conservative voxelization while maintaining connectivity and gap-free properties [S2]. The method adapts the triangle/box overlap test from conservative voxelization by using criteria from Huang et al. for plane and line voxelization [S1]. It is used in GPU-based voxelization pipelines [S2].
