# Homogeneous Media

Also called: Homogeneous Medium Density Integration, Homogeneous Volume, Homogeneous volume representation, Uniform Participating Medium

## Overview
Homogeneous Media is a volumetric representation where the participating medium has constant optical properties (density, absorption, scattering) throughout its entire volume [S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of analytically integrating the density along a ray segment, allowing for closed-form solutions for transmittance and density accumulation without numerical quadrature [S3, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its siblings by its assumption of spatial uniformity, which enables exact integration of the density term $P(x)$, whereas [Absorption Term Restriction](1521-absorption-term-restriction.md) restricts absorption terms and [Particle Model](2005-particle-model.md) / [Point-Sampled Spectrum Model](3468-point-sampled-spectrum-model.md) rely on discrete sampling or spectral point representations rather than continuous uniform fields [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Camera origin $c$ (3D vector).
- Ray direction $\omega$ (normalized 3D vector).
- Intersection point $x$ on the scene geometry (3D vector).
- Constant density factor $\varrho'$ (scalar, $\varrho' \ge 0$) [S3, unofficial].
- Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ (scalar), where $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$ [S1, unofficial]. $\sigma_a$ is absorption, $\sigma_s$ is scattering [S1, unofficial].
- Geometric boundary object defining the volume extent [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Transmittance $T_r(c, x)$ from camera to intersection point [S1, unofficial].
- Integrated density $P(x_{2D})$ along the ray segment [S3, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The volume is represented by a class `HomogeneousVolume` which inherits from `Object` [S2, unofficial].
- **Fields:**
  - `Object *boundary_`: Pointer to the enclosing geometric boundary (e.g., a mesh or primitive) [S2, unofficial].
  - `double density_`: The constant density value $\varrho'$ used in intersection and scattering calculations [S2, unofficial].
  - `Material *material_`: Associated material properties (implied by constructor signature in [S2, unofficial], though specific fields for $\sigma_a/\sigma_s$ are not detailed in the provided snippets, they are referenced in [S1, unofficial] as $\sigma_a, \sigma_s$).
- **Constructor:** `HomogeneousVolume(Object *boundary, double density, Material *material)` [S2, unofficial].

### Formulas
1. **Integrated Density:** For a homogeneous medium, the particle density $\varrho(x) = \varrho'$ is constant. The integrated density $P$ along a line segment from camera $c$ to point $x$ is:
   $$ P(\mathbf{x}_{2D}) = \int_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathbf{x}} \varrho(\mathbf{x}') \, d\mathbf{x}' = \varrho' \cdot D $$
   where $D = \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c} \|$ is the Euclidean distance between the camera and the intersection point [S3, unofficial].

2. **Transmittance:** The transmittance $T_r$ from point $x$ to $x_t$ (or from camera to $x$) is:
   $$ T_r(x, x_t) = \exp(-\sigma_t t) $$
   where $t$ is the distance between $x$ and $x_t$, and $\sigma_t$ is the extinction factor [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
- $\varrho'$: Constant density factor, $\varrho' \ge 0$ [S3, unofficial].
- $\sigma_a$: Absorption factor [S1, unofficial].
- $\sigma_s$: Scattering factor [S1, unofficial].
- $\sigma_t$: Extinction factor, $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$ [S1, unofficial].
- `density_`: The value of $\varrho'$ stored in the volume object [S2, unofficial].

### Steps
1. **Ray-Volume Intersection:** Determine if the ray intersects the `boundary_` geometry. If it does, find the entry point $x_{entry}$ and exit point $x_{exit}$ (or the first intersection $x$ if the ray starts inside or outside) [S2, unofficial].
2. **Distance Calculation:** Compute the distance $D$ from the camera origin $c$ to the intersection point $x$ along the ray direction $\omega$: $D = \| x - c \|$ [S3, unofficial].
3. **Density Integration:** Calculate the integrated density $P$ using the constant density factor $\varrho'$: $P = \varrho' \cdot D$ [S3, unofficial].
4. **Transmittance Calculation:** Compute the transmittance from the camera to the intersection point using the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$: $T_r = \exp(-\sigma_t \cdot D)$ [S1, unofficial].
5. **Scattering/Rendering:** Use the transmittance and density to modulate the radiance contribution from the medium (e.g., in the emission-absorption model) [S1, S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
import math

class HomogeneousVolume:
    def __init__(self, boundary, density, material):
        """
        boundary: Object with intersect method
        density: float, constant density factor rho'
        material: Material object with sigma_a, sigma_s attributes
        """
        self.boundary = boundary
        self.density = density
        self.material = material

    def compute_transmittance(self, camera_origin, intersection_point):
        """
        Computes transmittance T_r from camera to intersection point.
        Returns: float transmittance
        """
        # Calculate distance D
        dx = intersection_point.x - camera_origin.x
        dy = intersection_point.y - camera_origin.y
        dz = intersection_point.z - camera_origin.z
        D = math.sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy + dz*dz)
        
        # Get extinction coefficient sigma_t
        sigma_t = self.material.sigma_a + self.material.sigma_s
        
        # Compute transmittance T_r = exp(-sigma_t * t)
        transmittance = math.exp(-sigma_t * D)
        
        return transmittance

    def compute_integrated_density(self, camera_origin, intersection_point):
        """
        Computes integrated density P for homogeneous medium.
        Returns: float integrated density P
        """
        # Calculate distance D
        dx = intersection_point.x - camera_origin.x
        dy = intersection_point.y - camera_origin.y
        dz = intersection_point.z - camera_origin.z
        D = math.sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy + dz*dz)
        
        # P = rho' * D
        P = self.density * D
        
        return P

    def intersect(self, ray_origin, ray_direction):
        """
        Intersects ray with the volume boundary.
        Returns: Hit object or None
        """
        # Delegate to boundary object's intersection logic
        # This is a placeholder for the specific intersection algorithm
        # of the boundary object (e.g., triangle mesh, sphere)
        hit = self.boundary.intersect(ray_origin, ray_direction)
        return hit
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Zero Distance:** If the camera is at the intersection point ($D=0$), transmittance is 1.0 and integrated density is 0.0.
- **Boundary Definition:** The volume is bounded by `boundary_`. If the ray does not intersect the boundary, the medium is not sampled [S2, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Exponential decay can lead to underflow for large $\sigma_t \cdot D$. Standard floating-point handling applies [S1, unofficial].
- **Uniformity Assumption:** This method fails if the medium is not homogeneous. For non-uniform media, numerical integration or other models (e.g., Exponential Medium [S3, unofficial]) are required.

## Subtopics

### Exponential Medium
A medium where density follows an exponential function, allowing analytical integration for altitude-dependent densities. Chose when density varies exponentially with height [S3, unofficial].

### Absorption Term Restriction
Restricts absorption terms for performance or stability. Chose when absorption is negligible or simplified [S1, unofficial].

### Particle Model
Represents medium as discrete particles. Chose for sparse media or when particle-based scattering is required [S2, unofficial].

### Point-Sampled Spectrum Model
Uses point samples for spectral rendering. Chose for spectral accuracy in participating media [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ for transmittance and density calculation given the intersection point, as it involves only basic arithmetic and an exponential function [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per volume instance, storing only boundary pointer, density, and material [S2, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Very fast due to closed-form solutions. No iterative sampling or numerical integration required [S3, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Exact integration, simple implementation, low computational cost [S3, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Only applicable to uniform media. Cannot represent complex density variations [S3, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** Scenes with uniform fog, smoke, or clouds where density is constant [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** Scenes with spatially varying density, such as atmospheric gradients or volumetric textures [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The homogeneous medium model is a fundamental concept in volume rendering, often introduced as the simplest case of participating media [S3, unofficial]. It is widely used in educational contexts and real-time rendering for its simplicity and analytical tractability [S1, S3, unofficial]. The model is derived from the radiative transfer equation, assuming constant extinction coefficient [S1, unofficial].
