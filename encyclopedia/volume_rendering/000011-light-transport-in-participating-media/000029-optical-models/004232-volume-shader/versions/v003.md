# Volume Shader

Also called: Participating Media Material, Principled Volume, Pyro Volume presets, Redshift Standard Volume shader, Standard Volume Material, Volume Material

## Overview
A volume shader is a material definition for participating media that occupies space, instructing the render engine how light interacts with the medium at every point within a volume [S1][S2, unofficial][S3]. It maps artistic parameters (Scattering, Absorption, Emission) to physical interactions, serving as the basis for implementations like the [Standard Volume Shader Implementation](10712-standard-volume-shader-implementation.md) and the [Volumetric Scattering Model](11410-volumetric-scattering-model.md) [S2, unofficial][S3].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Ray position, ray direction, and spatial data (density, coefficients) at the current position [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Output:** Outgoing radiance contributed by the medium at that point [S2, unofficial][S3].

**Data Structures**
The shader defines three primary ingredients per spatial point:
1.  **Scattering:** The volume’s counterpart to a diffuse color, defining how much light bounces off microscopic particles and in which color (e.g., white for clouds, grey for smoke) [S2, unofficial][S3].
2.  **Absorption:** The counterpart to transparency, defining how much light the medium swallows (e.g., making thick smoke dark) [S2, unofficial][S3].
3.  **Emission:** The counterpart to incandescence, letting the medium give off light of its own (e.g., fire and explosions) [S2, unofficial][S3].

**Formulas**

**Parameters**
*   **Density:** Multiplies the channel grid value, setting the overall thick or thin character of the medium [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Scatter Color & Scale:** Sets what the volume reflects and how brightly [S2, unofficial].
*   **Absorption:** Defines the darkness or opacity of the volume [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Emission:** Defines self-illumination [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Anisotropy Mode:** Chooses the phase function: 'Henyey-Greenstein' (slider -1 to +1, all-rounder for isotropic smoke) or 'Jendersie-d’Eon' (cloud specialist modeling light through water droplets) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Transparency Color & Depth:** Implements per-wavelength travel distances [S2, unofficial]. Measured in centimeters, set in relation to the real-world size of the volume [S2, unofficial]. Darker means thicker, brighter means thinner, and any color tints the volume with its opposite at depth [S2, unofficial].
*   **Emission Channel & Mode:** Points to the temperature grid for fire/explosions. 'Blackbody Mode' converts temperatures into physically correct colors on the Kelvin scale (deep red around 3,000 K to white/blue-white beyond 9,000 K). 'Color Mode' uses a classic black-to-red-to-yellow flame gradient for art-directed fire [S2, unofficial].
*   **Velocity Field:** Input for motion blur in VDB sequences [S2, unofficial]. Values far beyond 1,000 are normal [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Grid Name Case Sensitivity:** The volume renders black or invisible if the grid name in the channel field is misspelled or has the wrong case, or if no light has volume contribution above zero [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Insufficient Samples:** The volume looks noisy if light samples are insufficient [S2, unofficial]. Lights dominate volume noise; scattering-heavy media need more samples than absorption-heavy ones [S2, unofficial].
*   **Low Resolution:** The volume looks blocky if simulation resolution is too low [S2, unofficial]. Shader remapping can soften it, but higher resolution cache is required to truly fix it [S2, unofficial].
*   **VRAM Limits:** The render aborts with an out of VRAM error if the VDB does not fit in GPU memory [S2, unofficial]. Volumes are excluded from out-of-core systems [S2, unofficial].
*   **Flat Clouds:** The cloud looks flat or grey if Volume Trace Depth is too low [S2, unofficial]. Fix by raising depth with Brute Force GI, keeping Extinction Falloff at 0.2, and lowering Shadow Density Scale [S2, unofficial].
*   **Washed Out Fog:** Fog washes out the frame if Environment Scattering value is too high [S2, unofficial]. Reduce drastically; even small values are powerful [S2, unofficial].
*   **Benchmark Confusion:** Do not confuse the volume shader material with 'Volume Shader BM', which is a GPU stress test benchmark, not a material [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Standard Volume Shader Implementation](10712-standard-volume-shader-implementation.md)
The Standard Volume Shader is a material definition for participating media that simulates light transport in heterogeneous environments where density varies per voxel, such as smoke, fog, or clouds. It distinguishes itself from subsurface scattering by applying random walk logic to non-polygonal, spatially varying density fields rather than uniform, bounded surfaces. It separates the geometric distribution of particles from their optical properties, using a scalar density grid for occupancy and distinct color channels for scattering albedo and wavelength-dependent absorption.

### [Volumetric Scattering Model](11410-volumetric-scattering-model.md)
Volumetric scattering models the interaction of light with participating media—substances like fog, smoke, clouds, and translucent solids (e.g., wax, skin) where the space itself participates in lighting. Unlike surface rendering, where radiance is constant along a ray between surfaces, participating media modify radiance through emission, absorption, out-scattering, and in-scattering. This method distinguishes itself by explicitly modeling the transport of light through volume using the radiative transfer equation, decomposed into extinction (absorption + out-scattering) and in-scattering terms, rather than approximating volume effects via surface shaders or simple opacity masks.

### World Volume
Applying a volume shader to the world to fill the entire space [S1]. Most useful for night time or other dark scenes [S1]. Assumes the background is infinitely far away, which is inaccurate for modeling effects like fog or atmospheric scattering because most distance between light sources (e.g., sun) and objects is empty space; for such effects, a volume object surrounding the scene is better [S1].

### Volume Absorption
Used to shade black smoke, colored glass objects, or mixed with Volume Scatter [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Provides a unified framework for defining complex volumetric behaviors (clouds, smoke, fire) through three intuitive parameters [S1][S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Limitations:** Requires gathering data at many positions within the volume for realistic rendering, which can be computationally expensive [S1][S2, unofficial][S3].

## Background
The concept of the volume shader as a material for participating media is rooted in the physics of light transport in heterogeneous media [S1][S2, unofficial][S3]. It is used by default for smoke and fire rendering when volume attributes are present [S1]. Modern render engines implement this through shaders like the Standard Volume shader, which supports scattering, absorption, and emission in a single node [S1][S2, unofficial].
