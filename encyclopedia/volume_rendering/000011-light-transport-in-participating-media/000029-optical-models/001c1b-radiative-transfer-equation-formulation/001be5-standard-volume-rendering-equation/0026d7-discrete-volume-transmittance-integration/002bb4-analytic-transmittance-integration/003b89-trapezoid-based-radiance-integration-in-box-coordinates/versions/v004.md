# Trapezoid-based Radiance Integration in Box Coordinates

Also called: Analytic Integration, Analytic Integration of Unoccluded Single Scattering in Box-Shaped Medium, Integration of Unoccluded Radiance, Trapezoid Segment Integration, Unoccluded Single Scattering Integration

## Overview
This method computes the exact integral of unoccluded single-scattering radiance for a homogeneous medium confined within a rectilinear box, using directional or collimated light [S1]. It differs from standard ray marching by decomposing the light-view plane intersection with the box into trapezoid segments, allowing the transmittance integral to be solved analytically in a single pass per segment [S1]. Unlike [Analytic Transmittance Integration](5116-analytic-transmittance-integration.md) which handles general analytic forms, this method specifically exploits the linear geometry of box faces and the collimated nature of the light to reduce the integration domain to a skewed 2D plane [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Camera origin $\mathbf{p}_o$ and direction $\omega_o$.
*   Light origin $\mathbf{p}_{light}$ and direction $\omega_i$ (collimated/directional).
*   Box geometry defined by transformation matrix $B_{3\times4}$ (orientation, scale, translation) and bounds $[-1, 1]^3$ in box coordinates.
*   Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ (constant within the box).
*   Emitted radiance $L_i$ from the light source.

**Outputs:**
*   Integrated unoccluded radiance contribution.

**Data Structures:**
*   Box Coordinates: A 3D space where the box is axis-aligned with bounds $[-1, 1]$.
*   Skewed Coordinate System: Defined by axes corresponding to the camera ray direction and light ray direction, with the origin at the camera position.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Coordinate Transformation:**
    Transform the camera origin, light origin, and light direction into box coordinates using $B_{3\times4}$.
    *   Camera origin in box coords: $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o = B_{3\times4} [\mathbf{p}_o, 1]^T$.
    *   Light direction in box coords (non-normalized): $\tilde{\omega}_i = B_{3\times4} [\omega_i, 0]^T$.
    *   Camera direction in box coords: $\tilde{\omega}_o = B_{3\times4} [\omega_o, 0]^T$.

2.  **Intersection and Bounds:**
    *   If the camera origin $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o$ is outside the box, move it to the first intersection point with the box, or assign 0 contribution if the ray does not intersect [S1].
    *   Compute the light ray exit point $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_L$ and distance $d_L$:
        $$ d_L = \text{IntersectBox}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o, \tilde{\omega}_i) $$
        $$ \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_L = \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o + d_L \tilde{\omega}_i \quad \text{[S1, Eq. 4]} $$
    *   Compute the camera ray exit point $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C$ and distance $t_C$:
        $$ t_C = \text{IntersectBox}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o, -\tilde{\omega}_o) $$
        $$ \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C = \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o - t_C \tilde{\omega}_o \quad \text{[S1, Eq. 5]} $$
    *   If the camera ray does not intersect the box, return 0 [S1].

3.  **Face Vector Computation:**
    Compute face vectors perpendicular to the box axes ($x, y, z$) and the light-view plane. These vectors define the traversal directions along the box edges.
    *   Determine the direction of progress towards the camera ray exit point $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C$.
    *   If the light plane is parallel to the camera ray direction, skip traversal and compute the integral directly [S1].
    *   If the vector connecting points is parallel to the light plane ($|v| < \epsilon$), select the direction along the camera ray instead of the face vector [S1].
    *   The final face vector $q$ is determined by multiplying the progress direction value $v$ by the candidate vector $a$ ($q = v a$) [S1].

4.  **Trapezoid Segment Integration Loop:**
    Iterate up to 3 times (maximum number of edges in a box section) [S1]. In each iteration $k$:
    
    a. **Find Next Corner:**
       *   Calculate progress along the current edge.
       *   Clamp progress to prevent overstepping the camera ray exit point:
           $$ t_{cut} = \min(1, g_{progress}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C - \tilde{\mathbf{p}}, \tilde{\mathbf{w}})) $$
           where $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ is the current position and $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ is the current traversal direction.
       *   Compute the end point on the edge:
           $$ \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge} = \tilde{\mathbf{p}} + t_{cut} \tilde{\mathbf{w}} $$
    
    b. **Compute Segment Geometry:**
       *   Compute the projected distance along the camera ray for the edge end point:
           $$ t_{C,E} = g_{progress}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge} - \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o, -\tilde{\omega}_o) $$
       *   Compute the final point on the camera ray:
           $$ \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{cam} \leftarrow \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o - t_{C,E} \tilde{\omega}_o $$
       *   Compute the distance from the camera ray to the edge:
           $$ d_{E,E} \leftarrow (\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge} - \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{cam}) \cdot \tilde{\omega}_{i,s2} $$
           where $\tilde{\omega}_{i,s2}$ is the corrected incident light vector.
       *   Let $t_{C,S}$ and $d_{E,S}$ be the camera ray distance and edge distance at the start of the segment.
    
    c. **Analytic Integration:**
       *   Compute the trapezoid domain slope:
           $$ c = 1 + \frac{d_{E,E} - d_{E,S}}{t_{C,E} - t_{C,S}} $$
       *   Compute the integral over transmittance for this trapezoid segment:
           $$ \mathcal{I}_{\text{trapezoid}} = e^{-(t_{C,S}+d_{E,S})\sigma_t} (1 - e^{-\sigma_t (t_{C,E}-t_{C,S})^c}) $$
           This can be expressed as $E_I (1 - E_u)$ where $E_I$ is the initial transmittance and $E_u$ is the untransmitted portion [S1].
    
    d. **Accumulate:**
       *   Add $\mathcal{I}_{\text{trapezoid}}$ to the total transmittance integral $T_{total}$.

5.  **Final Result:**
    The total integral is the sum of all segment integrals:
    $$ T_{total} = \sum_{k=1}^{3} \mathcal{T}_k $$
    The final radiance is $L_i \cdot T_{total}$ (assuming uniform emitted radiance $L_i$) [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def trapezoid_integration(p_o, w_o, p_light, w_i, B_3x4, sigma_t, L_i):
    # 1. Transform to box coordinates
    p_o_box = transform_point(B_3x4, p_o)
    w_i_box = transform_vector(B_3x4, w_i)
    w_o_box = transform_vector(B_3x4, w_o)
    
    # 2. Intersect with box
    # IntersectBox returns distance to intersection along ray
    # If no intersection, return 0
    if not ray_intersects_box(p_o_box, w_o_box):
        return 0.0
    
    # Move camera origin to first intersection if outside
    if not point_in_box(p_o_box):
        dist = intersect_box_distance(p_o_box, w_o_box)
        p_o_box = p_o_box + dist * w_o_box
        
    # Light ray exit
    d_L = intersect_box_distance(p_o_box, w_i_box)
    p_L = p_o_box + d_L * w_i_box
    
    # Camera ray exit
    t_C = intersect_box_distance(p_o_box, -w_o_box)
    p_C = p_o_box - t_C * w_o_box
    
    # 3. Compute face vectors
    # Determine traversal directions based on progress towards p_C
    # Handle parallel cases
    if is_parallel(w_i_box, w_o_box):
        # Direct computation, skip loop
        return direct_integral(p_o_box, p_L, p_C, sigma_t)
        
    face_vectors = compute_face_vectors(p_o_box, p_C, w_i_box, w_o_box)
    
    # 4. Iterate up to 3 times
    total_transmittance = 0.0
    current_pos = p_L # Start from light entry/exit depending on traversal direction
    # Note: The algorithm traverses the boundary outline of the light-view plane intersection
    # The starting point is determined by the intersection of the light ray with the box
    
    for k in range(3):
        # Find next corner
        # Determine which face vector to follow
        w = select_face_vector(current_pos, face_vectors, p_C)
        
        # Calculate progress to cut at camera ray exit
        # g_progress computes the parameter along the vector w to reach p_C
        # This is a simplified representation of the geometric projection
        t_cut = min(1.0, compute_progress_to_target(current_pos, w, p_C))
        
        p_edge = current_pos + t_cut * w
        
        # Project edge point onto camera ray to get t_C,E
        # g_progress(p_o_box, -w_o_box) gives distance along camera ray
        t_C_E = compute_progress(p_edge - p_o_box, -w_o_box)
        
        # Point on camera ray
        p_cam = p_o_box - t_C_E * w_o_box
        
        # Distance from camera ray to edge
        # Corrected incident light vector needed here
        w_i_s2 = correct_light_vector(w_i_box, w_o_box)
        d_E_E = dot(p_edge - p_cam, w_i_s2)
        
        # Previous segment values (t_C_S, d_E_S)
        # For the first segment, these are derived from p_L and p_o_box geometry
        # For subsequent segments, they are the current t_C_E and d_E_E
        if k == 0:
            t_C_S = compute_progress(p_L - p_o_box, -w_o_box)
            d_E_S = compute_distance_to_camera_ray(p_L, p_o_box, w_o_box, w_i_s2)
        else:
            t_C_S = t_C_E_prev
            d_E_S = d_E_E_prev
            
        # Compute slope
        delta_t = t_C_E - t_C_S
        if abs(delta_t) < epsilon:
            delta_t = epsilon # Guard against division by zero
            
        c = 1.0 + (d_E_E - d_E_S) / delta_t
        
        # Analytic integral
        # E_I = exp(-(t_C_S + d_E_S) * sigma_t)
        # E_u = exp(-sigma_t * (t_C_E - t_C_S)^c)
        # I_trapezoid = E_I * (1 - E_u)
        
        exponent_term = (t_C_S + d_E_S) * sigma_t
        E_I = exp(-exponent_term)
        
        diff_t = t_C_E - t_C_S
        exponent_u = sigma_t * (diff_t ** c)
        E_u = exp(-exponent_u)
        
        I_trapezoid = E_I * (1.0 - E_u)
        
        total_transmittance += I_trapezoid
        
        # Update previous values for next iteration
        t_C_E_prev = t_C_E
        d_E_E_prev = d_E_E
        
        # Update current position for next corner
        current_pos = p_edge
        
    return L_i * total_transmittance
```

## Subtopics

### [Analytic Quadrature for Volume Radiance Integration](11019-analytic-quadrature-for-volume-radiance-integration.md)
Discretizes radiance and extinction individually to derive an analytic form, improving accuracy over Newton-Cotes rules for dense volumes with high extinction.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Constant time relative to the number of samples, as it requires only a single sample in unoccluded areas to compute the whole integral solution [S1]. The loop runs a maximum of 3 times regardless of resolution [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low, requiring storage for a few scalar values and vectors per ray [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Provides exact integration for unoccluded single scattering in box-shaped media, reducing variance compared to ray marching [S1]. It is efficient for real-time applications [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Restricted to rectilinear box-shaped media and collimated/directional light sources [S1]. Does not handle occlusion directly; requires a ratio estimator or other technique for visibility [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering of homogeneous volumetric light scattering in box-constrained environments (e.g., indoor scenes, room-scale experiences) [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Non-box geometries, non-collimated lights, or when occlusion is the primary concern without a complementary visibility estimator [S1].

## Background
This method was introduced by Zdravko Velinov and Kenny Mitchell from Disney Research Los Angeles [S1]. It is part of a set of algorithms for efficiently computing single scattering by box-constrained media, applicable to real-time volumetric lighting [S1]. The work extends to multiple scattering and convex media, and includes importance sampling schemes for Monte Carlo integration [S1].
