# Trapezoid-based Radiance Integration in Box Coordinates

Also called: Analytic Integration, Analytic Integration of Unoccluded Single Scattering in Box-Shaped Medium, Integration of Unoccluded Radiance, Trapezoid Segment Integration, Unoccluded Single Scattering Integration

## Overview
This method computes the exact integral of unoccluded single-scattered radiance within a homogeneous, rectilinear box-shaped medium illuminated by a collimated light source [S1]. It solves the problem of efficiently evaluating the transmittance-weighted radiance contribution along a camera ray without relying on stochastic sampling or ray marching for the unoccluded portion of the path [S1]. Unlike sibling methods such as [Trapezoid Integration for Transmittance](9006-trapezoid-integration-for-transmittance.md) which may approximate transmittance, or [Analytically Integrable Participating Media Rendering](9088-analytically-integrable-participating-media-rendering.md) which handles general media, this method specifically exploits the geometric constraints of a box and collimated light to decompose the integration domain into trapezoidal segments, allowing for a closed-form solution with a single sample in unoccluded areas [S1].

## How it works
The method operates in a skewed coordinate system aligned with the camera and light rays, transforming the 3D integration problem into a 2D traversal over the intersection of the light-view plane and the box [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Camera origin $\mathbf{p}_o$ and direction $\omega_o$.
    *   Light origin $\mathbf{p}_{L,o}$ and direction $\omega_i$ (collimated).
    *   Box geometry defined by transformation matrix $B_{3\times4}$ and bounds $[-1, 1]^3$ in box coordinates.
    *   Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ (assumed homogeneous).
    *   Light radiance $L_i$ (assumed uniform).
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Total transmittance integral $T_{total}$ over the unoccluded path segments.

### Data Structures
*   **Box Coordinates:** Points are represented as 3D vectors $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ where components are in $[-1, 1]$.
*   **Transformation Matrix:** $B_{3\times4}$ maps world coordinates to box coordinates.
*   **Face Vectors:** Three vectors $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_x, \tilde{\mathbf{w}}_y, \tilde{\mathbf{w}}_z$ perpendicular to the box axes and the light-view plane, defining traversal directions.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Coordinate Transformation:**
    Transform the camera origin, light ray origin, and light direction into box coordinates using $B_{3\times4}$.
    *   Camera origin: $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o = B_{3\times4} [\mathbf{p}_o, 1]^T$.
    *   Light direction (non-normalized): $\tilde{\omega}_i = B_{3\times4} [\omega_i, 0]^T$.
    *   Camera direction (non-normalized): $\tilde{\omega}_o = B_{3\times4} [\omega_o, 0]^T$.
    *   *Pitfall:* If $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o$ is outside the box, move it to the first intersection with the box, or assign 0 contribution if the camera ray does not intersect the box [S1].

2.  **Compute Exit Points:**
    Determine the entry point from the light ray and the exit point along the camera ray within the box.
    *   Light ray exit point (start of traversal):
        $$d_L = \text{IntersectBox}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o, \tilde{\omega}_i)$$
        $$\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_L = \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o + d_L \tilde{\omega}_i$$
    *   Camera ray exit point (end of traversal):
        $$t_C = \text{IntersectBox}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o, -\tilde{\omega}_o)$$
        $$\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C = \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o - t_C \tilde{\omega}_o$$
    *   *Note:* $\text{IntersectBox}$ returns the distance to the first intersection of the ray with the box boundaries $[-1, 1]^3$ [S1].

3.  **Compute Face Vectors:**
    Calculate the traversal directions. The face vectors are perpendicular to the box axes and the light-view plane.
    *   Determine the direction of progress towards $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C$ or the shortest path.
    *   *Edge Case:* If the light plane is parallel to the camera ray direction, skip traversal and compute the integral directly [S1].
    *   *Edge Case:* If the vector connecting two points is parallel to the light plane ($|v| < \epsilon$), select the direction along the camera ray instead of the face vector [S1].
    *   The final face vector $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ is determined by multiplying the progress direction scalar $q$ by the candidate vector $\mathbf{a}$: $\tilde{\mathbf{w}} = q \mathbf{a}$ [S1].

4.  **Iterative Trapezoid Integration:**
    Iterate up to 3 times (maximum number of edges in a box section). In each iteration $k$:
    *   **Find Next Corner:** Identify the next corner of the box section intersection.
    *   **Compute Edge End Point:**
        Calculate progress along the edge and clamp to prevent overstepping the camera ray exit point:
        $$t_{cut} = \min(1, g_{progress}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_C - \tilde{\mathbf{p}}, \tilde{\mathbf{w}}))$$
        $$\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge} = \tilde{\mathbf{p}} + t_{cut} \tilde{\mathbf{w}}$$
        where $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ is the current position (start of segment) [S1].
    *   **Project to Camera Ray:**
        Compute the projected distance along the camera ray for the edge end point:
        $$t_{C,E} = g_{progress}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge} - \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o, -\tilde{\omega}_o)$$
        Compute the final point on the camera ray:
        $$\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{cam} \leftarrow \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_o - t_{C,E} \tilde{\omega}_o$$
    *   **Compute Distances:**
        *   Distance from camera ray to the edge end point:
            $$d_{E,E} \leftarrow (\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge} - \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{cam}) \cdot \tilde{\omega}_{i,s2}$$
            where $\tilde{\omega}_{i,s2}$ is the corrected incident light vector [S1].
        *   Distance from camera ray to the edge start point ($d_{E,S}$) is carried over from the previous iteration or computed for the first segment [S1].
        *   Distance along camera ray at start ($t_{C,S}$) is carried over (0 for the first segment) [S1].
    *   **Integrate Segment:**
        Compute the analytical integral for the trapezoid segment defined by $(t_{C,S}, t_{C,E}, d_{E,S}, d_{E,E})$.
        *   Compute trapezoid domain slope:
            $$c = 1 + \frac{d_{E,E} - d_{E,S}}{t_{C,E} - t_{C,S}}$$
        *   Compute the integral:
            $$\mathcal{I}_{\text{trapezoid}} = e^{-(t_{C,S}+d_{E,S})\sigma_t} (1 - e^{-\sigma_t (t_{C,E}-t_{C,S})^c})$$
            Let $E_I = e^{-(t_{C,S}+d_{E,S})\sigma_t}$ and $E_u = e^{-\sigma_t (t_{C,E}-t_{C,S})^c}$. Then $\mathcal{I}_{\text{trapezoid}} = E_I (1 - E_u)$ [S1].
    *   **Update State:**
        Set $\tilde{\mathbf{p}} \leftarrow \tilde{\mathbf{p}}_{edge}$, $t_{C,S} \leftarrow t_{C,E}$, $d_{E,S} \leftarrow d_{E,E}$ for the next iteration.

5.  **Sum Results:**
    Sum the transmittance integrals of all segments:
    $$T_{total} = \sum_{k=1}^{3} \mathcal{T}_k$$
    where $\mathcal{T}_k$ is the radiance contribution of segment $k$ (proportional to $\mathcal{I}_{\text{trapezoid}}$) [S1].

def intersect_box(p, d):
    """
    Returns distance t >= 0 to the first intersection of ray p + t*d with box [-1,1]^3.
    Returns None if no intersection.
    """
    # Standard slab method
    t_min = -inf
    t_max = inf
    
    for i in range(3):
        if abs(d[i]) < 1e-8:
            if p[i] < -1.0 or p[i] > 1.0:
                return None
        else:
            t1 = (-1.0 - p[i]) / d[i]
            t2 = (1.0 - p[i]) / d[i]
            t_enter = min(t1, t2)
            t_exit = max(t1, t2)
            t_min = max(t_min, t_enter)
            t_max = min(t_max, t_exit)
            
    if t_min > t_max or t_max < 0:
        return None
    return t_min

### Parameters
*   **Light Source Type:** Directional or collimated light source masked by a gobo [S1].
*   **Medium Geometry:** Rectilinear box-shaped medium [S1].
*   **Box Transformation Matrix ($B_{3\times4}$):** Expresses orientation, scale, and translation of the box medium [S1].
*   **Extinction Coefficient ($\sigma_t$):** Homogeneous density [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Camera Outside Box:** If the camera origin is outside the box, it is moved to the first intersection. If the camera ray does not intersect the box, the contribution is 0 [S1].
*   **Parallel Light Plane:** If the light plane is parallel to the camera ray direction, traversal is skipped and the integral is computed directly [S1].
*   **Parallel Vector:** If the vector connecting two points is parallel to the light plane ($|v| < \epsilon$), the direction along the camera ray is selected instead of the face vector [S1].
*   **Precision:** The method relies on floating-point arithmetic for the exponential terms. High extinction coefficients may lead to underflow, but the analytic form avoids the noise of ray marching [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Analytic Quadrature for Volume Radiance Integration](11019-analytic-quadrature-for-volume-radiance-integration.md)
This method computes the radiance contribution of a volume slice by analytically integrating the product of transmittance and radiance, rather than approximating their product directly as in Newton-Cotes rules. It improves accuracy over the trapezoid rule for dense volumes with high extinction while remaining faster than Simpson's rule. It builds on [Trapezoid-based Radiance Integration in Box Coordinates](9001-trapezoid-based-radiance-integration-in-box-coordinates.md) by refining the integration step within each segment.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The algorithm performs a fixed number of iterations (up to 3) per ray segment, making it $O(1)$ with respect to the number of samples, compared to $O(N)$ for ray marching.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only a few scalar variables and vectors per ray.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method requires only a single sample in unoccluded areas to compute the whole integral solution, whereas previous methods required multiple samples or ray marching [S1]. It provides greater convergence in the rest of the scene [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Exact integration for unoccluded paths in homogeneous boxes; real-time applicability; variance reduction when combined with ratio estimators [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Restricted to box-shaped media and collimated light sources; does not handle occlusion within the box directly (requires a ratio estimator or other technique for visibility) [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time rendering of homogeneous volumetric effects (e.g., fog, smoke) in box-shaped containers with directional lighting [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Non-box geometries, non-collimated lights, or when occlusion within the medium is significant and not handled by a separate estimator [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by Zdravko Velinov and Kenny Mitchell at Disney Research Los Angeles [S1]. It was published in 2022 in the IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics [S1]. The work aims to solve the integration and sampling of single-scattered collimated light in box-shaped media for real-time applications, extending to multiple scattering and convex media [S1]. It addresses the limitations of ray marching by providing an analytic solution for the unoccluded term, which is then combined with ratio estimators to handle occlusion [S1].
