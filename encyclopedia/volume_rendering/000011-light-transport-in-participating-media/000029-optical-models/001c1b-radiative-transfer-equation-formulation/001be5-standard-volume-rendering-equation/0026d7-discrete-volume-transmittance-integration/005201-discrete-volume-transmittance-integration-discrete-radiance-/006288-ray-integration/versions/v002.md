# Ray Integration

Also called: Kajiya's Volume Rendering Equation, Light Integration, Scattering Integration, Volume Lighting

## Overview
Ray Integration computes the color and brightness accumulated as light passes through the interior of a participating medium, accounting for absorption, scattering, and emission effects [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of determining the final radiance along a viewing ray by integrating the interaction of light with the medium's density and optical properties [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$, ray direction $\mathbf{d}$, medium properties (extinction coefficient $\sigma(t)$, intrinsic color/intensity $c(t)$), and intersection bounds $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Final color or brightness $C$ of the ray [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1.  For each pixel, cast a ray through the volume [S1, unofficial].
2.  Intersect the ray with the medium (e.g., a tetrahedral mesh or voxel grid) to determine $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
3.  Discretize the ray into $N$ steps of length $\Delta t = (t_{far} - t_{near}) / N$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  For each step, sample the medium at the midpoint to obtain $\sigma$ and $c$ [S2, unofficial].
5.  Compute transmittance at the step start using the accumulated optical depth [G].
6.  Accumulate the step contribution $T(t) \cdot \sigma(t) \cdot c(t) \cdot \Delta t$ to the total color [S2, unofficial].
7.  Update the accumulated optical depth by adding $\sigma \cdot \Delta t$ [G].

**Formulas:**
The core equation for Ray Integration is defined as [S2, unofficial]:

$$
C = \int_{t_{near}}^{t_{far}} T(t) \cdot \sigma(t) \cdot c(t) \, dt
$$

Where:
*   $C$ is the final color or brightness (energy) of the ray [S2, unofficial].
*   $\sigma(t)$ is the sum of absorption and scattering coefficients at position $t$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $c(t)$ is the medium’s intrinsic color and intensity information at position $t$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ are the start and end points where the ray intersects the medium [S2, unofficial].

The transmittance $T(t)$, which describes the attenuation of light intensity as it passes through the object, is defined as [S2, unofficial]:

$$
T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{t_{\text{near}}}^{t} \sigma(s) \, ds\right)
$$

This is a generalized form of the Beer-Lambert Law [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `num_steps`: Not given in the sources.
*   `step_size`: Derived from `num_steps`.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Analytic Solution:** An analytic solution is essentially impossible for general media, necessitating numerical integration [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Accumulating optical depth can lead to precision issues if the optical depth is very large; clamping or using higher precision arithmetic is required [G].

**Pseudocode:**
The sources note that obtaining an analytic solution is essentially impossible, requiring discretization (e.g., Ray Marching) [S2, unofficial]. The following pseudocode implements the discrete approximation of the integral.

```python
import math

def ray_integration(ray_origin, ray_direction, medium_sampler, t_near, t_far, num_steps):
    """
    Computes the color C by discretizing the ray integration integral.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: 3D vector, origin of the ray.
        ray_direction: 3D vector, direction of the ray (normalized).
        medium_sampler: Function that returns (sigma, c) at a given 3D position.
        t_near: Float, start parameter of the ray intersection.
        t_far: Float, end parameter of the ray intersection.
        num_steps: Integer, number of discrete steps for integration.
        
    Returns:
        color: 3D vector, the final color C.
    """
    step_size = (t_far - t_near) / num_steps
    total_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    accumulated_tau = 0.0
    
    for i in range(num_steps):
        t_current = t_near + i * step_size
        t_mid = t_current + step_size / 2.0
        pos = ray_origin + ray_direction * t_mid
        sigma, c = medium_sampler(pos)
        
        # Transmittance at the start of the current step
        transmittance = math.exp(-accumulated_tau)
        
        # Add contribution for this step
        total_color += transmittance * sigma * c * step_size
        
        # Update accumulated optical depth for the next step
        accumulated_tau += sigma * step_size
        
    return total_color
```

## Subtopics

### Ray Marching
A discretization technique for approximating the ray integration integral. It samples the medium at discrete intervals along the ray. Choose it when real-time performance is required and analytic solutions are intractable. [Ray Marching](14607-ray-marching.md)

### Discrete Radiance Step Integration
Computes the outgoing radiance after traversing a small step of length $ds$ through a participating medium. It is a local step-based approach that builds on the principles of ray integration. [Discrete Radiance Step Integration](14606-discrete-volume-transmittance-integration-discrete-radiance-.md)
