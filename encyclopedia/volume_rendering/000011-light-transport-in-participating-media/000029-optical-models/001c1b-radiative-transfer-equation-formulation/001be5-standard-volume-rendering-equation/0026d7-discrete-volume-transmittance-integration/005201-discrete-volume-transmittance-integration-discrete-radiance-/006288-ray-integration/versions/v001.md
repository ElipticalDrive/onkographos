# Ray Integration

Also called: Kajiya's Volume Rendering Equation, Light Integration, Scattering Integration, Volume Lighting

## Overview
Ray Integration computes the color and brightness accumulated as light passes through the interior of a participating medium, accounting for absorption, scattering, and emission effects [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of determining the final radiance along a viewing ray by integrating the interaction of light with the medium's density and optical properties [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its formulation as a continuous integral over the ray parameter $t$, which is then discretized for computation [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$, ray direction $\mathbf{d}$, medium properties (extinction coefficient $\sigma(t)$, intrinsic color/intensity $c(t)$), and intersection bounds $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Final color or brightness $C(t)$ of the ray [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Not given in the sources.

**Formulas:**
The core equation for Ray Integration is defined as [S2, unofficial]:

$$
C(t) = \int_{t_{near}}^{t_{far}} T(t) \cdot \sigma(t) \cdot c(t) \, dt
$$

Where:
*   $C(t)$ is the final color or brightness (energy) of the ray [S2, unofficial].
*   $\sigma(t)$ is the sum of absorption and scattering coefficients at position $t$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $c(t)$ is the medium’s intrinsic color and intensity information at position $t$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ are the start and end points where the ray intersects the medium [S2, unofficial].

The transmittance $T(t)$, which describes the attenuation of light intensity as it passes through the object, is defined as [S2, unofficial]:

$$
T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{t_{\text{near}}}^{t} \sigma(s) \, ds\right)
$$

This is a generalized form of the Beer-Lambert Law [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  For each pixel, cast a ray through the volume [S1, unofficial].
2.  Intersect the ray with the medium (e.g., a tetrahedral mesh or voxel grid) to determine $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
3.  Compute the transmittance $T(t)$ along the ray by integrating the extinction coefficient $\sigma(s)$ from $t_{near}$ to the current point $t$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  Integrate the product of transmittance, extinction, and intrinsic color over the interval $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ to obtain the final color $C(t)$ [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
The sources note that obtaining an analytic solution is essentially impossible, requiring discretization (e.g., Ray Marching) [S2, unofficial]. The following pseudocode represents the discrete approximation of the integral described in the sources.

```python
def ray_integration(ray_origin, ray_direction, medium_sampler, t_near, t_far, num_steps):
    """
    Computes the color C(t) by discretizing the ray integration integral.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: 3D vector, origin of the ray.
        ray_direction: 3D vector, direction of the ray (normalized).
        medium_sampler: Function that returns (sigma, c) at a given 3D position.
        t_near: Float, start parameter of the ray intersection.
        t_far: Float, end parameter of the ray intersection.
        num_steps: Integer, number of discrete steps for integration.
        
    Returns:
        color: 3D vector, the final color C(t).
    """
    step_size = (t_far - t_near) / num_steps
    total_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    accumulated_sigma = 0.0
    
    # Pre-compute or integrate transmittance? 
    # The formula C(t) = integral(T(t) * sigma(t) * c(t) dt) requires T(t) at each step.
    # T(t) = exp(-integral(sigma(s) ds) from t_near to t).
    
    for i in range(num_steps):
        # Current position along the ray
        t_current = t_near + i * step_size
        t_next = t_near + (i + 1) * step_size
        t_mid = (t_current + t_next) / 2.0
        
        # Sample medium at midpoint for stability
        pos = ray_origin + ray_direction * t_mid
        sigma, c = medium_sampler(pos)
        
        # Update accumulated extinction for transmittance calculation
        # T(t_mid) depends on integral from t_near to t_mid
        # We approximate the integral of sigma for the current step
        accumulated_sigma += sigma * step_size
        
        # Calculate transmittance at the midpoint
        # T(t) = exp(-integral(sigma(s) ds))
        transmittance = exp(-accumulated_sigma)
        
        # Add contribution to total color
        # dC = T(t) * sigma(t) * c(t) * dt
        contribution = transmittance * sigma * c
        total_color += contribution * step_size
        
    return total_color
```

**Parameters:**
*   `num_steps`: Not given in the sources. Typical values depend on the desired accuracy and performance constraints.
*   `step_size`: Derived from `num_steps`.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Analytic Solution:** An analytic solution is essentially impossible for general media, necessitating numerical integration [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Accumulating `accumulated_sigma` can lead to precision issues if the optical depth is very large; clamping or using higher precision may be required [G].
*   **Boundaries:** Incorrect determination of $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$ leads to incorrect integration bounds [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Ray Marching
A discretization technique for approximating the ray integration integral. It samples the medium at discrete intervals along the ray. Choose it when real-time performance is required and analytic solutions are intractable. [Ray Marching](14607-ray-marching.md)

### Discrete Radiance Step Integration
Computes the outgoing radiance after traversing a small step of length $ds$ through a participating medium. It is a local step-based approach that builds on the principles of ray integration. [Discrete Radiance Step Integration](14606-discrete-volume-transmittance-integration-discrete-radiance-.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of integration steps (samples) along the ray.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per ray, assuming the medium sampler is efficient.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Physically based; accounts for absorption, scattering, and emission.
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive for high-resolution volumes or complex media due to the need for many samples. Analytic solutions are generally unavailable.
*   **When to use:** When physical accuracy is required and computational resources allow for sufficient sampling.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and approximate methods (like simple alpha blending) are sufficient.

## Background
Ray Integration was first introduced by James T. Kajiya in his 1984 Siggraph paper, "Ray Tracing Volume Densities" [S2, unofficial]. It is a fundamental technique in volume rendering for simulating light transport in participating media.
