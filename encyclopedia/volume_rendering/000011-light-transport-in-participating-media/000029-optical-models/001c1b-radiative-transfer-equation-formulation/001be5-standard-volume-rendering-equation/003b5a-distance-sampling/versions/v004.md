# Distance Sampling

Also called: Distance Sampling for Free Paths, Inverse CDF sampling for transmittance, Monte Carlo with Distance Sampling (MCDS)

## Overview
Distance sampling is a Monte Carlo technique for estimating the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) by importance sampling the medium extinction transmittance to determine the free flight distance (distance to the next collision) [S2, unofficial]. It distributes samples according to the transmittance along the current path segment, ignoring the overall shape of the medium [S1]. Unlike [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md), which accounts for global geometry to reduce variance in occluded areas, distance sampling relies on simple local geometry assumptions [S1]. It performs well at very low sample counts but exhibits a lower convergence rate at higher sample counts compared to methods that model complex occluders [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient $\mu_t$ (constant throughout the volume) [S2, unofficial].
- A uniform random number $\xi \in [0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Sampled free path distance $t$ [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- Not applicable for the core sampling step; requires only scalar storage for $\mu_t$ and $\xi$.

**Steps:**
1. Generate a uniform random number $\xi$ in the interval $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
2. Define the probability density function (PDF) for the free path distance in a homogeneous medium as the normalized transmittance:
   $$ p(t) = \mu_t e^{-\mu_t t} = \mu_t T(t) $$
   where $T(t) = e^{-\mu_t t}$ is the transmittance [S2, unofficial].
3. Compute the cumulative distribution function (CDF) $F(t)$ by integrating the PDF from $0$ to $t$:
   $$ F(t) = \int_0^t p(t') dt' = \int_0^t \mu_t e^{-\mu_t t'} dt' = 1 - e^{-\mu_t t} $$
4. Set the CDF equal to the random number $\xi$ and solve for $t$:
   $$ \xi = 1 - e^{-\mu_t t} $$
5. Rearrange to isolate the exponential term:
   $$ e^{-\mu_t t} = 1 - \xi $$
6. Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
   $$ -\mu_t t = \ln(1 - \xi) $$
7. Solve for $t$:
   $$ t = \frac{-\ln(1 - \xi)}{\mu_t} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def sample_distance_sampling(mu_t: float, rng: float) -> float:
    """
    Sample the free path distance in a homogeneous medium using distance sampling.
    
    Args:
        mu_t: Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient (constant).
        rng: A uniform random number in [0, 1].
        
    Returns:
        The sampled distance to the next collision.
    """
    # Avoid log(0) if rng is exactly 1.0, though probability is 0.
    # In practice, rng is typically in [0, 1).
    if rng == 1.0:
        rng = 0.9999999999
        
    # Formula: t = -ln(1 - xi) / mu_t
    # Note: Since xi is uniform in [0, 1), 1 - xi is also uniform in (0, 1].
    # Some implementations use -ln(xi) / mu_t directly for efficiency,
    # which is statistically equivalent for uniform [0,1).
    # We follow the derivation in [S2, unofficial] strictly:
    distance = -math.log(1.0 - rng) / mu_t
    
    return distance
```

**Parameters:**
- $\mu_t$: Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient. Must be constant throughout the volume [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Heterogeneous Media:** This method assumes $\mu_t$ is constant. For heterogeneous media where $\mu_t$ varies with position, the transmittance is not invertible in closed form, and this technique cannot be applied directly [S2, unofficial]. Other strategies are required for such cases [S2, unofficial].
- **Precision:** If $\xi = 1$, $\ln(0)$ is undefined. In floating-point implementations, ensure $\xi$ is strictly less than 1 or handle the boundary case [G].
- **Variance:** Because it ignores the overall shape of the medium and distributes samples proportional only to the distance to the observer along the current segment, it does not perfectly match the transmittance integrand in complex scenes, leading to higher variance than [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md)
Importance samples the integration domain according to the geometry-dependent transmittance of the entire participating medium. Choose when occlusion variance is critical.

### [Closed-form Tracking](9223-closed-form-tracking.md)
Samples free-flight distance where the extinction coefficient follows a simple analytical form. Choose when local analytical invertibility is available.

### [Rejection Sampling for Arbitrary Media](9576-rejection-sampling-for-arbitrary-media.md)
Unbiased Monte Carlo technique for arbitrary non-exponential extinction distributions. Choose when media heterogeneity prevents closed-form inversion.

### [Parametric Transmittance Sampling](14526-parametric-transmittance-sampling.md)
Uses parametric families (e.g., Gamma) for non-exponential distributions. Choose when parametric approximations fit the medium profile better than rejection bounds.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, involving one logarithm and one division [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only scalar storage for $\mu_t$ and the random number [S2, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Performs well at very low sample counts [S1]. However, it has a lower convergence rate at higher sample counts due to simple geometry assumptions [S1]. It is outperformed by [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) in terms of convergence speed and variance reduction, especially in scenes with complex occluders [S1].
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement; unbiased for homogeneous media; efficient for low sample counts [S1][S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** High variance in complex/occluded scenes; inapplicable to heterogeneous media without modification [S1][S2, unofficial].
- **When to use:** Homogeneous media with simple geometry or when computational budget is extremely limited [S2, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** Heterogeneous media or scenes with significant occlusion where variance reduction is prioritized over simplicity [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background
Distance sampling is a foundational Monte Carlo technique for estimating the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) [S2, unofficial]. It is derived from the statistical description of light transport in participating media, where absorption and scattering are modeled by coefficients $\mu$ [S2, unofficial]. The method relies on the inverse cumulative distribution function (CDF) to map uniform random numbers to the exponential distribution of free path lengths in homogeneous media [S2, unofficial]. It is often contrasted with ray marching, which distributes samples at equal distances, and [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md), which considers global geometry [S1]. The technique is detailed in standard graphics literature on participating media [S2, unofficial].
