# Distance Sampling

Also called: Distance Sampling for Free Paths, Inverse CDF sampling for transmittance, Monte Carlo with Distance Sampling (MCDS)

## Overview
Distance sampling is a Monte Carlo technique used to estimate the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) by importance sampling the non-recursive terms, specifically the medium extinction transmittance, to determine the free flight distance (distance to the next collision) [S2, unofficial]. It differs from its siblings, such as [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) and [Ray Marching](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md), by distributing samples proportional to the transmittance along the current path segment while ignoring the overall shape of the medium or global geometry [S1]. This makes it suitable for homogeneous media but prone to higher variance in complex, occluded scenes compared to global importance sampling methods [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient $\mu_t$ (constant throughout the volume) [S2, unofficial].
- A uniform random number $\xi$ (or $u$) drawn from $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Sampled free path distance $t$ [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No complex data structures are required for the basic homogeneous case. The method relies on scalar arithmetic [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Generate a uniform random number $\xi \in [0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
2. Define the probability density function (PDF) for the free path distance in a homogeneous medium. The PDF is derived by normalizing the transmittance function $T(t)$:
   $$ p(t) = \mu_t e^{-\mu_t t} = \mu_t T(t) $$
   where $T(t) = e^{-\mu_t t}$ is the transmittance over distance $t$ [S2, unofficial].
3. Compute the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) $P(t)$ by integrating the PDF from $0$ to $t$:
   $$ P(t) = \int_0^t p(t') dt' = \int_0^t \mu_t e^{-\mu_t t'} dt' = 1 - e^{-\mu_t t} $$
4. Invert the CDF to solve for $t$ given $\xi = P(t)$:
   $$ \xi = 1 - e^{-\mu_t t} $$
   $$ e^{-\mu_t t} = 1 - \xi $$
   $$ -\mu_t t = \ln(1 - \xi) $$
   $$ t = \frac{-\ln(1 - \xi)}{\mu_t} $$
5. Return $t$ as the sampled distance to the next collision [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math
import random

def sample_distance_homogeneous(mu_t: float) -> float:
    """
    Samples the free path distance in a homogeneous medium with extinction coefficient mu_t.
    
    Args:
        mu_t: Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient (constant).
        
    Returns:
        t: The sampled distance to the next collision.
    """
    # Step 1: Generate uniform random number in [0, 1]
    xi = random.random()
    
    # Step 2-4: Inverse CDF sampling
    # t = -ln(1 - xi) / mu_t
    # Note: Since xi is uniform in [0, 1), 1-xi is also uniform in (0, 1].
    # Using -ln(xi) is numerically equivalent and avoids subtraction if xi can be 0.
    # However, strictly following the derivation t = -ln(1-xi)/mu_t:
    if xi == 1.0:
        xi = 0.9999999999 # Guard against log(0), though random() usually returns [0, 1)
        
    t = -math.log(1.0 - xi) / mu_t
    
    return t
```

**Parameters:**
- $\mu_t$: Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient. It is constant throughout the volume [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Heterogeneous Media:** This specific closed-form inversion works only for homogeneous media where $\mu_t$ is constant. For heterogeneous media, $\mu_t$ varies with position, making the transmittance integral non-invertible analytically, requiring different strategies like [Rejection Sampling for Arbitrary Media](9576-rejection-sampling-for-arbitrary-media.md) or [Closed-form Tracking](9223-closed-form-tracking.md) with specific density functions [S2, unofficial].
- **Precision:** If $\xi$ is exactly 1.0, $\ln(1-\xi)$ becomes undefined. Standard uniform random generators typically return values in $[0, 1)$, but guards should be implemented [G].
- **Variance in Complex Geometry:** Distance sampling ignores the overall shape of the medium and global occluders. It distributes samples proportional to the distance to the observer along the ray segment, which does not perfectly match the transmittance integrand in complex scenes, leading to higher variance than methods like [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md)
Importance samples the integration domain according to the geometry-dependent transmittance of the entire participating medium, reducing variance in occluded areas.

### [Closed-form Tracking](9223-closed-form-tracking.md)
Samples free-flight distance where the extinction coefficient follows a simple analytical form, relying on local analytical invertibility.

### [Rejection Sampling for Arbitrary Media](9576-rejection-sampling-for-arbitrary-media.md)
An unbiased Monte Carlo technique for heterogeneous media using a constant upper bound for rejection steps.

### [Parametric Transmittance Sampling](14526-parametric-transmittance-sampling.md)
Uses non-exponential statistical distributions (e.g., Gamma, Uniform) to sample free-flight distance in complex extinction profiles.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample for homogeneous media, involving a logarithm and division [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only scalar storage for $\mu_t$ and the random number [S2, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:** In benchmarks on an RTX 8000 GPU, distance sampling (MCDS) performed well at very low sample counts but exhibited a lower convergence rate at higher sample counts compared to [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) (MCWVDS) due to its simple geometry assumptions [S1]. It is generally slower to converge than MCWVDS in complex scenes [S1].
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement; unbiased; efficient for homogeneous media; good convergence at very low sample counts [S2, unofficial][S1].
- **Limitations:** High variance in complex, occluded scenes; ignores global geometry; not directly applicable to heterogeneous media without modification [S1][S2, unofficial].
- **When to use:** Homogeneous participating media where global geometry effects are negligible or when computational simplicity is prioritized over convergence speed in complex scenes [S2, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** Heterogeneous media, or scenes with complex occluders where global importance sampling (e.g., [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md)) is required for acceptable variance [S1].

## Background
Distance sampling originates from Monte Carlo integration techniques for the Volume Rendering Equation. It is a standard approach for solving the VRE by importance sampling the non-recursive terms [S2, unofficial]. The technique relies on the statistical independence of atoms in the medium and the exponential distribution of free paths in homogeneous media [S2, unofficial]. It is often contrasted with ray marching, which distributes samples at equal distances, and whole-volume methods that account for global geometry [S1]. The method is foundational in path tracing frameworks for participating media [S1].
