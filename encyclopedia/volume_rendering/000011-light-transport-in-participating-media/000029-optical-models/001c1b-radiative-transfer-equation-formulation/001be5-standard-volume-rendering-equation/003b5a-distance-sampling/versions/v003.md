# Distance Sampling

Also called: Distance Sampling for Free Paths, Inverse CDF sampling for transmittance, Monte Carlo with Distance Sampling (MCDS)

## Overview
Distance sampling is a Monte Carlo technique used to estimate the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) by importance sampling the medium extinction transmittance to determine the free flight distance (distance to the next collision) [S2, unofficial]. It is primarily applied in homogeneous media where the extinction coefficient is constant [S2, unofficial]. Unlike [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md), which accounts for global geometry to reduce variance in finite media, distance sampling ignores the overall shape of the medium and distributes samples proportional only to the local transmittance along the current path segment [S1]. This simplification leads to higher variance in complex scenes with occluders compared to whole-volume methods, though it performs adequately at very low sample counts [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $\mu_t$: The homogeneous medium extinction coefficient (constant throughout the volume) [S2, unofficial].
- $\xi$: A uniform random number drawn from $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- $t$: The sampled free path distance to the next collision [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No complex data structures are required. The method relies on scalar arithmetic operations [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Generate a uniform random number $\xi \in [0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
2. Define the probability density function (PDF) for the free path distance in a homogeneous medium. The PDF is the normalized transmittance function:
   $$ p(t) = \mu_t e^{-\mu_t t} = \mu_t T(t) $$
   where $T(t) = e^{-\mu_t t}$ is the transmittance [S2, unofficial].
3. Compute the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) by integrating the PDF from $0$ to $t$:
   $$ F(t) = \int_0^t p(t') dt' = 1 - e^{-\mu_t t} $$
4. Set the CDF equal to the random number $\xi$ and solve for $t$:
   $$ \xi = 1 - e^{-\mu_t t} $$
5. Rearrange the equation to isolate $t$:
   $$ e^{-\mu_t t} = 1 - \xi $$
   $$ -\mu_t t = \ln(1 - \xi) $$
   $$ t = \frac{-\ln(1 - \xi)}{\mu_t} $$
6. Return $t$ as the sampled distance [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math
import random

def sample_distance_sampling(mu_t: float) -> float:
    """
    Sample the free path distance in a homogeneous medium using distance sampling.
    
    Args:
        mu_t: Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient (must be > 0).
        
    Returns:
        t: The sampled distance to the next collision.
    """
    # Step 1: Generate uniform random number in [0, 1]
    xi = random.random()
    
    # Step 2-5: Inverse CDF sampling
    # t = -ln(1 - xi) / mu_t
    # Note: Since xi is uniform in [0, 1), 1-xi is also uniform in (0, 1].
    # Using 1-xi directly avoids potential log(0) if xi=1, though random.random() 
    # typically excludes 1.0.
    t = -math.log(1.0 - xi) / mu_t
    
    return t
```

**Parameters:**
- $\mu_t$: Homogeneous medium extinction coefficient. Typical values depend on the specific medium (e.g., fog, smoke) and are not given in the sources [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Heterogeneous Media:** This method assumes $\mu_t$ is constant. For heterogeneous media where $\mu_t$ varies with position, the transmittance is not analytically invertible, and this method fails or requires different strategies [S2, unofficial].
- **Zero Extinction:** If $\mu_t = 0$, the formula involves division by zero. In practice, $\mu_t$ must be strictly positive for this sampling technique [S2, unofficial].
- **Precision:** If $\xi$ is exactly 1.0, $\ln(0)$ is undefined. Standard random number generators usually return values in $[0, 1)$, but guard clauses or using $1-\xi$ directly is recommended [G].

## Subtopics

### [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md)
Importance samples the integration domain according to the geometry-dependent transmittance of the entire participating medium. Choose this for finite media with complex occluders to reduce variance.

### [Closed-form Tracking](9223-closed-form-tracking.md)
Samples free-flight distance where the extinction coefficient follows a simple analytical form. Choose this when the cumulative distribution function is analytically invertible for local sampling.

### [Rejection Sampling for Arbitrary Media](9576-rejection-sampling-for-arbitrary-media.md)
Unbiased Monte Carlo technique using rejection sampling for arbitrary non-exponential extinction distributions. Choose this for heterogeneous media where analytical inversion is not possible.

### [Parametric Transmittance Sampling](14526-parametric-transmittance-sampling.md)
Importance samples medium extinction using non-exponential statistical distributions. Choose this for media with complex extinction profiles that can be approximated by parametric families.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, involving a logarithm and division [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, requiring only scalar storage for $\mu_t$ and $\xi$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Distance sampling performs well at very low sample counts but exhibits a lower convergence rate at higher sample counts compared to [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) due to its simple geometry assumptions [S1]. It fails to distribute samples proportional to the transmittance integrand in occluded areas, leading to higher variance than MCWVDS [S1].
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement; efficient for homogeneous media without complex geometry interactions [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** High variance in scenes with complex occluders or heterogeneous media; ignores global geometry [S1].
- **When to use:** Homogeneous media where computational simplicity is prioritized over convergence speed in complex scenes [S2, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** Heterogeneous media, or scenes with significant occlusion where variance reduction is critical [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background
Distance sampling is a standard Monte Carlo technique for solving the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) [S2, unofficial]. It is based on the statistical description of light transport in participating media, where absorption, scattering, and emission are modeled with coefficients $\mu$ [S2, unofficial]. The technique relies on the inverse cumulative distribution function (CDF) to map uniform random numbers to the exponential distribution of free path lengths in homogeneous media [S2, unofficial]. It is considered a "previous work" or baseline technique in modern volume rendering research, often compared against more advanced methods like [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md) [S1].
