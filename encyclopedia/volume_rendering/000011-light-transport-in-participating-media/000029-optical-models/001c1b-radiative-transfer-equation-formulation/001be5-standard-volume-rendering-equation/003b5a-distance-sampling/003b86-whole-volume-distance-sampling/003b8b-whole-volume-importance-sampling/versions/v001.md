# Whole Volume Importance Sampling
Also called: Geometry-Dependent Transmittance Sampling, Importance Sampling Scheme

## Overview
Whole Volume Importance Sampling is a Monte Carlo strategy that samples the geometry of the entire participating medium, incorporating both camera and light rays, rather than limiting sampling to transmittance along a single ray. It differs from classic techniques that restrict sampling to the current light or camera ray, delivering lower variance images in path tracing frameworks.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: The geometry of the finite medium (typically a box), camera ray, light source(s), and medium properties.
- Output: A sample position and direction for Monte Carlo integration.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on caching variables describing trapezoid-shaped segments. These segments are formed by traversing the edges of the medium's bounding box from corner to corner to connect incident and outgoing exit points. The specific layout of these cached variables is not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1. **Geometry Traversal:** Break down the geometry of the medium into trapezoid-shaped segments. This is done by traversing the edges of the box from corner to corner to connect the incident and outgoing exit points from the medium [S1].
2. **Segment Selection:** Select a segment based on the derived sampling strategy. The strategy matches the distribution of the geometry-dependent transmittance along the camera ray [S1].
3. **Caching:** Cache variables describing each segment to avoid repeated traversal for each sample [S1].
4. **Integration:** Use the selected sample in an optimized Monte Carlo integration scheme [S1].

## Operating characteristics
The method yields visible noise reduction and achieves superior convergence compared to independent sampling with existing algorithms [S1]. It requires only a single sample in unoccluded areas to compute the whole integral solution [S1]. It is directly applicable to indoor scene rendering in room-scale interactive experiences [S1]. It extends to multiple light sources [S1].

## Background
Originated in the paper "Collimated Whole Volume Light Scattering in Homogeneous Finite Media" [S1]. The method is applicable in path tracing frameworks where existing approaches enable combining multiple sampling strategies [S1]. It generalizes to multiple scattering and progressive rendering [S1].
