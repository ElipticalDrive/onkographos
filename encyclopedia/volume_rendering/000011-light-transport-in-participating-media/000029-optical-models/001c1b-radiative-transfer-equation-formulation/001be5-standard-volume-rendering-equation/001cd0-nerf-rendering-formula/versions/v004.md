# NeRF Rendering Formula

Also called: Discrete Volumetric Rendering Equation, NeRF-style Volume Rendering, Radiance Field Rendering Formula, Standard Volumetric Rendering, Volume Rendering (NeRF-style), Volumetric RGB Rendering

## Overview
The NeRF Rendering Formula is a discrete approximation of the volume rendering equation used to composite raw neural network predictions (color and density/opacity) into final pixel colors, depths, and accumulation maps [S3, unofficial][S4][S5, unofficial]. It differs from the continuous Standard volume rendering equation by operating on a fixed set of discrete sample points along a ray, assuming single-path light transport without explicit path tracing [S3, unofficial]. It is the core reconstruction function for NeRFs and 3D Gaussian Splats, enabling differentiable rendering of implicit or explicit radiance fields [S2][S5, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `rays_o`: Ray origins, shape `(*bs, 3)` [S5, unofficial].
*   `rays_d`: Ray directions, shape `(*bs, 3)` [S5, unofficial].
*   `z_vals`: Sample depths along the ray, shape `(*bs, K)` where $K$ is the number of samples per ray [S5, unofficial].
*   `implicit_fn`: A function (e.g., MLP or Gaussian field) that takes 3D positions and returns raw density $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$ and color features $f$ [S1, unofficial][S4].
*   `white_bkgd`: Boolean flag to composite onto a white background [S5, unofficial].
*   `raw_noise_std`: Standard deviation for noise added to density during training [S5, unofficial].
*   `background_color`: Can be 'random', 'last_sample', 'black', 'white', or a Tensor of shape `(3)` or `(*bs, 3)`, default 'random' [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `rgb_map`: Composite RGB color, shape `(*bs, 3)` [S5, unofficial].
*   `depth_map`: Expected depth, shape `(*bs,)` [S5, unofficial].
*   `acc_map`: Accumulated opacity, shape `(*bs,)` [S5, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   `raw`: Tensor of shape `(*bs, K, 4)` containing concatenated color features and density predictions from `implicit_fn` [S5, unofficial].
*   `alpha`: Tensor of shape `(*bs, K)` containing computed opacity values [S5, unofficial].
*   `transmittance`: Tensor of shape `(*bs, K)` containing cumulative transmittance [S5, unofficial].
*   `weights`: Tensor of shape `(*bs, K)` containing the final rendering weights [S5, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Sample and Predict:**
    For each ray, sample $K$ 3D positions $x_i$ along the ray using `z_vals` and ray parameters [S6, unofficial]. Pass $x_i$ through `implicit_fn` to get raw density $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$ and color features $f$ [S1, unofficial][S4].
    $$ \text{raw} = \text{implicit\_fn}(x) \quad \text{shape: } (*bs, K, 4) $$
    The first 3 channels are color features, the 4th is raw density [S5, unofficial].

2.  **Compute Raw Color:**
    Apply sigmoid activation to the color features to bound them to $[0, 1]$ [S5, unofficial].
    $$ c_i = \sigma(\text{raw}_{i, :, :3}) $$

3.  **Compute Raw Density with Noise (Training Only):**
    If `raw_noise_std` > 0, add Gaussian noise to the raw density to prevent overfitting to sparse samples [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \sigma_{\text{noisy}} = \text{relu}(\text{raw}_{i, :, 3} + \mathcal{N}(0, \text{raw\_noise\_std})) $$
    If `raw_noise_std` is 0 or during inference, $\sigma_{\text{noisy}} = \text{relu}(\text{raw}_{i, :, 3})$ [S5, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Segment Distances:**
    Calculate the distance between adjacent samples [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \text{dists}_i = z_{i, j+1} - z_{i, j} \quad \text{for } j = 0 \dots K-2 $$
    Append a large value ($10^{10}$) for the last segment to approximate the infinite tail [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \text{dists}_i = \text{concat}(\text{dists}_i, 10^{10}) $$
    Convert to real-world distance by multiplying by the norm of the ray direction (if directions are not unit length) [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \Delta t_i = \text{dists}_i \cdot \| \text{rays\_d}_i \| $$

5.  **Compute Opacity ($\alpha$):**
    Convert density and segment length to opacity using the exponential attenuation model [S1, unofficial][S5, unofficial].
    $$ \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_{\text{noisy}, i} \cdot \Delta t_i) $$
    Values are constrained between 0 and 1 [S3, unofficial].

6.  **Compute Transmittance ($T$):**
    Compute cumulative transmittance using exclusive cumulative product [S5, unofficial].
    $$ T_i = \prod_{j=0}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    Implementation detail: Use `cumprod` on $(1 - \alpha)$ with a small epsilon ($10^{-10}$) added for numerical stability, shifted to be exclusive [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \text{ones} = \text{ones\_like}(\alpha[:, :1]) $$
    $$ T = \text{cumprod}(\text{concat}(\text{ones}, 1 - \alpha + 10^{-10}), \text{axis}=-1)[:, :-1] $$

7.  **Compute Weights ($w$):**
    The weight for each sample is the product of transmittance and the opacity of the current sample [S1, unofficial][S5, unofficial].
    $$ w_i = T_i \cdot \alpha_i $$
    Alternatively expressed as $w = T \cdot (1 - \exp(-\sigma \cdot \Delta t))$ [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Aggregate Outputs:**
    Compute the final pixel color, depth, and accumulation map using the weights [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \text{rgb\_map} = \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i \cdot c_i $$
    $$ \text{depth\_map} = \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i \cdot z_i $$
    $$ \text{acc\_map} = \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i $$

9.  **Background Composite (Optional):**
    If `white_bkgd` is True, composite the result onto a white background using the accumulated opacity [S5, unofficial].
    $$ \text{rgb\_map} = \text{rgb\_map} + (1 - \text{acc\_map}) $$
    For packed samples (when `ray_indices` is not None), use `accumulate_along_rays` for `comp_rgb` and `accumulated_weight` [S1, unofficial]. Raise `NotImplementedError` if `background_color` is 'last_sample' for packed samples [S1, unofficial]. Check for global `BACKGROUND_COLOR_OVERRIDE` and use it if set [S1, unofficial]. Compute final RGB: $\text{comp\_rgb} + \text{background\_color} \cdot (1.0 - \text{accumulated\_weight})$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def nerf_rendering_formula(rays_o, rays_d, z_vals, implicit_fn, white_bkgd=False, raw_noise_std=0.0):
    """
    rays_o: (N, 3) ray origins
    rays_d: (N, 3) ray directions
    z_vals: (N, K) sample depths along rays
    implicit_fn: callable returning (color_features, density) for 3D points
    """
    # 1. Compute 3D positions from ray origins, directions, and depths
    # x shape: (N, K, 3)
    x = rays_o[:, None, :] + z_vals[:, :, None] * rays_d[:, None, :]
    
    # 2. Predict raw density and color features
    # raw shape: (N, K, 4)
    raw = implicit_fn(x)
    
    # 3. Compute Color via sigmoid
    rgb = torch.sigmoid(raw[..., :3]) # (N, K, 3)
    
    # 4. Compute Density with optional noise
    raw_density = raw[..., 3]
    if raw_noise_std > 0:
        noise = torch.randn_like(raw_density) * raw_noise_std
        raw_density = raw_density + noise
    sigma = F.relu(raw_density) # (N, K)
    
    # 5. Compute Distances between adjacent samples
    dists = z_vals[:, 1:] - z_vals[:, :-1] # (N, K-1)
    dists = torch.cat([dists, torch.ones_like(dists[:, :1]) * 1e10], dim=-1) # (N, K)
    
    # Convert to real-world distance using ray direction norm
    dists = dists * torch.norm(rays_d, dim=-1, keepdim=True) # (N, K)
    
    # 6. Compute Alpha (opacity)
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * dists) # (N, K)
    
    # 7. Compute Transmittance (exclusive cumprod)
    ones = torch.ones_like(alpha[:, :1])
    transmittance = torch.cumprod(torch.cat([ones, 1.0 - alpha + 1e-10], dim=-1), dim=-1)[:, :-1] # (N, K)
    
    # 8. Compute Weights
    weights = alpha * transmittance # (N, K)
    
    # 9. Aggregate outputs
    rgb_map = torch.sum(weights[..., None] * rgb, dim=-2) # (N, 3)
    depth_map = torch.sum(weights * z_vals, dim=-1) # (N,)
    acc_map = torch.sum(weights, dim=-1) # (N,)
    
    # 10. Background Composite
    if white_bkgd:
        rgb_map = rgb_map + (1.0 - acc_map[..., None])
        
    return rgb_map, depth_map, acc_map
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Stability:** When $\alpha_i \approx 1$, transmittance becomes 0, effectively switching off contributions from subsequent pixels [S6, unofficial]. Adding epsilon ($10^{-10}$) to $(1-\alpha)$ prevents `cumprod` from becoming exactly 0 too early due to floating-point errors, ensuring stable gradients [S5, unofficial].
*   **Opaque Surfaces:** Once an opaque surface is encountered ($\alpha_i \approx 1$), $T_i$ for subsequent samples becomes 0, effectively switching off contributions from behind the surface [S6, unofficial].
*   **Density Bounds:** Raw density is passed through `relu` to ensure non-negative values, as negative density is physically invalid and breaks the exponential attenuation model [S5, unofficial].
*   **Infinite Distance:** The last segment distance is set to a large value ($10^{10}$) to approximate the infinite tail of the ray, ensuring that if the last sample has non-zero density, it contributes to the final color [S5, unofficial].
*   **Packed Samples:** The 'last_sample' background color mode is not implemented for packed samples (when `ray_indices` is not None) [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Concentrate-and-Scatter Method
A post-processing rendering technique for neural radiance fields that applies depth-of-field blur [S7]. It computes a signed defocus map based on concentrated depth, aperture parameters, and focus distance [S7]. Radiance is scattered from in-focus pixels to out-of-focus neighbors using a pixel-wise algorithm that traverses neighboring pixels and calculates scattering weights based on the defocus radius [S7].

### Transparency-aware depth estimation
A method to compute depth maps from NeRF for transparent objects by finding the first sample along a ray where density exceeds a fixed threshold, avoiding noisy depth maps produced by standard weighted sum estimation [S8, unofficial]. For each pixel, it iterates through samples $i=1$ to $N$, checking if $\sigma_i > m$ [S8, unofficial]. If true, depth $D[u, v]$ is set to sample distance $\delta_i$ and stops; otherwise, the pixel is considered empty/background [S8, unofficial]. Parameter $m$ is the density threshold, typically 15; low values cause noise, high values create holes [S8, unofficial]. Standard NeRF depth $D[u, v] = \sum w_i \delta_i$ produces noisy maps for transparent objects, while the threshold method produces cleaner, albeit potentially incomplete, depth maps [S8, unofficial].

### ScatterNeRF
Integrates the Koschmieder scattering model into the process of volume rendering to acquire a distinct understanding of scattering volumes and scene objects [S9]. It utilizes physical heuristic losses for scene reconstruction learning and allows optimization across various perspectives in multi-view sequences [S9]. Confirmed through field and controlled environment multi-view data, it surpasses current techniques in rendering, de-fogging, and depth estimation of foggy environments [S9]. It might encounter difficulties managing severe foggy environments with high fog inconsistency [S9].

### NeRD
Assumes full light source visibility, ignoring self-occlusions and assuming all light sources in the upper hemisphere above any surface are fully visible [S10, unofficial]. It accelerates rendering by representing environment lighting and scene BRDFs as mixtures of spherical Gaussians, enabling the hemispherical integral of incoming light multiplied by the BRDF to be computed in closed form [S10, unofficial].

*   **Strengths:** Differentiable, allowing end-to-end training of scene representations from images; simple to implement and integrate with standard deep learning frameworks [S3, unofficial][S4].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes single-path light transport, ignoring complex effects like caustics or multiple scattering [S3, unofficial]; requires dense sampling for high-quality results [S4].

*   **When Not to Use:** When physically accurate global illumination effects (multiple bounces) are critical, or when real-time performance is required with very high sample counts without optimization [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The NeRF Rendering Formula is derived from the discrete form of the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) [S3, unofficial]. It was popularized by the NeRF paper [S3, unofficial][S5, unofficial] and is also used in 3D Gaussian Splats [S3, unofficial][S5, unofficial]. The method approximates the continuous VRE by assuming single-path summation along the ray trajectory, avoiding the need for path tracing [S3, unofficial]. The learned parameters (color and opacity) are not strictly physically literal but serve as an approximation learnable from offline samples, effectively approximating surface rendering for opaque objects using a volume-based formulation [S4].
