# Standard volume rendering equation

Also called: Accumulation Rendering, Ray Marching without Lights, Standard Volume Rendering, Volume Rendering (No Lights), Volume Rendering Formula, Volumetric Ray Marching (Max's Quadrature), Volumetric Rendering Equation, Volumetric Rendering Formula

## Overview
The Standard volume rendering equation computes the final pixel color by integrating emitted and scattered light along a camera ray passing through a participating medium, attenuated by extinction [S1, S3, S10, unofficial]. It solves the problem of visualizing volumetric data (such as smoke, clouds, or neural radiance fields) by combining the background light transmitted through the volume with the light emitted or scattered by the volume itself [S1, S3, unofficial]. It differs from the general [Volume Rendering Equation](2098-volume-rendering-equation.md) by typically assuming no explicit light transport simulation (in-scattering from other light sources) in its basic form, relying instead on direct emission or ambient approximation, and from [Similarity Theory / High-Order Similarity Relations](10236-similarity-theory-high-order-similarity-relations.md) by using a direct integral solution rather than statistical similarity transformations [S1, S3, S10, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A ray defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$, bounded by near plane $t_n$ and far plane $t_f$ [S10, S29, unofficial]. A volume representation providing density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and color/radiance $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ at any 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ [S10, S26, unofficial]. A background color $\mathbf{C}_{bg}$ [S1, S15, unofficial].
*   **Output:** The final pixel color $\mathbf{C}$ [S9][S10, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray:** Parametric line $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ for $t \in [t_n, t_f]$ [S10, S29, unofficial].
*   **Samples:** A sequence of $N$ points $\{s_i\}_{i=1}^N$ along the ray, where $s_i = \mathbf{r}(t_i)$ [S11, unofficial]. The distance between adjacent samples is $\delta_i = \|s_i - s_{i-1}\|$ [S11, unofficial]. In uniform sampling, $\delta_i$ is constant [S14, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The continuous volume rendering equation is:
$$
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) \, dt + \mathbf{C}_{bg} T(t_f)
$$
where:
*   $T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) \, ds\right)$ is the transmittance from the near plane to $t$ [S10, S26, unofficial].
*   $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is the volume density (extinction coefficient) [S26, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ is the emitted radiance or color [S26, unofficial].

The discrete approximation (Max's quadrature) sums contributions from $N$ samples:
$$
\mathbf{C} = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i + \mathbf{C}_{bg} T_{N+1}
$$
where:
*   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity of sample $i$ [S14, S26, unofficial].
*   $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the accumulated transmittance to sample $i$ [S14, S26, unofficial].
*   $T_{N+1} = \prod_{j=1}^{N} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance through the entire volume [S14, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{c}_i = \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t_i), \mathbf{d})$ is the color at sample $i$ [S14, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of samples per ray. Typical values range from 64 to 128 for coarse/fine sampling in NeRF [S14, S22, unofficial].
*   $\sigma_i$: Volume density. Units are inverse length (e.g., $m^{-1}$) [S26, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$: Color/Radiance. Typically RGB [S26, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Define the ray $\mathbf{r}(t)$ from camera origin $\mathbf{o}$ through pixel center with direction $\mathbf{d}$, bounded by $t_n$ and $t_f$ [S10, S29, unofficial].
2.  Sample $N$ positions $t_i$ along the ray, typically equidistantly [S11, S14, unofficial].
3.  For each sample $i$:
    a. Query density $\sigma_i$ and color $\mathbf{c}_i$ at position $\mathbf{r}(t_i)$ [S10, S26, unofficial].
    b. Compute opacity $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S14, S26, unofficial].
    c. Compute transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ [S14, S26, unofficial].
4.  Accumulate color: $\mathbf{C} = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i$ [S14, S26, unofficial].
5.  Compute final transmittance $T_{final} = \prod_{j=1}^N (1 - \alpha_j)$ [S14, unofficial].
6.  Composite with background: $\mathbf{C}_{final} = \mathbf{C} + \mathbf{C}_{bg} T_{final}$ [S1, S14, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def standard_volume_rendering(ray_origin, ray_direction, t_near, t_far, num_samples, background_color, volume_query):
    """
    ray_origin: vec3
    ray_direction: vec3 (normalized)
    t_near: float
    t_far: float
    num_samples: int
    background_color: vec3
    volume_query: function(x, d) -> (sigma, color)
    """
    # Initialize accumulators
    accumulated_color = vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    accumulated_transmittance = 1.0
    
    # Sample points along the ray
    # Using uniform spacing for simplicity
    step_size = (t_far - t_near) / num_samples
    
    for i in range(num_samples):
        # Calculate sample position
        t = t_near + (i + 0.5) * step_size  # Midpoint sampling
        position = ray_origin + ray_direction * t
        
        # Query volume for density and color
        sigma, color = volume_query(position, ray_direction)
        
        # Compute opacity for this segment
        # alpha = 1 - exp(-sigma * delta)
        alpha = 1.0 - math.exp(-sigma * step_size)
        
        # Accumulate color contribution
        # weight = transmittance_to_sample * alpha
        accumulated_color += accumulated_transmittance * alpha * color
        
        # Update transmittance for next sample
        # T_next = T_current * (1 - alpha)
        accumulated_transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha)
        
        # Early exit if fully opaque (optional optimization)
        if accumulated_transmittance < 1e-4:
            break
            
    # Composite with background
    final_color = accumulated_color + background_color * accumulated_transmittance
    
    return final_color
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** If density $\sigma$ is very large, $\alpha$ approaches 1, and transmittance approaches 0. Floating-point underflow in transmittance calculation can cause artifacts if not handled [S1, S26, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The ray must correctly intersect the volume bounds. If the ray does not intersect, return background color [S1, S17, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Zero density results in no contribution and full transmittance [S26, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Rendering Formula](1362-nerf-rendering-formula.md)
The NeRF Rendering Formula transforms raw neural network predictions (density and color features) into final RGB, depth, and accumulation maps by discretizing the volume rendering integral along camera rays. It differs from generic volume rendering by explicitly handling sigmoid color activation, density noise injection for regularization, and the specific discrete weighting scheme required for differentiable training of implicit representations.

### [Discrete Volume Transmittance Integration](3905-discrete-volume-transmittance-integration.md)
Discrete Volume Transmittance Integration computes the fraction of light that survives extinction along a ray segment in a participating medium by numerically integrating the extinction coefficient. It sets itself apart from analytic methods (like [Analytic Transmittance Integration](5116-analytic-transmittance-integration.md)) by using fixed-step sampling to approximate the integral, making it suitable for heterogeneous media where closed-form solutions are unavailable. It builds on the [Standard volume rendering equation](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md) by providing the transmittance term $T$ required to weight emitted and scattered light contributions.

### [Sub-interval Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) Combination](3971-sub-interval-radiative-transfer-equation-rte-combination.md)
Sub-interval RTE Combination is a method for computing full-interval radiance and optical depth by partitioning the integration path of the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) at an intermediate point, allowing solutions from multiple slice planes to be combined. It solves the problem of visualizing participating media (such as smoke and fire) by rendering one partially transparent slice at a time and merging them using video hardware, rather than integrating over the entire volume in a single pass. It differs from [Standard volume rendering equation](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md) by processing data in discrete 2D slices rather than a continuous ray integral, and from [Discrete Volume Transmittance Integration](3905-discrete-volume-transmittance-integration.md) by relying on hardware compositing of pre-solved slice radiance rather than per-sample accumulation.

### [Volume Integral Attenuation](7263-volume-integral-attenuation.md)
Volume Integral Attenuation is a method for computing cumulative attenuated information along a ray in participating media, accounting for light angle and density decay. It differs from the standard volume rendering equation by explicitly integrating the product of incident light, density, and an exponential attenuation term over the ray parameter, rather than accumulating discrete sample contributions with pre-computed transmittance. It is distinct from NeRF Rendering Formula [1362-nerf-rendering-formula.md] which uses neural networks for density, and Discrete Volume Transmittance Integration [3905-discrete-volume-transmittance-integration.md] which focuses on the transmittance calculation itself.

### [Distance Sampling](8978-distance-sampling.md)
Distance sampling is a Monte Carlo technique used to estimate the Volume Rendering Equation (VRE) by importance sampling the non-recursive terms, specifically the medium extinction transmittance, to determine the free flight distance (distance to the next collision). It is a stochastic integrator that distributes samples according to transmittance along the current path segment, ignoring the overall shape of the medium. Unlike [Whole Volume Distance Sampling](9002-whole-volume-distance-sampling.md), which accounts for medium geometry via histograms, distance sampling assumes a homogeneous medium where the extinction coefficient is constant, making it simpler but less accurate for complex occluders.

### Neural Volumes rendering formula
Computes ray color using a transmittance formulation where opacity is treated as absolute and ray-independent, clipped to a maximum value of 1 [S7]. The transmittance is defined as $T_i = \min \left\{ 1, \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \exp(-\delta_i \sigma_i) \right\}$ and color as $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N (T_i - T_{i+1}) \mathbf{c}_i$ [S7]. On 8 synthetic NeRF scenes at fixed resolution $256^3$ with RMSProp optimizer, it achieves a mean PSNR of 26.05, SSIM of 0.893, and LPIPS of 0.160 [S7]. In this formula, $\alpha_i$ depends only on the opacity and sampling distance of sample $i$, whereas the Max formula makes it dependent on all preceding samples [S7].

### Physical Quantity Measurement via Optical Depth
Uses opacities in the radiative transfer equation to compute physical quantities (mass, momentum, kinetic energy) by measuring optical depth instead of intensity [S36]. Opacities are defined as $\alpha_M = \rho$ (mass), $\alpha_P = \rho v$ (momentum), $\alpha_E = 0.5 \rho v^2$ (kinetic energy), where $\rho$ is gas density and $v$ is velocity [S36]. Useful when outflows are misaligned with grid axes, making direct measurement from simulation data difficult [S36].

### Path Integral Formulation of VRE
A path integral formulation of the VRE where the measurement contribution function $f(X)$ includes transmittance $T$ and phase function $\rho_m$ for volume interactions, unlike the vacuum rendering equation which uses BRDF [S33, unofficial]. The functions $G$ (geometry), $L_e$ (emission), and $\rho_m$ (phase function) must differentiate between points on a surface (reducing to rendering equation) and points in the medium (applying transmission coefficients) [S33, unofficial].

### Multi-scale Volumetric Rendering Equation
A volumetric rendering equation describing radiative transfer in discrete random media, extending the conventional VRE to support spatially-varying particle size distributions (PSD) and multi-scale optical properties [S37].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ per ray, where $N$ is the number of samples [S14, S26, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per ray if density/color are queried on the fly, or $O(V)$ if pre-sampled [S10, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** In NeRF, with 64 coarse and 128 fine samples, rendering time is significant but manageable for offline training [S14, unofficial]. Neural Volumes achieves mean PSNR 26.05 on synthetic scenes [S7].
*   **Scaling:** Linear with number of samples and pixels [S10, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement, differentiable (enabling neural network training), handles arbitrary density fields [S10, S26, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow for high-resolution volumes without acceleration structures, assumes no complex light transport (in-scattering) in basic form [S1, S10, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Neural radiance fields, simple volumetric visualization, differentiable rendering [S10, S26, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Real-time rendering of complex participating media with multiple scattering, where path tracing or precomputed methods are preferred [S1, S31, unofficial].

## Background
The standard volume rendering equation is derived from the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) [S3, unofficial][S19]. It was formalized by Max in 1995 for discrete quadrature approximation [S14, unofficial][S35]. It became foundational for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S10, S11, unofficial]. The equation represents the integral solution to the RTE, describing radiance accumulation along a ray [S3, unofficial][S19].
