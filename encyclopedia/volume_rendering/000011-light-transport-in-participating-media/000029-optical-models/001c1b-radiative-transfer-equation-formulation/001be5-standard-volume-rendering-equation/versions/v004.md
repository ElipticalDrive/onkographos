# Standard volume rendering equation

Also called: Analytical Volume Rendering, Beer-Lambert Volume Rendering, Max volume rendering formula, Ray-Marching: Uniform Slab Rendering, Raymarching with Beer's Law, Standard volume rendering integration

## Overview
The standard volume rendering equation computes the color of a view ray by accumulating weighted contributions from samples along the ray, where each sample's contribution is modulated by the transmittance of all preceding samples [S3]. It treats opacity as a ray-dependent quantity representing the fraction of incoming light absorbed by each sample, distinguishing it from formulas that treat opacity as absolute and ray-independent [S3].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Primary ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the origin and $\mathbf{d}$ is the normalized direction [S4, unofficial].
*   Volume intersection bounds $[t_0, t_1]$ [S4, unofficial].
*   Step size $\Delta s$ [S3].
*   Volume density function or lookup returning extinction coefficient $\sigma_{ext}$ [S4, unofficial].
*   Surface reflected radiance $\mathbf{L}_{surf}$ from a previous pass [S4, unofficial].
*   Light source parameters: intensity $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}_{sun}$ [S4, unofficial].
*   Material coefficients: extinction $\sigma_{ext}$, scattering $\sigma_{sca}$, absorption $\sigma_{abs}$ [S4, S5, unofficial].
*   Phase function $p(\mathbf{d}_{in}, \mathbf{d}_{out})$ [S4, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Final pixel color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ [S4, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   `transmittance` (float): Accumulated transmittance from ray origin to current sample. Initialized to $1.0$ [S4, unofficial].
*   `scattered_light` (vec3): Accumulated in-scattered radiance. Initialized to $\mathbf{0}$ [S4, unofficial].
*   `step_transmittance` (float): Transmittance over the current step interval [S4, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Compute the primary ray for the pixel [S4, unofficial].
2.  Determine the entry ($t_0$) and exit ($t_1$) points of the ray within the volume bounds [S4, unofficial].
3.  Initialize accumulators: $\mathbf{T} \leftarrow 1.0$, $\mathbf{L}_{scat} \leftarrow \mathbf{0}$, and load surface radiance $\mathbf{L}_{surf}$ [S4, unofficial].
4.  Iterate through the volume from $t_0$ to $t_1$ with step size $\Delta s$:
    a.  Calculate sample position: $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} + \mathbf{d} \cdot (t_0 + k \cdot \Delta s)$, where $k$ is the step index [S4, unofficial].
    b.  Sample density: $\rho = \text{volume.sampleDensity}(\mathbf{p})$ [S4, unofficial].
    c.  If $\rho > 0$:
        i.  Get coefficients: `coeff = volume.getCoefficients(density)` [S4, unofficial].
        ii. Compute direct lighting: $\mathbf{L}_{direct} = \mathbf{L}_{sun} \cdot p(\mathbf{d}_{sun}, -\mathbf{d}) \cdot \mathbf{V}_{shadow}$ [S4, unofficial].
        iii. Compute incoming light: $\mathbf{L}_{in} = \text{coeff.scattering} \cdot \mathbf{L}_{direct}$ [S4, unofficial].
        iv. Compute step transmittance: $\mathbf{T}_{step} = \exp(-\text{coeff.extinction} \cdot \Delta s)$ [S4, unofficial].
        v.  Compute integration step for scattered light using the improved method by Sébastien Hillaire: $\mathbf{L}_{step} = \frac{\mathbf{L}_{in} - \mathbf{L}_{in} \cdot \mathbf{T}_{step}}{\text{coeff.extinction}}$ [S4, unofficial].
        vi. Accumulate scattered light: $\mathbf{L}_{scat} \leftarrow \mathbf{L}_{scat} + \mathbf{L}_{step} \cdot \mathbf{T}$ [S4, unofficial].
        vii. Update transmittance: $\mathbf{T} \leftarrow \mathbf{T} \cdot \mathbf{T}_{step}$ [S4, unofficial].
5.  Compute final color: $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{L}_{surf} \cdot \mathbf{T} + \mathbf{L}_{scat}$ [S4, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The transmittance to sample $i$, $T_i$, is the product of step transmittances:
$$ T_i = \exp \left( - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right) $$
where $\sigma_j$ is volume density at sample $j$ and $\delta_j$ is the step size [S3].

The rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ is:
$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N (T_i - T_{i+1}) \mathbf{c}_i $$
where $N$ is the number of samples, $T_{N+1}=0$, and $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the emitted/scattered light at sample $i$ [S3].

The extinction coefficient is the sum of absorption and scattering coefficients:
$$ \sigma_{extinction} = \sigma_{absorption} + \sigma_{scattering} $$
[S5, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_{extinction}$: Typical value for dense clouds is $0.03-0.04 \, \text{m}^{-1}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   $\sigma_{absorption}$: For clouds, typically $0.000005$ to $0.00003 \, \text{m}^{-1}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   $\sigma_{scattering}$: For clouds, typically $0.01$ to $0.06 \, \text{m}^{-1}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   $\Delta s$: Must correspond to the units of $\sigma$ (e.g., if $\sigma$ is in $\text{m}^{-1}$, $\Delta s$ is in meters) [S5, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Unit Mismatch:** If $\sigma_{ext}$ is high (e.g., $0.04$) but scene distances are small (single digits), no noticeable absorption occurs. Distances must correspond with coefficient values [S5, unofficial].
*   **Normalization:** The accumulated illumination sum must be multiplied by $\Delta s$ (or integrated properly) to normalize the result; otherwise, the value depends on the number of steps [S5, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** High extinction coefficients with large step sizes can lead to underflow in transmittance calculations if not handled carefully [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def standard_volume_rendering(ray_origin, ray_dir, t0, t1, step_size, volume, light_intensity, light_dir, surface_radiance):
    """
    Renders a volume using the standard (Max) volume rendering equation.
    Assumes ray_dir is normalized.
    """
    transmittance = 1.0
    scattered_light = vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    t = t0
    
    while t < t1:
        pos = ray_origin + ray_dir * t
        density = volume.sample_density(pos)
        
        if density > 0.0:
            coeff = volume.get_coefficients(density)
            sigma_ext = coeff.extinction
            sigma_sca = coeff.scattering
            
            # Sources do not specify shadow ray implementation; typically marches to light distance or volume bounds.
            shadow = 1.0 
            phase = volume.phase_function_eval(dot(light_dir, -ray_dir))
            direct_lighting = light_intensity * phase * shadow
            incoming_light = sigma_sca * direct_lighting
            
            step_transmittance = exp(-sigma_ext * step_size)
            
            if sigma_ext > 1e-6:
                integration_step = (incoming_light - incoming_light * step_transmittance) / sigma_ext
            else:
                integration_step = incoming_light * step_size
                
            scattered_light += integration_step * transmittance
            transmittance *= step_transmittance
            
            if transmittance < 1e-4:
                break
        t += step_size

    result = surface_radiance * transmittance + scattered_light
    return result
```

## Subtopics

### Neural Volumes rendering formula
Computes ray color using a transmittance formulation where opacity is treated as absolute and ray-independent, clipped to a maximum value of $1$ [S3]. The transmittance $T_i$ is defined as $T_i = \min \left\{ 1, \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \exp(-\delta_j \sigma_j) \right\}$ [S3]. Unlike the standard formula, the contribution of sample $i$ depends only on its own opacity and sampling distance, not on preceding samples [S3]. On 8 synthetic NeRF scenes at fixed resolution $256^3$ with RMSProp optimizer, the Neural Volumes formula achieves a mean PSNR of 26.05, SSIM of 0.893, and LPIPS of 0.160 [S3].

### [NeRF Rendering Formula](1362-nerf-rendering-formula.md)
Uses the standard volume rendering equation structure but typically implemented with neural networks predicting density and color. It computes weights $w = T \cdot (1 - \exp(-\sigma \cdot \Delta t))$ where $T$ is transmittance and $\sigma$ is density. The transmittance $T$ is computed using density $\sigma$ and segment length $\Delta t$. This formulation is mathematically equivalent to the standard equation but is optimized for differentiable rendering in neural radiance fields.

## Background
The standard volume rendering equation, often referred to as the "Max formula" in the context of neural rendering comparisons, is derived from the integral form of the radiative transfer equation [S3]. The specific discrete integration routine described here, which treats opacity as ray-dependent, is the standard approach in many volumetric rendering implementations [S3][S4, unofficial]. It is distinct from the "Neural Volumes" formula, which was proposed for neural implicit representations but is less physically accurate in terms of opacity accumulation [S3].
