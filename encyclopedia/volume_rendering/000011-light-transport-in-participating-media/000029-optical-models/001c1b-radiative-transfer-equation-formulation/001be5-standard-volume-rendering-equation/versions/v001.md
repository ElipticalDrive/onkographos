# Standard volume rendering equation

Also called: Max volume rendering formula, Naive sampling routine, Raymarching with Beer's Law, Simple Volumetric Ray Marching, Single in-scattering, Standard volume rendering integration, Volume Rendering via Raymarching, Volumetric Raymarching

## Overview
The standard volume rendering equation computes the color of a view ray by accumulating weighted contributions from samples along the ray, where the contribution of each sample is modulated by the transmittance (opacity) of all preceding samples [S1][S3, unofficial]. It differs from the Neural Volumes formula by treating opacity as a ray-dependent quantity representing the fraction of incoming light absorbed by each sample, rather than an absolute, ray-independent value [S1]. It builds on [Optical models](33-optical-models.md) by providing the specific discrete integration routine used to solve the emission-absorption and single-scattering components of the volume rendering equation.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Primary ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the origin and $\mathbf{d}$ is the direction [S2, unofficial].
*   Volume intersection bounds $[t_0, t_1]$ [S2, unofficial].
*   Step size $\Delta s$ (or `stepSize`) [S3, unofficial].
*   Volume density function $\rho(\mathbf{p})$ or lookup [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   Surface reflected radiance $\mathbf{L}_{surf}$ (from a previous pass) [S2, unofficial].
*   Light source parameters: intensity $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$, direction $\mathbf{d}_{sun}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   Material coefficients: extinction $\sigma_{ext}$, scattering $\sigma_{sca}$, absorption $\sigma_{abs}$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   Phase function $p(\mathbf{d}_{in}, \mathbf{d}_{out})$ [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Final pixel color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   `transmittance` (float3 or float): Accumulated transmittance from ray origin to current sample. Initialized to 1.0 [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   `scatteredLight` (float3): Accumulated in-scattered radiance. Initialized to 0.0 [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   `stepTransmittance` (float3): Transmittance over the current step interval.

**Steps:**
1.  Compute the primary ray for the pixel [S2, unofficial].
2.  Determine the entry ($t_0$) and exit ($t_1$) points of the ray within the volume bounds [S2, unofficial].
3.  Initialize accumulators:
    *   $\mathbf{T} \leftarrow 1.0$
    *   $\mathbf{L}_{scat} \leftarrow 0.0$
    *   Load surface radiance $\mathbf{L}_{surf}$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  Iterate through the volume from $t_0$ to $t_1$ with step size $\Delta s$:
    a.  Calculate sample position: $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} + \mathbf{d} \cdot (t_0 + k \cdot \Delta s)$, where $k$ is the step index [S2, unofficial].
    b.  Sample density: $\sigma_{ext} = \text{volume.sampleDensity}(\mathbf{p})$ [S2, S3, unofficial]. Note: In some implementations, density is multiplied by material coefficients to get $\sigma_{ext}$ [S3, unofficial].
    c.  If $\sigma_{ext} > 0$:
        i.  Compute step transmittance: $\mathbf{T}_{step} = \exp(-\sigma_{ext} \cdot \Delta s)$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
        ii. Calculate in-scattering contribution $\mathbf{L}_{in}$:
            *   Trace shadow ray to light source to get volumetric shadow $\mathbf{V}_{shadow}$ [S2, unofficial].
            *   Evaluate phase function: $p = p(\mathbf{d}_{sun}, -\mathbf{d})$ [S2, unofficial].
            *   Compute direct lighting: $\mathbf{L}_{direct} = \mathbf{L}_{sun} \cdot p \cdot \mathbf{V}_{shadow}$ [S2, unofficial].
            *   Compute incoming light: $\mathbf{L}_{in} = \sigma_{sca} \cdot \mathbf{L}_{direct}$ [S2, unofficial].
            *   *Simplified Ambient Approximation:* If shadow rays are not used, $\mathbf{L}_{in}$ may be approximated as $\sigma_{sca} \cdot \mathbf{L}_{ambient}$ [S3, unofficial].
        iii. Compute integration step for scattered light. Two common methods exist:
            *   *Naive/Riemann Sum:* $\mathbf{L}_{step} = \mathbf{L}_{in} \cdot \Delta s$ [S3, unofficial].
            *   *Improved Integration (Hillaire):* $\mathbf{L}_{step} = \frac{\mathbf{L}_{in} - \mathbf{L}_{in} \cdot \mathbf{T}_{step}}{\sigma_{ext}}$ [S2, unofficial]. This accounts for the variation of light within the step [S2, unofficial].
        iv. Accumulate scattered light: $\mathbf{L}_{scat} \leftarrow \mathbf{L}_{scat} + \mathbf{L}_{step} \cdot \mathbf{T}$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
        v.  Update transmittance: $\mathbf{T} \leftarrow \mathbf{T} \cdot \mathbf{T}_{step}$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
5.  Compute final color: $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{L}_{surf} \cdot \mathbf{T} + \mathbf{L}_{scat}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The transmittance to sample $i$, $T_i$, is the product of step transmittances:
$$ T_i = \exp \left( - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \Delta s_j \right) $$
where $\sigma_j$ is the extinction coefficient at sample $j$ and $\Delta s_j$ is the step size [S1][S3, unofficial].

The rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ is:
$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N (T_i - T_{i+1}) \mathbf{c}_i $$
where $N$ is the number of samples, $T_{N+1}=0$, and $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the emitted/scattered light at sample $i$ [S1]. Note that $T_i - T_{i+1} = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \Delta s_i))$ [S4].

The extinction coefficient is the sum of absorption and scattering coefficients:
$$ \sigma_{extinction} = \sigma_{absorption} + \sigma_{scattering} $$
[S3, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_{extinction}$: Typical value for dense clouds is $0.03-0.04 \, \text{m}^{-1}$ [S3, unofficial].
*   $\sigma_{absorption}$: For clouds, typically $0.000005$ to $0.00003 \, \text{m}^{-1}$ [S3, unofficial].
*   $\sigma_{scattering}$: For clouds, typically $0.01$ to $0.06 \, \text{m}^{-1}$ [S3, unofficial].
*   $\Delta s$: Must correspond to the units of $\sigma$ (e.g., if $\sigma$ is in $\text{m}^{-1}$, $\Delta s$ is in meters) [S3, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Unit Mismatch:** If $\sigma_{ext}$ is high (e.g., 0.04) but scene distances are small (single digits), no noticeable absorption occurs. Distances must correspond with coefficient values [S3, unofficial].
*   **Normalization:** The accumulated illumination sum must be multiplied by $\Delta s$ (or integrated properly) to normalize the result; otherwise, the value depends on the number of steps [S3, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** High extinction coefficients with large step sizes can lead to underflow in transmittance calculations if not handled carefully [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def standard_volume_rendering(ray_origin, ray_dir, t0, t1, step_size, volume, light_source, surface_radiance):
    """
    Renders a volume using the standard (Max) volume rendering equation.
    """
    transmittance = 1.0
    scattered_light = vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    
    # Pre-calculate light direction and intensity
    light_dir = normalize(light_source.direction)
    light_intensity = light_source.intensity
    
    # Current distance along ray
    current_dist = t0
    
    while current_dist < t1:
        # Sample position
        pos = ray_origin + ray_dir * current_dist
        
        # Sample density/extinction coefficient
        # volume.sample_density returns sigma_ext directly or density * material_sigma_ext
        sigma_ext = volume.sample_density(pos)
        
        if sigma_ext > 0.0:
            # Step transmittance: T_step = exp(-sigma_ext * delta_s)
            step_transmittance = exp(-sigma_ext * step_size)
            
            # Calculate in-scattering
            # 1. Shadow ray
            shadow = trace_shadow_ray(pos, light_dir, volume)
            
            # 2. Phase function
            # Assuming Henyey-Greenstein or similar, simplified here as constant 1.0 for isotropic
            # or dot product approximation if not provided. 
            # Standard implementation often uses a phase function p(cos_theta)
            phase = volume.phase_function(dot(light_dir, -ray_dir))
            
            # Direct lighting at sample point
            direct_lighting = light_intensity * phase * shadow
            
            # Out-scattering coefficient (part of extinction)
            # sigma_sca is typically provided by volume or derived from sigma_ext
            sigma_sca = volume.scattering_coefficient(pos)
            
            # Incoming light scattered towards viewer
            incoming_light = sigma_sca * direct_lighting
            
            # Integration step
            # Using Improved Integration (Hillaire) for better accuracy with fewer steps
            # L_step = (L_in - L_in * T_step) / sigma_ext
            if sigma_ext > 1e-6:
                integration_step = (incoming_light - incoming_light * step_transmittance) / sigma_ext
            else:
                integration_step = incoming_light * step_size # Fallback for zero extinction
            
            # Accumulate scattered light weighted by transmittance from camera to sample
            scattered_light += integration_step * transmittance
            
            # Update transmittance
            transmittance *= step_transmittance
            
            # Early exit if fully opaque
            if transmittance < 1e-4:
                break
        
        current_dist += step_size

    # Final color: Surface radiance attenuated by total transmittance + accumulated scattered light
    result = surface_radiance * transmittance + scattered_light
    return result
```

## Subtopics

### Neural Volumes rendering formula
Computes ray color using a transmittance formulation where opacity is treated as absolute and ray-independent, clipped to a maximum value of 1 [S1]. The transmittance $T_i$ is defined as $T_i = \min \left\{ 1, \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \exp(-\delta_i \sigma_i) \right\}$ [S1]. Unlike the standard formula, the contribution of sample $i$ depends only on its own opacity and sampling distance, not on preceding samples [S1]. On 8 synthetic NeRF scenes at fixed resolution $256^3$ with RMSProp optimizer, it achieves a mean PSNR of 26.05, SSIM of 0.893, and LPIPS of 0.160, compared to the standard formula's 30.57 PSNR [S1].

### NeRF Rendering Formula
Uses the standard volume rendering equation structure but typically implemented with neural networks predicting density and color [S4]. It computes weights $w = T \cdot (1 - \exp(-\sigma \cdot \Delta t))$ where $T$ is transmittance and $\sigma$ is density [S4]. The transmittance $T$ is computed using density $\sigma$ and segment length $\Delta t$ [S4]. This formulation is mathematically equivalent to the standard equation but is optimized for differentiable rendering in neural radiance fields [S4].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $S$ is the number of steps per ray. Shadow ray tracing adds significant overhead if included [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per pixel for accumulators, plus $O(V)$ for the volume data structure [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The standard formula is robust and widely used. On synthetic NeRF scenes, it achieves higher PSNR (30.57) and SSIM (0.950) than the Neural Volumes formula (26.05 PSNR, 0.893 SSIM) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Physically plausible, handles varying density well, supports complex lighting via shadow rays [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive due to many samples and shadow rays [S2, unofficial]. Sensitive to step size and coefficient units [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When physical accuracy is required and computational resources allow for many samples. When using implicit volumes or grid-based densities [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and approximate lighting is acceptable (consider single scattering or precomputed lighting). When the volume is very sparse and sparse voxel structures are more efficient [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The standard volume rendering equation, often referred to as the "Max formula" in the context of neural rendering comparisons, is derived from the integral form of the radiative transfer equation [S1][S3, unofficial]. It was popularized in computer graphics for rendering participating media such as fog, smoke, and clouds [S3, unofficial]. The specific discrete integration routine described here, which treats opacity as ray-dependent, is the standard approach in many volumetric rendering implementations [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is distinct from the "Neural Volumes" formula, which was proposed for neural implicit representations but is less physically accurate in terms of opacity accumulation [S1]. The method is foundational in both traditional ray-marching shaders and modern neural rendering pipelines like NeRF [S4].
