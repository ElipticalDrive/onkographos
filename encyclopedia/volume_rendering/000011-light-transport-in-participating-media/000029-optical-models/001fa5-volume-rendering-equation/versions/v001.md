# Volume Rendering Equation

Also called: Density Volume Ray Tracing, Kajiya & Von Herzen method, Radiative Scattering Algorithm, Ray Tracing Volume Densities, Transmittance Equation, Volume Rendering Integral, Volumetric Rendering Model

## Overview
The Volume Rendering Equation is the fundamental optical model for light transport in participating media, calculating the color of a pixel by integrating emitted and in-scattered light along a ray, attenuated by transmittance [S3, S5, unofficial]. It generalizes the rendering problem by accounting for emission, absorption, and scattering within a volume grid (e.g., clouds, fog, flames) [S2, S4, unofficial]. Unlike the [Absorption-only model](839-absorption-only-model.md) or [Emission-only Model](1016-emission-only-model.md), it combines both effects into a single integral, and unlike the [Single Scattering Model](1053-single-scattering-model.md), it serves as the basis for approximating full radiative scattering [S2, unofficial]. It differs from the [Standard volume rendering equation](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md) primarily in its historical derivation context and specific notation regarding opacity $\sigma$ as a product of density and cross-section, though they describe the same physical phenomenon [S4, S5, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Ray parameterization $r(t)$ for $t \in [0, 1]$, where $r(0)$ is the entry point (viewer's eye) and $r(1)$ is the exit point of the volume [S5, unofficial].
*   Density field $\sigma(x)$, representing the opacity per unit length at point $x$. Defined as $\sigma = A \rho$, where $A$ is the occlusion area (cross-section) and $\rho$ is the particle density [S4, unofficial]. Range $[0, 1]$ per unit length, though typically treated as a continuous value in integration [S4, unofficial].
*   Emission field $c(x)$, representing the emitted light intensity per unit area by particles in the medium [S4, unofficial].
*   In-scattered radiance $L_i(x_s, \omega)$, which depends on light source visibility (shadows), phase function, and light intensity [S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   $C(D)$ or $I_V$: The accumulated color/radiance at the camera for the ray traversing distance $D$ [S4, S5, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method operates on continuous fields defined over the volume. In implementation, these are typically discretized into:
*   **Density Grid:** A 3D array (or sparse structure like VDB) storing $\sigma(x)$ values.
*   **Emission Grid:** A 3D array storing $c(x)$ values (RGB vectors).
*   **Ray Data:** Origin, direction, and step size $\Delta t$ for marching.

### Formulas
The continuous solution for accumulated color $C(D)$ at distance $D$ from the viewer's eye (origin) is given by the Volume Rendering Integral [S4, S5, unofficial]:

$$ C(D) = \int_0^D \sigma(s) c(s) T(s) \, ds $$

where $T(s)$ is the transmittance from the viewer to point $s$ [S4, unofficial]:

$$ T(s) = e^{-\int_0^s \sigma(t) \, dt} $$

Alternatively, expressed as a differential equation governing intensity change along the ray parameter $s$ [S4, unofficial]:

$$ \frac{dC(s)}{ds} = \sigma(s) c(s) - \sigma(s) C(s) $$

For in-scattering contributions, the light arriving at the camera is the sum of in-scattered light at every point $x_s$ along the ray, attenuated by transmittance [S3, unofficial]:

$$ L(x, \omega) = \int_0^D T(x, x_s) \sigma_s(x_s) L_i(x_s, \omega) \, ds $$

where $\sigma_s$ is the scattering coefficient and $L_i$ is the in-scattered radiance [S3, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   $\sigma(s)$: Opacity/density. Product of particle density $\rho$ and occlusion area $A$ ($\sigma = A \rho$) [S4, unofficial].
*   $c(s)$: Emitted light intensity per unit area [S4, unofficial].
*   $T(s)$: Transmittance, probability of light passing through the medium without interaction [S4, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Initialize:** Set accumulated color $C = 0$ and accumulated transmittance $T = 1.0$ [G].
2.  **March:** For each step $s$ along the ray from $0$ to $D$:
    a. Evaluate density $\sigma(s)$ and emission $c(s)$ at current position [S4, unofficial].
    b. Calculate in-scattered radiance $L_i(s)$ if scattering is modeled [S3, unofficial].
    c. Compute contribution: $dC = \sigma(s) (c(s) + L_i(s)) \Delta s$ [G, S4, S3].
    d. Update accumulated color: $C \leftarrow C + T \cdot dC$ [G].
    e. Update transmittance: $T \leftarrow T \cdot e^{-\sigma(s) \Delta s}$ [G].
    f. If $T$ falls below a threshold, terminate early [G].
3.  **Output:** Return $C$ as the pixel color [S4, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def volume_rendering_equation(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_density, volume_emission, max_distance, step_size):
    """
    Computes the color along a ray using the volume rendering equation.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: (x, y, z) entry point of the ray.
        ray_direction: (dx, dy, dz) normalized direction vector.
        volume_density: Function sigma(x, y, z) returning density at point.
        volume_emission: Function c(x, y, z) returning emitted color at point.
        max_distance: Distance D to integrate to.
        step_size: Step size delta_s for numerical integration.
        
    Returns:
        accumulated_color: RGB color at the end of the ray.
    """
    accumulated_color = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    accumulated_transmittance = 1.0
    
    # Current position along the ray
    current_pos = ray_origin
    distance_traveled = 0.0
    
    while distance_traveled < max_distance and accumulated_transmittance > 1e-4:
        # Evaluate density and emission at current position
        sigma = volume_density(current_pos.x, current_pos.y, current_pos.z)
        emission = volume_emission(current_pos.x, current_pos.y, current_pos.z)
        
        # Calculate contribution from emission and absorption
        # dC = sigma * c * ds * T
        # Note: In-scattering L_i is omitted for basic emission-absorption model
        # If in-scattering is included, add sigma_s * L_i to emission term
        contribution = emission * sigma * step_size
        
        accumulated_color += accumulated_transmittance * contribution
        
        # Update transmittance: T_new = T_old * exp(-sigma * ds)
        transmittance_factor = math.exp(-sigma * step_size)
        accumulated_transmittance *= transmittance_factor
        
        # Move to next step
        current_pos = ray_origin + ray_direction * (distance_traveled + step_size)
        distance_traveled += step_size
        
    return accumulated_color
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Coordinate System Origin:** The derivation assumes the origin is at the viewer's eye, integrating from $0$ to $D$. This differs from some literature (e.g., 'Optical Models for Volumetric Rendering') which originates from the back of the volume. Care must be taken to align the integration direction with the transmittance calculation [S4, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Exponential decay in transmittance can lead to underflow if density is high and step size is large. Using floating-point precision carefully is required [G].
*   **Occlusion:** The generalized form does not specify the inner workings of $L_i$ and may fail to account for occlusion or interreflection unless explicitly extended [S1, unofficial].
*   **Density Definition:** $\sigma$ is defined as $A \rho$. If $\sigma$ is interpreted directly as density without the cross-section factor, physical accuracy may be lost [S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Single Scattering Model](1053-single-scattering-model.md)
Approximates in-scattering by considering only one scattering event. Choose when performance is critical and multiple scattering is negligible.

### [Standard volume rendering equation](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md)
The standard integral form often used in offline rendering. Choose for high-quality offline rendering where full scattering is needed.

### [Emission-Absorption Ray Marching](3171-emission-absorption-ray-marching.md)
Discrete implementation of the emission-absorption part of the equation. Choose for real-time applications where scattering is ignored.

### [Homogeneous Medium Transmittance](1022-homogeneous-medium-transmittance.md)
Analytical solution for constant density. Choose when the medium is uniform to avoid numerical integration.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of steps along the ray. Depends on density variation and step size [S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$ where $V$ is the volume of the density grid, if stored in memory. Sparse structures can reduce this [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Real-time evaluation is expensive. Approximations like screen-space god rays are used for performance [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Physically based, handles complex lighting and shadows within volumes [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive for full scattering. Requires efficient data structures for large volumes [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** High-quality offline rendering, or real-time with approximations (single scattering, screen-space).
*   **When not to use:** When performance is critical and simple opacity is sufficient (use [Absorption-only model](839-absorption-only-model.md)).

## Background
The Volume Rendering Equation was introduced by James T. Kajiya and Brian Von Herzen in 1984 in the paper "Ray tracing volume densities" [S2, unofficial]. They developed light scattering equations and presented an approximate solution to the full three-dimensional radiative scattering problem suitable for computer graphics [S2, unofficial]. The rendering equation, a related concept by Kajiya in 1986, generalizes the rendering problem for surfaces, while the volume rendering equation extends this to participating media [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is widely used in rendering clouds, fog, flames, and dust [S2, S5, unofficial].
