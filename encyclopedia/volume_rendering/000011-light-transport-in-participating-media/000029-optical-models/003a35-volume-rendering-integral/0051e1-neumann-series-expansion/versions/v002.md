# Neumann Series Expansion

Also called: Neumann Series, Neumann Series Solution

## Overview
The Neumann Series Expansion solves the Volume Rendering Integral by expressing the incoming radiance at a point as an infinite sum of contributions from light sources that have undergone $0, 1, 2, \dots$ scattering events [S1, unofficial]. It operates directly on the integral equation operators rather than explicitly sampling path geometries [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** The volume rendering equation in operator form, specifically the transport operator $\mathbf{G}$, the scattering operator $\mathbf{K}$, and the emitted radiance $L_e$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** The incoming radiance $L_i$ at a specific point and direction [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on operator notation rather than explicit discrete data structures in its definition [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{G}$: The transport operator, representing free-streaming and extinction [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{K}$: The scattering operator, representing the integral over the sphere of directions weighted by the phase function and scattering coefficient [S1, unofficial].
*   $L_e$: The emitted radiance (from volume sources or surface sources entering the volume) [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The incoming radiance $L_i$ is defined as the sum of terms where light is attenuated by $\mathbf{G}$ and scattered by $\mathbf{K}$:

$$
L_i = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (\mathbf{G}\mathbf{K})^n \mathbf{G} L_e = \mathbf{G} L_e + \mathbf{G}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{G} L_e + (\mathbf{G}\mathbf{K})^2 \mathbf{G} L_e + \cdots
$$ [S1, unofficial].

**Symbol Definitions:**
*   $n$: The number of scattering events [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{G} L_e$: Represents light that travels from the source to the point without scattering ($n=0$), but still undergoes attenuation [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{G} L_e$: Represents light that scatters once ($n=1$) [S1, unofficial].
*   $(\mathbf{G}\mathbf{K})^n \mathbf{G} L_e$: Represents light that has undergone $n$ scattering events [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Identify the emitted radiance $L_e$ contributing to the point of interest [S1, unofficial].
2.  Apply the transport operator $\mathbf{G}$ to $L_e$ to get the direct (unscattered) contribution [S1, unofficial].
3.  For each subsequent term $n=1, 2, \dots$: apply the scattering operator $\mathbf{K}$ to the result of the previous term's $\mathbf{G}$ application, then apply the transport operator $\mathbf{G}$ to the result of $\mathbf{K}$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  Sum the terms until a desired truncation depth $N$ is reached [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def neumann_series_expansion(L_e, G, K, max_terms):
    """
    Computes the incoming radiance L_i using the Neumann Series Expansion.
    
    Args:
        L_e: Emitted radiance function or value.
        G: Transport operator function (attenuation + streaming).
        K: Scattering operator function (integral over sphere).
        max_terms: Maximum number of scattering events to compute.
        
    Returns:
        L_i: Approximated incoming radiance.
    """
    L_i = 0.0
    term = G(L_e)
    L_i += term
    for n in range(1, max_terms):
        term = G(K(term))
        L_i += term
    return L_i
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Attenuation:** Even with 0 scatterings, attenuation ($\mathbf{G}$) must be applied [S1, unofficial].
*   **Truncation:** Truncating the series introduces bias [G].

## Subtopics

### [Path Integral Formulation](14634-path-integral-formulation.md)
Expresses the integral as a sum over paths of scattering events, allowing for arbitrary sequences of 2D surface locations and 3D positions. It transforms the measurement equation into a sum over path lengths $k$, where each term is an integral over a specific path space $\mathcal{P}_k^{\vec{c}}$. It is a prerequisite for constructing participating medium-aware bidirectional integrators. Choose this when explicit path sampling is required for variance reduction in complex scenes.
