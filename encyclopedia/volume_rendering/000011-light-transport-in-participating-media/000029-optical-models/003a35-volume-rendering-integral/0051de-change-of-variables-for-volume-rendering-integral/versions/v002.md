# Change of Variables for Volume Rendering Integral
Also called: Change of Variables: Volume, Surface Integral Formulation of VRI, Volume Rendering Integral Change of Variables

## Overview
This method transforms the volume rendering integral from a parameterization over ray direction and distance into a direct integration over the 3D spatial volume [S1, unofficial]. It replaces directional queries with direct spatial coordinates, enabling path integral formulations where the integration domain is the volume $\mathcal{V}$ rather than the solid angle $\mathcal{S}^2$ and distance $r$ [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A view point $\bx$, a function $f(\bx')$ representing the integrand, and the visibility function $V(\bx' \to \bx)$ [S1, unofficial].
- Output: The integral value over the volume $\mathcal{V}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- $\bx$: View point position (3D vector) [S1, unofficial].
- $\bx'$: Integration variable position (3D vector) [S1, unofficial].
- $\mathcal{V}$: The volume domain of integration [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. Start with the integral over solid angle $d\bo$ and distance $dr$ [S1, unofficial].
2. The position $\bx'$ is defined as $\bx + r\bo$ [G].
3. The differential volume element in spherical coordinates centered at $\bx$ is $dV = r^2 dr d\bo = \|\bx' - \bx\|^2 dr d\bo$ [G].
4. Therefore, $dr d\bo = \frac{1}{\|\bx' - \bx\|^2} dV(\bx')$ [G].
5. Substitute this into the integral. The limits of integration for $r$ (from 0 to the boundary) become the extent of the volume $\mathcal{V}$ along the ray, which is handled by the visibility function $V(\bx' \to \bx)$ and the domain $\mathcal{V}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formula:**
The change of variables for the volume rendering integral is given by [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \int_{\mathcal{S}^2} \int_0^{\|\raycast(\bx, \bo) - \bx\|} f(\bx + r\bo) \, dr \, d\bo = \int_{\mathcal{V}} \frac{1}{\|\bx' - \bx\|^2} V(\bx' \to \bx) f(\bx') \, dV(\bx') $$

**Symbol Definitions:**
- $\mathcal{S}^2$: The unit sphere of directions [S1, unofficial].
- $\bx$: The camera or sensor position [S1, unofficial].
- $\bo$: A unit direction vector on $\mathcal{S}^2$ [S1, unofficial].
- $r$: Distance along the ray from $\bx$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\raycast(\bx, \bo)$: The intersection point of the ray starting at $\bx$ in direction $\bo$ with the boundary of the volume [S1, unofficial].
- $f(\bx')$: The integrand evaluated at position $\bx'$, typically including emission and scattering terms attenuated by transmittance from $\bx'$ to $\bx$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\mathcal{V}$: The volume of the participating medium [S1, unofficial].
- $\|\bx' - \bx\|$: The Euclidean distance between the integration point $\bx'$ and the view point $\bx$ [S1, unofficial].
- $V(\bx' \to \bx)$: The visibility function, which is 1 if the path from $\bx'$ to $\bx$ is unoccluded within the medium, and 0 otherwise [S1, unofficial].
- $dV(\bx')$: The differential volume element at $\bx'$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Singularity at $\bx' = \bx$**: The term $\frac{1}{\|\bx' - \bx\|^2}$ becomes singular when the integration point coincides with the view point [G]. In practice, this is handled by the behavior of $f(\bx')$ (which may include transmittance $T(\bx \to \bx')$ that goes to 1, but emission/scattering terms must be well-behaved) or by excluding a small neighborhood around $\bx$ [G].
- **Visibility Calculation**: Computing $V(\bx' \to \bx)$ for every point in the volume is computationally expensive and may require additional ray casting or precomputed visibility data [S1, unofficial].
- **Boundary Handling**: The original integral limits $r$ to the boundary of the volume [S1, unofficial]. The volume integral implicitly handles this via the domain $\mathcal{V}$ and the visibility function [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def change_of_variables_volume_integral(view_point, volume_domain, integrand_func, visibility_func, num_samples):
    """
    Computes the volume rendering integral using the change of variables formulation.
    
    Args:
        view_point (tuple): The camera/sensor position bx.
        volume_domain (callable): A function that returns a random sample bx_prime uniformly distributed in V.
        integrand_func (callable): A function f(bx_prime) that returns the unattenuated radiance contribution at bx_prime.
        visibility_func (callable): A function V(bx_prime, bx) that returns 1.0 if bx_prime is visible from bx, 0.0 otherwise.
        num_samples (int): Number of Monte Carlo samples.
        
    Returns:
        float: The estimated value of the integral.
    """
    total_integral = 0.0
    epsilon = 1e-8  # Small constant to avoid division by zero [G]
    
    for _ in range(num_samples):
        bx_prime = volume_domain.sample()
        dx = (bx_prime[0] - view_point[0], bx_prime[1] - view_point[1], bx_prime[2] - view_point[2])
        distance_sq = dx[0]*dx[0] + dx[1]*dx[1] + dx[2]*dx[2]
        
        if distance_sq < epsilon:
            continue
            
        weight = 1.0 / distance_sq
        visibility = visibility_func(bx_prime, view_point)
        f_value = integrand_func(bx_prime)
        
        contribution = weight * visibility * f_value
        total_integral += contribution
        
    # Monte Carlo estimator scales by volume / samples [G]
    return total_integral * volume_domain.volume() / num_samples
```

## Subtopics

### Change of Variables for Surface Integration
This is the surface analog of the volume change of variables [S1, unofficial]. It transforms the integral over solid angle $\mathcal{S}^2$ to an integral over surface area $\mathcal{M}$ [S1, unofficial]. It replaces directional queries with direct spatial coordinates on the surface [S1, unofficial].
**Formula:**
$$ \int_{\mathcal{S}^2} f(\raycast(\bx, \bo)) \, d\bo = \int_{\mathcal{M}} f(\bx') \frac{|\vec{n}' \cdot \bo_{\bx \bx'}|}{\|\bx' - \bx\|^2} V(\bx' \to \bx) \, dA(\bx') $$
**Parameters:**
- $V(\bx' \to \bx)$: Visibility function (1 if visible, 0 if occluded) [S1, unofficial].
- $\bo_{\bx \bx'}$: Direction from $\bx$ to $\bx'$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\vec{n}'$: Normal vector at $\bx'$ [S1, unofficial].
- $dA(\bx')$: Differential area element at $\bx'$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Depends on the numerical integration method used over the volume. For Monte Carlo, it scales with the number of samples. The evaluation of the visibility function $V(\bx' \to \bx)$ can be a significant bottleneck if not precomputed or approximated [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity**: Depends on the representation of the volume $\mathcal{V}$ and the visibility function [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Provides a unified framework for path integral formulations, treating volume and surface contributions similarly. It can simplify the analysis of light transport in participating media by removing the explicit ray parameterization [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: The $1/\|\bx' - \bx\|^2$ term can lead to high variance in Monte Carlo integration if not sampled carefully. The visibility function is difficult to compute efficiently for complex scenes [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use**: When formulating path integrals for participating media or when a direct spatial integration approach is preferred over ray marching [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use**: For simple homogeneous volumes where analytical solutions exist, or when real-time performance is critical and ray marching is more efficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This formulation is derived from the principles of light transport in participating media and is used in the context of the volume rendering equation [S1, unofficial]. It is presented in the notes on the Volume Rendering Equation 2, which discusses the measurement equation and the path integral formulation of light transport [S1, unofficial]. The concept is analogous to the surface integral formulation widely used in path tracing and bidirectional path tracing [G].
