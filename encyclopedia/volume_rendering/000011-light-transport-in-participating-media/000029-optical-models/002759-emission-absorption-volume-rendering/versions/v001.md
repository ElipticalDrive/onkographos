# Emission-Absorption Volume Rendering

Also called: Back-to-Front Recursive Volume Rendering, Beer's Law Volume Rendering, Discrete Volume Rendering (Smokeview Approximation), Simple Volume Rendering, Smokeview RTE Discretization, Volumetric Rendering of Smoke

## Overview
Emission-Absorption Volume Rendering is a method for visualizing participating media (specifically smoke and fire) by approximating the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) while neglecting the in-scattering integral term [S1, unofficial]. It combines absorption and out-scattering into a total extinction coefficient and solves for radiance along a line of sight using a discrete back-to-front recursion [S1, unofficial]. Unlike the [Standard volume rendering equation](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md) which includes in-scattering, this model assumes that light is only lost from the view ray via extinction and gained via local emission, making it computationally simpler and suitable for real-time visualization of smoke flows [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the [Optical models](33-optical-models.md) family by providing a specific discrete solution for the emission-absorption case.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A ray defined by origin $x_0$ (farthest from observer) and endpoint $x_N$ (closest to observer).
*   Total extinction coefficient $\sigma_t(x) = \sigma_a(x) + \sigma_s(x)$ sampled at discrete points.
*   Emissive term $C_e(x)$ (radiance emitted per unit length).
*   Background radiance $C_0$ at $x_0$.
*   Number of segments $N$.

**Outputs:**
*   Final radiance $C_N$ at the observer.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on a discretized path split into $N$ segments of length $\Delta x = (x_N - x_0)/N$. Sample points are $x_i = x_0 + i\Delta x$ for $i=0 \dots N$.
*   $\sigma_{a,i}$: Absorption coefficient at $x_i$.
*   $\sigma_{t,i}$: Total extinction coefficient at $x_i$.
*   $C_{e,i}$: Emission term at $x_i$.
*   $\tau_i$: Segment transmittance from $x_i$ to $x_{i+1}$.
*   $\tau_i^{N-1}$: Cumulative transmittance from $x_i$ to $x_{N-1}$ (front-to-back from observer perspective, but indexed by distance from background).
*   $\hat{C}_{e,i}$: Discrete emitted radiance contribution of segment $i$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Discretize the Path:**
    Divide the ray interval $[x_0, x_N]$ into $N$ equal segments of length $\Delta x$. Define sample points $x_i = x_0 + i\Delta x$ for $i = 0, \dots, N$. Note that $x_0$ is the background (farthest) and $x_N$ is the observer (closest) [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Compute Segment Transmittance:**
    For each segment $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$, calculate the transmittance $\tau_i$ through that segment using the Beer-Lambert law approximation [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \tau_i = \exp(-\sigma_{t,i} \Delta x) $$
    Here, $\sigma_{t,i}$ is the total extinction coefficient at $x_i$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Cumulative Transmittance (Back-to-Front):**
    Calculate the cumulative transmittance $\tau_i^{N-1}$ from segment $i$ to the end of the ray ($N-1$). This represents the fraction of light surviving from segment $i$ to the observer. The recursion is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \tau_i^{N-1} = \tau_{i+1}^{N-1} \tau_i $$
    Initialize with $\tau_{N-1}^{N-1} = 1$ (or $\tau_N^{N-1}=1$ depending on indexing convention, effectively the transmittance after the last segment is 1 if no further attenuation occurs, but the sum stops at $N-1$) [S1, unofficial]. Specifically, the text defines $\tau_{N-1}^{N-1} = 1$ and recurses $i$ from $N-1$ down to $0$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Discrete Emission Contributions:**
    For each segment $i$, calculate the emitted radiance contribution $\hat{C}_{e,i}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \hat{C}_{e,i} = \sigma_{a,i} C_{e,i} \Delta x $$
    $C_{e,i}$ is the emission term, which can be derived from a blackbody temperature curve or a colormap [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Compute Final Radiance (Back-to-Front Summation):**
    The final radiance $C_N$ is computed by summing the attenuated background and the accumulated emitted light [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ C_N = \tau_0^{N-1} C_0 + \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \tau_i^{N-1} \hat{C}_{e,i} $$
    This formula weights the background radiance $C_0$ by the total transmittance and adds the emitted contributions from each segment, weighted by the transmittance from that segment to the observer [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Early Termination (Front-to-Back Implementation):**
    To allow early termination for performance, the sum is computed recursively from front to back (closest to farthest, i.e., $i$ from $N-1$ down to $0$). Define $\hat{C}_j^N$ as the partial sum of emitted contributions from segment $j$ to $N-1$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \hat{C}_j^N = \hat{C}_{j+1}^N + \tau_j^{N-1} \hat{C}_{e,j} $$
    Initialize $\hat{C}_N^N = 0$ [S1, unofficial]. The final radiance is then:
    $$ C_N = \tau_0^{N-1} C_0 + \hat{C}_0^N $$
    The recursion can be terminated early if the term $\tau_j^{N-1} \hat{C}_{e,j}$ becomes negligible [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def emission_absorption_volume_rendering(C0, sigma_a, sigma_t, C_e, N, dx):
    """
    C0: Background radiance at x0 (farthest point)
    sigma_a: Array of absorption coefficients [sigma_a,0 ... sigma_a,N-1]
    sigma_t: Array of total extinction coefficients [sigma_t,0 ... sigma_t,N-1]
    C_e: Array of emission terms [C_e,0 ... C_e,N-1]
    N: Number of segments
    dx: Length of each segment
    """
    
    # Step 2: Compute segment transmittance
    tau = [0.0] * N
    for i in range(N):
        tau[i] = exp(-sigma_t[i] * dx)
        
    # Step 3: Compute cumulative transmittance tau_i^{N-1}
    # tau_cum[i] represents tau_i^{N-1}
    tau_cum = [0.0] * N
    tau_cum[N-1] = 1.0
    for i in range(N-2, -1, -1):
        tau_cum[i] = tau_cum[i+1] * tau[i]
        
    # Step 4: Compute discrete emission contributions
    C_e_hat = [0.0] * N
    for i in range(N):
        C_e_hat[i] = sigma_a[i] * C_e[i] * dx
        
    # Step 5 & 6: Compute final radiance using front-to-back recursion for early termination
    # We compute the partial sum C_hat_j^N recursively from j = N-1 down to 0
    # However, to implement early termination, we iterate from j = N-1 (closest) to 0 (farthest)
    # and accumulate.
    
    current_partial_sum = 0.0
    final_radiance = 0.0
    
    # We need to compute C_hat_0^N. 
    # The recursion is C_hat_j^N = C_hat_{j+1}^N + tau_cum[j] * C_e_hat[j]
    # We can compute this iteratively from j = N-1 down to 0.
    
    # Initialize for j = N-1
    # C_hat_{N}^N = 0
    # C_hat_{N-1}^N = 0 + tau_cum[N-1] * C_e_hat[N-1]
    
    # Let's compute C_hat_0^N directly
    C_hat_0 = 0.0
    for j in range(N-1, -1, -1):
        # C_hat_j^N = C_hat_{j+1}^N + tau_cum[j] * C_e_hat[j]
        # In the loop, 'current_partial_sum' holds C_hat_{j+1}^N from the previous iteration
        # But since we are going backwards, we need to be careful.
        # Let's just compute the sum directly as per the formula C_N = tau_0^{N-1} C0 + sum(tau_i^{N-1} C_e_hat[i])
        # To allow early termination, we sum from i = N-1 down to 0.
        pass

    # Correct implementation for early termination:
    # Sum = sum_{i=0}^{N-1} tau_cum[i] * C_e_hat[i]
    # We iterate i from N-1 down to 0.
    
    emission_sum = 0.0
    for i in range(N-1, -1, -1):
        term = tau_cum[i] * C_e_hat[i]
        emission_sum += term
        # Early termination check: if term is negligible, break
        # if abs(term) < epsilon: break
        
    final_radiance = tau_cum[0] * C0 + emission_sum
    
    return final_radiance
```

**Splitting:**
If the ray passes through multiple meshes or sub-intervals, the solution can be combined. For an interval $[x_0, x_N]$ split at $D$ into $[x_0, D]$ and $[D, x_N]$, compute radiance $C_1$ and transmittance $\tau_1$ for the first interval and $C_2, \tau_2$ for the second. The combined radiance $C$ is derived by treating the output of the first interval as the background for the second [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Closed-Form Volume Rendering with Gaussian Transfer Functions](4362-closed-form-volume-rendering-with-gaussian-transfer-function.md)
A volume rendering technique where the transfer function is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian primitive, allowing the volume rendering integral over a line segment to be solved analytically in closed form, assuming linear variation of data values between sampled points. Choose this when analytical integration is preferred over discrete summation for smooth data.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ per ray, where $N$ is the number of segments. Early termination can reduce the average case.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store coefficients and transmittance values.
*   **Performance:** The method is computationally efficient because it neglects the in-scattering integral, which is the most expensive part of the full RTE [S1, unofficial]. It was designed for implementation in video hardware for real-time visualization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple implementation, fast, suitable for smoke and fire visualization where in-scattering is less critical than absorption and emission [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Does not account for in-scattering, which can lead to inaccurate lighting in dense or highly scattering media [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time visualization of smoke and fire where performance is critical and in-scattering effects are negligible or approximated by other means [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring accurate light transport with significant in-scattering, such as fog or clouds where multiple scattering is important [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method was implemented in Smokeview (Version 6), a tool for visualizing Fire Dynamics Simulation (FDS) data [S1, unofficial]. It was inspired by the software tool named Frames written by James Sims [S1, unofficial]. It represents the first method Smokeview implemented for visualizing smoke flow [S1, unofficial]. The method approximates the Radiation Transport Equation (RTE) by neglecting the in-scattering integral term and combining absorption and out-scattering into a total extinction coefficient [S1, unofficial]. It uses a discrete back-to-front recursion to solve for radiance along a line of sight [S1, unofficial].
