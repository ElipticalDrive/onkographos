# Emission-Absorption Volume Rendering

Also called: Back-to-Front Recursive Volume Rendering, Beer's Law Volume Rendering, Discrete Volume Rendering (Smokeview Approximation), Simple Volume Rendering, Smokeview RTE Discretization, Volumetric Rendering of Smoke

## Overview
Emission-Absorption Volume Rendering is a method for visualizing participating media, specifically smoke and fire, by approximating the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) while neglecting the in-scattering integral term [S1, unofficial]. It combines absorption and out-scattering into a total extinction coefficient and solves for radiance along a line of sight using a discrete back-to-front recursion based on Beer's Law [S1, unofficial]. Unlike the [Standard volume rendering equation](1165-standard-volume-rendering-equation.md) which includes in-scattering, or [Single Scattering Model](1053-single-scattering-model.md) which handles scattering explicitly, this method treats the medium as a non-scattering absorber with emission, making it computationally simpler and suitable for real-time visualization of smoke flow [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Emission-Absorption Ray Marching](3171-emission-absorption-ray-marching.md) by its specific discrete recursive formulation derived for grid-based data integration [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A ray defined by origin and direction; a volumetric dataset providing extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$, absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$, and emission term $C_e$ at discrete sample points along the ray; background radiance $C_0$ from behind the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Final radiance $C_N$ at the observer [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
The method operates on a discretized path of $N$ segments [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sample Points:** $x_i = x_0 + i \Delta x$ for $i = 0 \dots N$, where $x_0$ is the farthest point (background) and $x_N$ is the closest point (observer) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Segment Length:** $\Delta x = (x_N - x_0) / N$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Coefficients:** $\sigma_{t,i}$ (total extinction), $\sigma_{a,i}$ (absorption), $C_{e,i}$ (emission) at each sample point $x_i$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Segment Transparency:** $\tau_i$ for segment $i$, representing the fraction of light transmitted through that segment [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas and Definitions**
1.  **Total Extinction:** $\sigma_t(x) = \sigma_a(x) + \sigma_s(x)$, combining absorption and out-scattering [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Approximate RTE:** $\frac{dC}{dx}(x) = -\sigma_t(x)C(x) + \sigma_a(x)C_e(x)$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Continuous Optical Depth:** $\tau(a, b) = \exp\left(-\int_{a}^{b} \sigma_t(s) ds\right)$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Segment Transparency (Discrete):** $\tau_i = \exp(-\sigma_{t,i} \Delta x)$ [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Cumulative Transparency:** $\tau_i^{N-1}$ is the transparency from sample $i$ to the closest end of the volume ($N-1$) [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Emitted Radiance Contribution:** $\hat{C}_{e,i} = \sigma_{a,i} C_{e,i} \Delta x$ [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps**
The solution is computed recursively from front to back (closest to farthest) to allow for early termination [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Initialize:** Set cumulative transparency $\tau_{N-1}^{N-1} = 1$ and partial radiance sum $\hat{C}_N^N = 0$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Iterate Forward (Closest to Farthest):** For $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
    a. Compute segment transparency: $\tau_i = \exp(-\sigma_{t,i} \Delta x)$.
    b. Update cumulative transparency: $\tau_i^{N-1} = \tau_{i+1}^{N-1} \tau_i$ [S1, unofficial].
    c. Compute emitted contribution: $\hat{C}_{e,i} = \sigma_{a,i} C_{e,i} \Delta x$ [S1, unofficial].
    d. Update partial radiance sum: $\hat{C}_i^N = \hat{C}_{i+1}^N + \tau_i^{N-1} \hat{C}_{e,i}$ [S1, unofficial].
    e. **Early Termination Check:** If the remaining term $\tau_i^{N-1} \hat{C}_{e,i}$ is sufficiently small, terminate the loop [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Final Radiance:** Compute the final radiance at the observer:
    $$ C_N = \tau_0^{N-1} C_0 + \hat{C}_0^N $$
    where $C_0$ is the background radiance [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def emission_absorption_volume_rendering(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data, background_radiance, threshold=1e-4):
    """
    Renders a ray through a volume using the emission-absorption approximation.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: vec3, origin of the ray (camera position).
        ray_direction: vec3, normalized direction of the ray.
        volume_data: Object providing sample(t) -> (sigma_t, sigma_a, C_e) along the ray.
        background_radiance: vec3, radiance from behind the volume.
        threshold: float, minimum cumulative transparency to continue integration.
        
    Returns:
        vec3, final radiance at the observer.
    """
    t_start = 0.0
    t_end = volume_data.max_distance()
    num_samples = volume_data.num_samples()
    delta_t = (t_end - t_start) / num_samples
    
    tau_cumulative = 1.0
    partial_sum = vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    
    # Iterate from closest (i=0) to farthest (i=num_samples-1)
    for i in range(num_samples):
        t = t_start + (i + 0.5) * delta_t
        pos = ray_origin + ray_direction * t
        
        sigma_t, sigma_a, C_e = volume_data.sample(pos)
        
        tau_segment = exp(-sigma_t * delta_t)
        tau_cumulative *= tau_segment  # Now tau_cumulative holds tau_i^{N-1}
        
        if tau_cumulative < threshold:
            break
            
        C_hat_e = sigma_a * C_e * delta_t
        partial_sum += tau_cumulative * C_hat_e
        
    final_radiance = tau_cumulative * background_radiance + partial_sum
    return final_radiance
```

**Splitting Sub-intervals**
If the volume is composed of multiple meshes or sub-intervals, the method allows combining solutions [S1, unofficial]. For an interval $[x_0, x_N]$ split at $D$ into $[x_0, D]$ and $[D, x_N]$, compute radiance $C_1$ and optical depth $\tau_1$ for the first interval and $C_2, \tau_2$ for the second [S1, unofficial]. The combined radiance is derived by treating the output of the first interval as the background for the second [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Closed-Form Volume Rendering with Gaussian Transfer Functions](4362-closed-form-volume-rendering-with-gaussian-transfer-function.md)
Solves the emission-absorption integral analytically using Gaussian primitives, eliminating discretization error for smooth transfer functions. Choose this when high accuracy for smooth data is required and analytical integration is feasible.

### [Emission-Absorption Ray Marching](3171-emission-absorption-ray-marching.md)
A general ray-marching implementation of the emission-absorption model [G]. Choose this for flexible, non-grid-based ray traversal or when using hardware ray tracing [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ per ray, where $N$ is the number of samples [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per ray, as the recursion is iterative [G].
*   **Performance:** Fast and suitable for real-time visualization, as implemented in Smokeview [S1, unofficial]. Performance depends heavily on the density of the medium; sparse media benefit greatly from early termination [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple implementation; efficient for smoke/fire visualization where in-scattering is negligible; supports early termination [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Neglects in-scattering, leading to inaccurate shadows and lighting interactions in dense, scattering media; discretization error depends on sample spacing [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time visualization of smoke, fire, or thin clouds where in-scattering is not visually critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes requiring accurate light transport with significant in-scattering, such as fog, thick clouds, or biological tissue [G].

## Background
The method was first implemented in Smokeview (Version 6) for visualizing fire dynamics simulation data, inspired by the software tool "Frames" by James Sims [S1, unofficial]. It approximates the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) by neglecting the in-scattering integral term and combining absorption and out-scattering into a total extinction coefficient [S1, unofficial]. The derivation follows the Beer-Lambert law and is detailed in the NIST Technical Reference Guide for Smokeview [S1, unofficial]. It is widely used in fire safety visualization tools [S1, unofficial].
