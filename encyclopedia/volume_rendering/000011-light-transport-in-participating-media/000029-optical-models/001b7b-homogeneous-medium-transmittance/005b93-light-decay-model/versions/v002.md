# Light Decay Model

Also called: Light Decay Type, emLightsDecayType

## Overview
The Light Decay Model controls how quickly a simulator's emissive light fades when travelling away from fire light sources [S1]. It defines the geometric attenuation function applied to light intensity based on distance from the emitter [S1]. It differs from [Homogeneous Medium Transmittance](1022-homogeneous-medium-transmittance.md), which handles absorption and scattering within the medium itself, by handling the vacuum-like geometric falloff of the source emission before medium interaction [G].

## How it works
The method applies a scalar decay factor to the emitted radiance based on the Euclidean distance $d$ between the light source and the point of evaluation [G].

**Inputs:**
- $I_{emit}$: The initial intensity of the light source [G].
- $d$: The distance from the light source to the point being shaded [G].

**Outputs:**
- $I_{decay}$: The attenuated intensity at distance $d$ [G].

**Data Structures:**
The decay type is represented by an integer or enumeration value:
- `0`: None [G].
- `1`: Inverse [G].
- `2`: Inverse Square [G].

**Steps:**
1. Calculate the distance $d$ from the light source origin to the current shading point [G].
2. Determine the decay factor $F(d)$ based on the selected decay type [G].
3. Compute the final intensity: $I_{decay} = I_{emit} \times F(d)$ [G].

**Formulas:**
The decay factor $F(d)$ is defined as:

For **None**:
$$ F(d) = 1 $$
The light intensity remains constant regardless of distance, unless obstructed by other geometry or medium transmittance [S1].

For **Inverse**:
$$ F(d) = \frac{1}{d} $$
At a distance of 5 units, the intensity is $1/5$ of the emitter intensity [S1].

For **Inverse Square**:
$$ F(d) = \frac{1}{d^2} $$
At a distance of 5 units, the intensity is $1/25$ of the emitter intensity [S1]. This model is physically correct for point sources in a vacuum [S1].

**Parameters:**
- `emLightsDecayType`: The selected decay mode [S1]. Typical values are `Inverse Square` for physical accuracy or `Inverse`/`None` for artistic control or specific simulation requirements [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Zero Distance:** If $d=0$, the Inverse and Inverse Square models result in division by zero [G]. Implementations must clamp $d$ to a minimum epsilon value or handle the singularity explicitly [G].
- **Performance:** Inverse Square decay is computationally slightly more expensive than Inverse due to the squaring operation, though negligible in modern hardware [G].
- **Artifacts:** Sharp circular banding artifacts may appear if the decay is too aggressive or if the sampling rate is insufficient [S1]. This is often addressed by adjusting the [Light Cut-off](#light-cut-off) parameter [S1].

## Subtopics

### Light Cut-off
Also called: emLightsCutoff
A V-Ray specific control that specifies a threshold for light intensity below which the light will not be computed [S1]. This limits the effect of the Phoenix light to a certain distance around the simulator [S1]. Larger values cut away more light; lower values make the light range larger [S1]. At 0.0, the light is calculated for all surfaces, but rendering may slow down significantly [S1]. If sharp circular banding artifacts become visible, this parameter should be reduced [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per shading point for the decay calculation itself [G]. However, the effective range of computation is limited by the [Light Cut-off](#light-cut-off), potentially reducing the number of rays traced or surfaces illuminated [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$. No additional data structures are required beyond the distance calculation [G].
- **Strengths:** Provides physically accurate lighting falloff (Inverse Square) or artistic control (Inverse/None) [S1].
- **Limitations:** Does not account for medium absorption or scattering; must be combined with [Homogeneous Medium Transmittance](1022-homogeneous-medium-transmittance.md) for full participating media effects [G].
- **When to use:** Use Inverse Square for physically based rendering. Use Inverse or None for stylized effects or when the fire is embedded in a medium where geometric falloff is less relevant than volumetric scattering [G].

## Background
The Light Decay Model is a feature of the Phoenix for Maya plugin, specifically within the V-Ray integration [S1]. It allows artists to control the visual behavior of emissive fire sources [S1]. The concept of inverse square law decay is a fundamental principle in physics (radiometry), while inverse and none are artistic approximations [G].
