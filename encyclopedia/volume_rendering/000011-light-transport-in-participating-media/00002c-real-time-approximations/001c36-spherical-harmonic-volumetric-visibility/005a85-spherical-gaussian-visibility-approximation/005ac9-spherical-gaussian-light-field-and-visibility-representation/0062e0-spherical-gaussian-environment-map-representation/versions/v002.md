# Spherical Gaussian Environment Map Representation

Also called: SG Environment Map, SG approximation of rendering equation, SG environment lighting, SG mixture, SG sky, Spherical-Gaussian Sky Representation

## Overview
This method represents the distant environment illumination (sky and horizon) as a mixture of Spherical Gaussian (SG) directional lobes, replacing traditional high-resolution HDR environment maps or cubemaps [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficient, authorable, and analytically differentiable image-based lighting (IBL) by allowing diffuse irradiance and specular prefiltered radiance to be computed in closed form [S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard pipelines that use separate assets or baked textures for appearance and lighting, this approach uses a single SG asset to serve both rendering (visual sky appearance) and lighting (IBL), enabling live derivation of lighting parameters without cubemap re-convolution [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- An HDR environment map (panorama or cubemap) or a set of hand-authored SG parameters [S2, unofficial].
- Surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ and view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (for appearance) [S2, unofficial].
- Reflection direction $\mathbf{R}$ and roughness $\alpha$ (for specular) [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- **Appearance:** Per-pixel sky color (radiance) [S2, unofficial].
- **Diffuse Lighting:** Irradiance $E(\mathbf{n})$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Specular Lighting:** Prefiltered radiance $L(\mathbf{R}, \text{roughness})$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Optional:** Analytic light sources for shadow casting [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The environment is represented as a list of $M$ SG lobes [S1][S2, unofficial]. Each lobe $k$ is defined by:
- $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k \in S^2$: Lobe axis (direction) [S2, unofficial].
- $\lambda_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Lobe sharpness [S2, unofficial].
- $\mathbf{a}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3_+$: Lobe amplitude (RGB color) [S2, unofficial].

The system supports a layer-based architecture where each layer has a type (SG or Point) and independent channels [S2, unofficial]:
1. **Appearance:** Weight and Color [S2, unofficial].
2. **Diffuse:** Weight and Color [S2, unofficial].
3. **Specular:** Weight and Color [S2, unofficial].
4. **PromoteToLight:** Boolean flag [S2, unofficial].

### Formulas
The fundamental SG function is defined as [S1][S2, unofficial]:
$$ G(\omega; \boldsymbol{\mu}, \lambda, \mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{a} \cdot \exp(\lambda (\boldsymbol{\mu} \cdot \omega - 1)) $$
where $\omega \in S^2$ is the input direction, $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in S^2$ is the axis, $\lambda > 0$ is the sharpness, and $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3_+$ is the amplitude [S2, unofficial].

The environment map $L_i(\omega)$ is the sum of $M$ lobes [S1]:
$$ L_i(\omega) = \sum_{k=1}^{M} G(\omega; \boldsymbol{\mu}_k, \lambda_k, \mathbf{a}_k) $$

**Energy (Integral over sphere)** [S2, unofficial]:
$$ \int_{S^2} G \, d\omega = \mathbf{a} \cdot \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(1 - e^{-2\lambda}) $$
This is used for normalization and aggregate-to-ambient reduction for point sources [S2, unofficial].

**Product of two SGs** [S2, unofficial]:
The product of two SGs is another SG, defined by:
$$ \lambda_m = \|\lambda_1 \boldsymbol{\mu}_1 + \lambda_2 \boldsymbol{\mu}_2\| $$
$$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_m = \frac{\lambda_1 \boldsymbol{\mu}_1 + \lambda_2 \boldsymbol{\mu}_2}{\lambda_m} $$
$$ \mathbf{a}_m = \mathbf{a}_1 \mathbf{a}_2 e^{\lambda_m - \lambda_1 - \lambda_2} $$
This property enables the computation of the SG-SG inner product, which approximates the convolution of the environment with a BRDF lobe [S2, unofficial].

**Diffuse Irradiance** [S2, unofficial]:
$$ E(\mathbf{n}) = \int_{S^2} L(\omega) \max(0, \mathbf{n} \cdot \omega) \, d\omega $$
Computed per-lobe using a closed-form approximation of the SG convolved with the clamped cosine function (referencing Wang et al. 2009) [S2, unofficial].

**Specular Prefiltered Radiance** [S2, unofficial]:
$$ L(\mathbf{R}, \text{roughness}) \approx \sum_i \langle G_{env,i}, G_{brdf} \rangle $$
The GGX BRDF is warped into an SG about the reflection direction $\mathbf{R}$ with sharpness $\lambda_r \approx f(\text{roughness})$ [S2, unofficial]. The result is the sum of SG-SG inner products [S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
- **M (Total Lobes):** 128 is a common default for the environment map representation [S1].
- **Irradiance Lobe Budget:** Approximately 12 lobes are sufficient for diffuse lighting [S2, unofficial].
- **Appearance Lobe Budget:** Approximately 300 lobes are used for visual sky appearance [S2, unofficial].

### Steps and Pseudocode

#### 1. Frequency Split and Initialization
The sky signal is split into low-frequency components (sun, moon, skyglow, clouds) represented by SG lobes, and high-frequency point sources (stars) represented by an additive point layer [S2, unofficial]. Stars are excluded from the lighting bake by default as they carry negligible energy; night illumination is derived from the moon (one bright SG) and skyglow (low-$\lambda$ SGs) [S2, unofficial].

#### 2. Fitting (Baking)
If starting from an HDRI, fit the SG mixture using a greedy or EM algorithm with a perceptual/log-domain loss to prevent the sun from dominating [S2, unofficial].
- **Determinism:** The random seed for initialization must be an explicit asset parameter to ensure byte-identical re-baking [S2, unofficial].

#### 3. Runtime Evaluation
For a given surface point with normal $\mathbf{n}$ and view direction $\mathbf{v}$:

```python
import math

def dot(v1, v2):
    return sum(a * b for a, b in zip(v1, v2))

def norm(v):
    return math.sqrt(dot(v, v))

def sg_convolve_cosine(lobe, normal):
    # Standard closed-form approximation of SG convolved with clamped cosine [G]
    return lobe.a * max(0.0, dot(lobe.mu, normal)) * (1.0 + lobe.lambda_val) / (2.0 * lobe.lambda_val)

def warp_ggx_to_sg(reflection_dir, roughness):
    # Warp GGX BRDF into an SG about reflection_dir [G]
    lambda_r = 2.0 / (roughness + 1e-4)
    a_r = [1.0 / (4.0 * lambda_r), 1.0 / (4.0 * lambda_r), 1.0 / (4.0 * lambda_r)]
    return {'mu': reflection_dir, 'lambda_val': lambda_r, 'a': a_r}

def is_visible(pos, view_dir):
    # Simple visibility test for point sprites [G]
    return dot(pos, view_dir) > 0.999

def evaluate_sg_environment(lobes, point_layer, normal, view_dir, roughness):
    appearance = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    diffuse_irradiance = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    specular_radiance = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]

    # 1. Appearance (Radiance)
    for lobe in lobes:
        dot_prod = dot(lobe.mu, view_dir)
        radiance = [lobe.a[i] * math.exp(lobe.lambda_val * (dot_prod - 1.0)) for i in range(3)]
        appearance = [appearance[i] + radiance[i] for i in range(3)]

    for star in point_layer:
        if is_visible(star.position, view_dir):
            appearance = [appearance[i] + star.intensity[i] for i in range(3)]

    # 2. Diffuse Irradiance
    for lobe in lobes:
        contrib = sg_convolve_cosine(lobe, normal)
        diffuse_irradiance = [diffuse_irradiance[i] + contrib[i] for i in range(3)]

    # 3. Specular Prefiltered Radiance
    reflection_dir = [-view_dir[0] + 2*dot(view_dir, normal)*normal[0],
                      -view_dir[1] + 2*dot(view_dir, normal)*normal[1],
                      -view_dir[2] + 2*dot(view_dir, normal)*normal[2]]
    brdf_lobe = warp_ggx_to_sg(reflection_dir, roughness)

    for lobe in lobes:
        # Product of two SGs is an SG
        combined_lambda = norm([lobe.lambda_val * lobe.mu[i] + brdf_lobe['lambda_val'] * brdf_lobe['mu'][i] for i in range(3)])
        combined_mu = [(lobe.lambda_val * lobe.mu[i] + brdf_lobe['lambda_val'] * brdf_lobe['mu'][i]) / combined_lambda for i in range(3)]
        combined_amplitude = [lobe.a[i] * brdf_lobe['a'][i] * math.exp(combined_lambda - lobe.lambda_val - brdf_lobe['lambda_val']) for i in range(3)]

        # SG-SG inner product
        dot_mu = dot(lobe.mu, brdf_lobe['mu'])
        inner_product = [lobe.a[i] * brdf_lobe['a'][i] * (2.0 * math.pi / (lobe.lambda_val + brdf_lobe['lambda_val'])) *
                         math.exp((lobe.lambda_val * brdf_lobe['lambda_val'] * (dot_mu - 1.0)) / (lobe.lambda_val + brdf_lobe['lambda_val'])) for i in range(3)]
        specular_radiance = [specular_radiance[i] + inner_product[i] for i in range(3)]

    return appearance, diffuse_irradiance, specular_radiance
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Ringing/Blur:** SGs are a smooth low-frequency basis and suffer from ringing or blurring on point-like features (same failure mode as Spherical Harmonics) [S2, unofficial].
- **Star Representation:** Representing ~5,000 stars as SG lobes would annihilate the compression win [S2, unofficial].
- **Determinism:** Greedy/EM initialization in the fitter uses randomness [S2, unofficial].
- **Scope Limitation:** This method is limited to environment IBL (sky reflected in surfaces) [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Evaluation is $O(M)$ per pixel, where $M$ is the number of lobes [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(M)$ storage for the lobe parameters [S2, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Enables live derivation of lighting [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Closed-form analytic solutions for irradiance and specular; authorable; deterministic; decoupled appearance and lighting channels [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Cannot represent high-frequency details (stars) as SGs; limited to environment lighting; not suitable for volumetric scattering or object-to-object reflections [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Spherical Gaussians have been used in AAA games since approximately 2015, specifically in *Physically Based Lighting in The Order: 1886* (Ready at Dawn, SIGGRAPH 2015) [S2, unofficial]. The method builds on the mathematical properties of SGs for efficient approximation of the rendering equation [S1]. The specific application to environment maps with layer/channel decoupling and live derivation is described in recent engine design discussions [S2, unofficial].
