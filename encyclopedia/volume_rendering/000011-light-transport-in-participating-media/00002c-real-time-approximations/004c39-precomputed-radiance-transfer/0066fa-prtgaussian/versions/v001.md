# PRTGaussian

Also called: Efficient Relighting Using 3D Gaussians with Precomputed Radiance Transfer

## Overview
PRTGaussian is a real-time relightable novel-view synthesis method that combines 3D Gaussian Splatting for explicit scene representation with Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT) for appearance modeling [S1]. It solves the problem of efficient, high-quality relighting from multi-view One-Light-At-A-Time (OLAT) data by decoupling geometry and light transport [S1]. Unlike implicit representations like NeRF or field-based methods like NRHints, PRTGaussian uses an explicit Gaussian representation that allows for significantly faster training and rendering speeds while maintaining high visual fidelity [S1]. It is specifically designed for diffuse materials, approximating light transport using high-order spherical harmonics to balance detail and computational efficiency [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Multi-view OLAT dataset consisting of images of an object under varying directional lighting. The dataset typically includes 25 camera positions and 200 lighting positions uniformly sampled on the upper hemisphere [S1].
*   **Output:** A set of 3D Gaussians with learned radiance transfer coefficients, enabling real-time rendering under arbitrary lighting conditions (including environment maps) [S1].

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian $g_k$ is defined by the following fields [S1]:
*   $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D position.
*   $R \in SO(3)$: Rotation matrix.
*   $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Per-axis scale factors.
*   $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity value.
*   $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Albedo (diffuse reflectance).

The radiance transfer for each Gaussian is not stored directly but is regressed by a neural network based on the encoded position $\mu$ [S1].

**Neural Network Architecture:**
*   **Encoder:** Multiresolution hash encoding $\phi(\mu)$ [S1].
    *   Number of levels: 32 [S1].
    *   Features per level: 8 [S1].
    *   Hash table size: $2^{20}$ [S1].
*   **Decoder:** Four-layer fully connected Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1].
    *   Each layer has 256 neurons [S1].
    *   Input: Encoded position vector $\phi(\mu)$.
    *   Output: Radiance transfer term $T_i$ (coefficients in SH space) [S1].

**Spherical Harmonics (SH) Basis:**
*   Order $n = 9$ (up to 8th order, totaling 9 bands) [S1].
*   Basis functions $B_j(i)$ where $j$ ranges from $1$ to $n^2$ [S1].
*   Light coefficients $l_j \in \mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ are obtained by projecting the input lighting into SH space [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initial Geometry Reconstruction (Stage 1):**
    *   Sum and average the illumination from the multi-view OLAT dataset to create uniformly illuminated multi-view images [S1].
    *   Use vanilla 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct coarse geometry from these images [S1].
    *   Generate a point cloud from the reconstructed geometry to initialize the positions $\mu$ of the 3D Gaussians [S1].

2.  **Gaussian Initialization (Stage 2):**
    *   Initialize the 3D Gaussians using the point cloud from Stage 1 [S1].
    *   Initialize other Gaussian properties (rotation $R$, scale $s$, opacity $\sigma$, albedo $\rho$) [S1].

3.  **Radiance Transfer Learning (Stage 2):**
    *   For each Gaussian $k$, compute the encoded position $\phi(\mu_k)$ using the hash encoder [S1].
    *   Pass $\phi(\mu_k)$ through the decoder MLP to obtain the radiance transfer term $T_k$ [S1].
    *   The radiance transfer term $T_k$ represents the integral of visibility and diffuse reflection for the Gaussian's position and normal, precomputed in SH space [S1].

4.  **Rendering:**
    *   Project each 3D Gaussian onto the 2D image plane. The 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is transformed to 2D covariance $\Sigma'$ [S1]:
        $$ \Sigma' = J V \Sigma V^T J^T $$
        where $J \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 3}$ is the Jacobian of the projective transformation, $V \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is the viewing transformation, and $\Sigma = R \text{diag}(s) \text{diag}(s)^T R^T$ [S1].
    *   Compute the opacity $\alpha_k$ for each Gaussian based on $\Sigma'$ and $\sigma$ [S1].
    *   Calculate the color $c_k$ for each Gaussian using the PRT equation [S1]:
        $$ c_k = \rho_k \sum_{j=1}^{n^2} l_j T_{k,j} $$
        where $\rho_k$ is the albedo, $l_j$ are the SH coefficients of the current lighting, and $T_{k,j}$ are the regressed transfer coefficients [S1].
    *   Render the final pixel color $C_p$ using alpha compositing over the sorted list of Gaussians $N$ [S1]:
        $$ C_p = \sum_{k \in N} c_k \alpha_k \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$

5.  **Training Optimization:**
    *   Compute the loss $L$ between the rendered image and the ground truth image [S1]:
        $$ L = (1 - \lambda)L1 + \lambda L_{D-SSIM} $$
        where $\lambda = 0.2$ [S1].
    *   Update the parameters of the encoder and decoder, as well as all Gaussian properties ($\mu, R, s, \sigma, \rho$), using the gradient of the loss [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Hard Shadows:** The low-frequency nature of the SH basis makes it inherently difficult to capture high-frequency effects such as hard shadows [S1].
*   **Specular Highlights:** The method currently only considers diffuse materials and does not account for specular highlights or other view-dependent effects [S1].
*   **Initialization:** Random initialization of Gaussians in a cube leads to poor results due to high ambiguity in inverse rendering; initial geometry reconstruction is critical [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** Approximately 15 minutes per scene on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 [S1]. This is significantly faster than field-based methods like NRHints, which take approximately 28 hours [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Real-time performance, with rendering times of approximately 0.0030 seconds per frame on the same hardware [S1]. This is orders of magnitude faster than NRHints (17.95s per frame) [S1].
*   **Quality:** Achieves a PSNR of 33.63 and SSIM of 0.9438 on synthetic data, compared to NRHints' PSNR of 30.77 and SSIM of 0.9729 [S1]. The method trades a slight decrease in SSIM for a significant gain in PSNR and speed [S1].
*   **Memory:** Memory usage is determined by the number of Gaussians and the hash table size ($2^{20}$ entries) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Explicit representation allows for efficient forward rendering; fast training and inference; high-quality relighting for diffuse objects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to diffuse materials; struggles with high-frequency details like hard shadows due to SH basis limitations; requires multi-view OLAT data for training [S1].

## Background
PRTGaussian was introduced by Libo Zhang, Yuxuan Han, Wenbin Lin, Jingwang Ling, and Feng Xu from Tsinghua University in 2024 [S1]. The method builds upon the principles of Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [S1]. It addresses the challenges of inverse rendering, specifically the ill-posed nature of decoupling lighting and reflectance, by leveraging the efficiency of explicit Gaussian representations [S1]. The work was presented at the Asia-Pacific Signal and Information Processing Association Annual Summit and Conference (APSIPA ASC) in 2025 [S1].
