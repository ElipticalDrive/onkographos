# Volumetric Fur Rendering with LDPRT

Also called: Lengyel et al. fur with PRT, Microgeometry PRT, PRT Shell Textures, Volumetric Fur with PRT

## Overview
This method renders realistic microgeometry (specifically fur) with global lighting effects, such as self-shadowing, at real-time frame rates by combining a volumetric fur model with Local, Deformable Precomputed Radiance Transfer (LDPRT) [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of capturing microgeometric self-shadowing in a volumetric fur model by precomputing light attenuation to each voxel in the fur volume and rendering the volume using shell textures [S1, unofficial]. Unlike general participating media methods, it specifically models the microgeometry of fur hairs using a multi-layered shell approach and defers the conversion of illuminance to luminous exitance to the reconstruction phase, allowing for heterogeneous lighting models [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates in four main phases: creating the volumetric fur model, precomputing illumination, rendering the volume, and reconstructing the lighting.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Target surface geometry, light direction vector $\mathbf{d}$, fur parameters (base width ranges, falloff rates, curl trajectories), and voxel grid resolution.
*   **Outputs:** A rendered image of the fur volume with self-shadowing effects.

### Data Structures
*   **Volumetric Fur Model:** A 3D grid (voxel volume) representing the fur. The volume is constructed by sampling the target geometry using a 3D box filter [S1, unofficial].
*   **Shell Textures:** The volume is stored and rendered using concentric 3D rings or shells. Shells are transparent everywhere except at the intersection of the shell and the microgeometry [S1, unofficial].
*   **Illumination Data:** Precomputed attenuation values stored per voxel.

### Steps

1.  **Create a realistic volumetric fur model:**
    *   Sample the target geometry with a 3D box filter to define the base of the fur volume [S1, unofficial].
    *   Model hairs with random base width, linear falloff, and parabolic curl trajectory in a random direction [S1, unofficial].
    *   Implement two layers of fur:
        *   **Undercoat:** Short, dense, small base width.
        *   **Overcoat:** Longer, thicker, larger base width [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Antialiasing:** To prevent jagged edges, approximate a box filter for each voxel the hair passes through. Slice the voxel with horizontal planes, calculate the intersecting hair area for each plane, and average the results [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Simplification:** Restrict hair width to at most 1 pixel. This limits intersections to 1, 2, 3, or 4 adjacent pixels per level. Compute areas using circle, wedge, and chord formulas for these specific cases [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Precompute illumination of the fur volume:**
    *   Calculate light attenuation to each voxel in the fur volume. This corresponds to Step 1 of Kajiya's method [S1, unofficial].
    *   Ignore scattering and microgeometry lighting models (Steps 2 and 3 of Kajiya's method are deferred or omitted) [S1, unofficial].
    *   The calculation assumes the light is at infinity in the direction defined by vector $\mathbf{d}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   For each voxel, march a ray from the center in direction $\mathbf{d}$ until it leaves the volume. Add the density of each voxel hit scaled by the length of the ray that passed through it [S1, unofficial].
    *   The transparency $T$ is calculated using the texel version of the attenuation formula:
        $$ T = e^{-r \sum_{t_{start}}^{t_{end}} \rho(\mathbf{e} + t\mathbf{d})} $$
        where:
        *   $T$ is the transparency.
        *   $r$ is the density factor.
        *   $\rho$ is the 3D density function.
        *   $\mathbf{e}$ is the ray origin (center of the voxel).
        *   $\mathbf{d}$ is the light direction vector.
        *   $t$ is the distance along the ray.
        *   $t_{start}$ and $t_{end}$ are the ray bounds within the volume.
        *   The sum replaces the integral to account for the discrete nature of the texel model, ensuring non-zero contributions from surface intersections [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Render the volume:**
    *   Render the volume using alpha blending on graphics hardware. This step corresponds to Step 3 of Kajiya's method: calculating the attenuation of light from the surface to the eye [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use the shell textures defined in Step 1.

4.  **Reconstruct the lighting:**
    *   Reconstruct the final lighting by applying the precomputed attenuation to the surface lighting.
    *   The conversion of illuminance to luminous exitance (Step 2 of Kajiya's method) is pushed back to this phase, allowing for heterogeneous lighting models that vary over position and/or time, although the basic implementation simply displays the light reaching each voxel [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def create_fur_volume(geometry, params):
    """
    Creates a volumetric fur model by sampling geometry and generating hair shells.
    """
    volume = initialize_3d_grid(geometry.bounds)
    
    # Sample geometry to define base
    base_points = sample_geometry_with_box_filter(geometry)
    
    for point in base_points:
        # Determine layer (undercoat or overcoat)
        layer = determine_layer(point)
        
        # Generate hair parameters
        base_width = random_range(layer.width_range)
        trajectory = parabolic_curl(random_direction())
        
        # Generate hair along trajectory
        hair = generate_hair(point, base_width, trajectory, layer.falloff)
        
        # Antialiasing: Approximate box filter for each voxel
        for voxel in hair.intersected_voxels:
            # Slice voxel with horizontal planes
            planes = slice_voxel(voxel)
            total_area = 0
            for plane in planes:
                # Calculate intersecting hair area
                area = calculate_intersection_area(hair, plane)
                total_area += area
            # Average results
            voxel.density = total_area / len(planes)
            
    return volume

def precompute_illumination(volume, light_direction):
    """
    Precomputes light attenuation for each voxel in the fur volume.
    """
    illumination = initialize_3d_grid(volume.bounds)
    
    for voxel in volume.voxels:
        # Ray march from voxel center in light direction
        ray_origin = voxel.center
        ray_direction = light_direction
        
        # Calculate transparency using texel sum formula
        # T = exp(-r * sum(rho(e + t*d)))
        density_sum = 0
        t = ray_origin
        while t is within volume bounds:
            current_voxel = get_voxel_at(volume, t)
            if current_voxel is not None:
                # Scale density by ray length through voxel
                ray_length = get_ray_length_through_voxel(ray_origin, ray_direction, current_voxel)
                density_sum += current_voxel.density * ray_length
            t += ray_direction * step_size
            
        # Apply density factor r
        transparency = exp(-params.density_factor * density_sum)
        illumination[voxel] = transparency
        
    return illumination

def render_fur(volume, illumination, camera):
    """
    Renders the fur volume using shell textures and alpha blending.
    """
    image = empty_image(camera.resolution)
    
    # Render shells back-to-front for alpha blending
    for shell in volume.shells:
        # Render shell geometry
        shell_image = render_shell(shell, camera)
        
        # Apply precomputed illumination as alpha/opacity
        # Note: The specific blending of illumination data with shell geometry
        # is implementation-dependent but relies on the precomputed T values.
        final_shell = apply_illumination(shell_image, illumination, shell)
        
        # Alpha blend into image
        image = alpha_blend(image, final_shell)
        
    return image
```

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Hair Width:** Restricted to at most 1 pixel to simplify intersection calculations to 1, 2, 3, or 4 adjacent pixels [S1, unofficial].
*   **Density Factor ($r$):** A scaling factor for the density function. Not given in the sources.
*   **Scattering:** The method currently ignores scattering. Adding a scattering term could increase realism [S1, unofficial].
*   **Lighting Model:** The current implementation does not take advantage of heterogeneous lighting models, simply displaying light reaching each voxel. This is a limitation of the specific implementation described, not necessarily the method itself [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Precomputation is $O(V \cdot N)$ where $V$ is the number of voxels and $N$ is the number of light directions or steps per ray. Rendering is $O(S)$ where $S$ is the number of shells.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$ for storing the volume and illumination data.
*   **Performance:** Capable of real-time frame rates for microgeometry rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Captures microgeometric self-shadowing; real-time performance; supports heterogeneous lighting models in reconstruction.
*   **Limitations:** Ignores scattering; implementation may not fully exploit heterogeneous lighting; restricted hair width for antialiasing simplification.

## Background
The method is based on the work of Lengyel et al. [2001] for real-time fur rendering and combines it with Local, Deformable Precomputed Radiance Transfer (LDPRT) [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the lighting model for hair developed by Kajiya et al. [1989], specifically adapting their three-step process (attenuation to volume, microgeometry lighting, attenuation to eye) by precomputing only the first step and deferring the others [S1, unofficial]. The use of shell textures for microgeometry rendering is also derived from Lengyel et al. [S1, unofficial].
