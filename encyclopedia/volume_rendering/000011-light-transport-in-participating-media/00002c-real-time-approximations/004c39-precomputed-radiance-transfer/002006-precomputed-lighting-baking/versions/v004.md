# Precomputed Lighting (Baking)

Also called: Baking Lighting, Baking the lighting, Direct Lighting Caching, Precomputed Light Transmissivity, Precomputed Transfer Function

## Overview
Precomputed Lighting (Baking) is a real-time volume rendering optimization that accelerates illumination by pre-calculating the incident radiance term ($L_i$) for each voxel in a grid and storing it in a texture, allowing the renderer to skip evaluating $L_i$ during the primary raymarching pass [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. Unlike [Voxel Cone Tracing](1454-voxel-cone-tracing.md) or [Froxel-based Volumetric Rendering](1919-froxel-based-volumetric-rendering.md), which approximate scattering and visibility dynamically per frame, this method decouples the expensive light transport calculation from the view-dependent rendering loop [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from [Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT)](9429-precomputed-radiance-transfer-prt.md) by storing absolute radiance values rather than transfer functions, making it faster at runtime but less flexible to dynamic lighting changes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D voxel grid representing the participating medium (density $\sigma_t$, absorption $\sigma_a$, scattering $\sigma_s$), light source positions, intensities, and directions.
*   **Output:** A 3D texture (or volume) storing the precomputed incident radiance ($L_i$) for each voxel.

**Data Structures:**
The precomputed lighting is stored in a 3D texture array or a single 3D texture.
*   **Format:** Typically 16-bit or 32-bit floating point per channel (RGB) to accommodate high dynamic range lighting.
*   **Resolution:** The resolution of the baking texture can be lower than the rendering voxel grid resolution because light attenuation and scattering are spatially smooth (blurry) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Layout:** To optimize cache performance during the baking pass, data should be iterated in memory order (e.g., x-fastest, z-slowest) [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Baking Pass (Precomputation):**
    For each voxel center $(x, y, z)$ in the grid:
    a.  Cast a ray from the voxel center towards each light source in the scene.
    b.  Perform a secondary raymarch (or shadow march) from the voxel center to the light source to calculate transmissivity (shadowing) [S2, unofficial].
    c.  Compute the incident radiance $L_i(x, y, z)$ by integrating the light contribution attenuated by the medium along the path to the light [S1, S2, unofficial].
    d.  Store the resulting $L_i$ vector in the baking texture at index $(x, y, z)$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Rendering Pass (Runtime):**
    For each camera ray:
    a.  Perform standard volumetric raymarching (e.g., front-to-back compositing).
    b.  At each sample point, sample the baking texture using trilinear interpolation to retrieve the precomputed $L_i$ [S2, unofficial].
    c.  Use the retrieved $L_i$ in the radiative transfer equation, skipping the expensive light visibility calculation [S1, unofficial].
    d.  Optionally, combine with a few real-time light samples for high-frequency details if the cache resolution is low [S3, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The incident radiance $L_i$ at voxel $v$ from a light source $L$ is computed as:
$$ L_i(v) = \int_{0}^{d_{light}} L_{source} \cdot T(v, t) \, dt $$
Where:
*   $L_{source}$ is the radiance emitted by the light.
*   $d_{light}$ is the distance to the light source.
*   $T(v, t)$ is the transmissivity from voxel $v$ to point $t$ along the ray to the light, calculated as $T(v, t) = e^{-\int_{v}^{t} \sigma_t(s) ds}$.
*   $\sigma_t$ is the extinction coefficient of the medium.

At runtime, the radiance $L$ accumulated at a sample point is:
$$ L = L_{in} \cdot T_{camera} + L_i(v) \cdot (1 - T_{camera}) $$
Where $L_{in}$ is the incoming radiance from the camera ray and $T_{camera}$ is the transmissivity from the camera to the sample point [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Cache Resolution:** Can be significantly lower than the simulation voxel resolution (e.g., 1/2 or 1/4 scale) due to the blurring nature of volumetric light [S3, unofficial].
*   **Light Samples:** In hybrid approaches, a small number of real-time light samples (~2) may be performed before sampling the cache to capture sharp shadows [S3, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Dynamic Lighting:** The technique requires re-baking whenever the lighting configuration (position, intensity, color) or the medium simulation (density changes) changes [S1, unofficial].
*   **View Independence:** The baked data is view-independent but scene and light-source dependent [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Ensure the baking texture format supports sufficient dynamic range to prevent clipping of bright light sources [S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def bake_lighting(grid, lights, resolution):
    """
    Precomputes incident radiance for each voxel.
    grid: 3D array of density/extinction coefficients.
    lights: List of light sources with position, intensity, direction.
    resolution: Dimensions of the voxel grid.
    Returns: 3D texture of incident radiance.
    """
    baking_texture = create_3d_texture(resolution, channels=3)
    
    # Iterate in memory-friendly order (x-fastest)
    for z in range(resolution.z):
        for y in range(resolution.y):
            for x in range(resolution.x):
                voxel_pos = get_voxel_center(x, y, z)
                total_radiance = vec3(0.0)
                
                for light in lights:
                    # Cast ray from voxel to light
                    ray_origin = voxel_pos
                    ray_direction = normalize(light.position - voxel_pos)
                    distance_to_light = length(light.position - voxel_pos)
                    
                    # Calculate transmissivity along the path to the light
                    transmissivity = 1.0
                    steps = calculate_steps(distance_to_light, grid)
                    
                    for step in range(steps):
                        sample_pos = ray_origin + ray_direction * (step * step_size)
                        density = sample_density(grid, sample_pos)
                        # Accumulate extinction
                        transmissivity *= exp(-density * step_size)
                    
                    # Add light contribution attenuated by transmissivity
                    light_contribution = light.intensity * transmissivity
                    total_radiance += light_contribution
                
                baking_texture.set(x, y, z, total_radiance)
    
    return baking_texture

def render_with_baked_lighting(camera_ray, grid, baking_texture, resolution):
    """
    Renders volume using precomputed lighting.
    """
    color = vec3(0.0)
    transmittance = 1.0
    ray_pos = camera_ray.origin
    ray_dir = camera_ray.direction
    
    while transmittance > 0.001 and ray_pos inside grid:
        # Sample density for raymarching
        density = sample_density(grid, ray_pos)
        
        if density > 0.0:
            # Retrieve precomputed incident radiance
            baked_li = baking_texture.sample_trilinear(ray_pos, resolution)
            
            # Update color with in-scattering from baked light
            # Assuming single scattering approximation for simplicity
            scattering_coeff = density * scattering_ratio # scattering_ratio is sigma_s / sigma_t
            color += baked_li * scattering_coeff * transmittance * step_size
            
            # Update transmittance
            transmittance *= exp(-density * step_size)
        
        ray_pos += ray_dir * step_size
        
    return color
```

## Subtopics

Decouples classification and shading, using precomputed reflectance maps for speed while maintaining visual fidelity through postclassification.

### [Pre-computation](2330-pre-computation.md)
Calculates intermediate radiance variables and density derivatives to avoid redundant calculations during ray marching, including first-order inscattering approximations.

### [Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT)](9429-precomputed-radiance-transfer-prt.md)
Models light interaction using transfer functions for arbitrary lighting setups at runtime, offering flexibility at the cost of higher runtime complexity compared to full baking.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(V \cdot L)$ for baking, where $V$ is the number of voxels and $L$ is the number of light sources. $O(1)$ per sample during rendering (texture lookup) [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$ to store the baked lighting texture [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces per-frame rendering cost by eliminating light visibility calculations during raymarching [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Fast runtime rendering; view-independent precomputation allows efficient caching [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot handle dynamic lights or dynamic geometry/medium changes without expensive re-baking [S1, unofficial]. Not suitable for real-time dynamic scenes with changing illumination [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Static scenes with static lighting; offline or pre-rendered content; scenarios where lighting changes are infrequent [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Dynamic lighting, real-time day/night cycles, or interactive scenes with moving light sources [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Precomputed Lighting (Baking) is a historical technique in volume rendering, often cited for reference rather than current practical utility in dynamic real-time applications [S1, unofficial]. It draws inspiration from traditional shadow mapping and deep shadow maps, which store density or depth information to accelerate visibility queries [S1, unofficial]. The method was popularized in early GPU-based volume rendering implementations to overcome the high cost of evaluating light transport integrals per sample [S2, unofficial]. It is closely related to the concept of "Precomputed Light Transmissivity" used in scientific visualization [S2, unofficial].
