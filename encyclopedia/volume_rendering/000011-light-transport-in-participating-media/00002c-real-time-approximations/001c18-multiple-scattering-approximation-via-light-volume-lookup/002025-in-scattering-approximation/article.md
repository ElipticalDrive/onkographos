# In-Scattering Approximation

Also called: Local Approximation of In-scattering

## Overview
In-Scattering Approximation is a real-time technique for estimating the contribution of multiple scattering in participating media, specifically treating the scattered portion of direct global light as an emittance source to capture short-range scattering effects [S1]. It solves the computational cost of integrating the full radiative transfer equation by approximating the in-scattering integral using either a discretized directional sum or a cheap analytical decay function [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Beam Spreading Width Model](1193-beam-spreading-width-model.md) which focuses on geometric spread, or [Precomputed Multiple Scattering Table](3324-precomputed-multiple-scattering-table.md) which relies on heavy precomputation, this method provides an immediate, low-cost approximation that brightens the inner parts of volumes (like clouds) while maintaining performance suitable for interactive frame rates [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates by approximating the in-scattering term $I_s$ in the rendering equation. Two primary implementation strategies exist in the literature: a discretized directional summation approach [S1] and an analytical exponential decay approximation [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Accumulated density ($\rho_{accum}$ or $\tau_{acc}$) along the view ray [S2, unofficial].
*   Sun direction dot product ($\mu_{sun}$ or $\cos\theta$) [S2, unofficial].
*   Base dimensional profile ($P$) or cloud thickness metric [S2, unofficial].
*   Direct light intensity ($I_d$) at sample points [S1].
*   Opacity ($\alpha$) at sample points [S1].
*   Number of scattering directions ($J$) [S1].
*   Scattering sphere radius ($R_\Omega$) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   In-scattering intensity contribution ($I_s$ or $I_e$) [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Stored Intensities ($I_{stored}$):** A buffer or texture holding the accumulated in-scattering result from previous incremental updates [S1].
*   **Ray Directions:** A set of $J$ unit vectors discretizing the scattering sphere $\Omega$. These are typically generated by subdividing a tetrahedron, icosahedron, or octahedron [S1].

### Steps

#### Strategy 1: Discretized Directional Summation [S1]
This approach approximates the integral in Eq. 9 by summing contributions from $J$ discrete directions.

1.  **Initialize:** Set $I_{stored} = 0$. Define $J$ ray directions covering the scattering sphere $\Omega$ with radius $R_\Omega$.
2.  **Iterate Directions:** For each direction $j$ from $0$ to $J-1$:
    a.  **March Ray:** For each sampling step $m$ from $0$ to $M-1$ along direction $j$:
        i.  Sample direct light $I_d(s_m)$ at position $s_m$.
        ii. Sample opacity $\alpha(s_i)$ for all previous steps $i < m$.
    b.  **Compute Directional Contribution:** Calculate the in-scattering contribution for direction $j$ using Eq. 11:
        $$ I_{final} = \sum_{m=0}^M I_d(s_m) \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} (1 - \alpha(s_i)) $$
        *Note: An isotropic phase function is used, weighting all directions equally [S1].*
    c.  **Incremental Blend:** Update the stored intensity using Eq. 13:
        $$ I_{blended} = \frac{1}{j+1} I_{final} + \left(1 - \frac{1}{j+1}\right) I_{stored} $$
        (Note: The source uses $j$ in the denominator, implying 1-based indexing for the blend factor or $j$ represents the count of processed directions. Implementation should use the count of processed directions $k=j+1$).
    d.  **Store:** $I_{stored} = I_{blended}$.
3.  **Finalize:** The final in-scattering intensity is $I_{stored}$.

#### Strategy 2: Analytical Exponential Decay [S2, unofficial]
This approach avoids ray marching for in-scattering by using a cheap function that accounts for phase function and depth.

1.  **March Towards Sun:** Perform a direct light march towards the sun to accumulate density, resulting in `inSunLightDensity` ($\tau_{acc}$) [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Calculate Approximation:** Compute the in-scattering factor $S$ using the function defined in Eq. 2 and the code snippet:
    $$ S = \exp(-\tau_{acc} \cdot R(\cos\theta, P)) $$
    Where $R$ is a remapping function:
    $$ R(\mu_{sun}, P) = \text{Remap}(\mu_{sun}, 0.0, 0.9, 0.25, \text{Remap}(P, 1.0, 0.0, 0.05, 0.25)) $$
3.  **Combine with Phase:** Multiply the result by the phase function (e.g., Henyey-Greenstein) and sun light intensity to get the final sample contribution [S2, unofficial].

### Formulas
*   **In-Scattering Integral (Continuous):**
    $$ I_e(s_0) = \int_{s_0}^{R_\Omega} q(s) e^{-\int_{s_0}^s \tau(t) dt} d\tau \quad (9) $$
    Where $q(s)$ is the direct global light distribution and $\tau(t)$ is optical depth [S1].

*   **Discretized In-Scattering Sum:**
    $$ I_s = \sum_{j=0}^J \Phi_j \sum_{m=0}^M I_d(s_m) \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} (1 - \alpha(s_i)) \quad (11) $$
    Where $\Phi_j$ is the normalized light contribution from direction $j$ (summing to 1), $I_d(s_m)$ is direct light, and $\alpha(s_i)$ is opacity [S1].

*   **Incremental Blend:**
    $$ I_{blended} = \frac{1}{j} I_{final} + \left(1 - \frac{1}{j}\right) I_{stored} \quad (13) $$
    Where $j$ is the current ray index (1-based count) [S1].

*   **Analytical Approximation:**
    $$ I = \exp(-\rho_{accum} \cdot \text{Remap}(\mu_{sun}, 0.0, 0.9, 0.25, \text{Remap}(P, 1.0, 0.0, 0.05, 0.25))) \quad (2) $$
    Where $\rho_{accum}$ is accumulated density, $\mu_{sun}$ is sun dot product, and $P$ is base dimensional profile [S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Scattering Sphere Radius ($R_\Omega$):** Radius of the sphere $\Omega$ over which in-scattering is integrated. Short-range effects are dominant [S1].
*   **Phase Function ($\phi$):** Weights incoming light. An isotropic phase function is used in the primary source, weighting light equally [S1]. Other implementations use Henyey-Greenstein [S2, unofficial].
*   **Ray Directions ($J$):** Number of directions discretizing the sphere. Found by subdividing a tetrahedron, icosahedron, or octahedron [S1].
*   **Artist-Tunable Parameters:** In some variants (like [S3, unofficial]), scaling factors for scattering, attenuation, and phase coefficients are used, though [S3, unofficial] notes this specific N-term sum cannot produce Dark Edge effects without additional Beer's-Powder attenuation [S3, unofficial].

```python
def remap(value, in_min, in_max, out_min, out_max):
    """Linear remapping of value from input range to output range."""
    if in_max == in_min:
        return out_min
    t = (value - in_min) / (in_max - in_min)
    return out_min + t * (out_max - out_min)

def in_scattering_approx_analytical(accumulated_density, sun_dot, base_profile):
    """
    Strategy 2: Analytical Exponential Decay [S2, unofficial].
    Returns the in-scattering factor.
    """
    # Remap base profile from [1.0, 0.0] to [0.05, 0.25]
    remapped_profile = remap(base_profile, 1.0, 0.0, 0.05, 0.25)
    # Remap sun dot from [0.0, 0.9] to [0.25, remapped_profile]
    remapped_sun = remap(sun_dot, 0.0, 0.9, 0.25, remapped_profile)
    
    # Calculate exponential decay
    return exp(-accumulated_density * remapped_sun)

def in_scattering_approx_discrete(direct_light_samples, opacity_samples, num_directions, num_steps):
    """
    Strategy 1: Discretized Directional Summation [S1].
    Returns the final blended in-scattering intensity.
    """
    stored_intensity = 0.0
    
    # Generate J directions (e.g., via icosahedron subdivision)
    directions = generate_sphere_directions(num_directions)
    
    for j_idx, direction in enumerate(directions):
        # 1-based count for blending formula
        j_count = j_idx + 1
        
        directional_sum = 0.0
        
        # March along the direction
        for m in range(num_steps):
            # Sample direct light at step m
            id_sm = direct_light_samples[direction][m]
            
            # Calculate transmissivity from previous steps
            transmissivity = 1.0
            for i in range(m):
                alpha_si = opacity_samples[direction][i]
                transmissivity *= (1.0 - alpha_si)
            
            directional_sum += id_sm * transmissivity
            
        # Blend with stored intensity
        # Eq. 13: I_blended = (1/j) * I_final + (1 - 1/j) * I_stored
        blended = (1.0 / j_count) * directional_sum + (1.0 - 1.0 / j_count) * stored_intensity
        stored_intensity = blended
        
    return stored_intensity
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Dark Edge Effects:** Standard multiple scattering approximations (like the N-term sum in [S3, unofficial]) cannot produce "Dark Edge" effects. To achieve this, Beer's-Powder attenuation functions must be combined with the approximation [S3, unofficial].
*   **Accuracy vs. Cost:** The approximation is not perfectly accurate but is effective because direct lighting is significantly stronger than multiple scattering [S2, unofficial].
*   **Color Bias:** If environment light multiple scattering is ignored, volumes may appear too blue (biased towards sky color) [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Depth-Aware Scatter Estimator](2585-depth-aware-scatter-estimator.md)
A neural network module that estimates primary transmission and depth-dependent scatter contributions. It computes Beer-Lambert transmission, accumulates a scatter source, and mixes it with an ASPP-conditioned gate.

### [Volumetric Scattering Pass](11254-volumetric-scattering-pass.md)
An additional rendering pass that incorporates indirect illumination and multiple scattering by exploiting the local nature of scattering influence. It models color bleeding from neighboring voxels using a discrete neighborhood sum and supports interactive light changes via Spherical Harmonics.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Strategy 1 is $O(J \cdot M)$ per voxel/pixel, where $J$ is the number of directions and $M$ is the number of steps. Strategy 2 is $O(1)$ per sample after the initial sun march.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Strategy 1 requires storing intermediate intensities for incremental refinement or $J$ ray buffers. Strategy 2 requires minimal extra memory.
*   **Performance:** Strategy 2 is described as "very cheap" and effective for real-time cloud rendering [S2, unofficial]. Strategy 1 allows for incremental refinement during interaction, speeding up updates [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Captures short-range scattering effects which are dominant [S1]. Brightens inner parts of clouds [S2, unofficial]. Interactive refinement possible [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot produce Dark Edge effects without additional techniques [S3, unofficial]. Approximation error exists compared to full integration [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from work on interactive global light propagation in direct volume rendering. The discretized approach is detailed in "Interactive Global Light Propagation in Direct Volume Rendering using Local Piecewise Integration" [S1]. The analytical approximation for clouds is popularized in real-time graphics tutorials and implementations, such as those by Jack Tollenaar [S2, unofficial] and various game engine implementations (UE4, Unity HDRP) discussed in industry articles [S3, unofficial].
