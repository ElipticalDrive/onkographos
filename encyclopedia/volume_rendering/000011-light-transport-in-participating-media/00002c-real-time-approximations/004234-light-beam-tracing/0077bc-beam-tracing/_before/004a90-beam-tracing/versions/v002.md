# Beam Tracing
## Overview
Beam tracing is an acceleration technique for ray tracing that represents a "thick" ray as a pyramid to shoot several rays at once, creating new beams at polygon surfaces [S1, unofficial]. It is a strategy where rays are considered only in a final sampling step, rather than being represented explicitly during traversal [S2]. It is competitive with frustum-based tracers for eye and shadow rays [S2].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
* **Input:** A scene defined by polygonal surfaces and a set of primary rays (e.g., eye rays or shadow rays) [S1, unofficial].
* **Output:** A set of sampled rays or final pixel values derived from beam intersections [S2].

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on a spatial acceleration structure, specifically a kd-tree, to manage beam traversal [S2]. No grid-based implementations are known [S2].

**Steps:**
1. **Beam Representation:** Represent a group of rays as a "thick" ray, specifically a pyramid [S1, unofficial]. Alternatively, a cone may be used to approximate the thick ray [S1, unofficial].
2. **Traversal:** Traverse the acceleration structure (kd-tree) using the beam's bounding volume. If the beam misses a bounding volume, it misses the enclosed objects [S1, unofficial].
3. **Intersection:** Compute the intersection of the beam (pyramid/cone) with polygon surfaces [S1, unofficial].
4. **Beam Creation:** At the surfaces of polygons, create new beams for subsequent tracing steps [S1, unofficial].
5. **Splitting:** If necessary, split the beam into smaller cones to maintain approximation quality [S1, unofficial].
6. **Sampling:** Perform the final sampling step to determine ray contributions, as rays are not represented explicitly until this stage [S2].

**Formulas and Parameters:**
Not given in the sources. Grid traversal constants ($T_{setup}$, $T_{step}$) are noted for grid-based methods but do not apply to beam tracing's kd-tree implementations [S2].

**Pitfalls:**
* **Complexity:** Computing beam-polygon intersections and determining good approximations is expensive [S1, unofficial].
* **Historical Viability:** At the time of the source documentation, the method was considered "too expensive" for practical use [S1, unofficial].
* **Implementation Gap:** There are no known implementations of grid-based beam tracers; existing work focuses exclusively on kd-tree based data structures [S2].

## Operating characteristics
* **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
* **Performance:** Beam tracing is competitive with frustum-based tracers for eye and shadow rays [S2]. However, it was historically considered too expensive due to the cost of beam-polygon intersection and approximation [S1, unofficial].
* **Strengths:** Exploits coherence by processing groups of rays; competitive with frustum-based methods for specific ray types [S2].
* **Limitations:** High computational cost for intersection and approximation; lack of grid-based implementations; considered too expensive in early assessments [S1, unofficial][S2].
* **When to use:** When using kd-tree structures for eye or shadow rays and seeking coherence exploitation [S2].
* **When not to use:** When grid-based acceleration is required, as no grid-based implementations exist [S2], or when computational resources are limited due to intersection costs [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Beam tracing was introduced as an acceleration technique in computer graphics to handle ray tracing efficiency [S1, unofficial]. It is discussed in the context of bounding volumes and acceleration data structures [S1, unofficial]. Overbeck et al. demonstrated its competitiveness with frustum-based tracers [S2]. It builds on [Light Beam Tracing](10717-light-beam-tracing.md).
