# Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT)
Also called: Directional surface prelighting
## Overview
Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT) models light interaction with surfaces using transfer functions to map incoming light to a radiance distribution description, avoiding direct operation on actual radiance values [S1, unofficial]. Unlike full baking, which is static and inflexible, PRT enables arbitrary lighting setups at runtime by leveraging the linearity of light transport, though it incurs higher runtime costs [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Precomputed Lighting (Baking)](2169-precomputed-lighting-baking.md).
## Shared principles
PRT relies on the linearity of light transport, allowing the contribution of each light to be computed independently and summed for the final lighting value [S1, unofficial]. This process defines a vector space where precomputed unit light contributions serve as basis vectors [S1, unofficial]. The final radiance $L(p)$ at point $p$ is calculated as:

$$L(p) = \sum_i L_i(p) W_i$$

where $L(p)$ is the final radiance for point $p$, $L_i(p)$ is the precomputed unit contribution of light $i$, and $W_i$ is the current brightness of light $i$ [S1, unofficial]. The basis function used for transfer is independent of the representation utilized to store the final lighting [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Sloan et al. introduced precomputed radiance transfer to computer graphics [S1, unofficial]. Although originally formulated using spherical harmonics, the method does not strictly require them [S1, unofficial]. Sloan et al. stored the scene's response to light using a distribution defined by spherical harmonic basis functions, modeling responses across multiple bands to support rendering under any arbitrary lighting setup [S1, unofficial].
