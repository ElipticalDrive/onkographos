# Deferred Voxel Shading

Also called: Deferred Voxel Cone Tracing, Villegas Voxel Cone Tracing, Villegas Voxel Shading

## Overview
Deferred Voxel Shading is a real-time global illumination technique that merges voxel cone tracing with deferred shading to approximate indirect lighting effects such as diffuse/specular reflection, color blending, emissive materials, and ambient occlusion [S2, unofficial]. Unlike forward rendering approaches that recompute lighting for overlapping geometry, this method delays lighting calculations until the fragment stage, allowing efficient computation of complex lighting interactions across the voxelized scene [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard Voxel Cone Tracing by integrating direct illumination and emission data directly into the voxelization pipeline and utilizing anisotropic directional volumes for more precise cone tracing results [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of four primary stages: Voxelization, Voxel Illumination, Anisotropic Voxel Generation, and Voxel Cone Tracing [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Scene geometry (triangles).
*   Material properties: Albedo ($\rho$), Surface Normal ($N$), Emission ($E$), Specular exponent (for BRDF matching).
*   Light sources: Point, Spot, Directional (with intensity $L_i$ and direction $\Psi$).
*   Camera parameters: Position, View Direction.

**Outputs:**
*   Final fragment color with indirect illumination, shadows, and ambient occlusion.

### Data Structures
The technique relies on several 3D textures bound as `uimage3D` (unsigned integer) or floating-point textures, depending on the implementation details of the radiance volume [S2, unofficial].

1.  **Voxelization Textures (Static/Dynamic):**
    *   `voxelAlbedo`: `r32ui` (or equivalent). Stores averaged albedo per voxel.
    *   `voxelNormal`: `r32ui` (or equivalent). Stores averaged surface normal per voxel.
    *   `voxelEmission`: `r32ui` (or equivalent). Stores emission per voxel.
    *   `flagVolume`: Single-value 3D texture. Identifies voxels as static (1) or dynamic (0). Used to prevent overwriting static geometry during dynamic updates [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Radiance Volume:**
    *   3D texture storing the maximum level of detail (LOD) for the voxelized scene. Contains the result of direct lighting computations per voxel [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Directional Volumes (Anisotropic Voxels):**
    *   Six 3D textures at half resolution of the Radiance Volume.
    *   One texture per axis direction (positive/negative X, Y, Z).
    *   Mipmap levels store directional values for anisotropic sampling [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Flag Volume:**
    *   Single-value 3D texture used to distinguish static vs. dynamic voxels for update management [S2, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

#### Step 1: Voxelization
The scene geometry is voxelized using conservative rasterization. Atomic operations are used to average values per voxel to handle overlapping fragments [S2, unofficial].

1.  For each fragment generated by the geometry:
    *   Determine voxel position $P$.
    *   Read `flagVolume` at $P$. If static, discard writing [S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute average albedo, normal, and emission for the voxel using atomic averages:
        ```python
        imageAtomicRGBA8Avg(voxelAlbedo, P, albedo)
        imageAtomicRGBA8Avg(voxelNormal, P, normal)
        imageAtomicRGBA8Avg(voxelEmission, P, emission)
        ```
    *   If the voxel is newly created or updated, write to `flagVolume` at $P$ indicating it is static (if geometry is static) or dynamic [S2, unofficial].

#### Step 2: Voxel Illumination
Direct lighting is computed per voxel in a compute shader using the stored normal, position, and albedo. This step handles point, spot, and directional lights without shadow maps [S2, unofficial].

1.  For each voxel $V$ at position $P$:
    *   Retrieve $N$ (normal), $\rho$ (albedo), $E$ (emission) from voxelization textures.
    *   Initialize outgoing radiance $V_r = 0$.
    *   For each light source $L$ with intensity $L_i$ and direction $\Psi$:
        *   **Voxel Occlusion Check:** Trace a ray from $P$ in direction $\Psi$. Sample the voxelized volume. If a voxel exists at the ray position, the voxel is occluded [S2, unofficial].
        *   **Soft Shadow Accumulation:** If occlusion is approximated via ray marching:
            *   Initialize $\nu = 0$.
            *   For each collision $\kappa$ along the traced distance $t$:
                $$ \nu = \nu + (1 - \nu) \frac{\kappa}{t} $$
            *   Final occlusion factor is $1 - \nu$ [S2, unofficial].
        *   **Normal-Weighted Attenuation:** To handle uneven normals within a voxel, calculate attenuation per face:
            $$ D_{x,y,z} = (\hat{i}\cdot\Psi, \hat{j}\cdot\Psi, \hat{k}\cdot\Psi) $$
            Select dominant faces based on the sign of the voxel averaged normal vector components $N_\omega$:
            $$ D_{\omega} = \begin{cases} \max\{D_\omega, 0\}, & N_\omega > 0 \\ \max\{-D_\omega, 0\}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        *   **Reflectance Model:** Compute the contribution:
            $$ W = N^2 $$
            $$ V_r = L_i \frac{\rho}{\pi} (W_x D_x + W_y D_y + W_z D_z) $$
            *Note: $N$ is the voxel normal vector, $\Psi$ is the light direction [S2, unofficial].*
        *   Add $V_r$ to the voxel's radiance.
    *   **Emission Handling:** Add the emission term $E$ to the direct illumination value:
        $$ V_r = V_r + E $$
        This ensures emissive voxels contribute indirect light even in occluded areas [S2, unofficial].
    *   Store $V_r$ in the Radiance Volume [S2, unofficial].

#### Step 3: Generate Anisotropic Voxels
To improve cone tracing precision, directional volumes are generated using volumetric integration in depth [S2, unofficial].

1.  For each mipmap level $m$ (from base level upwards):
    *   For each voxel $V$ at level $m$:
        *   Execute a compute shader thread.
        *   Perform volumetric integration in depth using values from level $m-1$.
        *   Average directional values to obtain six directional components (one per axis direction) [S2, unofficial].
        *   Store these values in the corresponding Directional Volumes [S2, unofficial].

#### Step 4: Voxel Cone Tracing
Performed in the fragment shader as a screen-space effect. Cones advance, sampling volume increases via mipmap levels [S2, unofficial].

1.  For each fragment:
    *   Determine cone origin $C_o$ (fragment position) and direction $C_d$ (view direction or BRDF lobe direction).
    *   **BRDF Matching:**
        *   **Diffuse:** Distribute a few large cones over the normal-oriented hemisphere [S2, unofficial].
        *   **Specular:** Use a single cone in the reflected direction. Aperture $\theta$ depends on the specular exponent [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Cone Tracing Loop:**
        *   Initialize accumulated color $c = 0$ and occlusion $\alpha = 0$.
        *   Current distance $t = 0$.
        *   While not fully occluded and within bounds:
            *   Calculate cone diameter:
                $$ d = 2t \times \tan(\theta / 2) $$
            *   Determine mipmap level:
                $$ V_{level} = \log_2(d / V_{size}) $$
                where $V_{size}$ is the size of a voxel at the maximum level of detail [S2, unofficial].
            *   **Step Distance:** Modify step distance $d'$ by factor $\beta$ ($\beta=1$ means $d'=d$). Lower $\beta$ increases quality but requires more samples [S2, unofficial].
            *   Advance $t$ by $d'$.
            *   **Sampling:**
                *   Determine which three Directional Volumes to sample based on cone direction.
                *   Retrieve outgoing radiance $c_2$ and occlusion $\alpha_2$ from the Radiance Volume and Directional Volumes using quadrilinear interpolation [S2, unofficial].
                *   **Volumetric Front-to-Back Accumulation:**
                    $$ c = \alpha c + (1 - \alpha) \alpha_2 c_2 $$
                    $$ \alpha = \alpha + (1 - \alpha) \alpha_2 $$
            *   If $\alpha \ge 1$, break [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Ambient Occlusion (Optional):**
        *   Using diffuse cones, accumulate occlusion $\alpha_2$.
        *   Weight by $f(r) = \frac{1}{1 + \lambda r}$, where $r$ is the current radius and $\lambda$ is a constant [S2, unofficial].
    *   Output final color $c$ [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
# Step 1: Voxelization (Compute Shader)
def voxelize_geometry(geometry, flag_volume, voxel_albedo, voxel_normal, voxel_emission):
    for fragment in geometry.rasterize():
        pos = fragment.voxel_position
        if flag_volume[pos] == STATIC:
            continue
        
        # Atomic averages
        voxel_albedo.atomic_avg(pos, fragment.albedo)
        voxel_normal.atomic_avg(pos, fragment.normal)
        voxel_emission.atomic_avg(pos, fragment.emission)
        
        if flag_volume[pos] == DYNAMIC:
            flag_volume[pos] = STATIC # Mark as static if geometry is static

# Step 2: Voxel Illumination (Compute Shader)
def compute_voxel_radiance(voxel_albedo, voxel_normal, voxel_emission, flag_volume, radiance_volume, lights):
    for voxel_pos in all_voxels:
        if flag_volume[voxel_pos] == DYNAMIC:
            continue # Skip dynamic for direct lighting if not updated, or handle separately
            
        N = voxel_normal[voxel_pos]
        rho = voxel_albedo[voxel_pos]
        E = voxel_emission[voxel_pos]
        
        V_r = 0.0
        
        for light in lights:
            # Occlusion check
            occluded = trace_ray(voxel_pos, light.direction, voxel_albedo)
            
            # Soft shadow approximation
            nu = 0.0
            if occluded:
                # Simplified soft shadow accumulation
                # nu = nu + (1 - nu) * kappa / t
                pass 
            
            # Normal-weighted attenuation
            Psi = light.direction
            D_x = dot(vec3(1,0,0), Psi)
            D_y = dot(vec3(0,1,0), Psi)
            D_z = dot(vec3(0,0,1), Psi)
            
            N_x, N_y, N_z = N
            
            D_x_dom = max(D_x, 0) if N_x > 0 else max(-D_x, 0)
            D_y_dom = max(D_y, 0) if N_y > 0 else max(-D_y, 0)
            D_z_dom = max(D_z, 0) if N_z > 0 else max(-D_z, 0)
            
            W = N * N
            contribution = light.intensity * (rho / PI) * (W.x * D_x_dom + W.y * D_y_dom + W.z * D_z_dom)
            
            V_r += contribution
            
        V_r += E
        radiance_volume[voxel_pos] = V_r

# Step 3: Anisotropic Voxels (Compute Shader)
def generate_anisotropic_voxels(radiance_volume, directional_volumes):
    for level in mipmap_levels:
        for voxel_pos in voxels_at_level:
            # Volumetric integration in depth from previous level
            # Average directional values
            dir_values = integrate_depth(radiance_volume, directional_volumes, voxel_pos, level)
            directional_volumes[level].write(voxel_pos, dir_values)

# Step 4: Voxel Cone Tracing (Fragment Shader)
def voxel_cone_tracing(fragment_pos, fragment_normal, radiance_volume, directional_volumes, camera):
    # Determine cones based on BRDF
    cones = get_cones_for_brdf(fragment_normal, camera.view_direction)
    
    final_color = vec3(0)
    
    for cone in cones:
        c = vec3(0)
        alpha = 0.0
        t = 0.0
        theta = cone.aperture
        
        while alpha < 1.0:
            d = 2 * t * tan(theta / 2)
            V_level = log2(d / V_size)
            
            # Step distance
            d_prime = d * beta # beta < 1 for higher quality
            
            t += d_prime
            
            # Determine directional volumes to sample
            dirs = get_directions_for_cone(cone.direction)
            
            # Sample radiance and occlusion
            c2, alpha2 = sample_voxel_structure(radiance_volume, directional_volumes, dirs, t, V_level)
            
            # Front-to-back accumulation
            c = alpha * c + (1 - alpha) * alpha2 * c2
            alpha = alpha + (1 - alpha) * alpha2
            
        final_color += weight_cone(cone) * c
        
    return final_color
```

### Parameters
*   **Beta ($\beta$):** Factor modifying the distance $d'$ between cone steps. $\beta = 1$ makes $d'$ equivalent to current diameter $d$. Values $< 1$ produce higher quality but require more samples [S2, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Size ($V_{size}$):** Size of a voxel at the maximum level of detail [S2, unofficial].
*   **Aperture Angle ($\theta$):** Depends on the specular exponent for specular cones [S2, unofficial].
*   **Ambient Occlusion Constant ($\lambda$):** Used in the weighting function $f(r) = \frac{1}{1+\lambda r}$ [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Dynamic Voxelization:** Requires careful management of the `flagVolume` to avoid overwriting static geometry. Dynamic voxels must be cleared before re-voxelization [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Averaging:** Uneven normals within a voxel can lead to inaccurate lighting. The normal-weighted attenuation method mitigates this but adds complexity [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Conservative voxelization is required to ensure geometry is fully captured. Atomic operations must be coherent [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Cone tracing is expensive. The number of cones and steps must be balanced. Anisotropic volumes increase memory usage [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Voxelization is $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of fragments. Voxel Illumination is $O(V \cdot L)$ where $V$ is the number of voxels and $L$ is the number of lights. Cone Tracing is $O(F \cdot C \cdot S)$ where $F$ is fragments, $C$ is cones per fragment, and $S$ is steps per cone.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires multiple 3D textures: 3 for voxelization, 1 for radiance, 6 for directional volumes, and 1 for flags. Resolution scales with scene size.
*   **Strengths:** Handles complex lighting effects (emissive, indirect shadows, multiple bounces) in real-time. Deferred shading avoids redundant lighting calculations.
*   **Limitations:** High memory usage. Quality depends on voxel resolution and cone tracing parameters. Dynamic updates can be costly.
*   **When to Use:** Scenes with many light sources, emissive materials, and complex indirect lighting requirements where real-time performance is critical.
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with very high dynamic geometry changes where voxelization overhead is prohibitive, or memory-constrained environments.

## Background
Deferred Voxel Shading was presented by Jose Villegas as an expansion upon rudimentary voxel cone tracing, merging it with deferred shading [S1, unofficial]. It aims to provide a comprehensive real-time global illumination solution by combining the spatial efficiency of voxelization with the flexibility of deferred rendering [S2, unofficial]. The technique was developed to address the limitations of forward rendering approaches in handling complex lighting interactions efficiently [S1, unofficial].
