# Deferred Voxel Shading

Also called: Deferred Voxel Cone Tracing, Villegas Voxel Cone Tracing, Villegas Voxel Shading

## Overview
Deferred Voxel Shading is a four-step real-time global illumination technique that merges voxel cone tracing with deferred shading to approximate indirect diffuse, specular, color-bleeding, emissive, and ambient occlusion effects [S2, unofficial]. Unlike forward rendering approaches that compute lighting per fragment during geometry passes, this method delays lighting calculations until a post-processing stage, avoiding redundant computations for overlapping polygons and improving performance in scenes with many light sources [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Real-time approximations](36-real-time-approximations.md) by discretizing the scene into voxels and precomputing lighting data to evaluate the radiative transfer equation efficiently [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of four primary stages: Voxelization, Voxel Illumination, Anisotropic Voxel Generation, and Voxel Cone Tracing [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:** Scene geometry (triangles), material properties (albedo $\rho$, surface normal $N$, emission $E$, specular exponent $n$), light sources (point, spot, directional with intensity $L_i$ and direction $\Psi$), and camera parameters (position, view matrix, projection matrix) [S2, unofficial].
**Outputs:** Screen-space indirect illumination buffer integrated into the deferred shading pipeline [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The technique relies on several 3D textures bound as `uimage3D` or standard 3D textures [S2, unofficial].
1. **Voxelization Storage:** Three 3D textures storing averaged scene data: `voxelAlbedo` (albedo), `voxelNormal` (normals), and `voxelEmission` (emission) [S2, unofficial]. Format is `r32ui` to support atomic averaging operations like `imageAtomicRGBA8Avg` [S2, unofficial].
2. **Flag Volume:** A single-value 3D texture that identifies voxels as static to prevent overwriting during dynamic updates [S2, unofficial].
3. **Radiance Volume:** A 3D texture storing the maximum level of detail for the voxelized scene, containing direct lighting computations per voxel (outgoing radiance) [S2, unofficial].
4. **Directional Volumes:** Six 3D textures at half the resolution of the Radiance Volume, one per axis direction (positive/negative X, Y, Z) [S2, unofficial]. Their mipmap levels store directional coherence for anisotropic sampling [S2, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps
1. **Voxelization:** The scene is voxelized using conservative rasterization to ensure complete coverage [S2, unofficial]. For each triangle, compute its bounding box in voxel space and iterate through all voxels within it [S2, unofficial]. For each occupied voxel, average the fragment's albedo, normal, and emission with stored values using atomic operations [S2, unofficial]. If the geometry is static, write `1` to the Flag Volume at that position [S2, unofficial]. For dynamic updates, clear previous dynamic voxels by reading the Flag Volume and discarding writes to static positions [S2, unofficial].
2. **Voxel Illumination:** Direct lighting is computed per voxel in a compute shader using stored normals, positions, and albedo [S2, unofficial]. For each light, calculate normal-weighted attenuation to handle uneven normals within a voxel: $D_{x,y,z} = (\hat{i}\cdot\Psi, \hat{j}\cdot\Psi, \hat{k}\cdot\Psi)$ [S2, unofficial]. Select dominant faces based on the sign of the averaged normal components: $D_{\omega} = \max\{D_\omega, 0\}$ if $N_\omega>0$ else $\max\{-D_\omega, 0\}$ [S2, unofficial]. Compute weights $W = N^2$ and reflected radiance $V_r = L_i \frac{\rho}{\pi}(W_x D_x + W_y D_y + W_z D_z)$ [S2, unofficial]. Determine occlusion by tracing a ray from the voxel in direction $\Psi$ and sampling the volume; if a voxel exists, it is occluded [S2, unofficial]. Approximate soft shadows by accumulating $\nu = \nu + (1 - \nu)\kappa \div t$ per collision, where $1-\nu$ is the final occlusion [S2, unofficial]. Add the voxel's emission term $E$ to the direct illumination and store the result in the Radiance Volume [S2, unofficial].
3. **Generate Anisotropic Voxels:** To support precise cone tracing, directional information is stored in mipmaps of the Directional Volumes [S2, unofficial]. For each mipmap level, execute a compute shader thread per voxel per directional volume [S2, unofficial]. Perform volumetric integration in depth using values from the previous level (or Radiance Volume for the base level) and average the directional values [S2, unofficial]. Store the result in the corresponding Directional Volume at the current mipmap level [S2, unofficial].
4. **Voxel Cone Tracing:** Performed in the fragment shader as a screen-space effect using a crude Monte Carlo approximation that partitions the hemisphere integral into cones [S2, unofficial]. Define a cone with origin $C_o$, direction $C_d$, and aperture $\theta$ [S2, unofficial]. For diffuse reflection, distribute several large cones over the normal-oriented hemisphere; for specular reflection, use a single cone in the reflected direction with aperture dependent on the specular exponent [S2, unofficial]. In each step, calculate cone diameter $d = 2t \times \tan(\theta \div 2)$ and determine the mipmap level $V_{level} = \log_2(d \div V_{size})$ [S2, unofficial]. Sample the three directional volumes corresponding to the cone's dominant axes using quadrilinear interpolation to retrieve outgoing radiance $c_2$ and occlusion $\alpha_2$ [S2, unofficial]. Update accumulated color and occlusion via front-to-back accumulation: $c = \alpha c + (1-\alpha)\alpha_2 c_2$ and $\alpha = \alpha + (1-\alpha)\alpha_2$ [S2, unofficial]. Advance the traced distance $t$ by $d' = \beta \times d$, where $\beta=1$ matches the step size to the current diameter and $\beta<1$ increases quality at the cost of more samples [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
import math
from typing import List, Tuple, Any

# Standard math/vector operations
def normalize(v: Tuple[float, float, float]) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
    mag = math.sqrt(v[0]**2 + v[1]**2 + v[2]**2)
    return (v[0]/mag, v[1]/mag, v[2]/mag) if mag > 0 else (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)

def dot(a: Tuple[float, float, float], b: Tuple[float, float, float]) -> float:
    return a[0]*b[0] + a[1]*b[1] + a[2]*b[2]

def max_val(a: float, b: float) -> float:
    return a if a > b else b

class Texture3D:
    def __init__(self, size: int, format: str):
        self.size = size
        self.format = format
        self.data = {}

    def read(self, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> Any:
        return self.data.get(pos, 0.0)

    def write(self, pos: Tuple[int, int, int], val: Any):
        self.data[pos] = val

    def atomic_avg(self, pos: Tuple[int, int, int], new_val: Any):
        old = self.read(pos)
        self.write(pos, (old + new_val) / 2.0)

def compute_voxel_bounds(triangle: Any) -> List[Tuple[int, int, int]]:
    return [] # Conservative rasterization bounding box

def triangle_intersects_voxel(triangle: Any, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> bool:
    return True # Conservative rasterization intersection check

def get_fragment_albedo(triangle: Any, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> float:
    return 1.0 # Fragment albedo

def get_fragment_normal(triangle: Any, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
    return (0.0, 1.0, 0.0) # Fragment normal

def get_fragment_emission(triangle: Any, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> float:
    return 0.0 # Fragment emission

def read_flag(volume: Texture3D, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> int:
    return volume.read(pos)

def write_flag(volume: Texture3D, pos: Tuple[int, int, int], val: int):
    volume.write(pos, val)

def is_occupied(volume: Texture3D, pos: Tuple[int, int, int]) -> bool:
    return volume.read(pos) > 0.0

def world_to_voxel(world_pos: Tuple[float, float, float], voxel_size: float, origin: Tuple[float, float, float]) -> Tuple[int, int, int]:
    return (int((world_pos[0]-origin[0])/voxel_size),
            int((world_pos[1]-origin[1])/voxel_size),
            int((world_pos[2]-origin[2])/voxel_size))

def read_mipmap(texture: Texture3D, pos: Tuple[int, int, int], level: int) -> Any:
    return texture.read(pos) # Simplified mipmap read

def integrate_depth(prev_val: Any, pos: Tuple[int, int, int], dir_idx: int) -> Any:
    return prev_val # Volumetric integration along depth axis

def read_mipmap_quadrilinear(texture: Texture3D, pos: Tuple[float, float, float], level: int) -> Tuple[float, float, float, float]:
    return (1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0) # Quadrilinear interpolation result

def dominant_axes(direction: Tuple[float, float, float]) -> List[int]:
    return [0, 1, 2] # Indices of three dominant axes based on cone direction

def interpolate_directional_samples(samples: List[Tuple[float, float, float, float]]) -> Tuple[float, float]:
    return samples[0][0], samples[0][1] # Interpolated c2 and alpha2

# Step 1: Voxelization
def voxelize_scene(geometry: List[Any], is_static: bool, voxel_size: float, origin: Tuple[float, float, float]):
    for triangle in geometry:
        bbox = compute_voxel_bounds(triangle)
        for voxel_pos in bbox:
            if not is_static and read_flag(flag_volume, voxel_pos) == 1:
                continue
            if triangle_intersects_voxel(triangle, voxel_pos):
                frag_albedo = get_fragment_albedo(triangle, voxel_pos)
                frag_normal = get_fragment_normal(triangle, voxel_pos)
                frag_emission = get_fragment_emission(triangle, voxel_pos)
                voxel_albedo.atomic_avg(voxel_pos, frag_albedo)
                voxel_normal.atomic_avg(voxel_pos, frag_normal)
                voxel_emission.atomic_avg(voxel_pos, frag_emission)
                if is_static:
                    write_flag(flag_volume, voxel_pos, 1)

# Step 2: Voxel Illumination
def compute_voxel_lighting(lights: List[Any], voxel_size: float, origin: Tuple[float, float, float]):
    for voxel_pos in grid:
        N = normalize(voxel_normal.read(voxel_pos))
        rho = voxel_albedo.read(voxel_pos)
        E = voxel_emission.read(voxel_pos)
        pos_world = (voxel_pos[0]*voxel_size + origin[0],
                     voxel_pos[1]*voxel_size + origin[1],
                     voxel_pos[2]*voxel_size + origin[2])
        radiance = E
        for light in lights:
            Psi = normalize((origin[0]-pos_world[0], origin[1]-pos_world[1], origin[2]-pos_world[2]))
            Li = light.intensity
            D_x = dot((1.0, 0.0, 0.0), Psi)
            D_y = dot((0.0, 1.0, 0.0), Psi)
            D_z = dot((0.0, 0.0, 1.0), Psi)
            D_x_dom = max_val(D_x, 0.0) if N[0] > 0 else max_val(-D_x, 0.0)
            D_y_dom = max_val(D_y, 0.0) if N[1] > 0 else max_val(-D_y, 0.0)
            D_z_dom = max_val(D_z, 0.0) if N[2] > 0 else max_val(-D_z, 0.0)
            W_x = N[0]**2
            W_y = N[1]**2
            W_z = N[2]**2
            Vr = Li * (rho / math.pi) * (W_x * D_x_dom + W_y * D_y_dom + W_z * D_z_dom)
            nu = 0.0
            t = 0.0
            max_dist = 10.0
            step = 0.1
            kappa = 1.0
            while t < max_dist:
                ray_pos = (pos_world[0] + Psi[0]*t, pos_world[1] + Psi[1]*t, pos_world[2] + Psi[2]*t)
                voxel_ray_pos = world_to_voxel(ray_pos, voxel_size, origin)
                if is_occupied(voxel_albedo, voxel_ray_pos):
                    nu = nu + (1.0 - nu) * kappa / t
                t += step
            occlusion = 1.0 - nu
            radiance += Vr * occlusion
        voxel_radiance.write(voxel_pos, radiance)

# Step 3: Anisotropic Voxels
def generate_anisotropic_voxels():
    for level in range(0, 5):
        for dir_idx in range(6):
            for voxel_pos in grid:
                prev_val = read_mipmap(directional_volumes[dir_idx], voxel_pos, level-1) if level > 0 else voxel_radiance.read(voxel_pos)
                dir_val = integrate_depth(prev_val, voxel_pos, dir_idx)
                directional_volumes[dir_idx].write(voxel_pos, dir_val)

# Step 4: Voxel Cone Tracing
def trace_cone(origin: Tuple[float, float, float], direction: Tuple[float, float, float], theta: float, beta: float, max_trace_distance: float, V_size: float):
    c = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    alpha = 0.0
    t = 0.0
    while t < max_trace_distance:
        d = 2 * t * math.tan(theta / 2.0)
        V_level = math.log2(d / V_size)
        samples = []
        for axis in dominant_axes(direction):
            val = read_mipmap_quadrilinear(directional_volumes[axis], origin, V_level)
            samples.append(val)
        c2, alpha2 = interpolate_directional_samples(samples)
        c = (alpha*c[0] + (1-alpha)*alpha2*c2, alpha*c[1] + (1-alpha)*alpha2*c2, alpha*c[2] + (1-alpha)*alpha2*c2)
        alpha = alpha + (1 - alpha) * alpha2
        t += beta * d
    return c
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** Atomic averaging of normals requires careful normalization after accumulation to prevent drift [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The number of cone steps is inversely proportional to $\beta$; values less than $1$ produce higher quality but require more samples, reducing performance [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for three base textures, one flag volume, one radiance volume, and six directional volumes (mipmapped) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles complex lighting effects (emissive, indirect shadows, color bleeding) in real-time [S1, S2, unofficial]. Deferred nature avoids overdraw penalties for lighting [S1, unofficial]. Compatible with standard light types without shadow maps [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** High memory footprint due to multiple 3D textures. Quality depends on voxel resolution; low resolution leads to blocky artifacts. Cone tracing requires more samples when $\beta < 1$, increasing fragment shader cost [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Scenes with dynamic geometry and complex lighting where real-time global illumination is desired, and hardware supports efficient 3D texture sampling and atomic operations [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
Deferred Voxel Shading was introduced by Jose Villegas as an expansion upon rudimentary voxel cone tracing [S1, unofficial]. It merges voxel cone tracing with deferred shading to handle complex lighting effects like emissive materials and multiple light bounces [S1, unofficial]. The technique was presented in a blog post detailing a real-time global illumination implementation [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of voxel cone tracing (Crassin) and deferred rendering, aiming to provide a comprehensive set of indirect illumination effects in real-time applications [S1, S2, unofficial].
