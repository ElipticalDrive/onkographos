# Deferred Voxel Shading

Also called: Deferred Voxel Cone Tracing, Villegas Voxel Cone Tracing, Villegas Voxel Shading

## Overview
Deferred Voxel Shading is a real-time global illumination technique that merges voxel cone tracing with deferred shading to compute indirect lighting effects such as diffuse/specular reflection, color blending, emissive materials, and ambient occlusion [S2, unofficial]. Unlike forward voxel cone tracing, it delays lighting computations until the fragment shader stage, avoiding redundant calculations for overlapping geometry and enabling efficient handling of complex lighting scenarios with multiple light sources [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of four sequential stages: Voxelization, Voxel Illumination, Anisotropic Voxel Generation, and Voxel Cone Tracing [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Scene geometry (vertices, normals, UVs), material properties (albedo, emission, specular exponent), light sources (position, direction, intensity, type), and camera parameters.
*   **Outputs:** Final fragment color with indirect illumination, ambient occlusion, and shadows.

### Data Structures
The technique relies on several 3D textures bound as `uimage3D` (unsigned integer) or standard 3D textures for interpolation [S2, unofficial]:

1.  **Voxelization Storage:** Three `r32ui` 3D textures for atomic averaging of per-fragment data:
    *   `voxelAlbedo`: Stores averaged albedo.
    *   `voxelNormal`: Stores averaged surface normals.
    *   `voxelEmission`: Stores averaged emission values.
2.  **Radiance Volume:** A 3D texture storing the maximum level of detail (LOD) for the voxelized scene, containing the computed direct lighting and emission per voxel.
3.  **Directional Volumes:** Six 3D textures at half the resolution of the Radiance Volume. One texture exists for each axis direction (positive/negative X, Y, Z). Mipmap levels store directional values for anisotropic sampling.
4.  **Flag Volume:** A single-value 3D texture used to identify voxels as static. Used to prevent overwriting static geometry during dynamic updates [S2, unofficial].

### Step 1: Voxelization
Scene geometry is voxelized using conservative rasterization to fill the 3D textures. Atomic operations are used to average values from multiple fragments covering the same voxel [S2, unofficial].

```python
# Pseudocode for conservative voxelization compute shader
# Inputs: vertex data, material properties
# Outputs: voxelAlbedo, voxelNormal, voxelEmission, flagVolume

for each triangle:
    for each voxel intersected by triangle:
        # Check if static voxel already exists
        if is_static(voxel_position, flagVolume):
            continue
        
        # Calculate fragment attributes for this voxel intersection
        albedo = compute_fragment_albedo()
        normal = compute_fragment_normal()
        emission = compute_fragment_emission()
        
        # Atomic average into 3D textures
        imageAtomicRGBA8Avg(voxelAlbedo, voxel_position, albedo)
        imageAtomicRGBA8Avg(voxelNormal, voxel_position, normal)
        imageAtomicRGBA8Avg(voxelEmission, voxel_position, emission)
        
        # Mark as static if geometry is static
        if geometry_is_static:
            write_flag(voxel_position, flagVolume, 1)
```

### Step 2: Voxel Illumination
Direct lighting is computed per voxel using a compute shader. For each voxel, the outgoing radiance is calculated based on its position, normal, albedo, and emission [S2, unofficial].

**Normal Weighted Attenuation:**
To handle uneven normals within a voxel, attenuation is calculated per face of the voxel axis [S2, unofficial]:
$$ D_{x,y,z} = (\hat{i}\cdot\Psi, \hat{j}\cdot\Psi, \hat{k}\cdot\Psi) $$
where $\Psi$ is the light direction.

Dominant faces are selected based on the sign of the voxel's averaged normal vector component $N_\omega$:
$$ D_{\omega} = \begin{cases} \max\{D_\omega, 0\}, & N_\omega>0 \\ \max\{-D_\omega, 0\}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$

The reflectance model for direct lighting is:
$$ W = N^2 $$
$$ V_{r} = L_{i}\frac{\rho}{\pi}(W_x D_x + W_y D_y + W_z D_z) $$
where $L_i$ is light intensity, $\rho$ is voxel albedo, and $N$ is the voxel normal vector [S2, unofficial].

**Emission Handling:**
The emission term from voxelization is added to the direct illumination value per voxel. These voxels contribute indirect light during cone tracing, even in occluded areas [S2, unofficial].

**Voxel Occlusion (Soft Shadows):**
A ray is traced from the voxel position in the direction of the light. Soft shadows are approximated by accumulating a value $\kappa$ per collision and dividing by traced distance $t$:
$$ \nu = \nu + (1 - \nu)\kappa\div t $$
where $1 - \nu$ represents the final occlusion value [S2, unofficial].

### Step 3: Generate Anisotropic Voxels
Anisotropic filtering is applied to generate mipmaps for more precise cone tracing. Six directional volumes store directional values per axis [S2, unofficial].

1.  Volumetric integration in depth is performed to compute directional values.
2.  A compute shader executes one thread per voxel at each mipmap level, using values from the previous level.
3.  Directional samples are obtained by linearly interpolating the three samples from the selected directional volumes based on the cone direction [S2, unofficial].

### Step 4: Voxel Cone Tracing
Performed in the fragment shader as a screen-space effect. Cones advance, and the sampling volume increases via mipmap levels. Quadrilinear interpolation is used for smooth variation [S2, unofficial].

**Cone Parameters:**
*   Origin $C_o$, Direction $C_d$, Aperture angle $\theta$.
*   Cone diameter at distance $t$: $d=2t\times\tan(\theta\div 2)$.
*   Mipmap level selection: $V_{level} = log_2(d\div V_{size})$, where $V_{size}$ is the voxel size at maximum LOD [S2, unofficial].

**Accumulation:**
For each step, the occlusion $\alpha$ and color $c$ are updated using volumetric front-to-back accumulation:
$$ c =\alpha c + (1-\alpha)\alpha_2c_2 $$
$$ \alpha=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\alpha_2 $$
where $c_2$ and $\alpha_2$ are the retrieved outgoing radiance and occlusion from the voxel structure [S2, unofficial].

**Step Size:**
The distance $d'$ between cone steps is modified by a factor $\beta$. With $\beta = 1$, $d'$ equals the current diameter $d$. Values $< 1$ produce higher quality but require more samples [S2, unofficial].

**Indirect Illumination Approximation:**
A crude Monte Carlo approximation partitions the hemisphere integral into cone-shaped regions. For Blinn-Phong materials:
*   **Diffuse:** Few large cones distributed over the normal-oriented hemisphere.
*   **Specular:** A single cone in the reflected direction, with aperture depending on the specular exponent [S2, unofficial].

**Ambient Occlusion:**
Approximated using the same cones as diffuse reflection. The accumulated occlusion value $\alpha_2$ is multiplied by a weighting function $f(r) = \frac{1}{1+\lambda r}$, where $r$ is the current radius and $\lambda$ is a parameter [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Dynamic Updates:** Static voxels must be protected from overwriting. The Flag Volume is read before writing; if the position is marked static, writing is discarded. Dynamic voxels are cleared by reading the Flag Volume and checking if the voxel exists and is dynamic [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Variance:** Uneven normals within a voxel can cause inaccurate lighting. The normal-weighted attenuation method mitigates this [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Directional lights may benefit from shadow maps for increased precision, though they are not strictly required [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dependent on the number of voxels, lights, and cone tracing steps. The use of mipmaps and anisotropic filtering reduces the number of samples needed for accurate results compared to uniform voxel sampling.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for multiple 3D textures: Albedo, Normal, Emission, Radiance, six Directional volumes (at half resolution), and Flag volume.
*   **Strengths:** Handles complex lighting effects (emissive, indirect shadows, multiple bounces) efficiently in deferred rendering. Avoids redundant lighting calculations for overlapping geometry. Compatible with point, spot, and directional lights without shadow maps [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a deferred rendering backend. Performance is sensitive to the number of cone steps and the $\beta$ factor. High-quality results may require many samples if $\beta < 1$.
*   **When to use:** Scenes with many light sources, complex indirect lighting requirements, and emissive materials where forward rendering would be inefficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Deferred Voxel Shading was presented by Jose Villegas [S1, unofficial]. It expands upon rudimentary voxel cone tracing by integrating deferred shading principles. The approach was detailed in a blog post and associated source code, providing a practical implementation for real-time global illumination [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
