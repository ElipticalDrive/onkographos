# Unidirectional Importance Sampling for Double-Scattering

Also called: Analytic unidirectional PDF sampling, Joint importance sampling of low-order volumetric scattering (unidirectional)

## Overview
Unidirectional importance sampling for double-scattering is a fully analytic method to importance sample the geometry terms along 3-segment paths (double-scattering) in isotropic participating media [S1]. It generalizes equiangular sampling for 2-segment paths [S1]. By sampling two distances and a direction to construct two intermediate vertices between a light source and a camera, it effectively cancels out singularities in the path contribution function, reducing variance compared to standard techniques [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Camera position $\mathbf{d}$ and viewing direction $\omega_{dc}$.
- Light source position $\mathbf{a}$.
- A fixed intermediate vertex $\mathbf{c}$ (typically on the camera ray or a previous path segment).
- Uniform random numbers $\xi, \xi_1, \xi_2 \in [0, 1)$.
- Maximum sampling distance $t_{dc}^{\max}$ along the ray $(\mathbf{d}, \omega_{dc})$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Intermediate vertex $\mathbf{b}$ defined by distance $t_{cb}$ and direction $\omega_{cb}$.
- Distance $t_{dc}$ along the ray $(\mathbf{d}, \omega_{dc})$.

**Data Structures:**
- Local coordinate frame at $\mathbf{c}$ where the vector $\mathbf{c} \to \mathbf{a}$ defines the local z-axis.
- Geometric quantities:
  - $h_{ca}^{\perp}$: Distance between $\mathbf{a}$ and its projection onto the ray $(\mathbf{c}, \omega_{cb})$.
  - $t_{ca}^{\perp}$: Distance between $\mathbf{c}$ and this projection.
  - $h_{da}^{\perp}$: Distance between $\mathbf{a}$ and its projection onto the ray $(\mathbf{d}, \omega_{dc})$.
  - $t_{da}^{\perp}$: Distance between $\mathbf{d}$ and this projection.
  - $h_{da}$: Distance between $\mathbf{a}$ and its projection onto the ray $(\mathbf{d}, \omega_{dc})$ (used in PDF evaluation).
  - $t_{da}$: Distance between $\mathbf{d}$ and this projection (used in PDF evaluation).

**Steps:**

1. **Sample Direction $\omega_{cb}$:**
   Generate uniform random numbers $\xi_1, \xi_2 \in [0, 1)$. Compute spherical coordinates relative to the local frame where $\mathbf{c} \to \mathbf{a}$ is the z-axis [S1]:
   $$ \theta_{cb} = \pi(1 - \sqrt{\xi_1}) $$
   $$ \phi_{cb} = 2\pi\xi_2 $$
   Convert $(\theta_{cb}, \phi_{cb})$ to the direction vector $\omega_{cb}$ in the local frame, then transform to world coordinates [S1].

2. **Sample Distance $t_{cb}$:**
   Given uniform random number $\xi$, compute $t_{cb}$ using the inverse CDF of the equiangular PDF [S1]:
   $$ t_{cb} = t_{ca}^{\perp} + h_{ca}^{\perp} \tan\left(\xi(\pi - \theta_{cb}) + \theta_{cb} - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) $$
   The vertex $\mathbf{b}$ is located at $\mathbf{c} + t_{cb} \omega_{cb}$ [S1].

3. **Sample Distance $t_{dc}$:**
   The PDF for sampling $t_{dc}$ along the ray $(\mathbf{d}, \omega_{dc})$ is:
   $$ p(t_{dc} | \Xi) = \frac{1}{C_{t_{dc}}^{\max}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{da}^2 + (t_{da} - t_{dc})^2}} $$
   where $C_{t_{dc}}^{\max}$ is the normalization constant [S1].
   
   Calculate the normalization constant $C_{\Xi}$ (which equals $1/C_{t_{dc}}^{\max}$) [S1]:
   $$ C_{\Xi} = \frac{1}{a \sinh\left(\frac{t_{dc}^{\max} - t_{da}^{\perp}}{h_{da}^{\perp}}\right) - a \sinh\left(\frac{-t_{da}^{\perp}}{h_{da}^{\perp}}\right)} $$
   Note: The source text uses "a sinh" which is interpreted as the hyperbolic sine function $\sinh$ based on standard mathematical notation in this context, despite the potential typo in the source snippet [S1].
   
   To sample $t_{dc}$, one must invert the CDF derived from $p(t_{dc} | \Xi)$. The source provides the PDF and normalization but does not explicitly provide the closed-form inverse CDF for $t_{dc}$ in the provided notes. However, the structure implies sampling from this specific distribution. **Joint PDF:**
The joint PDF for the double-scattering path is [S1]:
$$ p(\mathbf{b}, t_{dc} | \Xi) = \frac{G(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) G(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})}{\pi^3 C_{t_{dc}}^{\max}} $$
This includes the geometry term $G(abcd)$ [S1]. The constant $C_{\Xi}$ incorporates the product of the three isotropic phase functions $(4\pi)^{-3}$ [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
- The PDF for $\omega_{cb}$ diverges when $\theta_{cb}$ approaches zero. This singularity is cancelled out by sampling from this specific PDF, reducing variance. The equiangular PDF has no singularity because $\omega_{cb}$ is fixed [S1].
- The integral for the normalization constant diverges at infinity; a maximum sampling distance $t_{dc}^{\max}$ must be set [S1].
- For double-scattering, the propagation distance is chosen proportionally to the inverse distance to $\mathbf{a}$, whereas for single-scattering (equiangular), it is chosen proportionally to the inverse squared distance to $\mathbf{a}$ [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Tabulated Importance Sampling for Anisotropic Scattering](4542-tabulated-importance-sampling-for-anisotropic-scattering.md)
A method to efficiently tabulate target PDFs for both unidirectional and bidirectional factorizations to handle anisotropic scattering, avoiding the need for analytic formulations which are unavailable for some conditional PDFs.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Fully analytic, requiring only basic trigonometric and hyperbolic functions. No iterative solvers or numerical integration required for sampling [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Minimal, storing only geometric quantities and random numbers [S1].
- **Strengths:** Reduces variance by cancelling singularities in the path contribution function. Generalizes equiangular sampling to double-scattering [S1].
- **Limitations:** Limited to isotropic participating media in its current analytic form. Requires a maximum sampling distance to avoid divergence in the normalization constant [S1].
- **When to use:** When rendering double-scattering effects in isotropic media and variance reduction is critical [S1].
- **When not to use:** For anisotropic scattering without tabulation, or when single-scattering is sufficient [S1].

## Background
Originated in the paper "Joint importance sampling of low-order volumetric scattering" [S1]. It generalizes Kulla and Fajardo's [2012] equiangular sampling for 2-segment paths and complements Kalos' [1963] OCF estimator [S1].
