# Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering Strategy

Also called: Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering with Volumetric Multiple Scattering Approximation, Hybrid Rendering Strategy for Hair/Fur, Hybrid light transport estimator, Volumetric Approximation for Hair/Fur Multiple Scattering

## Overview
This method approximates multiple scattering in dense hair and fur by combining explicit geometric ray tracing for direct illumination with a volumetric participating medium model for higher-order scattering [S1]. It solves the computational bottleneck of simulating dozens of light bounces among fibers by treating the aggregate fiber distribution as an anisotropic medium, preserving high-frequency strand details while efficiently capturing soft, low-frequency volumetric glow [S1]. Unlike microflake models that assume opaque particles or dual scattering methods that rely on local uniformity assumptions, this approach normalizes the hair BCSDF to define the phase function and uses spectral reweighting to maintain accurate color shifts [S1].

## How it works
The method operates in two main phases: precomputation of volumetric properties and a hybrid rendering pass.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Explicit hair/fur geometry (strands), hair scattering model (BCSDF), lighting environment, camera parameters.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered image with direct and multiple scattering contributions.

### Data Structures
1.  **Volumetric Grid:** A 3D grid (voxelization) of the hair volume.
    *   **Fields:**
        *   `density`: Local fiber density.
        *   `direction`: Average fiber orientation vector.
        *   `sigma_t`: Total extinction coefficient (derived from density and fiber properties).
        *   `sigma_s`: Scattering coefficient.
        *   `sigma_a`: Absorption coefficient ($\sigma_a = \sigma_t - \sigma_s$).
        *   `phase_function`: Anisotropic phase function derived from the normalized BCSDF.
        *   `albedo`: Spectral albedo, precomputed as a 1D function of incident inclination angle $\theta_i$ relative to the local fiber direction [S1].
2.  **Hair BCSDF:** The base single-fiber reflectance model, specifically the double-cylinder fur model by Yan et al. [2017a] accounting for cuticle, cortex, and medulla layers [S1].

### Parameters
*   $N$: Number of octaves in the multiple scattering summation.
*   $a$: Scalar controlling effective attenuation, allowing deeper light penetration for higher-order terms.
*   $\gamma$: Scalar gain parameter in $[0, 2]$ to moderate excessive color saturation [S1].
*   $c$: Parameter in $[0, 1]$ controlling the rate at which higher-order scattering becomes isotropic [S1].
*   *Note:* These parameters are obtained via numerical optimization using Ceres Solver with L1 loss on a controlled studio lighting setup [S1].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Precomputation (Voxelization):**
    *   Rasterize hair geometry to accumulate per-pixel visibility and hair density in screen space [S1].
    *   Compute local fiber densities and average orientations to populate the volumetric grid with directionally varying coefficients and the anisotropic phase function [S1].

2.  **First Geometric Interaction (Direct Lighting):**
    *   Trace camera rays to find the first intersection with explicit hair geometry [S1].
    *   Compute direct illumination at this intersection using the hair BCSDF and standard next-event estimation [S1].
    *   *Constraint:* The same BCSDF used here must be used to derive the volumetric phase function [S1].

3.  **Transition to Volumetric Domain:**
    *   Sample an outgoing direction $\omega_o$ from the hair BCSDF at the first hit [S1].
    *   Propagate the light path into the surrounding hair volume.
    *   *Constraint:* Restrict subsequent geometric interactions to at most one (the initial hit). All further transport is handled exclusively in the equivalent participating medium [S1].

4.  **Single Scattering Estimation in Medium:**
    *   Sample a scattering point $p$ along the view ray within the equivalent medium using delta tracking [S1].
    *   Select a random light source direction $\omega_i$ [S1].
    *   Evaluate transmittance from $p$ to the light source by ray marching through the heterogeneous medium [S1].
    *   Compute the single-scattering estimate $L_{single}$ at $p$ using the direct illumination from the light source and the medium's scattering properties [S1].

5.  **Multiple Scattering Approximation (Spectral Reweighting):**
    *   Approximate higher-order scattering by reweighting the single-scattering estimate $L_{single}$ using a sum of scaled terms over $N$ octaves [S1].
    *   The total volumetric contribution $L$ is calculated as:
        $$ L = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} L_i $$
    *   Each octave term $L_i$ is defined as:
        $$ L_i = \sigma_s(p, \omega_i) (\gamma \cdot \text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i))^i L_{light}(\omega_i) P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) \exp\left(-a i \int_0^t \sigma_t(x(s), \omega_i) ds\right) $$
        Where:
        *   $L_{light}(\omega_i)$ is the incident radiance from the light source.
        *   $\text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i)$ is the spectral albedo at point $p$ for direction $\omega_i$ [S1].
        *   $P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ is the modified phase function for octave $i$.
        *   The integral represents the optical depth along the shadow ray [S1].
    *   **Modified Phase Function:**
        $$ P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \text{lerp}(P(\omega_i, \omega_o), P_{iso}, (1 - c)^i) $$
        Where $P$ is the original hair phase function and $P_{iso}$ is an isotropic phase function [S1].
    *   **Evaluation:** The summation can be evaluated deterministically or stochastically by sampling a single octave according to its relative contribution [S1].

6.  **Compositing:**
    *   Combine the direct geometric contribution and the approximated multiple scattering volumetric contribution to produce the final pixel color [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def hybrid_hair_rendering(camera_ray, hair_geometry, light_sources, vol_grid):
    # 1. First Geometric Interaction
    hit = ray_intersect_hair(camera_ray, hair_geometry)
    if hit is None:
        return background_color

    # Compute direct illumination using hair BCSDF
    # Note: BCSDF must be consistent with vol_grid phase function derivation
    direct_radiance = compute_direct_illumination(hit, hair_bcsdf, light_sources)

    # 2. Transition to Volumetric Domain
    # Sample outgoing direction from BCSDF
    omega_o = sample_bcsdf_outgoing(hit.normal, hit.tangent, hair_bcsdf)
    
    # 3. Single Scattering in Equivalent Medium
    # Sample scattering point p using delta tracking in heterogeneous medium
    p = delta_track_sample(camera_ray, vol_grid)
    
    if p is None:
        return direct_radiance

    # Select random light direction
    light_idx = random_int(0, len(light_sources))
    light = light_sources[light_idx]
    omega_i = vector_to_light(p, light)

    # Evaluate transmittance via ray marching
    transmittance = ray_march_transmittance(p, omega_i, vol_grid)
    
    # Get incident radiance from light
    L_light = light_radiance(omega_i)

    # Compute single scattering estimate
    sigma_s = vol_grid.get_sigma_s(p, omega_i)
    albedo = vol_grid.get_albedo(p, omega_i)
    phase = vol_grid.get_phase_function(p, omega_i, omega_o)
    
    L_single = sigma_s * albedo * L_light * phase * transmittance

    # 4. Multiple Scattering Approximation
    # Stochastic sampling of octave
    octave = sample_octave_stochastically(L_single, N, a, gamma, c, sigma_s, albedo, phase, transmittance, L_light)
    
    # Compute contribution for sampled octave
    L_ms = compute_octave_contribution(
        p=p, 
        omega_i=omega_i, 
        omega_o=omega_o, 
        octave=octave, 
        N=N, 
        a=a, 
        gamma=gamma, 
        c=c, 
        sigma_s=sigma_s, 
        albedo=albedo, 
        phase=phase, 
        transmittance=transmittance, 
        L_light=L_light
    )

    # 5. Composite
    return direct_radiance + L_ms

def compute_octave_contribution(p, omega_i, omega_o, octave, N, a, gamma, c, sigma_s, albedo, phase, transmittance, L_light):
    i = octave
    # Modified phase function: lerp between original and isotropic
    # P_iso is assumed to be a constant 1/(4*pi) or similar isotropic distribution
    P_iso = isotropic_phase_function()
    P_prime = lerp(phase, P_iso, (1 - c) ** i)
    
    # Attenuation factor for higher order
    # Note: The integral in the source is approximated by the transmittance computed for single scattering
    # scaled by 'i' and 'a'. 
    # Source: exp(-a * i * integral(sigma_t ds))
    # We reuse the optical depth from single scattering transmittance calculation
    # Assuming transmittance = exp(-integral(sigma_t ds))
    optical_depth = -log(transmittance) if transmittance > 0 else 0
    ms_transmittance = exp(-a * i * optical_depth)
    
    term = sigma_s * (gamma * albedo) ** i * L_light * P_prime * ms_transmittance
    return term

def sample_octave_stochastically(L_single, N, a, gamma, c, sigma_s, albedo, phase, transmittance, L_light):
    # Compute weights for each octave to sample one
    weights = []
    for i in range(N):
        P_prime = lerp(phase, isotropic_phase_function(), (1 - c) ** i)
        optical_depth = -log(transmittance) if transmittance > 0 else 0
        ms_transmittance = exp(-a * i * optical_depth)
        w = sigma_s * (gamma * albedo) ** i * L_light * P_prime * ms_transmittance
        weights.append(w)
    
    # Normalize and sample
    total_weight = sum(weights)
    if total_weight == 0:
        return 0
    normalized_weights = [w / total_weight for w in weights]
    return categorical_sample(normalized_weights)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Spectral Sampling:** The derived phase function is spectrally varying. Normally this requires per-channel sampling, but this method avoids it by evaluating the phase function only for a deterministic outgoing direction during single-scattering estimation [S1].
*   **Color Saturation:** Using a scalar contribution factor instead of spectral albedo leads to severe color loss. Repeated application of the albedo term can cause excessive saturation, necessitating the gain parameter $\gamma$ [S1].
*   **Tightly Coiled Hair:** The method struggles to reproduce localized bright spots from intense multiple backscattering in highly scattering, tightly coiled hair (e.g., white Afro), approximating them as macroscopic energy distribution instead [S1].
*   **Flyaway Strands:** Unlike voxel-based radiance accumulation methods (e.g., Moon et al. [2008]), this method does not underestimate multiple scattering for lifted flyaway strands, as it relies on explicit geometric interaction for the first bounce [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** Achieves a speedup of 7.1× to 11.06× compared to ground truth path tracing at the same noise level, and 2.7× to 3.26× compared to Extended Dual Scattering [S1].
*   **Timing:** On human hair, renders an image in 1.17 minutes on average, compared to 9.98 minutes for path tracing (8.53× speedup) and 2.27 minutes for Dual Scattering (1.94× speedup) [S1]. On fur scenes, achieves an average speedup of 3.84× (1.17 minutes vs. 4.49 minutes) compared to Extended Dual Scattering [S1].
*   **Samples:** Requires 256 SPP for equal visual quality, whereas path tracing and Dual Scattering require 512 SPP [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency strand details via explicit geometry while efficiently capturing soft volumetric glow. Robust under various lighting conditions (studio, environment, directional, spot) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot reproduce localized bright spots from intense backscattering in tightly coiled hair. Relies on precomputed parameters optimized for a specific lighting setup, though it remains robust under different setups [S1].
*   **Hardware/Resolution:** Tested on AMD Ryzen 9 9950X at 1024 × 1024 resolution [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed by Hu, Zhu, Lin, and Zhang (Nanjing University/Shandong University) in 2026 [S1].
*   **Development:** Builds on the insight that dense hair behaves like an anisotropic participating medium at the scale of multiple scattering. It adapts the single-to-multiple scattering approximation from Wrenninge et al. [2013] to handle spectral and angular characteristics of hair, using the Yan et al. [2017a] double-cylinder fur model for single-fiber reflectance [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented in Mitsuba 0.6 [S1].
