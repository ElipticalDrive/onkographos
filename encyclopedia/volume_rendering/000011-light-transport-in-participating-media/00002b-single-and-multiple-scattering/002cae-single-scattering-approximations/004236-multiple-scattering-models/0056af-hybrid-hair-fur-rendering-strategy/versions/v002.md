# Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering Strategy

Also called: Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering with Volumetric Multiple Scattering Approximation, Hybrid Rendering Strategy for Hair/Fur, Hybrid light transport estimator, Volumetric Approximation for Hair/Fur Multiple Scattering

## Overview
This method approximates multiple scattering in dense hair and fur by combining explicit geometric ray tracing for direct illumination with a volumetric participating medium model for higher-order scattering [S1]. It solves the problem of high computational cost and visual artifacts (such as overly dry or blurred appearances) associated with simulating dozens of light bounces in fibrous media [S1]. Unlike microflake models that assume opaque particles or prior volumetric methods used only for visibility acceleration, this approach normalizes the hair BCSDF to define a spectrally varying phase function, preserving intricate light-fiber interactions while treating the aggregate volume as an anisotropic medium [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Hair/fur geometry (explicit strands), material properties (BCSDF parameters), lighting configuration (area lights, environment maps), and camera parameters.
**Outputs:** Final rendered image with direct and multiple-scattered illumination.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Voxel Grid:** Stores local fiber density $\rho$, average fiber direction $\mathbf{d}$, and precomputed optical properties.
    *   Fields: `density` (float), `direction` (vec3, normalized), `extinction` (vec3), `scattering` (vec3), `absorption` (vec3).
    *   The extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$, scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$, and absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$ are derived from the fiber distribution and are directionally varying [S1].
2.  **Phase Function:** Derived from the hair BCSDF $S(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ by normalizing over the hemisphere/sphere to ensure energy conservation, resulting in a spectrally varying phase function $P(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ [S1].
3.  **Albedo Map:** Precomputed as a 1D function of the incident inclination angle $\theta_i$ relative to the local fiber direction, using the standard angular parameterization of hair scattering models [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of octaves in the multiple scattering summation.
*   $a$: Scalar controlling effective attenuation, allowing deeper light penetration for higher-order terms.
*   $\gamma$: Scalar gain parameter in $[0, 2]$ to moderate excessive color saturation [S1].
*   $c$: Parameter in $[0, 1]$ controlling the rate at which higher-order scattering becomes isotropic [S1].
*   Base single-fiber reflectance model: Yan et al. [2017a] (double-cylinder fur model) [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Voxelization (Precomputation):**
    Voxelize the hair geometry to compute local fiber densities and average orientations. This yields an equivalent volumetric medium with directionally varying extinction, absorption, and scattering coefficients, as well as an anisotropic phase function [S1].

2.  **Rasterization Pass:**
    Accumulate per-pixel visibility and hair density in screen space. This step prepares the data for the subsequent shading pass [S1].

3.  **Shading Pass (First Geometric Interaction):**
    For each pixel, trace the camera ray to find the first intersection with explicit hair geometry.
    *   Compute direct illumination at this intersection using an arbitrary hair BCSDF and standard next-event estimation.
    *   Ensure the same scattering model used here is consistent with the volumetric representation [S1].

4.  **Transition to Volume:**
    Sample an outgoing direction from the hair BCSDF at the intersection point. Propagate the light path into the surrounding hair volume.
    *   Restrict subsequent geometric interactions to at most one (the initial hit). All further transport is handled exclusively in the equivalent participating medium [S1].

5.  **Single Scattering in Medium:**
    Within the equivalent medium, explicitly sample at most one volumetric scattering event.
    *   Sample a scattering point $p$ along the view ray using delta tracking [Woodcock et al. 1965] in the heterogeneous medium [S1].
    *   At point $p$, estimate direct illumination by connecting to a randomly selected light source.
    *   Evaluate transmittance along the shadow ray using ray marching through the medium. This yields a single-scattering estimate $L_{ss}$ [S1].

6.  **Multiple Scattering Approximation:**
    Approximate higher-order scattering by reweighting the single-scattering estimate. The total volumetric contribution $L$ is a sum of scaled single-scattering terms over $N$ octaves:
    $$ L = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} L_i $$
    where each term $L_i$ is defined as:
    $$ L_i = \sigma_s(p, \omega_i) (\gamma \cdot \text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i))^i L_{\text{light}}(\omega_i) P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) \exp\left(-a_i \int_0^t \sigma_t(x(s), \omega_i) ds\right) $$
    Here, $p$ is the volumetric scattering location, $\omega_i$ is the incident direction, $L_{\text{light}}$ is the incident radiance, and the integral represents optical depth [S1].

    *   **Spectral Albedo:** Use local RGB albedo $\text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i)$ to control spectral energy redistribution. Using a scalar factor causes severe color loss [S1].
    *   **Gain Parameter:** Apply $\gamma \in [0, 2]$ to moderate excessive color saturation from repeated albedo application [S1].
    *   **Modified Phase Function:** Define $P'_i$ as a linear interpolation between the original hair phase function $P$ and an isotropic phase function $P_{\text{iso}}$:
        $$ P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \text{lerp}(P(\omega_i, \omega_o), P_{\text{iso}}, 1 - c^i) $$
        where $c \in [0, 1]$ controls the rate of isotropization. This avoids the need for an eccentricity parameter, which is not available for the general hair phase function [S1].
    *   **Evaluation:** Evaluate the summation stochastically by sampling a single octave according to its relative contribution, or deterministically by accumulating all terms. The stochastic approach is preferred for variance-performance trade-offs [S1].

7.  **Compositing:**
    Composite the direct geometric contribution and the approximated multiple-scattering volumetric contribution to produce the final pixel color [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def render_hybrid_hair(camera_ray, light_sources, voxel_grid, bcsdf, params):
    """
    Renders a single pixel using the hybrid hair/fur strategy.
    
    Args:
        camera_ray: Ray origin and direction.
        light_sources: List of light sources.
        voxel_grid: Heterogeneous medium data structure.
        bcsdf: Hair/Bidirectional Curve Scattering Distribution Function.
        params: Dictionary containing N, a, gamma, c.
        
    Returns:
        Color value (RGB).
    """
    
    # 1. First Geometric Interaction
    hit = trace_geometry(camera_ray)
    if hit is None:
        return background_color()
    
    # Compute direct illumination at the first hit
    # Uses standard next-event estimation with the hair BCSDF
    direct_color = compute_direct_illumination(hit, light_sources, bcsdf)
    
    # 2. Transition to Volume
    # Sample outgoing direction from BCSDF to enter the volume
    # Note: In a rasterization-based pipeline, this direction is often 
    # derived from the view ray or sampled stochastically for the 
    # volumetric pass. Here we assume we are evaluating the volumetric 
    # contribution for the pixel.
    
    # 3. Single Scattering in Equivalent Medium
    # Sample scattering point p using delta tracking in the heterogeneous medium
    p = delta_track(camera_ray, voxel_grid)
    
    if p is None:
        return direct_color
    
    # Select a random light source for the shadow ray
    light = random_light(light_sources)
    
    # Evaluate transmittance from p to light using ray marching
    transmittance = ray_march_transmittance(p, light.direction, voxel_grid)
    
    # Get incident radiance from light
    L_light = light.intensity * transmittance
    
    # Get incident direction relative to local fiber frame at p
    omega_i = light.direction
    omega_o = camera_ray.direction # Outgoing direction towards camera
    
    # Evaluate single scattering term (octave 0)
    # Note: sigma_s and sigma_t are looked up from voxel_grid at p
    sigma_s = voxel_grid.get_scattering_coefficient(p, omega_i)
    sigma_t = voxel_grid.get_extinction_coefficient(p, omega_i)
    
    # Albedo is precomputed as function of inclination angle theta_i
    theta_i = angle_between(omega_i, voxel_grid.get_direction(p))
    albedo = voxel_grid.get_albedo(theta_i)
    
    # Original phase function P
    P = bcsdf.normalize_phase_function(omega_i, omega_o)
    
    # Isotropic phase function
    P_iso = 1.0 / (4.0 * pi)
    
    # Modified phase function for octave 0
    # c is params['c']
    P_prime_0 = lerp(P, P_iso, 1.0 - params['c']**0)
    
    # Optical depth integral for octave 0
    # a is params['a']
    # For octave 0, a_0 = a * 0? No, a_i scales the attenuation.
    # The formula uses a_i. The text says "a controls the effective attenuation... 
    # allowing deeper light penetration for higher-order scattering terms".
    # Typically a_i = a * i or similar. Let's assume a_i = a * i based on 
    # "higher-order scattering terms" implying i-th term has different attenuation.
    # However, the formula shows exp(-a_i * integral). 
    # If i=0, a_0=0, then exp(0)=1. This matches the single scattering term 
    # having no extra attenuation scaling for the "multiple" part? 
    # Actually, L_0 is the base single scattering. 
    # Let's look at the formula: L_i = ... exp(-a_i * integral).
    # If i=0, a_0 should be 0 to represent the direct single scattering 
    # without the "multiple scattering approximation" attenuation?
    # Or does a_i apply to all? The text says "a controls... for higher-order".
    # Let's assume a_i = a * i.
    
    optical_depth = ray_march_optical_depth(p, light.direction, voxel_grid)
    a_i = params['a'] * 0 # For i=0
    
    L_0 = sigma_s * (params['gamma'] * albedo)**0 * L_light * P_prime_0 * exp(-a_i * optical_depth)
    
    # 4. Multiple Scattering Approximation
    total_ms_color = L_0
    
    # Stochastic evaluation: sample a single octave
    # Compute weights for each octave i=1 to N-1
    weights = []
    for i in range(1, params['N']):
        sigma_s_i = voxel_grid.get_scattering_coefficient(p, omega_i)
        P_prime_i = lerp(P, P_iso, 1.0 - params['c']**i)
        a_i = params['a'] * i
        optical_depth_i = ray_march_optical_depth(p, light.direction, voxel_grid)
        
        term = sigma_s_i * (params['gamma'] * albedo)**i * L_light * P_prime_i * exp(-a_i * optical_depth_i)
        weights.append(abs(term))
        
    # Normalize weights
    total_weight = sum(weights)
    if total_weight > 0:
        weights = [w / total_weight for w in weights]
    else:
        weights = [1.0 / (params['N'] - 1)] * (params['N'] - 1)
        
    # Sample octave index
    if params['N'] > 1:
        i_sample = sample_categorical(weights)
        # Recompute L_i_sample
        sigma_s_i = voxel_grid.get_scattering_coefficient(p, omega_i)
        P_prime_i = lerp(P, P_iso, 1.0 - params['c']**i_sample)
        a_i = params['a'] * i_sample
        optical_depth_i = ray_march_optical_depth(p, light.direction, voxel_grid)
        
        L_i = sigma_s_i * (params['gamma'] * albedo)**i_sample * L_light * P_prime_i * exp(-a_i * optical_depth_i)
        
        # Scale by inverse probability (unbiased estimator)
        # Note: The sum includes L_0. If we sample i > 0, we replace L_0?
        # The text says "sum of scaled single-scattering terms".
        # Stochastic sampling of the sum:
        # If we sample i=0, value is L_0 / prob(0).
        # If we sample i>0, value is L_i / prob(i).
        # But L_0 is already computed.
        # Let's implement the full sum stochastically.
        
        all_L = [L_0] + weights # Wait, weights were for i=1..N-1.
        # Let's rebuild the list of all terms including L_0
        all_terms = [L_0]
        for i in range(1, params['N']):
             # Recompute term for weight calculation if not stored
             # For simplicity, assume we stored them or recompute
             sigma_s_i = voxel_grid.get_scattering_coefficient(p, omega_i)
             P_prime_i = lerp(P, P_iso, 1.0 - params['c']**i)
             a_i = params['a'] * i
             optical_depth_i = ray_march_optical_depth(p, light.direction, voxel_grid)
             term = sigma_s_i * (params['gamma'] * albedo)**i * L_light * P_prime_i * exp(-a_i * optical_depth_i)
             all_terms.append(term)
             
        term_weights = [abs(t) for t in all_terms]
        total_w = sum(term_weights)
        if total_w > 0:
            term_weights = [w / total_w for w in term_weights]
        else:
            term_weights = [1.0 / params['N']] * params['N']
            
        i_sample = sample_categorical(term_weights)
        ms_color = all_terms[i_sample] / term_weights[i_sample]
    else:
        ms_color = L_0
        
    return direct_color + ms_color

def delta_track(ray, voxel_grid):
    """
    Samples a scattering point along the ray using delta tracking.
    """
    # Implementation of delta tracking [Woodcock et al. 1965]
    # 1. Find max sigma_t in the medium
    sigma_t_max = voxel_grid.get_max_extinction()
    
    # 2. Sample distance
    # z = -ln(u) / sigma_t_max
    u = random_float()
    z = -log(u) / sigma_t_max
    
    # 3. Sample point
    p = ray.origin + ray.direction * z
    
    # 4. Accept/Reject
    sigma_t_at_p = voxel_grid.get_extinction_coefficient(p, ray.direction)
    if random_float() < sigma_t_at_p / sigma_t_max:
        return p
    else:
        # Reject and repeat? Or return None for this pass?
        # In a single-scattering pass, if no point is found, return None
        return None

def ray_march_transmittance(origin, direction, voxel_grid):
    """
    Evaluates transmittance along a shadow ray using ray marching.
    """
    # Standard ray marching through voxel grid
    # Accumulate optical depth
    optical_depth = ray_march_optical_depth(origin, direction, voxel_grid)
    return exp(-optical_depth)

def ray_march_optical_depth(origin, direction, voxel_grid):
    """
    Computes the integral of sigma_t along the ray.
    """
    # Sample points along the ray
    # Integrate sigma_t(x) dx
    # Simplified: sum of sigma_t * step_size
    total_od = 0.0
    current_pos = origin
    step = voxel_grid.get_voxel_size()
    # March until exit or max distance
    while current_pos is inside_volume(voxel_grid):
        sigma_t = voxel_grid.get_extinction_coefficient(current_pos, direction)
        total_od += sigma_t * step
        current_pos += direction * step
    return total_od

def lerp(a, b, t):
    return a * (1.0 - t) + b * t

def sample_categorical(weights):
    """
    Samples an index based on weights.
    """
    u = random_float()
    cumulative = 0.0
    for i, w in enumerate(weights):
        cumulative += w
        if u <= cumulative:
            return i
    return len(weights) - 1
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method achieves a speedup of 7.1× to 11.06× compared to ground truth path tracing at the same noise level, and 2.7× to 3.26× compared to Extended Dual Scattering [S1]. On human hair, it renders an image in 1.17 minutes on average, compared to 9.98 minutes for path tracing (8.53× speedup) and 2.27 minutes for Dual Scattering (1.94× speedup) [S1]. On fur scenes, it achieves an average speedup of about 3.84× (1.17 minutes vs. 4.49 minutes) compared to Extended Dual Scattering [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the voxel grid (density, direction, optical coefficients) and precomputed albedo maps. The voxelization step adds memory overhead proportional to the volume of the hair/fur asset [S1].
*   **Performance:** Under equal visual quality, the method requires 256 SPP, whereas path tracing and Dual Scattering require 512 SPP [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency details via explicit geometry for direct lighting; efficiently approximates soft, low-frequency multiple scattering; robust under various lighting conditions; preserves spectral color shifts better than scalar-based approximations [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Struggles to reproduce localized bright spots from intense multiple backscattering in highly scattering and tightly coiled hair (e.g., white Afro), approximating them as macroscopic energy distribution instead [S1]. The derived phase function is spectrally varying, but the method avoids per-channel sampling by evaluating it only for a deterministic outgoing direction [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed by Hu, Zhu, Lin, and Zhang, and published in ACM Transactions on Graphics (SIGGRAPH) in July 2026 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of prior approximations like dual scattering and fur BSSRDF, which often fail for dense hair/fur, yielding overly dry or blurred appearances [S1]. The approach builds on the insight that densely packed hair fibers collectively behave like a continuous scattering medium, allowing the use of volumetric light transport techniques [S1]. It is implemented in Mitsuba 0.6 [S1]. Parameters are obtained by numerical optimization using Ceres Solver with an L1 loss on a scene containing only hair geometry under a studio-style configuration of three area lights [S1].
