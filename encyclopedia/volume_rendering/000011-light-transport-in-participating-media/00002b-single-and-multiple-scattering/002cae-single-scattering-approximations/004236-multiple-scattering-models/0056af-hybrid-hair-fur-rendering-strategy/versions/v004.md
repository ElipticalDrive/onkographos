# Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering Strategy

Also called: Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering with Volumetric Multiple Scattering Approximation, Hybrid Rendering Strategy for Hair/Fur, Hybrid light transport estimator, Volumetric Approximation for Hair/Fur Multiple Scattering

## Overview
This method addresses the computational cost of simulating multiple scattering in dense hair and fur by treating the aggregate fiber volume as an anisotropic participating medium [S1]. It solves the problem of "dry" or blurred appearances in prior approximations (like Dual Scattering) by combining explicit geometric ray tracing for direct illumination with a restricted volumetric estimator for higher-order scattering [S1]. Unlike microflake models that assume opaque particles, this approach normalizes the hair BCSDF to define the phase function, preserving complex transmission paths while achieving a 7–11× speedup over ground-truth path tracing [S1].

## How it works
The method employs a hybrid geometric–volumetric representation. Explicit fiber geometry is used for high-frequency direct illumination, while an equivalent anisotropic volumetric medium approximates the low-frequency, soft contributions of multiple scattering [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Hair/fur geometry (strands), hair material parameters (BCSDF), lighting environment, camera parameters.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered image with direct and multiple scattering contributions.

### Data Structures
1.  **Volumetric Medium Grid:** A 3D grid (voxelization) of the hair volume.
    *   **Fields:**
        *   `density`: Local fiber density.
        *   `fiber_direction`: Average fiber orientation vector per voxel.
        *   `sigma_t`: Extinction coefficient (derived from density and fiber properties).
        *   `sigma_s`: Scattering coefficient.
        *   `sigma_a`: Absorption coefficient ($\sigma_a = \sigma_t - \sigma_s$).
        *   `phase_function`: Anisotropic phase function derived from the normalized hair BCSDF.
2.  **Hair BCSDF:** The base single-fiber reflectance model, specifically the double-cylinder fur model by Yan et al. [2017a], which accounts for cuticle, cortex, and medulla layers [S1].
3.  **Albedo Map:** A 1D function of the incident inclination angle $\theta_i$ relative to the local fiber direction, precomputed from the BCSDF [S1].

### Parameters
*   $N$: Number of octaves in the multiple scattering summation.
*   $a$: Scalar controlling effective attenuation, allowing deeper light penetration for higher-order terms.
*   $\gamma$: Scalar gain parameter in $[0, 2]$ to moderate excessive color saturation [S1].
*   $c$: Parameter in $[0, 1]$ controlling the rate at which higher-order scattering becomes isotropic [S1].
*   *Note:* These parameters are obtained via numerical optimization using Ceres Solver with L1 loss on a studio-style lighting setup [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Precomputation (Voxelization):**
    Voxelize the hair geometry to compute local fiber densities and average orientations. Derive the equivalent volumetric medium properties ($\sigma_t, \sigma_s, \sigma_a$) and the anisotropic phase function $P(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ by normalizing the hair BCSDF $S(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ over the sphere $\Omega$ [S1]:
    $$ P(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \frac{S(\omega_i, \omega_o)}{\int_\Omega S(\omega_i, \omega') d\omega'} $$
    The albedo is precomputed as a function of the incident inclination angle $\theta_i$ relative to the local fiber direction [S1].

2.  **Rasterization Pass:**
    Accumulate per-pixel visibility and hair density in screen space. This provides the necessary density information for the subsequent volumetric shading pass [S1].

3.  **Shading Pass (First Geometric Interaction):**
    Trace camera rays. Evaluate the first interaction explicitly on the hair geometry using standard path tracing techniques. Compute direct illumination using an arbitrary hair BCSDF and standard next-event estimation. Ensure the same scattering model is used consistently for the volumetric representation [S1].

4.  **Transition to Medium:**
    Sample an outgoing direction $\omega_o$ from the hair BCSDF at the first geometric hit. Propagate the light path into the surrounding hair volume. Restrict subsequent geometric interactions to at most one (the initial hit); all further transport is handled in the equivalent participating medium [S1].

5.  **Single Scattering in Medium:**
    Within the equivalent medium, explicitly sample at most one volumetric scattering event at point $p$ using delta tracking [Woodcock et al. 1965] in the heterogeneous medium [S1].
    *   Sample a random light direction $\omega_i$.
    *   Estimate direct illumination by connecting $p$ to the light source.
    *   Evaluate transmittance along the shadow ray using ray marching through the medium:
        $$ T = \exp \left( -\int_0^t \sigma_t(x(s), \omega_i) ds \right) $$
    *   This yields the single-scattering estimate $L_{single}$ [S1].

6.  **Multiple Scattering Approximation:**
    Approximate higher-order scattering by reweighting the single-scattering estimate. The total volumetric contribution $L$ is a sum of scaled single-scattering terms over $N$ octaves [S1]:
    $$ L = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} L_i $$
    where each term $L_i$ is defined as:
    $$ L_i = \sigma_s(p, \omega_i) (\gamma \cdot \text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i))^i L_{light}(\omega_i) P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) \exp \left( -a_i \int_0^t \sigma_t(x(s), \omega_i) ds \right) $$
    *   $L_{light}(\omega_i)$: Incident radiance from the light source.
    *   $\text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i)$: Local RGB albedo at point $p$ for direction $\omega_i$. Using spectral albedo (rather than a scalar) preserves characteristic color shifts [S1].
    *   $\gamma$: Gain parameter to prevent excessive saturation from repeated albedo application [S1].
    *   $P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o)$: Modified phase function for octave $i$. Since the hair phase function is not parameterized by a single eccentricity, it is defined as a linear interpolation between the original phase function $P$ and an isotropic phase function $P_{iso}$:
        $$ P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \text{lerp}(P(\omega_i, \omega_o), P_{iso}, 1 - c^i) $$
        Here, $c \in [0, 1]$ controls the rate of isotropization [S1].
    *   $a_i$: Attenuation scalar for octave $i$ (typically $a_i = a \cdot i$ or similar, allowing deeper penetration for higher orders) [S1].

    The summation is evaluated stochastically by sampling a single octave $i$ according to its relative contribution, rather than deterministically accumulating all terms [S1].

7.  **Compositing:**
    Composite the direct illumination from Step 3 and the volumetric multiple scattering estimate from Step 6 to produce the final pixel color [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def render_hair_fur(camera_ray, scene):
    # 1. First Geometric Interaction
    hit = trace_geometry(camera_ray, scene.hair_strands)
    if hit is None:
        return background_color

    # Compute direct illumination on geometry
    L_direct = compute_direct_illumination(hit.point, hit.normal, hit.tangent, scene.lights)

    # 2. Transition to Medium
    # Sample outgoing direction from hair BCSDF
    omega_o = sample_bcsdf(hit.point, hit.tangent, hit.normal, scene.hair_model)
    
    # 3. Single Scattering in Equivalent Medium
    # Sample scattering point p using delta tracking in heterogeneous medium
    p = delta_track(scene.medium, hit.point, omega_o)
    
    # Sample light direction
    omega_i, L_light = sample_light(scene.lights)
    
    # Evaluate transmittance from p to light
    transmittance = ray_march_transmittance(scene.medium, p, omega_i)
    
    # Get local properties at p
    sigma_s = scene.medium.get_sigma_s(p)
    sigma_t = scene.medium.get_sigma_t(p)
    albedo = scene.medium.get_albedo(p, omega_i) # RGB albedo
    phase_func = scene.medium.get_phase_function(p, omega_i, omega_o)
    
    # 4. Multiple Scattering Approximation
    # Stochastically sample one octave
    octave = sample_octave(scene.num_octaves, sigma_s, albedo, phase_func, transmittance, L_light)
    
    # Compute contribution for the sampled octave
    # Modified phase function: lerp(original, isotropic, 1 - c^octave)
    c = scene.params.c
    gamma = scene.params.gamma
    a = scene.params.a
    
    # Isotropic phase function
    P_iso = 1.0 / (4.0 * pi)
    
    # Interpolate phase function
    weight = 1.0 - (c ** octave)
    P_prime = lerp(phase_func, P_iso, weight)
    
    # Attenuation factor for this octave
    # Note: a_i is typically scaled by octave index for deeper penetration
    a_i = a * (octave + 1) 
    attenuation = exp(-a_i * integral_sigma_t(scene.medium, p, omega_i))
    
    # Albedo raised to octave power, modulated by gamma
    albedo_term = (gamma * albedo) ** octave
    
    L_volumetric = sigma_s * albedo_term * L_light * P_prime * attenuation
    
    # 5. Composite
    return L_direct + L_volumetric
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Spectral Phase Function:** The derived phase function is spectrally varying. Normally, this requires separate sampling per color channel. This method avoids this pitfall by evaluating the phase function only for a deterministic outgoing direction during single-scattering estimation, not for sampling [S1].
*   **Color Saturation:** Using a scalar contribution factor instead of spectral albedo leads to severe color loss. Using spectral albedo can lead to excessive saturation; the parameter $\gamma$ mitigates this [S1].
*   **Tightly Coiled Hair:** The method struggles to reproduce localized bright spots from intense multiple backscattering in highly scattering, tightly coiled hair (e.g., white Afro). It approximates these as macroscopic energy distribution, missing concentrated highlights [S1].
*   **Local Similarity Assumption:** Unlike Dual Scattering, this method does not assume local similarity, preventing overly darkened appearances in tightly curly hairstyles where fiber orientations change rapidly [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Significantly faster than path tracing. Achieves a 7.1× to 11.06× speedup compared to ground truth path tracing at the same noise level [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the voxelized medium grid (density, direction, coefficients) and precomputed albedo maps.
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   On human hair: 1.17 minutes average render time vs. 9.98 minutes for path tracing (8.53× speedup) and 2.27 minutes for Dual Scattering (1.94× speedup) [S1].
    *   On fur scenes: 1.17 minutes vs. 4.49 minutes for Extended Dual Scattering (3.84× speedup) [S1].
    *   Requires 256 SPP for equal visual quality, whereas path tracing and Dual Scattering require 512 SPP [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency details via explicit geometry; captures soft volumetric illumination; robust under various lighting conditions; avoids the "dry" appearance of dual scattering.
*   **Limitations:** Cannot reproduce localized bright spots from intense backscattering in very dense, coiled hair; relies on precomputed parameters optimized for specific lighting setups [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Production rendering of dense hair and fur where performance is critical and high-frequency direct lighting is important.
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes requiring accurate reproduction of intense, localized backscattering highlights in tightly coiled hair; scenes with highly dynamic fiber orientations that violate the voxelization assumptions (though less sensitive than dual scattering) [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed by Hu, Zhu, Lin, and Zhang, published in ACM Transactions on Graphics (July 2026) [S1]. It builds on the observation that dense hair behaves like an anisotropic participating medium at the scale of multiple scattering. It improves upon Dual Scattering [Zinke et al. 2008] and Extended Dual Scattering [Yan et al. 2017b], which often fail for dense hair due to local similarity assumptions. It also differs from microflake models (SGGX, SpongeCake) by preserving transmission paths through normalization of the BCSDF [S1]. The implementation is available in Mitsuba 0.6 [S1].
