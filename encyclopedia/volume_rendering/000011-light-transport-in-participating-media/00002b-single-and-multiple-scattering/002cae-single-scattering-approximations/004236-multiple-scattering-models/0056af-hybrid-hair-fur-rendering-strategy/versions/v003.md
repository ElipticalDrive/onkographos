# Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering Strategy

Also called: Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering with Volumetric Multiple Scattering Approximation, Hybrid Rendering Strategy for Hair/Fur, Hybrid light transport estimator, Volumetric Approximation for Hair/Fur Multiple Scattering

## Overview
This method approximates multiple scattering in dense hair and fur by treating the aggregate fiber volume as an anisotropic participating medium, while retaining explicit geometric ray tracing for direct illumination to preserve high-frequency strand details [S1]. It differs from siblings like [Heterogeneous In-scattering with Multiple Scattering](4934-heterogeneous-in-scattering-with-multiple-scattering.md) and [Riley et al. Angular Distribution Renderer](12249-riley-et-al-angular-distribution-renderer.md) by restricting geometric interactions to the first hit and handling all subsequent transport via a voxelized medium, thereby avoiding the "dry" appearance and local similarity assumptions that plague dual scattering models [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Hair/fur geometry (explicit strands).
*   Hair BCSDF model (e.g., Yan et al. [2017a] double-cylinder model) [S1].
*   Lighting configuration (area lights, environment maps, etc.) [S1].
*   Parameters: $N$ (number of octaves), $a$ (attenuation scalar), $\gamma$ (gain scalar, $[0, 2]$), $c$ (isotropy rate, $[0, 1]$) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Rendered image with direct illumination and approximated multiple scattering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Voxel Grid:** Stores local fiber density, average fiber direction, and precomputed volumetric coefficients ($\sigma_t, \sigma_s, \sigma_a$) and phase function $P(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ for each voxel. The coefficients are derived from the hair BCSDF and fiber distribution [S1].
2.  **Albedo Map:** A 1D function of incident inclination angle $\theta_i$ relative to local fiber direction, precomputed from the hair BCSDF [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Voxelization (Precomputation):**
    *   Rasterize hair geometry to accumulate per-pixel visibility and hair density in screen space [S1].
    *   Compute local fiber densities and average orientations to define the equivalent anisotropic participating medium with directionally varying extinction, absorption, and scattering coefficients [S1].

2.  **First Geometric Interaction (Direct Illumination):**
    *   Trace camera rays and evaluate the first intersection with explicit hair geometry [S1].
    *   Compute direct illumination using the hair BCSDF and standard next-event estimation [S1].
    *   Sample an outgoing direction from the hair BCSDF [S1].

3.  **Transition to Volumetric Domain:**
    *   Propagate the light path from the first geometric hit into the surrounding hair volume [S1].
    *   Restrict all subsequent light transport to the equivalent participating medium; no further geometric interactions are allowed [S1].

4.  **Single Scattering Estimation in Medium:**
    *   Sample a scattering point $p$ along the view ray within the equivalent medium using delta tracking [S1].
    *   Select a random light source and march along the light direction to evaluate transmittance and direct illumination at $p$ [S1].
    *   Evaluate the transmittance using ray marching through the heterogeneous medium [S1].
    *   This yields a single-scattering estimate $L_{ss}$ [S1].

5.  **Multiple Scattering Approximation (Spectral Reweighting):**
    *   Approximate higher-order scattering by reweighting the single-scattering estimate using a sum of scaled terms over $N$ octaves [S1].
    *   The total volumetric contribution $L$ is calculated as:
        $$ L = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} L_i $$
        where each octave term $L_i$ is:
        $$ L_i = \sigma_s(p, \omega_i) (\gamma \cdot \text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i))^i L_{\text{light}}(\omega_i) P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) \exp\left(-a_i \int_0^t \sigma_t(x(s), \omega_i) ds\right) $$
        *   $p$: Volumetric scattering location.
        *   $\omega_i$: Incident direction from light.
        *   $\omega_o$: Outgoing direction to camera.
        *   $L_{\text{light}}(\omega_i)$: Incident radiance from the light source.
        *   $\text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i)$: Local RGB albedo from the precomputed 1D function [S1].
        *   $\gamma$: Scalar gain parameter ($[0, 2]$) to moderate color saturation [S1].
        *   $P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o)$: Modified phase function for octave $i$.
        *   $a_i$: Attenuation scalar for octave $i$ (often $a_i = a \cdot i$ or similar, allowing deeper penetration for higher orders) [S1].
        *   $\sigma_s, \sigma_t$: Scattering and extinction coefficients at $p$ [S1].
    *   The modified phase function $P'_i$ is defined as a linear interpolation between the original hair phase function $P$ and an isotropic phase function $P_{\text{iso}}$:
        $$ P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \text{lerp}(P(\omega_i, \omega_o), P_{\text{iso}}, 1 - c^i) $$
        *   $c \in [0, 1]$: Controls the rate at which higher-order scattering becomes isotropic [S1].
    *   **Stochastic Evaluation:** To reduce variance, sample a single octave $i$ according to its relative contribution rather than summing all terms deterministically [S1].

6.  **Compositing:**
    *   Combine the direct illumination from Step 2 and the approximated multiple scattering from Step 5 to produce the final pixel color [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def render_hybrid_hair(camera_ray, hair_geometry, voxel_grid, lights, params):
    """
    Renders a single pixel using the hybrid hair/fur strategy.
    
    Args:
        camera_ray: Ray from camera origin with direction.
        hair_geometry: Explicit hair strand data for rasterization/intersection.
        voxel_grid: Voxelized medium data (density, direction, coeffs).
        lights: List of light sources.
        params: Dict containing N, a, gamma, c.
        
    Returns:
        Color (RGB) for the pixel.
    """
    # Step 1: First Geometric Interaction
    hit = hair_geometry.intersect(camera_ray)
    if hit is None:
        return background_color
    
    # Compute direct illumination using hair BCSDF
    # Assume BCSDF provides direct lighting contribution
    direct_color = compute_direct_illumination(hit, hair_geometry.bcsdf, lights)
    
    # Sample outgoing direction from BCSDF for volumetric transport
    # Note: In practice, this direction might be determined by the view ray 
    # or sampled based on the BCSDF lobe. The method propagates into the volume.
    outgoing_dir = hit.normal # Simplified: assuming transport starts from hit point
    # More accurately, the path continues from the hit point into the volume.
    # The "view ray" for the volumetric pass is effectively the ray from the hit point 
    # towards the camera (or the original camera ray if we consider the hit point as the start).
    
    # Step 2: Transition to Medium
    # We are now at 'hit.point'. We need to sample a scattering point in the volume.
    # The method samples a point along the view ray using delta tracking.
    # Here, 'view ray' refers to the ray from the hit point back to the camera 
    # or the original camera ray if the hit is on the front surface.
    # Let's assume we sample along the original camera ray segment inside the volume.
    
    # Delta tracking to find scattering point p
    p = delta_track_sample(camera_ray, voxel_grid)
    
    if p is None:
        return direct_color # No volumetric scattering found
    
    # Step 3: Single Scattering Estimation
    # Select a random light source
    light = random.choice(lights)
    
    # March along light direction to evaluate transmittance
    # Shadow ray from p to light
    shadow_ray = Ray(origin=p, direction=light.direction)
    transmittance = ray_march_transmittance(shadow_ray, voxel_grid)
    
    # Evaluate direct illumination at p from light
    # L_light is the radiance from the light source
    L_light = light.radiance
    
    # Get local properties at p
    sigma_s = voxel_grid.get_scattering_coefficient(p, light.direction)
    albedo = voxel_grid.get_albedo(p, light.direction) # 1D function lookup
    
    # Get original phase function P
    P = voxel_grid.get_phase_function(p, light.direction, camera_ray.direction)
    
    # Step 4: Multiple Scattering Approximation (Stochastic Octave Sampling)
    # Sample a single octave i based on relative contribution
    # Relative contribution depends on albedo^i and attenuation
    # We approximate weights for sampling
    weights = []
    for i in range(params['N']):
        # Calculate weight for octave i
        # Weight ~ (gamma * albedo)^i * exp(-a_i * optical_depth)
        # Note: a_i is not explicitly defined as a function of i in the text, 
        # but 'a' controls effective attenuation. We assume a_i scales with i.
        # Let a_i = a * (i + 1) or similar. The text says 'a' controls attenuation 
        # allowing deeper penetration for higher orders.
        # We will use a_i = a * (i + 1) as a reasonable implementation detail [G].
        a_i = params['a'] * (i + 1)
        
        # Optical depth along shadow ray
        # We already have transmittance = exp(-optical_depth)
        # So exp(-a_i * optical_depth) = transmittance ^ a_i
        octave_weight = (params['gamma'] * albedo) ** i * (transmittance ** a_i)
        weights.append(octave_weight)
        
    # Normalize weights and sample octave
    total_weight = sum(weights)
    if total_weight == 0:
        return direct_color
        
    # Sample octave i
    i = sample_stochastic_index(weights)
    
    # Calculate L_i
    # Modified phase function P'_i
    # P_iso is isotropic: 1 / (4 * pi)
    P_iso = 1.0 / (4.0 * math.pi)
    
    # lerp(P, P_iso, 1 - c^i)
    lerp_factor = 1.0 - (params['c'] ** i)
    P_prime = lerp(P, P_iso, lerp_factor)
    
    L_i = sigma_s * (params['gamma'] * albedo) ** i * L_light * P_prime * (transmittance ** (params['a'] * (i + 1)))
    
    # Step 5: Composite
    final_color = direct_color + L_i
    
    return final_color

def delta_track_sample(ray, voxel_grid):
    """
    Samples a scattering point along the ray using delta tracking.
    Returns the point p or None if no scattering occurs.
    """
    # Standard delta tracking algorithm
    # 1. Find max sigma_t along ray
    sigma_t_max = voxel_grid.get_max_sigma_t(ray)
    
    # 2. Sample distance
    # u ~ Uniform(0, 1)
    u = random.uniform(0, 1)
    distance = -math.log(1 - u) / sigma_t_max
    
    # 3. Sample point
    p = ray.origin + ray.direction * distance
    
    # 4. Check if scattering occurs
    # Probability of scattering is sigma_s / sigma_t
    # We need to evaluate sigma_t and sigma_s at p
    sigma_t_p = voxel_grid.get_extinction_coefficient(p)
    sigma_s_p = voxel_grid.get_scattering_coefficient(p, ray.direction)
    
    if sigma_t_p == 0:
        return None
        
    v = random.uniform(0, 1)
    if v < sigma_s_p / sigma_t_p:
        return p
    else:
        # No scattering, continue? 
        # The method says "explicitly sample at most one volumetric scattering event".
        # If delta tracking rejects, we might return None or continue.
        # Given "at most one", if the first sample is rejected, we stop.
        return None

def ray_march_transmittance(ray, voxel_grid):
    """
    Evaluates transmittance along the ray by ray marching.
    Returns exp(-integral(sigma_t ds)).
    """
    # Simple ray march
    t = 0.0
    dt = 0.01 # Step size
    sigma_t_sum = 0.0
    
    while t < ray.length:
        p = ray.origin + ray.direction * t
        sigma_t = voxel_grid.get_extinction_coefficient(p)
        sigma_t_sum += sigma_t * dt
        t += dt
        
    return math.exp(-sigma_t_sum)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spectral Phase Function:** The derived phase function is spectrally varying. However, the method avoids per-channel sampling by evaluating the phase function only for a deterministic outgoing direction (the view direction) during single-scattering estimation [S1].
*   **Color Saturation:** Using a scalar contribution factor instead of local RGB albedo leads to severe color loss. The method uses local RGB albedo to preserve spectral energy redistribution [S1].
*   **Excessive Saturation:** Repeated application of the albedo term can cause excessive color saturation. The scalar gain parameter $\gamma$ is introduced to moderate this [S1].
*   **Tightly Coiled Hair:** The method struggles to reproduce localized bright spots from intense multiple backscattering in highly scattering, tightly coiled hair (e.g., white Afro). It approximates these as macroscopic energy distribution instead [S1].
*   **Local Similarity Assumption:** Unlike Dual Scattering, this method avoids the local similarity assumption, preventing overly darkened appearances in tightly curly hairstyles where fiber orientations change rapidly [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Significantly faster than path tracing. Achieves a 7.1× to 11.06× speedup compared to ground truth path tracing at the same noise level [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the voxel grid (density, direction, coefficients) and precomputed albedo maps. Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   On human hair: 1.17 minutes average render time vs. 9.98 minutes for path tracing (8.53× speedup) and 2.27 minutes for Dual Scattering (1.94× speedup) [S1].
    *   On fur scenes: Average speedup of 3.84× compared to Extended Dual Scattering (1.17 minutes vs. 4.49 minutes) [S1].
    *   Samples per pixel (SPP): Requires 256 SPP for equal visual quality, whereas path tracing and Dual Scattering require 512 SPP [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency details via explicit geometry; captures soft volumetric illumination; robust under various lighting conditions; avoids "dry" appearance of dual scattering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot reproduce localized bright spots from intense multiple backscattering in tightly coiled hair; relies on precomputed parameters optimized for specific lighting setups [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Production rendering of dense hair and fur where performance is critical and high-frequency detail is needed [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes requiring accurate reproduction of intense localized backscattering highlights in tightly coiled hair [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed by Hu, Zhu, Lin, and Zhang in 2026 [S1].
*   **History:** Builds on the insight that dense hair behaves like an anisotropic participating medium at the scale of multiple scattering [S1]. It improves upon prior approximations like Dual Scattering [Zinke et al. 2008] and Extended Dual Scattering [Yan et al. 2017b], which often fail for dense hair by yielding overly dry or blurred appearances [S1]. It also differs from microflake models (e.g., SGGX) which assume opaque particles [S1].
*   **Implementation:** Implemented in Mitsuba 0.6 [S1]. Parameters were obtained by numerical optimization using Ceres Solver with an L1 loss on a scene with only hair geometry under a studio-style configuration of three area lights [S1].
