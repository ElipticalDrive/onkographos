# Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering Strategy

Also called: Hybrid Hair/Fur Rendering with Volumetric Multiple Scattering Approximation, Hybrid Rendering Strategy for Hair/Fur, Hybrid light transport estimator, Volumetric Approximation for Hair/Fur Multiple Scattering

## Overview
This method approximates multiple scattering in dense hair and fur by combining explicit geometric ray tracing for direct illumination with a volumetric participating medium model for higher-order scattering [S1]. It solves the problem of expensive path tracing in fibrous media by treating the aggregate fiber volume as an anisotropic participating medium, allowing efficient estimation of soft, multi-bounce light transport while preserving high-frequency details on individual strands [S1]. Unlike microflake models that assume opaque particles, this approach normalizes the hair BCSDF to define the phase function, preserving complex transmission paths [S1].

## How it works
The method operates in two main phases: precomputation of volumetric properties from geometry, and a hybrid rendering pass that transitions from geometric to volumetric light transport.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Explicit hair/fur geometry (strands), hair scattering model (BCSDF), lighting environment, camera parameters.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered image with direct and multiple-scattered illumination.

### Data Structures
1.  **Volumetric Medium Grid:** A 3D grid (voxelization) of the hair volume.
    *   **Fields:**
        *   `density`: Local fiber density.
        *   `fiber_direction`: Average fiber orientation vector per voxel.
        *   `sigma_t`: Extinction coefficient (derived from density and fiber properties).
        *   `sigma_s`: Scattering coefficient.
        *   `sigma_a`: Absorption coefficient ($\sigma_a = \sigma_t - \sigma_s$).
        *   `phase_function`: Anisotropic phase function $P(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ derived from the normalized BCSDF.
        *   `albedo`: Spectral albedo $\omega_0 = \sigma_s / \sigma_t$, precomputed as a function of incident inclination angle $\theta_i$ relative to the local fiber direction [S1].
2.  **Hair BCSDF:** The base single-fiber reflectance model, specifically the double-cylinder fur model by Yan et al. [2017a] accounting for cuticle, cortex, and medulla layers [S1].

### Parameters
*   $N$: Number of octaves in the multiple scattering summation.
*   $a$: Scalar controlling effective attenuation, allowing deeper light penetration for higher-order terms.
*   $\gamma$: Scalar gain parameter in $[0, 2]$ to moderate excessive color saturation [S1].
*   $c$: Parameter in $[0, 1]$ controlling the rate at which higher-order scattering becomes isotropic [S1].
*   Typical values are obtained via numerical optimization using Ceres Solver with L1 loss on a studio lighting setup [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Precomputation (Voxelization):**
    *   Rasterize hair geometry to accumulate per-pixel visibility and hair density in screen space [S1].
    *   Compute local fiber densities and average orientations in 3D space to yield the equivalent volumetric medium with directionally varying coefficients [S1].

2.  **First Geometric Interaction:**
    *   Trace camera rays to find the first intersection with explicit hair geometry [S1].
    *   Compute direct illumination at this intersection using an arbitrary hair BCSDF and standard next-event estimation [S1].
    *   Ensure the same scattering model is used consistently for the subsequent volumetric representation [S1].

3.  **Transition to Medium:**
    *   Sample an outgoing direction from the hair BCSDF at the intersection point [S1].
    *   Propagate the light path into the surrounding hair volume.
    *   Restrict further geometric interactions to at most one (the initial hit); all subsequent transport is handled in the equivalent medium [S1].

4.  **Single Scattering in Medium:**
    *   Sample a scattering point $p$ along the view ray within the equivalent medium using delta tracking [S1].
    *   Select a random light source direction $\omega_i$ [S1].
    *   Evaluate transmittance from $p$ to the light source by ray marching through the heterogeneous medium [S1].
    *   Compute the direct illumination contribution at $p$ from the light source [S1].

5.  **Multiple Scattering Approximation:**
    *   Reweight the single-scattering estimate to approximate higher-order scattering using a sum of scaled single-scattering terms over $N$ octaves [S1].
    *   The total volumetric contribution $L$ is estimated by stochastically sampling a single octave $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$ according to its relative contribution [S1].
    *   The contribution for octave $i$ is given by:
        $$ L_i = \sigma_s(p, \omega_i) (\gamma \cdot \text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i))^i L_{\text{light}}(\omega_i) P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) \exp\left(-a_i \int_0^t \sigma_t(x(s), \omega_i) ds\right) $$
        where:
        *   $p$ is the volumetric scattering location.
        *   $\omega_i$ is the incident direction.
        *   $\text{Albedo}(p, \omega_i)$ is the local RGB albedo.
        *   $L_{\text{light}}(\omega_i)$ is the incident radiance from the light.
        *   $P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ is the modified phase function.
        *   The integral represents the optical depth along the path to the light.
    *   The modified phase function $P'_i$ is defined as a linear interpolation between the original hair phase function $P$ and an isotropic phase function $P_{\text{iso}}$:
        $$ P'_i(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \text{lerp}(P(\omega_i, \omega_o), P_{\text{iso}}, 1 - c^i) $$
        where $c \in [0, 1]$ controls the isotropization rate [S1].

6.  **Compositing:**
    *   Composite the direct geometric contribution and the approximated multiple-scattering volumetric contribution to produce the final pixel color [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def render_hair_fur(camera_ray, scene, medium_grid, bcsdf, params):
    """
    Renders a single pixel using the hybrid hair/fur strategy.
    
    Args:
        camera_ray: Ray from camera origin through pixel.
        scene: Contains hair geometry and light sources.
        medium_grid: Voxelized hair volume with density, sigma_t, sigma_s, phase function.
        bcsdf: Hair/BCSDF model (e.g., Yan et al. 2017a).
        params: (N, a, gamma, c) scattering approximation parameters.
        
    Returns:
        Color (RGB) for the pixel.
    """
    
    # Step 1: First Geometric Interaction
    hit = trace_geometry(camera_ray, scene.hair_geometry)
    
    if hit is None:
        return scene.background_color
        
    # Compute direct illumination at first hit
    direct_color = compute_direct_illumination(hit, scene.lights, bcsdf)
    
    # Step 2: Transition to Medium
    # Sample outgoing direction from BCSDF
    omega_o = sample_bcsdf_outgoing(hit.normal, hit.tangent, bcsdf)
    
    # Step 3: Single Scattering in Medium
    # Sample scattering point p in the medium using delta tracking
    p = delta_track_sample(camera_ray, medium_grid)
    
    if p is None:
        return direct_color
        
    # Select random light source
    light_source = random_light_source(scene.lights)
    omega_i = light_source.direction_to(p)
    
    # Evaluate transmittance to light
    transmittance = ray_march_transmittance(p, omega_i, medium_grid)
    
    # Get incident radiance from light
    L_light = light_source.radiance(omega_i)
    
    # Get medium properties at p
    sigma_s = medium_grid.get_sigma_s(p)
    albedo = medium_grid.get_albedo(p, omega_i)
    phase_func = medium_grid.get_phase_function(p, omega_i, omega_o)
    
    # Step 4: Multiple Scattering Approximation
    # Stochastically sample one octave i from 0 to N-1
    N = params.N
    i = sample_octave(N)
    
    # Calculate octave-specific parameters
    # Note: 'a' in formula is a_i, but source uses 'a' as scalar controlling attenuation.
    # Assuming a_i = a * (some scaling) or just 'a' if constant. Source says "a controls...".
    # We use 'a' directly as per formula structure.
    a_i = params.a 
    
    gamma = params.gamma
    c = params.c
    
    # Modified phase function
    # P_iso is isotropic phase function (1 / 4pi)
    P_iso = 1.0 / (4.0 * PI)
    P_prime = lerp(phase_func, P_iso, 1.0 - (c ** i))
    
    # Albedo term raised to power i, scaled by gamma
    albedo_term = (gamma * albedo) ** i
    
    # Optical depth integral (approximated by ray march)
    # The formula has exp(-a_i * integral). 
    # We already computed transmittance = exp(-integral).
    # So we need exp(-a_i * optical_depth).
    # If transmittance = exp(-optical_depth), then exp(-a_i * optical_depth) = transmittance^(a_i)
    spectral_transmittance = transmittance ** a_i
    
    # Compute L_i
    L_i = sigma_s * albedo_term * L_light * P_prime * spectral_transmittance
    
    # Step 5: Composite
    final_color = direct_color + L_i
    
    return final_color

def sample_octave(N):
    """
    Stochastically sample an octave i from 0 to N-1.
    In practice, this requires computing the relative contribution of each octave
    to form a probability distribution. For simplicity, if uniform sampling is used
    (as implied by 'stochastically by sampling a single octave according to its 
    relative contribution' but no distribution given), we assume a weighted sample.
    Since the exact weights are not provided in the notes, we assume a uniform 
    sample for the placeholder, but note that proper implementation requires 
    computing weights w_i = ||L_i|| and sampling i ~ w_i / sum(w).
    """
    # Placeholder for weighted sampling. 
    # The source states: "evaluated stochastically by sampling a single octave 
    # according to its relative contribution."
    # Without the exact weight formula, we cannot implement the weighting.
    # We return a random integer for structure.
    return random.randint(0, N - 1)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Spectral Phase Function:** The derived phase function is spectrally varying. Normally this requires per-channel sampling, but this method avoids it by evaluating the phase function only for a deterministic outgoing direction during single-scattering estimation [S1].
*   **Color Saturation:** Using a scalar contribution factor instead of spectral albedo leads to severe color loss. Repeated application of the albedo term can cause excessive saturation, mitigated by the gain parameter $\gamma$ [S1].
*   **Tightly Coiled Hair:** The method struggles to reproduce localized bright spots from intense multiple backscattering in highly scattering, tightly coiled hair (e.g., white Afro), approximating them as macroscopic energy distribution instead [S1].
*   **Flyaway Strands:** Unlike voxel-based radiance accumulation methods (e.g., Moon et al. [2008]), this method does not underestimate multiple scattering for lifted flyaway strands, as it uses explicit geometry for the first hit [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** Achieves a speedup of 7.1× to 11.06× compared to ground truth path tracing at the same noise level [S1]. It is 2.7× to 3.26× faster than Extended Dual Scattering [S1].
*   **Timing:** On human hair, it renders an image in 1.17 minutes on average, compared to 9.98 minutes for path tracing (8.53× speedup) and 2.27 minutes for Dual Scattering (1.94× speedup) [S1]. On fur scenes, it achieves an average speedup of 3.84× (1.17 minutes vs. 4.49 minutes) compared to Extended Dual Scattering [S1].
*   **Samples:** Requires 256 SPP for equal visual quality, whereas path tracing and Dual Scattering require 512 SPP [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency details via explicit geometry; captures soft volumetric illumination; robust under various lighting conditions; avoids the 'dry' appearance of dual scattering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot reproduce localized bright spots from intense backscattering in tightly coiled hair; relies on precomputed volumetric properties which may be memory-intensive for very large scenes [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed by Hu, Zhu, Lin, and Zhang, and published in ACM Transactions on Graphics (SIGGRAPH) in July 2026 [S1]. It builds on the insight that dense hair fibers behave like an anisotropic participating medium at the scale of multiple scattering [S1]. It improves upon prior approximations like Dual Scattering [Zinke et al. 2008] and Extended Dual Scattering [Yan et al. 2017b], which often fail for dense hair by yielding overly dry or blurred appearances [S1]. The implementation was done in Mitsuba 0.6 [S1].
