# Most Probable Paths (MPP) Lighting Model

Also called: General Participating Media Lighting Model, MPP model, MPP-based lighting model, Most Probable Path (MPP) Multiple Scattering Model, Multiple Scattering approximation using MPP, Premože et al. 2004 model

## Overview
The Most Probable Paths (MPP) model approximates multiple scattering in general participating media by summing contributions only from paths close to the "Most Probable Path" (MPP), assuming strong forward scattering [S1]. It splits radiance into object, single-scattered, and multiple-scattered components, using MPPs to approximate scattering integrals [S1]. Unlike diffusion-based methods that fail near boundaries or in low-density regions, this model handles inhomogeneous media and avoids artifacts by determining the effective scattering angle locally based on the phase function and scattering history [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume density field $\rho(x)$ or scattering coefficient $b(x)$.
*   Absorption coefficient $a(x)$.
*   Extinction coefficient $c(x) = a(x) + b(x)$.
*   Phase function $P(\theta)$, assumed strongly forward-scattering with mean square scattering angle $\langle \theta^2 \rangle$.
*   Directional light source intensity $L_0$ and direction $\vec{\omega}_0$.
*   Camera ray origin and direction $\vec{\omega}$.
*   Object image $L_{obj}$ (rendered without media effects, only shadows).

**Outputs:**
*   Radiance $L(x, \vec{\omega})$ at sample point $x$ along the viewing ray.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Light Volume:** A 3D data structure storing $\ell_{light}$ (scattering events from point to light) for every volume cell. The z-axis is aligned with the light direction $\vec{\omega}_0$. This acts as a deep shadow map [S1].
2.  **Object Image:** 2D texture containing $L_{obj}(\vec{\omega})$, the unfiltered object radiance [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\ell$: Number of scattering events along a path.
*   $s$: Physical length of the path segment.
*   $\gamma$: Rate adjustment parameter for multiple scattering attenuation. $\gamma=1$ for the original model; $\gamma=0.45$ provides better fits to Monte Carlo simulations for denser media [S1].
*   $\bar{b}$: Global average of the scattering coefficient, used for area estimation in blurred radiance calculation [S1].

**Formulas:**
1.  **Scattering Events:** The number of scattering events $\ell$ along a path is:
    $$ \ell = \int_{path} b(x) ds $$
    Integrals for other coefficients are derived by multiplying $\ell$ by the ratio of coefficients (e.g., $\int c(x) ds = (c/b)\ell$) [S1].

2.  **Blurring Width:** The standard deviation $w$ of the Gaussian weighting incident radiance is:
    $$ w(\ell, s)^2 = \frac{\langle \theta^2 \rangle \ell s^2}{24} \left(1 + \frac{\langle \theta^2 \rangle (a/b) \ell^2}{12}\right) $$
    where $\langle \theta^2 \rangle$ is the mean square scattering angle, $s$ is physical length, and $a/b$ is the absorption-to-scattering ratio [S1].

3.  **Multiple Scattering Phase Function:**
    $$ P_{MS}(\theta, \ell) = N \cdot P\left(\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{\ell(1 - \exp(-\ell))}}\right) $$
    where $N$ is a normalization constant [S1].

4.  **Blurred Incident Radiance:**
    $$ L_{blurred}(x_0, w) = \frac{\bar{b}}{\pi \langle \theta^2 \rangle} \int L_0(x) \exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{2w^2}\right) dA $$
    Note: The factor $\frac{\bar{b}}{\pi \langle \theta^2 \rangle}$ accounts for the effective area per path to ensure correct dimensions (radiance) [S1].

5.  **Object Image Weight:**
    $$ W = \left(1 + \ell_{obj} + \frac{2\bar{b}^2 w^2 \ell_{obj}}{\langle \theta^2 \rangle}\right) \exp\left(-c\ell_{obj}/b\right) $$
    where $\ell_{obj}$ is the scattering events from the object to the eye [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Preprocessing (Light Volume):**
    For every voxel in the volume, compute $\ell_{light}$ by integrating the scattering coefficient $b(x)$ along a straight line from the voxel center towards the light source direction $\vec{\omega}_0$ until it exits the volume or hits an object. Store this in the Light Volume [S1].

2.  **Ray Marching (Single Sweep):**
    For each pixel, cast a ray and sample the volume. Maintain accumulators for single-scattered radiance $L_{SS}$ and multiple-scattered radiance $L_{MS}$. Initialize $L_{SS} = 0, L_{MS} = 0$.

3.  **Single Scattering Contribution:**
    At sample point $x$ along segment $\Delta x$:
    *   Retrieve $\ell_{eye}$ (scattering events from $x$ to eye) and $\ell_{light}$ (from $x$ to light) from the Light Volume.
    *   Compute unscattered light at $x$: $L_{unscat}(x, \vec{\omega}_0) = L_0 \exp(-(c/b)\ell_{light})$.
    *   Update $L_{SS}$:
        $$ \Delta L_{SS} = L_{unscat}(x, \vec{\omega}_0) P(x, \vec{\omega}, \vec{\omega}_0) \exp\left(-\frac{c}{b}\ell_{eye}\right) b(x) \Delta x $$
    *   Accumulate: $L_{SS} \leftarrow L_{SS} + \Delta L_{SS}$ [S1].

4.  **Multiple Scattering Contribution:**
    *   Compute total scattering events $\ell = \ell_{eye} + \ell_{light}$.
    *   Compute physical length $s$ of the path (approximated as distance from eye to $x$ plus distance from $x$ to light, or derived from $\ell$ and average density).
    *   Compute blurring width $w(\ell, s)$ using Equation 4.
    *   Compute blurred incident radiance $L_{blurred}$ using the global average $\bar{b}$ and the object image $L_{obj}$ sampled at the appropriate location corresponding to the light direction [S1].
    *   Update $L_{MS}$:
        $$ \Delta L_{MS} = L_{blurred}(x_0, w(\ell)) \cdot \ell \cdot \exp\left(-\gamma \frac{c}{b} \ell\right) $$
        Note: The term $\ell$ accounts for the number of contributing paths/area, and $\gamma$ adjusts attenuation [S1].
    *   Accumulate: $L_{MS} \leftarrow L_{MS} + \Delta L_{MS}$ [S1].

5.  **Final Radiance:**
    After marching through the volume, combine components:
    $$ L(x, \vec{\omega}) = W \cdot L_{obj}(x, \vec{\omega}) + L_{SS}(x, \vec{\omega}) + L_{MS}(x, \vec{\omega}) $$
    where $W$ is computed using $\ell_{obj}$ (total scattering events from object to eye) and the final $w$ value [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Backscattering:** The original model underestimates backscattering because it penalizes short paths with large angle changes via a curvature term. Dropping the curvature term corrects this [S1].
*   **Dimensional Consistency:** Integrating weighted radiance in the incident plane yields radiance $\times$ area. An effective area factor (derived from $\bar{b}$ and $\langle \theta^2 \rangle$) must be included to get correct radiance units [S1].
*   **Object Image Filtering:** Using the blurred object image for $L_{MS}$ is more accurate but computationally expensive. Using the unfiltered object image is a valid approximation that avoids runtime filtering penalties [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Time complexity is linear with respect to the number of volume samples per ray, as single and multiple scattering are computed in a single sweep [S1]. Preprocessing requires one pass per light direction to build the Light Volume [S1].
*   **Memory:** Requires storage for the volume density, the Light Volume (3D texture), and the object image. The Light Volume size depends on the volume resolution and light direction alignment [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles inhomogeneous media and general geometries without artifacts near boundaries or in low-density regions where diffusion approximations fail [S1]. Efficiently approximates multiple scattering without iterative solvers [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes strong forward scattering. Accuracy depends on the validity of the MPP approximation. The use of global averages ($\bar{b}$) for area estimation is a simplification that may introduce errors in highly heterogeneous media [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time or interactive rendering of participating media with significant multiple scattering, especially in heterogeneous environments.
*   **When not to use:** Media with isotropic or backward-scattering dominant phase functions, or where high physical accuracy of backscattering is critical without the curvature correction [S1].

## Background
The MPP model was introduced by Premože et al. in 2004 [S1]. It builds on pioneering work by Tessendorf (1987) and Premože et al. (2003), who introduced the concept of the Most Probable Path for strongly forward-scattering inhomogeneous media [S1]. The 2004 paper extended this to a general lighting model for participating media, addressing limitations of previous methods that assumed planar surfaces, uniform media, and optical thickness (diffusion approximation) [S1]. It was implemented in OpenGL using NVIDIA CG, demonstrating interactive rendering capabilities [S1].
