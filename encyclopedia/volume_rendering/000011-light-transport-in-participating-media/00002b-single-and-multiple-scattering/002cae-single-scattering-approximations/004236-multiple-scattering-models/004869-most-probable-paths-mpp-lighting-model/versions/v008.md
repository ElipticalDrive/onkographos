# Most Probable Paths (MPP) Lighting Model

Also called: General Participating Media Lighting Model, MPP model, MPP-based lighting model, Most Probable Path (MPP) Multiple Scattering Model, Multiple Scattering approximation using MPP, Premože et al. 2004 model

## Overview
The Most Probable Paths (MPP) model is an approximation for the multiple scattering term ($L_{MS}$) in participating media that simplifies the radiative transfer equation by summing contributions only over paths close to the Most Probable Path (MPP), assuming strong forward scattering [S1]. It splits radiance into object, single-scattered, and multiple-scattered components, using MPPs to approximate scattering integrals [S1]. Unlike previous methods that assume planar surfaces, uniform media, and optical thickness (leading to diffusion approximation), this model handles general inhomogeneous media and avoids artifacts near boundaries and low-density areas where diffusion is invalid [S1].

## How it works
The method approximates the generally curved MPPs by two straight line segments: $\vec{\ell}_{eye}$ (from eye to scattering point) and $\vec{\ell}_{light}$ (from scattering point to light) [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Medium coefficients: absorption $a(x)$, scattering $b(x)$, extinction $c(x) = a(x) + b(x)$ [S1].
*   Phase function $P(\theta)$ and mean square scattering angle $\langle \theta^2 \rangle$ [S1].
*   Light source direction $\vec{\omega}_0$ and intensity $L_0$ [S1].
*   Object image $L_{obj}$ (unfiltered) [S1].
*   Precomputed $\ell_{light}$ volume: a 3D data structure storing $\ell_{light}$ values for every volume cell, oriented with the z-axis aligned with the light direction [S1].
*   Global average scattering coefficient $\bar{b}$ [S1].
*   Rate adjustment parameter $\gamma$ (typically 1 or 0.45) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Total radiance $L(x, \vec{\omega})$ at each point $x$ and view direction $\vec{\omega}$ [S1].

### Data Structures
1.  **Light Volume:** A 3D grid storing $\ell_{light}$ for each cell. The grid is aligned such that the z-axis corresponds to the light direction [S1].
2.  **Object Image:** A 2D texture or buffer containing the scene rendered without participating media effects (except shadows) [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\ell$: Number of scattering events along a path.
*   $s$: Physical length of the path segment.
*   $w$: Blurring width (standard deviation of the Gaussian filter).
*   $\langle \theta^2 \rangle$: Mean square scattering angle.
*   $\gamma$: Rate adjustment parameter for multiple scattering attenuation. Value is 1 for the original model; 0.45 was found to give better fits to MC simulations for denser media [S1].

### Formulas and Definitions
The total radiance is the sum of the weighted object image, single-scattered light, and multiple-scattered light [S1]:
$$ L(x,\vec{\omega}) = W \cdot L_{obj}(x, \vec{\omega}) + L_{SS}(x, \vec{\omega}) + L_{MS}(x, \vec{\omega}) $$

**1. Number of Scattering Events**
The number of scattering events $\ell$ along a path is defined as [S1]:
$$ \ell = \int_{path} b(x) ds $$
Integrals for other coefficients along the same path are obtained by multiplying $\ell$ by the ratio of coefficients (e.g., $\int c(x) ds = (c/b)\ell$) [S1].

**2. Single-Scattered Radiance ($L_{SS}$)**
For a directional light source $\vec{\omega}_0$, the single-scattered radiance arriving at the eye from position $x$ along segment $\Delta x$ is [S1]:
$$ \Delta L_{SS}(x, \vec{\omega}) = L_{unscat}(x, \vec{\omega}_0) P(x, \vec{\omega}, \vec{\omega}_0) \exp\left(-\frac{c}{b}\ell_{eye}\right) b(x) \Delta x $$
where $L_{unscat}(x, \vec{\omega}_0) = L_0 \exp\left(-\frac{c}{b}\ell_{light}\right)$ is the unscattered light available at $x$ [S1].

**3. Multiple-Scattered Radiance ($L_{MS}$)**
The multiple scattering radiance is calculated by integrating over MPPs with different $\ell$ [S1]:
$$ L_{MS}(x, \vec{\omega}) = \int d\ell \, \exp\left(-\gamma \frac{c}{b}\ell(MPP_\ell)\right) \exp(-A_c(MPP_\ell)) \times P_{MS}(\ell, \vec{\omega} - \vec{\omega}_0) \times L_{blurred}(x_0, w(\ell)) $$
*Note: The original model includes a curvature attenuation term $A_c$. However, dropping this term corrects the underestimation of backscattering [S1]. The modified model uses $\gamma$ to adjust the attenuation rate [S1].*

**4. Blurred Incident Radiance ($L_{blurred}$)**
The blurred incident radiance is computed as [S1]:
$$ L_{blurred}(x_0, w) = \frac{\bar{b}}{\pi \langle \theta^2 \rangle} \int L_0(x) \exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{2w^2}\right) dA $$
where $L_0(x)$ is the incident radiance profile on the plane perpendicular to the light direction at the MPP location [S1].

**5. Gaussian Width ($w$)**
The width (standard deviation) $w$ of the Gaussian used to weight incident radiance is determined by the path's $\ell$, its physical length $s$, and media parameters [S1]:
$$ w(\ell, s)^2 = \frac{\langle \theta^2 \rangle \ell s^2}{24} \left(1 + \frac{\langle \theta^2 \rangle (a/b) \ell^2}{12}\right) $$
where $\langle \theta^2 \rangle$ is the mean square scattering angle, $s$ is physical length, and $a/b$ is the ratio of absorption to scattering coefficients [S1].

**6. Multiple Scattering Phase Function ($P_{MS}$)**
The multiple scattering phase function is related to the single scattering phase function $P$ by [S1]:
$$ P_{MS}(\theta, \ell) = N \cdot P\left(\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{\ell(1 - \exp(-\ell))}}\right) $$
where $N$ is a normalization constant [S1].

**7. Object Image Weight ($W$)**
The weight $W$ for the object image is [S1]:
$$ W = \left(1 + \ell_{obj} + \frac{2\bar{b}^2 w^2 \ell_{obj}}{\langle \theta^2 \rangle}\right) \exp\left(-\frac{c}{b}\ell_{obj}\right) $$
where $\ell_{obj}$ is the scattering events from the object to the eye [S1].

    """

    # The light volume is aligned with the light direction.

    # Assuming light_volume is a 3D array indexed by (i, j, k) corresponding to 
    # coordinates projected onto the light direction basis.
    l_light = light_volume.get_value_at(x, light_direction)
    
    # 2. Compute unscattered light at x

    c_x, b_x = medium_volume.get_extinction_and_scattering(x)
    if b_x == 0:
        return object_image.get_value(view_direction) # No scattering, just object
    
    ratio_cb = c_x / b_x
    
    # Attenuation from light to x
    attenuation_light = exp(-gamma * ratio_cb * l_light)
    L_unscat = light_intensity * attenuation_light
    
    # 3. Compute Single Scattering Contribution (L_SS)
    # Delta L_SS = L_unscat * P(x, w, w0) * exp(-(c/b)*l_eye) * b(x) * Delta_x
    # We need l_eye: scattering events from x to eye.
    # This requires marching from x towards the eye or using a precomputed volume.
    # The source mentions "marching through the media starting at the eye" for L_SS.
    # For a single point evaluation, we assume l_eye is available or computed.
    # Let's assume we have a function to get l_eye for a given direction.
    l_eye = medium_volume.get_scattering_events_from_eye(x, view_direction)
    
    attenuation_eye = exp(-ratio_cb * l_eye)
    
    # Phase function P(x, w, w0)
    # Angle between view_direction and light_direction
    cos_theta = dot(view_direction, light_direction)
    theta = acos(cos_theta)
    P_val = medium_volume.get_phase_function(theta)
    
    # Delta_x is the step size. For a point evaluation, we consider the contribution per unit length
    # or assume a small segment. The final radiance is the integral.
    # In a ray marcher, this is accumulated. Here we return the density.
    L_SS_density = L_unscat * P_val * attenuation_eye * b_x
    
    # 4. Compute Multiple Scattering Contribution (L_MS)
    # L_MS = integral over l of [ exp(-gamma * (c/b) * l) * P_MS * L_blurred ]
    # The source reformulates this to avoid the integral by using the blurred object image.
    # Eq 10 and 11 provide the weight W for the object image to account for MS.
    # L_MS contribution is effectively embedded in the weighting of L_obj and the L_blurred term.
    
    # Calculate w for the path from object to eye (l_obj)
    # l_obj is the total scattering events from object to eye.
    # l_obj = l_eye + l_light? No, l_obj is from object surface to eye.
    # The source defines l_obj as scattering events from object to eye.
    # In the context of the eye sample, l_obj is the path length in scattering events from the visible object point to the eye.
    # Let's assume we have l_obj.
    l_obj = medium_volume.get_scattering_events_from_object_to_eye(x, view_direction)

    # The source uses s as the physical length of the path.
    # For the object weight, s is the distance from object to eye.
    s_obj = distance(x, object_intersection_point(view_direction))

    # And the unscattered object contribution is L_obj * exp(-c*l_obj/b).
    # So the total object-related term is L_obj * exp(-c*l_obj/b) * (1 + 2 * b_bar^2 * w^2 * l_obj / theta^2).

    # The source derivation is complex. We will use the final Weight W formula.
    
    # Global average scattering coefficient
    b_bar = medium_volume.get_global_average_scattering()
    
    # Weight W
    W = (1 + l_obj + (2 * b_bar**2 * w**2 * l_obj) / mean_square_angle) * \
        exp(-ratio_cb * l_obj)

    L_obj = object_image.get_value(view_direction)
    
    # Total Radiance
    # Note: L_SS is a density. In a ray marcher, you integrate L_SS * Delta_x.
    # Here we return the radiance value assuming the caller handles the integration or Delta_x=1.
    # For a single point, L_MS is approximated by the weighted object image.

    # But Eq 11 is derived for W.
    # And Eq 10 is L_MS.
    # If W includes the MS part, then L_MS in Eq 1 might be zero or redundant?
    # "Putting equations 8 and 10 together and adding the unscattered object contribution... we obtain the weight W"
    # This suggests W * L_obj replaces (Unscattered Object + L_MS).
    # So L_MS in Eq 1 should be 0 if W is used?
    # Or is L_MS in Eq 1 the contribution from the volume itself, not the object?
    # The source says: "We split the radiance... into two components... object part... light part".
    # "For the object part we simply use a weighted image...".
    # "L_MS(x, w) = integral...".

    # Actually, Eq 10 is L_MS(w). Eq 11 is W.
    # If we use W, we are using the approximation for the object image.
    # The source says: "We therefore take L_obj out of the integral... leading to Eq 10... Putting 8 and 10 together... we obtain W".
    # This implies W * L_obj = Unscattered Object + L_MS (from object).
    # So L_MS in Eq 1 should be 0 if we use W?
    # Or is L_MS in Eq 1 the volume scattering?
    # The source says "L_MS(x, w) = ...".
    # I will assume L_MS in Eq 1 is the volume multiple scattering, and W * L_obj is the object + its MS.
    # But the source doesn't give a separate L_MS for the volume in the final Eq 1.
    # It says "L(x) = W * L_obj + L_SS + L_MS".
    # And then derives W.
    # It is possible L_MS is negligible or included in W for the dominant direction.
    # I will calculate L_MS using the blurred radiance formula if needed, but the source emphasizes W.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Backscattering:** The original model underestimates backscattering because it penalizes short paths with large angle changes via the curvature term. Dropping the curvature term corrects this behavior [S1].
*   **Dimensional Consistency:** Integrating weighted radiance in the incident plane produces a result with dimension radiance times area, which is incorrect. An effective area per path must be included, handled by the factor $\frac{\bar{b}}{\pi \langle \theta^2 \rangle}$ in $L_{blurred}$ [S1].
*   **Object Image Filtering:** Using the unfiltered object image $L_{obj}$ instead of a blurred version avoids runtime filtering penalties, as results are not visually objectionable [S1].
*   **Homogeneous vs. Inhomogeneous:** The model assumes constant $c/b$ for simple path integrals but can handle inhomogeneous media by evaluating coefficients locally [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The model allows single and multiple scattering computation in a single volume sweep, unlike previous methods requiring separate 3D blurring volumes and adaptive step sizes [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires a "Light Volume" (3D data structure storing $\ell_{light}$) and the main medium density volume [S1]. This makes the memory footprint significant compared to methods without precomputed light volumes.
*   **Strengths:** Handles general inhomogeneous media; avoids artifacts near boundaries and low-density areas where diffusion is invalid; incorporates volumetric shadows and shadows of the medium onto objects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes strong forward scattering; the approximation of MPPs by straight line segments may be inaccurate for highly curved paths; requires precomputation of the Light Volume [S1].
*   **When to use:** When rendering general inhomogeneous participating media with strong forward scattering, especially where diffusion approximations fail.
*   **When not to use:** For media with isotropic or backward-scattering dominant phase functions, or when memory is severely constrained.

## Background
The MPP model was introduced by Premože et al. in 2004 [S1]. It builds on pioneering work by Tessendorf (1987) and Premože et al. (2003), who introduced the concept of the Most Probable Path (MPP) for strongly forward-scattering inhomogeneous media [S1]. The 2004 paper extended this to a general lighting model for participating media, splitting radiance into object, single-scattered, and multiple-scattered components [S1]. It was designed to overcome limitations of previous methods that assumed planar surfaces, uniform media, and optical thickness, which led to diffusion approximation artifacts [S1].
