# 3D-Filter / Concrete Directions

Also called: 3D-Filter / Nishita, Nishita et al. 3D-Filter, 3D-Filter / N Directions, Concrete Directions Method

## Overview
The 3D-Filter method is an implicit representation technique for computing multiple scattering in participating media, specifically designed for volumetric phenomena like clouds and snow [S1][S2, book]. It approximates the radiance contribution from second- and third-order scattering by precomputing a "contribution-ratio pattern" (the 3D-filter) based on a mean density grid, which is then applied to the actual scene geometry [S1][S2, book]. Unlike explicit path-tracing methods, it decouples the geometric light transport calculation from the specific density distribution of the object, allowing for efficient rendering of complex shapes by storing directional energy distribution in a grid [S1]. It sets itself apart from diffusion-based methods by retaining directional information for specific scattering orders rather than solving a global diffusion equation, and from explicit multiple scattering models by using a precomputed filter to avoid per-voxel path integration [S1][S2, book].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Scene Geometry:** The spatial domain containing the participating medium (e.g., cloud volume or snow shape).
*   **Density Field:** The extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ and scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$ distribution. The method assumes a "mean density" for the precomputation phase [S1][S2, book].
*   **Lighting:** Sunlight direction and intensity, and sky light (skylight) distribution [S2, book].
*   **Viewing Direction:** The primary axis for the voxel grid orientation [S1][S2, book].

**Outputs:**
*   **Radiance $L(x)$:** The total radiance at each point $x$ in the viewing direction, composed of direct object radiance, single-scattered light, and multiple-scattered light (specifically 2nd and 3rd order) [S1][S2, book].

### Data Structures
1.  **Voxel Grid:** The space including the medium is subdivided into voxels. The grid is oriented such that the viewing direction is the principal axis [S1][S2, book].
2.  **3D-Filter (Contribution-Ratio Pattern):** A data structure stored for each voxel in a smaller reference space defined by the mean density of the cloud. It stores the precomputed contribution ratios (extended form factors) from surrounding voxels to the center voxel for paths involving one or two scatterings (resulting in 2nd and 3rd order contributions) [S1][S2, book].
    *   *Layout:* For a center voxel, the filter contains values representing the energy transfer from other voxels in the grid, weighted by the phase function and extinction, specifically for the viewing direction [S1].

### Steps
1.  **Precomputation (Filter Generation):**
    1.  Define a smaller reference space using the **mean density** of the cloud/medium [S1][S2, book].
    2.  Subdivide this space into voxels, aligning the principal axis with the viewing direction [S1][S2, book].
    3.  Compute the **contribution ratios** (extended form factors) from all other voxels to the center voxel. This computation must account for:
        *   The sunlight direction [S1][S2, book].
        *   The fact that scattering in clouds is mainly forward [S1][S2, book].
        *   Only paths with one or two scatterings are considered for the filter generation, as these contribute to the second and third orders of scattering [S1][S2, book].
    4.  To optimize computation, use a **stochastic method** to select only those voxels that effectively contribute to the center voxel, concentrating effort on the small solid angle where forward scattering energy lies [S1][S2, book].
    5.  Store these ratios in the 3D-filter for the center voxel [S1][S2, book].

2.  **Rendering (Application):**
    1.  Subdivide the actual scene space (with its specific density distribution) into voxels, maintaining the viewing direction as the principal axis [S1][S2, book].
    2.  Compute **single scattering** separately for the scene [S1][S2, book]. This includes skylight single-scattered by particles [S2, book].
    3.  Apply the precomputed 3D-filter to the voxels in the whole space. For each voxel, multiply the filter's stored contribution ratios by the actual energy at the related points in the scene [S1].
    4.  Accumulate these results to obtain the multiple-scattered radiance $L_{MS}(x)$ for the second and third orders [S1].
    5.  Combine the results: $L(x) = L_{obj} + L_{SS} + L_{MS}$, where $L_{MS}$ is the result from the filter application [S1][S2, book].

### Formulas
The radiance $L(x)$ is decomposed as:
$$ L = L_{obj} + L_{SS} + L_{MS} $$
Where $L_{MS}$ is approximated by the 3D-filter method [S1][S2, book].

The contribution of multiple scattering is calculated by multiplying the precomputed form factors $F$ by the energy $E$ at related points:
$$ L_{MS}(x) = \sum_{i} F_i \cdot E_i $$
Where $F_i$ represents the contribution ratio from voxel $i$ to the center voxel, and $E_i$ is the energy at voxel $i$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Scattering Orders:** Only the first three orders of scattering are considered for sunlight multiple scattering to save computation time. Single scattering is computed separately [S1][S2, book].
*   **Mean Density:** Used to define the smaller reference space for precomputing the filter [S1][S2, book].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Density Variations:** The method relies on a "mean density" for precomputation. If the actual density varies significantly from the mean, the accuracy of the 3D-filter may degrade [S1][S2, book].
*   **Forward Scattering Assumption:** The optimization relies on scattering being mainly forward. For media with strong backscattering, the stochastic selection of contributing voxels may miss significant energy contributions [S1].
*   **Voxel Alignment:** The grid must be aligned with the viewing direction. Changing the view requires reorientation or interpolation of the filter, which is not explicitly detailed in the sources [S1][S2, book].

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_3d_filter(mean_density, grid_resolution, viewing_direction, sun_direction):
    """
    Precomputes the 3D-filter (contribution-ratio pattern) for a mean density.
    """
    # Create a smaller reference space with mean density
    ref_space = create_voxel_grid(mean_density, grid_resolution)
    
    # Align grid with viewing direction
    ref_space.align_axis(viewing_direction)
    
    filter_data = {}
    
    for center_voxel in ref_space.voxels:
        # Stochastic selection of contributing voxels
        # Focus on forward scattering directions
        contributing_voxels = stochastic_select_contributors(
            center_voxel, 
            ref_space, 
            sun_direction, 
            max_scatterings=2
        )
        
        contribution_ratios = []
        for other_voxel in contributing_voxels:
            # Compute extended form factor considering extinction and phase function
            # Only paths with 1 or 2 scatterings are computed here
            ratio = compute_extended_form_factor(
                center_voxel, 
                other_voxel, 
                ref_density=mean_density,
                sun_direction=sun_direction
            )
            contribution_ratios.append(ratio)
        
        filter_data[center_voxel.id] = contribution_ratios
        
    return filter_data

def render_with_3d_filter(scene_voxels, filter_data, single_scattering_radiance, sun_direction, sky_light):
    """
    Applies the precomputed 3D-filter to the actual scene to compute multiple scattering.
    """
    # Align scene voxels with viewing direction (same as filter generation)
    scene_voxels.align_axis(viewing_direction)
    
    total_radiance = {}
    
    for voxel in scene_voxels.voxels:
        # Get precomputed contribution ratios for this voxel's position in the filter
        # Note: In practice, interpolation from filter_data may be needed if scene density differs
        ratios = filter_data.get(voxel.id, [])
        
        multiple_scattering_contribution = 0.0
        
        for ratio in ratios:
            # Find the corresponding energy at the related point in the scene
            # This requires mapping the filter's reference space to the actual scene space
            related_voxel = map_to_scene_voxel(ratio.source_voxel_id, scene_voxels)
            
            # Multiply form factor by energy at related point
            # Energy includes sunlight and sky light contributions
            energy = compute_energy_at_voxel(related_voxel, sun_direction, sky_light)
            
            multiple_scattering_contribution += ratio.value * energy
            
        # Combine with single scattering and direct object radiance
        direct_radiance = compute_direct_radiance(voxel)
        ss_radiance = single_scattering_radiance[voxel.id]
        
        total_radiance[voxel.id] = direct_radiance + ss_radiance + multiple_scattering_contribution
        
    return total_radiance
```

## Subtopics

### Diffusion Approximation
Uses a diffusion equation to solve multiple scattering between blobs, characterized by two functions (source radiance expansion). It avoids expensive shooting operations between blobs but loses directional detail compared to the 3D-filter [S1][S2, book].

### Stochastic Path Methods
Solve the transport equation by random sampling of paths. They are more general but computationally expensive compared to the precomputed 3D-filter approach [S1].

### Scattering Patterns
Represent directional distribution by considering a specific set of directions. This is a related implicit representation technique [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Precomputation is expensive due to the calculation of extended form factors, but it is done once for the mean density. Rendering time is reduced as the filter application is a simple multiplication and accumulation [S1][S2, book].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the 3D-filter for each voxel in the reference space. The size depends on the number of contributing voxels selected stochastically [S1][S2, book].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient for scenes with high albedo and forward scattering (like clouds and snow). Allows for complex shapes (e.g., metaballs) without per-voxel path tracing [S1][S2, book].
*   **Limitations:** Accuracy depends on the similarity between the actual density and the mean density used for precomputation. Limited to second and third-order scattering [S1][S2, book].
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for rendering clouds and snow where multiple scattering is significant and forward scattering dominates [S1][S2, book].
*   **When Not to Use:** Not suitable for media with strong backscattering or where high-order scattering effects are critical and density varies wildly from the mean [S1][S2, book].

## Background
The 3D-Filter method was proposed by Nishita et al. for displaying clouds taking into account multiple scattering and sky light [S1][S2, book]. It was later applied to render snow, which has a high albedo and looks white due to multiple scattering, with shape and density modeled using metaballs [S1]. The method is part of a broader effort to efficiently render participating media by using implicit representations of directional radiance distribution [S1].
