# 3D-Filter / Concrete Directions

Also called: 3D-Filter / Nishita, Nishita et al. 3D-Filter, 3D-Filter / N Directions, Concrete Directions Method

## Overview
The 3D-Filter method is an approximation for rendering participating media, specifically clouds and snow, that accounts for multiple scattering of sunlight and single scattering of skylight [S1][S2, book]. It solves the problem of efficiently computing the radiance contribution from higher-order scattering events without the prohibitive cost of full path tracing [S2, book]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Heterogeneous In-scattering with Multiple Scattering](4934-heterogeneous-in-scattering-with-multiple-scattering.md) by using a precomputed, grid-based "contribution-ratio pattern" (the 3D-filter) to implicitly represent directional radiance distribution, rather than explicit recursive ray casting or angular distribution functions [S1][S2, book].

## How it works
The method approximates the radiative transfer equation by separating the light source contributions: sunlight is treated with multiple scattering (up to the third order), while skylight is treated with single scattering [S2, book]. The core mechanism relies on a 3D grid (voxelization) aligned with the viewing direction to precompute how energy from neighboring voxels contributes to a center voxel [S1][S2, book].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Scene Geometry:** The medium (e.g., cloud or snow) defined by density distribution. For snow, this is often modeled using metaballs [S1][S2, book].
*   **Optical Properties:** Scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$, absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$, and extinction coefficient $\sigma_t = \sigma_s + \sigma_a$. The single scattering albedo is $\omega_0 = \sigma_s / \sigma_t$ [S2, book].
*   **Lighting:** Sunlight direction $\omega_s$ and intensity; Skylight intensity (assumed isotropic or from a hemisphere) [S2, book].
*   **Viewing Direction:** $\omega_v$ (used to align the voxel grid) [S1][S2, book].
*   **Scattering Orders:** The method considers only the first three orders of scattering for sunlight to save computation time [S1][S2, book].

**Outputs:**
*   **Radiance $L(x)$:** The final radiance at each point $x$ in the viewing direction, accumulated from single scattering (skylight), single scattering (sunlight), and multiple scattering (sunlight, 2nd and 3rd orders) [S1][S2, book].

### Data Structures
1.  **Voxel Grid:** The space containing the medium is subdivided into voxels. The grid is oriented such that the viewing direction $\omega_v$ is a principal axis [S1][S2, book].
2.  **3D-Filter (Contribution-Ratio Pattern):** A precomputed data structure representing the contribution ratios of radiance from other voxels to the center voxel in the viewing direction [S1][S2, book]. This filter encodes the "extended form factors" for paths with one or two scatterings [S1][S2, book].
3.  **Energy Buffer:** A buffer storing the light scattered due to second and third-order scattering for each voxel in the viewing direction [S1][S2, book].

### Steps

1.  **Voxelization:** Subdivide the space including the cloud/medium into voxels, aligning the grid with the viewing direction $\omega_v$ [S1][S2, book].
2.  **Define Reference Space:** Set up a smaller reference space using the mean density of the cloud/medium. This space is used to compute the contribution ratios [S1][S2, book].
3.  **Compute 3D-Filter (Precomputation):**
    *   Compute the contribution ratios to the radiance of a center voxel (in the viewing direction) from other voxels in the reference space [S1][S2, book].
    *   These ratios account for the sunlight direction $\omega_s$ [S1][S2, book].
    *   **Optimization:** Since scattering in clouds is mainly forward, energy scattered at a point lies within a small solid angle. Use a stochastic method to select only those voxels that effectively contribute to the center voxel for paths with one or two scatterings [S1][S2, book]. This concentrates effort on relevant voxels, speeding up the computation of extended form factors [S1][S2, book].
    *   The result is the 3D-filter, a pattern of contribution ratios [S1][S2, book].
4.  **Apply Filter to Scene:**
    *   Apply the 3D-filter to the voxels in the whole scene space [S1][S2, book].
    *   For each voxel, compute the light scattered due to second and third-order scattering in the viewing direction by multiplying the precomputed filter values by the energy at the related points (neighboring voxels) and accumulating the results [S1][S2, book].
    *   Store these accumulated values in the Energy Buffer for each voxel [S1][S2, book].
5.  **Compute Single Scattering:**
    *   Compute single scattering separately for both sunlight and skylight [S1][S2, book].
    *   For skylight, compute single scattering by particles [S2, book].
    *   For sunlight, compute single scattering along the viewing ray [S2, book].
6.  **Accumulate Radiance:**
    *   For each point $x$ on the viewing ray, retrieve the multiple scattering contribution (2nd and 3rd orders) from the Energy Buffer [S1][S2, book].
    *   Add the single scattering contributions (sunlight and skylight) [S1][S2, book].
    *   The final radiance is:
        $$ L(x) = L_{single, sun}(x) + L_{single, sky}(x) + L_{multi, sun}(x) $$
        where $L_{multi, sun}(x)$ is the accumulated result from the 3D-filter application [S1][S2, book].

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_3d_filter(mean_density, voxel_size, sun_direction, num_samples):
    """
    Precomputes the 3D-filter (contribution-ratio pattern) for a reference space.
    Uses stochastic sampling to find contributing voxels for 1-2 scattering paths.
    """
    filter_grid = create_empty_grid(mean_density, voxel_size)
    
    # Iterate over center voxels in the reference space
    for center_voxel in filter_grid:
        contributions = []
        
        # Stochastic selection of contributing voxels
        # Focus on forward scattering directions relative to sun
        for _ in range(num_samples):
            # Sample a direction biased towards forward scattering
            sample_direction = sample_forward_scattering(sun_direction)
            
            # Determine target voxel along this direction
            target_voxel = get_voxel_at_offset(center_voxel, sample_direction, voxel_size)
            
            if target_voxel is valid:
                # Compute geometric and optical contribution ratio
                # This involves form factors and transmittance
                ratio = compute_contribution_ratio(
                    center=center_voxel,
                    source=target_voxel,
                    sun_direction=sun_direction,
                    density=mean_density
                )
                contributions.append(ratio)
        
        # Store average contribution ratio for this center voxel
        filter_grid[center_voxel] = mean(contributions)
        
    return filter_grid

def render_cloud(viewing_ray, cloud_voxel_grid, sun_direction, skylight_intensity, filter_grid):
    """
    Renders the cloud using the precomputed 3D-filter.
    """
    radiance = 0.0
    
    # Step 1: Compute Single Scattering (Sunlight)
    single_sun = compute_single_scattering(viewing_ray, sun_direction, cloud_voxel_grid)
    
    # Step 2: Compute Single Scattering (Skylight)
    single_sky = compute_single_scattering(viewing_ray, skylight_directions, cloud_voxel_grid)
    
    # Step 3: Compute Multiple Scattering (Sunlight, 2nd and 3rd orders)
    # Apply the 3D-filter to the cloud voxel grid
    multi_sun = 0.0
    
    for voxel in cloud_voxel_grid:
        # Retrieve precomputed filter value for this voxel's position relative to viewing direction
        filter_value = filter_grid.get_filter_value(voxel.position, viewing_ray.direction)
        
        # Multiply by energy at related points (neighboring voxels)
        # This step accumulates contributions from 2nd and 3rd order scattering
        energy_contribution = compute_energy_contribution(
            voxel=voxel,
            filter_value=filter_value,
            sun_direction=sun_direction,
            cloud_voxel_grid=cloud_voxel_grid
        )
        multi_sun += energy_contribution
        
    # Step 4: Accumulate
    radiance = single_sun + single_sky + multi_sun
    
    return radiance
```

### Parameters
*   **Scattering Orders:** Only the first three orders of scattering are considered for sunlight [S1][S2, book].
*   **Voxel Size:** Determines the resolution of the grid [S1][S2, book].
*   **Mean Density:** Used to define the smaller reference space for filter computation [S1][S2, book].
*   **Stochastic Samples:** Number of samples used to select contributing voxels during filter precomputation [S1][S2, book].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Forward Scattering Assumption:** The method relies on the fact that scattering in clouds is mainly forward. If the medium has strong backscattering, the stochastic selection of voxels may miss significant contributions [S1][S2, book].
*   **Voxel Alignment:** The grid must be aligned with the viewing direction. Changing the camera view requires re-voxelization or interpolation of the filter [S1][S2, book].
*   **Density Variation:** The filter is computed using mean density. High density variations may lead to inaccuracies if not properly accounted for in the energy contribution step [S1][S2, book].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Precomputation of the 3D-filter depends on the number of voxels in the reference space and the number of stochastic samples [S1][S2, book]. Rendering time is linear with respect to the number of voxels along the viewing ray, as the filter application is a lookup and multiplication [S1][S2, book].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the voxel grid of the scene and the 3D-filter [S1][S2, book].
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently handles multiple scattering for clouds and snow without explicit path tracing [S1][S2, book]. The stochastic optimization reduces precomputation cost [S1][S2, book].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to specific media (clouds, snow) where forward scattering is dominant [S1][S2, book]. Requires precomputation and voxelization [S1][S2, book]. Accuracy depends on the mean density assumption [S1][S2, book].
*   **When to Use:** Rendering clouds and snow where multiple scattering is visually significant and performance is a concern [S1][S2, book].
*   **When Not to Use:** Media with strong backscattering or complex heterogeneous density variations that cannot be approximated by mean density [S1][S2, book].

## Background
The 3D-Filter method was proposed by Nishita et al. [S1][S2, book]. It was originally developed to display clouds taking into account multiple scattering of sunlight and single scattering of skylight [S2, book]. The method was later applied to render snow, which has a high albedo and appears white due to multiple scattering [S1][S2, book]. The approach is part of a broader family of methods that use implicit representations of directional radiance, such as scattering patterns and diffusion equations [S1][S2, book].
