# Riley et al. Angular Distribution Renderer

Also called: Eye Ray Rendering with Angular Distribution, Final Gather with Angular Distribution, Riley et al

## Overview
The Riley et al. Angular Distribution Renderer is a single-pass volume rendering method designed to efficiently approximate multiple scattering effects in participating media, specifically targeting large atmospheric particles like water and ice [S2]. It solves the problem of premature darkening in low-albedo clouds by using pre-computed angular distribution functions to direct in-scattered light into the eye ray, capturing chromatic effects such as rainbows and halos [S1, S2]. Unlike explicit path-tracing multiple scattering models, it avoids recursive ray casting by approximating the angular distribution of light after a fixed number of scattering events, leveraging a half-angle slicing scheme for hardware efficiency [S2].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Volume density field $\sigma_t(x)$ (extinction coefficient).
*   Single scattering albedo $\omega_0(x) = \sigma_s(x) / \sigma_t(x)$.
*   Phase function $p(\Omega, \Omega_{lt})$ describing angular scattering distribution.
*   Pre-computed multiple scattering angular distribution functions $A_{m\lambda}^{\lfloor n \rfloor}(\Omega - \Omega_{lt})$ for specific scattering orders [S1, S2].
*   Light direction $\Omega_{lt}$ (e.g., sun).
*   View direction $\Omega$.
*   User-adjustable backscatter term $\xi$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Eye buffer: Accumulated radiance $L(s, \Omega)$ and total transparency $\alpha$ [S1, S2].
*   Light buffer: Attenuation of light traversing the volume [S2].

### Data Structures
*   **Eye Buffer:** Stores accumulated radiance and alpha channel for the final image [S2].
*   **Light Buffer:** Stores the attenuation of light as it traverses the volume, used to modulate direct sunlight [S2].
*   **Angular Distribution Tables:** Pre-computed values $A_{m\lambda}^{\lfloor n \rfloor}$ mapping scattering order and angle to intensity [S1].

### Steps
1.  **Half-Angle Slicing:** Slice the volume data at an angle halfway between the light direction $\Omega_{lt}$ and the view direction $\Omega$ [S2]. This orientation allows a single pass to serve both the light attenuation calculation and the eye ray accumulation [S2].
2.  **Light Buffer Pass:** Render the volume to the light buffer to compute the attenuation of direct light (sunlight) as it traverses the medium [S2].
3.  **Eye Ray Accumulation:** Perform a single front-to-back slicing pass through the volume to accumulate in-scattered intensity into the eye ray [S1].
4.  **Scattering Order Estimation:** For each sample point $s$ along the eye ray, estimate the expected number of scattering events $n(s)$ that light has undergone before reaching this point [S1].
5.  **Angular Distribution Application:** Apply the angular distribution function for $n(s) + 1$ scattering events. The $+1$ accounts for the final scattering event that directs light into the eye ray [S1].
6.  **Backscatter Correction:** Add the user-adjustable term $\xi$ to the phase function to model light received from multiple scattering further along the path, preventing premature darkening at the cloud's illuminated edge [S1].
7.  **Direct Sunlight Integration:** Add the direct contribution of the sun. The total transparency stored in the eye buffer's alpha channel attenuates the sunlight, combined with atmospheric attenuation [S1].
8.  **Skylight and Aerial Perspective:** Add skylight and aerial perspective effects based on a simplified single-scattering analytic model to increase realism [S2].

### Formulas
The accumulation for a pixel, assuming front-to-back compositing starting from the near point $s_f$, is given by [S1]:

$$ L(s, \Omega) = \sum_{i=front}^{back} T(s, s_f + i m \Omega \Delta) I(s_f + i m \Omega \Delta) $$

Where the intensity contribution $I$ at sample $i$ is approximated by [S1]:

$$ I(s + m \Omega_{lt} \Delta) = I(s) T(s, s + m \Omega_{lt} \Delta) + I(s) (1 - T(s, s + m \Omega_{lt} \Delta)) \times A_{m\lambda}^{\lfloor n(s_f + i m \Omega \Delta) + 1 \rfloor}(\Omega - \Omega_{lt}) + \xi \beta_{sca} \Delta $$

*Note: The sources indicate that the exact product form in Equation 24 is not practical for graphics hardware due to floating-point texture interpolation limitations. The implementation uses a linear interpolation approximation for intensity loss for the worst-case attenuation (highest number of scattering events) for efficiency [S1].*

*   $T(s_1, s_2)$: Transmittance between points $s_1$ and $s_2$.
*   $n(s)$: Expected number of scattering events at position $s$.
*   $A_{m\lambda}^{\lfloor n \rfloor}$: Pre-computed angular distribution function for scattering order $n$.
*   $\xi$: User-adjustable term for backscatter [S1].
*   $\beta_{sca}$: Scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$ [S1].
*   $\Delta$: Step size.

### Parameters
*   $\xi$: User-adjustable term added to the phase function. Typical values are not specified, but it is critical for preventing premature darkening in low-albedo regions [S1].
*   Scattering Order $n$: Determined by the optical depth and albedo. The renderer uses $n+1$ for the final eye-directed scatter [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Premature Darkening:** Rendering without backscattered light causes the region where light first strikes the cloud to appear unnaturally dark. This is corrected by the term $\xi$ [S1].
*   **Hardware Limitations:** The exact product form of the scattering integral is not practical for implementation on programmable graphics hardware due to the lack of floating-point texture interpolation at the time of publication. The method uses an approximation for intensity loss based on the worst-case attenuation [S1].
*   **Dense Regions:** The model is least accurate in dense inner regions of the cloud, where the single-pass approximation may fail to capture complex light transport [S1].

### Pseudocode
```python
def render_angular_distribution(volume, light_dir, view_dir, xi):
    """
    Renders the volume using the Riley et al. Angular Distribution Renderer.
    
    Args:
        volume: Volume data structure with density and albedo.
        light_dir: Direction of the light source (e.g., sun).
        view_dir: Camera view direction.
        xi: User-adjustable backscatter term.
        
    Returns:
        eye_buffer: Accumulated radiance and alpha.
        light_buffer: Light attenuation.
    """
    # 1. Half-angle slicing orientation
    slice_normal = normalize(light_dir + view_dir)
    
    # 2. Light Buffer Pass: Compute attenuation of direct light
    light_buffer = render_volume_to_buffer(volume, light_dir, slice_normal, mode='light')
    
    # 3. Eye Ray Accumulation Pass
    eye_buffer = initialize_buffer()
    
    for each pixel:
        ray = create_eye_ray(pixel, view_dir)
        accumulated_radiance = 0.0
        accumulated_alpha = 0.0
        current_transmittance = 1.0
        
        # Front-to-back march
        for step in ray.march(volume, slice_normal):
            s = step.position
            delta = step.distance
            sigma_t = volume.get_extinction(s)
            sigma_s = volume.get_scattering(s)
            
            # Transmittance to current step
            T = exp(-sigma_t * delta)
            
            # Estimate scattering order n(s)
            # Note: Implementation detail for n(s) is not fully specified in sources [G]
            n_s = estimate_scattering_order(s, volume)
            
            # Angular distribution for n(s) + 1 events
            # A_m_lambda is pre-computed table
            angle_diff = angle_between(s, light_dir, view_dir)
            scattering_intensity = get_angular_distribution(n_s + 1, angle_diff)
            
            # In-scattered light contribution
            # Approximation for hardware efficiency [S1]
            # I(s) * (1 - T) * (Scattering + Backscatter)
            in_scatter = scattering_intensity + xi * sigma_s * delta
            
            # Direct light attenuation from light buffer
            light_attenuation = light_buffer.get_attenuation(s, light_dir)
            
            # Contribution to eye ray
            contribution = current_transmittance * light_attenuation * in_scatter * sigma_s * delta
            
            accumulated_radiance += contribution * current_transmittance
            accumulated_alpha += (1.0 - T) * current_transmittance
            current_transmittance *= T
            
            # Break if alpha is too high (early ray termination)
            if accumulated_alpha > 0.99:
                break
        
        # Add direct sunlight (attenuated by total transparency)
        direct_sun = get_sun_intensity() * exp(-accumulated_alpha * sigma_t_avg)
        accumulated_radiance += direct_sun * (1.0 - accumulated_alpha)
        
        # Add skylight and aerial perspective [S2]
        accumulated_radiance += get_skylight_contribution(s, view_dir)
        
        eye_buffer.set_pixel(pixel, accumulated_radiance, accumulated_alpha)
        
    return eye_buffer, light_buffer
```

## Subtopics

### Half-Angle Slicing
A technique to orient volume slices halfway between light and view directions, allowing a single pass to compute both light attenuation and eye ray accumulation [S2].

### Angular Distribution Functions
Pre-computed functions $A_{m\lambda}^{\lfloor n \rfloor}$ that approximate the angular distribution of light after a specific number of scattering events, enabling efficient single-pass rendering [S1, S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Single pass through the volume, making it highly efficient for real-time or near-real-time rendering on programmable graphics hardware [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for eye buffer, light buffer, and pre-computed angular distribution tables [S1, S2].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient single-pass implementation; captures fine angularly dependent chromatic effects (rainbows, halos, glories); avoids premature darkening in low-albedo clouds [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Least accurate in dense inner regions of the cloud; relies on pre-computed distributions which may not adapt dynamically to complex scene changes; approximation for hardware efficiency may lose precision [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Rendering atmospheric phenomena with large water/ice particles where performance is critical and chromatic effects are desired [S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes requiring high accuracy in dense, complex participating media where single-pass approximations fail [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed by Riley et al. in the context of fast rendering of atmospheric effects [S2]. It is part of a multiple model system designed to handle the forward multi-scattering behavior of large water and ice atmospheric particles [S2]. The work is cited as [76] in the survey by Crassin et al. [S2] and is detailed in "Efficient Rendering of Atmospheric Phenomena" [S1]. It builds on the half-angle slicing scheme introduced by Kniss et al. [45] [S2].
