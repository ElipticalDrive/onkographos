# Optically Thin Approximation Correction

Also called: Exospheric Optical Depth Correction, Self-absorption and Secondary Scattering Correction

## Overview
The Optically Thin Approximation Correction improves the accuracy of the single-scattering approximation by modeling photon loss due to absorption and secondary scattering along the light path. It addresses the error introduced when assuming no attenuation between the light source, the scattering volume, and the observer. Unlike the base [Single Scattering Approximation](5264-single-scattering-approximation.md), it applies exponential reduction factors for optical depth along the Sun-to-scatterer path and the scatterer-to-observer path.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Density field $\rho$ defined on a voxel grid.
- Line-of-sight (LOS) parameterization $S$ for each pixel, defined by origin, direction, and step size $\Delta l$.
- Index arrays $r, e, a$ defining the voxel traversal along $S$.
- Cross-section $\sigma$, dependent on local exospheric temperature.
- Forward tomographic operator $F$ (or its sunward variant $F_1$).

**Outputs:**
- Corrected radiance $I_{corr}$ for each pixel.

**Data Structures:**
- Arrays $r, e, a$: Indices mapping the LOS traversal to the voxel grid. Size: $N_{steps}$ per LOS.
- Array $\Delta l$: Step lengths along the LOS. Size: $N_{steps}$ per LOS.
- Array $\rho$: Density values at voxel centroids. Size: $N_{voxels}$.

**Formulas:**
The corrected radiance is given by:
$$ I_{corr} = \int F \rho e^{-\tau_1} e^{-\tau_2} dl $$
where:
- $F$ is the forward tomographic operator.
- $\tau_1$ is the optical depth along path $S_1$ (Sun to scattering point $P_s$).
- $\tau_2$ is the optical depth along path $S_2$ (scattering point $P_s$ to spacecraft).
- $\sigma$ is the cross-section, a function of local exospheric temperature [S1].

The optical depths are calculated as cumulative sums of $\sigma \rho \Delta l$ along the respective paths.

**Steps:**
1. Calculate $\tau_1$ for each scattering point $P_s$ along the LOS. This requires a sunward ray trace from $P_s$ to the Sun.
2. Calculate $\tau_2$ for each scattering point $P_s$ along the LOS. This is the optical depth from $P_s$ to the observer along the LOS $S$.
3. Compute the correction factors $e^{-\tau_1}$ and $e^{-\tau_2}$.
4. Discretize the integral as an inner product using the custom raytracer operator $F_1$ and cumulative summation.

    # 1. Compute tau_1 and tau_2.
    # 2. Apply corrections to the integrand.
    # 3. Integrate along the LOS.
    
    integrand = rho[r] * correction_1 * correction_2 # Assuming F=1 or included in rho

    # The radiance is the sum of contributions from all points.
    I_corr = np.sum(integrand * delta_l)

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Voxel Centroid Approximation:** The operator $F_1$ is an approximation because voxel centroids do not lie directly on the path $S_1$ (Sun to scatterer) [S1]. This introduces geometric error.
- **Temperature Dependence:** The cross-section $\sigma$ depends on local exospheric temperature. Accurate temperature models are required [S1].
- **Performance:** Computing $\tau_1$ requires a separate ray trace for each scattering point, which is computationally expensive.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{pixels} \cdot N_{steps})$ for the base approximation. The correction adds $O(N_{pixels} \cdot N_{steps})$ for $\tau_2$ (reuse of LOS arrays) and $O(N_{pixels} \cdot N_{steps\_sunward})$ for $\tau_1$ (sunward ray trace). The sunward trace is the bottleneck.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_{voxels})$ for density and cross-section arrays. Additional $O(N_{steps})$ per pixel for intermediate calculations.
- **Strengths:** Improves accuracy of optically thin approximations in planetary exospheres by accounting for self-absorption and secondary scattering.
- **Limitations:** Computationally expensive due to the need for sunward ray tracing. Approximation error from voxel centroid alignment.
- **When to use:** When high accuracy is required for optically thin media, such as planetary exospheres, and computational resources allow for additional ray tracing.
- **When not to use:** For real-time applications where performance is critical, or for optically thick media where multiple scattering dominates.

## Background
The method was introduced by J. H. Zoennchen et al. (2024) in the context of the TomoSphero framework for planetary and solar tomography [S1]. It extends the single-scattering approximation to better model photon loss in exospheric environments.
