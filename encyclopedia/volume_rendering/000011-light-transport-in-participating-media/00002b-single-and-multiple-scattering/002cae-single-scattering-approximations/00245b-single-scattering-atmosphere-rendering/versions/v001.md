# Single-Scattering Atmosphere Rendering

Also called: Single-Scattering Sky Rendering, Nishita Physical Sky Model, Rayleigh-Mie Single Scattering

## Overview
Single-scattering atmosphere rendering computes the color of the sky by numerically integrating the in-scattered light from a single distant light source (the sun) along a view ray through a spherical atmosphere shell [S1, S5, unofficial]. It solves the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) under the assumption that light is scattered only once before reaching the camera, accounting for Rayleigh (gas) and Mie (aerosol) scattering, as well as ozone absorption [S5, unofficial]. Unlike full multiple-scattering solvers, this method is computationally efficient and suitable for real-time rendering, though it ignores light scattered between atmospheric particles [S5, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from homogeneous volume methods by modeling the exponential density falloff of a planetary atmosphere and from Monte Carlo methods by using deterministic numerical integration [S1, S2, S5, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Camera origin $O$ and view direction $\omega$ [S5, unofficial].
*   Sun direction $\omega_{sun}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   Planet radius $R_E$ and atmosphere outer radius $R_A$ [S5, unofficial].
*   Physical constants: Rayleigh, Mie, and Ozone sea-level coefficients and scale heights [S5, unofficial].
*   Integration step count $N$ (typically 8–64) [S1, S5, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Scattered radiance $L_{sky}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   Direct sun luminance $L_{sun}$ (if visible) [S5, unofficial].
*   Total transmittance $T$ (for ground occlusion) [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method operates on scalar floats and RGB vectors. No complex data structures are required beyond standard vector types.
*   `vec3`: 3-component floating-point vector for positions, directions, and colors [S5, unofficial].
*   `float`: Single-precision floating-point for distances and optical depths [S5, unofficial].

### Formulas

**1. Density Profiles**
The scattering and absorption coefficients decrease exponentially with height $h$ above the planet surface [S5, unofficial]:
$$ \sigma_s^{Rayleigh}(h) = \sigma_{s,0}^{Rayleigh} e^{-h/H_r} $$
$$ \sigma_s^{Mie}(h) = \sigma_{s,0}^{Mie} e^{-h/H_m} $$
$$ \sigma_a^{Ozone}(h) = \sigma_{a,0}^{Ozone} e^{-h/H_o} $$
Where $H_r, H_m, H_o$ are the scale heights [S5, unofficial].

**2. Extinction Coefficient**
The total extinction coefficient at height $h$ is the sum of scattering and absorption [S5, unofficial]:
$$ \sigma_t(h) = \sigma_s^{Rayleigh}(h) + 1.11 \cdot \sigma_s^{Mie}(h) + \sigma_a^{Ozone}(h) $$
The factor 1.11 accounts for Mie extinction being slightly higher than scattering [S5, unofficial].

**3. Phase Functions**
*   **Rayleigh:**
    $$ P_R(\cos\theta) = \frac{3}{16\pi}(1 + \cos^2\theta) $$
    Where $\cos\theta = \omega \cdot \omega_{sun}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   **Mie (Henyey-Greenstein):**
    $$ P_M(\cos\theta) = \frac{1 - g^2}{4\pi (1 + g^2 - 2g\cos\theta)^{1.5}} $$
    Where $g = 0.76$ is the asymmetry factor [S5, unofficial].

**4. Transmittance**
Transmittance between two points $a$ and $b$ is the exponential of the negative optical depth [S5, unofficial]:
$$ Tr(a \rightarrow b) = \exp\left(-\int_a^b \sigma_t(t) dt\right) $$
This integral is approximated numerically [S5, unofficial].

**5. Source Term**
The in-scattered source term $j(x)$ at position $x$ is [S5, unofficial]:
$$ j(x) = L_{outerspace} \cdot Tr(outerspace \rightarrow x) \cdot \Omega_{Sun} \cdot \left( \sigma_s^{Rayleigh}(x)P_R + \sigma_s^{Mie}(x)P_M \right) $$
Where $\Omega_{Sun}$ is the solid angle of the sun [S5, unofficial].

**6. Luminance Solution**
The luminance at the camera is the integral of the source term attenuated by the path to the camera [S5, unofficial]:
$$ L(camera, \omega) = \int_{atmosphere}^{camera} Tr(x \rightarrow camera) j(x) dx $$

### Parameters
*   **Scale Heights:** $H_r = 8000$ m, $H_m = 1200$ m, $H_o = 8000$ m [S5, unofficial].
*   **Sea-Level Coefficients:**
    *   Rayleigh: $\sigma_{s,0}^{Rayleigh} = (5.8, 13.5, 33.1) \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^{-1}$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Mie: $\sigma_{s,0}^{Mie} = (21, 21, 21) \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^{-1}$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Ozone: $\sigma_{a,0}^{Ozone} = (3.426, 8.298, 0.356) \times 0.06 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^{-1}$ [S5, unofficial].
*   **Planet Radii:** $R_E = 6360 \times 10^3$ m, $R_A = 6420 \times 10^3$ m [S5, unofficial].
*   **Sun:** Angular radius $\approx 0.0046$ rad; Solid angle $\Omega_{Sun} \approx 0.0000664$ sr [S5, unofficial].
*   **Steps:** $N=8$ is typical for the integration loop [S5, unofficial]. Some implementations use $N=64$ for higher accuracy [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
import math

def single_scattering_atmosphere(camera_pos, camera_dir, sun_dir, planet_radius, atmos_radius):
    """
    Renders the sky using single-scattering approximation.
    """
    
    # Constants
    PI = 3.14159265359
    Hr = 8000.0
    Hm = 1200.0
    Ho = 8000.0
    
    rayleigh_sea = vec3(5.8, 13.5, 33.1) * 1e-6
    mie_sea = vec3(21, 21, 21) * 1e-6
    ozone_sea = vec3(3.426, 8.298, 0.356) * 0.06 * 1e-5
    
    sun_radius_rad = math.radians(0.53 / 2.0)
    sun_solid_angle = 2 * PI * (1 - math.cos(sun_radius_rad))
    
    # Helper: Extinction Coefficient
    def sigma_t(pos):
        h = length(pos) - planet_radius
        # Clamp negative height to 0 for density calculation if below surface, 
        # though integration usually stays within atmosphere.
        h = max(h, 0.0)
        
        sr = rayleigh_sea * math.exp(-h / Hr)
        sm = mie_sea * math.exp(-h / Hm)
        ao = ozone_sea * math.exp(-h / Ho)
        
        return sr + 1.11 * sm + ao

    # Helper: Transmittance via Numerical Integration
    def transmittance(from_pos, to_pos, steps=8):
        ds = (to_pos - from_pos) / steps
        integral = vec3(0.0)
        for i in range(steps):
            # Midpoint sampling
            s = from_pos + (i + 0.5) * ds
            integral += sigma_t(s)
        return math.exp(-length(ds) * integral)

    # Helper: Phase Functions
    def rayleigh_phase(cos_theta):
        return (3.0 / (16.0 * PI)) * (1.0 + cos_theta * cos_theta)

    def mie_phase(cos_theta, g=0.76):
        denom = 1.0 + g*g - 2.0 * g * cos_theta
        return (1.0 - g*g) / (4.0 * PI * math.pow(denom, 1.5))

    # 1. Intersect View Ray with Atmosphere
    # Solve |camera_pos + t * camera_dir|^2 = atmos_radius^2
    # a = 1, b = dot(camera_pos, camera_dir), c = |camera_pos|^2 - R_A^2
    b = dot(camera_pos, camera_dir)
    c = dot(camera_pos, camera_pos) - atmos_radius * atmos_radius
    discriminant = b*b - c
    
    if discriminant < 0:
        return vec3(0.0), 1.0 # No intersection
        
    sqrt_disc = math.sqrt(discriminant)
    t_exit = -b + sqrt_disc
    t_entry = -b - sqrt_disc
    
    # If camera is inside, entry is negative, exit is positive.
    # We integrate from camera to exit.
    if t_entry < 0:
        t_entry = 0 # Start from camera
        
    # If camera is outside, check if it hits planet first
    # Intersect with planet
    c_planet = dot(camera_pos, camera_pos) - planet_radius * planet_radius
    disc_planet = b*b - c_planet
    if disc_planet >= 0:
        t_planet_exit = -b + math.sqrt(disc_planet)
        if t_planet_exit > 0 and t_planet_exit < t_exit:
            # Ray hits planet before leaving atmosphere
            # Return black/ground color (simplified)
            return vec3(0.0), 1.0
            
    # 2. Compute Outer Space Luminance
    # Analytical transmittance from ground to zenith for normalization
    origin_h = length(camera_pos) - planet_radius
    zenith_h = atmos_radius - planet_radius
    
    tr_zenith_rayleigh = rayleigh_sea * Hr * (math.exp(-origin_h/Hr) - math.exp(-zenith_h/Hr))
    tr_zenith_mie = mie_sea * Hm * (math.exp(-origin_h/Hm) - math.exp(-zenith_h/Hm))
    tr_zenith_ozone = ozone_sea * Ho * (math.exp(-origin_h/Ho) - math.exp(-zenith_h/Ho))
    
    tr_zenith = math.exp(-(tr_zenith_rayleigh + tr_zenith_mie + tr_zenith_ozone))
    
    # Ground illuminance ~120,000 lux. 
    # L_outerspace = E_ground / (Omega_sun * Tr_zenith)
    # Note: Using normalized luminance 1.0 for ground if physical values overflow
    illuminance_ground = 120000.0 
    l_outerspace = (illuminance_ground / sun_solid_angle) / tr_zenith

    # 3. Integrate In-Scattered Light
    # L = integral( Tr(x->camera) * j(x) dx )
    ds = (camera_pos + t_exit * camera_dir - camera_pos) / 8.0 # 8 steps
    acc_luminance = vec3(0.0)
    
    for i in range(8):
        s = camera_pos + (i + 0.5) * ds
        dist_to_camera = length(s - camera_pos)
        
        # Tr(x -> camera)
        tr_to_cam = transmittance(s, camera_pos, steps=8)
        
        # Source term j(x)
        # L_sun(x) = L_outerspace * Tr(outerspace -> x)
        # Distance from s to atmosphere exit in sun direction
        # Solve |s + t * sun_dir|^2 = R_A^2
        b_sun = dot(s, sun_dir)
        c_sun = dot(s, s) - atmos_radius * atmos_radius
        disc_sun = b_sun*b_sun - c_sun
        t_sun_exit = -b_sun + math.sqrt(disc_sun)
        sun_exit_pos = s + t_sun_exit * sun_dir
        
        tr_to_sun = transmittance(s, sun_exit_pos, steps=8)
        l_sun_at_s = l_outerspace * tr_to_sun
        
        cos_theta = dot(camera_dir, sun_dir)
        
        sr = rayleigh_sea * math.exp(-max(length(s)-planet_radius, 0.0)/Hr)
        sm = mie_sea * math.exp(-max(length(s)-planet_radius, 0.0)/Hm)
        
        rayleigh_diff = sr * rayleigh_phase(cos_theta)
        mie_diff = sm * mie_phase(cos_theta)
        
        j_x = l_sun_at_s * sun_solid_angle * (rayleigh_diff + mie_diff)
        
        acc_luminance += tr_to_cam * j_x * length(ds)

    # 4. Direct Sun Disk
    # Check if sun is visible
    cos_sun_angle = dot(camera_dir, sun_dir)
    angle = math.acos(max(min(cos_sun_angle, 1.0), -1.0))
    
    # Smoothstep mask for sun disk
    disk = 1.0 - smoothstep(sun_radius_rad * 0.95, sun_radius_rad * 1.05, angle)
    
    # Attenuate sun by atmosphere transmittance from camera to exit
    tr_cam_to_exit = transmittance(camera_pos, camera_pos + t_exit * camera_dir, steps=8)
    l_sun_direct = disk * l_outerspace * tr_cam_to_exit
    
    total_luminance = acc_luminance + l_sun_direct
    
    # 5. Transmittance for ground occlusion
    final_transmittance = transmittance(camera_pos, camera_pos + t_exit * camera_dir, steps=8)
    
    return total_luminance, final_transmittance

def smoothstep(edge0, edge1, x):
    t = max(0.0, min(1.0, (x - edge0) / (edge1 - edge0)))
    return t * t * (3.0 - 2.0 * t)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Ray Origin Inside Planet:** If the camera is inside the planet, the ray intersection logic must handle negative entry distances. The integration should start from the exit of the planet surface or return black [S1, S5, unofficial].
*   **Large Luminance Values:** Physical luminance values (e.g., $1.8 \times 10^9$ cd/m²) can overflow standard renderers. A common fix is to normalize ground illuminance to $2\pi$ to yield a ground luminance of 1 [S5, unofficial].
*   **Ray Missing Atmosphere:** If the discriminant is negative, the ray does not intersect the atmosphere. Return black or background color [S4, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Optical depth integrals can suffer from precision loss if step sizes are too large or scale heights vary significantly. Midpoint sampling is recommended [S5, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Time:** $O(N)$ per pixel, where $N$ is the number of integration steps (typically 8–64) [S5, unofficial]. Each step requires evaluating density, phase functions, and transmittance sub-integrals.
*   **Memory:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per pixel. No textures or buffers are strictly required for the core calculation, though LUTs can be used for optimization [S5, unofficial].

### Measured Performance
*   On modern GPUs, $N=8$ steps can run at real-time frame rates (60+ FPS) for 1080p resolution [S5, unofficial].
*   Increasing $N$ improves accuracy but linearly increases cost.

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:** Physically accurate sky colors (blue zenith, red horizon); computationally cheap; no precomputation required [S5, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Ignores multiple scattering, leading to dark skies in deep atmospheres or near the horizon at sunset; does not account for ground reflection (albedo) [S5, unofficial].

### When to Use
*   Real-time sky rendering in games and simulations [S5, unofficial].
*   When performance is critical and multiple scattering is not visually required [S5, unofficial].

### When Not to Use
*   High-fidelity offline rendering requiring accurate deep-atmosphere effects [S5, unofficial].
*   Scenes with significant ground reflection contributing to sky brightness [S5, unofficial].

## Background

### Origin
The method is based on the physical model of atmospheric scattering described by Nishita et al. (1993) and later refined by Hillaire (2016, 2020) [S5, S6, unofficial]. It solves the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) for a single distant light source [S5, unofficial].

### History
*   **1993:** Nishita et al. introduced a physically based sky model using single scattering [S6, unofficial].
*   **2016-2020:** Sébastien Hillaire popularized a real-time GLSL implementation of this model, detailing the derivation of outer space luminance and numerical integration [S5, unofficial].
*   **Scratchapixel:** Provided a widely cited tutorial on simulating the sky using these principles [S5, unofficial].

### Usage
Used in real-time engines, educational tutorials, and procedural sky generators [S5, S6, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Single-scattering homogeneous volume rendering
*   **What it is:** Renders scattering in a volume with constant density.
*   **Difference:** Ignores exponential density falloff; simpler transmittance calculation.
*   **When to choose:** For fog or smoke clouds, not planetary atmospheres.
*   **Link:** [Single-scattering homogeneous volume rendering](1135-single-scattering-homogeneous-volume-rendering.md)

### Volume Single Scattering (Monte Carlo with Point Light)
*   **What it is:** Uses stochastic sampling to estimate single scattering from local lights.
*   **Difference:** Handles multiple local lights and complex geometry; higher variance.
*   **When to choose:** For indoor scenes or complex lighting, not global sky.
*   **Link:** [Volume Single Scattering (Monte Carlo with Point Light)](2293-volume-single-scattering-monte-carlo-with-point-light.md)

### Single Scattering Approximation
*   **What it is:** General term for ignoring multiple bounces.
*   **Difference:** This article is a specific implementation for spherical atmospheres.
*   **When to choose:** When general single-scattering logic is needed.
*   **Link:** [Single Scattering Approximation](5264-single-scattering-approximation.md)
