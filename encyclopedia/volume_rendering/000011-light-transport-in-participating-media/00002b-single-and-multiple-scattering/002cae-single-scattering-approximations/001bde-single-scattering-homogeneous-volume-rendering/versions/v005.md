# Single-scattering homogeneous volume rendering

Also called: Single monochromatic homogeneous volume with absorption and single-scattering, SingleScatterTracer, Vol path tracing 2

## Overview
This method renders a participating medium by solving the radiative transfer equation under the assumption that light scatters exactly once within the volume before reaching the camera [S2, S3]. It builds on [Single Scattering Approximations](5340-single-scattering-approximations.md) by adding single-scattering contributions from surface-emitting lights, assuming a single homogeneous medium where absorption and scattering coefficients are spatially constant and monochromatic [S2, S3]. The method is distinguished from multiple scattering models by ignoring light that bounces more than once within the medium [S2, S3].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\omega$, scene geometry (manifold $\mathcal{M}$), homogeneous medium coefficients $\sigma_a$ (absorption) and $\sigma_s$ (scattering), phase function $\rho$, and a random number generator [S2, S3].
*   **Output:** Radiance $L_2(\mathbf{o}, \omega)$ at the camera [S2, S3].

**Data Structures:**
The method is encapsulated in a volume integration method struct, typically defined as:
```julia
struct SingleScatterTracer <: VolumeIntegrationMethod end
```
[S1, unofficial]

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_a$: Absorption coefficient (constant) [S2, S3].
*   $\sigma_s$: Scattering coefficient (constant) [S2, S3].
*   $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$: Extinction coefficient, used for transmittance calculations representing energy loss due to absorption and out-scattering [S2, S3].
*   $\rho(\mathbf{p}, \omega, \omega')$: Phase function describing the probability of scattering from direction $\omega'$ to $\omega$ at point $\mathbf{p}$ [S2, S3].
*   $L_e(\mathbf{p}', \omega')$: Emission radiance from surface point $\mathbf{p}'$ [S2, S3].
*   The medium is monochromatic: $\sigma_a$ and $\sigma_s$ have identical values across color channels [S2, S3].

**Assumptions:**
1.  There is a single, homogeneous volume [S2, S3].
2.  Surfaces only emit light ($L_e$) and do not reflect or transmit light [S2, S3].
3.  Light scatters exactly once within the volume [S2, S3].
4.  No environment maps are present in the scene [S2, S3].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Ray Intersection:** Intersect the camera ray with the scene geometry to find the hit distance $t_{\text{hit}}$ and the hit point $\mathbf{p}(t_{\text{hit}})$ [S2, S3].
2.  **Sample Scattering Distance:** Generate a uniform random number $u \in [0, 1]$ [S2, S3].
3.  **Inverse Transform Sampling:** Compute the sampled scattering distance $t$ using the inverse CDF of the exponential distribution derived from importance sampling the transmittance term $\exp(-\sigma_t t)$:
    $$ t = \frac{\log(1-u)}{-\sigma_t} $$
    [S2, S3]
4.  **Branching:**
    *   **Case A: Surface Hit ($t \geq t_{\text{hit}}$):** The ray hits the surface before scattering. The contribution is the surface emission attenuated by the transmittance to the surface:
        $$ \text{Contribution} = \exp(-\sigma_t t_{\text{hit}}) L_e(\mathbf{p}(t_{\text{hit}})) $$
        [S2, S3]
    *   **Case B: Volume Scattering ($t < t_{\text{hit}}$):** The ray scatters at point $\mathbf{p}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$. The contribution is the single-scattering radiance $L_{\text{scatter1}}$ at $\mathbf{p}(t)$, attenuated by the transmittance from the camera to the scattering point, weighted by the scattering coefficient and divided by the PDF of the sampling distribution:
        $$ \text{Contribution} = \frac{\exp(-\sigma_t t) \sigma_s L_{\text{scatter1}}(\mathbf{p}(t), \omega)}{p(t)} $$
        where the PDF is $p(t) = \sigma_t \exp(-\sigma_t t)$ [S2, S3]. The term $\frac{\exp(-\sigma_t t) \sigma_s}{p(t)}$ simplifies to $\frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_t}$ [G].
5.  **Compute Single-Scattering Radiance:** Calculate $L_{\text{scatter1}}(\mathbf{p}(t), \omega)$ by integrating over the scene manifold $\mathcal{M}$:
    $$ L_{\text{scatter1}}(\mathbf{p}, \omega) = \int_{\mathcal{M}} \rho(\mathbf{p}, \omega, \omega') L_e(\mathbf{p}', \omega') \exp(-\sigma_t \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}'\|) \frac{|\omega' \cdot \mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{p}'}|}{\|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}'\|^2} \text{visible}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{p}') d\mathbf{p}' $$
    [S2, S3]
    Here, $\mathbf{p}'$ is a point on the emitting surface, $\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{p}'}$ is its normal, $\omega' = \frac{\mathbf{p}' - \mathbf{p}}{\|\mathbf{p}' - \mathbf{p}\|}$, and $\text{visible}$ is the visibility function (1 if unoccluded, 0 otherwise) [S2, S3]. This integral is evaluated using Monte Carlo sampling over the scene geometry [S2, S3].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def single_scatter_homogeneous_volume_render(camera_ray_origin, camera_ray_direction, scene_geometry, sigma_a, sigma_s, rng):
    sigma_t = sigma_a + sigma_s
    
    # Standard ray-scene intersection [G]
    t_hit, p_hit = ray_intersect(scene_geometry, camera_ray_origin, camera_ray_direction)
    
    # Sample uniform random number
    u = rng.uniform(0.0, 1.0)
    # Inverse CDF for exponential distribution
    t = log(1.0 - u) / -sigma_t
    
    if t >= t_hit:
        # Case A: Hit surface
        transmittance = exp(-sigma_t * t_hit)
        # Standard surface emission query [G]
        L_e = scene_geometry.get_emission(p_hit, -camera_ray_direction)
        return transmittance * L_e
    else:
        # Case B: Scatter in volume
        p_scatter = camera_ray_origin + t * camera_ray_direction
        # Monte Carlo estimate of L_s1 [G]
        L_s1 = estimate_single_scattering_radiance(p_scatter, camera_ray_direction, scene_geometry, sigma_t, rng)
        # Weight by scattering coefficient ratio
        return (sigma_s / sigma_t) * L_s1

def estimate_single_scattering_radiance(p, omega, scene_geometry, sigma_t, rng):
    # Standard scene point sampling [G]
    p_prime, omega_prime, normal, pdf_surface = sample_geometry(scene_geometry, rng)
    if p_prime is None:
        return 0.0
        
    # Standard visibility check [G]
    if not check_visibility(p, p_prime, scene_geometry):
        return 0.0
        
    dist = distance(p, p_prime)
    transmittance = exp(-sigma_t * dist)
    
    # Jacobian term: |omega' . n_p'| / dist^2
    # omega_prime is direction from p to p'
    cos_theta = abs(dot(omega_prime, normal))
    jacobian = cos_theta / (dist * dist)
    
    # Phase function evaluation [G]
    # omega is direction towards camera, omega_in is direction from p' to p
    omega_in = -omega_prime
    phase = evaluate_phase(p, omega, omega_in)
    
    # Standard emission query [G]
    L_e = scene_geometry.get_emission(p_prime, omega_in)
    
    contribution = phase * L_e * transmittance * jacobian
    return contribution / pdf_surface
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Ray Differentials:** Determining ray differentials for volumetric scattering is an unsolved problem in the referenced context. Ray differentials should be disabled by setting `RayDifferential ray_diff = RayDifferential{Real(0), Real(0)}` [S2, S3].
*   **Zero Extinction:** If $\sigma_t = 0$, the sampling formula involves division by zero. This case is not explicitly handled in the sources but implies no attenuation [G].
*   **Monochromatic Assumption:** The method assumes monochromatic coefficients. Applying it to RGB requires running the calculation for each channel independently or ensuring $\sigma_a$ and $\sigma_s$ are scalar values applied to all channels [S2, S3].
*   **No Environment Maps:** The method assumes no environment maps; all light comes from surface emission $L_e$ [S2, S3].

## Operating characteristics
**Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The method requires one ray intersection per pixel for the primary ray, plus additional ray intersections for sampling the single-scattering integral over the scene manifold [S2, S3].
**Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The method only requires storing the scene geometry and medium coefficients [S1, unofficial][S2, S3].
**Strengths:**
*   Physically plausible single-scattering effects [S2, S3].
*   Simple implementation compared to multiple scattering methods [S2, S3].
*   Efficient for optically thin volumes or when single scattering dominates [S2, S3].
**Limitations:**
*   Ignores multiple scattering, leading to inaccuracies in optically thick volumes [S2, S3].
*   Limited to homogeneous media [S2, S3].
*   Limited to scenes with only emitting surfaces (no reflection) [S2, S3].
*   Ray differentials are not supported [S2, S3].
**When to Use:**
*   When rendering homogeneous participating media with single scattering dominance [S2, S3].
*   When computational resources are limited and multiple scattering is not required [S2, S3].
*   When the scene consists of emitting surfaces and no environment maps [S2, S3].
**When Not to Use:**
*   For optically thick volumes where multiple scattering is significant [S2, S3].
*   For heterogeneous media [S2, S3].
*   For scenes with reflective surfaces or environment maps [S2, S3].

## Background
This method is part of a series of volumetric path tracing homework assignments from the University of California, San Diego (CSE 272) [S2, S3]. It builds upon the absorption-only model by adding single-scattering contributions [S2, S3]. The derivation follows the radiative transfer equation, converting the differential form into an integral form suitable for Monte Carlo sampling [S2, S3]. The specific implementation details, such as the handling of ray differentials and the absence of environment maps, are specific to the homework context [S2, S3]. The term "SingleScatterTracer" appears in the Lyr.jl library documentation [S1, unofficial].
