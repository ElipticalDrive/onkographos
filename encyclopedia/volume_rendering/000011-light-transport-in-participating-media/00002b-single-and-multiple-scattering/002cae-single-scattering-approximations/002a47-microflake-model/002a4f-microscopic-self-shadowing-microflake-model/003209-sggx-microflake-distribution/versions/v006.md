# SGGX Microflake Distribution

Also called: Micro-flake normal distribution, SGGX Phase Function Representation

## Overview
The SGGX microflake distribution is a parametric model for the normal distribution function of microflakes in participating media, introduced by Heitz et al. [HDCD15] [S1]. It models anisotropic scattering using a 3×3 symmetric positive definite matrix $S$ [S2]. It sets itself apart from previous distributions by supporting fast and exact evaluation of attenuation coefficients, as well as efficient evaluation and sampling of specular phase functions [S1]. It serves as the foundational component for the [Microscopic Self-Shadowing Microflake Model](4746-microscopic-self-shadowing-microflake-model.md) [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A unit direction vector $m$ representing the microflake normal, and a 3×3 symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrix $S$ [S2].
- Output: The probability density value $D(m)$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
- $S$: A 3×3 symmetric positive definite matrix defining the SGGX phase function [S2]. In practice, it is stored as 6 floating-point values representing the upper triangular part [G].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. Compute the inverse of $S$, denoted $S^{-1}$ [G].
2. Compute the determinant $|S|$ [G].
3. Compute the quadratic form $q = m^T S^{-1} m$ [G].
4. Compute the denominator $denom = \pi \sqrt{|S|} q^2$ [G].
5. Return $1 / denom$ [G].

**Formula:**
The probability density function for the microflake normal $m$ is defined as [S2]:
$$D(m) = \frac{1}{\pi \sqrt{|S|} (m^T S^{-1} m)^2}$$
Where:
- $|S|$ is the determinant of matrix $S$ [S2].
- $S^{-1}$ is the inverse of matrix $S$ [S2].
- $m$ is a unit vector representing the microflake normal [S2].
- The term $(m^T S^{-1} m)$ represents the quadratic form of the inverse matrix [S2].

**Parameters:**
- $S$: The 3×3 SPD matrix defining the anisotropy and spread of the microflake normals [S2]. Typical values are not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **SPD Constraint:** The matrix $S$ must remain symmetric positive definite to ensure the distribution is valid [S2]. If $S$ becomes singular or indefinite, the determinant or inverse becomes undefined [S2].
- **Precision:** Numerical stability depends on accurate computation of the determinant and inverse, particularly when $S$ is ill-conditioned [G].

## Subtopics

### [GGX Mixture NDF](10859-ggx-mixture-ndf.md)
The GGX Mixture NDF is a compact representation of the marginal distribution of surface normals, $p_N(n)$, used to model glossiness and anisotropy in participating media and microflake models. It approximates the surface NDF as a weighted sum of Single Scattering GGX (SGGX) distributions. This method sets itself apart from single-lobe SGGX models by allowing the representation of complex, multi-modal normal distributions, and from aggregated microfacet convolutions by providing the base component that is subsequently convolved with isotropic spherical distributions to model surface roughness effects.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The evaluation of $D(m)$ is $O(1)$ assuming fixed-size matrix operations for 3×3 matrices [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** The method requires $O(1)$ memory per distribution, storing only the 6 unique elements of the SPD matrix $S$ [G].
- **Performance:** The SGGX distribution is computationally less expensive than state-of-the-art models [Jakob et al. 2010; Zhao et al. 2011] while offering similar representative power for anisotropic scattering [S2].
- **Strengths:** The method supports fast and exact evaluation of attenuation coefficients, as well as efficient evaluation and sampling of specular phase functions [S1].
- **Limitations:** The single-lobe representation cannot capture complex multi-modal normal distributions without mixing [S1]. The implementation requires careful maintenance of the SPD property for $S$ [S2].
- **When to use:** The method should be used when modeling anisotropic scattering in participating media where computational efficiency is critical and the microflake orientation distribution can be approximated by a single lobe [S1].
- **When not to use:** The method should not be used when the normal distribution is highly multi-modal or requires complex correlations that a single SPD matrix cannot capture [S1].

## Background
The SGGX distribution was introduced by Heitz et al. in 2015 [HDCD15] [S1]. It was developed to address limitations in previous microflake normal distributions, specifically regarding the efficiency of evaluating attenuation coefficients and sampling phase functions [S1]. The model has been used in various volume rendering contexts, including downsampling volume data [S1, S2] and implementing simplified self-shadowing models [S1].
