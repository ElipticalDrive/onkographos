# Gaussian Fiber Distribution

## Overview
The Gaussian Fiber Distribution is a microflake normal distribution function (NDF) used within the Microflake Model to define the statistical orientation of microflakes in anisotropic participating media [S1]. It provides a more efficient implementation than the Power of Sine/Cosine distributions introduced by Jakob et al. [JAM* 10] [S1]. Unlike later models, it does not provide a closed-form expression for the attenuation coefficient, relying instead on an efficient approximation [Jak10] [S1].

## Subtopics

### Power of Sine/Cosine Distributions
These distributions were introduced by Jakob et al. [JAM* 10] and serve as a baseline for comparison [S1]. The surface distribution is defined as $D_{\text{surf}}(\omega) = \cos^n(\mathbf{v}, \omega)$, and the fiber distribution as $D_{\text{fiber}}(\omega) = \sin^n(\mathbf{v}, \omega)$, where $\mathbf{v}$ is the microflake normal direction and $\omega$ is the view direction [S1]. Neither distribution has a closed-form expression for the attenuation coefficient, and the original work used spherical harmonic approximations for evaluation and sampling [S1]. The Gaussian Fiber Distribution is cited as more efficient than the original implementation of these distributions [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Efficiency:** The implementation by Zhao et al. [ZJMB11] is more efficient than the original implementation of Jakob et al. [JAM* 10] [S1].
- **Attenuation calculation:** It does not provide a closed-form expression for the attenuation coefficient and relies on an efficient approximation [Jak10] [S1].
- **Sampling:** Uses a rejection algorithm for sampling the distribution of visible normals [S1].
- **Limitations:** The rejection algorithm based on sampling the normal distribution is inefficient in some configurations [S1].
- **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
- **Origin:** The distribution was used by Zhao et al. [ZJMB11] [S1].
- **Context:** It was developed within the microflake model framework to provide efficient anisotropic scattering distributions [S1].
- **Development:** Zhao et al. provided an efficient implementation but did not provide a closed-form attenuation coefficient, relying on Jakob's approximation [Jak10] [S1]. Subsequent work by Heitz et al. [HDCD15] introduced the SGGX distribution to overcome limitations of previous distributions, including the lack of closed-form attenuation and efficient sampling [S1].
