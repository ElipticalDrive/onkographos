# Gaussian Fiber Distribution

## Overview
The Gaussian Fiber Distribution is a microflake normal distribution function (NDF) used within the Microflake Model to simulate anisotropic participating media, such as fur or hair. It serves as an efficient alternative to the Power of Sine/Cosine distributions introduced by Jakob et al. [JAM* 10]. While it offers improved computational efficiency, it does not provide a closed-form expression for the attenuation coefficient, relying instead on an efficient approximation [Jak10].

## How it works
The method defines the statistical orientation of microflakes (fibers) within a volume. The distribution is parameterized by a mean normal direction $\mathbf{v}$ and a sharpness parameter $n$.

## Subtopics

### Power of Sine/Cosine Distributions
These are the distributions proposed by Jakob et al. [JAM* 10], which the Gaussian Fiber Distribution is compared against. They include:
- **Surface Distribution**: $D_{surf}(\omega) = \cos^n(\mathbf{v}, \omega)$
- **Fiber Distribution**: $D_{fiber}(\omega) = \sin^n(\mathbf{v}, \omega)$
where $\mathbf{v}$ is the microflake normal direction and $\omega$ is the view direction.
- **Characteristics**: These distributions do not have closed-form expressions for attenuation coefficients. Spherical harmonic approximations were used for evaluation and sampling in the original work [S1].
- **Comparison**: The Gaussian Fiber Distribution is cited as more efficient than the original implementation of these distributions [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Efficiency**: The Gaussian Fiber Distribution implementation by Zhao et al. [ZJMB11] is more efficient than the original implementation of Jakob et al. [JAM* 10] [S1].
- **Attenuation Calculation**: It does not provide a closed-form expression for the attenuation coefficient. It relies on an efficient approximation [Jak10] [S1].
- **Sampling**: Uses a rejection algorithm for sampling the distribution of visible normals [S1].
- **Limitations**: The rejection algorithm based on sampling the normal distribution is inefficient in some configurations [S1].
- **Complexity**: Not given in the sources.

## Background
- **Origin**: The Gaussian Fiber Distribution was used by Zhao et al. [ZJMB11].
- **Context**: It was developed as part of the microflake model framework, aiming to provide efficient anisotropic scattering distributions. It is an alternative to the Power of Sine/Cosine distributions introduced by Jakob et al. [JAM* 10].
- **Development**: The source [S1] notes that while Zhao et al. provided an efficient implementation, they did not provide a closed-form attenuation coefficient, relying on Jakob's approximation [Jak10]. Later work by Heitz et al. [HDCD15] introduced the SGGX distribution to overcome limitations of previous distributions, including the lack of closed-form attenuation and efficient sampling [S1].
