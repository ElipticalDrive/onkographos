# Path Tracing Estimators for Refractive Radiative Transfer

Also called: BDPT

## Overview
Path tracing estimators for refractive radiative transfer solve the problem of unbiasedly estimating light transport in participating media where the refractive index varies spatially, causing light rays to follow curved trajectories rather than straight lines [S1]. What sets this family apart from standard path tracing in homogeneous media is the requirement that direct connections between vertices must satisfy Hamilton's equations to find valid curved paths, rather than simply drawing straight lines [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves generating subpaths and forming complete paths via direct connections [S1]. Subpaths are generated by alternating between ray tracing events and scattering events [S1]. In refractive radiative transfer, the ray tracing step is non-linear due to spatially varying refractive indices [S1]. Complete paths are formed by performing direct connections between vertices [S1]. When direct connections are made between two points in a heterogeneous refractive medium, the connection cannot be a straight line [S1]. Instead, the algorithm must find a curved path connecting the two points that satisfies Hamilton's equations [S1]. This is the primary computational distinction from homogeneous radiative transfer [S1].

## Subtopics

### Path Tracing (PT)
* **What it is:** A unidirectional estimator generating a single subpath from the sensor [S1].
* **Differentiation:** Relies on next-event estimation to connect subpath vertices to light sources [S1].
* **When to choose:** Use when computational resources are limited or when scene geometry makes bidirectional connections difficult to sample efficiently [G].
* **Link:** [Path Tracing](52-path-tracing.md)

### Bidirectional Path Tracing (BDPT)
* **What it is:** A bidirectional estimator generating two independent subpaths, one from the light source and one from the sensor [S1].
* **Differentiation:** Connects any two vertices from the source and sensor subpaths, allowing for more robust sampling of complex light paths compared to PT [S1].
* **When to choose:** Use when the scene contains complex light transport scenarios that are difficult to capture with a single sensor-originated subpath [G].
* **Link:** [Bidirectional Path Tracing](6666-bidirectional-path-tracing.md)

## Background
The concept of path tracing estimators for radiative transfer was formalized to extend standard computer graphics techniques to the domain of refractive participating media [S1]. The foundational techniques of Path Tracing (PT) and Bidirectional Path Tracing (BDPT) were originally proposed by Arvo (1995) and Veach and Guibas (1995), respectively [S1]. These techniques are known for combining computational efficiency with physical accuracy, producing intensity estimates that are both unbiased and consistent [S1]. The specific application to refractive radiative transfer addresses the gap where standard linear ray tracing assumptions fail due to spatially varying refractive indices [S1].
