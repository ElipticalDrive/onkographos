# Weighted Next-Fight Estimator

Also called: Integral Formulation of Transmittance Estimators, Next-flight estimator

## Overview
The Weighted Next-Fight Estimator is a Monte Carlo method for estimating transmittance (uncollided radiance) in participating media by modifying the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) to account for losses and a point source of unit radiance [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard delta tracking by explicitly adding an underestimated uncollided contribution at each collision point and using null collision sampling to correct for the initial underestimation, thereby providing an unbiased estimator for the transmittance term in light transport [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** Starting position $\mathbf{x}_0$, direction $\omega$, remaining distance to the point source $d_i$, combined extinction coefficient function $\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}) = \mu_t(\mathbf{x}) + \mu_n(\mathbf{x})$, null collision coefficient function $\mu_n(\mathbf{x})$, and PDF of sampled free paths $p(t)$ [S1].
- **Output:** The estimated transmittance $L(\mathbf{x}_0, \omega)$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- No specific complex data structures are required beyond the coefficient functions $\bar{\mu}$ and $\mu_n$, which are typically accessed via volume lookup or analytical evaluation [G].
- The estimator maintains a running weight product and a sum of scores [G].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. Initialize the Monte Carlo score sum $W = 0$ and the current weight product $w_{\text{prod}} = 1$ [G].
2. Set the current position $\mathbf{x}_{\text{curr}} = \mathbf{x}_0$ and remaining distance $d_{\text{rem}} = d_i$ [G].
3. Sample a free path distance $t$ from the PDF $p(t)$ [S1].
4. Update the current position: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{next}} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{curr}} + t\omega$ [G].
5. Calculate the transmittance from $\mathbf{x}_{\text{curr}}$ to $\mathbf{x}_{\text{next}}$ through the combined medium:
   $$ T_{\text{curr}} = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_s) \, ds\right) $$
   where $\mathbf{x}_s = \mathbf{x}_{\text{curr}} + s\omega$ for $s \in [0, t]$ [S1].
6. Add the underestimated uncollided contribution to the score [S1]. If the path segment does not exceed the remaining distance to the source ($t \le d_{\text{rem}}$), the contribution is:
   $$ \text{score\_add} = T_{\text{curr}} \times w_{\text{prod}} $$
   If the path segment exceeds the remaining distance ($t > d_{\text{rem}}$), the contribution is the transmittance to the source:
   $$ \text{score\_add} = \exp\left(-\int_0^{d_{\text{rem}}} \bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_s) \, ds\right) \times w_{\text{prod}} $$
   Update the total score: $W \leftarrow W + \text{score\_add}$ [G].
7. If the path segment exceeds the remaining distance ($t > d_{\text{rem}}$), terminate the estimator and return $W$ [G].
8. Update the weight product for the next iteration by multiplying by the ratio of null collision to combined extinction at the collision point $\mathbf{x}_{\text{next}}$:
   $$ w_{\text{prod}} \leftarrow w_{\text{prod}} \times \frac{\mu_n(\mathbf{x}_{\text{next}})}{\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{next}})} $$
   [S1]
9. Update the remaining distance: $d_{\text{rem}} \leftarrow d_{\text{rem}} - t$ [G].
10. Update the current position: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{curr}} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{\text{next}}$ [G].
11. Repeat from step 3 [G].

**Formulas:**
The estimator is derived from the modified RTE:
$$ (\omega \cdot \nabla) L(\mathbf{x}, \omega) = -[\mu_t(\mathbf{x}) + \mu_n(\mathbf{x})]L(\mathbf{x}, \omega) + \delta(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_d) + \mu_n(\mathbf{x}) \int_{S^2} \delta(\omega - \bar{\omega}) L(\mathbf{x}, \bar{\omega}) d\bar{\omega} $$
where $\mathbf{x}_d = \mathbf{x}_0 - d\omega$ is the position of the point source of unit radiance [S1].

The integral formulation is:
$$ L(\mathbf{x}, \omega) = \exp\left(-\int_0^{d_i} \bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_s) \, ds\right) + \int_0^{d_i} p(t) \frac{\mu_n(\mathbf{x}_t)}{\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_t)} L(\mathbf{x}_t, \omega) \, dt $$
where $L(\mathbf{x}, \omega)$ is the radiance, $\bar{\mu}$ is the combined extinction coefficient, $d_i$ is the remaining distance to the point source, $p(t)$ is the PDF of sampled free paths, $\mu_n$ is the null collision coefficient, and $\mathbf{x}_t$ is the position at distance $t$ [S1].

The Monte Carlo score for a path $\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_0, \dots, \mathbf{x}_k\}$ where $k$ is the index of the first collision after exceeding distance $d$ from $\mathbf{x}_0$ is:
$$ w(\mathbf{X}) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \exp\left(-\int_0^{d_i} \bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_s) \, ds\right) \prod_{j=1}^i \frac{\mu_n(\mathbf{x}_j)}{\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}_j)} $$
where $w(\mathbf{X})$ is the Monte Carlo score, $k$ is the index of the first collision after exceeding distance $d$ from $\mathbf{x}_0$, $\bar{\mu}$ is the combined extinction coefficient, $\mu_n$ is the null collision coefficient, and $\mathbf{x}_j$ are the collision points [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x})$: Combined extinction coefficient. Must be known or bounded [S1].
- $\mu_n(\mathbf{x})$: Null collision coefficient. Must satisfy $\mu_n(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0$ and $\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}) \ge \mu_t(\mathbf{x})$ [S1].
- $p(t)$: PDF of sampled free paths [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** The integrals $\int \bar{\mu} ds$ must be computed accurately. In heterogeneous media, this may require numerical integration or precomputed lookup tables [G].
- **Boundaries:** The estimator assumes the point source is at a finite distance $d_i$. If $d_i \to \infty$, the transmittance term vanishes, and the estimator may become inefficient or require modification [G].
- **Degenerate Input:** If $\bar{\mu}(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ anywhere along the path, the transmittance is 1, and the weight product update becomes undefined if $\mu_n(\mathbf{x})$ is also 0. Handle this by checking for zero extinction and skipping weight updates or treating it as a vacuum [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def weighted_next_flight_estimator(x0, omega, d_i, mu_bar_func, mu_n_func, p_sample_func, max_steps=1000):
    """
    Estimates transmittance using the Weighted Next-Fight Estimator.
    
    Args:
        x0: Starting position (vector).
        omega: Direction (vector, normalized).
        d_i: Remaining distance to the point source (scalar).
        mu_bar_func: Function returning combined extinction coefficient at position.
        mu_n_func: Function returning null collision coefficient at position.
        p_sample_func: Function sampling free path distance t from PDF p(t).
        max_steps: Maximum number of collision steps to prevent infinite loops.
        
    Returns:
        Estimated transmittance L(x0, omega).
    """
    W = 0.0
    w_prod = 1.0
    x_curr = x0
    d_rem = d_i
    
    for step in range(max_steps):
        # Step 3: Sample free path distance t from PDF p(t)
        t = p_sample_func()
        
        # Step 4: Update current position
        x_next = x_curr + t * omega
        
        # Step 5: Calculate transmittance from x_curr to x_next through combined medium
        # [G] Numerical integration of combined extinction along ray segment.
        # Standard quadrature (e.g., trapezoidal or Simpson's rule) is used.
        num_quad = 16
        step_size = t / num_quad
        integral_val = 0.0
        for i in range(num_quad):
            s = (i + 0.5) * step_size
            x_s = x_curr + s * omega
            integral_val += mu_bar_func(x_s) * step_size
        T_curr = exp(-integral_val)
        
        # Step 6: Add underestimated uncollided contribution to score
        if t <= d_rem:
            score_add = T_curr * w_prod
        else:
            # Transmittance to the source
            step_size_src = d_rem / num_quad
            integral_val_src = 0.0
            for i in range(num_quad):
                s = (i + 0.5) * step_size_src
                x_s = x_curr + s * omega
                integral_val_src += mu_bar_func(x_s) * step_size_src
            T_to_source = exp(-integral_val_src)
            score_add = T_to_source * w_prod
        W += score_add
        
        # Step 7: If path exceeds remaining distance, terminate
        if t > d_rem:
            break
            
        # Step 8: Update weight product for next iteration
        mu_n_next = mu_n_func(x_next)
        mu_bar_next = mu_bar_func(x_next)
        
        if mu_bar_next > 0:
            w_prod *= (mu_n_next / mu_bar_next)
        # If mu_bar_next == 0, weight product remains unchanged (vacuum)
            
        # Step 9 & 10: Update remaining distance and current position
        d_rem -= t
        x_curr = x_next
        
    return W
```

## Subtopics

### [Sampling Proportional to Transmittance](14989-sampling-proportional-to-transmittance.md)
Sampling Proportional to Transmittance is a technique for generating free-flight distances distributed according to the transmittance function $T(t)$, rather than the standard extinction-weighted distribution. It solves the problem of needing to sample from $T(t)$ for unbiased inverse volume rendering estimators without requiring explicit evaluation of the normalization constant $C = \int_0^{t_s} T(t') dt'$, which is generally intractable. It sets itself apart from standard delta tracking by producing samples proportional to $T(t)$ directly, whereas standard delta tracking produces samples proportional to $\sigma_t(t)T(t)$ (the probability of the *first* interaction). It builds on [Weighted Next-Fight Estimator](9226-weighted-next-fight-estimator.md) by providing the specific sampling distribution required for the uncollided radiance term.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The complexity depends on the number of collisions $k$ before exceeding the distance $d$ [G]. In homogeneous media, $k$ is small. In highly heterogeneous media with many null collisions, $k$ can be large, leading to higher computational cost [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory, assuming the coefficient functions are accessible in $O(1)$ time [G].
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources [S1].
- **Scaling:** Performance degrades in media with high variance in extinction coefficients, as the number of null collisions increases [G].
- **Strengths:** Provides an unbiased estimator for transmittance. Integrates naturally with null scattering methods [G].
- **Limitations:** Can suffer from high variance if the null collision coefficient is not chosen optimally. Requires accurate evaluation of the transmittance integral [G].
- **When to use:** When estimating uncollided radiance in participating media, especially in conjunction with null scattering methods [G].
- **When not to use:** When the medium is homogeneous and simple analytical solutions are available, or when the computational cost of evaluating the transmittance integral is prohibitive [G].

## Background
The Weighted Next-Fight Estimator was presented by Cramer in 1978 [S1]. This document claims it is the first derivation of the next-flight estimator directly from a modified version of the RTE and its first description in computer graphics literature [S1]. It is used in volume rendering for estimating transmittance, which is a key component in solving the radiative transfer equation for participating media [S1].
