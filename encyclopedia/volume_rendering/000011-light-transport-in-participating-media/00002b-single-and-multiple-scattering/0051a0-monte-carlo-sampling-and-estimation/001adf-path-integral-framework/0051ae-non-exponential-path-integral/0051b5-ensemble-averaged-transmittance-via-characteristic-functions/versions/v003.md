# Ensemble-averaged transmittance via characteristic functions

Also called: CF-based transmittance, Probabilistic ensemble-averaging

## Overview
This method computes the ensemble-averaged transmittance of non-exponential participating media by modeling the line-averaged micro-scale density as a random variable and evaluating its characteristic function (CF) [S1]. It solves the problem of calculating light transport in media with spatial correlations (non-exponential decay) without the computational cost of instantiating discrete particles [S1]. Unlike the [Non-exponential transport kernel via macroscopic optical depth](14530-non-exponential-transport-kernel-via-macroscopic-optical-dep.md) which relies on macroscopic optical depth approximations, this approach derives closed-form transmittance directly from the probability distribution of the density fluctuations [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Distance $t$, medium parameters $\psi = \{\mu_\sigma, C, \beta\}$, and the probability density function (PDF) of the line-averaged density $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ [S1].
*   **Output:** Ensemble-averaged transmittance $\text{ff}(t)$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Parameter Vector $\psi$:**
    *   $\mu_\sigma$: Mean extinction coefficient [S1].
    *   $C$: Constant controlling the overall amplitude of variation [S1].
    *   $\beta$: Spectral exponent ($-1 \le \beta \le 1$) controlling spatial correlations [S1].
*   **Random Variable:** $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$, representing the line-averaged micro-scale density over distance $t$ [S1].

**Steps**
1.  **Model Density:** Define the micro-scale density field $\sigma_\mu(\mathbf{x})$ as a stochastic process, typically a 3D fractal where 1D transects are characterized by $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise [S1].
2.  **Determine PDF:** Determine the probability density function $\text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t)$ for the line-averaged density $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ given distance $t$ and parameters $\psi$ [S1].
3.  **Identify Characteristic Function:** Identify the characteristic function $\varphi_{\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(r)$ corresponding to the PDF from Step 2 [S1].
4.  **Evaluate Transmittance:** Evaluate the characteristic function at the imaginary argument $r = ti$ to obtain the closed-form transmittance [S1]:
    $$ \text{ff}(t) = \varphi_{\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(ti) $$
    where $i = \sqrt{-1}$ [S1].

**Formulas**
The ensemble-averaged transmittance is the expectation of the exponential attenuation weighted by the PDF of the line-averaged density [S1]:
$$ \text{ff}(t) = \left\langle e^{-t\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)} \right\rangle = \int_0^\infty e^{-t\bar{\sigma}_\mu} \text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t) \, d\bar{\sigma}_\mu \quad (30) $$
By Jensen's inequality, this value is greater than or equal to the transmittance of the mean density:
$$ \left\langle e^{-t \overline{\sigma}_\mu(t)} \right\rangle \geq e^{-t \langle \overline{\sigma}_\mu(t) \rangle} \quad (29) $$
Equality holds only if the medium is homogeneous ($\sigma_\mu(t)$ is constant) [S1].

The characteristic function of a random variable $X$ is defined as:
$$ \varphi_X(r) = \langle e^{irX} \rangle $$
Comparing Equation (30) and the definition of the characteristic function, the ensemble-averaged transmittance is the CF evaluated at $ti$ [S1]:
$$ \text{ff}(t) = \varphi_{\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(ti) \quad (32) $$

**Parameters**
*   $\mu_\sigma$: Mean extinction coefficient [S1].
*   $C$: Amplitude constant [S1].
*   $\beta$: Spectral exponent, ranging from $-1$ (blue noise) to $1$ (pink noise) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Homogeneous Medium:** If the medium has no density fluctuations, the ensemble average equals the standard exponential transmittance [S1].
*   **Tabulation Complexity:** While this method avoids discrete particle instantiation, parametric control can be cumbersome due to the need for tabulation of the CF or PDF for different distances $t$ [S1].
*   **Distribution Choice:** The method requires a distribution with a well-known closed-form characteristic function [S1].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def ensemble_averaged_transmittance(t, psi, characteristic_function):
    """
    Computes ensemble-averaged transmittance using the characteristic function.
    
    Args:
        t (float): Distance along the ray.
        psi (dict): Medium parameters {mu_sigma, C, beta}.
        characteristic_function (callable): Function phi(r) returning the CF value.
        
    Returns:
        float: Ensemble-averaged transmittance ff(t).
    """
    # The ensemble-averaged transmittance is the characteristic function
    # of the line-averaged density evaluated at imaginary argument ti.
    # r = t * i
    imaginary_arg = t * 1j
    
    # Evaluate the characteristic function at the imaginary argument
    ff_t = characteristic_function(imaginary_arg)
    
    # The result is a complex number; the transmittance is the real part
    # (assuming the PDF is real and symmetric or the CF properties hold)
    # Note: In practice, numerical stability should be checked.
    return ff_t.real
```
*Note: The specific form of `characteristic_function` depends on the chosen PDF (e.g., Gamma, Gaussian) as detailed in the Subtopics.*

## Subtopics

### [Gamma-distributed 1/f^β noise transmittance model](14552-gamma-distributed-1-f-noise-transmittance-model.md)
Computes ensemble-averaged transmittance by modeling line-averaged micro-scale density as a Gamma-distributed random variable. It ensures strictly non-negative extinction, making it suitable for heterogeneous media with low mean extinction relative to variance.

### [Gaussian 1/f^β noise transmittance model](14555-gaussian-1-f-noise-transmittance-model.md)
Uses a Gaussian distribution for density fluctuations. It offers a simpler analytic solution but allows negative extinction values, which is physically inconsistent.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the evaluation of the characteristic function. Closed-form CFs allow for $O(1)$ evaluation per distance $t$ [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it does not require storing discrete particle positions or large lookup tables for micro-scale realizations [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Avoids the complexity of instantiating billions of discrete particles; provides closed-form solutions for transmittance; naturally handles non-exponential decay tails [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Parametric control can be cumbersome due to tabulation needs; requires the density distribution to have a known closed-form characteristic function [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When rendering participating media with spatial correlations (non-exponential decay) and a closed-form PDF for the line-averaged density is available [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the density distribution does not have a known closed-form characteristic function or when high-fidelity discrete particle effects are required [S1].

## Background
This method was introduced in the context of a radiative transfer framework for non-exponential media [S1]. It builds on the observation that ensemble-averaged transmittance can be viewed as the expectation of the exponential attenuation over the probability distribution of the line-averaged density [S1]. The approach leverages the mathematical properties of characteristic functions to derive closed-form solutions, avoiding the need for stochastic sampling of micro-scale density fields [S1]. The specific application to $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise follows the work of Davis and Mineev-Weinstein [S1].
