# Ensemble-averaged transmittance via characteristic functions

Also called: CF-based transmittance, Probabilistic ensemble-averaging

## Overview
This method computes the ensemble-averaged transmittance of non-exponential media by modeling the line-averaged micro-scale density as a random variable and evaluating its characteristic function (CF) [S1]. It solves the problem of computing light transport in heterogeneous media with spatial correlations without instantiating billions of discrete particles, providing a closed-form alternative to tabulation-based approaches [S1]. Unlike the [Non-exponential transport kernel via macroscopic optical depth](14530-non-exponential-transport-kernel-via-macroscopic-optical-dep.md) which relies on macroscopic optical depth approximations, this method explicitly derives the transmittance from the probability distribution of the line-averaged extinction coefficient [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Distance $t$ along the ray segment.
*   Medium parameters $\psi = \{\mu_\sigma, C, \beta\}$, where $\mu_\sigma$ is the mean extinction coefficient, $C$ controls the amplitude of variation, and $\beta$ is the spectral exponent [S1].
*   The probability density function (PDF) of the line-averaged micro-scale density $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$, derived from the chosen stochastic model (e.g., $1/f^\beta$ noise) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   The ensemble-averaged transmittance $\text{ff}(t)$ for a boundary-to-boundary segment of length $t$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   Parameter vector $\psi$: A tuple or struct containing three floating-point values: `mu_sigma` (mean extinction), `C` (amplitude constant), and `beta` (spectral exponent) [S1].
*   Characteristic Function $\varphi$: A mathematical function mapping a complex argument to a complex value, specific to the chosen PDF of $\bar{\sigma}_\mu$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Model Micro-Scale Density:** Define the micro-scale density field $\sigma_\mu(\mathbf{x})$ as a random process, typically modeled as a 3D fractal where 1D transects are characterized by $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise [S1]. The parameter $\beta$ controls spatial correlations: $\beta=0$ is uncorrelated white noise, $\beta>0$ is positively correlated, and $\beta<0$ is negatively correlated [S1].
2.  **Determine Line-Averaged PDF:** Determine the probability density function $\text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t)$ for the line-averaged density $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ over distance $t$ given parameters $\psi$ [S1]. The variance of this distribution is controlled by $\beta$, with lower $\beta$ leading to more rapid decay of variance with distance [S1].
3.  **Identify Characteristic Function:** Identify the characteristic function $\varphi_{\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(r)$ corresponding to the PDF from Step 2 [S1]. The characteristic function is defined as the Fourier transform of the PDF: $\varphi_X(r) = \langle e^{irX} \rangle$ [S1].
4.  **Evaluate Transmittance:** Evaluate the characteristic function at the imaginary argument $r = ti$ to obtain the closed-form transmittance $\text{ff}(t)$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
The ensemble-averaged transmittance is the expectation of the exponential attenuation weighted by the PDF of the line-averaged density:
$$ \text{ff}(t) = \left\langle e^{-t\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)} \right\rangle = \int_0^\infty e^{-t\bar{\sigma}_\mu} \text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t) \, d\bar{\sigma}_\mu \quad (30) $$
Jensen's inequality states that for a random variable $X$ and a concave function $f$, $\langle f(X) \rangle \geq f(\langle X \rangle)$ [S1]. Substituting the exponential for $f$ and $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ for $X$ yields:
$$ \left\langle e^{-t \overline{\sigma}_\mu(t)} \right\rangle \geq e^{-t \langle \overline{\sigma}_\mu(t) \rangle} \quad (29) $$
This inequality becomes an equality only if $\sigma_\mu(t)$ is constant for all $t$, meaning the medium has no density fluctuations [S1]. The integral in Equation (30) is equivalent to the characteristic function $\varphi$ of the random variable $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ evaluated at $ti$:
$$ \text{ff}(t) = \varphi_{\overline{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(ti) \quad (32) $$
where $i = \sqrt{-1}$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\mu_\sigma$: Mean extinction coefficient.
*   $C$: Constant controlling overall amplitude of variation.
*   $\beta$: Spectral exponent, ranging from $-1$ (blue noise) to $1$ (pink noise) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Homogeneous Limit:** If $\sigma_\mu(t)$ is constant (no density fluctuations), the inequality in (29) becomes an equality, and the method reduces to standard exponential transmittance [S1].
*   **Negative Extinction:** Standard Gaussian approximations for the PDF may allow negative extinction values, which are physically invalid. Variants like the Gamma-distributed model are required to ensure strictly non-negative extinction [S1].
*   **Parametric Control:** While avoiding discrete particle instantiation, parametric control can be cumbersome if the CF does not have a simple closed form or requires tabulation [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cf_transmittance(t: float, mu_sigma: float, C: float, beta: float) -> float:
    """
    Computes ensemble-averaged transmittance by evaluating the characteristic function at r = t * i.
    """
    # The PDF of the line-averaged density depends on the chosen stochastic model.
    # The sources do not provide a single universal PDF; variants use Gamma or Gaussian distributions.
    # The exact mapping from psi and t to distribution parameters is not given in the sources.
    
    # Evaluate the characteristic function at r = t * i.
    # For a Gamma distribution with shape k and scale theta: phi(r) = (1 - i*r*theta)^(-k)
    # For a Gaussian distribution with mean m and variance v: phi(r) = exp(i*r*m - 0.5*r^2*v)
    # We compute the CF for a Gamma distribution as a concrete example.
    shape = 1.0
    scale = 1.0
    r = t * 1j
    cf_value = (1 - r * scale) ** (-shape)
    
    return cf_value.real
```

## Subtopics

### [Gamma-distributed 1/f^β noise transmittance model](14552-gamma-distributed-1-f-noise-transmittance-model.md)
Computes ensemble-averaged transmittance by modeling line-averaged micro-scale density as a Gamma-distributed random variable. It ensures strictly non-negative extinction coefficients, making it suitable for heterogeneous media with low mean extinction relative to variance.

### [Gaussian 1/f^β noise transmittance model](14555-gaussian-1-f-noise-transmittance-model.md)
Uses a Gaussian distribution for line-averaged extinction derived from $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise. It offers a simpler analytic solution but allows negative extinction values, which is a physical inconsistency.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per segment if the characteristic function has a closed-form expression [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Avoids the complexity of instantiating billions of discrete particles; provides closed-form solutions for transmittance; naturally handles spatial correlations via the spectral exponent $\beta$ [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Parametric control can be cumbersome if closed-form CFs are not available; requires careful selection of PDF to avoid unphysical negative extinction values [S1].
*   **When to use:** When rendering non-exponential media with spatial correlations and a closed-form CF is available for the chosen density distribution [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When the medium's density distribution does not have a well-known characteristic function or when high-fidelity discrete particle effects are required [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of a radiative transfer framework for non-exponential media [S1]. It builds on the observation that ensemble-averaged transmittance can be viewed as the expectation of the exponential attenuation over the probability distribution of the line-averaged density [S1]. The approach leverages the mathematical properties of characteristic functions to derive closed-form solutions, avoiding the need for stochastic sampling of individual scatterers [S1]. The modeling of micro-scale density as $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise follows the work of Davis and Mineev-Weinstein [S1].
