# Monte Carlo Integration

Also called: MCRT Estimation, Monte Carlo Estimation, Progressive MCRT Estimation

## Overview
Monte Carlo Integration is a technique to estimate integrals by evaluating them multiple times at random points weighted by their probability density function (pdf) and averaging the results [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of computing high-dimensional integrals in the Path Integral Framework for light transport where analytical solutions are unavailable [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from deterministic quadrature methods by its dimension-independent convergence rate of $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ regardless of function smoothness, making it the standard approach for rendering equations [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A function $f(x)$ to integrate over a domain, a probability density function $p(x)$ from which samples are drawn, and an integer $N$ representing the number of samples [S2, unofficial].
- Output: An estimate $F_N$ of the integral $\int f(x) dx$ [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No complex data structures are required. The method relies on a sequence of $N$ independent random samples $X_i$ and the evaluation of $f(X_i)$ and $p(X_i)$ [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Generate $N$ independent random samples $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_N$ from the domain, distributed according to the pdf $p(x)$ [S2, unofficial].
2. For each sample $X_i$, evaluate the function value $f(X_i)$ and the probability density $p(X_i)$ [S2, unofficial].
3. Compute the importance sampling ratio for each sample: $w_i = \frac{f(X_i)}{p(X_i)}$ [S2, unofficial].
4. Average the ratios to obtain the estimator: $F_N = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{f(X_i)}{p(X_i)}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The integral is estimated as the expected value of the ratio $f(x)/p(x)$:
$$ \int f(x) dx = E \left[ \frac{f(x)}{p(x)} \right] = \int \frac{f(x)}{p(x)} p(x) dx \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{f(X_i)}{p(X_i)} \quad [S2, unofficial] $$
The estimator $F_N$ is defined as:
$$ F_N = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{f(X_i)}{p(X_i)} \quad [S2, unofficial] $$
where $I_i$ can also denote the estimate for a specific pixel or path component $i$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $N$: The number of samples taken. The estimator $F_N$ is a random variable whose values depend on $N$ [S2, unofficial]. Typical convergence is measured using Normalized Root Mean Squared (NRMS) error, with 10% NRMS reached in a fraction of a second for typical datasets [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
- If $p(x) = 0$ where $f(x) \neq 0$, the ratio is undefined. The support of $p(x)$ must cover the support of $f(x)$ [G].
- High variance in $f(x)/p(x)$ leads to noisy estimates, requiring larger $N$ for convergence [S1].
- Progressive rendering allows convergence monitoring, unlike frame-based DVR [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def monte_carlo_integration(f, sample_generator, N):
    """
    Estimates the integral of f using Monte Carlo integration.
    
    Args:
        f: A callable function f(x) representing the integrand.
        sample_generator: A callable that returns a tuple (x, p) where x is a sample
                          and p is the probability density p(x) at x.
        N: Integer, the number of samples to draw.
        
    Returns:
        float: The estimated integral value.
    """
    total = 0.0
    for _ in range(N):
        x, p = sample_generator()
        if p == 0:
            continue
        total += f(x) / p
    return total / N
```

## Subtopics

### [Monte Carlo Estimator of Edge Integrals](10285-monte-carlo-estimator-of-edge-integrals.md)
This method estimates the edge integrals that arise from discontinuities in radiance across boundary curves in participating media. It distinguishes itself from standard volumetric path tracing by explicitly handling the discontinuities in surface emission and scattering that occur when a ray intersects a boundary curve or sharp edge, ensuring unbiased estimation of the radiance jump[10299-monte-carlo-estimator-of-radiance-change.md].

### [Residual Color Estimation Scheme](10577-residual-color-estimation-scheme.md)
The Residual Color Estimation Scheme is a Monte Carlo integration technique designed to reduce estimation bias in Fourier coefficient calculation caused by nonuniform sampling. It sets itself apart from the standard [Monte Carlo Integration](9784-monte-carlo-integration.md) by explicitly subtracting the estimated direct current (DC) component from the signal before integration, and from the [Monte Carlo Estimator of Edge Integrals](10285-monte-carlo-estimator-of-edge-integrals.md) by focusing on spectral coefficient bias rather than edge integral estimation.

### Monte Carlo Estimation of Derivatives
This variant provides Monte Carlo estimates for path integrals involving BSSRDF and BRDF lobes [S3]. For a path integral with BSSRDF, the estimate is $\langle I_3 \rangle = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\hat{f}(p_0 p_1 p_2 p_3)}{p(y_1)p(y_2)}$ [S3]. For a path integral with BRDF, the estimate is $\langle I_2 \rangle = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\hat{f}(p_0 p_1 p_2)}{p(y_1)}$ [S3].

### Truncated Ellipsoid Primitive Projected Area Estimation
An efficient Monte Carlo estimator to calculate the projected area of the intersection of a cube and an ellipsoid [S4, unofficial]. It uniformly samples points on the visible ellipsoid by sampling on a unit disk, lifting to the upper hemisphere, warping via Cholesky decomposition of the inverse ellipsoid matrix block, and transforming to world space [S4, unofficial]. A fixed sample budget of $N=16$ is used in practice [S4, unofficial]. An early-exit optimization skips the intersection test if the ellipsoid is fully contained by the cube or vice versa [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of samples. Each sample requires one evaluation of $f(x)$ and $p(x)$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary space, assuming samples are generated on the fly [S2, unofficial].
- **Convergence:** The rate of convergence is $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ in any dimension, regardless of the smoothness of the function to be integrated [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Dimensionality independence; simplicity of implementation; unbiased estimation [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Slow convergence rate compared to deterministic methods in low dimensions; high variance if the pdf $p(x)$ is not well-matched to $f(x)$ [S1].
- **Performance:** Progressive rendering allows convergence monitoring, with 10% NRMS reached in a fraction of a second for typical datasets [S1].

## Background
Monte Carlo integration is a fundamental technique in computer graphics, particularly for solving the rendering equation [S2, unofficial]. The path integral derivation of the famous rendering equation (Kajiya 1986) is evaluated using Monte Carlo integration [S2, unofficial]. It is widely used in volume rendering processes, including absorption, scattering, and emission, to solve the radiance transfer equation and volume rendering equation [S2, unofficial].
