# Beam × Beam 1D estimate

Also called: 1D kernel density estimation, Beam × Beam 1D, Beam × Beam 2D, Beam × Beam 2D1 estimate, Photon Density Estimation, Transient 1D Kernel Density Estimation

## Overview
The Beam × Beam 1D estimate is a radiance estimation technique for participating media that combines a camera beam with a photon beam to estimate in-scattered light without ray marching [S1, S2]. It solves the problem of efficiently computing volumetric light transport by merging nearby subpath endpoints and applying a 1D spatial blur, offering better image quality than photon points while being computationally simpler than the 3D variant [S1]. The 1D variant is preferred for implementation simplicity compared to the 2D and 3D Beam × Beam estimators [S1].

## How it works
The method estimates radiance by merging the endpoints of a light subpath (photon beam) and a camera subpath (camera beam) [S2]. It assumes the use of "long" beams, where the beam represents a continuous trajectory rather than a discrete point [S2]. The core operation is a 1D kernel density estimation along the camera ray [S3].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1. **Photon beam endpoint** $\mathbf{b}$ and direction $\omega_a$ [S2].
2. **Camera beam endpoint** $\mathbf{c}$ and direction $\omega_d$ [S2].
3. **Medium properties:** Scattering coefficient $\mu_s$ and phase function $\rho(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$ [S2, S3].
4. **Subpath terms:** Transmittance $T_r(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})$ and $T_r(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d})$ from the light and camera subpaths, and distance PDFs $p_{\tau,s-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})$ and $p_{\tau,t-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{z}})$ [S2].
5. **Kernel:** Normalized 1D kernel function $K_1$ [S2].

**Outputs:**
* Estimated radiance $\langle I \rangle_{B-B1D,s}$ or $L_{b|1D}$ at the query point [S2, S3].

### Data Structures
* **Beam:** Stores position, direction, length, and flux [S2, S3].
* **Kernel:** A normalized 1D function $K_1(u)$ where $u$ is the distance along the ray [S2].

### Numbered Steps
1. **Identify Endpoints:** Let $\mathbf{b}$ be the endpoint of the photon beam (light subpath) and $\mathbf{c}$ be the endpoint of the camera beam (camera subpath) [S2].
2. **Calculate Geometry:** Compute the distance $d_{bc} = \|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{c}\|$ and the angle $\theta_{ad}$ between the photon beam direction $\omega_a$ and the camera beam direction $\omega_d$ [S2].
3. **Evaluate Kernel:** The spatial blur is performed over a line, spreading beam energy on the ray at a single point from a single point of the beam [S3]. Evaluate the normalized 1D kernel $K_1$ at the distance between the endpoints [S2].
4. **Compute Radiance Estimate:** Apply the steady-state estimator formula [S2]:
   $$ \langle I \rangle_{B-B1D,s} = T_r(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) \frac{\rho(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})}{\sin \theta_{ad}} T_r(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d}) $$
   For the transient formulation, the temporal function reduces to a Dirac delta $\delta(t - t_b)$, yielding [S3]:
   $$ L_{b|1D}(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_c, t) = K_{1D}(R_b) \Phi_b \rho(\boldsymbol{\theta}_b) \mu_s \frac{e^{-\mu_s t_b} e^{-\mu_s s_r}}{\sin \theta_b} \delta(t - t_b) $$
5. **Weight by PDF:** Divide the estimator value by the probability density function to obtain the unbiased radiance contribution [S2]. The PDF for the B-B1D estimator is [S2]:
   $$ P_{B-B1D,s} = \frac{\sin \theta_{ad}}{p_{\tau,s-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})p_{\tau,t-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{z}})} $$

### Formulas and Symbols
* $\langle I \rangle_{B-B1D,s}$: Radiance estimator for the Beam-Beam 1D case with $s$ light subpath vertices [S2].
* $T_r(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$: Transmittance between points $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ [S2].
* $\rho(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$: Phase function value for scattering from direction $\mathbf{b}$ to $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].
* $K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$: Normalized 1-dimensional kernel evaluated at the distance between $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].
* $\theta_{ad}$: Angle between the photon beam direction $\omega_a$ and camera beam direction $\omega_d$ [S2].
* $p_{\tau,s-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})$: PDF of the distance sampled on the light subpath [S2].
* $p_{\tau,t-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{z}})$: PDF of the distance sampled on the camera subpath [S2].
* $\Phi_b$: Total flux of the photon beam [S3].
* $\mu_s$: Scattering coefficient [S3].
* $t_b$: Time of arrival of the photon beam [S3].
* $\delta(\cdot)$: Dirac delta function [S3].

### Parameters
* **Kernel Radius ($R_b$):** Determines the spatial extent of the blur. Not given in the sources.
* **Beam Length:** Assumed to be "long" beams [S2]. Specific lengths depend on the scene and sampling strategy.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
* **Beam-Point Estimator:** The Beam-Point estimator is excluded from consideration because it is stated to be too costly to evaluate [S2].
* **Singularity:** The term $\sin \theta_{ad}$ in the denominator approaches zero if the photon and camera beams are parallel, potentially causing numerical instability [G]. Implementers should clamp or handle this case.
* **Temporal Precision:** In the transient formulation, the Dirac delta function $\delta(t - t_b)$ implies that the estimate is non-zero only at the exact arrival time of the photon beam [S3].

### Pseudocode
```python
def beam_beam_1d_estimate(T_ab, b, c, omega_a, omega_d, rho, K1, T_cd, p_tau_light, p_tau_camera):
    """
    Estimates radiance using the Beam x Beam 1D method.
    
    Args:
        T_ab: Transmittance T_r(a, b) from light subpath
        b: Photon beam endpoint (3D vector)
        c: Camera beam endpoint (3D vector)
        omega_a: Photon beam direction (unit vector)
        omega_d: Camera beam direction (unit vector)
        rho: Phase function value rho(b, c)
        K1: Normalized 1D kernel function K1(dist)
        T_cd: Transmittance T_r(c, d) from camera subpath
        p_tau_light: Distance PDF p_{tau,s-1}^->(y) w.r.t Euclidean length
        p_tau_camera: Distance PDF p_{tau,t-1}^->(z) w.r.t Euclidean length
        
    Returns:
        radiance: Unbiased radiance contribution
    """
    # Compute distance between endpoints
    dx, dy, dz = b[0]-c[0], b[1]-c[1], b[2]-c[2]
    dist_bc_sq = dx*dx + dy*dy + dz*dz
    dist_bc = dist_bc_sq**0.5
    
    # Compute angle between beams
    cos_theta = omega_a[0]*omega_d[0] + omega_a[1]*omega_d[1] + omega_a[2]*omega_d[2]
    sin_theta = (1.0 - cos_theta**2)**0.5
    
    # Handle singularity when beams are parallel
    if sin_theta < 1e-6:
        return 0.0
        
    # Evaluate 1D kernel
    kernel_val = K1(dist_bc)
    
    # Compute estimator numerator from [S2]
    numerator = T_ab * (rho * kernel_val / sin_theta) * T_cd
    
    # Compute PDF denominator from [S2]
    denominator = sin_theta / (p_tau_light * kernel_val * p_tau_camera)
    
    # Return unbiased radiance contribution
    return numerator / denominator
```

## Subtopics

### [Steady-State Nonlinear Beam Radiance Estimation](16479-steady-state-nonlinear-beam-radiance-estimation.md)
A technique for estimating radiance along curved beam trajectories in continuously refractive (heterogeneous) participating media. It solves the problem of accurately computing in-scattering light transport when the medium's refractive index varies spatially, which causes light paths to curve. Unlike its sibling [Beam × Beam 1D estimate](4776-beam-beam-1d-estimate.md), which assumes straight-line propagation, this method integrates transmittance and scattering along numerically computed curved ray trajectories, accounting for the $n^{-2}$ term in the radiative transfer equation. It builds on [Beam × Beam 1D estimate](4776-beam-beam-1d-estimate.md) by extending the beam query to handle non-linear paths.

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of photon beams in the neighborhood of the camera ray, assuming efficient spatial indexing (e.g., BVH or grid) for beam queries [G]. The 1D kernel evaluation is $O(1)$ per beam [S1].
* **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the storage of photon beams. Each beam requires storing position, direction, length, and flux [S2].
* **Performance:** The 1D variant is significantly faster than the 3D variant because it avoids expensive 3D convolution [S1]. It provides better image quality than photon points, avoiding over-blurring and noise associated with point-based reconstructions [S1].
* **Strengths:** Implementation simplicity, good image quality, and efficient handling of area light sources [S1].
* **Limitations:** Assumes straight-line propagation (unlike the nonlinear variant) [S1]. The Beam-Point estimator is too costly and thus not used [S2].

## Background
The Beam × Beam 1D estimate is part of the Photon Density Estimation (PDE) framework for volumetric light transport [S2]. It was introduced as a practical alternative to the more complex 3D Beam × Beam estimate, which requires 3D convolution [S1]. The method builds on the theoretical foundation of photon beams and density estimation in participating media [S1, S2].
