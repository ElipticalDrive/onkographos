# Ensemble-averaged transmittance via characteristic functions

Also called: CF-based transmittance, Probabilistic ensemble-averaging

## Overview
This method computes the ensemble-averaged transmittance of non-exponential participating media by treating the line-averaged micro-scale density as a random variable and evaluating its characteristic function (CF). It solves the problem of modeling light transport in heterogeneous media with spatial correlations, where the standard exponential Beer-Lambert law fails due to density fluctuations [S1]. Unlike [Non-exponential transport kernel via macroscopic optical depth](14530-non-exponential-transport-kernel-via-macroscopic-optical-dep.md), which relies on macroscopic optical depth approximations, this approach derives closed-form transmittance directly from the probability density function (PDF) of the density fluctuations, ensuring physical consistency with Jensen's inequality for concave attenuation functions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Distance $t$ along the ray.
- Medium parameters $\psi = \{\mu_\sigma, C, \beta\}$, where $\mu_\sigma$ is the mean extinction coefficient, $C$ controls the amplitude of variation, and $\beta$ is the spectral exponent of the fractal noise [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Ensemble-averaged transmittance $\text{ff}(t)$.

**Data Structures:**
- The method assumes the micro-scale density $\sigma_\mu(x)$ is modeled as a 3D fractal process with 1D transects characterized by $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise [S1].
- The line-averaged density $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ is treated as a random variable with a specific PDF $\text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t)$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1. **Define the density field:** Model the micro-scale density $\sigma_\mu(x)$ as a random process, specifically a fractal noise field with spectral exponent $\beta$ [S1].
2. **Determine the PDF:** Identify the probability density function $\text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t)$ for the line-averaged density $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$ over distance $t$ given parameters $\psi$ [S1].
3. **Identify the Characteristic Function:** Find the characteristic function $\varphi_{\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(r)$ corresponding to the PDF from Step 2. The characteristic function is defined as the Fourier transform of the PDF:
   $$ \varphi_X(r) = \langle e^{irX} \rangle $$
   where $X$ is the random variable and $i = \sqrt{-1}$ [S1].
4. **Evaluate Transmittance:** Evaluate the characteristic function at the imaginary argument $r = ti$ to obtain the closed-form transmittance:
   $$ \text{ff}(t) = \varphi_{\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(ti) $$
   [S1].

**Formulas:**
The ensemble-averaged transmittance is the expectation of the exponential attenuation weighted by the PDF of the line-averaged density:
$$ \text{ff}(t) = \left\langle e^{-t\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)} \right\rangle = \int_0^\infty e^{-t\bar{\sigma}_\mu} \text{pd}(\bar{\sigma}_\mu \mid t) \, d\bar{\sigma}_\mu \quad (30) $$
[ S1 ].

Due to Jensen's inequality, for a random variable $X$ and concave function $f$, $\langle f(X) \rangle \geq f(\langle X \rangle)$. Substituting the exponential function and $\bar{\sigma}_\mu(t)$, the ensemble-averaged transmittance is always greater than or equal to the transmittance of the mean density, with equality only if the medium is homogeneous ($\sigma_\mu(t)$ is constant) [S1]:
$$ \left\langle e^{-t \overline{\sigma}_\mu(t)} \right\rangle \geq e^{-t \langle \overline{\sigma}_\mu(t) \rangle} \quad (29) $$
[ S1 ].

The equivalence to the characteristic function allows for closed-form solutions if the PDF has a known CF [S1]:
$$ \text{ff}(t) = \varphi_{\overline{\sigma}_\mu(t)}(ti) \quad (32) $$
[ S1 ].

**Parameters:**
- $\mu_\sigma$: Mean extinction coefficient [S1].
- $C$: Constant controlling the overall amplitude of variation [S1].
- $\beta$: Spectral exponent, ranging from $-1$ (blue noise) to $1$ (pink noise), controlling spatial correlations. $\beta=0$ corresponds to uncorrelated white noise [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Homogeneous Limit:** If the medium has no density fluctuations, the inequality becomes an equality, reducing to the standard exponential transmittance [S1].
- **Tabulation Complexity:** While avoiding the instantiation of discrete particles, parametric control can be cumbersome due to the need for tabulation of the CF or PDF for different distances $t$ [S1].
- **Distribution Choice:** The method requires a distribution with a well-known characteristic function. The specific PDF depends on the noise model (e.g., Gaussian vs. Gamma) used for the fractal medium [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Gamma-distributed 1/f^β noise transmittance model](14552-gamma-distributed-1-f-noise-transmittance-model.md)
Computes ensemble-averaged transmittance by modeling line-averaged density as a Gamma-distributed random variable. It ensures strictly non-negative extinction, suitable for heterogeneous media with low mean extinction relative to variance.

### [Gaussian 1/f^β noise transmittance model](14555-gaussian-1-f-noise-transmittance-model.md)
Uses a Gaussian distribution for density fluctuations. It offers a simpler analytic solution but allows negative extinction values, which is physically inconsistent.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The evaluation is $O(1)$ if the characteristic function has a closed-form expression. Otherwise, it depends on the complexity of evaluating the specific CF.
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on whether the CF is pre-tabulated or computed on the fly. The sources note that parametric control can be cumbersome due to tabulation needs [S1].
- **Strengths:** Provides closed-form solutions for non-exponential media; physically consistent with Jensen's inequality; avoids discrete particle instantiation [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires the PDF of the line-averaged density to have a known characteristic function; parametric control can be complex [S1].
- **When to Use:** When modeling participating media with spatial correlations (fractal noise) where the standard exponential law is insufficient, and a closed-form transmittance is desired [S1].

## Background
This method was introduced by Jensen et al. in "A radiative transfer framework for non-exponential media" (2019) [S1]. It builds on the work of Davis and Mineev-Weinstein [2011] regarding fractal media [S1]. The approach models micro-scale density variability using $1/f^\beta$ fractal noise, where $\beta$ determines the roughness and spatial correlations of the noise [S1]. The key insight is the link between ensemble-averaged transmittance and the characteristic function of the density distribution, allowing for efficient computation of non-exponential light transport [S1].
