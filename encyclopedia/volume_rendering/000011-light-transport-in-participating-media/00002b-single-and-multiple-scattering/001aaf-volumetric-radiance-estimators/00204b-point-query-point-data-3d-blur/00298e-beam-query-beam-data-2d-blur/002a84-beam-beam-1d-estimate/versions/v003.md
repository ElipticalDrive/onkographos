# Beam × Beam 1D estimate

Also called: 1D kernel density estimation, Beam × Beam 1D, Beam × Beam 2D, Beam × Beam 2D1 estimate, Photon Density Estimation, Transient 1D Kernel Density Estimation

## Overview
The Beam × Beam 1D estimate is a radiance estimation technique for participating media that combines a camera subpath with a light subpath by finding nearby endpoints and merging them, resulting in one less vertex than the sum of vertices of the subpaths [S2]. It avoids the expensive 3D convolution required by the 3D variant and the ray marching associated with point-based methods, offering better image quality than photon points while being computationally simpler to implement [S1]. It is a specific case of Photon Density Estimation (PDE) where both the light and camera data are represented as beams, and the spatial blur is performed over a line (1D) [S2, S3].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  **Light Subpath:** A sequence of vertices sampled from the light, ending at vertex $\mathbf{a}$ (denoted as $\mathbf{y}_{s-2}$ in [S2]). The ray from $\mathbf{a}$ in direction $\omega_a$ defines the **photon beam** [S2].
2.  **Camera Subpath:** A sequence of vertices sampled from the camera, ending at vertex $\mathbf{d}$ (denoted as $\mathbf{z}_{t-2}$ in [S2]). The ray from $\mathbf{d}$ in direction $\omega_d$ defines the **camera beam** [S2].
3.  **Photon Beam Endpoint:** A point $\mathbf{b}$ sampled along the photon beam ray at distance $s_b$ from $\mathbf{a}$ (denoted as $\mathbf{y}_{s-1}$ in [S2]).
4.  **Camera Beam Endpoint:** A point $\mathbf{c}$ sampled along the camera beam ray at distance $s_c$ from $\mathbf{d}$ (denoted as $\mathbf{z}_{t-1}$ in [S2]).
5.  **Beam Properties:**
    *   $\Phi_b$: Photon beam power/flux [S3].
    *   $\rho(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$: Phase function value for scattering from $\mathbf{b}$ to $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].
    *   $\mu_s$: Scattering coefficient of the medium [S3].
    *   $t_b$: Time of flight associated with the photon beam [S3].
    *   $R_b$: Kernel radius for the 1D spatial blur [S3].
    *   $\theta_{ad}$: Angle between the photon beam direction $\omega_a$ and the camera beam direction $\omega_d$ [S2].

**Outputs:**
*   Estimated radiance $L$ at the connection point (or along the ray) for a specific time $t$ [S3].

### Data Structures
*   **Photon Beam:** Defined by origin $\mathbf{a}$, direction $\omega_a$, and stored properties ($\Phi_b, \rho, \mu_s, t_b$) [S2, S3].
*   **Camera Beam:** Defined by origin $\mathbf{d}$, direction $\omega_d$ [S2].
*   **Kernel:** A normalized 1-dimensional kernel function $K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$ or $K_{1D}(R_b)$ [S2, S3].

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Sample Light Subpath:** Generate a light subpath up to vertex $\mathbf{a}$ [S2]. Define the photon beam ray originating at $\mathbf{a}$ with direction $\omega_a$ [S2].
2.  **Sample Camera Subpath:** Generate a camera subpath up to vertex $\mathbf{d}$ [S2]. Define the camera beam ray originating at $\mathbf{d}$ with direction $\omega_d$ [S2].
3.  **Find Nearby Endpoints:** Identify a photon beam endpoint $\mathbf{b}$ on the photon beam ray and a camera beam endpoint $\mathbf{c}$ on the camera beam ray [S2]. These points are considered "nearby" if they fall within the kernel support [S2].
4.  **Calculate Geometric Distances:** Calculate the distance $s_b$ from $\mathbf{a}$ to $\mathbf{b}$, the distance $s_c$ from $\mathbf{d}$ to $\mathbf{c}$, and the distance between $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ [S2]. In the 1D case, the spatial blur is performed over the line connecting the beams [S3]. The distance metric $R_b$ refers to the spatial blur radius [S3].
5.  **Evaluate Transmittance:** Calculate transmittance $T_r(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})$ from $\mathbf{a}$ to $\mathbf{b}$, and transmittance $T_r(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d})$ from $\mathbf{c}$ to $\mathbf{d}$ [S2].
6.  **Evaluate Kernel:** Compute the 1D kernel value $K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$ based on the distance between $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].
7.  **Compute PDF:** Calculate the probability density function (PDF) for the sampling of the distances $s_b$ and $s_c$ [S2]. Let $p_{\tau,s-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})$ be the PDF for the light subpath distance sampling and $p_{\tau,t-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{z}})$ be the PDF for the camera subpath distance sampling [S2].
8.  **Calculate Radiance Estimate:** Combine the terms using the estimator formula [S2].

### Formulas
The radiance estimate $\langle I \rangle_{B-B1D,s}$ for a light subpath with $s$ vertices is given by [S2]:

$$
\langle I \rangle_{B-B1D,s} = T_r(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) \frac{\rho(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}) K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})}{\sin \theta_{ad}} T_r(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d})
$$

Where:
*   $T_r(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})$ is the transmittance from $\mathbf{a}$ to $\mathbf{b}$ [S2].
*   $\rho(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$ is the phase function evaluated at the scattering event from $\mathbf{b}$ to $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].
*   $K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c})$ is a normalized 1-dimensional kernel [S2].
*   $\theta_{ad}$ is the angle between the photon beam direction $\omega_a$ and the camera beam direction $\omega_d$ [S2].
*   $T_r(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d})$ is the transmittance from $\mathbf{c}$ to $\mathbf{d}$ [S2].

The corresponding PDF $P_{B-B1D,s}$ is given by [S2]:

$$
P_{B-B1D,s} = \frac{\sin \theta_{ad}}{p_{\tau,s-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{y}}) K_1(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}) p_{\tau,t-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{z}})}
$$

Where:
*   $p_{\tau,s-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})$ is the distance PDF with respect to Euclidean length for the light subpath [S2].
*   $p_{\tau,t-1}^{\rightarrow}(\bar{\mathbf{z}})$ is the distance PDF with respect to Euclidean length for the camera subpath [S2].

For the transient case, the temporal function reduces to a Dirac delta function [S3]:

$$
W_{1D}(t - t_b) = \delta(t - t_b)
$$

The full radiance estimate including temporal and beam properties is [S3]:

$$
L_{b|1D}(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_c, t) = K_{1D}(R_b) \Phi_b \rho(\boldsymbol{\theta}_b) \mu_s \frac{e^{-\mu_s t_b} e^{-\mu_s s_r}}{\sin \theta_b} \delta(t - t_b)
$$

Where:
*   $K_{1D}(R_b)$ is the 1D kernel with radius $R_b$ [S3].
*   $\Phi_b$ is the photon beam power [S3].
*   $\rho(\boldsymbol{\theta}_b)$ is the phase function [S3].
*   $\mu_s$ is the scattering coefficient [S3].
*   $t_b$ is the time of the photon beam [S3].
*   $s_r$ is the distance along the ray [S3].
*   $\theta_b$ is the angle related to the beam geometry [S3].

### Parameters
*   **Kernel Radius ($R_b$):** Not given in the sources.
*   **Beam Length:** Long beams are assumed for the estimators [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Beam-Point Estimator:** The Beam-Point estimator is not included in the standard set because it is stated to be too costly to evaluate [S2].
*   **3D Convolution:** The 3D variant is expensive due to 3D convolution requirements [S1]. The 1D variant avoids this [S1].
*   **Over-blurring:** Compared to photon points, the 1D estimate avoids over-blurring and noise associated with point-based reconstructions [S1].
*   **Angle Singularity:** The term $\sin \theta_{ad}$ in the denominator of the PDF and estimator suggests potential instability if the photon and camera beams are parallel ($\theta_{ad} \approx 0$ or $\pi$) [S2]. A guard is required to prevent division by zero [G].

### Pseudocode

```python
import math

def phase_function_eval(direction):
    """Standard isotropic phase function evaluation [G]."""
    return 1.0 / (4.0 * math.pi)

def beam_beam_1d_estimate(photon_beam, camera_beam, photon_endpoint, camera_endpoint, p_light, p_camera):
    """
    Computes the Beam x Beam 1D radiance estimate.
    
    Args:
        photon_beam: Object containing origin 'a', direction 'omega_a', 
                     power 'Phi_b', scattering_coeff 'mu_s', time 't_b', kernel_radius 'R_b'.
        camera_beam: Object containing origin 'd', direction 'omega_d'.
        photon_endpoint: Point 'b' on the photon beam ray.
        camera_endpoint: Point 'c' on the camera beam ray.
        p_light: PDF for light subpath distance sampling p_{tau,s-1}^{->}(y_bar) [S2].
        p_camera: PDF for camera subpath distance sampling p_{tau,t-1}^{->}(z_bar) [S2].
        
    Returns:
        radiance_estimate: Float value of the radiance estimate.
        pdf: Float value of the probability density function.
    """
    
    # 1. Geometric Setup
    a = photon_beam.origin
    d = camera_beam.origin
    omega_a = photon_beam.direction
    omega_d = camera_beam.direction
    b = photon_endpoint
    c = camera_endpoint
    
    # 2. Distances
    dist_ab = math.sqrt(sum((b[i] - a[i])**2 for i in range(3)))
    dist_dc = math.sqrt(sum((c[i] - d[i])**2 for i in range(3)))
    dist_bc = math.sqrt(sum((b[i] - c[i])**2 for i in range(3)))
    
    # 3. Transmittance
    # T_r(a, b) and T_r(c, d) using exponential attenuation [S2, S3]
    sigma_t = photon_beam.mu_s
    T_ab = math.exp(-sigma_t * dist_ab)
    T_cd = math.exp(-sigma_t * dist_dc)
    
    # 4. Phase Function
    # rho(b, c) is the phase function for scattering from b to c [S2]
    dir_bc = [(c[i] - b[i]) / dist_bc for i in range(3)]
    rho_val = phase_function_eval(dir_bc)
    
    # 5. Kernel
    # K_1(b, c) is the normalized 1D kernel [S2]
    R_b = photon_beam.kernel_radius
    if dist_bc <= R_b:
        K_1 = 1.0 / (2.0 * R_b)
    else:
        K_1 = 0.0
    
    # 6. Angle between beams
    # theta_ad is the angle between omega_a and omega_d [S2]
    cos_theta = sum(omega_a[i] * omega_d[i] for i in range(3))
    cos_theta = max(-1.0, min(1.0, cos_theta))
    theta_ad = math.acos(cos_theta)
    sin_theta_ad = math.sin(theta_ad)
    
    # 7. Estimate
    # Check for singularity [G]
    if sin_theta_ad < 1e-6:
        return 0.0, 0.0
        
    estimate = T_ab * (rho_val * K_1 / sin_theta_ad) * T_cd
    
    # 8. PDF for MIS
    # P_{B-B1D,s} formula [S2]
    pdf = sin_theta_ad / (p_light * K_1 * p_camera)
    
    return estimate, pdf
```

## Subtopics

### Beam × Beam 2D estimate
Uses a 2D kernel for spatial blur. More complex than 1D but potentially more accurate. [Link](4556-beam-beam-2d-estimate.md)

### Beam × Beam 3D estimate
Uses a 3D kernel. Most accurate but computationally expensive due to 3D convolution. [Link](4557-beam-beam-3d-estimate.md)

### Photon Density Estimation (PDE)
The general framework combining camera and light subpaths. Beam × Beam 1D is a specific instance of PDE. [Link](4554-photon-density-estimation.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of photon beams and camera rays. Avoids the $O(N^3)$ or expensive convolution of the 3D variant [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of stored photon beams.
*   **Performance:** Significantly faster than the 3D variant and more efficient than point-based methods for similar image quality, especially with area lights [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Avoids ray marching, better image quality than photon points, simpler implementation than 3D variant [S1].
*   **Limitations:** 1D blur may introduce bias if the beam geometry is complex. Potential singularity when beams are parallel [S2].
*   **When to Use:** When computational efficiency is critical and 3D convolution is too expensive. When better quality than photon points is needed [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When high accuracy is required and 3D convolution is feasible. When beams are likely to be parallel [S1, S2].

## Background
The Beam × Beam 1D estimate is part of the Photon Density Estimation (PDE) framework, which combines camera and light subpaths [S2]. It was developed as part of the Unified Points, Beams, and Paths (UPBP) method [S2]. The 1D variant was chosen for its implementation simplicity and efficiency compared to the 3D variant [S1]. It builds on the work of Jarosz et al. on kernel density estimation for photon beams [S3].
