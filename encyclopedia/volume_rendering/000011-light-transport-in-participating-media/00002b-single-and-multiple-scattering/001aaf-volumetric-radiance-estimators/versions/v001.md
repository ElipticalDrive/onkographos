# Volumetric Radiance Estimators

Also called: B-B1D, B-B2D, B-B3D, B-P2D, B-P3D, P-B2D, P-B3D, P-P3D

## Overview
Volumetric radiance estimators are a family of nine distinct methods introduced by Jarosz et al. [2011a] that estimate in-scattered radiance in participating media by aggregating discrete samples (photons or photon beams) using spatial blurring kernels [S1]. They solve the problem of reconstructing continuous equilibrium radiance from discrete photon data, differing from unbiased path integral estimators by lacking a general path contribution function and pdf, instead relying on specific query-data pairings and kernel dimensions [S1]. They are distinguished from siblings like [Delta tracking](985-delta-tracking.md) by their reliance on pre-computed photon maps and kernel-based density estimation rather than stochastic ray marching through the medium.

## Shared principles
All estimators in this family share the following mechanism and mathematical structure:

1.  **Data Representation**: The equilibrium radiance is represented by discrete samples stored in a photon map. These samples are either **Photon Points** (individual photons with position, power, and incident direction) or **Photon Beams** (extended segments of light transport).
2.  **Query Type**: The estimation is performed at a specific location, which can be queried as a **Point** (a single 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}$) or as a **Beam** (a segment or line).
3.  **Kernel Blurring**: To reconstruct the continuous radiance from discrete samples, a normalized $d$-dimensional kernel $K_d$ is applied. The dimension $d$ determines the shape of the blur:
    *   $d=1$: Line kernel.
    *   $d=2$: Square/planar kernel.
    *   $d=3$: Cube/spherical kernel.
4.  **Scattering Function $\rho$**: The contribution of a sample is weighted by a scattering function $\rho$ evaluated at the query location. The definition of $\rho$ depends on whether the query location is on a surface or in the medium [S1]:
    $$
    \rho(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) = \begin{cases} 
    \rho_s(\mathbf{x}_{i-1} \rightarrow \mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j \rightarrow \mathbf{x}_{j+1}) & \mathbf{x}_j \text{ on surface} \\ 
    \rho_p(\mathbf{x}_{i-1} \rightarrow \mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j \rightarrow \mathbf{x}_{j+1}) \sigma_s(\mathbf{x}_j) & \mathbf{x}_j \text{ in medium}
    \end{cases}
    $$
    Where $\mathbf{x}_i$ is the sample location and $\mathbf{x}_j$ is the query location. Note that the scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$ is folded into $\rho$ for medium queries [S1].
5.  **Naming Convention**: The family uses a specific naming convention: `[Data]-[Query][KernelDim]`.
    *   **Data**: `P` for Photon Points, `B` for Photon Beams.
    *   **Query**: `P` for Point query, `B` for Beam query.
    *   **KernelDim**: `1D`, `2D`, or `3D`.
    *   Example: `B-P3D` means Beam Data, Point Query, 3D Kernel. This convention opposes the original convention used by Jarosz et al. [2011a] [S1].
6.  **Long vs. Short Beams**:
    *   **Long Beams ($B_l$)**: Extend until the closest surface, with transmittance included in the estimator [S1].
    *   **Short Beams ($B_s$)**: Finite extent beams where transmittance is replaced by a constant 1 (step function approximation), also known as "progressive deep shadow maps" [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Point Query × Point Data, 3D Blur](2235-point-query-point-data-3d-blur.md)
[Point Query × Point Data, 3D Blur](2235-point-query-point-data-3d-blur.md) estimates in-scattered radiance at a specific query point by aggregating the energy of the $n$ closest photons within a spherical kernel. It distinguishes itself by using point-based data and 3D blur, averaging photon power over the volume of the query region. Choose this when high-quality reconstruction of in-scattering from discrete photons is required and computational cost for spatial search is acceptable.

### [Extended Balance Heuristic](5601-extended-balance-heuristic.md)
[Extended Balance Heuristic](5601-extended-balance-heuristic.md) weights estimates from a mixture of sampling techniques (unbiased path sampling and extended space volumetric estimators) to reduce variance. It is derived from the balance heuristic. Choose this when combining multiple estimators to minimize overall variance in the final image.

### Short Beam Estimator via Russian Roulette
A technique to convert a long beam estimator into a short beam estimator by treating the beam length sampling as a Russian Roulette (RR) decision on the contribution [S1].
*   **Step**: Make a zero contribution whenever the beam length $l$ is less than the distance to the query point $t$. Otherwise, divide the original long beam estimator result by the RR probability $\Pr\{l > t\}$ [S1].
*   **Formula**: The probability that a short beam of length $l$ contributes at distance $t_0$ is $\Pr\{l > t_0\} = \int_{t_0}^{\infty} p(t') dt'$, where $p(t)$ is the pdf used to sample the beam length [S1]. The short beam estimator is calculated as $\langle I \rangle_{\text{short}} = \langle I \rangle_{\text{long}} \frac{H(l - t)}{\Pr\{l > t\}}$, where $H$ is the Heaviside step function [S1].
*   **Comparison**: This approach provides an intuitive explanation and generalization of Jarosz et al.'s [2011b] unbiased progressive deep shadow maps [S1].

## Background
The family of volumetric radiance estimators was introduced by Jarosz et al. in 2011 [S1]. The original paper [2011a] presented nine distinct estimators that differed in the representation of equilibrium radiance, the radiometric quantities estimated, and the dimension of blur used to reconstruct the desired quantity from samples. These estimators were evaluated on segments along light and eye subpaths. A subsequent paper [2011b] introduced "progressive deep shadow maps," an unbiased approximation of transmittance using step functions, which corresponds to the "short beam" variant of these estimators. The framework was later unified with unbiased path tracing methods (like BPT) and other sampling techniques in the Unified Points, Beams, and Paths (UPBP) algorithm [S1]. These methods are primarily used in volumetric path tracing and photon mapping contexts to efficiently estimate in-scattered radiance in participating media.
