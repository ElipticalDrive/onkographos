# Transient Photon Beams

Also called: PTPB, Progressive Spatio-Temporal Beam Density Estimation, Progressive Transient Photon Beams

## Overview
Transient Photon Beams is a numerical method for rendering participating media in the transient state, extending the steady-state Photon Beam algorithm by accounting for photon time-of-flight along continuous beam trajectories [S1, S2]. It solves the time-resolved Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) by combining photon beam tracing with 2D density estimation to mitigate variance in the temporal domain [S1]. Unlike standard kernel density estimation which remains biased even with infinite photons, the Progressive variant uses a spatio-temporal kernel reduction scheme to converge to the correct solution with finite memory [S2]. It differs from [Photon Beam Diffusion](3768-photon-beam-diffusion.md) by preserving the full directional and temporal structure of light transport rather than diffusing it, and from [Photon Marching](4775-photon-marching.md) by using continuous beam segments for density estimation rather than discrete point interactions [S1, S2].

## How it works
The method consists of two main phases: Beam Tracing (Pass 1) and Ray Tracing with Density Estimation (Pass 2) [S1]. A progressive variant iteratively refines the result by reducing kernel bandwidths [S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Scene geometry and participating media properties (extinction $\sigma_t$, scattering $\sigma_s$, index of refraction $\eta_m$) [S1, S2].
*   Light source emission function $L_e$ [S2].
*   Camera ray $R_b$ defined by origin $\mathbf{x}_r$ and direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_r$ [S1].
*   Number of iterations $n$ (for progressive mode) [S2].
*   Kernel parameters: spatial radius $R_b$, temporal width $\mathcal{T}$, and convergence parameters $\alpha, \beta_R, \beta_T$ [S2].

**Outputs:**
*   Transient radiance $L(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r, t)$ at the camera for specific time instances $t$ [S2].

### Data Structures
**Photon Beam Map:**
A map storing photon beams. Each beam $b$ contains:
*   $\mathbf{x}_b$: Position (origin of the beam segment).
*   $\boldsymbol{\omega}_b$: Direction (unit vector).
*   $\Phi_b$: Power (flux) of the beam.
*   $t_{b_0}$: Starting time of the beam (time of creation/emission).
*   $s_{max}$: Length of the beam segment (distance to next interaction or scene boundary).

### Numbered Steps

#### Pass 1: Beam Tracing
1.  **Initialize Light Subpath:** Start a random walk from the light source. Define a light subpath $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_l = \mathbf{x}_0 \dots \mathbf{x}_k$, where $\mathbf{x}_0$ is at the light source and $k$ is the number of vertices [S2].
2.  **Trace Interactions:** For each vertex $\mathbf{x}_j$ in the subpath (where $j > 0$):
    *   Compute the time delay $\Delta t$ from the previous vertex $\mathbf{x}_{j-1}$ to $\mathbf{x}_j$. For a medium with constant index of refraction $\eta_m$:
        $$ \Delta t(\mathbf{x}_{j-1} \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_j) = \frac{\eta_m}{c} \|\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_{j-1}\| $$
        where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum [S2].
    *   Compute the beam creation time $t_{b_j}$:
        $$ t_{b_j} = \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} \tau_i + \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} \Delta t(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_{i+1}) $$
        where $\tau_i$ represents any additional scattering delays (e.g., fluorescence) [S2].
    *   Define the photon beam $b_j$ originating at $\mathbf{x}_{b_j} = \mathbf{x}_j$ with direction:
        $$ \boldsymbol{\omega}_{b_j} = \frac{\mathbf{x}_{j+1} - \mathbf{x}_j}{\|\mathbf{x}_{j+1} - \mathbf{x}_j\|} $$
    *   Compute the beam power $\Phi_{b_j}$:
        $$ \Phi_{b_j} = \frac{L_e(\mathbf{x}_0 \to \mathbf{x}_j, \tau_0) T(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j, \bar{\tau}_j)}{M \prod_{i=0}^{j} p(\mathbf{x}_i, \tau_i)} $$
        where $M$ is the total number of photon random walks, $p$ is the probability of sampling the vertex, and $T$ is the throughput:
        $$ T(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j, \bar{\tau}_j) = \left[ \prod_{i=1}^{j-1} \rho(\mathbf{x}_i, \tau_j) \right] \left[ \prod_{i=0}^{j-1} G(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_{i+1}) V(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_{i+1}) \right] $$
        with $\rho$ as the phase function, $G$ as the geometry term, and $V$ as the visibility term [S2].
    *   Store beam $b_j$ in the map.

#### Pass 2: Ray Tracing & Density Estimation (Standard)
1.  **Trace Camera Ray:** For a camera ray $\mathbf{x}_r + s_r \boldsymbol{\omega}_r$, intersect it against the photon beam map to find all intersecting beams $b \in R_b$ [S1].
2.  **Compute Intersection Limits:** For each beam $b$, determine the ray-beam intersection interval $[s_r^-, s_r^+]$ and the corresponding beam interval $[s_b^-, s_b^+]$ [S1].
3.  **Calculate Time Intervals:** Compute the temporal footprint of the beam relative to the ray intersection:
    *   Time at beam start: $t_{b_0}$ (from Pass 1).
    *   Time delay from beam start to intersection point $s_{b_i}$:
        $$ \Delta t(s_{b_0} \leftrightarrow s_{b_i}) = \frac{\eta_m}{c} s_{b_i} $$
    *   Time delay from intersection point $s_{r_i}$ to camera:
        $$ \Delta t(s_{r_i} \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_r) = \frac{\eta_m}{c} s_{r_i} $$
    *   The total time interval $[t^-, t^+]$ over which radiance is distributed is:
        $$ t^- = t_r^- + t_b^- $$
        $$ t^+ = t_r^+ + t_b^+ $$
        where $t_r^\pm$ and $t_b^\pm$ are the light travel times corresponding to the limits $[s_r^-, s_r^+]$ and $[s_b^-, s_b^+]$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Radiance Contribution:** Calculate the integrated radiance $L_b$ for the beam using the 2D density estimator for homogeneous media:
    $$ L_b(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r) = \frac{\mu_s}{\Omega_R(r^2)} \rho(\theta_B) \Phi_B \int_{s_r^-}^{s_r^+} T_r(s_r) T_b(s_B(s_r)) ds_r $$
    This integral has a closed-form solution:
    $$ L_b = \frac{e^{-\mu_s(s_B^- + s_r^-)} - e^{-\mu_s(s_B^- + \cos \theta_B(s_r^+ - s_r^-) + s_r^+)}}{\mu_s \cos \theta_B} $$
    where $\mu_s$ is the scattering coefficient, $\Omega_R(r^2)$ is the 2D kernel area, $\rho$ is the phase function, $\theta_B$ is the angle between beam and ray directions, and $T$ are transmittances [S1].
5.  **Distribute Radiance:** Evenly distribute the integrated radiance $L_b$ across the temporal interval $[t^-, t^+]$ for the corresponding pixel in the time domain [S1].
6.  **Sum Contributions:** The total radiance is the sum of contributions from all intersecting beams:
    $$ L(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r) \approx \sum_{b \in R_b} L_b(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r) $$
    [S1].

#### Progressive Variant (Spatio-Temporal)
To reduce bias and achieve consistency, the method iterates $n$ times, accumulating results with progressively reduced kernel bandwidths [S2].

1.  **Initialize Estimator:**
    $$ \hat{L}_n(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r, t) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^n \sum_{b \in B_i} L_b(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r, t) $$
    where $B_i$ is the set of photon beams from iteration $i$ [S2].
2.  **Spatio-Temporal Kernel:** Use a 1D spatial kernel $K_{1D}(R_b)$ and a temporal kernel $K_{\mathcal{T}}(t - t_b)$:
    $$ L_{b|1D}(\mathbf{x}_r, \boldsymbol{\omega}_r, t) = K_{1D}(R_b) \Phi_b \rho(\theta_b) \mu_s \frac{e^{-\mu_s s_b} e^{-\mu_s s_r}}{\sin \theta_b} K_{\mathcal{T}}(t - t_b) $$
    [S2].
3.  **Update Kernel Bandwidths:** For iteration $j+1$, reduce the spatial radius $R_b$ and temporal width $\mathcal{T}$:
    $$ \frac{R_{b|j+1}}{R_{b|j}} = \left( \frac{j+\alpha}{j+1} \right)^{\beta_R} $$
    $$ \frac{\mathcal{T}_{j+1}}{\mathcal{T}_j} = \left( \frac{j+\alpha}{j+1} \right)^{\beta_{\mathcal{T}}} $$
    where $\beta_{\mathcal{T}} = 1 - \beta_R$ [S2].
4.  **Optimal Parameters:** For optimal asymptotic convergence rate $O(n^{-2/3})$, use $\alpha = 2/3$ and $\beta_{\mathcal{T}} = 1/2$ (thus $\beta_R = 1/2$) [S2].

    return (eta_m / c) * distance

def compute_throughput(scattering_events, geometry_terms, visibility_terms):
    prod_rho = 1.0
    for rho in scattering_events:
        prod_rho *= rho
    prod_GV = 1.0
    for G, V in zip(geometry_terms, visibility_terms):
        prod_GV *= (G * V)
    return prod_rho * prod_GV

def compute_beam_power(emission, throughput, M, prob_product):
    return (emission * throughput) / (M * prob_product)

def compute_kernel_radiance_1d(beam, camera_ray, R_b, T, eta_m, c, mu_s, phase_func):
    """
    Computes the spatio-temporal kernel density estimate for a single beam.
    """
    spatial_kernel = math.exp(-0.5 * (R_b ** 2))
    temporal_kernel = math.exp(-0.5 * ((T - beam.t_b0) ** 2))
    cos_theta = dot(beam.direction, camera_ray.direction)
    theta = math.acos(max(-1.0, min(1.0, cos_theta)))
    base_term = beam.power * phase_func(theta) * mu_s * math.exp(-mu_s * beam.length) / math.sin(theta)
    return spatial_kernel * base_term * temporal_kernel

    for beam in beams:
        sr_min, sr_max, sb_min, sb_max = intersect_ray_beam(
            camera_ray.origin, camera_ray.direction,
            beam.origin, beam.direction, beam.length
        )
        if sr_min is None:
            continue
        t_b_min = compute_time_delay(sb_min, eta_m, c)
        t_b_max = compute_time_delay(sb_max, eta_m, c)
        t_r_min = compute_time_delay(sr_min, eta_m, c)
        t_r_max = compute_time_delay(sr_max, eta_m, c)
        t_min = t_r_min + t_b_min
        t_max = t_r_max + t_b_max
        cos_theta = dot(beam.direction, camera_ray.direction)
        theta = math.acos(max(-1.0, min(1.0, cos_theta)))
        s_B_minus = sb_min
        s_r_minus = sr_min
        s_r_plus = sr_max
        exp1 = math.exp(-mu_s * (s_B_minus + s_r_minus))
        exp2 = math.exp(-mu_s * (s_B_minus + cos_theta * (s_r_plus - s_r_minus) + s_r_plus))
        if abs(cos_theta) < 1e-6:
            L_b = 0.0
        else:
            L_b = (exp1 - exp2) / (mu_s * cos_theta)
        time_bin_width = 0.1
        num_bins = int((t_max - t_min) / time_bin_width)
        for k in range(num_bins):
            t_idx = t_min + k * time_bin_width
            radiance_at_times[t_idx] = radiance_at_times.get(t_idx, 0.0) + L_b / num_bins
    return radiance_at_times

### Parameters
*   $r$: Radius of the photon beam, used in the 2D kernel $\Omega_R(r^2)$ [S1].
*   $\eta_m$: Index of refraction of the medium. Typically 1.0 (vacuum) for results, 1.5 for glass [S1, S2].
*   $c$: Speed of light in vacuum [S2].
*   $\alpha$: Parameter controlling variance increase rate per iteration, range $[0, 1]$. Optimal value is $2/3$ [S2].
*   $\beta_R, \beta_T$: Control the reduction ratio of spatial and temporal kernels. Optimal $\beta_T = 1/2$, implying $\beta_R = 1/2$ [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Temporal Bias:** Evenly distributing radiance across the temporal interval $[t^-, t^+]$ introduces temporal bias because photon energy varies due to transmittance across the blur region [S1].
*   **Straight-Line Assumption:** The straight-line distance formula for $\Delta t$ is only valid for constant $\eta$. In media with varying index of refraction, light follows the Eikonal equation, and the straight-line formula is invalid [S2].
*   **Small Temporal Footprint:** The temporal footprint of density estimation might be arbitrarily small, making the probability of finding a beam at a specific time very low [S2].
*   **Standard KDE Bias:** Standard kernel density estimation does not converge to the actual solution even with infinite photons due to bias [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method involves tracing photon random walks (Pass 1) and intersecting camera rays against a beam map (Pass 2) [S1, S2]. The complexity depends on the number of beams $M$ and the number of camera rays [G].
*   **Performance:** Results were rendered on a desktop PC with Intel i7 and 4GB RAM [S1, S2].
*   **Convergence:** The progressive variant has an asymptotic convergence rate of $O(n^{-2/3})$ with optimal parameters [S2]. This is slower than Progressive Transient Path Tracing with temporal KDE ($O(n^{-4/5})$), but presents better temporal behavior with much less variance on later timings and requires fewer iterations to achieve similar MSE [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Robust, consistent, and efficient for transient rendering in participating media. Handles complex caustic light transport. Reduces variance in the temporal domain using spatial continuity [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Introduces temporal bias due to even distribution of radiance. Straight-line time delay assumption limits applicability to constant IOR media without modification [S2].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method was introduced by Julio Marco, Ibón Guillén, Wojciech Jarosz, Diego Gutierrez, and Adrian Jarabo [S2]. It extends the steady-state Photon Beams algorithm [JNSJ11] to the transient state [S1].
*   **Development:** The initial transient extension was presented in 2017 [S1]. A progressive version with optimal convergence rates was later published in 2018 [S2].
*   **Usage:** Implemented for scenes including Armadillo, Cornell blocks, Cornell spheres, Mirrors, Soccer, and Pumpkin [S1, S2].
