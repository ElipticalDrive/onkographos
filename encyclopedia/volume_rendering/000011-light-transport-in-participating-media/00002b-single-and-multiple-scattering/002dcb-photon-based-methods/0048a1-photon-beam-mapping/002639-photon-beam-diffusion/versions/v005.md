# Photon Beam Diffusion

Also called: Photon Beam Diffusion for Rendering

## Overview
Photon Beam Diffusion (PBD) is a hybrid numerical method for rendering subsurface scattering in translucent materials that interprets incident light as a continuous beam of photons inside the material and uses Monte Carlo integration to solve the diffusion equation [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently computing accurate multiple-scattering profiles without the quantization errors or computational overhead of sum-of-Gaussians approximations used in Quantized Diffusion (QD) [S1, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Transient Photon Beams](3296-transient-photon-beams.md) which handles time-dependent effects, or [Photon Marching](4775-photon-marching.md) which marches through media, PBD focuses on steady-state spatial diffusion using an extended source integral, offering faster convergence and numerical stability than standard Photon Diffusion (PD) [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Material optical properties (absorption coefficient $\mu_a$, scattering coefficient $\mu_s$, anisotropy $g$, refractive index $n$), surface geometry, and incident light direction/intensity.
*   **Outputs:** Radiant exitance profile (BSSRDF) for multiple scattering, optionally combined with an exact single-scattering term.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Photon Beams:** Instead of discrete photon points, the method stores continuous, directional photon beams entering the surface. Each beam is defined by its entry point, direction, and weight [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sample Distances:** An exponentially-varying set of distances $x$ is used for integration. The set is defined as $x = 0, x_1, x_1 \cdot 1.2, x_1 \cdot 1.2^2, \dots$ with a step size of 1.2 and $x_1 \approx 0.01/\mu_t$ [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Beam Storage:** During the lighting pass (or photon tracing), store continuous, directional photon beams entering the surface instead of discrete photon points inside the medium [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Monte Carlo Integration Setup:** Formulate the solution as a Monte Carlo integration problem of the extended source integral. This eliminates the need for quantization and sum-of-Gaussians approximations [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Sample Generation:** For each shading point, generate Monte Carlo samples along the stored photon beams. The number of samples required is typically 3 to 5 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use equiangular/exponential MIS sampling for photon beam diffusion to achieve faster multiple scattering convergence, especially at oblique light angles [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Weighted Monopole Evaluation:** View each Monte Carlo sample of the beam integral as a weighted photon (monopole) located at position $\vec{x}_r(t_i)$ inside the medium [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Boundary Condition Mirroring:** Mirror the monopole exactly as in Photon Diffusion (PD) to compute the three boundary conditions (dipole, multipole, quadrupole) as proposed by Donner and Jensen [DJ07]. Blend between these boundary conditions for oblique illumination on arbitrary geometries [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Profile Evaluation:** Evaluate the improved diffusion model at the sample locations using the stored photon beams as extended beam sources [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Single-Scattering Integration (Optional):** Compute a novel diffuse exact single-scattering term.
    *   If the surface separates volumes with different indices of refraction, use a separate sample sequence starting from the critical angle, as contributions below the critical angle are zero [S1, unofficial].
    *   This term accounts for phase function and Fresnel terms explicitly [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Combination:** Add the single-scattering and multi-scattering profiles. They can be computed simultaneously using the same points or rays, adding minimal overhead [S1, unofficial].

    """
    Computes the subsurface scattering profile using Photon Beam Diffusion.
    
    Args:
        shading_point: The point on the surface where shading is computed.
        photon_beams: List of continuous directional photon beams entering the surface.
        material_params: Object containing mu_a, mu_s, g, n, etc.
        
    Returns:
        float: The total radiant exitance (single + multi scattering).
    """
    # 1. Setup exponentially-varying distances for integration
    mu_t = material_params.mu_a + material_params.mu_s
    x1 = 0.01 / mu_t
    step_size = 1.2
    distances = [0.0]
    current_dist = x1
    while current_dist < 10.0 / mu_t: # Arbitrary upper bound for convergence
        distances.append(current_dist)
        current_dist *= step_size

    # 2. Initialize accumulators
    multi_scatter_sum = 0.0
    single_scatter_sum = 0.0
    
    # 3. Iterate over stored photon beams
    for beam in photon_beams:
        # 4. Generate Monte Carlo samples along the beam
        # Typically 3-5 samples are sufficient
        num_samples = 5
        for i in range(num_samples):
            # Sample position along the beam
            t = distances[i % len(distances)]
            sample_pos = beam.entry_point + beam.direction * t
            
            # 5. Evaluate diffusion profile at sample position
            # This involves mirroring the monopole for boundary conditions
            # and evaluating the improved diffusion model
            profile_value = evaluate_improved_diffusion(sample_pos, shading_point, material_params)
            
            # Weight the contribution based on beam weight and MIS
            weight = beam.weight * get_mis_weight(beam, sample_pos)
            multi_scatter_sum += profile_value * weight

    # 6. Compute single-scattering term if needed
    if material_params.include_single_scattering:
        # Use separate sample sequence starting from critical angle if refraction exists
        if material_params.has_refraction:
            critical_angle = math.asin(material_params.n_out / material_params.n_in)
            # Sample from critical angle onwards

        else:
            # Standard single scattering integration
            single_scatter_sum = compute_exact_single_scattering(shading_point, beam.direction, material_params)

    return multi_scatter_sum + single_scatter_sum

**Parameters:**
*   **Number of Monte Carlo samples:** 3 to 5 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Step size for exponentially-varying distances:** 1.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Minimum distance ($x_1$):** $\approx 0.01/\mu_t$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Refraction:** If the surface separates volumes with different indices of refraction, single-scattering contributions below the critical angle are zero. A separate sample sequence starting from the critical angle must be used to guarantee a smooth single-scattering profile [S1, unofficial].
*   **Oblique Illumination:** Requires careful handling of boundary conditions. Blending between dipole, multipole, and quadrupole boundary conditions is necessary for arbitrary geometries [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The method is designed to be numerically stable, avoiding the quantization errors of QD [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Transient Photon Beams
Handles time-dependent light transport in participating media. Choose when simulating time-resolved effects or short-pulse lasers. Link: [Transient Photon Beams](3296-transient-photon-beams.md).

### Photon Marching
Marches photons through the medium to handle complex scattering. Choose when high-order scattering in highly scattering media is required without diffusion approximation. Link: [Photon Marching](4775-photon-marching.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering with PBD and single-scattering typically takes about 15% longer than using the classic dipole diffusion model [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stores continuous photon beams instead of discrete points, offering a more compact representation than storing thousands of photons [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   Speedup factor of 3.1 for a full profile when comparing 5 PBD samples with 44 Gaussians in QD [S1, unofficial].
    *   Speedup of 21.6 when only one sample of a profile is needed [S1, unofficial].
    *   Render time for a human head scene: 7 minutes on a multi-core PC [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** The method scales well with the number of samples, converging faster than Photon Diffusion (PD), especially at oblique light angles [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Faster, more accurate, and numerically stable than Quantized Diffusion (QD). Simpler implementation. Supports finite and multi-layer materials. Directional incident effects.
*   **Limitations:** Assumes the exitant boundary is diffuse (though reshaped by Fresnel). Less suitable for smooth Fresnel boundaries (marble/milk) compared to Jensen et al.'s approach [S1, unofficial]. Multi-layered materials may be faster and more elegant in QD than with explicit integration [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** For translucent materials like skin, wax, or food where accurate subsurface scattering is needed with better performance than QD and more accuracy than dipole diffusion.
*   **When Not to Use:** For materials with smooth Fresnel boundaries (marble, milk) where Jensen et al.'s approach is more appropriate, or for time-dependent effects (use Transient Photon Beams).

## Background
Photon Beam Diffusion was introduced by Ralf Habel, Per H. Christensen, and Wojciech Jarosz in 2013 at the Eurographics Symposium on Rendering [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the improved diffusion model [dI11] and addresses the limitations of previous methods like Quantized Diffusion (QD) and classic dipole diffusion. The method is implemented in research and production rendering systems, including Mitsuba (point-based subsurface scattering), PRMan (point-based and ray-traced subsurface scattering), and on top of a progressive photon beam tracer [S1, unofficial].
