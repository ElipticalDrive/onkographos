# GPU Texture Photon Mapping
Also called: Czuczor et al. GPU Texture Photon Mapping
## Overview
GPU Texture Photon Mapping is a photon-based estimation method that accelerates the photon gathering stage by storing photon collision data in GPU texture memory and utilizing hardware texture filtering to approximate nearest-neighbor searches, thereby replacing the computationally expensive KD-tree construction and traversal [S1]. It is distinct from its siblings because it trades the spatial accuracy of explicit neighbor searching for the speed of texture interpolation, but it is limited to surface rendering and is not effective for volume rendering [S1].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of photons emitted from light sources, where each photon carries position, direction, and energy.
*   **Output:** A rendered image where surface radiance is estimated by gathering nearby photons via texture filtering.

**Data Structures:**
*   **GPU Texture:** A 3D or 2D texture buffer (depending on scene dimensionality) where photon positions are stored. The exact layout is not specified in the sources, but it serves as a spatial hash or grid approximation [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Photon Emission:** Photons are emitted from light sources and traced through the scene. When a photon hits a surface, its interaction data (position, energy, direction) is recorded [S1].
2.  **Texture Storage:** The recorded photon collision information is stored into GPU texture memory. The position of the photon is mapped to texture coordinates [S1].
3.  **Rendering Pass:** During the rendering pass, for each surface point being shaded, the algorithm samples the GPU texture around the point's location [S1].
4.  **Texture Filtering:** Instead of performing a K-nearest neighbor search using a KD-tree, the method uses hardware texture filtering (e.g., bilinear or trilinear interpolation) to gather contributions from nearby photons stored in the texture [S1].
5.  **Radiance Estimation:** The filtered texture values are used to estimate the radiance at the surface point [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Texture Resolution:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Filter Kernel:** The specific type of texture filtering (e.g., bilinear, trilinear) is not specified, though hardware filtering is implied [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Surface Only:** The method is only effective for surface rendering. It is not suitable for volume rendering (participating media) [S1].
*   **Accuracy vs. Speed:** Using texture filtering approximates the nearest-neighbor search, which may introduce smoothing artifacts or inaccuracies compared to exact KD-tree searches [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Voxel Adaptation for Photon Mapping](22441-voxel-adaptation-for-photon-mapping.md)
Voxel Adaptation for Photon Mapping adapts photon mapping for real-time voxel raytracing by decoupling photon gathering from the rendering pass, storing indirect lighting data per-voxel to eliminate per-ray gathering overhead during trace. It differs from standard photon mapping by replacing kd-tree photon storage with a uniform grid and moving the gathering step to an offline pass, trading off estimation accuracy for real-time performance.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The photon gathering step is accelerated by using texture filtering, which is generally faster than KD-tree nearest neighbor searches [S1]. However, the exact complexity is not specified.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires GPU texture memory to store photon data. The size depends on the number of photons and texture resolution, which is not specified [S1].
*   **Performance:** Designed for real-time rendering on surfaces [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Fast photon gathering due to hardware texture filtering; avoids KD-tree construction overhead [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Only effective for surface rendering; not suitable for volume rendering; potential loss of accuracy due to texture filtering approximation [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time surface rendering where speed is prioritized over exact photon gathering accuracy.
*   **When not to use:** Volume rendering or participating media; scenes requiring high-accuracy photon gathering.
## Background
The method was proposed by Czuczor et al. [S1]. It is part of the evolution of photon mapping techniques aimed at improving performance, following earlier work by Havran et al. on reverse photon mapping [S1]. It contrasts with real-time KD-tree approaches by Zhou et al. [S1] and hierarchical methods by Spencer and Jones [S1].
