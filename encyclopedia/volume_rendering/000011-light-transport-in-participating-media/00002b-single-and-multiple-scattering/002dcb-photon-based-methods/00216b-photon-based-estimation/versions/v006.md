# Photon-based Estimation
## Overview
Photon-based estimation methods solve the light transport problem in participating media by decoupling the simulation of light paths from the final image synthesis [G]. Instead of integrating the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) directly via ray marching or path tracing, these methods store photon interactions (positions, directions, and energy) in a spatial data structure and estimate radiance at render time via density estimation [S1]. Density estimation approaches are the most commonly used solutions for participating media rendering, since they support all types of scattering [S1].

## Shared principles
Photon-based estimation relies on the following shared mechanisms and mathematical foundations:

1. **Photon Emission and Tracing:** Photons are emitted from light sources and traced through the scene [G]. When a photon enters a participating medium, it undergoes a random walk [G]. The step size $t$ between interactions is sampled from the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$:
   $$ t = -\frac{\ln(r)}{\sigma_t} $$
   where $r$ is a uniform random number in $[0, 1]$ [G].

2. **Interaction Handling:** At each step, the photon's fate is determined by the ratio of absorption $\sigma_a$ and scattering $\sigma_s$ to the total extinction $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$ [G].
   - **Scattering:** The photon's direction is updated using a phase function $p(\omega_i, \omega_o)$, and its energy is attenuated by the scattering albedo $\omega = \sigma_s / \sigma_t$ [G].
   - **Absorption:** The photon is terminated or its energy is reduced [G].
   - **Null Collisions:** In heterogeneous media, a majorant $\sigma_{\text{max}} \ge \sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$ is used [G]. Steps are sampled using $\sigma_{\text{max}}$, and a null collision occurs with probability $P_{\text{null}} = (\sigma_{\text{max}} - \sigma_t)/\sigma_{\text{max}}$, where the photon continues without interaction but with transmittance adjustment [G].

3. **Storage:** Interactions are stored in a spatial data structure tailored to the specific variant [G]. Implementations use KD-trees for nearest-neighbor queries [G], volume textures for GPU acceleration [2190-volumetric-photon-mapping.md], or bounding volume hierarchies for beam-based representations [2275-photon-beam-storage-in-bvh.md].

4. **Density Estimation (Gathering):** During rendering, for a point $\mathbf{x}$ on a camera ray, the in-scattered radiance $L_s(\mathbf{x})$ is estimated by gathering nearby stored photons [G]. The general form is:
   $$ L_s(\mathbf{x}) \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{P_i}{V_i} K\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i\|}{r}\right) p(\omega_i, \omega_o) $$
   where $N$ is the number of photons in the search radius $r$, $P_i$ is the power of the $i$-th photon, $V_i$ is the volume of influence or a normalization factor, $K$ is a kernel function, and $p(\omega_i, \omega_o)$ is the phase function [G].

5. **Integration:** The estimated in-scattered radiance is integrated along the camera ray using the transmittance $T$:
   $$ L(\mathbf{x}_0, \omega_0) = L_0 T(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_n) + \sum_{j=1}^{n} T(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_{j-1}) \sigma_s(\mathbf{x}_j) L_s(\mathbf{x}_j) \Delta s_j $$
   [G].

```python
def photon_based_estimation_shared_pipeline(scene, camera, num_photons):
    # Phase 1: Photon Emission and Tracing
    photon_map = []
    for _ in range(num_photons):
        photon = emit_photon(scene.light_sources)
        while photon_alive(photon):
            sigma_t = scene.extinction(photon.position)
            step = -math.log(random()) / sigma_t
            photon.position += photon.direction * step
            # Interaction handling (simplified)
            if random() < scene.scattering_albedo(photon.position):
                photon.direction = sample_phase_function(scene.phase, photon.direction)
                photon.energy *= scene.scattering_albedo(photon.position)
            else:
                photon_alive = False
            photon_map.append(photon)
    # Phase 2: Density Estimation and Integration
    image = zeros(camera.resolution)
    for pixel in camera.rays:
        ray = pixel.ray
        total_radiance = 0.0
        t = 0.0
        while t < ray.max_distance:
            sample_pos = ray.origin + ray.direction * t
            gathered_energy = density_estimation(photon_map, sample_pos, search_radius=0.7)
            transmittance = exp(-scene.extinction(sample_pos) * t)
            total_radiance += transmittance * gathered_energy * scene.scattering_coefficient(sample_pos) * step_size
            t += step_size
        image[pixel] = total_radiance
    return image
```

## Subtopics

### [Compute Shader Photon Integration](2111-compute-shader-photon-integration.md)
A GPU-accelerated Monte Carlo method that processes photons in parallel using compute shaders. It differs from traditional photon mapping by directly updating radiance feedback buffers per-frame rather than storing photons in a spatial structure for later density estimation. Suitable for dynamic scenes and real-time feedback.

### [Volumetric Photon Mapping](2190-volumetric-photon-mapping.md)
A global illumination technique that decouples indirect illumination simulation from direct illumination. Solves realistic in-scattering and absorption in complex volumes by storing photon interactions in a spatial data structure and estimating radiance via density estimation. Sets itself apart from beam-based methods by using discrete point-to-point density estimation.

### [Photon Beam Storage in BVH](2275-photon-beam-storage-in-bvh.md)
Organizes photon beams into a Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH) to accelerate spatial queries during volumetric radiance estimation. Solves efficient retrieval of all beams intersecting a specific eye ray. Modifies BVH construction to handle highly overlapping beams by splitting them into sub-beams with unit aspect ratios.

### [Point-Based Light Transport in Participating Media](3763-point-based-light-transport-in-participating-media.md)
A global illumination method for homogeneous translucent materials with refractive boundaries. Decorrelates scene complexity from illumination computation by replacing the scene with a shaded point cloud (volume samples) for indirect illumination. Computes single, double, and multiple scattering contributions separately using precomputed light surface and volume samples, handling refraction at boundaries which breaks screen-space coherence.

### Generalized Photon Surface Estimation
A method by Deng et al. that generalizes photon planes to photon surfaces, introducing estimators like photon cone, cylinder, and sphere, combined with Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) for robustness [S1]. Couples the light and camera subpaths using a delta kernel to avoid bias [S1]. Reduces variance significantly compared to prior work and handles any scattering event, including single scattering [S1]. However, it is unable to handle medium interactions immediately following scattering from a surface, such as volumetric caustics [S1].

## Background
Photon-based methods for participating media originated from the adaptation of photon mapping techniques, originally developed for surface rendering by Jensen [G]. Early applications focused on homogeneous media, but the complexity of heterogeneous media and multiple scattering led to the development of specialized estimators [S1]. The survey by [S1] highlights the evolution from simple photon planes to more complex photon surfaces and beam-based representations to handle singularities and improve variance. The method is widely used in offline rendering for realistic effects like caustics in fog or smoke, and in real-time applications via GPU-accelerated variants like Compute Shader Photon Integration [G].
