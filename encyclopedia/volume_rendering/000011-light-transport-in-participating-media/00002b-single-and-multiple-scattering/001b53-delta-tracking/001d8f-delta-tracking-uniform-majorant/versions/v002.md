# Delta Tracking (Uniform Majorant)

Also called: DeltaTrackingIter<false>, Simple Delta Tracking

## Overview
Delta Tracking (Uniform Majorant) is a simplified Monte Carlo tracking method that assumes a uniform majorant density and total extinction coefficient of 1.0 [S1, unofficial]. It samples the distance to the next interaction directly using the inverse CDF of the exponential distribution, avoiding local extinction evaluations but becoming inefficient when the majorant significantly exceeds the actual extinction in heterogeneous media [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `rng`: Random number generator providing uniform floats in $[0, 1)$.
- `ray_tnear`: Near clipping plane distance.
- `ray_tfar`: Far clipping plane distance.
- `ray_t`: Current accumulated distance along the ray.

**Outputs:**
- `rayt`: Absolute distance to the next interaction point.
- `bool`: `true` if the interaction occurs within $[ray\_tnear, ray\_tfar]$, `false` otherwise.

**Data Structures:**
- Scalar constants only; no complex data structures are required [S1, unofficial].

**Constants:**
- `sigma_t = 1.0`: Total extinction coefficient.
- `majorant = 1.0`: Uniform majorant density/extinction bound.

**Steps:**
1. Initialize constants: Set `sigma_t = 1.0` and `majorant = 1.0` [S1, unofficial].
2. Sample a uniform random number $u$ from $[0, 1)$ using `rng.get_floats().x` [S1, unofficial].
3. Calculate the distance to the next interaction $t$ using the inverse CDF of the exponential distribution:
   $$ t = \frac{-\ln(1.0 - u)}{majorant \cdot \sigma_t} $$
   Since `majorant` and `sigma_t` are 1.0, this simplifies to $t = -\ln(1.0 - u)$ [S1, unofficial].
4. Calculate the absolute ray position:
   $$ rayt = ray\_tnear + t $$
5. Check bounds: Return `true` if $rayt \le ray\_tfar$, otherwise return `false` [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def delta_tracking_uniform_majorant(rng, ray_tnear, ray_tfar, ray_t):
    """
    Simulates a single step in Delta Tracking with a uniform majorant of 1.0.
    """
    sigma_t = 1.0
    majorant = 1.0
    
    u = rng.get_floats().x
    t = -math.log(1.0 - u)
    
    rayt = ray_tnear + t
    hit = rayt <= ray_tfar
    
    return rayt, hit
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Inefficiency in Heterogeneous Media:** The method assumes `majorant` and `sigma_t` are constant at 1.0. It is inefficient for media with varying density or if the majorant is much larger than the actual extinction, leading to many rejected samples [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Exponential Sampling
A strategy for choosing propagation distances in homogeneous media where sampling is exponentially-decreasing with a PDF proportional to the source term $Q(t)$ [S2, unofficial]. It effectively places dipole sources at different depths and weights them according to the inverse exponential PDF, canceling out the exponential variation in $Q(t)$ [S2, unofficial]. The formula is $t_i = \frac{\log(1 - \xi_i)}{-t_0}$ with $pdf_{exp}(t_i) = t_0 e^{-t_0 t_i}$, where $\xi_i \in [0, 1)$ is a uniform random number [S2, unofficial]. This PDF only depends on $t_0$ and is independent of $\vec{x}$ and $\vec{\omega}$ [S2, unofficial]. Exponential sampling guarantees that the tail of the profile converges when taking just 1 sample, but it underestimates the peak near $x=0$ and introduces smoothing [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling:** Does not scale well with heterogeneous media. The uniform majorant assumption makes it unsuitable for scenes with high contrast in extinction coefficients [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Eliminates the need to evaluate the local extinction coefficient at every step [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Highly inefficient for media where the actual extinction is significantly lower than the majorant (fixed at 1.0), implying it only works well if the media's extinction is close to 1.0 everywhere [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** Only when the medium is homogeneous with an extinction coefficient of exactly 1.0, or as a baseline for comparison [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** For heterogeneous volumes or when the extinction coefficient varies significantly from 1.0 [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method is a specific instance of the broader delta tracking family, which was developed to handle participating media with varying density by using a majorant to homogenize the medium [Parent](985-delta-tracking.md). The concept of exponential sampling for homogeneous media is also discussed in the context of photon beam diffusion, where it is contrasted with equiangular sampling [S2, unofficial].
