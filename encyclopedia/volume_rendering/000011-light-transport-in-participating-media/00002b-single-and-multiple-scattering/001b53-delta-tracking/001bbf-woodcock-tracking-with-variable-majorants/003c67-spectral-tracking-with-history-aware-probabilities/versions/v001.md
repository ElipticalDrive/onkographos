# Spectral Tracking with History-Aware Probabilities

Also called: Average-based Spectral Probabilities with Path History, History-Average-Based Spectral Tracking, History-Maximum-Based Spectral Tracking, History-aware Average-based Probabilities

## Overview
Spectral tracking is a method for rendering participating media with spectrally varying coefficients that maintains a single path throughput vector across all wavelengths, suppressing the geometric growth of path throughput and preventing fireflies in chromatic media [S1]. It builds on [Woodcock Tracking with Variable Majorants](1084-woodcock-tracking-with-variable-majorants.md) by incorporating path history into the collision probabilities, ensuring that the average throughput remains equal to 1 regardless of the number of scattering events [S1]. Unlike naive delta tracking which handles wavelengths independently, this method reduces estimation noise and RMSE at the cost of increased render time due to smaller step sizes [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Current position $\mathbf{x}$, direction $\omega$, current throughput vector $\mathbf{w}_D$ of size $N_\lambda$ (one value per wavelength), extinction majorant $\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x})$, and spectral coefficients $\mu_a(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)$, $\mu_s(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)$, $\mu_n(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)$ for absorption, scattering, and non-scattering (e.g., emission or out-scattering) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Updated throughput vector $\mathbf{w}_D$, new position $\mathbf{x}$, new direction $\omega$, or termination signal [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $\mathbf{w}_D$: A vector of length $N_\lambda$, where $N_\lambda$ is the number of sampled wavelengths. Initialized to $(1, \dots, 1)$ [S1].
*   $\mu_a, \mu_s, \mu_n$: Functions mapping $(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)$ to scalar coefficients.

**Parameters:**
*   $N_\lambda$: Number of wavelengths.
*   $\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x})$: A majorant for the total extinction $\mu_t(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) = |\mu_a(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)| + |\mu_s(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)| + |\mu_n(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)|$ across all $\lambda$. It must satisfy $\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x}) \ge \max_\lambda \mu_t(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialize:** Set $\mathbf{w}_D \leftarrow (1, \dots, 1)$ of size $N_\lambda$ [S1].
2.  **Sample Free Path:** Generate a uniform random number $\zeta \in [0, 1)$. Compute the step size $t$:
    $$ t = -\frac{\ln(1 - \zeta)}{\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x})} $$
    [S1]
3.  **Update Position:** Move the ray: $\mathbf{x} \leftarrow \mathbf{x} - t \times \omega$ [S1].
4.  **Evaluate Probabilities:** Calculate collision probabilities $P_a(\mathbf{x})$, $P_s(\mathbf{x})$, and $P_n(\mathbf{x})$ using history-aware average-based or maximum-based schemes.
    *   Let $\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{X}_{j-1}, \lambda)$ be the throughput vector from the previous vertex.
    *   **Average-based (Recommended):**
        $$ P_a(\mathbf{x}) = \text{avg}_\lambda(|\mu_a(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) \mathbf{w}(\mathbf{X}_{j-1}, \lambda)|) \cdot c^{-1} $$
        $$ P_s(\mathbf{x}) = \text{avg}_\lambda(|\mu_s(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) \mathbf{w}(\mathbf{X}_{j-1}, \lambda)|) \cdot c^{-1} $$
        $$ P_n(\mathbf{x}) = \text{avg}_\lambda(|\mu_n(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) \mathbf{w}(\mathbf{X}_{j-1}, \lambda)|) \cdot c^{-1} $$
        where $c = P_a(\mathbf{x}) + P_s(\mathbf{x}) + P_n(\mathbf{x})$ [S1].
    *   **Maximum-based:** Replace $\text{avg}_\lambda$ with $\max_\lambda$ in the numerators above [S1].
5.  **Select Collision Type:** Generate a uniform random number $\xi \in [0, 1)$.
    *   If $\xi < P_a(\mathbf{x})$: **Absorption**. Return the current contribution $\mathbf{w}_D \circ \frac{\mu_a(\mathbf{x})}{\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x}) P_a(\mathbf{x})} \circ L_e(\mathbf{x}, \omega)$ and terminate the path segment [S1]. Note: $\circ$ denotes element-wise multiplication.
    *   Else if $\xi < P_a(\mathbf{x}) + P_s(\mathbf{x})$: **Scattering**. Update throughput:
        $$ \mathbf{w}_D \leftarrow \mathbf{w}_D \circ \frac{\mu_s(\mathbf{x})}{\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x}) P_s(\mathbf{x})} $$
        Sample new direction $\omega$ from the phase function $f_p(\omega)$ [S1].
    *   Else: **Non-scattering**. Update throughput:
        $$ \mathbf{w}_D \leftarrow \mathbf{w}_D \circ \frac{\mu_n(\mathbf{x})}{\bar{\mu}_t(\mathbf{x}) P_n(\mathbf{x})} $$
        Continue to next iteration (or terminate if $\mu_n$ represents out-scattering/termination) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Majorant Underestimation:** Using a free-path-sampling coefficient $\bar{\mu}_t$ that underestimates the true extinction bound (e.g., scaled down by 0.4) causes variance and LTUV to rise abruptly due to positive and negative fireflies, especially in non-absorbing volumes [S1].
*   **Throughput Growth:** In maximum-based schemes, local collision weights can exceed 1, leading to geometric growth of path throughput if multiple such collisions occur [S1]. History-aware average-based probabilities bound the throughput, preventing this [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def spectral_tracking(x, omega, mu_a, mu_s, mu_n, mu_bar_t, N_lambda):
    """
    x: current position
    omega: current direction
    mu_a, mu_s, mu_n: functions returning extinction coefficients for a given (x, lambda)
    mu_bar_t: function returning the majorant extinction at x
    N_lambda: number of wavelengths
    """
    # Initialize throughput vector
    w_D = [1.0] * N_lambda
    
    while True:
        # 1. Sample free path
        zeta = random.uniform(0, 1)
        t = -log(1 - zeta) / mu_bar_t(x)
        
        # 2. Update position
        x = x - t * omega
        
        # 3. Evaluate coefficients for all wavelengths
        # Vectorized operations assumed for lambda dimension
        abs_coeffs = [abs(mu_a(x, l)) for l in range(N_lambda)]
        sca_coeffs = [abs(mu_s(x, l)) for l in range(N_lambda)]
        non_coeffs = [abs(mu_n(x, l)) for l in range(N_lambda)]
        
        # 4. Calculate history-aware average-based probabilities
        # Weight coefficients by current throughput history
        weighted_abs = [abs_coeffs[l] * w_D[l] for l in range(N_lambda)]
        weighted_sca = [sca_coeffs[l] * w_D[l] for l in range(N_lambda)]
        weighted_non = [non_coeffs[l] * w_D[l] for l in range(N_lambda)]
        
        # Average over wavelengths
        p_a_num = sum(weighted_abs) / N_lambda
        p_s_num = sum(weighted_sca) / N_lambda
        p_n_num = sum(weighted_non) / N_lambda
        
        c = p_a_num + p_s_num + p_n_num
        
        # Avoid division by zero if all coefficients are zero
        if c == 0:
            return w_D # No collision possible, terminate or continue?
            
        p_a = p_a_num / c
        p_s = p_s_num / c
        p_n = p_n_num / c
        
        # 5. Select collision type
        xi = random.uniform(0, 1)
        
        if xi < p_a:
            # Absorption
            # Return contribution: w_D * (mu_a / (mu_bar_t * P_a))
            # Note: mu_a here is the vector of coefficients
            factor = [abs_coeffs[l] / (mu_bar_t(x) * p_a) for l in range(N_lambda)]
            contribution = [w_D[l] * factor[l] for l in range(N_lambda)]
            return contribution
            
        elif xi < p_a + p_s:
            # Scattering
            factor = [sca_coeffs[l] / (mu_bar_t(x) * p_s) for l in range(N_lambda)]
            w_D = [w_D[l] * factor[l] for l in range(N_lambda)]
            
            # Sample new direction (placeholder for phase function sampling)
            omega = sample_phase_function(omega)
            
        else:
            # Non-scattering
            factor = [non_coeffs[l] / (mu_bar_t(x) * p_n) for l in range(N_lambda)]
            w_D = [w_D[l] * factor[l] for l in range(N_lambda)]
            
            # Continue loop for next segment
```

## Subtopics

### Maximum-based Spectral Probabilities
Uses $\max_\lambda$ instead of $\text{avg}_\lambda$ in the probability calculation [S1]. When all collision coefficients are non-negative, the local collision weight $w(\mathbf{x}, \lambda)$ is bounded by 3 (the number of collision types) for all $\lambda$, regardless of $N_\lambda$ [S1]. However, incorporating path history into maximum-based probabilities only slows down throughput growth but does not limit it, unlike the average-based variant [S1]. Local collision weights can exceed 1, leading to geometric growth of path throughput if multiple such collisions occur [S1]. It yields slightly better performance (lower LTUV) than average-based in some scenes but is less robust to high albedos [S1].

### Average-based Spectral Probabilities
Uses $\text{avg}_\lambda$ in the probability calculation [S1]. Incorporating path history into average-based probabilities bounds the throughput, keeping the sum of throughputs over all wavelengths equal to $N_\lambda$ and the average throughput equal to 1 [S1]. This makes it immune to fireflies and geometric growth of path throughput [S1]. It is more robust to high albedos than the maximum-based variant [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Increases render time by 25%–30% compared to delta tracking in chromatic scenes [S1]. This is due to taking smaller steps on average, as the free-path-sampling coefficient must bound the extinction across all wavelengths [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing a throughput vector of size $N_\lambda$ [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** In a color explosion scene, spectral tracking achieved 40%–45% lower RMSE and 2.06×–2.46× lower LTUV than delta tracking [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Suppresses geometric growth of path throughput, reduces estimation noise, avoids bright chromatic outliers ("fireflies"), and keeps average throughput at 1 [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Higher computational cost than delta tracking; sensitive to underestimation of the majorant $\bar{\mu}_t$ [S1].
*   **When to use:** Rendering chromatic media with spectrally varying absorption, scattering, and extinction coefficients where noise and fireflies are problematic [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When performance is critical and chromatic noise is acceptable, or if the majorant cannot be tightly bounded [S1].

## Background
Spectral tracking was introduced by researchers from Disney Animation in the paper "Spectral and Decomposition Tracking for Rendering Heterogeneous Volumes" [S1]. It addresses the limitations of naive delta tracking in chromatic media, where independent tracking of wavelengths leads to high variance and fireflies [S1]. The method extends Woodcock tracking by incorporating path history into collision probabilities to bound throughput [S1].
