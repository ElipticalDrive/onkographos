# Dwivedi Sampling

Also called: Biasing Random Walk, Slab Approximation Sampling

## Overview
Dwivedi Sampling is a variance reduction technique for path tracing in participating media, specifically designed for high-scattering, isotropic environments. It addresses the problem of "lost paths" where random walks become trapped deep within dense volumes, failing to contribute to the final image. Unlike standard distance sampling which treats the medium as infinite or uniform, Dwivedi Sampling biases the random walk to exit the medium as quickly as possible by approximating the local geometry as a slab [S1][S2, unofficial]. This approach leverages the principle that paths near the boundary have higher contribution than those deep within the volume [S1]. It is distinct from [Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching](1532-single-scattering-volume-rendering-with-ray-marching.md) by explicitly modeling the exit probability of the medium, and from [Sphere Light Sampling with Multiple Strategies](2207-sphere-light-sampling-with-multiple-strategies.md) by focusing on geometric escape rather than light source visibility.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Current scattering position $\mathbf{p}$ inside the volume.
*   Incoming direction $\omega_i$ (from previous scattering or camera).
*   Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ (assumed constant or locally averaged for the slab approximation).
*   Phase function $p(\omega_i, \omega_s)$ (typically isotropic).
*   Probe ray length $L_{probe}$ (sufficient to exit the object or hit a boundary).

**Outputs:**
*   New scattering direction $\omega_s$.
*   Distance to next interaction $s$.
*   Weight adjustment for Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Slab Model:** Defined by two parallel planes. The entry plane is tangent to the local surface at $\mathbf{p}$ (or defined by the probe ray entry), and the exit plane is parallel at a distance $d$ (thickness).
*   **Thickness $d$:** Estimated via a probe ray.

**Steps:**
1.  **Probe Ray Execution:** Shoot a ray from $\mathbf{p}$ in the direction of the incoming light or the previous segment to estimate the local thickness of the participating object. Let the intersection distance be $d$. If no intersection is found within a large bound, $d$ is set to a large value or the method falls back to standard sampling [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Slab Approximation:** Approximate the local volume geometry as a slab of thickness $d$. The transmittance through the slab is $T_{slab} = \exp(-\sigma_t d)$.
3.  **Biased Distance Sampling:** Instead of sampling the distance $s$ from the standard exponential distribution $p(s) = \sigma_t e^{-\sigma_t s}$, sample $s$ from a distribution biased towards the boundaries. The probability density function (PDF) for the distance $s$ within the slab $[0, d]$ is often derived from the analytic solution of light transport in a slab, favoring small $s$ (exit) and $s \approx d$ (exit other side) [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   The PDF $p_{bias}(s)$ is proportional to the transmittance from the entry to $s$ plus the transmittance from $s$ to the exit, normalized over $[0, d]$.
    *   A common implementation mixes classical distance sampling with this biased sampling [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Direction Sampling:** Sample the scattering direction $\omega_s$ using the phase function. If using the slab approximation, the direction may be biased to favor directions pointing towards the slab boundaries [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS):** Combine the biased sampling with classical distance/direction sampling.
    *   For RGB renders, mix 6 methods (3 for distance, 3 for direction, or combinations thereof) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Use modified heuristic weights (based on [Kd14]) to balance the contribution of the biased samples vs. classical samples [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def dwivedi_sampling(p, omega_in, sigma_t, phase_func, d_thickness):
    """
    Perform Dwivedi sampling for a slab of thickness d_thickness.
    
    Args:
        p: Current position vector.
        omega_in: Incoming direction vector.
        sigma_t: Extinction coefficient.
        phase_func: Phase function object with sample() method.
        d_thickness: Estimated slab thickness.
        
    Returns:
        omega_out: Outgoing direction.
        s: Distance to next interaction.
        weight: MIS weight.
    """
    # 1. Sample distance using biased distribution
    # The biased PDF favors boundaries. 
    # A simple approximation is to sample u ~ Uniform(0,1) and map it.
    # More accurately, integrate the slab transmittance profile.
    # For implementation simplicity, we mix classical and biased.
    
    u_dist = random_uniform()
    
    # Classical distance sampling
    s_classical = -log(1.0 - u_dist) / sigma_t
    
    # Biased distance sampling (Slab approximation)
    # PDF proportional to exp(-sigma_t * s) + exp(-sigma_t * (d - s))
    # Normalization constant Z = integral_0^d (exp(-sigma_t * s) + exp(-sigma_t * (d - s))) ds
    # Z = (1 - exp(-sigma_t * d)) / sigma_t * 2
    # CDF inversion is required for exact sampling.
    # Simplified approach: Sample from a distribution that peaks at 0 and d.
    # Let's use a mixture: 50% classical, 50% biased towards boundaries.
    
    if u_dist < 0.5:
        s = s_classical
        pdf_classical = sigma_t * exp(-sigma_t * s)
        pdf_biased = 0.0 # Not used in this branch
    else:
        # Biased sampling: favor small s or s close to d
        # Approximation: sample u2 ~ Uniform(0,1)
        # If u2 < 0.5, s = -log(u2) / sigma_t (clamped to d)
        # If u2 >= 0.5, s = d + log(1 - u2) / sigma_t (clamped to 0)
        # This is a heuristic [S2, unofficial]
        u2 = random_uniform()
        if u2 < 0.5:
            s = min(-log(u2) / sigma_t, d_thickness)
        else:
            s = max(d_thickness + log(1.0 - u2) / sigma_t, 0.0)
        
        # Calculate PDF for biased sampling
        # p_biased(s) ~ exp(-sigma_t * s) + exp(-sigma_t * (d - s))
        pdf_biased = exp(-sigma_t * s) + exp(-sigma_t * (d_thickness - s))
        pdf_classical = 0.0 # Not used in this branch

    # 2. Sample direction
    omega_out = phase_func.sample(omega_in)
    
    # 3. MIS Weighting
    # Use heuristic weights from [Kd14] modified for distance/direction [S2, unofficial]
    # w = pdf_biased^2 / (pdf_biased^2 + pdf_classical^2)
    # If one PDF is 0, weight is 1.0
    if pdf_classical == 0 and pdf_biased == 0:
        weight = 1.0
    elif pdf_classical == 0:
        weight = 1.0
    elif pdf_biased == 0:
        weight = 1.0
    else:
        weight = (pdf_biased ** 2) / (pdf_biased ** 2 + pdf_classical ** 2)
        
    return omega_out, s, weight
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Probe Ray Length:** Must be large enough to exit the object. Not given in the sources. [G]
*   **Mixing Ratio:** The proportion of biased vs. classical sampling. [S2, unofficial] suggests mixing, but exact ratio is not specified. [G]
*   **MIS Heuristic Weights:** Based on [Kd14], modified for distance and direction. [S2, unofficial]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Thin Geometries:** Unsuitable for thin geometries (such as ears) with strong backlight, as the slab approximation fails to capture the complex light transport [S1].
*   **High Scattering Only:** Best suited for high-scattering, isotropic media. Not effective for low-scattering or anisotropic media [S1].
*   **Thickness Estimation Error:** If the probe ray fails to estimate the correct thickness (e.g., due to holes or complex shapes), the biasing may be incorrect, leading to increased variance [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Meng et al. Biasing Sampling
A variant that addresses the limitations of Dwivedi sampling for thin geometries. It takes geometric characteristics into account to guide scattering away from the object as quickly as possible [S1]. It proposes two methods: **Closest Points**, which searches for the closest point to the boundary at every scattering vertex to increase escape chances in thin geometry, and **Incident Illumination Sampling**, which chooses light vertices proportional to their emission using importance sampling to improve variance in backlit cases [S1]. Only suitable for high-order scattering and isotropic participating media [S1].

### Adjoint-guided Tracking
A future work technique where the probabilities of absorption ($P_a$), scattering ($P_s$), and null collision ($P_n$) are biased based on scene information (e.g., light distribution) to reduce variance [S3]. This includes increasing absorption rates in emissive parts or scattering more in brightly lit regions [S3]. It cites Dwivedi (1982) for advanced zero-variance schemes [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per scattering event, plus the cost of the probe ray. The probe ray requires additional ray-marching or intersection tests, increasing per-sample cost compared to standard distance sampling [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, as it only requires local geometric information (thickness) [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Leads to lower variance in high-scattering, isotropic media [S1]. Convergence is faster because fewer paths are lost inside the object [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces noise in thick, highly scattering volumes. Simple to implement with slab approximation [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Fails for thin geometries with strong backlight [S1]. Assumes isotropy. Requires accurate thickness estimation [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** High-scattering, isotropic participating media where paths tend to get trapped.
*   **When Not to Use:** Thin geometries, anisotropic media, or low-scattering media [S1].

## Background
Dwivedi Sampling originates from Dwivedi (1982), who proposed advanced zero-variance schemes for radiation transport [S3]. The method was adapted for computer graphics to bias random walks in participating media. It is cited in [S1] as a method to reduce variance by exploiting the higher contribution of paths near boundaries. The technique is also referenced in [S3] as a precursor to importance sampling techniques in volume rendering.
