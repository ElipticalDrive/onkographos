# Dwivedi Sampling

Also called: Biasing Random Walk

## Overview
Dwivedi Sampling is a variance reduction technique for path tracing in participating media that biases the random walk to exit the medium as quickly as possible, based on the principle that paths near the boundary have higher contribution than those deep within the volume [S1]. It addresses the problem of lost paths trapped inside dense volumes by approximating the local geometry as a slab and mixing classical sampling with biased sampling, combined via Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is distinct from [Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching](1532-single-scattering-volume-rendering-with-ray-marching.md) by explicitly modeling boundary exit probability, and from [Sphere Light Sampling with Multiple Strategies](2207-sphere-light-sampling-with-multiple-strategies.md) by focusing on geometric escape rather than light source visibility [G].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Current scattering position $\mathbf{p}$ inside the volume [G].
*   Incoming direction $\omega_i$ (from previous scattering or camera) [G].
*   Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ (assumed constant or locally averaged for the slab approximation) [S1].
*   Phase function $p(\omega_i, \omega_s)$ (typically isotropic) [S1].
*   Probe ray length $L_{probe}$ (sufficient to exit the object or hit a boundary) [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   New scattering direction $\omega_s$ [G].
*   Distance to next interaction $s$ [G].
*   Weight adjustment for Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Slab Model:** Defined by two parallel planes. The entry plane is tangent to the local surface at $\mathbf{p}$ (or defined by the probe ray entry), and the exit plane is parallel at a distance $d$ (thickness) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Thickness $d$:** Estimated via a probe ray [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Probe Ray Execution:** Shoot a ray from $\mathbf{p}$ in the direction of the incoming light or the previous segment to estimate the local thickness of the participating object. Let the intersection distance be $d$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Slab Approximation:** Approximate the local volume geometry as a slab of thickness $d$ [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Biased Distance Sampling:** Instead of sampling the distance $s$ from the standard exponential distribution $p(s) = \sigma_t e^{-\sigma_t s}$, sample $s$ from a distribution biased towards the boundaries [S1][S2, unofficial]. The PDF $p_{bias}(s)$ is proportional to the transmittance from the entry to $s$ plus the transmittance from $s$ to the exit, normalized over $[0, d]$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Direction Sampling:** Sample the scattering direction $\omega_s$ using the phase function. If using the slab approximation, the direction may be biased to favor directions pointing towards the slab boundaries [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS):** Combine the biased sampling with classical distance/direction sampling [S2, unofficial]. For RGB renders, mix 6 methods (3 for distance, 3 for direction, or combinations thereof) [S2, unofficial]. Use modified heuristic weights (based on [Kd14]) to balance the contribution of the biased samples vs. classical samples [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def random_uniform():
    """Returns a uniform random float in [0, 1)."""
    # Implementation depends on the renderer's RNG
    pass

def dwivedi_sampling(p, omega_in, sigma_t, phase_func, d_thickness):
    """
    Perform Dwivedi sampling for a slab of thickness d_thickness.
    """
    # 1. Sample distance using a mixture of classical and biased distributions
    u = random_uniform()
    
    # Classical distance sampling: p(s) = sigma_t * exp(-sigma_t * s)
    s_classical = -math.log(1.0 - u) / sigma_t
    pdf_classical = sigma_t * math.exp(-sigma_t * s_classical)
    
    # Biased distance sampling: favors boundaries (s near 0 or d)
    # The exact PDF formula is not given in the sources; implement a boundary-biased sampler [S2, unofficial].
    # A common approach is to sample from a distribution proportional to transmittance to boundaries.
    u2 = random_uniform()
    if u2 < 0.5:
        s_biased = -math.log(u2) / sigma_t
    else:
        s_biased = d_thickness + math.log(1.0 - u2) / sigma_t
    s_biased = max(0.0, min(d_thickness, s_biased))
    
    # PDF for biased sampling (unnormalized heuristic)
    pdf_biased = math.exp(-sigma_t * s_biased) + math.exp(-sigma_t * (d_thickness - s_biased))
    
    # 2. Sample direction
    omega_out = phase_func.sample(omega_in)
    
    # 3. MIS Weighting
    # Use modified heuristic weights from [Kd14] for distance and direction sampling [S2, unofficial]
    # w = pdf_biased^2 / (pdf_biased^2 + pdf_classical^2)
    if pdf_classical == 0 and pdf_biased == 0:
        weight = 1.0
    elif pdf_classical == 0:
        weight = 1.0
    elif pdf_biased == 0:
        weight = 1.0
    else:
        weight = (pdf_biased ** 2) / (pdf_biased ** 2 + pdf_classical ** 2)
        
    return omega_out, s_biased, weight
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Probe Ray Length:** Must be large enough to exit the object. Not given in the sources. [G]
*   **Mixing Ratio:** The proportion of biased vs. classical sampling. [S2, unofficial] suggests mixing, but exact ratio is not specified. [G]
*   **MIS Heuristic Weights:** Based on [Kd14], modified for distance and direction. [S2, unofficial]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Thin Geometries:** Unsuitable for thin geometries (such as ears) with strong backlight, as the slab approximation fails to capture the complex light transport [S1].
*   **High Scattering Only:** Best suited for high-scattering, isotropic media. Not effective for low-scattering or anisotropic media [S1].
*   **Thickness Estimation Error:** If the probe ray fails to estimate the correct thickness (e.g., due to holes or complex shapes), the biasing may be incorrect, leading to increased variance [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Meng et al. Biasing Sampling
A variant that addresses the limitations of Dwivedi sampling for thin geometries. It takes geometric characteristics into account to guide scattering away from the object as quickly as possible [S1]. It proposes two methods: **Closest Points**, which searches for the closest point to the boundary at every scattering vertex to increase escape chances in thin geometry, and **Incident Illumination Sampling**, which chooses light vertices proportional to their emission using importance sampling to improve variance in backlit cases [S1]. Only suitable for high-order scattering and isotropic participating media [S1].

### Adjoint-guided Tracking
A future work technique where the probabilities of absorption ($P_a$), scattering ($P_s$), and null collision ($P_n$) are biased based on scene information (e.g., light distribution) to reduce variance [S3]. This includes increasing absorption rates in emissive parts or scattering more in brightly lit regions [S3]. It cites Dwivedi (1982) for advanced zero-variance schemes [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per scattering event, plus the cost of the probe ray. The probe ray requires additional ray-marching or intersection tests, increasing per-sample cost compared to standard distance sampling [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, as it only requires local geometric information (thickness) [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Leads to lower variance in high-scattering, isotropic media [S1]. Convergence is faster because fewer paths are lost inside the object [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces noise in thick, highly scattering volumes. Simple to implement with slab approximation [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Fails for thin geometries with strong backlight [S1]. Assumes isotropy. Requires accurate thickness estimation [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** High-scattering, isotropic participating media where paths tend to get trapped.
*   **When Not to Use:** Thin geometries, anisotropic media, or low-scattering media [S1].

## Background
Dwivedi Sampling originates from Dwivedi (1982), who proposed advanced zero-variance schemes for radiation transport [S3]. The method was adapted for computer graphics to bias random walks in participating media. It is cited in [S1] as a method to reduce variance by exploiting the higher contribution of paths near boundaries. The technique is also referenced in [S3] as a precursor to importance sampling techniques in volume rendering.
