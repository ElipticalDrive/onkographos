# Principal-Ordinates Propagation


## Overview
Principal-Ordinates Propagation (POP) is a deterministic finite-element method for real-time simulation of anisotropic light transport in heterogeneous participating media [S1]. It solves the problem of false scattering and ray effects inherent in previous finite-element methods by dynamically decomposing illumination into directional and point sources, propagating them in independent discrete domains aligned with principal light directions [S1]. Unlike methods using general bases like spherical harmonics, POP uses a computationally efficient unimodal basis implicitly aligned with the respective principal ordinate [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Scene geometry (implicit or explicit for ray marching), medium properties (spatially varying scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$, absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$), light sources (distant/parallel or point), observer position $\mathbf{c}$, and method parameters (number of principal ordinates $N$, grid resolution $R$, number of iterations $M$) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Final radiance $L_o$ at the observer position $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
The method maintains two propagation grids, `L_curr` and `L_next`, of size $R^3$ (e.g., $20^3$ or $50^3$) [S1]. Each cell $i$ stores:
*   `L[i]`: Radiance magnitude (scalar) [S1].
*   `a[i]`: Henyey-Greenstein (HG) anisotropy parameter for the directional distribution (scalar, range $[-1, 1]$) [S1].
*   `sigma_s[i]`: Scattering coefficient (scalar, units $m^{-1}$) [S1].
*   `sigma_a[i]`: Absorption coefficient (scalar, units $m^{-1}$) [S1].
*   `x[i]`: Cell center position (vector) [S1].

An accumulated radiance grid `L_acc` of the same size is maintained to sum contributions over iterations [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $g$: HG phase function scattering anisotropy, range $[0, 1]$ [S1]. Typical values: $0.9, 0.96$ [S1].
*   $N$: Number of principal ordinates. Typical values: $64, 125$ [S1].
*   $R$: Grid resolution. Typical values: $20^3, 50^3$ [S1].
*   $M$: Number of propagation iterations. Typical values: $10, 30$ [S1] [S1].
*   $\sigma_s$: Typical values: $2.9, 3.6, 4.2 \, m^{-1}$ [S1].
*   $\sigma_a$: Typical values: $3.4, 3.35, 3.4 \, m^{-1}$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps**

1.  **Determine Principal Ordinates:**
    Identify $N$ principal directions $\mathbf{d}_k$ for $k=1 \dots N$. For distant lights, these align with the light direction. For point lights, they are distributed around the source [S1]. Importance sampling may be used to select ordinates based on source intensity [S1].

2.  **Initialize Propagation Grids:**
    For each principal ordinate $k$:
    *   Create a propagation grid aligned with $\mathbf{d}_k$.
        *   If $\mathbf{d}_k$ corresponds to a distant light, use a rectilinear grid aligned with $\mathbf{d}_k$ [S1].
        *   If $\mathbf{d}_k$ corresponds to a point light, use a spherical grid centered at the source [S1].
    *   Initialize `L_curr` and `L_next` to zero. Initialize `L_acc` to zero [S1].
    *   For each cell $i$ in the grid:
        *   Compute transmittance $T_i$ from the light entry point to cell center $\mathbf{x}_i$ along $\mathbf{d}_k$ using ray marching and the medium's $\sigma_a$ [S1].
        *   Set `L_curr[i]` = $L_{in}(\mathbf{d}_k) \cdot T_i$, where $L_{in}$ is the incident radiance from the light source [S1].
        *   Set `a[i]` = $1.0$ (representing a Dirac delta function in direction $\mathbf{d}_k$) [S1].

3.  **Propagation Loop:**
    Iterate $m$ from $1$ to $M$:
    *   For each cell $dst$ in the grid:
        *   Initialize `L_next[dst]` = $0$ and `a_next[dst]` = $0$.
        *   For each of the six neighbors $src$ of $dst$:
            *   Compute distance $t = \|\mathbf{x}_{dst} - \mathbf{x}_{src}\|$ [S1].
            *   Compute average extinction $\sigma_t = \sigma_{a,src} + \sigma_{s,src}$ (or averaged over the path) [S1].
            *   Compute transmittance $T_{src \to dst} = e^{-\sigma_t \cdot t}$ [S1].
            *   Determine solid angle parametrization $(\theta_1, \theta_2, |\phi_1 - \phi_2|)$ for $dst$ relative to $src$ based on relative position [S1]:
                $$
                (\theta_1, \theta_2, |\phi_1 - \phi_2|) =
                \begin{cases}
                (0, \pi/4, 2\pi) & \text{if } dst \text{ is in front of } src \\
                (\pi/4, 3\pi/4, 2) & \text{if } dst \text{ is next to } src \\
                (3\pi/4, \pi, 2\pi) & \text{if } dst \text{ is behind } src
                \end{cases}
                $$
            *   Compute radiance contribution $\Delta L_{src \to dst}$:
                $$
                \Delta L_{src \to dst} = L_{src} \cdot T_{src \to dst} \cdot |\phi_1 - \phi_2| \cdot \left( F_{hg}(\cos \theta_1, a_{src}) - F_{hg}(\cos \theta_2, a_{src}) \right)
                $$
                where $F_{hg}$ is the HG Cumulative Distribution Function:
                $$
                F_{hg}(\mu, g) = \frac{1-g}{4\pi g} \left( \frac{1}{1 + g - 2g\mu} - \frac{1}{1+g} \right)
                $$
                [S1].
            *   Compute anisotropy contribution $\Delta a_{src \to dst}$:
                $$
                \Delta a_{src \to dst} = a_{src} \cdot g \cdot \sigma_{s,src} \cdot t
                $$
                [S1].
            *   Accumulate contributions:
                $$
                L_{next}[dst] \leftarrow L_{next}[dst] + \Delta L_{src \to dst}
                $$
                $$
                a_{next}[dst] \leftarrow a_{next}[dst] + \Delta a_{src \to dst}
                $$
        *   Normalize anisotropy:
            $$
            a_{next}[dst] = \text{clamp}\left( \frac{\sum_{src} \Delta a_{src \to dst}}{\sum_{src} \Delta L_{src \to dst}}, -1, 1 \right)
            $$

4.  **Accumulate Results:**
    After $M$ iterations, accumulate the final radiance distribution into `L_acc`:
    $$
    L_{acc}[i] = L_{acc}[i] + L_{curr}[i] \cdot F_{hg}(\mu, a_{curr}[i])
    $$
    where $\mu = \mathbf{d}_k \cdot \mathbf{v}$, and $\mathbf{v}$ is the view direction from cell $i$ to observer $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

5.  **Rendering:**
    Use ray marching from the observer position $\mathbf{c}$ through the accumulated radiance field `L_acc` to compute the final pixel color [S1].

**Pitfalls**
*   **Anisotropy Averaging:** Combining multiple HG distributions with different anisotropy values cannot generally be represented by a single HG distribution. The method uses a weighted arithmetic average, which is an approximation [S1].
*   **Optical Thickness:** In optically thick media, a low number of iterations may be sufficient, but the grid resolution must be adequate to capture the decay [S1].
*   **Precision:** Transmittance calculations should handle small distances and high extinction carefully to avoid underflow/overflow [G].

## Subtopics

### [Isotropic Residuum Propagation](4122-isotropic-residuum-propagation.md)
A technique to reduce propagation costs by switching to cheaper isotropic propagation once the radiance anisotropy falls below a threshold, merging grids into a single global grid.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of principal ordinates $N$, grid resolution $R$, and iterations $M$. Each iteration processes $6 \cdot R^3$ edges. Total complexity is $O(N \cdot M \cdot R^3)$ [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot R^3)$ for storing the propagation grids and accumulated radiance [S1].
*   **Performance:** Renders dense smoke with strong multiple anisotropic scattering at 25 Hz on an NVidia GeForce GTX 770 without precomputations [S1]. Rendering speed for clouds with $g=0.96$ is 35 Hz using 64 ordinates and $20^3$ grid resolution for each cloud, with 15 propagation iterations [S1].
*   **Typical Frame Breakdown:** 5 ms importance propagation, 2 ms determining ordinates, 2 ms grid initialization, 12 ms propagation, 2 ms residuum propagation, 4 ms grid merging and upsampling, 5 ms ray-marching [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time performance on GPUs; handles heterogeneous media; handles arbitrary scattering anisotropy and albedo; no precomputation required; reduces false scattering and ray effects compared to other finite-element methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Biased method; requires recomputation on observer position change; anisotropy averaging is an approximation; struggles with complex light paths that deviate significantly from principal directions (e.g., "upwards" deviation from isotropic sources) [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering of participating media with strong anisotropic scattering (e.g., smoke, clouds) where precomputation is not feasible [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When unbiased results are required; when the medium has very complex, non-local light paths that cannot be captured by local propagation [S1].

## Background
Principal-Ordinates Propagation was introduced by Elek et al. from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology in 2014 [S1]. It was developed to address the challenges of real-time light transport simulation in participating media, specifically aiming to overcome the limitations of Monte-Carlo methods (high variance, slow convergence) and other deterministic methods like Spherical Harmonics Discrete Ordinates Method (SHDOM) (false scattering, precomputation requirements) [S1]. The method is based on the conservation of energy for a differential cylinder of light passing through a medium [S1].
