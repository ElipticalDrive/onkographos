# Differential Radiative Transfer

Also called: DRTE, Differentiable Radiative Transfer

## Overview
Differential Radiative Transfer (DRTE) is a framework for computing the total derivative (gradient) of the interior radiance field $L$ with respect to arbitrary scene parameters $\pi$ by differentiating the integral operators in the combined Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) and Radiance Equation (RE) [S2, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of obtaining unbiased gradients for volumetric lighting effects, which standard path tracing cannot provide due to discontinuities in visibility and geometry [S2, S2, unofficial]. What sets it apart from its siblings is its rigorous handling of the RTE's transport and collision operators, including boundary integrals arising from geometric discontinuities, whereas methods like [Discrete Ordinates Method](1046-discrete-ordinates-method.md) solve for the radiance itself without differentiation, and [Li et al. 2018a] only considers the RE for reflected/refracted radiance, neglecting the volumetric phenomena governed by the RTE [S2, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Scene parameters $\pi \in \mathbb{R}^m$ (geometry, material properties, camera position); medium domain $\Omega$ with boundary $\partial \Omega$; extinction $\sigma_t$, scattering $\sigma_s$, phase function $f_p$, BSDF $f_s$, boundary radiance $L_s$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** The partial derivative of the interior radiance $\hat{L}(x, \omega) := \partial_\pi L(x, \omega)$ [S2, S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
*   **Scene Representation:** Implicit or explicit boundary representation $\partial \Omega(\pi)$ allowing computation of distance $D$ and its derivative $\hat{D}$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Discontinuity Curves:** Set of 3D boundary curves $\partial^2 \Omega(x)$ capturing visibility changes projected onto the unit sphere [S2, S2, unofficial].

**Core Definitions**
The steady-state RTE in operator form is:
$$ L = K_T K_C L + Q $$
where $K_T$ is the transport operator, $K_C$ is the collision operator, and $Q$ is the source term [S2, S2, unofficial].

The transport operator $K_T$ maps a function $g$ to:
$$ (K_T g)(x, \omega) = \int_0^D T(x', x) g(x', \omega) d\tau $$
where $x' = x - \tau \omega$, $D$ is the distance to the boundary, and $T(x', x) = \exp\left(-\int_0^\tau \sigma_t(x - \tau' \omega) d\tau'\right)$ is the transmittance [S1][S2, S2, unofficial].

The collision operator $K_C$ maps $L$ to:
$$ (K_C L)(x, \omega) = \sigma_s(x) \int_{S^2} f_p(x, -\omega', \omega) L(x, \omega') d\omega' = L_{ins}(x, \omega) $$
where $L_{ins}$ is the in-scattered radiance [S1][S2, S2, unofficial].

**Assumptions**
1.  Volumetric emission term $Q$ is neglected (non-emissive media) [S1, S1].
2.  Parameters $\sigma_t, \sigma_s, f_p, f_s, L_e$ are continuous spatially and directionally [S1, S1].
3.  No zero-measure light sources or ideal specular surfaces [S1, S1].

**Step-by-Step Derivation and Estimation**

1.  **Differentiate the Transport Operator ($K_T K_C L$):**
    The derivative $\partial_\pi (K_T K_C L)$ yields an interior integral and a boundary term [S2, S2, unofficial]:
    $$ (\partial_\pi K_T K_C L)(x, \omega) = \int_0^D T(x', x) \sigma_s(x') \hat{L}_{ins}(x', \omega) d\tau + \int_0^D T(x', x) [\hat{\sigma}_s(x') - \Sigma_t(x, \omega, \tau) \sigma_s(x')] L_{ins}(x', \omega) d\tau + \hat{D} T(x_0, x) \sigma_s(x_0) L_{ins}(x_0, \omega) $$
    where $x_0 = x - D\omega$, and $\Sigma_t(x, \omega, \tau) := \int_0^\tau \hat{\sigma}_t(x - \tau' \omega) d\tau'$ [S1, S1][S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $\hat{\sigma}_t$ and $\hat{\sigma}_s$ are material derivatives: $\hat{\sigma}(x) = \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial \pi} + \langle \hat{x}, \nabla \sigma(x) \rangle$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $\hat{D}$ is the change rate of the travel distance $D$. For piecewise-linear shapes with face normal $n_{face}$ and point $p_{face}$:
        $$ \hat{D} = \partial_\pi \frac{\langle n_{face}, x - p_{face} \rangle}{\langle n_{face}, \omega \rangle} $$
        [S2, S2, unofficial].

2.  **Compute In-Scattered Radiance Derivative ($\hat{L}_{ins}$):**
    $L_{ins}$ involves an integral over $S^2$. Differentiation requires handling discontinuities in $L(x, \omega')$ caused by geometric edges (boundary, silhouette, sharp edges) [S2, S2, unofficial].
    $$ \hat{L}_{ins}(x, \omega) = \int_{S^2} \partial_\pi [f_p(x, -\omega', \omega) L(x, \omega')] d\omega' + \int_{\partial^2 \Omega(x)} \langle n_\perp, \partial_\pi (y \to x) \rangle f_p(x, x \to y, \omega) \Delta L(x, y \to x) V(x, y) \frac{\sin \theta}{\|y - x\|} d\ell(y) $$
    *   The first term computes the partial derivative of the phase function and radiance integrand with respect to $\pi$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   The second term is the boundary integral over 3D boundary curves $\partial^2 \Omega(x)$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $n_\perp = \frac{(p - x) \times (q - x)}{\|(p - x) \times (q - x)\|}$ for an edge with endpoints $p, q$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $\Delta L(x, \omega') = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0^-} L(x, \omega' + \epsilon n_\perp) - \lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} L(x, \omega' + \epsilon n_\perp)$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $\partial_\pi (y \to x)$ is the derivative of the direction vector, computed as:
        $$ \hat{\omega}' = \frac{\hat{x} - \hat{y}}{\|x - y\|} - \omega' \frac{\langle \hat{x} - \hat{y}, \omega' \rangle}{\|x - y\|} $$
        [S2, S2, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Boundary In-Scattered Radiance ($L_{ins}(x_0, \omega)$):**
    At the boundary $x_0$, light transport differs on both sides. $L_{ins}(x_0, \omega)$ is the limit from the interior:
    $$ L_{ins}(x_0, \omega) = \int_{H^+} f_p(x_0, -\omega', \omega) L(x_0, \omega') d\omega' + \int_{H^-} f_s(x_0, -\omega', \omega) L_s(x_0, \omega') d\omega' $$
    where $H^+ = \{\omega' : \langle n(x_0), \omega' \rangle > 0\}$ (outward) and $H^- = \{\omega' : \langle n(x_0), \omega' \rangle < 0\}$ (inward), and $n(x_0)$ is the outward normal [S2, S2, unofficial].

4.  **Differentiate the Source Term ($Q$):**
    The source term derivative $\hat{Q}$ combines the derivative of the interfacial scattering and emission:
    $$ \hat{Q}(x, \omega) = T(x_0, x) \left( -\Sigma_t(x, \omega, D) + \hat{D} \sigma_t(x_0) \right) L_s(x_0, \omega) + \hat{L}_s(x_0, \omega) $$
    where $\hat{L}_s = \hat{L}_{rs} + \hat{L}_{es}$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $\hat{L}_{rs}$ is the derivative of reflected/refracted radiance, analogous to $\hat{L}_{ins}$ but using BSDF $f_s$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   Under the non-emissive assumption, volumetric emission is neglected, so $\hat{L}_{es} = 0$ and $L_s$ represents interfacial radiance [S1][S2, S2, unofficial].

5.  **Monte Carlo Estimation (Algorithm 1):**
    Direct terms are estimated using Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS), while indirect terms are sampled recursively according to the local BSDF [S2, S2, unofficial]. Edge terms are estimated via an `EdgeIntegral` function that samples points on discontinuity curves and computes $\Delta L$ via side paths [S2, S2, unofficial]. Dividing by $p(\tau) = T(x_0, x)$ yields simplified estimators:
    $$ \langle L^{(1)} \rangle = L_{rs} $$
    $$ \langle \hat{L}^{(1)} \rangle = -\beta L_{rs} + \hat{L}_{rs} + \hat{D} \sigma_s L_{ins} $$
    where $\beta$ is a term calculated from the transmittance derivative [S2, S2, unofficial].

def compute_boundary_distance_and_derivative(x, omega, n_face, p_face, dx, dn_face, dp_face):
    """Computes D and dD using the implicit face equation."""
    num = np.dot(n_face, x - p_face)
    den = np.dot(n_face, omega)
    D = num / den
    dD = (np.dot(dn_face, x - p_face) + np.dot(n_face, dx - dp_face)) / den - \
         num * (np.dot(dn_face, omega) + np.dot(n_face, 0)) / (den ** 2)
    return D, dD

def compute_transmittance_derivative(x, omega, D, sigma_t, d_sigma_t):
    """Computes T and dT using the Beer-Lambert law."""
    tau = 0.0
    d_tau = 0.0
    steps = 100
    dt = D / steps
    for i in range(steps):
        pos = x - (i + 0.5) * dt * omega
        tau += sigma_t(pos) * dt
        d_tau += d_sigma_t(pos) * dt

## Subtopics

### Volumetric Edge Sampling
An extension of edge sampling to participating media by differentiating the radiative transfer equation [S3, unofficial]. It derives derivatives of individual terms ($K_T, K_C, Q$) and combines them, decomposing differentiation into interior and boundary terms [S3, unofficial]. It estimates the differentiation by combining the edge sampling process with volumetric path tracing [S3, unofficial].

### Differentiation of Transport and Collision Operators
Focuses on the specific formulas for $\Sigma_t(x, \omega, \tau) := \int_0^\tau \hat{\sigma}_t(x - \tau' \omega) d\tau'$ and $\hat{D}$ [S2, S2, unofficial]. It handles the in-scattered radiance at the boundary $L_{ins}(x_0, \omega)$, which is the limit from the interior, using $H^+$ (interior radiance $L$) and $H^-$ (interfacial radiance $L_s$) hemispheres [S2, S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method requires recursive evaluation of derivatives, leading to high computational cost [S2, S2, unofficial]. Estimating $\Delta L$ and side paths for edge sampling adds significant overhead [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Unbiased but noisy for small samples due to the complexity of the estimators [S2, S2, unofficial]. Performance scales with the number of discontinuities and the depth of recursion [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles both volumetric (RTE) and surface (RE) phenomena [S2, S2, unofficial]. Provides exact gradients for optimization tasks involving participating media [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Complex implementation [S2, S2, unofficial]. Sensitive to geometric discontinuities [S2, S2, unofficial]. Requires robust edge sampling and side-path tracing [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When differentiable gradients of volumetric effects are required for inverse rendering or optimization [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** For real-time rendering or when only forward rendering is needed [S2, S2, unofficial].

## Background
Differential Radiative Transfer was introduced by Zhang et al. [42] (Zhang, Wu, Zheng, Gkioulekas, Ramamoorthi, and Zhao, 2019) as a theory for computing the partial derivative of interior radiance with respect to scene parameters [S1, S1]. It extends differentiable rendering to participating media, addressing the limitations of prior work like Li et al. [2018a] which only handled the Radiance Equation [S2, S2, unofficial]. The method computes the partial derivative of interior radiance by differentiating each operator on the right-hand side of the steady-state RTE [S1, S1].
