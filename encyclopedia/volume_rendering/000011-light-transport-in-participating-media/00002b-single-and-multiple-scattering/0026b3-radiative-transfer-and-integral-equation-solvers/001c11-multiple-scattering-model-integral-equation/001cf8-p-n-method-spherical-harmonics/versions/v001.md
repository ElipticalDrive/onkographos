# P-N Method (Spherical Harmonics)
Also called: SH expansion, Spherical Harmonics Expansion for Scattering Equation, Spherical harmonic method, Spherical harmonics expansion

## Overview
The P-N method solves the multiple scattering integral equation by expanding the radiance $I(X, \omega)$ into spherical harmonics over the unit sphere direction $\omega$, converting the integral equation into a coupled system of partial differential equations (PDEs) for the harmonic coefficients [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished from the Diffusion Approximation by retaining higher-order angular moments (up to truncation level $N$), allowing it to model directional effects more accurately than diffusion while remaining computationally cheaper than full angular discretization [S2, unofficial]. It is mathematically equivalent to the Discrete Ordinates Method with $M$ properly placed directions, which specify the directional intensity distribution to the same detail as $M$ spherical harmonic coefficients [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** The scattering source term $Z(z, s)$ (or $Z(X, \omega)$), which includes emission and single-scattering contributions.
*   **Output:** The spherical harmonic coefficients $I_l^m(z)$, from which the radiance $I(X, \omega)$ is reconstructed.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Spherical Harmonic Coefficients:** A set of complex or real-valued coefficients $I_l^m$ for $0 \le l \le N$ and $-l \le m \le l$.
*   **Phase Function Matrix:** A diagonal matrix in the spherical harmonic basis, derived from the phase function $p(\Omega \cdot \Omega')$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Expansion:** Expand the source term $Z(z, s)$ into spherical harmonics in direction $s$:
    $$ Z(z, s) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} I_l^m(z) Y_l^m(s) $$
    where $Y_l^m$ are the normalized spherical harmonics of degree $l$ and order $m$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Projection:** Substitute the expansion into the scattering equation. Multiply the resulting equation by the conjugate spherical harmonic $Y_{l'}^{m'*}(s)$ and integrate over the sphere to isolate the coefficients. This yields a coupled set of first-order PDEs for $I_l^m(z)$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \langle Y_{l'm'}(s) | \mathbf{s} \cdot \nabla I(z, s) \rangle - I_l^m(z) \langle Y_{l'm'}(s) | Y_{lm}(s) \rangle + \langle Y_{l'm'}(s) | \mathcal{P} I(z, s) \rangle = 0 $$
    where $\mathcal{P}$ is the phase integral operator [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Matrix Element Calculation (Direction Operator):** Calculate the matrix elements for the direction operator $\mathbf{s}$. The matrix element for each component of $\mathbf{s}$ is computed using recursion relations for associated Legendre polynomials $P_{l,m}(\mu)$:
    $$ P_{l,m+1}(\mu)(1-\mu^2)^{1/2} = k_0 P_{l+1,m} - k_1 P_{l-1,m} $$
    where:
    $$ k_0 = \frac{(l-m+1)(l-m+2)}{2l+1}, \quad k_1 = \frac{(l+m-1)(l+m)}{2l+1} $$
    Using these relations, the direction operator matrix elements couple adjacent $l$ orders [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Matrix Element Calculation (Phase Operator):** Use Laplace's formula for the product of Legendre polynomials to show that the phase function matrix is diagonal with respect to spherical harmonics. No scattering occurs between different spherical harmonics. The matrix element is:
    $$ \langle Y_{l'm'}(s) | p | Y_{lm}(s) \rangle = \delta_{ll'} \delta_{mm'} \frac{4\pi}{2l+1} \int_{-1}^{1} p(\mu) P_l(\mu) d\mu $$
    This diagonal property simplifies the system significantly [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Truncation:** Truncate the expansion after a specific $l=N$ term. For graphics applications, truncation at $l=1$ (p-wave) is typically sufficient for a convincing image [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Solution:** Solve the resulting coupled system of first-order PDEs for the coefficients $I_l^m(z)$ using relaxation or other numerical PDE solvers [S1, unofficial].

    phase_moments = []
    for l in range(N + 1):

def reconstruct_radiance(I, position_z, direction_s):
    """
    Reconstructs radiance from spherical harmonic coefficients.
    I: Array of coefficients I[l][m]
    position_z: Position z
    direction_s: Direction vector s (theta, phi)
    """
    radiance = 0.0
    for l in range(len(I)):
        for m in range(-l, l + 1):
            # Get coefficient I[l][m]
            coeff = I[l][m + l] # Adjust index for negative m
            # Evaluate spherical harmonic Y_l^m(s)
            Y_lm = evaluate_spherical_harmonic(l, m, direction_s)
            radiance += coeff * Y_lm
    return radiance

**Parameters:**
*   **Truncation Level ($N$):** The maximum degree of spherical harmonics. $N=1$ (p-wave) is used for graphics applications [S1, unofficial]. Higher $N$ increases accuracy but also computational cost.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **High Albedo:** The method may require many terms to converge if the albedo $w_0$ is close to one, as the series is geometric in $w_0$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Ray Effects:** While the P-N method is equivalent to the Discrete Ordinates Method, the latter can suffer from ray effects if directions are not properly placed. The P-N method avoids these by using a continuous angular basis [S2, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Proper boundary conditions must be applied to the PDE system, which can be non-trivial in complex geometries.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the truncation level $N$. The number of coefficients is $(N+1)^2$. Solving the coupled PDE system scales with the number of coefficients and the spatial discretization.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to $(N+1)^2$ coefficients per spatial point.
*   **Strengths:** Provides a smooth angular representation, avoiding ray effects associated with discrete ordinates. Mathematically equivalent to discrete ordinates with optimal direction placement [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May require high $N$ for high-albedo media, leading to convergence issues [S1, unofficial]. Solving coupled PDEs can be complex to implement compared to simpler approximations like diffusion.
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for graphics applications where $N=1$ provides a convincing image with low computational cost [S1, unofficial]. Also used in thermal engineering and neutron transport [S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Not suitable for very high-albedo media without high $N$, which may be computationally prohibitive.

## Background
The P-N method originated in thermal engineering and neutron transport, where it is known as the P-N method [S2, unofficial]. It was applied to volume rendering by Kajiya and Von Herzen, who expanded the source term in spherical harmonics to solve the scattering equation [S2, unofficial]. The method is detailed in Kajiya's 1984 paper "Ray Tracing for Volume Densities" [S1, unofficial]. It is an alternative to the Diffusion Approximation and the Discrete Ordinates Method [S2, unofficial].
