# Bidirectional Path Tracing

Also called: Bidirectional transmittance-based path sampling, Vertex-vertex connection

## Overview
Bidirectional Path Tracing constructs a full light transport path by independently generating a light subpath and an eye subpath, then connecting their endpoints with a deterministic segment [S1]. It differs from unidirectional path tracing by including both emitted radiance and sensor importance in the path probability density function (PDF), thereby reducing variance in scenes with complex light transport [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Light source distribution, eye camera parameters, scene geometry and media.
**Outputs:** Radiance estimate for the pixel.

**Data Structures:**
*   `LightSubpath`: Array of vertices $L_0, \dots, L_s$. $L_0$ is on the light.
*   `EyeSubpath`: Array of vertices $E_0, \dots, E_t$. $E_0$ is on the camera.
*   `ConnectionSegment`: The deterministic link between $L_s$ and $E_t$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Generate Light Subpath:** Use unidirectional sampling to construct a subpath with $s$ vertices starting from the light source [S1].
2.  **Generate Eye Subpath:** Use unidirectional sampling to construct a subpath with $t$ vertices starting from the eye [S1].
3.  **Connect Endpoints:** Deterministically connect the endpoint of the light subpath ($L_s$) and the endpoint of the eye subpath ($E_t$) with a segment [S1]. This segment involves 0 random decisions [S1].
4.  **Estimate Radiance:** Compute the path contribution using the product of transmittances, scattering terms, and the geometric term of the connection segment, divided by the combined PDF of the two subpaths and the connection [S1].

**Formulas:**
The connection segment has a geometric term $G(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}) = \frac{\cos \theta_b \cos \theta_c}{\|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{c}\|^2}$ [S1].
The PDF for the connection is not importance sampled; it is derived from the product of the subpath PDFs and the deterministic nature of the link [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $s, t$: Number of vertices in light and eye subpaths. Typical values are small integers (e.g., 1-5).
*   $s=1$ or $t=1$ corresponds to Next Event Estimation for path or light tracing respectively [S1].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **High Variance:** Vertex-vertex connections can lead to high variance because none of the terms for the connecting segment are importance sampled [S1].
*   **Infinite Variance:** The variance of the estimator can be infinite in certain configurations [S1].

## Subtopics

### Equiangular sampling
A technique that importance samples the geometry term between a vertex and positions on a ray by sampling propagation distance proportional to the inverse squared length of the connecting edge [S1]. Given end vertices $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ and a direction $\omega_{\text{dc}}$, the distance $t_{\text{dc}}$ is sampled with PDF $p(\mathbf{t}_{\text{dc}} | \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{d}, \omega_{\text{dc}}) \propto G(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}) = 1/\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}\|^2$ [S1]. This creates a 1-random-decision connection subpath [S1]. It often results in lower variance than transmittance sampling [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Time complexity depends on the number of vertices $s$ and $t$. Memory complexity is proportional to the path length.
*   **Performance:** Reduces variance compared to unidirectional sampling in complex scenes [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Includes both emitted radiance and sensor importance [S1].
*   **Limitations:** High variance or infinite variance due to deterministic connection of vertices [S1].

## Background
Originated as a method to address the high variance of unidirectional sampling by incorporating both emitted radiance and sensor importance into the path PDF [S1]. Generalized to vertex-segment connections in bidirectional sampling [S1].
