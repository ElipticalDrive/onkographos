# Bidirectional Path Tracing

Also called: Bidirectional transmittance-based path sampling, Vertex-vertex connection

## Overview
Bidirectional Path Tracing constructs a full light transport path by independently generating a light subpath and an eye subpath, then connecting their endpoints with a deterministic segment. It addresses the high variance of unidirectional sampling by ensuring both emitted radiance and sensor importance are included in the path probability density function (PDF) [S1]. Unlike its sibling Bridge Sampling, it uses a zero-random-decision connection, which results in potentially infinite variance for the connecting segment terms [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Scene geometry, participating media properties, light source distribution, camera parameters.
**Outputs:** Radiance estimate for the pixel.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Light Subpath:** Sequence of vertices $L_0, L_1, \dots, L_s$. $L_0$ is on the light source.
*   **Eye Subpath:** Sequence of vertices $E_0, E_1, \dots, E_t$. $E_0$ is on the camera sensor.
*   **Connection Segment:** Deterministic segment between $L_s$ and $E_t$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Generate Light Subpath:** Use unidirectional sampling to construct a subpath with $s$ vertices starting from the light source [S1].
2.  **Generate Eye Subpath:** Use unidirectional sampling to construct a subpath with $t$ vertices starting from the eye [S1].
3.  **Connect Endpoints:** Deterministically connect the endpoint of the light subpath ($L_s$) to the endpoint of the eye subpath ($E_t$) with a segment [S1]. This segment involves 0 random decisions [S1].
4.  **Estimate Radiance:** Compute the radiance contribution along the full path. The connection segment's terms are not importance sampled [S1].

**Formulas:**
The path PDF includes terms for the light subpath, the eye subpath, and the connection. The connection segment is deterministic, meaning its probability contribution is handled differently than sampled segments [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $s$: Number of vertices in the light subpath.
*   $t$: Number of vertices in the eye subpath.
*   Typical values: $s=1$ or $t=1$ corresponds to Next Event Estimation for path tracing or light tracing respectively [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Infinite Variance:** Vertex-vertex connections can lead to high variance since none of the terms for the connecting segment are importance sampled. In fact, the variance is infinite [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Standard path tracing boundary conditions apply for subpath generation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Variance:** High variance due to the deterministic connection segment. The variance is infinite for the connecting segment terms because they are not importance sampled [S1].
*   **Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Includes both emitted radiance and sensor importance in the path PDF, addressing high variance of unidirectional sampling [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Infinite variance for the connection segment [S1].
*   **When to use:** When both light and sensor importance are critical, and variance mitigation techniques for the connection are employed or acceptable.
*   **When not to use:** When low variance is required for the connection segment without additional sampling strategies.

## Background
Bidirectional path tracing was introduced in the context of joint importance sampling of low-order volumetric scattering [S1]. It builds on the concept of bidirectional sampling to improve the efficiency of light transport simulation in participating media [S1].
