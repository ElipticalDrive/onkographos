# Light Color Transmission


## Overview
Light Color Transmission is a technique for computing direct illumination in participating media by attenuating the light source's color as it travels through the volume to the shading point, and then modulating the material's appearance with that transmitted light color [S1, unofficial]. It modifies the standard ray marching procedure to account for lights by multiplying material color by light color and attenuating light color by the volume material between the light and the ray march point [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $C_{light}$: Light color triplet $(F_r, F_g, F_b)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $C_{mat}$: Material color triplet $(r, g, b)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $T$: Transmittance from the light source to the shading point, calculated via ray marching through the volume material [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   $C_{out}$: Final illuminated color triplet [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   Color Triplet: A vector of three floating-point values representing Red, Green, and Blue channels [S1, unofficial].
*   Transmittance $T$: A scalar or triplet value representing the fraction of light surviving the path [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Transmittance:** Determine the transmittance $T$ from the light source to the current shading point $x_i$ by integrating the extinction coefficient along the light direction during ray marching [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Attenuate Light Color:** Multiply the light color by the transmittance to account for volumetric absorption [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Modulate Material Color:** Multiply the material color by the transmitted light color using a component-wise product [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
$$ C_{transmitted} = C_{light} \odot T $$
$$ C_{out} = C_{mat} \odot C_{transmitted} $$
Where $\odot$ denotes component-wise multiplication, $C_{light} = (F_r, F_g, F_b)$, and $C_{mat} = (r, g, b)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\kappa$: Extinction coefficient. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Black Materials:** Standard multiplication can result in black pixels if a material reflects no light at a specific wavelength (e.g., a blue object under red light) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Component-wise multiplication requires careful handling of floating-point precision to avoid underflow in deep volumes [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def light_color_transmission(C_light, C_mat, T):
    """
    Computes final color by attenuating light color by transmittance and multiplying by material color.
    
    Args:
        C_light (tuple): Light color triplet (Fr, Fg, Fb)
        C_mat (tuple): Material color triplet (r, g, b)
        T (float or tuple): Transmittance from light to point
        
    Returns:
        tuple: Final color triplet
    """
    # Attenuate light color by transmittance
    if isinstance(T, (int, float)):
        C_transmitted = (C_light[0] * T, C_light[1] * T, C_light[2] * T)
    else:
        C_transmitted = (C_light[0] * T[0], C_light[1] * T[1], C_light[2] * T[2])
        
    # Modulate material color via component-wise product
    C_out = (C_mat[0] * C_transmitted[0], 
             C_mat[1] * C_transmitted[1], 
             C_mat[2] * C_transmitted[2])
             
    return C_out
```

## Subtopics

### Deferred Lighting with Colorization
A variant used in deferred rendering pipelines that enhances standard light color transmission with artistic control and screen-space effects [S2, unofficial]. It differs from the basic method by allowing additive light transforms and post-processing saturation passes to prevent "black" results and enhance vibrancy [S2, unofficial]. Choose this when artistic control over colorization and performance via dynamic light culling are priorities [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Physically plausible attenuation of light color through volume; allows for artistic colorization effects not possible with simple multiplication [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can produce black pixels for non-matching material/light colors without additional techniques; computationally expensive if many light rays are marched [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When volumetric absorption of light color is desired and artistic control over material appearance is needed [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When performance is critical and volumetric effects are minimal, or when physically accurate color absorption (resulting in black) is required without artistic modification [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of modifying ray marching to account for lights by multiplying material color by light color and attenuating light by volume material was described in production volume rendering contexts [S1, unofficial]. The deferred lighting variant with colorization was implemented in the VoxelQuest engine, which utilized point lights, SSAO, and ray-marched shadows to achieve specific visual styles [S2, unofficial].
