# Room Light

Also called: Single-probe bounce approximation, Spherical harmonic ambient bounce

## Overview
Room Light estimates the colour of bounced light in a room by sampling what colour light lands on surfaces around the player, then skewing the ambient lighting by that answer [S1, unofficial]. It differs from full bounce solvers or probe lattices by using a single probe per room, avoiding the cost of spatial variation machinery which produces blotches rather than light [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [World-Lattice Sun Sampling](19311-world-lattice-sun-sampling.md) to handle direct sunlight contributions.

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Player position and orientation.
- World geometry (voxel data) for ray marching.
- Sun direction and sky parameters.
- Previous frame's spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients.

**Outputs:**
- A band-1 spherical harmonic representation of the ambient light field at the player's position, used to tint global illumination.

### Data Structures
The method maintains a `RoomLight` struct with the following fields [S1, unofficial]:
- `dirs: Vec<Vec3>`: An array of 512 unit vectors representing sample directions, distributed via a golden spiral.
- `rad: Vec<Vec3>`: An array of 512 radiance values (RGB), persistent across frames.
- `next: usize`: An index for the circular buffer, tracking which direction to sample next.
- `sh: [Vec3; 4]`: The current band-1 SH coefficients (4 vectors: $Y_0, Y_1, Y_2, Y_3$).
- `lit_sh: [Vec3; 4]`: Cached SH coefficients derived from the sunlit world lattice.
- `tick: u32`: A frame counter.

### Parameters
- `DIRS = 512`: Total number of sample directions [S1, unofficial].
- `PER_FRAME = 48`: Number of directions re-marched each frame [S1, unofficial].
- `REACH = 40.0`: Maximum ray length in blocks. Rays exceeding this are treated as escaping to the sky [S1, unofficial].
- `LIT_EVERY = 4`: Frames between full sweeps of the sunlit world lattice [S1, unofficial].
- `LATTICE = 1.0`: Spacing of the world lattice of candidate surfaces in blocks [S1, unofficial].
- `RANGE = 18.0`: Maximum distance a lit surface can be from the player and still contribute [S1, unofficial].
- `FADE = 5.0`: Width of the fade-out at the `RANGE` boundary [S1, unofficial].
- `PROBE_UP = 20.0`: Vertical offset above a lattice column where surface search begins [S1, unofficial].
- `SUN_REACH = 96.0`: Distance to cast a ray toward the sun to determine if a lattice surface is sunlit [S1, unofficial].
- `SETTLE = 0.5`: Time constant in seconds for temporal smoothing [S1, unofficial].
- `DIRECTIONAL = false`: When false, band-1 SH terms are zeroed to remove directional bias [S1, unofficial].
- `LIT_GAIN = 8.0`: Multiplier for how strongly the computed room lit colour tints ambient lighting [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Initialize Directions**:
    Generate `DIRS` unit vectors using a golden spiral distribution [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    y_i = 1.0 - 2.0 \cdot \frac{i + 0.5}{\text{DIRS}}
    $$
    $$
    r_i = \sqrt{\max(0.0, 1.0 - y_i^2)}
    $$
    $$
    t_i = \pi \cdot (3.0 - \sqrt{5.0}) \cdot i
    $$
    $$
    \text{dirs}[i] = (r_i \cos(t_i), y_i, r_i \sin(t_i))
    $$
    where $i \in [0, \text{DIRS}-1]$.

2.  **Per-Frame Sampling**:
    Loop `PER_FRAME` times. In each iteration:
    - Pick index `i = next`.
    - Advance `next = (next + 1) % DIRS`.
    - Call `sample_dir(world, eye, dirs[i], sky_params, face)` to get radiance `r` for that direction.
    - Store `r` in `rad[i]`.
    
    The function `sample_dir` performs a ray march from the player's eye position along `dirs[i]` up to `REACH`. If the ray hits a surface, it returns the colour of that surface (potentially modulated by sky visibility). If it escapes, it returns the sky colour.

3.  **Rebuild Spherical Harmonics**:
    Compute new SH coefficients from the full `dirs` and `rad` arrays [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \text{sh}[0] = \sum_{i=0}^{\text{DIRS}-1} \text{rad}[i] \cdot 0.282095
    $$
    $$
    \text{sh}[1] = \sum_{i=0}^{\text{DIRS}-1} \text{rad}[i] \cdot (0.488603 \cdot \text{dirs}[i].y)
    $$
    $$
    \text{sh}[2] = \sum_{i=0}^{\text{DIRS}-1} \text{rad}[i] \cdot (0.488603 \cdot \text{dirs}[i].z)
    $$
    $$
    \text{sh}[3] = \sum_{i=0}^{\text{DIRS}-1} \text{rad}[i] \cdot (0.488603 \cdot \text{dirs}[i].x)
    $$

4.  **Apply Monte-Carlo Sphere Weight**:
    Multiply all `sh` coefficients by the sphere weight $w = \frac{4.0 \cdot \pi}{\text{DIRS}}$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Integrate Sunlit Lattice (Periodic)**:
    If `tick % LIT_EVERY == 0`:
    - Compute `lit_sh` from the sunlit world lattice using [World-Lattice Sun Sampling](19311-world-lattice-sun-sampling.md). This involves sampling candidate surfaces spaced by `LATTICE`, checking if they are sunlit via a ray of length `SUN_REACH`, and projecting their contribution into SH space within `RANGE` and `FADE` bounds.
    - Add `lit_sh` to `sh`.
    - Cache `lit_sh` for reuse in subsequent frames until the next sweep.

6.  **Cosine Convolution**:
    Apply cosine convolution for diffuse lighting by multiplying band-1 terms (`sh[1..3]`) by $\frac{2.0}{3.0}$. Band-0 (`sh[0]`) remains unchanged [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Remove Directional Bias (Optional)**:
    If `DIRECTIONAL` is false, zero out `sh[1..3]` to prevent visible brightening when crossing sunbeams or walking past light sources [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Temporal Smoothing**:
    Smooth the result to prevent flashing [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    k = 1.0 - \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{\text{SETTLE}}\right)
    $$
    $$
    \text{sh}_{\text{new}} = \text{sh}_{\text{old}} + (\text{sh} - \text{sh}_{\text{old}}) \cdot k
    $$

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Sparkling/Flashing**: Using a moving sample set causes frame-to-frame disagreement (sparkles). A fixed set causes bias but holds still. Temporal smoothing prevents walls from flashing as the player walks [S1, unofficial].
- **Directional Bias**: Without zeroing band-1 terms when `DIRECTIONAL` is false, the lighting may visibly brighten when the player crosses sunbeams [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: $O(\text{PER\_FRAME} \cdot \text{RayMarchCost})$ per frame. The lattice update is $O(\text{LatticePoints})$ but occurs every `LIT_EVERY` frames.
- **Memory Complexity**: $O(\text{DIRS})$ for storing directions and radiance.
- **Performance**: Constant per-frame cost due to `PER_FRAME` limiting active samples. The remaining `DIRS - PER_FRAME` samples retain last frame's answer [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Low cost, no spatial variation machinery, stable lighting.
- **Limitations**: Single probe per room, no spatial variation, potential directional bias if not handled.
