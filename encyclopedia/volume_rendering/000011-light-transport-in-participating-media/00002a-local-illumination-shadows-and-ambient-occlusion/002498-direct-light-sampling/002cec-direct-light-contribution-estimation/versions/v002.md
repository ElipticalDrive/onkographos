# Direct Light Contribution Estimation

Also called: Direct Light Contribution

## Overview
Direct Light Contribution Estimation computes the direct illumination intensity at a specific point in a participating medium by combining local and global opacity information to account for occlusion by the volume itself [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by explicitly separating the near-field occlusion (handled with high-resolution local data) from the far-field transmittance (handled with interpolated global data), thereby improving visual fidelity over methods that use a single opacity source for the entire light ray [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $s_0$: The current voxel or sample position in the volume.
*   $I_0$: The light source intensity (user-defined parameter) [S1].
*   $\alpha_l(s_0)$: Local opacity at $s_0$, derived from local piecewise integration [S1].
*   $\alpha_g(s_1)$: Global opacity interpolated from the Sparse Voxel Representation (SVR) for the segment from $s_1$ to the light source [S1].
*   $s_1$: The starting point of the global segment, typically the boundary of the local integration volume around $s_0$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   $I_d(s_0)$: The estimated direct light intensity at $s_0$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Local Opacity Storage:** Stores $\alpha_l(s_0)$ with the highest resolution available for the immediate vicinity of $s_0$ [S1].
*   **Sparse Voxel Representation (SVR):** A hierarchical structure (e.g., sparse voxel octree) used to store and interpolate global opacity $\alpha_g$ for distant segments [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  Retrieve the local opacity $\alpha_l(s_0)$ from the local piecewise integration results for the current voxel $s_0$ [S1].
2.  Determine the point $s_1$, which marks the end of the local integration segment and the beginning of the global segment towards the light source [S1].
3.  Interpolate the global opacity $\alpha_g(s_1)$ from the SVR for the ray segment extending from $s_1$ to the light source [S1].
4.  Calculate the direct light contribution using the formula:
    $$I_d(s_0) = I_0 \cdot (1 - \alpha_g(s_1)) \cdot (1 - \alpha_l(s_0))$$
    where $(1 - \alpha_l(s_0))$ represents the transmittance through the local high-resolution segment, and $(1 - \alpha_g(s_1))$ represents the transmittance through the global volume [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Local Occlusion Accuracy:** Using only $\alpha_g$ for the entire ray length reduces quality because it fails to capture fine-grained occlusion details in the near vicinity [S1]. The method requires $\alpha_l(s_0)$ to ensure local occlusion is calculated at the highest possible resolution [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Direct Illumination Derivative (Spatial Form)](7662-direct-illumination-derivative-spatial-form.md)
This method computes the derivative of the direct illumination integral with respect to scene parameters $\pi$ by differentiating the surface integral directly over the evolving surface geometry. It is distinguished from the Material-Form variant by integrating over the physical surface domain $L(\pi)$, which depends on $\pi$, thereby requiring the estimation of a boundary term that accounts for the motion of the integration domain's edge.

### Sun Light Estimation
Estimation of lighting from a delta light (sun) using multiple importance sampling (MIS). Since the light is a delta function, the light PDF is always 1.0 and is not taken into account in the calculation [S2, unofficial]. The steps are: 1. Find the phase function PDF using the sun light direction and the ray direction. 2. Calculate the transmittance to the light source. 3. Multiply the phase function PDF, transmittance, and the sun's power [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method adds a minor computational cost compared to using only global opacity, primarily due to the retrieval of local opacity and the interpolation from the SVR [S1].
*   **Quality:** Improves final visual quality by ensuring local occlusion is calculated at the highest possible resolution, reducing artifacts caused by large differences in occlusion between neighboring voxels [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the SVR for global opacity interpolation and the correctness of the local piecewise integration for $\alpha_l(s_0)$ [S1].

## Background
The method is described in the context of Interactive Global Light Propagation in Direct Volume Rendering using Local Piecewise Integration [S1]. It addresses the need for accurate direct light computation in volumes where local occlusion plays a significant role, particularly when light sources and large differences in occlusion exist between neighboring voxels [S1].
