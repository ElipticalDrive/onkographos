# Unbiased Monte Carlo Estimator for Radiance and Radiance Derivatives

Also called: Joint Estimation of L(1) and L̂(1)

## Overview
This method provides an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator for the first-order radiance derivative $\hat{L}^{(1)}$ and the radiance $L^{(1)}$ in participating media, handling volumetric and interfacial scattering events separately based on the free-flight distance relative to the boundary distance $D$ [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard volumetric path tracing by explicitly propagating scene derivatives alongside radiance values, enabling the computation of gradients required for differentiable rendering [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Volumetric Path Tracing](1863-volumetric-path-tracing.md), which computes only radiance, this estimator jointly tracks the sensitivity of the solution to scene parameters [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The estimator operates by recursively computing radiance and its derivative for a given position $\mathbf{x}$, scene derivative $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, direction $\omega$, and scene derivative direction $\hat{\omega}$. The core logic branches based on whether the sampled free-flight distance $\tau$ is less than the distance to the nearest boundary $D$.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   $\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}$: Current position and its scene derivative.
    *   $\omega, \hat{\omega}$: Current direction and its scene derivative.
    *   $n$: Current path depth (used for Russian Roulette).
*   **Outputs**:
    *   $L^{(1)}$: The estimated radiance.
    *   $\hat{L}^{(1)}$: The estimated radiance derivative.

### Data Structures and Variables
*   $D$: Distance from $\mathbf{x}$ to the nearest boundary $\partial \Omega$ along $\omega$.
*   $\hat{D}$: Scene derivative of $D$.
*   $\tau$: Sampled free-flight distance.
*   $\sigma_s(\mathbf{x})$: Scattering coefficient.
*   $\sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$: Extinction coefficient.
*   $\hat{\sigma}_s(\mathbf{x})$: Scene derivative of the scattering coefficient.
*   $\Sigma_t(\mathbf{x}, \omega, \tau)$: Optical depth from $\mathbf{x}$ to $\mathbf{x} - \tau \omega$.
*   $f_p(\mathbf{x}, \omega', \omega)$: Phase function.
*   $\hat{f}_p(\mathbf{x}, \omega', \omega)$: Scene derivative of the phase function.
*   $f_s(\mathbf{x}, \omega', \omega)$: BSDF at the boundary.
*   $\hat{f}_s(\mathbf{x}, \omega', \omega)$: Scene derivative of the BSDF.
*   $L^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}, \omega)$: Zero-order radiance (emission or direct illumination estimate).

### Parameters
*   **Russian Roulette**: A survival probability $q$ is used to terminate paths stochastically to avoid infinite recursion [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Calculate Boundary Distance**: Compute $D$ and $\hat{D}$ based on $\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}, \omega, \hat{\omega}$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Sample Free-Flight Distance**: Draw $\tau$ from the probability density $p(\tau) = \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}') T(\mathbf{x}', \mathbf{x})$, where $T$ is the transmittance [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Branch on Event Type**:
    *   **Case 1: Volumetric Event ($\tau < D$)**
        1.  Compute new position and derivative: $\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} - \tau \omega$ and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}' = \hat{\mathbf{x}} - \tau \hat{\omega}$ [S1, unofficial]. Ensure $\mathbf{x}' \in \Omega \setminus \partial \Omega$ [S1, unofficial].
        2.  Compute scattering ratio $\alpha$ and derivative term $\beta$:
            $$ \alpha = \frac{\sigma_s(\mathbf{x}')}{\sigma_t(\mathbf{x}')} $$
            $$ \beta = \frac{\hat{\sigma}_s(\mathbf{x}') - \Sigma_t(\mathbf{x}, \omega, \tau) \sigma_s(\mathbf{x}')}{\sigma_t(\mathbf{x}')} \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        3.  Draw new direction $\omega'$ from the phase function $f_p(\mathbf{x}', -\omega', \omega)$ [S1, unofficial].
        4.  **Recursive Call**: Call `radiance(x', x̂', ω', 0)` to obtain $(L^{(1)}, \hat{L}^{(1)})$ for the indirect term [S1, unofficial].
        5.  Initialize indirect radiance derivative $\hat{L}_{ins}$:
            $$ \hat{L}_{ins} = \hat{L}^{(1)} + \left( \frac{\hat{f}_p(\mathbf{x}', -\omega', \omega)}{f_p(\mathbf{x}', -\omega', \omega)} \right) L^{(1)} \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        6.  Estimate direct volumetric term $L_{ins}$ by integrating the source term over the hemisphere $S^2$:
            $$ L_{ins} \leftarrow L_{ins} + \int_{S^2} f_p(\mathbf{x}', -\omega', \omega) L^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}', \omega') d\omega' \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        7.  Estimate direct derivative term $\hat{L}_{ins}$ by integrating the derivative of the source term:
            $$ \hat{L}_{ins} \leftarrow \hat{L}_{ins} + \int_{S^2} \partial_\pi [f_p(\mathbf{x}', -\omega', \omega) L^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}', \omega')] d\omega' \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        8.  Add edge integral contribution:
            $$ \hat{L}_{ins} \leftarrow \hat{L}_{ins} + \text{edgeIntegral}(\mathbf{x}', \hat{\mathbf{x}}', \omega, f_p) \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        9.  Compute final volumetric estimates:
            $$ L^{(1)} = \alpha L_{ins} $$
            $$ \hat{L}^{(1)} = \alpha \hat{L}_{ins} + \beta L_{ins} \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$

    *   **Case 2: Interfacial Event ($\tau \ge D$)**
        1.  Compute intersection point and derivative: $\mathbf{x}_0 = \mathbf{x} - D \omega$ and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 = \hat{\mathbf{x}} - D \hat{\omega}$ [S1, unofficial]. Ensure $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \partial \Omega$ [S1, unofficial].
        2.  Draw new direction $\omega'$ with probability $p(\omega')$ from the local BSDF [S1, unofficial].
        3.  Compute BSDF ratio $\alpha$ and derivative term $\beta$:
            $$ \alpha = \frac{f_s(\mathbf{x}_0, \omega', \omega)}{p(\omega')} $$
            $$ \beta = \Sigma_t(\mathbf{x}, \omega, D) + \hat{D} \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}_0) \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        4.  **Recursive Call**: Call `radiance(x0, x̂0, ω', 0)` to obtain $(L^{(1)}, \hat{L}^{(1)})$ for the reflected/scattered radiance [S1, unofficial].
        5.  Compute reflected radiance $L_{rs}$ and its derivative $\hat{L}_{rs}$:
            $$ L_{rs} = \alpha L^{(1)} $$
            $$ \hat{L}_{rs} = \alpha \hat{L}^{(1)} + \left( \frac{\hat{f}_s(\mathbf{x}_0, -\omega', \omega)}{p(\omega')} \right) L^{(1)} \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        6.  Estimate direct interfacial term $L_{rs}$ by integrating the source term:
            $$ L_{rs} \leftarrow L_{rs} + \int_{S^2} f_s(\mathbf{x}_0, -\omega', \omega) L^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_0, \omega') d\omega' \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        7.  Estimate direct derivative term $\hat{L}_{rs}$ by integrating the derivative of the source term:
            $$ \hat{L}_{rs} \leftarrow \hat{L}_{rs} + \int_{S^2} \partial_\pi [f_s(\mathbf{x}_0, -\omega', \omega) L^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_0, \omega')] d\omega' \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        8.  Add edge integral contribution:
            $$ \hat{L}_{rs} \leftarrow \hat{L}_{rs} + \text{edgeIntegral}(\mathbf{x}_0, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0, \omega, f_s) \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$
        9.  Estimate in-scattered radiance $L_{ins}$ at the boundary using the method from Appendix B of [S1, unofficial].
        10. Compute final interfacial estimates:
            $$ L^{(1)} = L_{rs} $$
            $$ \hat{L}^{(1)} = -\beta L_{rs} + \hat{L}_{rs} + \hat{D} \sigma_s(\mathbf{x}_0) L_{ins} \quad \text{[S1, unofficial]} $$

4.  **Return**: Return $(L^{(1)}, \hat{L}^{(1)})$ [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def radiance(x, x_hat, omega, omega_hat, n):
    """
    Computes unbiased Monte Carlo estimates for radiance L(1) and its derivative L_hat(1).
    
    Args:
        x: Current position.
        x_hat: Scene derivative of position.
        omega: Current direction.
        omega_hat: Scene derivative of direction.
        n: Current path depth.
        
    Returns:
        L_1: Radiance estimate.
        L_hat_1: Radiance derivative estimate.
    """
    # 1. Calculate boundary distance D and its derivative D_hat
    D, D_hat = calculate_boundary_distance(x, x_hat, omega, omega_hat)
    
    # 2. Sample free-flight distance tau
    # p(tau) = sigma_t(x') * T(x', x)
    tau = sample_free_flight(x, omega)
    
    # Russian Roulette to avoid infinite recursion
    if n > 0 and random() > q: # q is survival probability
        return 0.0, 0.0

    if tau < D:
        # --- Volumetric Event ---
        
        # 3. Compute new position and derivative
        x_prime = x - tau * omega
        x_hat_prime = x_hat - tau * omega_hat
        
        # 4. Compute alpha and beta
        sigma_s_prime = get_scattering_coefficient(x_prime)
        sigma_t_prime = get_extinction_coefficient(x_prime)
        alpha = sigma_s_prime / sigma_t_prime
        
        sigma_t_x_tau = get_optical_depth(x, omega, tau)
        sigma_s_hat_prime = get_scattering_coefficient_derivative(x_prime)
        beta = (sigma_s_hat_prime - sigma_t_x_tau * sigma_s_prime) / sigma_t_prime
        
        # 5. Draw new direction from phase function
        omega_prime = sample_phase_function(x_prime, -omega, omega)
        
        # 6. Recursive call for indirect term
        L_1_rec, L_hat_1_rec = radiance(x_prime, x_hat_prime, omega_prime, 0, n + 1)
        
        # 7. Initialize indirect radiance derivative
        f_p = get_phase_function(x_prime, -omega_prime, omega)
        f_p_hat = get_phase_function_derivative(x_prime, -omega_prime, omega)
        L_hat_ins = L_hat_1_rec + (f_p_hat / f_p) * L_1_rec
        
        # 8. Estimate direct volumetric term
        # Integral over hemisphere S^2
        L_ins_direct = integrate_hemisphere(lambda w: f_p * get_zero_order_radiance(x_prime, w))
        L_hat_ins += integrate_hemisphere(lambda w: derivative_pi(f_p * get_zero_order_radiance(x_prime, w)))
        
        # 9. Add edge integral contribution
        L_hat_ins += edge_integral(x_prime, x_hat_prime, omega, get_phase_function)
        
        # 10. Final volumetric estimates
        L_1 = alpha * L_ins_direct
        L_hat_1 = alpha * L_hat_ins + beta * L_ins_direct
        
    else:
        # --- Interfacial Event ---
        
        # 11. Compute intersection point and derivative
        x_0 = x - D * omega
        x_hat_0 = x_hat - D * omega_hat
        
        # 12. Draw new direction from BSDF
        omega_prime = sample_bsdf(x_0, -omega, omega)
        p_omega = get_bsdf_probability(x_0, omega_prime, omega)
        
        # 13. Compute alpha and beta
        f_s = get_bsdf(x_0, omega_prime, omega)
        alpha = f_s / p_omega
        
        sigma_t_x_D = get_optical_depth(x, omega, D)
        sigma_t_x0 = get_extinction_coefficient(x_0)
        beta = sigma_t_x_D + D_hat * sigma_t_x0
        
        # 14. Recursive call
        L_1_rec, L_hat_1_rec = radiance(x_0, x_hat_0, omega_prime, 0, n + 1)
        
        # 15. Compute reflected radiance and derivative
        L_rs = alpha * L_1_rec
        f_s_hat = get_bsdf_derivative(x_0, -omega_prime, omega)
        L_hat_rs = alpha * L_hat_1_rec + (f_s_hat / p_omega) * L_1_rec
        
        # 16. Estimate direct interfacial term
        L_rs_direct = integrate_hemisphere(lambda w: f_s * get_zero_order_radiance(x_0, w))
        L_hat_rs += integrate_hemisphere(lambda w: derivative_pi(f_s * get_zero_order_radiance(x_0, w)))
        
        # 17. Add edge integral contribution
        L_hat_rs += edge_integral(x_0, x_hat_0, omega, get_bsdf)
        
        # 18. Estimate in-scattered radiance at boundary
        L_ins_boundary = in_scattered_radiance(x_0, omega)
        
        # 19. Final interfacial estimates
        L_1 = L_rs_direct
        L_hat_1 = -beta * L_rs_direct + L_hat_rs + D_hat * get_scattering_coefficient(x_0) * L_ins_boundary

    return L_1, L_hat_1
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision**: Calculations involving optical depth $\Sigma_t$ and derivatives $\hat{D}$ require careful handling of numerical precision, especially near boundaries where $D \to 0$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries**: The condition $\tau < D$ strictly separates volumetric and interfacial events. If $\tau = D$, the interfacial case applies [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input**: If the phase function or BSDF is zero, division by zero may occur in the derivative terms involving ratios like $\hat{f}/f$. Guard clauses or alternative sampling strategies are required [G].
*   **Edge Integrals**: The `edgeIntegral` function requires tracing additional side paths and depends on the difference $\Delta L$ of radiance across boundary curves [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The method involves recursive calls and integrals over the hemisphere, making it computationally expensive. The complexity is proportional to the number of scattering events and the cost of evaluating derivatives [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing scene derivatives and intermediate radiance values along the path. Memory usage scales with path length [G].
*   **Measured Performance**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths**: Provides unbiased estimates of radiance derivatives, enabling gradient-based optimization in differentiable rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: High variance due to the complexity of the derivative terms and the need for edge integrals. Computationally intensive compared to standard path tracing [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use**: When gradients of the radiance with respect to scene parameters are required, such as in inverse rendering or material optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use**: For standard rendering where only radiance values are needed, or when computational resources are limited [G].

## Background
The method originates from "A Differential Theory of Radiative Transfer" [S1, unofficial]. It extends the theory of radiative transfer to include derivatives, providing a framework for differentiable rendering in participating media. The approach handles both volumetric and interfacial scattering events, offering a comprehensive solution for computing radiance derivatives in complex scenes [S1, unofficial].
