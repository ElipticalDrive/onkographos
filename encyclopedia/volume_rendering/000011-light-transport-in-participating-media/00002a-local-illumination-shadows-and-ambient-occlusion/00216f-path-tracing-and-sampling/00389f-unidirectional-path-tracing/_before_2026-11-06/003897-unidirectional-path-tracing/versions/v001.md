# Unidirectional Path Tracing
Also called: Unidirectional Volumetric Path Tracing

## Overview
Unidirectional Path Tracing is a Monte Carlo method for simulating light transport in participating media by extending classical path tracing to allow rays to enter volumetric regions, sample scattering events, and continue random walks [S1][S4]. It solves the problem of computing radiance in scenes with fog, smoke, or clouds by modeling absorption, emission, and scattering within the medium [S1]. Unlike its sibling [Volumetric Radiance Estimators](818-volumetric-radiance-estimators.md), which may use deterministic or semi-analytic approaches, this method relies on stochastic sampling. It differs from [Multiple Scattering Volume Path Tracing with Phase Function Sampling](1043-multiple-scattering-volume-path-tracing-with-phase-function-.md) by lacking explicit light sampling (Next-Event Estimation) and surface scattering support, relying instead on delta tracking for distance selection and phase function sampling for direction [S4]. It is often used as a ground-truth baseline for debugging more sophisticated algorithms due to its simplicity [S4].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family are derived from the conservation of energy for a differential cylinder of light passing through a medium [S1]. They share the following core definitions and mechanisms:

**1. Fundamental Interactions**
Any volumetric effect is composed of three fundamental characteristics [S1]:
*   **Absorption:** The loss of radiant energy along a ray due to energy conversion (e.g., to heat). It is described by the absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$ (units: $m^{-1}$) [S1].
*   **Emission:** The addition of radiant energy where the medium is luminous (e.g., flames). It is described by the emissive term $L_e(\mathbf{p}, \boldsymbol{\omega})$, representing radiance emitted per unit length [S1].
*   **Scattering:** The redirection of light rays upon collision with particles. It is split into:
    *   *In-scattering:* Light from other directions reflected into the view ray.
    *   *Out-scattering:* Light from the view ray reflected away.
    In most lighting calculations, out-scattering is folded into the **extinction** term because its net effect on the view ray is identical to absorption [S1].

**2. Notation**
*   $\mathbf{p}$: Position of interaction.
*   $\boldsymbol{\omega}$: Ray direction (unit vector).
*   $L$: Radiance quantity.
*   $L_i$: Incoming radiance.
*   $L_o$: Outgoing radiance.
*   $\sigma_t$: Total extinction coefficient, $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$ [S1].

**3. Differential Forms**
The change in radiance $dL$ over a differential path length $dt$ is governed by the balance of loss and gain terms [S1]:
*   **Absorption Model:** $dL = -\sigma_a L_i dt$ [S1].
*   **Emission Model:** $dL = \sigma_e dt$ (where $\sigma_e$ is the emission coefficient) [S1].
*   **General RTE:** Combines extinction (loss) and source terms (gain from emission and in-scattering) [S1].

**4. Transmittance**
The fraction of light surviving transmission through a medium is defined by the Beer-Lambert law [S1]:
$$ T = e^{-\tau} $$
where $\tau$ is the optical depth (integrated extinction) [S1].

**5. Phase Functions**
Scattering directionality is modeled by a phase function $p(\omega_i, \omega_o)$, defined in terms of $a = \cos(\theta)$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the incident direction $\omega_i$ and the scattered direction $\omega_o$ [S1]. The choice of phase function depends on the ratio of particle radius $r$ to wavelength $\lambda$ [S1]:
*   **Rayleigh Scattering:** Used when $r \ll \lambda$ (e.g., smoke, dust, water molecules).
    $$ P_R(a) = \frac{3}{4}(1+a)^2 $$
*   **Mie Scattering:** Used when $r \approx \lambda$ (e.g., fog, water droplets). Common variants include:
    *   Haziness: $P_{HM}(a) = 1 + 9 \left( \frac{1+a}{2} \right)^8$
    *   Murkiness: $P_{MM}(a) = 1 + 50 \left( \frac{1+a}{2} \right)^{32}$
*   **Henyey-Greenstein (HG):** An approximation to Mie scattering, widely used in graphics for its efficiency and asymmetry parameter $g$:
    $$ P_{HG}(a) = \frac{1 - g^2}{(1 + g^2 - 2ga)^{\frac{3}{2}}} $$
*   **Schlick's Approximation:** A computationally cheaper approximation to HG that avoids fractional exponentiation:
    $$ P_S(a) = \frac{1-g^2}{(1-ga)^2} $$

## Subtopics

### [Volumetric Path Tracing](1863-volumetric-path-tracing.md)
A Monte Carlo method that extends classical path tracing to participating media. It uses delta tracking for distance selection and phase function sampling for direction, lacking explicit light sampling and surface scattering support. It serves as a ground-truth baseline for debugging.

### [Volumetric-Primitives Path Tracer](4107-volumetric-primitives-path-tracer.md)
A forward rendering method modeling the scene as 3D kernel-based volumetric primitives. It supports multiple scattering via Next-Event Estimation and provides closed-form solutions for transmittance and free-flight distance sampling, avoiding probabilistic grid-based approximations.

### CPU-based Volumetric Path Tracing with MIS
A rendering technique using a CPU-based volumetric path tracer with Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) to render generated volumetric data across different LoDs. It uses Woodcock’s Delta Tracking technique for transmittance estimation during the path tracing process [S1].

## Background
The foundational principles of light transport in participating media, including the Radiative Transfer Equation (RTE) and the Beer-Lambert law, are derived from the conservation of energy for a differential cylinder of light [S1]. The specific implementation of Unidirectional Path Tracing using delta tracking for volumetric path tracing is described in [S4]. The variant using CPU-based path tracing with MIS for Level of Detail rendering is described in [S1].
