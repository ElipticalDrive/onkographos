# Bidirectional Volume Boundary Estimator

Also called: Bidirectional estimator, Multi-directional volume boundary sampling

## Overview
A Monte Carlo estimator that samples volume boundary paths by first drawing the volume boundary vertex, then constructing two subpaths to connect it to the light source and the detector. It addresses the variance reduction problem inherent in using a single sampling strategy for boundary paths, particularly when dealing with homogeneous translucent objects [S1]. Unlike the unidirectional estimator, which builds paths from the detector, or the bidirectional estimator, which samples the boundary vertex first, MIS allows both strategies to contribute to the final estimate, weighting their contributions by their respective sampling probabilities [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Scene geometry and material properties.
- Camera position and lens parameters.
- Light source positions and emission profiles.
- Volume boundary definition (implicit or explicit surface).

**Outputs:**
- Radiance estimate for the pixel.

**Data Structures:**
- Volume boundary vertex $p^B$: A point on the volume boundary.
- Subpath vertices: Points connecting $p^B$ to light and detector.

**Steps:**
1. Draw a volume boundary vertex $p^B$ from the volume boundary path space [S1].
2. Construct a subpath from $p^B$ to the light source using unidirectional sampling with next-event estimation [S1].
3. Construct a subpath from $p^B$ to the detector using unidirectional sampling with next-event estimation [S1].

**Formulas:**
The contribution of a path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ sampled by the bidirectional estimator is weighted using the balanced heuristic [Vea97] when combined with the unidirectional estimator [S1]:

$$
\langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}_{\text{bnd}} \rangle_{\text{mis}} = \frac{f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K1})}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)} + \frac{f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K2})}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)}
$$

Where:
- $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ is the path sampled by the unidirectional estimator.
- $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ is the path sampled by the bidirectional estimator.
- $f(\cdot)$ is the path throughput.
- $v_{\perp}(\cdot)$ is the visibility term.
- $\text{pdf}_1(\cdot)$ and $\text{pdf}_2(\cdot)$ are the probability density functions of the unidirectional and bidirectional estimators, respectively [S1].

**Parameters:**
- Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Precision issues may arise when computing the PDFs for the balanced heuristic if the sampling strategies have vastly different likelihoods [S1].
- The method assumes that the volume boundary vertex can be sampled effectively [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def bidirectional_volume_boundary_estimator(scene, camera, light):
    # Step 1: Draw a volume boundary vertex p^B from the volume boundary path space
    p_B = sample_volume_boundary_vertex(scene)
    
    # Step 2: Construct a subpath from p^B to the light source using unidirectional sampling with next-event estimation
    subpath_to_light = unidirectional_subpath_to_light(p_B, scene, light)
    
    # Step 3: Construct a subpath from p^B to the detector using unidirectional sampling with next-event estimation
    subpath_to_detector = unidirectional_subpath_to_detector(p_B, scene, camera)
    
    # Calculate the path throughput and visibility
    throughput = calculate_throughput(subpath_to_light, subpath_to_detector)
    visibility = calculate_visibility(subpath_to_light, subpath_to_detector)
    
    # Return the radiance estimate
    return throughput * visibility
```

## Subtopics

### [Multiple Importance Sampling for Boundary Paths](6397-multiple-importance-sampling-for-boundary-paths.md)
This method combines the unidirectional and bidirectional volume boundary estimators using path-level Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) with the balanced heuristic. It addresses the variance reduction problem inherent in using a single sampling strategy for boundary paths, particularly when dealing with homogeneous translucent objects. Unlike the unidirectional estimator, which builds paths from the detector, or the bidirectional estimator, which samples the boundary vertex first, MIS allows both strategies to contribute to the final estimate, weighting their contributions by their respective sampling probabilities.

### Multi-Directional Sampling of Boundary Paths
A method to numerically estimate the boundary integral in Eq. 3.3 by avoiding explicit silhouette detection. It samples the material boundary segment $p_{S0} p_{D0}$ and uses standard methods (unidirectional/bidirectional path sampling) to construct source and detector subpaths [S2, unofficial]. Instead of sampling endpoints $p_{S0}$ and $p_{D0}$ directly, sample a point $x_B \in M$ and a direction $\omega_B \in S^2$ such that $x_B$ lies in the interior of the segment $x_{S0} x_{D0}$ and $\omega_B$ determines the segment directions. Compute endpoints via: $x_{S0} = \text{rayIntersect}(x_B, -\omega_B)$ and $x_{D0} = \text{rayIntersect}(x_B, \omega_B)$ [S2, unofficial]. Explicit silhouette detection (e.g., Monte Carlo edge sampling) is prohibitively expensive for complex geometry. This method avoids it because $x_{S0}$ and $x_{D0}$ are guaranteed to be on each other's visibility boundary by construction [S2, unofficial].

- **Strengths:** Reduces variance compared to unidirectional sampling by exploring the path space from both the light and detector perspectives [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires efficient sampling of the volume boundary vertex, which can be challenging for complex geometries [S1].
- **When to Use:** When dealing with homogeneous translucent objects where variance reduction is critical [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** When the volume boundary vertex sampling is inefficient or when the scene geometry is too complex for effective boundary sampling [S1].

## Background
The bidirectional estimator was introduced as an extension to the unidirectional volume boundary estimator to improve the sampling of volume boundary paths [S1]. It is conceptually similar to the multi-directional technique introduced by Zhang et al. [ZMY* 20] for sampling ordinary boundary paths [S1]. The method is part of the broader field of path-space differentiable volume rendering, which aims to efficiently compute gradients with respect to scene parameters [S1].
