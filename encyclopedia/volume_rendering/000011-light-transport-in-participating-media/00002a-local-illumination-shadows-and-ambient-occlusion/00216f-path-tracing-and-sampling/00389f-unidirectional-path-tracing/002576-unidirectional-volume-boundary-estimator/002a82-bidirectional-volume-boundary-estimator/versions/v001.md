# Bidirectional Volume Boundary Estimator

Also called: Bidirectional estimator, Multi-directional volume boundary sampling

## Overview
A Monte Carlo estimator that samples volume boundary paths by first drawing the volume boundary vertex, then constructing two subpaths to connect it to the light source and the detector. It builds on [Unidirectional Volume Boundary Estimator](3538-unidirectional-volume-boundary-estimator.md) by reversing the dependency: instead of extending from a main path, it samples the boundary interaction point directly. This approach is conceptually similar to the multi-directional technique by Zhang et al. [ZMY* 20] for sampling ordinary boundary paths, but applied to volume boundary paths [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Camera ray, scene geometry, volume density field, light sources.
**Outputs:** Radiance contribution from volume boundary paths.

**Data Structures:**
*   `VolumeBoundaryVertex`: Represents the interaction point $p^B$ on the volume boundary.
*   `Subpath`: A sequence of vertices connecting $p^B$ to a source (light or camera).

**Steps:**
1.  **Sample Volume Boundary Vertex:** Draw a volume boundary vertex $p^B$ from the volume boundary path space. The probability density function (PDF) for this sample is denoted as $pdf_{boundary}(p^B)$.
2.  **Construct Light Subpath:** From $p^B$, construct a subpath to the light source using unidirectional sampling with next-event estimation (NEE). This involves sampling directions from $p^B$ and checking for intersections with light sources. Let the PDF for this subpath be $pdf_{light}(p^B \to \text{light})$.
3.  **Construct Detector Subpath:** From $p^B$, construct a subpath to the detector (camera) using unidirectional sampling with next-event estimation. This involves tracing rays from $p^B$ towards the camera or sampling camera rays that intersect $p^B$. Let the PDF for this subpath be $pdf_{camera}(p^B \to \text{camera})$.
4.  **Estimate Contribution:** Compute the radiance contribution using the product of the subpath PDFs and the boundary scattering term. If multiple boundary paths are sampled (e.g., from both unidirectional and bidirectional estimators), apply Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) using the balanced heuristic [Vea97].

**Formula:**
The contribution of a bidirectional volume boundary path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ is weighted by:
$$ w_2 = \frac{f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K2})}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)} $$
where $f$ is the scattering function, $v_{\perp}$ is the projected solid angle term, and $\text{pdf}_1, \text{pdf}_2$ are the PDFs of the unidirectional and bidirectional estimators respectively [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Number of subpath vertices: Typically 1-2 for the boundary connection, depending on the complexity of the volume and light interaction.
*   MIS weights: Balanced heuristic is used [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Visibility:** Ensuring that the subpaths from $p^B$ to the light and camera are valid (unoccluded) is critical. NEE helps but requires robust ray tracing.
*   **PDF Calculation:** Accurately computing the PDF for the volume boundary vertex sample and the subpaths is essential for unbiased results.
*   **Complex Geometry:** For complex scenes, sampling the volume boundary vertex directly can be challenging. Variants like Multi-Directional Sampling can help [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Multi-Directional Sampling of Boundary Paths
A method to numerically estimate the boundary integral by avoiding explicit silhouette detection. It samples a point $x_B$ on the material boundary and a direction $\omega_B$ such that $x_B$ lies in the interior of the segment between the source and detector endpoints. The endpoints are then computed as $x_{S0} = \text{rayIntersect}(x_B, -\omega_B)$ and $x_{D0} = \text{rayIntersect}(x_B, \omega_B)$ [S2, unofficial]. This guarantees that the endpoints are on each other's visibility boundary, avoiding expensive silhouette detection [S2, unofficial]. Choose this when explicit silhouette detection is prohibitively expensive for complex geometry [S2, unofficial].

### Multiple Importance Sampling for Boundary Paths
Zhang et al. (2020) decompose the boundary integral into direct and indirect terms using next-event estimation. The direct term accounts for boundary paths where the vertex $x_{S0}$ is on a light source. For area lights, sample a point $x_{S0}$ on the light surface and set $\omega_B = \text{normalize}(x_B - x_{S0})$. For complex illumination (e.g., image-based), directly sample the direction $\omega_B$ because sampling from the light source often yields invalid (occluded) directions [S2, unofficial]. Choose this for complex illumination conditions where direct light sampling is inefficient [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the cost of sampling the volume boundary vertex and constructing the subpaths. Generally comparable to unidirectional methods but may require more sophisticated sampling strategies for the boundary vertex.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it does not store large path structures beyond the current subpaths.
*   **Performance:** Can be more efficient than unidirectional methods in scenes with complex lighting or geometry where direct sampling of the boundary is difficult. However, it may introduce higher variance if the boundary sampling is not well-balanced with the subpath sampling.
*   **Strengths:** Avoids the need to extend from a main path, allowing for more flexible sampling of the boundary interaction. Effective in combination with unidirectional methods via MIS.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate PDF computation for the boundary vertex and subpaths. Can be complex to implement correctly.
*   **When to use:** When unidirectional sampling is inefficient or when the boundary interaction is difficult to reach from the main path.
*   **When not to use:** In simple scenes where unidirectional sampling is sufficient and efficient.

## Background
The bidirectional estimator for volume boundary paths was introduced in the context of path-space differentiable volume rendering [S1]. It builds on the multi-directional technique for ordinary boundary paths proposed by Zhang et al. [ZMY* 20] [S1]. The method aims to improve the efficiency and accuracy of volume boundary path sampling in participating media.
