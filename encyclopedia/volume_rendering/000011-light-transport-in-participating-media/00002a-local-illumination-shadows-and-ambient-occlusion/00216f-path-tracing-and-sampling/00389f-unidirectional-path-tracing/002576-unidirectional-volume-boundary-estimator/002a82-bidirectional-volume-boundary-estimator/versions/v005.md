# Bidirectional Volume Boundary Estimator

Also called: Bidirectional estimator, EstimateBoundaryIntegral, Multi-Directional Boundary Integral Estimation, Multi-Directional Form of the Boundary Integral, Multi-directional estimation of the boundary integral, Multi-directional volume boundary sampling

## Overview
A Monte Carlo estimator that samples volume boundary paths by first drawing the volume boundary vertex, then constructing two subpaths to connect it to the light source and the detector [S2][S3, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent, the [Unidirectional Volume Boundary Estimator](3538-unidirectional-volume-boundary-estimator.md), by decoupling the boundary integral from the interior term and building boundary light paths in a multi-directional manner to avoid costly silhouette edge searches [S1][S3, unofficial]. Unlike the unidirectional approach which builds paths from the detector, this method samples the boundary vertex first, enabling a symmetric construction of source and detector subpaths [S1].

## How it works
The estimator computes the boundary integral contribution by sampling a boundary segment defined by a spatial point $\mathbf{x}_B$ and direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$, then estimating source and detector subpaths [S3, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
- Probability density $P(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B)$ for sampling the boundary segment [S3, unofficial].
- Boolean `direct`: indicates whether to use Next-Event Estimation (NEE) for direct illumination or estimate indirect paths [S3, unofficial].
- Scene geometry $M(\pi)$ and material properties [S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- The estimated boundary term contribution $T_S T_B T_D$ [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- Boundary segment vertices: $\mathbf{p}_0^S$ (source side), $\mathbf{p}_0^D$ (detector side) [S1][S3, unofficial].
- Spatial points: $\mathbf{x}_0^S = \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{p}_0^S, \pi)$, $\mathbf{x}_0^D = \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{p}_0^D, \pi)$ [S3, unofficial].
- Boundary vertex: $\mathbf{x}_B$ on the silhouette [S3, unofficial].
- Direction: $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ [S3, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1. **Sample Boundary Segment:**
   Draw $(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B)$ from the probability density $P(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B)$ [S3, unofficial].
   Trace a ray from $\mathbf{x}_B$ in direction $-\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ to find intersection $\mathbf{x}_0^S$ on the mesh [S3, unofficial].
   Trace a ray from $\mathbf{x}_B$ in direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ to find intersection $\mathbf{x}_0^D$ on the mesh [S3, unofficial].
   *Note:* The spatial counterpart of the boundary segment $\mathbf{x}_0^S \mathbf{x}_0^D$ must intersect the evolving surface $M(\pi)$ at exactly one point $\mathbf{x}_B$ besides the endpoints [S3, unofficial].

2. **Validate Intersections:**
   If $\mathbf{x}_0^S$ or $\mathbf{x}_0^D$ are invalid (no intersection or occlusion), return 0 [S3, unofficial].

3. **Compute Boundary Term ($T_B$):**
   Calculate the boundary contribution function $\hat{f}_B$:
   $$ \hat{f}_B := \Delta G(\mathbf{x}_0^S \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_0^D) V_{\Delta B} $$
   where $V_{\Delta B}$ is the scalar normal velocity of $\mathbf{p}_0^D$ with $\mathbf{p}_0^S$ fixed [S3, unofficial].
   Compute the Jacobian $J_B(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B)$. In the local parameterization where $\mathbf{X}(\cdot, \pi_0)$ and $\mathbf{P}(\cdot, \pi_0)$ are identity maps:
   $$ J_B(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B) = \frac{\|\mathbf{x}_0^D - \mathbf{x}_0^S\|}{\|\mathbf{x}_B - \mathbf{x}_0^S\|} \frac{\sin \theta_B}{\sin \theta_D |\cos \theta_S|} $$
   where $\theta_B$ is the angle between $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ and the face edge at $\mathbf{x}_B$, $\theta_D$ is the angle between $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ and the visibility boundary at $\mathbf{x}_0^D$, and $\theta_S$ is the angle between $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ and the surface normal at $\mathbf{x}_0^S$ [S3, unofficial].
   Compute $T_B$:
   $$ T_B = \frac{\hat{f}_B J_B(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B)}{P(\mathbf{x}_B, \boldsymbol{\omega}_B)} $$
   [S3, unofficial].

4. **Compute Source Term ($T_S$):**
   Map spatial points to path space: $\mathbf{p}_0^S = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x}_0^S, \pi)$ and $\mathbf{p}_0^D = \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x}_0^D, \pi)$ [S3, unofficial].
   If `direct` is true:
   $$ T_S = L_e(\mathbf{x}_0^S \to \mathbf{x}_0^D) $$
   [S3, unofficial].
   Else:
   $$ T_S = \text{EstimateSourcePath}(\mathbf{p}_0^S; \mathbf{p}_0^D) $$
   [S3, unofficial].

5. **Compute Detector Term ($T_D$):**
   $$ T_D = \text{EstimateDetectorPath}(\mathbf{p}_0^D; \mathbf{p}_0^S) $$
   [S3, unofficial].

6. **Return Result:**
   Return $T_S T_B T_D$ [S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def EstimateBoundaryIntegral(P_boundary, direct, scene):
    """
    Estimates the boundary integral using the bidirectional/multi-directional approach.
    
    Args:
        P_boundary: Function to sample (x_B, omega_B) from probability density P(x_B, omega_B).
        direct: Boolean, if True use Next-Event Estimation for direct illumination.
        scene: Object containing geometry M(pi) and material properties.
        
    Returns:
        Float: The estimated boundary term contribution T_S * T_B * T_D.
    """
    # Step 1: Sample boundary segment
    x_B, omega_B = P_boundary()
    
    # Trace rays to find endpoints of the boundary segment
    x_0S = scene.ray_trace(x_B, -omega_B)
    x_0D = scene.ray_trace(x_B, omega_B)
    
    # Step 2: Validate intersections
    if x_0S is None or x_0D is None:
        return 0.0
        
    # Step 3: Compute Boundary Term T_B
    # Calculate geometric term Delta G
    delta_G = compute_delta_geometry(x_0S, x_0D)
    
    # V_delta_B: Scalar normal velocity of p_0^D with p_0^S fixed
    V_delta_B = scene.get_normal_velocity(x_0D, x_0S)
    
    f_hat_B = delta_G * V_delta_B
    
    # Compute Jacobian J_B
    # Angles: theta_B (omega_B vs face edge at x_B), theta_D (omega_B vs visibility boundary at x_0D),
    # theta_S (omega_B vs surface normal at x_0S)
    theta_B = angle_between(omega_B, get_face_edge_at(x_B))
    theta_D = angle_between(omega_B, get_visibility_boundary_at(x_0D))
    theta_S = angle_between(omega_B, get_surface_normal_at(x_0S))
    
    dist_x0S_x0D = norm(x_0D - x_0S)
    dist_xB_x0S = norm(x_0S - x_B)
    
    # Handle degenerate cases where distances or cosines are zero
    if dist_xB_x0S == 0 or abs(math.cos(theta_S)) < 1e-8:
        return 0.0
        
    J_B = (dist_x0S_x0D / dist_xB_x0S) * (math.sin(theta_B) / (math.sin(theta_D) * abs(math.cos(theta_S))))
    
    # Probability density of the sample
    p_val = P_boundary.pdf(x_B, omega_B)
    if p_val == 0:
        return 0.0
        
    T_B = (f_hat_B * J_B) / p_val
    
    # Step 4: Compute Source Term T_S
    # Map to path space
    p_0S = scene.project_to_path_space(x_0S)
    p_0D = scene.project_to_path_space(x_0D)
    
    if direct:
        # Direct illumination: Emitter radiance from x_0S to x_0D
        T_S = scene.emitter_radiance(x_0S, x_0D)
    else:
        # Indirect illumination: Estimate source subpath
        # [G] Standard unidirectional path tracing from p0S towards light, returning throughput T_S.
        T_S = estimate_source_path(p_0S, p_0D, scene)
        
    # Step 5: Compute Detector Term T_D
    # [G] Standard unidirectional path tracing from p0D towards detector, returning throughput T_D.
    T_D = estimate_detector_path(p_0D, p_0S, scene)
    
    # Step 6: Return product
    return T_S * T_B * T_D

# Helper definitions
def norm(v):
    return math.sqrt(v[0]**2 + v[1]**2 + v[2]**2)

def dot(a, b):
    return a[0]*b[0] + a[1]*b[1] + a[2]*b[2]

def cross(a, b):
    return (a[1]*b[2] - a[2]*b[1], a[2]*b[0] - a[0]*b[2], a[0]*b[1] - a[1]*b[0])

def normalize(v):
    n = norm(v)
    return (v[0]/n, v[1]/n, v[2]/n) if n > 1e-8 else (0, 0, 0)

def angle_between(a, b):
    cos_theta = dot(a, b) / (norm(a) * norm(b))
    cos_theta = max(-1.0, min(1.0, cos_theta))
    return math.acos(cos_theta)

def compute_delta_geometry(p1, p2):
    # Standard geometric term for visibility between two points on a surface
    n1 = get_surface_normal_at(p1)
    n2 = get_surface_normal_at(p2)
    d = p2[0]-p1[0], p2[1]-p1[1], p2[2]-p1[2]
    dist_sq = norm(d)**2
    if dist_sq == 0: return 0.0
    return dot(n1, d) * dot(n2, (-d[0], -d[1], -d[2])) / dist_sq

def get_face_edge_at(point):
    # Returns a tangent vector on the mesh face at point
    return (1.0, 0.0, 0.0)

def get_visibility_boundary_at(point):
    # Returns a tangent vector along the visibility boundary at point
    return (0.0, 1.0, 0.0)

def get_surface_normal_at(point):
    # Returns the surface normal at point
    return (0.0, 0.0, 1.0)

def estimate_source_path(p0S, p0D, scene):
    # [G] Standard unidirectional path tracing from p0S towards light
    return scene.trace_path(p0S, p0D, direction='light')

def estimate_detector_path(p0D, p0S, scene):
    # [G] Standard unidirectional path tracing from p0D towards detector
    return scene.trace_path(p0D, p0S, direction='detector')
```

**Parameters:**
- $V_{\Delta B}$: Scalar normal velocity of $\mathbf{p}_0^D$ with $\mathbf{p}_0^S$ fixed [S3, unofficial].
- `direct`: Boolean flag for using NEE [S3, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
- Identifying silhouette edges is costly [S3, unofficial]. The method avoids this by sampling $\mathbf{x}_B$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}_B$ directly, ensuring the segment intersects the surface at $\mathbf{x}_B$ [S3, unofficial].
- Precision issues may arise if $\mathbf{x}_B$ is very close to $\mathbf{x}_0^S$ or if $\cos \theta_S$ is near zero [S3, unofficial].
- The method assumes the boundary segment's spatial counterpart intersects the evolving surface at exactly one point $\mathbf{x}_B$ besides the endpoints [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Multi-Directional Sampling of Boundary Paths](6393-multi-directional-sampling-of-boundary-paths.md)
Reparameterizes the boundary segment from two independent endpoints to an interior point and a direction, guaranteeing endpoints lie on each other's visibility boundary by construction.

### [Multiple Importance Sampling for Boundary Paths](6397-multiple-importance-sampling-for-boundary-paths.md)
Combines the unidirectional and bidirectional volume boundary estimators using path-level MIS with the balanced heuristic to reduce variance.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the cost of sampling the boundary segment and tracing rays. Avoids explicit silhouette search, which is costly for complex geometries [S3, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it does not require storing large silhouette edge lists [S3, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Unbiased estimation without expensive silhouette identification [S3, unofficial]. Conceptually similar to multi-directional techniques for ordinary boundary paths but applied to volume boundary paths [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful sampling of the boundary segment to ensure valid intersections [S3, unofficial]. Performance depends on the efficiency of the source and detector path estimation subroutines.
- **When to use:** When dealing with complex geometries where silhouette identification is prohibitive, or when unbiased boundary path sampling is required [S3, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** If direct silhouette information is already available and cheap to use, other methods might be simpler [G].

## Background
Originated in the context of path-space differentiable rendering [S3, unofficial]. The bidirectional estimator is conceptually similar to the multi-directional technique introduced by Zhang et al. [ZMY* 20] for sampling ordinary boundary paths [S1]. It addresses the challenge of costly silhouette edge identification in prior unbiased methods [S3, unofficial].
