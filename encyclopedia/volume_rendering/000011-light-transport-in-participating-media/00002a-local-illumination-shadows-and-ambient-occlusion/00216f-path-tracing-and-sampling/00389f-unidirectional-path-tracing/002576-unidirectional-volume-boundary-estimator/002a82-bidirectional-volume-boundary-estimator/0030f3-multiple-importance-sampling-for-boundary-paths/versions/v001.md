# Multiple Importance Sampling for Boundary Paths

Also called: MIS for direct boundary paths

## Overview
This method combines the unidirectional and bidirectional volume boundary estimators using path-level Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) with the balanced heuristic [S1]. It addresses the variance reduction problem inherent in using a single sampling strategy for boundary paths, particularly when dealing with homogeneous translucent objects [S1]. Unlike the unidirectional estimator, which builds paths from the detector, or the bidirectional estimator, which samples the boundary vertex first, MIS allows both strategies to contribute to the final estimate, weighting their contributions by their respective sampling probabilities [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Two independently sampled volume boundary paths, $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ (sampled via the unidirectional method) and $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ (sampled via the bidirectional method).
*   **Outputs:** The combined radiance estimate $\langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}_{\text{bnd}} \rangle_{\text{mis}}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_i$:** A sequence of vertices representing a volume boundary path. The length $K_i$ (number of vertices) may differ between $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ [S1].
*   **PDFs:** $\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}})$ and $\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}})$ represent the probability densities of sampling the specific path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ using the unidirectional and bidirectional methods, respectively.

**Formulas:**
The combined estimator uses the balanced heuristic [Vea97]:

$$
\langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}_{\text{bnd}} \rangle_{\text{mis}} = \frac{f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K1})}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)} + \frac{f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K2})}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)}
$$

Where:
*   $f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_i)$ is the path throughput (product of phase functions, extinction coefficients, and geometric terms) for path $i$.
*   $v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{Ki})$ is the perpendicular visibility term at the last vertex of the path.
*   $\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)$ is the probability of generating $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ using the unidirectional method.
*   $\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)$ is the probability of generating $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ using the bidirectional method (evaluated for the path generated by the other method).
*   $\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)$ is the probability of generating $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ using the unidirectional method.
*   $\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)$ is the probability of generating $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ using the bidirectional method.

**Steps:**
1.  Sample a volume boundary path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ using the unidirectional estimator. Record its throughput $f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)$, visibility $v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K1})$, and sampling probability $\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)$ [S1].
2.  Sample a volume boundary path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ using the bidirectional estimator. Record its throughput $f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)$, visibility $v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K2})$, and sampling probability $\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)$ [S1].
3.  Compute the probability density of path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ under the bidirectional sampling strategy, denoted $\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)$ [S1].
4.  Compute the probability density of path $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ under the unidirectional sampling strategy, denoted $\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)$ [S1].
5.  Calculate the contribution of $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ using the balanced heuristic weight:
    $$ w_1 = \frac{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)} $$
    Contribution: $w_1 \cdot f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K1})$ [S1].
6.  Calculate the contribution of $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ using the balanced heuristic weight:
    $$ w_2 = \frac{\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)}{\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) + \text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)} $$
    Contribution: $w_2 \cdot f(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{K2})$ [S1].
7.  Sum the contributions to obtain the final estimate [S1].

**Direct Boundary Path Sampling (Next-Event Estimation):**
For the direct component of the boundary integral, the vertex $x_{S0}$ is located on a light source [S2, unofficial].
1.  Select the boundary vertex $x_B$.
2.  Sample the direction $\omega_B$ of the boundary segment.
    *   **For area lights:** Sample a point $x_{S0}$ on the light surface. Set $\omega_B = \text{normalize}(x_B - x_{S0})$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   **For complex illumination (e.g., image-based):** Directly sample the direction $\omega_B$ from the environment. Sampling from the light source is often inefficient because the resulting direction is frequently occluded [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Homogeneous Optical Density:** The MIS estimator is specifically used for objects with homogeneous optical densities [S1].
*   **Spatially Varying Albedo:** For objects with spatially varying albedo, the bidirectional estimator is used exclusively, not the MIS combination [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Delta Tracking:** Computing $\text{pdf}_1$ and $\text{pdf}_2$ is difficult when delta tracking is used to sample free-flight distances, as the probability density calculation becomes complex [S1].
*   **Path Lengths:** The paths $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2$ can have varying lengths, and $K_1$ does not have to equal $K_2$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method requires two independent path samples and the computation of cross-probabilities ($\text{pdf}_2(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_1)$ and $\text{pdf}_1(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_2)$).
*   **Strengths:** Reduces variance by combining the strengths of unidirectional and bidirectional sampling. Effective for homogeneous translucent objects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Difficult to implement with delta tracking due to PDF calculation complexity. Not suitable for spatially varying albedo (use bidirectional only) [S1].
*   **When to use:** When rendering homogeneous translucent volumes where both unidirectional and bidirectional sampling strategies are viable and efficient.
*   **When not to use:** For volumes with spatially varying albedo, or when delta tracking is the primary method for free-flight distance sampling and PDF calculation is prohibitive [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by Zhang et al. (2020) as part of a framework for differentiable volume rendering [S2, unofficial]. It builds upon the unidirectional and bidirectional volume boundary estimators [S1]. The implementation in the source paper utilizes the Enzyme automatic differentiation framework [S1]. The balanced heuristic for MIS is attributed to Veach [Vea97].
