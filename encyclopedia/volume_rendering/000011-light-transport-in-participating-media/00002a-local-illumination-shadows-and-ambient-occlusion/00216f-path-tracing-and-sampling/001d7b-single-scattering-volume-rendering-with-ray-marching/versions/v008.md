# Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching

Also called: Ray Marching for Volume Rendering, Single Scattering Ray Marching, Single-Scattering Volume Rendering with Fixed-Length Ray Marching, Volume integration via ray marching

## Overview
Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching is a real-time technique for computing volumetric light shafts in participating media by numerically integrating the radiative transport equation under the assumption that light scatters only once before reaching the camera [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of rendering physically plausible light interaction with fog, smoke, or dust while avoiding the prohibitive cost of multi-scattering simulations [S4, unofficial]. Unlike screen-space god ray techniques which are limited to visible light sources and suffer from edge artifacts, this method steps along each camera ray through the volume, sampling shadow maps or distance fields to determine local illumination, thereby handling multiple light sources, varying density, and correct occlusion from any viewing angle [S3, unofficial]. It builds on [Basic Volume Rendering and Ray Marching Loop with Absorption & Scattering](6821-basic-volume-rendering-and-ray-marching-loop-with-absorption.md) by explicitly integrating the light transport path from the volume sample to the light source, rather than relying on pre-computed ambient terms [S2][S4, unofficial].

## How it works
The method approximates the radiative transport equation by ignoring multiple scattering, meaning the in-scattering term $L_i$ no longer depends on the unknown radiance $L$ [S2]. The total radiance $L_i(c, -v)$ entering the camera at position $c$ along direction $-v$ is the sum of the attenuated surface radiance and the accumulated in-scattered light from volume emittance and light sources [S3, unofficial]:

$$
L_i(c,-v) = T_r(p,c)L_o(p,v) + \int_{0}^{d} T_r(x_t,c) \left[L_e^V(x_t,v) + \sigma_s(x_t)\int_{S^2}f_p(x_t,w,v)L_i(x_t,w) dw \right]dt
$$

Where:
*   $c$: Camera position.
*   $p$: Surface point intersected by the camera ray.
*   $d = |c-p|$: Distance from camera to surface.
*   $v = (c-p)/d$: Unit direction from surface to camera.
*   $x_t = c - vt$: Sample point along the ray for $0 \leq t \leq d$.
*   $T_r$: Transmittance from point to camera.
*   $L_o(p,v)$: Surface radiance at $p$.
*   $L_e^V(x_t,v)$: Volume emittance (self-emission).
*   $\sigma_s(x_t)$: Scattering coefficient.
*   $f_p(x_t, w, v)$: Phase function describing scattering probability from direction $w$ to $v$.
*   $L_i(x_t, w)$: Incident radiance at $x_t$ from direction $w$.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: Camera position $c$, surface intersection point $p$, surface radiance $L_o(p,v)$, volume density field (e.g., SDF or voxel grid), light positions and intensities, shadow maps (for opaque occlusion), and material parameters ($\sigma_a, \sigma_s, g$) [S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
*   **Outputs**: Final pixel radiance $L_{final}$ [S3, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Parameters**: Origin $c$, direction $v$, length $d$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Accumulators**: `accumLight` (in-scattering), `transmittance` (camera-to-point), `tau` (optical depth) [S4, unofficial].
*   **Light Visibility**: Computed via secondary ray march from sample point to light source [S5, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialize Ray Parameters**:
    Given camera $c$ and surface $p$, calculate distance $d = \text{length}(c-p)$, direction $v = (c-p)/d$, and step size $step = d / n$, where $n$ is the number of march steps (typically 30) [S3, unofficial]. Initialize `accumLight` to 0 and `transmittance` to 1.0 [S4, unofficial].

2.  **Iterate Along Camera Ray**:
    Loop $i$ from 0 to $n-1$:
    a.  Calculate sample position: $x_t = c - v \cdot (i \cdot step)$ [S3, unofficial].
    b.  **Compute Extinction**: Query the extinction coefficient $\sigma_e(x_t) = \sigma_a(x_t) + \sigma_s(x_t)$ [S3, unofficial]. Update optical depth: $\tau \leftarrow \tau + \sigma_e(x_t) \cdot step$ [S3, unofficial].
    c.  **Compute Transmittance**: Calculate current transmittance from $x_t$ to camera: $T_r(x_t, c) = e^{-\tau}$ [S3, unofficial].
    d.  **Compute In-Scattering**:
        i.  Determine visibility from $x_t$ to each light source $l_i$. This involves a secondary ray march from $x_t$ towards the light to calculate absorption using the Beer-Lambert law, combined with shadow map lookups for opaque occlusion [S3, unofficial][S5, unofficial]. Let $f_v(x_t, p_{light_i})$ be this visibility function [S3, unofficial].
        ii. Calculate the phase function value $f_p(x_t, l_i, v)$, typically using the Henyey-Greenstein function with parameter $g$ [S3, unofficial].
        iii. Calculate scattering contribution: $L_{scat}(x_t, v) = \pi \sigma_s(x_t) \sum_{i=1}^{m} f_p(x_t, l_i, v) f_v(x_t, p_{light_i}) L_{light_i}(x_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
        iv. Add to accumulator: `accumLight` $\leftarrow$ `accumLight` + $T_r(x_t, c) \cdot L_{scat}(x_t, v) \cdot step$ [S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
    e.  **Update Transmittance for Next Step**: The `transmittance` variable in the simplified code [S4, unofficial] is equivalent to $T_r(x_t, c)$ computed in step 2c. In the full formulation, $T_r(x_t, c)$ is derived from the accumulated $\tau$ [S3, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Final Radiance**:
    a.  Calculate final surface transmittance: $T_r(p, c) = e^{-\tau}$ [S3, unofficial].
    b.  Compute surface contribution: $L_{surf} = T_r(p, c) \cdot L_o(p, v)$ [S3, unofficial].
    c.  Combine terms: $L_a = \text{accumLight} + L_e$ (where $L_e$ is volume emittance, if any) [S3, unofficial].
    d.  Return $L_{final} = L_{surf} + L_a$ [S3, unofficial].

### Formulas
*   **Beer-Lambert Law (Absorption)**: $A = e^{-c \cdot d}$, where $A$ is transmitted light fraction, $c$ is absorption coefficient, $d$ is distance [S5, unofficial].
*   **Transmittance**: $T_r(p,c) = e^{-\tau}$, where $\tau = \int_{p}^{c} \sigma_e(x) |dx|$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Visibility Function**: $f_v(x_t, p_{light_i}) = S(x_i, p_{light_i}) T_r(x_i, p_{light_i})$, where $S$ is shadow function (occlusion by opaque objects) and $T_r$ is transmittance from light to point [S3, unofficial].
*   **In-Scattering Approximation**: $L_{scat}(x_t,v) = \pi \sigma_s(x_t) \sum_{i=1}^n f_p(x_t,l_i,v)f_v(x_t,p_{light_i})L_{light_i}(x_t)$ [S3, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Ray march steps $n$**: 30 for the main volume rendering loop [S3, unofficial].
*   **Shadow ray steps**: 15 for computing transmittance in visibility/shadow calculations [S3, unofficial].
*   **Number of lights**: 3 in the implementation example [S3, unofficial].
*   **Absorption Coefficient**: Material parameter; low for clear volumes (water), high for thick volumes (milk) [S5, unofficial].
*   **Phase Function Parameter $g$**: Anisotropy factor for Henyey-Greenstein [S3, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Light Leaking**: Opaque objects rendered before the volume do not account for volume shadowing, causing light to leak through the volume [S5, unofficial]. Fix by calling `GetLightVisibility` for opaque object lighting calculations [S5, unofficial].
*   **Hard Edges**: Volumes with sharp SDF boundaries appear unnatural because clouds diffuse with surrounding empty space, requiring soft blending [S5, unofficial]. Soften edges by blending density based on distance to the volume boundary (e.g., using SDF value) [S5, unofficial].
*   **Lack of Self-Shadowing**: Without marching towards lights, all parts of the volume receive equal light, which is physically incorrect as volume density between the point and light affects incoming light [S5, unofficial]. This must be fixed by computing light visibility via secondary ray marches [S5, unofficial].
*   **Precision**: Summing $L_{is}$ and $L_e$ into an accumulator $L_a$ before adding to $L_{surf}$ improves computational precision [S3, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def single_scattering_volume_rendering(camera_pos, surface_point, surface_radiance, 
                                       volume_density_func, lights, shadow_map, 
                                       num_steps=30, shadow_steps=15, g=0.8):
    """
    Computes single scattering volume rendering for a camera ray.
    """
    # 1. Initialize Ray Parameters
    ray_dir = normalize(camera_pos - surface_point)
    ray_length = length(camera_pos - surface_point)
    step_size = ray_length / num_steps
    
    tau = 0.0
    accum_light = vec3(0.0)
    
    # 2. Iterate Along Camera Ray
    for i in range(num_steps):
        t = i * step_size
        sample_pos = camera_pos - ray_dir * t
        
        # Query extinction coefficient (sigma_a + sigma_s)
        sigma_e = volume_density_func.get_extinction(sample_pos)
        
        # Update optical depth
        tau += sigma_e * step_size
        
        # Current transmittance from sample to camera
        transmittance = exp(-tau)
        
        # 2d. Compute In-Scattering
        light_contribution = vec3(0.0)
        for light in lights:
            # Calculate visibility from sample to light
            light_dir = normalize(light.position - sample_pos)
            light_dist = length(light.position - sample_pos)
            
            # Compute visibility function f_v = S * T_r
            visibility = compute_light_visibility(sample_pos, light_dir, light_dist, 
                                                  volume_density_func, shadow_map, shadow_steps)
            
            if visibility > 0.0:
                # Phase function (Henyey-Greenstein)
                phase = henyey_greenstein(dot(ray_dir, light_dir), g)
                
                # Scattering contribution
                sigma_s = volume_density_func.get_scattering(sample_pos)
                light_radiance = light.intensity * visibility
                
                # L_scat term approximation
                scatter = pi * sigma_s * phase * light_radiance
                light_contribution += scatter
                
        # Add to accumulator: T_r(x_t, c) * L_scat * step
        accum_light += transmittance * light_contribution * step_size
        
    # 3. Compute Final Radiance
    # Final transmittance from surface to camera
    final_transmittance = exp(-tau)
    surface_radiance_attenuated = final_transmittance * surface_radiance
    
    # Combine surface and accumulated in-scattering
    # L_a = L_is + L_e (assuming L_e=0 for non-emissive volumes)
    final_radiance = surface_radiance_attenuated + accum_light
    
    return final_radiance

def compute_light_visibility(origin, direction, max_dist, volume_density_func, shadow_map, num_steps):
    """
    Computes light visibility by marching from origin towards direction.
    Returns the fraction of light that reaches the point.
    """
    step_size = max_dist / num_steps
    current_transmittance = 1.0
    
    for i in range(num_steps):
        t = i * step_size
        pos = origin + direction * t
        
        # Check if inside volume
        if volume_density_func.is_inside(pos):
            sigma_e = volume_density_func.get_extinction(pos)
            current_transmittance *= exp(-sigma_e * step_size)
            
            # Early exit if fully absorbed
            if current_transmittance < 1e-5:
                return 0.0
        
        # Check shadow map for opaque occlusion
        if shadow_map.is_in_shadow(pos, direction):
            return 0.0
            
    return current_transmittance

def henyey_greenstein(cos_theta, g):
    """
    Standard Henyey-Greenstein phase function.
    cos_theta: dot product of incident and outgoing directions.
    g: anisotropy factor (-1 to 1).
    """
    g_sq = g * g
    denom = 1.0 + g_sq - 2.0 * g * cos_theta
    return (1.0 - g_sq) / (4.0 * pi * pow(denom, 1.5))
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity**: $O(n \cdot m \cdot k)$, where $n$ is camera ray steps, $m$ is number of lights, and $k$ is light ray steps [S4, unofficial].
*   **Performance**: More expensive than screen-space methods but handles multiple lights, colored fog, and varying density [S3, unofficial]. Used in Unreal Engine 5, Unity HDRP, Red Dead Redemption 2, and The Last of Us Part II [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Physically correct behavior from any viewing angle, handles multiple light sources, supports varying density and colored fog [S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: High computational cost due to nested ray marches; single scattering approximation fails for dense media where multi-scattering is significant (e.g., thick fog blurring) [S4, unofficial].
*   **Scaling**: Performance degrades linearly with the number of lights and volume density. Optimizations like early termination and light visibility checks are critical [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the need for real-time volumetric lighting in games, where full Monte Carlo solutions to the radiative transport equation are too slow [S1, unofficial]. It was popularized by techniques like those in *Crysis* (2007) for screen-space god rays, but evolved into ray marching approaches in engines like Unreal Engine 5 and Unity HDRP to support multiple lights and correct occlusion [S3, unofficial]. The theoretical basis lies in the volume rendering equation, with single-scattering approximations allowing for real-time evaluation by decoupling the in-scattering term from unknown radiance [S1, unofficial].
