# Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching

Also called: Single Scattering Ray Marching, Volumetric Ray Marching, Single-Scattering Volume Rendering with Fixed-Length Ray Marching

## Overview
Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching is a real-time technique for rendering participating media (fog, smoke, dust) by simulating light interaction with particles, assuming light scatters only once before reaching the camera [S1][S4, unofficial]. It solves the problem of generating volumetric light-shafts and self-shadowing in real-time by approximating the radiative transport equation, ignoring multiple scattering to avoid the computational cost of distant fog blurring [S1][S4, unofficial]. Unlike screen-space god-ray techniques which are limited to single visible lights and suffer from edge artifacts, this method handles multiple light sources, colored fog, varying density, and physically correct behavior from any viewing angle by stepping along each camera ray and sampling shadow maps [S3, unofficial]. It builds on [Path Tracing and Sampling](2526-path-tracing-and-sampling.md) by applying local illumination principles to volume segments.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Camera position $c$, surface point $p$ (on the opaque geometry behind the volume), surface radiance $L_o(p,v)$, light sources (position, color, intensity), shadow map data, volume density field (e.g., SDF or texture), scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$, absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$, and extinction coefficient $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$.
- **Outputs:** Final radiance $L_i(c, -v)$ at the camera.

**Data Structures:**
- **Ray:** Origin $c$, direction $v = (c-p)/|c-p|$, length $d = |c-p|$.
- **Step Parameters:** Fixed step size $step = d/n$ for camera ray, where $n$ is the number of march steps (typically 30) [S2, unofficial]. For light visibility, step size is derived from distance to light with $n=15$ steps [S2, unofficial].
- **Accumulators:** `accumLight` (in-scattering), `transmittance` (camera ray attenuation), `tau` (optical depth).

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialize Ray Parameters:**
    Calculate ray direction $v = (c-p)/d$ and step size $step = d/n$ [S2, unofficial]. Initialize `accumLight` = 0, `transmittance` = 1.0, and `tau` = 0.0 [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Camera Ray Marching Loop:**
    Iterate $i$ from 0 to $n-1$:
    a.  Calculate sample position: $x_t = c - v \cdot (i \cdot step)$ [S2, unofficial].
    b.  Update optical depth: $\tau \leftarrow \tau + \sigma_t(x_t) \cdot step$ [S2, unofficial].
    c.  Calculate current transmittance: $T_r(x_t, c) = \exp(-\tau)$ [S2, unofficial].
    d.  **Light Visibility Calculation:** For each light $i$:
        i.  Determine direction to light $l_i$.
        ii. Calculate visibility $f_v(x_t, p_{light_i})$ by marching from $x_t$ to the light source [S2, unofficial]. This involves summing extinction along the path and multiplying by shadow map occlusion $S$ [S2, unofficial].
        iii. If not in shadow, calculate scattering contribution:
        $$ L_{scat}(x_t, v) = \pi \sigma_s(x_t) \sum_{i=1}^{N_{lights}} f_p(x_t, l_i, v) f_v(x_t, p_{light_i}) L_{light_i}(x_t) $$
        where $f_p$ is the phase function (e.g., Henyey-Greenstein) [S2, unofficial].
    e.  Accumulate in-scattering:
        $$ L_{is} \leftarrow L_{is} + T_r(x_t, c) \cdot L_{scat}(x_t, v) \cdot step $$
        [S2, unofficial].
    f.  Update transmittance for the next step (or use the calculated $T_r$ directly) [S3, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Final Radiance:**
    a.  Calculate surface transmittance: $T_r(p, c) = \exp(-\tau)$ [S2, unofficial].
    b.  Calculate surface contribution: $L_{surf} = T_r(p, c) \cdot L_o(p, v)$ [S2, unofficial].
    c.  Combine terms: $L_a = L_{is} + L_e$ (where $L_e$ is volume emittance, often 0) [S2, unofficial].
    d.  Return $L_i(c, -v) = L_{surf} + L_a$ [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
- **Volume Rendering Equation (Single Scattering):**
  $$ L_i(c,-v) = T_r(p,c)L_o(p,v) + \int_{0}^{d} T_r(x_t,c) \left[L_e^V(x_t,v) + \sigma_s(x_t)\int_{S^2}f_p(x_t,w,v)L_i(x_t,w) dw \right]dt $$
  [S2, unofficial].
- **Transmittance (Beer-Lambert Law):**
  $$ T_r(p,c) = e^{-\tau}, \quad \tau = \int_{p}^{c}\sigma_e(x)|dx| $$
  where $\sigma_e = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Visibility Function:**
  $$ f_v(x_t,p_{light_i}) := S(x_i,p_{light_i})T_r(x_i,p_{light_i}) $$
  where $S$ is shadow function and $T_r$ is transmittance from light to point [S2, unofficial].
- **Henyey-Greenstein Phase Function:**
  $$ P(\theta) = \frac{1 - g^2}{4\pi (1 + g^2 - 2g\cos\theta)^{1.5}} $$
  where $g$ is the asymmetry parameter and $\theta$ is the angle between view and light rays [S5, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $n$ (camera ray steps): 30 [S2, unofficial].
- $n$ (light ray steps): 15 [S2, unofficial].
- $g$ (asymmetry): ~0.8 for forward scattering, ~-0.2 for backward scattering [S5, unofficial].
- $ABSORPTION\_COEFFICIENT$: Material-dependent; low for water, high for milk [S4, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Light Leaking:** Opaque objects rendered before the volume do not account for volume shadowing. Fix by calling `GetLightVisibility` for opaque object lighting calculations [S4, unofficial].
- **Hard Edges:** Volumes diffuse with empty space. Soften edges by modulating density with SDF distance: `density *= min(abs(sdf), max_multiplier)` [S4, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Sum $L_{is}$ and $L_e$ into an accumulator $L_a$ before adding to $L_{surf}$ to improve computational precision [S2, unofficial].
- **Self-Shadowing:** Without marching towards lights, all volume parts receive equal light. Physically incorrect [S4, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def single_scattering_volume_rendering(camera_pos, surface_point, surface_radiance, lights, shadow_map, sigma_s, sigma_a, sigma_t, phase_function):
    # Initialize
    ray_dir = normalize(camera_pos - surface_point)
    ray_length = length(camera_pos - surface_point)
    num_steps = 30
    step_size = ray_length / num_steps
    
    accum_light = vec3(0.0)
    transmittance = 1.0
    tau = 0.0
    
    # Camera Ray Marching
    for i in range(num_steps):
        # Sample position along camera ray
        sample_pos = camera_pos - ray_dir * (i * step_size)
        
        # Update optical depth
        tau += sigma_t(sample_pos) * step_size
        
        # Current transmittance from sample to camera
        current_transmittance = exp(-tau)
        
        # In-scattering calculation
        light_contribution = vec3(0.0)
        for light in lights:
            # Check if point is lit
            in_shadow = sample_shadow_map(shadow_map, light, sample_pos)
            
            if not in_shadow:
                # Calculate visibility (self-shadowing)
                light_dir = normalize(light.position - sample_pos)
                light_visibility = calculate_light_visibility(sample_pos, light_dir, light.position, sigma_t, shadow_map)
                
                # Phase function
                phase = phase_function(ray_dir, light_dir)
                
                # Scattering contribution
                scattering = sigma_s(sample_pos) * phase * light.intensity * light_visibility
                light_contribution += scattering
                
        # Accumulate in-scattering
        accum_light += current_transmittance * light_contribution * step_size
        
        # Update transmittance for next step (optional if using current_transmittance directly)
        transmittance *= exp(-sigma_t(sample_pos) * step_size)
        
    # Surface contribution
    surface_transmittance = exp(-tau)
    surface_radiance = surface_transmittance * surface_radiance
    
    # Final radiance
    final_radiance = surface_radiance + accum_light
    return final_radiance

def calculate_light_visibility(origin, direction, target, sigma_t, shadow_map):
    # March from point to light
    light_dir = normalize(target - origin)
    distance_to_light = length(target - origin)
    num_light_steps = 15
    step_size = distance_to_light / num_light_steps
    
    tau_light = 0.0
    for i in range(num_light_steps):
        t = i * step_size
        pos = origin + light_dir * t
        tau_light += sigma_t(pos) * step_size
        
    # Shadow map check (simplified)
    in_shadow = sample_shadow_map(shadow_map, light, origin) # In practice, check along path
    
    transmittance = exp(-tau_light)
    return transmittance * (1.0 if not in_shadow else 0.0)
```

## Subtopics

### [Basic Volume Rendering and Ray Marching Loop with Absorption & Scattering](6821-basic-volume-rendering-and-ray-marching-loop-with-absorption.md)
This method renders participating media by simulating light interaction with particles assuming single scattering events, avoiding the computational cost of multi-scattering effects like distant fog blurring. It differs from its sibling [Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching](1532-single-scattering-volume-rendering-with-ray-marching.md) by explicitly detailing the ray marching loop structure, distance sampling, and throughput accumulation for homogeneous and heterogeneous media.

### Volumetric Lighting with Phase Functions
Uses secondary raymarching (4-6 steps) toward the sun to integrate optical thickness and applies the Henyey-Greenstein phase function for directional scattering [S5, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by focusing on cloud rendering with specific asymmetry parameters ($g \approx 0.8$ forward, $g \approx -0.2$ backward) for edge highlights [S5, unofficial]. Choose this for cloud rendering where silver linings and forward scattering are critical.

### Optimization Strategies for Volumetric Lighting
Employs half-resolution rendering, bilateral upsampling, temporal reprojection, blue noise dithering, and early termination (transmittance < 0.01) to maintain performance [S3, unofficial]. It differs by focusing on rendering efficiency rather than physical accuracy. Choose this for real-time applications on constrained hardware.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(n \cdot m \cdot k)$, where $n$ is camera ray steps, $m$ is number of lights, and $k$ is light ray steps [S4, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on shadow map size and volume density field storage.
- **Measured Performance:** More expensive than screen-space methods but handles multiple lights and varying density [S3, unofficial]. Used in Unreal Engine 5, Unity HDRP, Red Dead Redemption 2, and The Last of Us Part II [S3, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Physically correct behavior from any viewing angle, handles multiple light sources, colored fog, and varying density [S3, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Computationally intensive, requires careful optimization for real-time use [S4, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** High-quality real-time volumetric effects where accuracy is prioritized over speed.
- **When Not to Use:** Mobile or low-end hardware where screen-space god rays are preferred [S3, unofficial].
