# Area Light Sampling with Ray-Origin Centering

Also called: Ray-Origin Centering for Area Lights

## Overview
This method generalizes [EquiAngular Sampling](2272-equiangular-sampling.md) to area lights of arbitrary shapes by using the sampled light point as the center for the ray-segment sampling distribution [S1]. It reduces high variance at light edges caused by approximating the distance $D$ with a single arbitrary point (like the light center), which fails for large, stretched, or textured lights [S1]. Unlike approximations where the $D^2$ term in the PDF dominates and causes noisy edges, this method distributes the error uniformly over the light's surface [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$, area light surface geometry, and phase function [S1].
**Outputs:** Sampled point $\mathbf{l}$ on the light surface, sampled point $\mathbf{p}$ on the ray segment, and PDF values for MIS [S1].
**Data Structures:** Light surface representation capable of evaluating position and PDF for uniform sampling, and ray structure with origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1].
**Steps:**
1. **Sample Light Point:** Sample a point $\mathbf{l}$ uniformly on the area light surface [S1]. Let $p_{light}(\mathbf{l})$ be the PDF of this sample.
2. **Calculate Distance:** Compute the distance $D$ from the sampled light point $\mathbf{l}$ to the ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ [S1]. Note that $D$ is now a function of the position sampled from the light: $D(\mathbf{l}) = \|\mathbf{l} - \mathbf{o}\|$ [S1].
3. **Determine Sampling Distribution:** Use the sampled light point $\mathbf{l}$ as the center for the equi-angular sampling equations [S1]. Specifically, use $\mathbf{l}$ in place of the fixed center used in standard equi-angular sampling to determine the sampling distribution along the ray segment between $\mathbf{o}$ and $\mathbf{l}$ [S1]. This involves calculating the angle $\theta$ between the ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ and the vector $\mathbf{l} - \mathbf{o}$, and sampling the distance $t$ along the ray based on the equiangular distribution centered at $\mathbf{l}$ [S1].
4. **Sample Ray Point:** Sample a point $\mathbf{p}$ on the ray segment using the distribution derived in Step 3 [S1]. Let $p_{ray}(\mathbf{p} | \mathbf{l})$ be the conditional PDF of this sample given $\mathbf{l}$ [S1].
5. **Apply MIS:** Apply Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) between the area light surface sampling and the phase function sampling to mask remaining noise [S1]. The combined weight uses the balance heuristic [G]:
   $$ w = \frac{p_{light}(\mathbf{l}) p_{ray}(\mathbf{p} | \mathbf{l})}{p_{light}(\mathbf{l}) p_{ray}(\mathbf{p} | \mathbf{l}) + p_{phase}(\mathbf{p}) p_{light}(\mathbf{l})} $$
   where $p_{phase}$ is the PDF of sampling the ray point based on the phase function alone [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def sample_area_light_with_ray_origin_centering(ray_origin, ray_dir, light):
    # Step 1: Sample a point on the area light surface
    l, pdf_light = light.sample_uniform()
    
    # Step 2: Calculate distance from sampled light point to ray origin
    vec_to_light = l - ray_origin
    D = length(vec_to_light)
    
    # Step 3: Use the sampled light point as the center for equi-angular sampling
    cos_theta = dot(normalize(ray_dir), normalize(vec_to_light))
    sin_theta = sqrt(1.0 - cos_theta * cos_theta)
    
    # Sample distance t along the ray using equi-angular distribution centered at l
    u = uniform(0.0, 1.0)
    t = D * tan(asin(sqrt(u) * sin_theta))
    
    # Step 4: Compute the sampled point on the ray
    p = ray_origin + t * ray_dir
    
    # Step 5: Calculate PDF for ray sampling given the light point
    pdf_ray = (sin_theta * sin_theta) / (D * D * (1.0 + (t / D) * (t / D)))
    
    # Step 6: Apply MIS with phase function
    phase_pdf = light.phase_function.pdf(ray_dir, normalize(l - p))
    
    # Balance heuristic weight
    numerator = pdf_light * pdf_ray
    denominator = numerator + pdf_light * phase_pdf
    weight = numerator / denominator
    
    return p, weight, pdf_light, pdf_ray
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Choosing a sampling center too close to the ray can cause a potential singularity. This method does not solve this singularity but distributes the error uniformly over the light's surface [S1].
- For very large lights, the approximation of using the sampled point as the center is more accurate than using the light center, but precision issues may arise if $D$ is very small [S1].

### Spherical Rectangle Solid Angle Sampling
A sampling technique for area lights that treats them as spherical rectangles to improve PDF accuracy at small solid angles [S2, unofficial]. It uses a threshold of $10^{-3}$ steradians for switching to spherical rectangle sampling [S2, unofficial]. This improves PDF for rectangular lights at small solid angles compared to standard planar sampling [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, assuming constant-time light sampling and distance calculation [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory [S1].
- **Performance:** Substantially reduces variance compared to approximations using the light center, especially for large, stretched, or textured area lights [S1].
- **Strengths:** Works for large, stretched, or textured area lights where arbitrary center approximations fail [S1]. Distributes error uniformly over the light's surface [S1].
- **Limitations:** Does not solve the singularity issue when the sampling center is too close to the ray [S1]. Requires accurate light sampling and distance calculation [S1].
- **When to Use:** When rendering scenes with large, stretched, or textured area lights in participating media [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** When light sources are very small and far away, where standard approximations may suffice, or when computational resources are extremely limited and variance reduction is not critical [S1].

## Background
This method was introduced as a generalization of equi-angular sampling for area lights in participating media [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous approximations that used arbitrary points like the light center, which failed for large lights and caused noisy edges due to the $D^2$ term dominating the PDF [S1]. The technique is applicable to path tracing and bidirectional path tracing [S1].
