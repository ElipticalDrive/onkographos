# Sphere Light Sampling with Multiple Strategies

Also called: getPixelColorWithSphereLightDBG

## Overview
Calculates direct lighting from a spherical light source using Monte Carlo integration with various sampling strategies, including Importance Solid Angle, Importance Surface Area, Uniform Solid Angle, and Phong-based sampling [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Single Scattering Volume Rendering with Ray Marching](1532-single-scattering-volume-rendering-with-ray-marching.md) which handles volume interactions, this method focuses on surface-shading direct illumination from extended spherical lights, and differs from [Sphere Light Shading with Importance Solid Angle Sampling](2236-sphere-light-shading-with-importance-solid-angle-sampling.md) by offering a switchable set of PDFs rather than a single solid-angle approach.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Surface point $X$ and surface normal $N$.
- Spherical light source defined by center $C$, radius $R$, and emitted radiance $L_i$.
- Material properties: type (DIFFUSE, SPECULAR/Phong) and exponent $k$ for Phong.
- Sampling mode enum: `Importance_Solid_Angle`, `Importance_Surface_Area`, `Uniform_Solid_Angle`, `Uniform_Hemisphere_Solid_Angle`, `Importance_Phong`, `Cosine_Weighted`.
- Number of samples $N$.

**Outputs:**
- Estimated direct radiance $L_r$ at point $X$.

**Data Structures:**
- `Vector3`: 3D floating-point vector with components $(x, y, z)$, supporting dot product, normalization, and cross-product.
- `Color`: 3D floating-point vector representing RGB radiance.
- `Ray`: Origin $O$, direction $D$, and time $t$.
- `Sphere`: Center $C$, radius $R$, color/radiance $L_i$, exponent $k$.
- `Object`: Base class for geometry, providing `findIntersection(Ray)`, `getNormalAt(Vector3)`, `getMaterial()`, and `getExponent()`.

**Steps:**

1. **Compute Geometry:**
   Calculate vector $V = C - X$ and distance $d = \|V\|$.
   Compute $\sin \theta_{max} = R / d$ and $\cos \theta_{max} = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 \theta_{max}}$ [S1, unofficial].
   If $d < R$, the point is inside the light; handle as special case or clamp [G].

2. **Sample Direction:**
   Call `getSample(Sphere, X, mode, N, sinThetaMax)` to generate a direction $D_{sample}$ towards the light based on the selected mode [S1, unofficial].
   - For `Importance_Solid_Angle` and `Cosine_Weighted`, the PDF depends on $\theta_{max}$ [S1, unofficial].
   - For `Uniform_Solid_Angle`, the PDF is constant over $4\pi$ [S1, unofficial].
   - For `Importance_Surface_Area`, the PDF is proportional to the projected surface area [S1, unofficial].

3. **Trace Light Intersection:**
   Construct a ray $R_{light}$ from $X$ in direction $D_{sample}$.
   Trace $R_{light}$ against the scene geometry.
   Let $t$ be the distance to the first intersection.
   If $t > \epsilon$ (where $\epsilon$ is a small epsilon, e.g., $10^{-9}$), the ray hits the light sphere (or another object).
   Check if the intersection point lies on the light sphere. If not, the light is occluded.

4. **Shadow Check:**
   If the ray hits an object other than the light, or if $t$ is very small, perform a shadow check.
   Construct a shadow ray $R_{shadow}$ from $X + \epsilon N$ (offset by $10^{-9}$ along normal to prevent self-intersection) to the intersection point on the light [S1, unofficial].
   Trace $R_{shadow}$.
   If an intersection occurs at distance $t_{near}$ such that $t_{near}^2 < d^2$, the point is in shadow ($inShadow = true$). Otherwise, $inShadow = false$ [S1, unofficial].

5. **Calculate Angles and BRDF:**
   Let $L_{dir}$ be the normalized direction from $X$ to the light sample point.
   Calculate $\cos \theta_i = \max(0, L_{dir} \cdot N)$ [S1, unofficial].
   Calculate $\cos \theta_o = \max(0, -N_{light} \cdot L_{dir})$, where $N_{light}$ is the normal at the light intersection point [S1, unofficial].
   
   Calculate BRDF $f_r$:
   - If Material is DIFFUSE: $f_r = 1 / \pi$ [S1, unofficial].
   - If Material is SPECULAR (Phong): 
     Let $R_{refl}$ be the reflection of the view direction about $N$.
     Let $temp = \max(0, R_{refl} \cdot L_{dir})$ [S1, unofficial].
     $f_r = \frac{k + 2}{2\pi} (temp)^k$ [S1, unofficial].

6. **Accumulate Radiance:**
   Initialize $L_r = 0$.
   For each of the $N$ samples:
   - Get PDF $p$ based on mode:
     - `Importance_Solid_Angle`: $p = 2\pi (1 - \cos \theta_{max})$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Uniform_Solid_Angle`: $p = 4\pi$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Uniform_Hemisphere_Solid_Angle`: $p = 2\pi$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Importance_Surface_Area`: $p = \frac{2\pi (1 - \sin \theta_{max}) R^2}{d^2}$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Importance_Phong`: PDF is not explicitly used in the final sum formula in the source, but the term is weighted differently [S1, unofficial].
     - `Cosine_Weighted`: PDF is not explicitly used in the final sum formula in the source [S1, unofficial].
   
   - Update $L_r$:
     - `Importance_Solid_Angle`: $L_r += f_r L_i (1 - inShadow) \cos \theta_i / p$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Importance_Surface_Area`: $L_r += L_i (1 - inShadow) \cos \theta_i \cos \theta_o \cdot Area \cdot f_r$, where $Area = 2\pi (1 - \sin \theta_{max}) R^2 / d^2$ [S1, unofficial]. Note: The source code snippet for `Importance_Surface_Area` does not divide by PDF but multiplies by Area, implying the PDF was $1/Area$ or the formula is rearranged. The text says "L_r += ... * Area * brdf". We follow the source code logic.
     - `Uniform_Solid_Angle`: $L_r += f_r L_i (1 - inShadow) \cos \theta_i \cdot 4\pi$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Uniform_Hemisphere_Solid_Angle`: $L_r += f_r L_i (1 - inShadow) \cos \theta_i \cdot 2\pi$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Importance_Phong`: $L_r += L_i (1 - inShadow) \cos \theta_i$ [S1, unofficial].
     - `Cosine_Weighted`: $L_r += L_i (1 - inShadow) f_r$ [S1, unofficial].

7. **Average:**
   $L_r = L_r / N$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import random

def get_sample_uniform_solid_angle(X, normal):
    """Samples a direction uniformly over the full sphere solid angle."""
    eta1 = random.random()
    eta2 = random.random()
    phi = 2.0 * math.pi * eta2
    cos_theta = 1.0 - 2.0 * eta1
    sin_theta = math.sqrt(1.0 - cos_theta * cos_theta)
    
    # Create local coordinate system
    # Assuming normal is not degenerate
    if abs(normal.x) < 0.9:
        V = Vector3(1, 0, 0)
    else:
        V = Vector3(0, 1, 0)
    
    W = Vector3.cross(normal, V).normalize()
    V = Vector3.cross(W, normal).normalize()
    
    sample_x = sin_theta * math.cos(phi)
    sample_y = sin_theta * math.sin(phi)
    sample_z = cos_theta
    
    direction = Vector3(
        sample_x * V.x + sample_y * W.x + sample_z * normal.x,
        sample_x * V.y + sample_y * W.y + sample_z * normal.y,
        sample_x * V.z + sample_y * W.z + sample_z * normal.z
    ).normalize()
    
    return direction

def get_sample_importance_solid_angle(sphere, X, normal, sin_theta_max):
    """Samples direction uniformly within the cone of the light."""
    # Similar to uniform but restricted to theta_max
    # PDF = 2 * pi * (1 - cos_theta_max)
    eta1 = random.random()
    eta2 = random.random()
    phi = 2.0 * math.pi * eta2
    cos_theta = 1.0 - eta1 * (1.0 - math.sqrt(1.0 - sin_theta_max * sin_theta_max))
    # Actually, uniform in cone: cos_theta = 1 - eta1 * (1 - cos_theta_max)
    cos_theta_max = math.sqrt(1.0 - sin_theta_max * sin_theta_max)
    cos_theta = 1.0 - eta1 * (1.0 - cos_theta_max)
    sin_theta = math.sqrt(1.0 - cos_theta * cos_theta)
    
    if abs(normal.x) < 0.9:
        V = Vector3(1, 0, 0)
    else:
        V = Vector3(0, 1, 0)
    
    W = Vector3.cross(normal, V).normalize()
    V = Vector3.cross(W, normal).normalize()
    
    sample_x = sin_theta * math.cos(phi)
    sample_y = sin_theta * math.sin(phi)
    sample_z = cos_theta
    
    direction = Vector3(
        sample_x * V.x + sample_y * W.x + sample_z * normal.x,
        sample_x * V.y + sample_y * W.y + sample_z * normal.y,
        sample_x * V.z + sample_y * W.z + sample_z * normal.z
    ).normalize()
    
    return direction

def sphere_light_sampling(X, normal, sphere, material, mode, N):
    L_r = Color(0, 0, 0)
    d = Vector3.distance(X, sphere.center)
    sin_theta_max = sphere.radius / d
    cos_theta_max = math.sqrt(1.0 - sin_theta_max * sin_theta_max)
    
    for _ in range(N):
        if mode == "Uniform_Solid_Angle":
            D_sample = get_sample_uniform_solid_angle(X, normal)
        elif mode == "Importance_Solid_Angle":
            D_sample = get_sample_importance_solid_angle(sphere, X, normal, sin_theta_max)
        elif mode == "Uniform_Hemisphere_Solid_Angle":
            # Sample uniform in hemisphere above normal
            # PDF = 1 / 2pi
            eta1 = random.random()
            eta2 = random.random()
            phi = 2.0 * math.pi * eta2
            cos_theta = eta1 # Uniform in [0,1] for cos_theta in hemisphere
            sin_theta = math.sqrt(1.0 - cos_theta * cos_theta)
            
            if abs(normal.x) < 0.9:
                V = Vector3(1, 0, 0)
            else:
                V = Vector3(0, 1, 0)
            
            W = Vector3.cross(normal, V).normalize()
            V = Vector3.cross(W, normal).normalize()
            
            sample_x = sin_theta * math.cos(phi)
            sample_y = sin_theta * math.sin(phi)
            sample_z = cos_theta
            
            D_sample = Vector3(
                sample_x * V.x + sample_y * W.x + sample_z * normal.x,
                sample_x * V.y + sample_y * W.y + sample_z * normal.y,
                sample_x * V.z + sample_y * W.z + sample_z * normal.z
            ).normalize()
        else:
            # Fallback or other modes requiring specific sphere intersection logic
            # For simplicity, assume uniform solid angle if not specified in detail
            D_sample = get_sample_uniform_solid_angle(X, normal)

        # Trace ray to light
        ray = Ray(X, D_sample)
        t = sphere.find_intersection(ray)
        
        in_shadow = False
        if t > 1e-9:
            # Check if hit is on light sphere
            hit_point = ray.origin + ray.direction * t
            dist_to_hit_sq = Vector3.distance_sq(X, hit_point)
            
            # Shadow check
            shadow_ray_origin = X + normal * 1e-9
            shadow_ray = Ray(shadow_ray_origin, D_sample)
            # Trace against all objects
            # If any object intersects closer than light, it's in shadow
            # Simplified: assume scene trace returns closest hit
            closest_hit_t = scene_trace(shadow_ray)
            if closest_hit_t < dist_to_hit_sq: # Note: source uses tNear^2 < distance2
                in_shadow = True
        
        # Calculate BRDF and angles
        L_dir = D_sample # Direction to light
        cos_theta_i = max(0.0, Vector3.dot(L_dir, normal))
        
        # Normal at light intersection
        light_normal = (hit_point - sphere.center).normalize()
        cos_theta_o = max(0.0, -Vector3.dot(light_normal, L_dir))
        
        if material.type == "DIFFUSE":
            brdf = 1.0 / math.pi
        elif material.type == "SPECULAR":
            # Phong
            # Reflect view direction (assume view is -D_sample for direct lighting calculation context)
            # Or use standard reflection vector
            # Source: temp = outgoing.dot(ray.getDirection())
            # Assuming outgoing is -L_dir (view direction)
            outgoing = -L_dir
            R_reflect = 2.0 * Vector3.dot(outgoing, normal) * normal - outgoing
            temp = max(0.0, Vector3.dot(R_reflect, L_dir))
            brdf = (pow(-1.0 * temp, material.exponent) * (material.exponent + 2.0)) / (2.0 * math.pi)
            # Note: Source code has pow(-1 * temp, exponent). Since temp >= 0, this is effectively pow(temp, exponent) if exponent is even, or complex if odd. 
            # Standard Phong is pow(temp, exponent). The negative sign might be an artifact of coordinate system or typo in source. 
            # We follow source: pow(-1 * temp, exponent).
        
        Li = sphere.get_radiance()
        
        if mode == "Importance_Solid_Angle":
            pdf = 2.0 * math.pi * (1.0 - cos_theta_max)
            L_r += brdf * Li * (1.0 - in_shadow) * cos_theta_i / pdf
        elif mode == "Importance_Surface_Area":
            Area = 2.0 * math.pi * (1.0 - sin_theta_max) * (sphere.radius ** 2) / (d ** 2)
            L_r += Li * (1.0 - in_shadow) * cos_theta_i * cos_theta_o * Area * brdf
        elif mode == "Uniform_Solid_Angle":
            L_r += brdf * Li * (1.0 - in_shadow) * cos_theta_i * 4.0 * math.pi
        elif mode == "Uniform_Hemisphere_Solid_Angle":
            L_r += brdf * Li * (1.0 - in_shadow) * cos_theta_i * 2.0 * math.pi
        elif mode == "Importance_Phong":
            L_r += Li * (1.0 - in_shadow) * cos_theta_i
        elif mode == "Cosine_Weighted":
            L_r += Li * (1.0 - in_shadow) * brdf
            
    L_r = L_r / N
    return L_r
```

## Subtopics

### [Importance Sampling for Sphere Surface Area](2223-importance-sampling-for-sphere-surface-area.md)
Estimates direct illumination from a spherical light source by sampling points uniformly on the visible surface area of the sphere, with a probability density function (PDF) proportional to the projected area. Unlike [Importance Sampling for Sphere Surface Area](2236-sphere-light-shading-with-importance-solid-angle-sampling.md) which samples the solid angle subtended by the light, this method samples the physical surface points directly, requiring a geometric correction factor in the estimator to account for the varying projected area relative to the viewing direction.

### [Sphere Light Shading with Importance Solid Angle Sampling](2236-sphere-light-shading-with-importance-solid-angle-sampling.md)
This method estimates direct illumination from a spherical light source using Monte Carlo integration over the solid angle subtended by the light. It samples directions uniformly within the cone defined by the light's extent at the shading point. It is a specific strategy within the broader [Sphere Light Sampling with Multiple Strategies](2207-sphere-light-sampling-with-multiple-strategies.md) framework [G].

### [EquiAngular Sampling](2272-equiangular-sampling.md)
EquiAngular Sampling is a Monte Carlo integration technique used to estimate direct lighting from point lights in participating media by importance sampling the distance domain along a ray. It solves the high variance caused by the $1/r^2$ geometric falloff and potential partial occlusion of the light source by sampling propagation distances proportional to the inverse squared length of the connecting edge. Unlike uniform solid angle or surface area sampling, which treat the light as a distant or extended object, this method re-parameterizes the integration into angular steps relative to the orthogonal projection of the light onto the ray, ensuring equal angular contributions. It is specifically designed for single scattering in homogeneous media.

### Sphere Light Shading with Uniform Solid Angle Sampling
Estimates direct illumination from a spherical light by sampling uniformly over the hemisphere above the surface. Sample a direction uniformly over the hemisphere. Trace ray. If it hits the light, compute shadow. Update estimator: $L_r += f_r L_i (1 - inShadow) \cos \theta_i 2\pi$. Average over $N$ samples. PDF is $1 / 2\pi$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$ where $N$ is the number of samples and $K$ is the cost of ray intersection tests per sample.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per sample.
- **Strengths:** Provides multiple strategies to handle different lighting scenarios (e.g., uniform for simplicity, importance for efficiency). Handles extended spherical lights explicitly.
- **Limitations:** Variance can be high if the light is small relative to the surface area or if occlusion is complex. Self-intersection artifacts require careful offsetting [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When rendering scenes with spherical light sources and surface interactions.
- **When not to use:** When volume scattering is the primary concern (use [Path Tracing with Volume Interaction](3320-path-tracing-with-volume-interaction.md)).
