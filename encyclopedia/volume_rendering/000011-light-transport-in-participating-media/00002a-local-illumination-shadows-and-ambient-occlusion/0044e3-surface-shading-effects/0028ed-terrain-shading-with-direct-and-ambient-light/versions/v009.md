# Terrain Shading with Direct and Ambient Light

Also called: Hemispheric Ambient Occlusion Shading

## Overview
This method computes the final color of a terrain surface point by combining surface albedo, direct sunlight (modulated by shadow and cloud shadow), and hemispheric ambient light (sky and ground bounce modulated by ambient occlusion) [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Surface Shading Effects](11369-surface-shading-effects.md) and distinguishes itself from siblings like the Powder Sugar Effect by explicitly separating direct illumination from a two-component hemispheric ambient model, and from Terrain Shader Lighting and Effects by relying on a specific baked normal map and bias-based shadow tracing rather than generic lighting parameters [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Surface position $\mathbf{p} = (x, y, z)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Surface normal $\mathbf{n} = (n_x, n_y, n_z)$, normalized [S1, unofficial].
*   Albedo color $\mathbf{C}_{albedo}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Sun direction $\mathbf{l}_{sun}$ and sun radiance $\mathbf{C}_{sun}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Sky ambient color $\mathbf{C}_{sky}$ and ground ambient color $\mathbf{C}_{ground}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Terrain grid cell dimension `field.cell` [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Final color $\mathbf{C}_{out}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   The normal $\mathbf{n}$ is baked into a texture and interpolated across the cell, rather than computed via finite differences, to avoid facet artifacts on coarse heightfields [S1, unofficial].

**Steps and Formulas:**
1.  Compute the dot product between the normal and sun direction:
    $$ N \cdot L = n_x l_x + n_y l_y + n_z l_z $$
    [S1, unofficial]
2.  Calculate a bias to offset the shadow trace origin, preventing self-shadowing artifacts on coarse grids:
    $$ \text{bias} = \text{field.cell} \times 0.5 + 0.5 $$
    [S1, unofficial]
3.  If $N \cdot L > 0$, trace a shadow ray from the biased position:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{start} = (p_x + n_x \cdot \text{bias},\; p_y + \text{bias},\; p_z + n_z \cdot \text{bias}) $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    Compute the cloud shadow factor $S_{cloud} = \text{cloudShadowAt}(p_x, p_z, p_y)$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  Compute the direct light scale:
    $$ \text{lightScale} = \max(0, N \cdot L) \times S_{shadow} \times S_{cloud} $$
    [S1, unofficial]
5.  Compute the direct color contribution:
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{direct} = \mathbf{C}_{sun} \times \text{lightScale} $$
    [S1, unofficial]
6.  Compute the ambient occlusion factor:
    $$ AO = \text{traceAmbientOcclusion}(p_x, p_y, p_z, n_x, n_y, n_z) $$
    [S1, unofficial]
7.  Compute the hemispheric sky factor based on the normal's Y component:
    $$ \text{sky} = 0.5 + 0.5 \times n_y $$
    [S1, unofficial]
8.  Interpolate between sky and ground ambient colors and apply occlusion:
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{ambient} = \left( \mathbf{C}_{sky} \times \text{sky} + \mathbf{C}_{ground} \times (1 - \text{sky}) \right) \times AO $$
    [S1, unofficial]
9.  Combine direct and ambient contributions, modulated by albedo:
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{out} = \mathbf{C}_{albedo} \times (\mathbf{C}_{direct} + \mathbf{C}_{ambient}) $$
    [S1, unofficial]

**Parameters:**
*   `field.cell`: Terrain grid cell dimension used for bias calculation [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{C}_{sky}$, $\mathbf{C}_{ground}$: Ambient color vectors [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{l}_{sun}$, $\mathbf{C}_{sun}$: Light source direction and radiance [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Self-shadowing:** The shadow origin must be biased clear of the surface (by half a cell plus 0.5) to prevent self-shadowing artifacts on coarse heightfields [S1, unofficial].
*   **Normal quality:** Using finite differences for normals on a bilinear heightfield results in piecewise constant normals, causing visible facet edges; baked normal maps interpolated across cells are required [S1, unofficial].
*   **Masking inputs:** Material masks require smooth inputs; per-cell slope thresholds create single-cell patches, and normalized fractal noise clusters tightly, leading to thin contour stripes. Slope fields must be blurred and moisture fields expanded around their midpoint before driving masks [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def terrain_shading(p, n, albedo, sun_dir, sun_rad, sky_amb, ground_amb, field_cell):
    # 1. Direct light
    ndotl = n.x * sun_dir.x + n.y * sun_dir.y + n.z * sun_dir.z
    bias = field_cell * 0.5 + 0.5

    if ndotl > 0:
        # Trace shadow ray from (p.x + n.x * bias, p.y + bias, p.z + n.z * bias) with jitter; source does not detail the marching routine [S1, unofficial]
        shadow = 1.0  # Source does not specify shadow calculation [S1, unofficial]
    else:
        shadow = 0.0

    # Cloud shadow lookup at (p.x, p.z, p.y); source does not specify implementation [S1, unofficial]
    cloud_shade = 1.0  # Source does not specify cloud shadow calculation [S1, unofficial]
    light_scale = max(0.0, ndotl) * shadow * cloud_shade
    direct = sun_rad * light_scale

    # 2. Ambient occlusion
    # Trace ambient occlusion at (p.x, p.y, p.z, n.x, n.y, n.z); source does not specify implementation [S1, unofficial]
    ao = 1.0  # Source does not specify AO calculation [S1, unofficial]

    # 3. Hemispheric ambient
    sky_factor = 0.5 + 0.5 * n.y
    ambient_base = sky_amb * sky_factor + ground_amb * (1.0 - sky_factor)
    ambient = ambient_base * ao

    # 4. Final color composition
    out = albedo * (direct + ambient)
    return out
```

## Subtopics

### [Planetary Surface Shading](9401-planetary-surface-shading.md)
Computes the radiance emitted or reflected by a planetary body's surface (ground or water) as seen by an observer, accounting for direct illumination, ambient scattering, and subsurface effects, before applying atmospheric attenuation. It distinguishes itself from pure atmospheric scattering by handling the distinct bidirectional reflectance distribution functions (BRDF) of diffuse terrain and specular water, while integrating precomputed spectral scattering data.

### Height Shading
A global luma darkening effect relative to the map ceiling, distinct from the local illumination of the main method [S2, unofficial]. It darkens terrain based on depth from the map ceiling using an exponential contrast curve, applied in-place to color buffers [S2, unofficial]. Normalized depth is calculated as $(\text{settings.MAP\_DEPTH} - z_{\text{centers}}) / \text{settings.MAP\_DEPTH}$, clipped to $[0.0, 1.0]$, raised to $\text{settings.HEIGHT\_SHADING\_EXPONENT}$, scaled by $\text{height\_shading\_factor} \times 100.0$, and subtracted from color buffers [S2, unofficial]. Parameters include `settings.MAP_DEPTH` (total map height), `settings.HEIGHT_SHADING_EXPONENT` (contrast curve exponent), and `height_shading_factor` (darkening multiplier) [S2, unofficial].

### Screen-Space Bent Cones
A screen-space method to compute directional occlusion, differing from the hemispheric approach by estimating a visibility cone axis and apex angle [S3, unofficial]. It relies on Z-Buffer data to compute screen-space bent cones, which are circular apertures like those precomputed by Oat and Sander [S3, unofficial]. When sampling the neighborhood of a pixel, it sums all unoccluded direction vectors; the length of the resulting vector estimates the apex angle of the visibility cone, and the direction determines the cone's axis [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides realistic terrain shading with distinct sky/ground ambient contributions and handles cloud shadows [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on baked normals for quality; finite differences cause artifacts [S1, unofficial]. Does not handle interreflections or small area lights well [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** For static or semi-static terrain rendering where baked data is feasible [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not:** For dynamic scenes requiring real-time normal updates without baking, or when high-fidelity global illumination is required [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the `theme1` project by mingderwang, which implements a procedural terrain renderer with specific lighting themes [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the need for realistic terrain appearance in procedural worlds, distinguishing itself by using baked normals and a specific hemispheric ambient model [S1, unofficial]. The concept of hemispheric ambient occlusion is also related to broader techniques in screen-space lighting, such as bent cones, which aim to improve directional occlusion quality [S3, unofficial].
