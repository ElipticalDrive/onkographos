# Shadow Ray Casting
Also called: Shadow Rays, Shadow Testing

## Overview
Shadow Ray Casting is a family of techniques that determine local visibility by casting rays from a point of interest toward light sources or into the environment to test for occlusion by geometry or participating media. It solves the problem of computing accurate shadows and ambient occlusion in volume rendering and surface shading, distinguishing itself from global illumination methods by evaluating direct visibility explicitly rather than through stochastic path exploration or precomputed light maps.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of constructing a ray from a query point $\mathbf{p}$ in a specific direction $\mathbf{d}$ and testing for intersections or transmittance.

**Ray Construction:**
A shadow ray is defined by an origin $\mathbf{o}$ and a direction $\mathbf{d}$ (unit vector). To prevent self-intersection artifacts (shadow acne), the origin is typically offset from the surface point $\mathbf{p}$ along the surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ by a small epsilon $\epsilon$:
$$ \mathbf{o} = \mathbf{p} + \epsilon \mathbf{n} $$
where $\epsilon$ is a small value, typically $0.01 \times \|\mathbf{n}\|$ or a fixed small constant [S3, unofficial][G].

**Visibility and Transmittance:**
The result of a shadow ray cast is a visibility factor $V \in [0, 1]$ or a transmittance $T \in [0, 1]$.
1.  **Geometric Occlusion:** If the ray intersects geometry at distance $t < d_{max}$ (where $d_{max}$ is the light distance or occlusion radius), the point is occluded ($V=0$). Otherwise, it is visible ($V=1$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** In participating media, the ray integrates the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$ along its path. The transmittance is:
    $$ T = \exp\left(-\int_{\mathbf{o}}^{\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}} \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x}\right) $$
    This integral is approximated via discrete sampling (e.g., 16 steps) or majorant-based sampling [S1, S6, unofficial].

**Integration:**
The visibility/transmittance result modulates the incoming radiance from the light source or environment. For direct illumination, this is combined with the geometric term $G$ and the BRDF. For ambient occlusion, it is integrated over the hemisphere.

## Subtopics

### [Ambient Occlusion via Shadow Rays](878-ambient-occlusion-via-shadow-rays.md)
Estimates local visibility by casting shadow rays into the hemisphere above the surface normal to determine how much of the environment is obscured by neighboring geometry. It differs from [Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays](2011-direct-illumination-with-shadow-rays.md) by integrating over the hemisphere to approximate indirect lighting rather than evaluating specific light sources, and from [Variance Shadow Mapping for NeRFs](3561-variance-shadow-mapping-for-nerfs.md) by using explicit ray casting rather than probabilistic depth distributions. It provides a view-independent occlusion factor that can be combined with arbitrary direct illumination, offering a computationally efficient alternative to full global illumination.

### [Shadow Receiving for Volumes](956-shadow-receiving-for-volumes.md)
Shadow Receiving for Volumes is a technique that allows volumetric data to receive shadows cast by polygonal geometry, enhancing depth perception and realism without requiring the volume to be rendered twice. Unlike standard shadow pipelines that render shadow-casting objects separately, this method integrates shadow occlusion checks directly into the volume ray traversal process, significantly reducing computational overhead. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Variance Shadow Mapping for NeRFs](3561-variance-shadow-mapping-for-nerfs.md) by relying on standard shadow maps applied to the volume's ray-marched samples rather than probabilistic variance techniques or neural field approximations.

### [Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays](2011-direct-illumination-with-shadow-rays.md)
Computes the direct lighting contribution at a surface point (or volume sample) by casting shadow rays toward light sources, accounting for attenuation by heterogeneous participating media between the point and the light. It differs from pure surface shadowing by integrating volumetric transmittance along the ray path, and from full path tracing by only evaluating direct illumination from explicit light sources rather than indirect bounces.

### [Variance Shadow Mapping for NeRFs](3561-variance-shadow-mapping-for-nerfs.md)
A shadow rendering method for NeRFs that uses SGs to render cast shadows, inspired by variance shadow-mapping. It avoids cumulative transmittance of light along sampling points on shadow rays by comparing depth inconsistencies from light and camera views.

### [Lookup Table Shadow Rendering](3588-lookup-table-shadow-rendering.md)
VolPack supports a fast one-pass shadow algorithm implemented with lookup tables.

### Volumetric Soft Shadowing
- **Definition:** A technique to estimate soft shadows in volume by sampling along random directions towards the light, weighting samples by density and distance [S1, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
    1. Initialize shadow accumulator: `vec4 accumulatedShadowContribution = vec4(0.0);` [S1, unofficial].
    2. Loop `uNumShadowSamples` times. Generate random direction: `randomDirection = normalize(vec3(-1.0 + vLight.x * rand(...), vLight.y + rand(...) * vLight.z, -1.0 + 2.0 * rand(seed).x)) * rand(...)` [S1, unofficial].
    3. Sample volume at `position + randomDirection * uLightRadious`. Calculate `volumeShadowSample` as `sample * 0.2`. Add to accumulator: `sample * volumeShadowSample * pow(length(randomDirection), 1.0)` [S1, unofficial].
    4. After loop, power accumulator to 1.0, divide by `uNumShadowSamples`, multiply by 20.0, clamp to [0,1] [S1, unofficial].
    5. Compute final shadow contribution: `uSoftShadowsContribution = mix(1.0 - uSoftShadowsContribution, accumulatedShadowContribution, 1.2)` [S1, unofficial].
    6. Normalize shadow contribution: `uSoftShadowsContribution /= 1.3;` and clamp [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameters:**
    - `uNumShadowSamples`: Number of shadow samples per pixel. Default 10 [S1, unofficial].
    - `uShadowsWeight`: Weight applied to the shadow contribution when mixing into the final color. Default 0.54 [S1, unofficial].

### Thin-Walled Glass with Airy Film
- **Definition:** A rendering mode for thin-walled glass (window panes, bulbs, soap bubbles) that skips refraction offset and interior medium, using an optional Airy film as the wall itself [S2, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
    1. Skip refraction offset and interior medium calculation [S2, unofficial].
    2. Pass shadow rays through with colored transmittance [S2, unofficial].
    3. Use the straight-through BSDF event to keep the previous vertex's MIS state, preventing double counting with other strategies [S2, unofficial].
- **Pitfalls:**
    - Must ensure MIS consistency between the straight-through BSDF and other sampling strategies to avoid double counting [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Shadow ray casting originated as a fundamental technique in ray tracing to solve the visibility problem for direct illumination. In the context of volume rendering, it was adapted to handle participating media, where shadows are not binary but attenuated by density [S3, unofficial]. The technique is widely used in real-time rendering (e.g., VTK's GPU volume ray casting) and offline renderers for efficient local illumination [S1, S2, unofficial].
