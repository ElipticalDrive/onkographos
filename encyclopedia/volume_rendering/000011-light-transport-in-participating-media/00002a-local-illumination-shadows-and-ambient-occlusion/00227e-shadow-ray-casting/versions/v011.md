# Shadow Ray Casting
## Overview
Shadow Ray Casting evaluates visibility or illumination at a specific point $\mathbf{p}$ within or on the boundary of a volume by casting rays toward light sources or environment directions to determine occlusion and transmittance [G]. It solves the problem of accurate shadowing and ambient occlusion in participating media by integrating the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ along the path from the source to $\mathbf{p}$, distinguishing itself from screen-space approximations by relying on direct geometric sampling of the volume density field [G]. Unlike [Two-Pass Shadow Casting (Kajiya and Von Herzen)](2196-two-pass-shadow-casting-kajiya-and-von-herzen.md) which uses a separate shadow pass, or [Lookup Table Shadow Rendering](3588-lookup-table-shadow-rendering.md) which relies on precomputed indices, this family directly samples the volume along the ray path during rendering [G].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of evaluating visibility or illumination at a specific point $\mathbf{p}$ within or on the boundary of a volume, based on a light source direction $\mathbf{l}$ or environment [G].

1.  **Visibility/Transmittance Evaluation:** The fundamental operation is determining how much light reaches $\mathbf{p}$ from a source [G]. This involves integrating the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$ along a path from the source to $\mathbf{p}$ (or vice versa) [G]. The transmittance $T$ is calculated as:
    $$ T = \exp\left(-\int_{\mathbf{p}_{source}}^{\mathbf{p}} \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x}\right) $$
    In discrete implementations, this integral is approximated by summing $\sigma_t \cdot \Delta s$ over samples along the ray [G].

2.  **Local Shading Models:** For surface-like appearance in volumes, normals are approximated from the volume gradient $\mathbf{g} = \nabla V$ [G]. Usually, a simple absorption and emission model is used because it is relatively cheap to evaluate and gives good enough results [G].

3.  **Shadowing Approximation:** Shadows are computed by testing occlusion along the direction to the light source [G].

4.  **Ambient Occlusion:** Indirect illumination is approximated by integrating over the hemisphere or using simplified models (e.g., upward ray for sky light) [G]. The ambient contribution $L_{ambient}$ is often modulated by a shadowing factor based on local density:
    $$ L_{ambient} = L_{sky} \cdot \exp(-D_{up}) \cdot AO_{factor} $$
    where $D_{up}$ is the integrated density along an upward ray [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_transmittance(origin, direction, volume_sampler, num_samples):
    """Computes transmittance T = exp(-sum(sigma_t * delta_s)) along a ray."""
    delta_s = 1.0 / num_samples
    optical_depth = 0.0
    for i in range(num_samples):
        pos = origin + direction * (i * delta_s)
        sigma_t = volume_sampler.extinction_at(pos)
        optical_depth += sigma_t * delta_s
    return math.exp(-optical_depth)
```

## Subtopics

### [Ambient Occlusion via Shadow Rays](878-ambient-occlusion-via-shadow-rays.md)
Approximates the lack of secondary (reflected) light rays by evaluating the geometric obscurance of a point $\mathbf{p}$ within or on the boundary of a volume. It solves the problem of flat, unshaded volume appearances by darkening crevices and areas shielded from the environment, providing a view-independent shading term that can be combined with arbitrary direct illumination. Choose it when view-independent ambient shading is needed without the cost of full path tracing.

### [Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays](2011-direct-illumination-with-shadow-rays.md)
Computes the direct lighting contribution at a surface point $\mathbf{p}$ by evaluating visibility and volumetric attenuation along the direction to each light source. It differs from [Ambient Occlusion via Shadow Rays](878-ambient-occlusion-via-shadow-rays.md) by calculating full directional light contributions (including BRDF terms) rather than just occlusion factors, and from [Lookup Table Shadow Rendering](3588-lookup-table-shadow-rendering.md) by performing real-time ray marching for transmittance rather than precomputing or querying static tables. It builds on [Shadow Ray Casting](2796-shadow-ray-casting.md) by explicitly integrating extinction coefficients along the shadow ray to handle participating media. Choose it for accurate direct lighting in participating media.

### [Lookup Table Shadow Rendering](3588-lookup-table-shadow-rendering.md)
Lookup Table Shadow Rendering is a fast, one-pass shadowing technique implemented within the VolPack volume rendering library. It solves the problem of computing shadows for participating media by precomputing visibility information into lookup tables, avoiding the per-ray integration costs of standard shadow casting. Unlike [Shadow Ray Casting](2796-shadow-ray-casting.md), which evaluates transmittance along rays during rendering, this method accelerates shadow determination by mapping density or depth values to pre-calculated shadow factors. Choose it when performance is critical and shadow maps or precomputed tables are acceptable.

### [Recursive Ray Tracing](5600-recursive-ray-tracing.md)
Recursive Ray Tracing extends basic ray casting by handling diffuse reflection, specular reflection, and transmission (refraction) through the recursive tracing of rays upon intersection with objects. It solves the problem of local illumination and shadowing in participating media by evaluating visibility to light sources and simulating surface interactions that influence the final pixel color. It is distinguished from its sibling [Shadow Ray Casting](2796-shadow-ray-casting.md) by explicitly modeling reflection and refraction via physical laws (Snell's law, Fresnel equation) rather than relying solely on shadow tests and ambient approximations. Choose it when global illumination effects like reflections and refractions are required.

### [Soft Shadows via Area Lights](7320-soft-shadows-via-area-lights.md)
This method computes soft shadows by modeling light sources with spatial extent, allowing points in space to be partially occluded rather than fully lit or fully shadowed. Unlike point source approximations that cast sharp shadows, this approach generates smooth penumbra transitions by sampling the light source area. It differs from its sibling [Soft Shadows via Ray Tracing](5136-soft-shadows-via-ray-tracing.md) by focusing on the area sampling mechanism itself, and from [Point Light Shadow Shader](13086-point-light-shadow-shader.md) by avoiding post-process Gaussian blurring in favor of direct area integration. Choose it when realistic penumbra effects from area lights are needed.

### [Bounded Directional Shadow Ray Generation](12387-bounded-directional-shadow-ray-generation.md)
This method generates a bounded set of directional shadow rays for hard, soft-area, contact, and hybrid shadow modes, converting authored metric distances into the tracer's voxel-index units. It differs from [Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays](2011-direct-illumination-with-shadow-rays.md) by decoupling ray generation from the primary shading pass into a dedicated dispatch loop that handles multiple samples per primary hit, and from [Soft Shadows via Ray Tracing](5136-soft-shadows-via-ray-tracing.md) by using a fixed maximum sample count and explicit bounding distances rather than adaptive depth or recursive tracing. Choose it for GPU-accelerated, bounded shadow ray dispatch in voxel-based renderers.

### [Shadow Resolve](12388-shadow-resolve.md)
Shadow Resolve aggregates discrete shadow ray casting results into a continuous visibility value and penumbra width for spatial denoising. It differs from sibling methods by operating as a post-processing or finalization step that converts binary hit/miss data from multiple samples into soft shadow metrics, rather than performing the ray traversal itself. Choose it to denoise and soften discrete shadow samples into continuous visibility maps.

### [Shadow Volume Atmospheric Scattering](13668-shadow-volume-atmospheric-scattering.md)
A real-time rendering method for atmospheric light scattering and volumetric shadows that computes the direct contribution of a participating medium on any surface using an analytical expression of the light transport equation, avoiding precomputation and texture memory. It differs from volume rendering methods (e.g., shadow maps) by not requiring texture memory and avoiding sampling artifacts for sharp edges like light shafts, and from other direct representation methods by integrating volumetric shadows. Choose it for real-time atmospheric scattering with sharp volumetric shadows without texture lookups.

### Thin-Walled Glass with Airy Film
- **Definition:** A rendering mode for thin-walled glass (window panes, bulbs, soap bubbles) that skips refraction offset and interior medium, using an optional Airy film as the wall itself [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** Skip refraction offset and interior medium calculation [S1, unofficial]. Pass shadow rays through with colored transmittance [S1, unofficial]. Use the straight-through BSDF event to keep the previous vertex's MIS state, preventing double counting with other strategies [S1, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** Must ensure MIS consistency between the straight-through BSDF and other sampling strategies to avoid double counting [S1, unofficial].

### Shadow Ray Marching
- **Definition:** Calculates sunlight contribution by casting a shadow ray from the surface towards the sun. If the shadow ray hits an object, the point is in shadow [S2, unofficial].
- **Step:** If `SUN_ENABLED`, calculate `sunDir` from `sceneData` [S2, unofficial]. Calculate `nDotL = max(dot(normal, sunDir), 0.0)` [S2, unofficial]. If `nDotL > 0`, create `shadowOrigin = position + normal * 0.001` [S2, unofficial]. March shadow ray using `March(shadowOrigin, sunDir, 64)` [S2, unofficial]. If `shadowRay.status != 2` (no hit), `sunContribution = SUN_COLOR * nDotL * sceneData.sunIntensity` [S2, unofficial].

### Mean Intensity Projection
- **Definition:** A variation of ray casting that projects the mean intensity along the ray [S3, unofficial].
- **Formula:** The output $C(R)$ is determined by the mean scalar value $B$ along the ray: $C(R) = B$ [S3, unofficial].

### Halfway Vector Slicing for Shadows
- **Definition:** A shadow computation method that modifies the slice axis to be the direction halfway between the view and light directions, allowing the same slice to be rendered from both eye and light points of view [S4].
- **Step:** 1. Calculate the dot product of light and view directions [S4]. 2. If dot product > 0: Slice along vector halfway between light and view directions [S4]. Render front-to-back relative to observer [S4]. 3. If dot product < 0: Slice along vector halfway between light and inverted view directions [S4]. Render back-to-front relative to observer [S4]. 4. In both cases, render front-to-back relative to the light [S4].
- **Pitfall:** Slice spacing maintenance: Care must be taken to maintain slice spacing along view and light directions when light or eye positions change [S4].
- **Comparison:** Differs from previous hardware shadow work [2] by using an off-screen render buffer instead of a volumetric shadow map, and by modifying the slice axis to allow shared slicing [S4].

### Heidelberg Ray-Tracing Model
- **Step:** Propagate shadow rays simultaneously slice-by-slice and in the same general direction as rendering [S5, unofficial].
