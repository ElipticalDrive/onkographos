# Shadow Ray Casting


## Overview
Shadow Ray Casting evaluates visibility at a specific point $\mathbf{p}$ by tracing rays toward light sources or into the hemisphere to determine occlusion by geometry or participating media. It differs from [Two-Pass Shadow Casting (Kajiya and Von Herzen)](2196-two-pass-shadow-casting-kajiya-and-von-herzen.md) by computing visibility dynamically per fragment rather than via precomputed buffers, and from [Variance Shadow Mapping for NeRFs](3561-variance-shadow-mapping-for-nerfs.md) by using explicit ray integration rather than probabilistic depth distributions.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of evaluating visibility or illumination at a specific point $\mathbf{p}$ within or on the boundary of a volume, based on a light source direction $\mathbf{l}$ or environment.

1.  **Visibility/Transmittance Evaluation:** The fundamental operation is determining how much light reaches $\mathbf{p}$ from a source. This involves integrating the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$ along a path from the source to $\mathbf{p}$ (or vice versa). The transmittance $T$ is calculated as:
    $$ T = \exp\left(-\int_{\mathbf{p}_{source}}^{\mathbf{p}} \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x}\right) $$
    In discrete implementations, this integral is approximated by summing $\sigma_t \cdot \Delta s$ over samples along the ray [S1, unofficial][S6, unofficial].

2.  **Local Shading Models:** For surface-like appearance in volumes, normals are approximated from the volume gradient $\mathbf{g} = \nabla V$. The shading contribution $C_{shade}$ is computed using standard local illumination models (e.g., Lambertian, Phong) [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ C_{shade} = k_d (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}) C_{diffuse} + k_s (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{h})^\alpha C_{specular} $$
    where $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{g}/\|\mathbf{g}\|$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Shadowing Approximation:** Shadows are computed by testing occlusion along the direction to the light source. This can be done via ray casting (checking for density/geometry along the path) or via precomputed buffers (shadow maps, illumination buffers) [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

4.  **Ambient Occlusion:** Indirect illumination is approximated by integrating over the hemisphere or using simplified models (e.g., upward ray for sky light). The ambient contribution $L_{ambient}$ is often modulated by a shadowing factor based on local density:
    $$ L_{ambient} = L_{sky} \cdot \exp(-D_{up}) \cdot AO_{factor} $$
    where $D_{up}$ is the integrated density along an upward ray [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Ambient Occlusion via Shadow Rays](878-ambient-occlusion-via-shadow-rays.md)
Estimates local visibility by casting shadow rays into the hemisphere above the surface normal. It differs from [Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays](2011-direct-illumination-with-shadow-rays.md) by integrating over the hemisphere to approximate indirect lighting rather than evaluating specific light sources, and from [Variance Shadow Mapping for NeRFs](3561-variance-shadow-mapping-for-nerfs.md) by using explicit ray casting rather than probabilistic depth distributions. It provides a view-independent occlusion factor that can be combined with arbitrary direct illumination, offering a computationally efficient alternative to full global illumination.

### [Shadow Receiving for Volumes](956-shadow-receiving-for-volumes.md)
Allows volumetric data to receive shadows cast by polygonal geometry by integrating shadow occlusion checks directly into the volume ray traversal process. Unlike standard shadow pipelines that render shadow-casting objects separately, this method significantly reduces computational overhead.

### [Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays](2011-direct-illumination-with-shadow-rays.md)
Computes direct lighting contribution by casting shadow rays toward light sources, accounting for attenuation by heterogeneous participating media. It differs from pure surface shadowing by integrating volumetric transmittance along the ray path, and from full path tracing by only evaluating direct illumination from explicit light sources rather than indirect bounces.

### [Variance Shadow Mapping for NeRFs](3561-variance-shadow-mapping-for-nerfs.md)
Adapts variance shadow mapping to implicit neural representations to compute soft shadows without expensive volumetric transmittance integration. It replaces the ray-casting transmittance integral with a depth-buffer comparison, transforming discontinuous depth information into continuous visibility probabilities.

### [Lookup Table Shadow Rendering](3588-lookup-table-shadow-rendering.md)
A fast, one-pass technique in VolPack that precomputes shadow contributions into lookup tables. It is distinguished from its siblings by its reliance on precomputed tables for immediate shading, whereas methods like [Shadow Ray Casting](2796-shadow-ray-casting.md) compute visibility dynamically per fragment.

### [Soft Shadows via Ray Tracing](5136-soft-shadows-via-ray-tracing.md)
Computes soft shadows by generating coherent packets of shadow rays from a primary ray hit point to samples on an area light source. It distinguishes itself from hard shadow techniques by sampling a spatial distribution of directions to approximate the penumbra, leveraging coherence to maintain interactive performance.

### [Recursive Ray Tracing](5600-recursive-ray-tracing.md)
Extends basic ray casting by handling diffuse reflection, specular reflection, and transmission through the recursive tracing of secondary rays. What sets it apart from its siblings is the explicit recursive generation of new rays based on material properties (specular/transmission) and physical laws (Fresnel, Snell's law), rather than just sampling for shadows or ambient occlusion.

### [Positional & Normal-Based Voxel Shadowing](6206-positional-normal-based-voxel-shadowing.md)
A deterministic, non-ray-tracing method for calculating per-face shadow multipliers in voxel-based rendering. It solves the problem of simulating directional shading in blocky environments by evaluating static geometric conditions—specifically block coordinates and face normals—rather than performing expensive visibility tests. Unlike [Shadow Ray Casting](2796-shadow-ray-casting.md), which computes transmittance via ray integration, this method applies a binary or scalar shadow intensity based on predefined spatial rules, offering high performance at the cost of physical accuracy. It is distinct from [Ambient Occlusion via Shadow Rays](878-ambient-occlusion-via-shadow-rays.md) by replacing stochastic or deterministic ray sampling with hardcoded coordinate thresholds.

### Volumetric Soft Shadowing
- **Definition:** A technique to estimate soft shadows in volume by sampling along random directions towards the light, weighting samples by density and distance [S1, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
    1. Initialize shadow accumulator: `vec4 accumulatedShadowContribution = vec4(0.0);` [S1, unofficial].
    2. Loop `uNumShadowSamples` times. Generate random direction: `randomDirection = normalize(vec3(-1.0 + vLight.x * rand(...), vLight.y + rand(...) * vLight.z, -1.0 + 2.0 * rand(seed).x)) * rand(...)` [S1, unofficial].
    3. Sample volume at `position + randomDirection * uLightRadious`. Calculate `volumeShadowSample` as `sample * 0.2`. Add to accumulator: `sample * volumeShadowSample * pow(length(randomDirection), 1.0)` [S1, unofficial].
    4. After loop, power accumulator to 1.0, divide by `uNumShadowSamples`, multiply by 20.0, clamp to [0,1] [S1, unofficial].
    5. Compute final shadow contribution: `uSoftShadowsContribution = mix(1.0 - uSoftShadowsContribution, accumulatedShadowContribution, 1.2);` [S1, unofficial].
    6. Normalize shadow contribution: `uSoftShadowsContribution /= 1.3;` and clamp [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameters:** `uNumShadowSamples` (Number of shadow samples per pixel, default 10) and `uShadowsWeight` (Weight applied to the shadow contribution when mixing into the final color, default 0.54) [S1, unofficial].

### Thin-Walled Glass with Airy Film
- **Definition:** A rendering mode for thin-walled glass (window panes, bulbs, soap bubbles) that skips refraction offset and interior medium, using an optional Airy film as the wall itself [S2, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
    1. Skip refraction offset and interior medium calculation [S2, unofficial].
    2. Pass shadow rays through with colored transmittance [S2, unofficial].
    3. Use the straight-through BSDF event to keep the previous vertex's MIS state, preventing double counting with other strategies [S2, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** Must ensure MIS consistency between the straight-through BSDF and other sampling strategies to avoid double counting [S2, unofficial].

### Soft Shadows via Area Lights
- **Steps:** Implement area light sources and sample the light source area instead of a single boolean shadow test to compute more natural appearing soft shadows [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of shadow rays originates from early ray tracing literature, where visibility was determined by casting rays from intersection points toward light sources [S3, unofficial]. The technique has been adapted for volume rendering to handle participating media, where transmittance integration is required in addition to geometric occlusion [S1, unofficial]. Modern implementations often combine shadow ray casting with other techniques like variance shadow mapping for neural representations or lookup tables for performance [S5, S1].
