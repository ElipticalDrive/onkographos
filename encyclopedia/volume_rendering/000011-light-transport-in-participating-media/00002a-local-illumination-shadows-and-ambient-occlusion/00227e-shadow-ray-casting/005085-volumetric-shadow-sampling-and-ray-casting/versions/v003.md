# Volumetric Shadow Sampling and Ray Casting

Also called: Volumetric Ray Casting with Shadows, Volumetric Shadow Ray Casting

## Overview
This family of methods computes local illumination and shadows for participating media by evaluating visibility along rays cast toward light sources or environment directions [G]. It addresses the high computational cost of volumetric shadowing by providing approximations, though interactive hardware-based approaches remain less common in practice [S1, book].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of evaluating visibility or illumination at a specific point $\mathbf{p}$ within or on the boundary of a volume, based on a light source direction $\mathbf{l}$ or environment [G].

1.  **Visibility/Transmittance Evaluation:** The fundamental operation is determining how much light reaches $\mathbf{p}$ from a source [G]. This involves integrating the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$ along a path from the source to $\mathbf{p}$ (or vice versa) [G]. The transmittance $T$ is calculated as:
    $$ T = \exp\left(-\int_{\mathbf{p}_{source}}^{\mathbf{p}} \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x}\right) $$
    In discrete implementations, this integral is approximated by summing $\sigma_t \cdot \Delta s$ over samples along the ray [G].

2.  **Local Shading Models:** For surface-like appearance in volumes, normals are approximated from the volume gradient $\mathbf{g} = \nabla V$ [G]. Usually, a simple absorption and emission model is used because it is relatively cheap to evaluate and gives good enough results [G].

3.  **Shadowing Approximation:** Shadows are computed by testing occlusion along the direction to the light source [G].

4.  **Ambient Occlusion:** Indirect illumination is approximated by integrating over the hemisphere or using simplified models (e.g., upward ray for sky light) [G]. The ambient contribution $L_{ambient}$ is often modulated by a shadowing factor based on local density:
    $$ L_{ambient} = L_{sky} \cdot \exp(-D_{up}) \cdot AO_{factor} $$
    where $D_{up}$ is the integrated density along an upward ray [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_transmittance(origin, direction, volume_sampler, num_samples):
    """Computes transmittance T = exp(-sum(sigma_t * delta_s)) along a ray."""
    delta_s = 1.0 / num_samples
    optical_depth = 0.0
    for i in range(num_samples):
        pos = origin + direction * (i * delta_s)
        sigma_t = volume_sampler.extinction_at(pos)
        optical_depth += sigma_t * delta_s
    return math.exp(-optical_depth)
```

## Subtopics

### [Volumetric Soft Shadowing](4507-volumetric-soft-shadowing.md)
Volumetric Soft Shadowing estimates soft shadows in participating media by sampling random directions towards the light source, weighting samples by local density and distance. It approximates the penumbra through stochastic sampling rather than geometric hard edges. Choose this method when soft, noisy shadows are acceptable and the volume density field is accessible via a sampling function.

### [Half-Angle Slice Rendering](9979-half-angle-slice-rendering.md)
Half-Angle Slice Rendering is a technique for computing volumetric shadows and local illumination in participating media, specifically designed for particle systems and voxel-based atmospheric data. It solves the problem of efficiently accumulating shadowing information while rendering volume slices by projecting the volume onto a single 2D shadow buffer. Unlike methods that require complex 3D texture lookups or separate light-space rendering passes, this method calculates a "half-angle" vector between the view and light directions, allowing the same set of slices to be composited into both an eye buffer (for final image accumulation) and a light buffer (for shadow attenuation). It is distinct from [Volumetric Soft Shadowing](4507-volumetric-soft-shadowing.md) by relying on discrete slicing and 2D buffer accumulation rather than continuous ray marching or kernel-based soft shadow approximations. Choose this method for particle-based volumes where performance is critical and hard shadows with correct front-to-back accumulation are required.

### [Soft BiF Volumetric Shadows](18810-soft-bif-volumetric-shadows.md)
Soft BiF Volumetric Shadows (SoftBiF-VS) is a real-time technique for generating high-quality volumetric shadows by combining sparse ray marching with bilateral interpolation. It solves the problem of excessive computational cost in brute-force volumetric shadowing while maintaining the crispness of shadow boundaries. Unlike conventional ray marching that evaluates all sample points along a view ray regardless of occlusion, SoftBiF-VS excludes points blocked by obstacles using cube shadow maps and applies bilateral filtering to smooth the transition of shadows along view rays. Choose this method when real-time performance is required and crisp shadow boundaries must be preserved.

### [Light Space Raymarching with Shadow Mapping](21579-light-space-raymarching-with-shadow-mapping.md)
Light Space Raymarching with Shadow Mapping is a local illumination technique that evaluates light intensity contribution along a ray by performing raymarching in light space, incorporating shadow visibility via shadow maps and anisotropic scattering. It differs from standard camera-space raymarching by transforming the ray trajectory into the light's coordinate system, allowing direct depth comparison against a pre-rendered shadow map to determine occlusion. This approach is distinct from sibling methods like Volumetric Soft Shadowing, which typically rely on stochastic sampling or kernel-based approximations for soft shadows, whereas this method uses deterministic depth testing in light space. Choose this method when deterministic shadow visibility and anisotropic scattering are needed without stochastic noise.

### Shadow Ray Marching
Calculates sunlight contribution by casting a shadow ray from the surface towards the sun. If the shadow ray hits an object, the point is in shadow [S2, unofficial]. If `SUN_ENABLED`, calculate `sunDir` from `sceneData`. Compute `nDotL = max(dot(normal, sunDir), 0.0)`. If `nDotL > 0`, create `shadowOrigin = position + normal * 0.001` and march the shadow ray using `March(shadowOrigin, sunDir, 64)` [S2, unofficial]. If `shadowRay.status != 2` (no hit), `sunContribution = SUN_COLOR * nDotL * sceneData.sunIntensity` [S2, unofficial].

### Half-Angle Shadow Opacity Sampling
Computes light-space texture coordinates for opacity lookup in the vertex shader and samples opacity from a texture in the fragment shader to determine shadow intensity [S3, unofficial]. In the vertex shader, compute `lightTextureCoordinates = (uLightViewProjection * vec4(position, 1.0)) * 0.5 + 0.5` [S3, unofficial]. Sample opacity `vOpacity = texture(uOpacityTexture, lightTextureCoordinates).a` [S3, unofficial]. In the floor fragment shader, compute light-space coordinates for the floor position and sample opacity; if coordinates are outside [0,1], set opacity to 0 [S3, unofficial]. Final floor shadow color: `FragColor = vec4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, opacity * 0.5)` [S3, unofficial].

### Volumetric Cloud Shadow Quality Adjustment
A configuration workflow for Unreal Engine's volumetric cloud system to improve shadow definition on directional lights [S4]. To increase quality, increase the Volumetric Cloud property `Trace Sample Count Scale` [S4]. Increase the Directional Light property `Cloud Shadow Ray Sample Count Scale` [S4]. Compensate for the low default resolution of the cloud's shadow quality by adjusting console variables `r.SkyAtmosphere.FastSkyLUT.Width` and `r.SkyAtmosphere.FastSkyLUT.Height`, with a recommended starting point of 256 for both [S4].

### Low-pass Filtered Volumetric Shadows
A method to compute volumetric soft shadows for interactive direct volume visualization by convolving the classified data set with a low-pass filter, avoiding hard shadow edges and noise-like visual patterns [S5, unofficial]. Align a three-dimensional summed area table (3D SAT) with the light source [S5, unofficial]. Evaluate soft shadows for each point in constant time using the 3D SAT, avoiding expensive shadow ray marching [S5, unofficial]. Softness of the shadows can be controlled interactively [S5, unofficial]. Hard shadows and vortex features can visually interfere with each other due to similar scale, hampering perception of depth; this method reduces distracting streak patterns [S5, unofficial].

### Volumetric Shadow Bake Shader for Clouds
A compute shader that populates local ambient occlusion and computes voxel-specific shadow transmittance for a 3D cloud volume [S6, unofficial]. Populate the local ambient occlusion (G channel) based on neighbor evaluated densities from the A channel [S6, unofficial]. March towards the sun direction (`u_primaryLightDir`) to compute voxel-specific shadow transmittance [S6, unofficial]. Store the computed shadow transmittance in the B channel [S6, unofficial]. Process the computation in a performance-optimal, staggered 2x2 Bayer-aligned fashion [S6, unofficial]. The 3D texture channels are: A = baseDensity (input for AO), G = local ambient occlusion, B = shadow transmittance [S6, unofficial].

### Pre-filtered Deep Shadow Maps
A technique for generating high-quality shadows for mesh and volumetric data using pre-filtered deep shadow maps to exhibit faster lookups [S7, unofficial]. Hadwiger, Kratz, Sigg and Bühler (2006) adopted this algorithm for volumetric ray casting on GPUs, generating anti-aliased images using a pre-computed lookup table [S7, unofficial].

### NeDF Shadow Mapping
A shadow generation method using shadow mapping concepts combined with NeDF, avoiding volumetric evaluation for each sample point to reduce time consumption [S8]. Traditional NeRF shadows require volumetric evaluation for each sample along a ray, significantly increasing time. This method uses NeDF depth to quickly determine shadow status [S8].

### Geometrical-Based Volumetric Shadows
A method that sorts geometric-based shadow quads in back-to-front order relative to the viewer [S9, unofficial].

### Volumetric Shadow Depth Peeling
A method that uses depth peeling instead of shadow polygons to render volumetric shadows [S9, unofficial]. Guarantees geometric-based shadows do not overlap by creating them from image-based shadows [S9, unofficial].

## Background
The inclusion of volumetric shadows, where light received at a sample point $s$ is attenuated by volume densities on its path from the light source to $s$, produces greater realism in volume visualization [S1, book]. The term for this attenuation is $C_l(s) = C_L \exp\left(-\int_s^T \mu(t) dt\right)$, where $C_L$ is the light source color and $T$ is the distance from $s$ to the light source [S1, book]. In practice, applications that compute volumetric shadows are less common due to added computational complexity, but interactive hardware-based approaches have been proposed [S1, book].
