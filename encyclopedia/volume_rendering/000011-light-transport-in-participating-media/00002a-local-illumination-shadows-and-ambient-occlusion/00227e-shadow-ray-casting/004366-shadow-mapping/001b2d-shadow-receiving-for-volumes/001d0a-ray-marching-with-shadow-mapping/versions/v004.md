# Ray Marching with Shadow Mapping

Also called: Deep Opacity Maps, OSM, Opacity Shadow Map Volume Rendering

## Overview
Ray Marching with Shadow Mapping is a technique for computing self-shadowing in participating media by precomputing transmittance values from the light's perspective into a 3D texture, avoiding the need to cast individual shadow rays for every sample during the main view-ray traversal [S1, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficient direct illumination occlusion in volumes by trading memory for computational speed, allowing the renderer to sample a pre-integrated opacity field instead of performing expensive light-space ray marches [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike [Shadow Volume Rendering](1556-shadow-volume-rendering.md), which constructs explicit volumetric shadow buffers, or [Volume Shadow Calculation via Light Strength Buffer](1914-volume-shadow-calculation-via-light-strength-buffer.md), which stores fractional light strength in 3D space, this method relies on a 2D-to-3D projection of transmittance layers [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is distinct from [Deep Shadow Maps](2166-deep-shadow-maps.md) in its specific implementation of uniform slicing and MRT usage, though they share the goal of capturing volumetric occlusion [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of two passes: a shadow map generation pass and a main volume rendering pass.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Volume density function $density(\mathbf{p})$.
*   Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t = \sigma_a + \sigma_s$ (absorption + scattering) [S1, unofficial].
*   Scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Light direction $\mathbf{l}$ (for directional lights) or light position (for point lights) [S1, unofficial].
*   Shadow volume range $[z_{min}, z_{max}]$ defining the depth interval in light space [S1, unofficial].
*   Number of slices/layers $N$ (must be $\le 8$ for standard MRT, or higher with multiple passes) [S1, unofficial].
*   Step size $\Delta z = (z_{max} - z_{min}) / N$ [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A 3D texture `opacityShadowMap` storing transmittance values [S1, unofficial].
*   Final pixel color with self-shadowing applied [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
**Opacity Shadow Map:**
*   Format: 3D texture (e.g., `sampler3D`) [S1, unofficial].
*   Dimensions: $W \times H \times N$, where $W, H$ are the shadow map resolution and $N$ is the number of depth slices [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   Channel: Single channel (e.g., Red) is required to allow correct trilinear interpolation [S1, unofficial]. Storing different layers in different color channels (R, G, B, A) prevents correct interpolation [S1, unofficial].
*   Content: The value at $(u, v, z)$ represents the transmittance from the light source to the depth $z$ at screen coordinates $(u, v)$ [S1, unofficial].

### Steps

1.  **Render Opacity Shadow Map (Light Pass):**
    *   Set up a camera from the light source's perspective. Use an orthographic camera for directional lights and a perspective camera for point lights [S1, unofficial].
    *   Ray march through the volume along the light direction (z-axis in light space) from $z_{min}$ to $z_{max}$ with uniform steps $\Delta z$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   For each slice $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
        *   Calculate the position $\mathbf{p}_i = \mathbf{p}_{start} + i \cdot \Delta z \cdot \mathbf{l}$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Sample density $d_i = density(\mathbf{p}_i)$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Accumulate optical depth and compute transmittance using Beer's Law [S1, unofficial]:
            $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=0}^{i} d_j \cdot \sigma_t \cdot \Delta z\right) $$
            *Note: The source code example accumulates density first, then applies the exponential to the total optical depth for that layer* [S1, unofficial].
        *   Store $T_i$ in the $i$-th layer of the 3D texture [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use Multiple Render Targets (MRT) to write up to 8 layers per pass. If $N > 8$, perform multiple passes [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Main Rendering Pass (View Pass):**
    *   For each pixel, ray march from the camera through the volume with step size $\Delta x$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Initialize `transmittance` = 1.0 and `illumination` = $\vec{0}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   For each sample position $\mathbf{p}$ along the view ray:
        *   Transform $\mathbf{p}$ to light space: $\mathbf{p}_L = M_{light} \cdot \mathbf{p}$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Normalize coordinates to sample the 3D texture [S1, unofficial]:
            $$ z = \frac{-\mathbf{p}_L.z - z_{min}}{z_{max} - z_{min}} $$
            $$ u = \frac{\mathbf{p}_L.x}{W_{light}} + 0.5 $$
            $$ v = \frac{\mathbf{p}_L.y}{H_{light}} + 0.5 $$
            *Note: The source uses `lightCamSize` and divides by 2.0, implying a normalized device coordinate range of [-1, 1] before offset* [S1, unofficial].
        *   Sample transmittance from the shadow map:
            $$ \tau = \text{texture}(\text{opacityShadowMap}, (u, v, z)).r $$
        *   Compute in-scattering [S1, unofficial]:
            $$ I_{in} = L_{ambient} + L_{direct} \cdot p(\mathbf{v}, -\mathbf{l}) \cdot \tau $$
            Where $p(\mathbf{v}, -\mathbf{l})$ is the phase function (e.g., Schlick's approximation) [S1, unofficial].
        *   Compute out-scattering [S1, unofficial]:
            $$ \sigma_s' = \sigma_s \cdot density(\mathbf{p}) $$
        *   Accumulate illumination [S1, unofficial]:
            $$ \text{illumination} += \text{transmittance} \cdot I_{in} \cdot \sigma_s' \cdot \Delta x $$
        *   Update view-ray transmittance [S1, unofficial]:
            $$ \text{transmittance} *= \exp(-density(\mathbf{p}) \cdot \sigma_t \cdot \Delta x) $$
    *   Return `vec4(illumination, transmittance)` [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
# Light Pass: Generate Opacity Shadow Map
def generate_opacity_shadow_map(light_cam_matrix, shadow_volume_range, slice_count, density_func, extinction_coef):
    # shadow_volume_range = [z_min, z_max]
    z_min, z_max = shadow_volume_range
    step_size = (z_max - z_min) / slice_count
    
    # Initialize layers array for MRT
    layers = [0.0] * slice_count
    total_optical_depth = 0.0
    
    # Ray march from light perspective
    # Assuming light direction is -Z in light space, marching from z_min to z_max
    for i in range(slice_count):
        # Position in light space
        z_pos = z_min + i * step_size
        
        # Transform to world space to sample density
        # Inverse light matrix to get world position from light space coordinates
        # Note: The source code uses localPosition - float(i) * l * stepSize
        # This implies marching backwards or forwards depending on coordinate system.
        # We assume standard ray march along light direction.
        world_pos = inverse(light_cam_matrix) * vec4(0.0, 0.0, z_pos, 1.0)
        
        d = density_func(world_pos.xyz)
        total_optical_depth += d * step_size * extinction_coef
        
        # Beer's Law
        layers[i] = exp(-total_optical_depth)
        
    return layers

# Main Pass: Ray Marching with Shadow Map Lookup
def raymarch_with_shadow_map(sample_pos, march_dir, step_count, step_size, light_cam_matrix, shadow_map, shadow_volume_range, light_cam_size, ambient_light, direct_light, scattering_coef, extinction_coef, phase_func):
    transmittance = 1.0
    illumination = vec3(0.0)
    
    for i in range(step_count):
        sample_pos += march_dir * step_size
        
        current_density = density_func(sample_pos)
        
        # Update view ray transmittance
        transmittance *= exp(-current_density * extinction_coef * step_size)
        
        # Sample Shadow Map
        # Transform to light space
        light_space_pos = light_cam_matrix * vec4(sample_pos, 1.0)
        
        # Normalize Z to [0, 1] range for texture lookup
        # Source: z = (-shadowMapPosition.z - shadowVolumeRange.x) / (shadowVolumeRange.y - shadowVolumeRange.x)
        z = (-light_space_pos.z - shadow_volume_range[0]) / (shadow_volume_range[1] - shadow_volume_range[0])
        
        # Normalize XY to [0, 1] range
        # Source: uv = shadowMapPosition.xy / lightCamSize / 2.0 + vec2(0.5)
        uv = light_space_pos.xy / light_cam_size / 2.0 + vec2(0.5)
        
        # Sample transmittance
        shadow_factor = texture(shadow_map, vec3(uv, z)).r
        
        # Compute In-Scattering
        # Phase function p(v, -l)
        phase = phase_func(march_dir, -normalize(light_space_pos.xyz)) # Approximation of view and light vectors
        
        in_scattering = ambient_light + direct_light * phase * shadow_factor
        
        # Compute Out-Scattering
        out_scattering = scattering_coef * current_density
        
        # Accumulate
        current_light = in_scattering * out_scattering
        illumination += transmittance * current_light * step_size
        
    return vec4(illumination, transmittance)
```

### Parameters
*   $z_{min}, z_{max}$: Define the depth range of the shadow volume in light space. If $z_{min}$ is too large, the shadow map slice starts inside the volume, causing the side facing the light to be incorrectly dark [S1, unofficial].
*   $k$: Anisotropy parameter for Schlick's phase function approximation [S1, unofficial].
*   $\alpha, \beta$: Azimuth and elevation angles controlling the directional light direction [S1, unofficial].
*   Step jitter: A small random offset added to the step size in the main pass to hide visible banding artifacts caused by low layer counts, at the cost of introducing noise [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Interpolation Artifacts:** Storing different layers in different color channels (e.g., Layer 1 in Red, Layer 2 in Green) prevents correct bilinear/trilinear interpolation during sampling. All layers must be stored in a single channel or rendered in separate passes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Banding:** Low number of layers ($N$) can cause visible banding. Solution: Add random jitter to the step size [S1, unofficial].
*   **Shadow Volume Bounds:** Incorrect $z_{min}$ leads to incorrect shadowing (too dark or too bright) [S1, unofficial].
*   **MRT Limitations:** Hardware limits the number of simultaneous render targets (minimum 8). For higher resolution in depth, multiple passes are required [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Shadow Volume Rendering](1556-shadow-volume-rendering.md)
Computes accumulated alpha to generate shadow maps, explicitly constructing a volumetric shadow buffer. Choose when you need explicit volumetric shadow buffers rather than projected opacity maps.

### [Volume Shadow Calculation via Light Strength Buffer](1914-volume-shadow-calculation-via-light-strength-buffer.md)
Stores shadow info in a 3D light strength buffer, computing fractional light strength at every point. Choose for correct penumbra rendering and semi-transparent shadows without aliasing artifacts of 2D buffers.

### [Deep Shadow Maps](2166-deep-shadow-maps.md)
Stores a curve of volume density vs. distance per pixel. Choose when you need to capture the entire volumetric structure along the light ray for accurate occlusion, rather than just discrete layers.

### [Light Injection Pass](2759-light-injection-pass.md)
Post-processing technique for volumetric light scattering using ray marching over a depth buffer. Choose for adding light shafts/glow as a post-process step using pre-existing shadow maps.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The light pass is $O(W \cdot H \cdot N)$ where $N$ is the number of slices. The main pass is $O(R \cdot S)$ where $R$ is resolution and $S$ is steps per ray. The shadow map lookup is $O(1)$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(W \cdot H \cdot N)$ for the 3D opacity shadow map [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** More efficient than sampling light rays for each view ray sample [S2, unofficial]. In a test case (640x480, 3 deep opacity maps, 7x512x512 transmittance array), it achieved 28.5 FPS on an AMD Ryzen 7 4700U [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient self-shadowing; avoids per-view-ray light sampling; allows trilinear interpolation for smooth shadows [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Limited by MRT count (usually 8); requires uniform grid for 3D texture interpolation (unlike Deep Opacity Maps which can start from surface); potential for banding and noise [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time volume rendering where self-shadowing is required and performance is critical.
*   **When not to use:** When penumbra accuracy is paramount (use Light Strength Buffer) or when non-uniform sampling is needed (use Deep Opacity Maps) [S1, S2, S1914, unofficial].

## Background
The technique was proposed by Benoit Mayaux in "Real-Time Volumetric Rendering" (2013) as an alternative to casting shadow rays for every sample [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the concept of Opacity Shadow Maps, which record transparency along different depths from the light's perspective [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on standard shadow mapping but extends it to participating media by ray marching through the volume from the light's view [S1, unofficial]. It is also referenced in Jan Wedekind's "Procedural Volumetric Clouds" (2023) as a method for efficient self-shadowing in clouds [S2, unofficial].
