# Terrain Shading with Direct and Ambient Light

Also called: Hemispheric Ambient Occlusion Shading

## Overview
This method computes the final color of a terrain surface point by combining diffuse albedo, direct sunlight (modulated by shadow and cloud shadow), and hemispheric ambient light (sky and ground bounce modulated by ambient occlusion) [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Local illumination, shadows and ambient occlusion](34-local-illumination-shadows-and-ambient-occlusion.md) by evaluating visibility and illumination at a surface point $\mathbf{p}$ using a heightfield representation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `albedo`: RGB color of the terrain surface.
- `sunRadiance`: RGB radiance of the sun.
- `skyAmbient`: RGB color of the sky ambient light.
- `groundAmbient`: RGB color of the ground ambient light.
- `normal` ($\mathbf{n}$): Unit surface normal at point $\mathbf{p}$.
- `position` ($\mathbf{p}$): 3D position of the point.
- `sunDirection` ($\mathbf{l}$): Unit vector pointing to the sun.
- `field.cell`: Size of the terrain grid cell.

**Outputs:**
- `out`: RGB final color.

**Data Structures:**
- The terrain is represented as a heightfield [S1, unofficial]. Normals are baked or interpolated smoothly to avoid facet artifacts common with finite differences on coarse grids [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Direct Illumination Calculation:**
   a. Compute the dot product between the surface normal and the sun direction:
      $$ NdotL = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l} = n_x l_x + n_y l_y + n_z l_z $$
   b. Calculate the bias offset:
      $$ \text{bias} = \text{field.cell} \times 0.5 + 0.5 $$
   c. Determine the shadow factor. If $NdotL > 0$, trace a shadow ray starting from $\mathbf{p}_{start} = (p_x + n_x \cdot \text{bias}, p_y + \text{bias}, p_z + n_z \cdot \text{bias})$ with jitter [S1, unofficial]. Let the result be `shadow` (1.0 if unshadowed, 0.0 if shadowed) [S1, unofficial]. If $NdotL \le 0$, `shadow` is 0.0.
   d. Get the cloud shadow factor: `cloudShade = cloudShadowAt(p.x, p.z, p.y)` [S1, unofficial].
   e. Calculate the direct light scale:
      $$ \text{lightScale} = \max(0, NdotL) \times \text{shadow} \times \text{cloudShade} $$
2. **Ambient Occlusion Calculation:**
   a. Compute the ambient occlusion factor:
      $$ \text{occlusion} = \text{traceAmbientOcclusion}(p_x, p_y, p_z, n_x, n_y, n_z) $$
3. **Hemispheric Ambient Light Calculation:**
   a. Calculate the sky factor based on the vertical component of the normal:
      $$ \text{sky} = 0.5 + 0.5 \times n_y $$
   b. Interpolate between sky and ground ambient colors:
      $$ \text{ambientColor} = \text{skyAmbient} \times \text{sky} + \text{groundAmbient} \times (1 - \text{sky}) $$
   c. Apply occlusion:
      $$ \text{ambient} = \text{ambientColor} \times \text{occlusion} $$
4. **Final Color Composition:**
   a. Calculate the direct contribution:
      $$ \text{direct} = \text{sunRadiance} \times \text{lightScale} $$
   b. Combine direct and ambient contributions, modulated by albedo:
      $$ \text{out} = \text{albedo} \times (\text{direct} + \text{ambient}) $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def terrain_shading(albedo, sun_radiance, sky_ambient, ground_ambient, normal, position, sun_direction, field_cell):
    # 1. Direct Illumination
    n_dot_l = normal[0]*sun_direction[0] + normal[1]*sun_direction[1] + normal[2]*sun_direction[2]
    
    # Bias to prevent self-shadowing [S1, unofficial]
    bias = field_cell * 0.5 + 0.5
    
    # Shadow calculation [S1, unofficial]
    if n_dot_l > 0:
        shadow_start = (position[0] + normal[0] * bias, position[1] + bias, position[2] + normal[2] * bias)
        # Trace shadow ray with jitter [S1, unofficial]
        # [G] Environment-specific ray march returning transmittance along sun_direction
        shadow = 1.0 
    else:
        shadow = 0.0
        
    # Cloud shadow [S1, unofficial]
    # [G] Environment-specific lookup or approximation at (px, pz, py)
    cloud_shade = 1.0
    
    # Light scale [S1, unofficial]
    light_scale = max(0.0, n_dot_l) * shadow * cloud_shade
    
    # 2. Ambient Occlusion [S1, unofficial]
    # [G] Environment-specific ray march or precomputed AO value
    occlusion = 1.0 
    
    # 3. Hemispheric Ambient [S1, unofficial]
    sky_factor = 0.5 + 0.5 * normal[1]
    ambient_color = (sky_ambient[0]*sky_factor + ground_ambient[0]*(1.0-sky_factor),
                     sky_ambient[1]*sky_factor + ground_ambient[1]*(1.0-sky_factor),
                     sky_ambient[2]*sky_factor + ground_ambient[2]*(1.0-sky_factor))
    ambient = (ambient_color[0]*occlusion, ambient_color[1]*occlusion, ambient_color[2]*occlusion)
    
    # 4. Final Color [S1, unofficial]
    direct = (sun_radiance[0]*light_scale, sun_radiance[1]*light_scale, sun_radiance[2]*light_scale)
    out = (albedo[0]*(direct[0]+ambient[0]), albedo[1]*(direct[1]+ambient[1]), albedo[2]*(direct[2]+ambient[2]))
    
    return out
```

**Parameters:**
- `field.cell`: Grid cell size. Typical value: Not given in the sources.
- `bias`: Fixed offset of `field.cell * 0.5 + 0.5` [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Self-Shadowing:** Failing to bias the shadow ray origin results in severe self-shadowing artifacts on coarse heightfields. The bias must be at least half a cell plus 0.5 units [S1, unofficial].
- **Normal Interpolation:** Using finite differences on a bilinear heightfield produces piecewise constant normals, causing visible facet edges. Normals should be baked or interpolated smoothly [S1, unofficial].
- **Material Masks:** Slope and moisture fields used for material masking require blurring or expansion to avoid thin, noisy contour stripes [S1, unofficial].
