# Frequency Domain Directional Shading

Also called: Frequency domain shading, Gradient moment shading, Gradient-Based Frequency Shading, Hemispherical Light Source Shading, Nusselt's analog shading

## Overview
Frequency Domain Directional Shading is a technique for implementing directional diffuse reflection in volume rendering by performing shading computations entirely in the frequency domain [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of applying shape cues (shading) to volume data without the computational overhead of spatial domain differentiation or the nonlinearity of standard Lambertian models [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. Unlike [Simplified Phong Lighting Model](885-simplified-phong-lighting-model.md) or [Gradient-based Shading with Phase Functions](1020-gradient-based-shading-with-phase-functions.md) which typically operate in the spatial domain, this method leverages the property that spatial differentiation is equivalent to frequency multiplication by a linear ramp, allowing the shading term to be computed as a moment of the spectrum [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. It builds on [Shading models](837-shading-models.md) by replacing spatial gradient calculations with frequency domain operations [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A premultiplied volume dataset $f(x)$, where $x$ is the spatial coordinate. The volume is assumed to be pre-multiplied by an opacity mask $p_m(x)$ [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   **Output:** The shaded slice intensity $I(s)$ for a specific projection direction, computed in the frequency domain before inverse transformation or during slice extraction [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Data:** 3D grid of scalar values $f(x)$.
*   **Frequency Spectrum:** Complex-valued array $F(s)$ representing the Fourier transform of the volume data.
*   **Interpolation Filter:** A frequency-domain filter $H(s)$ used to reconstruct the pure spectrum from the premultiplied volume [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $C_{amb}$: Ambient color constant.
*   $L_{amb}$: Ambient light color.
*   $C_{dif}$: Directional shading strength constant.
*   $L_{dif}$: Directional light color.
*   $L$: Unit light vector. Typically set perpendicular to the view vector $V$ to maximize shading effect and eliminate adjustment terms in combined depth cueing/shading [S2, unofficial].
*   $s$: Frequency coordinate vector.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Gradient Magnitude Pre-computation:** Since the gradient magnitude $|\nabla f(x)|$ cannot be computed simply in the frequency domain, it must be computed in the spatial domain first.
    *   Compute the gradient vector $\nabla f(x)$ in the spatial domain.
    *   Compute the magnitude $|\nabla f(x)|$.
    *   Premultiply by the opacity mask: $|\nabla f(x)| p_m(x)$.
    *   Compute the Fourier transform: $G(s) = \mathcal{F}\{|\nabla f(x)| p_m(x)\}$ [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
2.  **Volume Spectrum Preparation:**
    *   Premultiply the volume data by the opacity mask: $f(x) p_m(x)$.
    *   Compute the Fourier transform: $F_{pre}(s) = \mathcal{F}\{f(x) p_m(x)\}$ [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
3.  **Filter Application:** Apply the interpolation filter $H(s)$ to reconstruct the pure spectrum of $f$ from the premultiplied volume.
    *   $F(s) = F_{pre}(s) H(s)$ [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
    *   Similarly, apply the filter to the gradient magnitude spectrum: $G_{filtered}(s) = G(s) H(s)$ [S3, unofficial].
4.  **Frequency Domain Shading Computation:** Compute the shaded spectrum $I(s)$ using the hemispherical light source model. The equation is derived from the linear approximation of diffuse reflection:
    $$ I(s) = \mathcal{F}\{f(x) p_m(x)\} H(s) \left[ C_{amb} L_{amb} + i C_{dif} L_{dif} (s \cdot L) + \frac{1}{2} C_{dif} L_{dif} \mathcal{F}\{|\nabla f(x)| p_m(x)\} H(s) \right] $$
    
    Breaking this down into terms:
    *   **Ambient Term:** $k_1 = C_{amb} L_{amb}$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Directional Shading Term:** $k_2 = i C_{dif} L_{dif} (s \cdot L)$. This term represents the first moment of the spectrum, applying directional shading via frequency multiplication [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Gradient Magnitude Term:** $k_3 = \frac{1}{2} C_{dif} L_{dif}$. This term scales the pre-computed gradient magnitude spectrum [S3, unofficial].
    
    The final frequency domain representation for the slice extraction is:
    $$ I(s) = F(s) \cdot k_1 + F(s) \cdot k_2 + G_{filtered}(s) \cdot k_3 $$
    Note: The sources describe the combination as:
    $$ \mathcal{F}\{f(x) p_m(x)\} H(s) [C_{amb} L_{amb}] + \mathcal{F}\{f(x) p_m(x)\} H(s) [i C_{dif} L_{dif} (s \cdot L)] + \frac{1}{2} C_{dif} L_{dif} \mathcal{F}\{|\nabla f(x)| p_m(x)\} H(s) $$
    [S3, unofficial].

5.  **Slice Extraction:** The shaded slice is obtained by applying the inverse Fourier transform or by extracting the relevant slice from the frequency domain representation during the rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def frequency_domain_directional_shading(volume, opacity_mask, light_direction, view_direction, 
                                       c_amb, l_amb, c_dif, l_dif, interpolation_filter_h):
    """
    Computes directional shading for a volume slice in the frequency domain.
    
    Args:
        volume: 3D array of volume data f(x).
        opacity_mask: 3D array of opacity mask p_m(x).
        light_direction: Unit vector L.
        view_direction: Unit vector V (used to determine L if not provided).
        c_amb: Ambient color constant.
        l_amb: Ambient light color.
        c_dif: Directional shading strength constant.
        l_dif: Directional light color.
        interpolation_filter_h: Function or array representing H(s).
        
    Returns:
        shaded_slice: 2D array representing the shaded slice.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute gradient magnitude in spatial domain
    # Gradient r(x) is approximated by computing partial derivatives
    grad_x = spatial_derivative(volume, axis=0)
    grad_y = spatial_derivative(volume, axis=1)
    grad_z = spatial_derivative(volume, axis=2)
    
    # Gradient magnitude |r(x)|
    grad_mag = sqrt(grad_x**2 + grad_y**2 + grad_z**2)
    
    # Premultiply gradient magnitude by opacity mask
    grad_mag_premult = grad_mag * opacity_mask
    
    # Step 2: Premultiply volume by opacity mask
    vol_premult = volume * opacity_mask
    
    # Step 3: Fourier Transform both volumes
    # Note: In practice, FFT is applied to the 3D volumes
    F_pre = fft3(vol_premult)
    G = fft3(grad_mag_premult)
    
    # Step 4: Apply interpolation filter H(s) to reconstruct pure spectrum
    # H(s) is applied in the frequency domain
    F = F_pre * interpolation_filter_h
    G_filtered = G * interpolation_filter_h
    
    # Step 5: Define constants
    k1 = c_amb * l_amb
    k2_coeff = 1j * c_dif * l_dif  # i * C_dif * L_dif
    k3 = 0.5 * c_dif * l_dif
    
    # Step 6: Compute frequency domain shading terms
    # Term 1: Ambient
    term_ambient = F * k1
    
    # Term 2: Directional shading (First moment)
    # s is the frequency coordinate grid corresponding to F
    s = frequency_coordinate_grid(F.shape)
    # Dot product s . L
    s_dot_L = dot_product(s, light_direction)
    term_directional = F * k2_coeff * s_dot_L
    
    # Term 3: Gradient magnitude contribution
    term_gradient = G_filtered * k3
    
    # Combine terms
    I_s = term_ambient + term_directional + term_gradient
    
    # Step 7: Extract slice or Inverse FFT to get spatial slice
    # Assuming slice extraction happens in frequency domain or via inverse transform
    # For a specific slice at depth z, we might extract the relevant frequency components
    # or perform inverse FFT to get the spatial slice.
    # The sources indicate computation is performed during slice extraction.
    shaded_slice = inverse_fft3(I_s) # Or specific slice extraction logic
    
    return shaded_slice

    if axis == 0:
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Gradient Magnitude Nonlinearity:** The gradient magnitude $|\nabla f(x)|$ does not have a simple frequency domain representation. This necessitates computing the gradient in the spatial domain and requiring a second spectrum extraction, meaning two slices must be extracted from two volumes (one for $f$, one for $|\nabla f|$) [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
*   **Sine Wave Depth Cueing:** If depth cueing is implemented as a sine wave, its transform consists of two impulses. The filter $H'$ must be computed by shifting $H$ and adding three copies with complex weights, which increases the filter kernel size [S1, unofficial].
*   **Negative Values:** A purely linear shading equation $C_{amb} L_{amb} f(x) + C_{dif} L_{dif} \nabla f(x)$ can be derived but requires restricting the upper bound of $C_{dif}$ to prevent negative values, which restricts the shading effect [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Hemisphere Illumination](1384-hemisphere-illumination.md)
A local shading model that simulates diffuse reflection from a virtual hemisphere light source. It enhances shape perception by weighting volume points based on orientation relative to a light direction. It builds on Frequency Domain Directional Shading by computing directional shading in the frequency domain using an orientation field.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method operates entirely in the frequency domain. Computing the moment (directional shading) incurs a few additional floating-point operations per sample on the slice, which is small relative to the convolution operations required for slice extraction [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. However, the requirement for a second spectrum (gradient magnitude) doubles the preprocessing cost for the gradient term [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing two spectra: one for the volume $f(x)$ and one for the gradient magnitude $|\nabla f(x)|$ [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. Due to the wide dynamic range and complex arithmetic of Fourier transforms, a pair of floating-point numbers is required for each voxel [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The method is efficient for slice-based rendering as it avoids spatial domain differentiation during the projection step. The additional expense of moment computation is minor compared to convolution [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Avoids the nonlinearity of the MAX function used in Lambertian shading [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
    *   Allows computation entirely in the frequency domain via moment computation [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
    *   Easily extended to multiple light sources by adding moment terms [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires two slices to be extracted from two volumes due to the lack of a simple frequency domain representation for gradient magnitude [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
    *   The shading effect is restricted if a single-volume linear equation is used to avoid the second spectrum, due to the need to prevent negative values [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Use when efficient frequency-domain volume rendering is required and directional shading is needed to enhance shape perception, particularly when avoiding spatial domain differentiation is beneficial.
*   **When Not to Use:** Avoid if memory is constrained (due to the need for two spectra) or if a single-volume processing pipeline is strictly required without the overhead of pre-computing gradients.

## Background
Frequency Domain Directional Shading was introduced by Takashi Totsuka and Marc Levoy in their 1993 paper "Frequency Domain Volume Rendering" [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial]. The method is based on Nusselt's analog, which relates the irradiance on a surface to the projection of a hemispherical light source [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial]. It was developed to address the limitations of spatial domain shading techniques in frequency domain volume rendering, specifically the nonlinearity of the Lambertian MAX function [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial]. The technique has been used in volume rendering applications to provide directional illumination cues, enhancing the perception of shape and spatial relationships in volume projections [S1, unofficial].
