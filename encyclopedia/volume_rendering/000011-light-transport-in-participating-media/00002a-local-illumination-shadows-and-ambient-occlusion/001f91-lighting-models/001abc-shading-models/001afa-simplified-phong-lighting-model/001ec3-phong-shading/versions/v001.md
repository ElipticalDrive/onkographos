# Phong Shading

Also called: Phong Illumination, Phong Lighting Model for Implicit Surfaces, Phong Reflection Model, Phong Shading Model

## Overview
Phong Shading is a local illumination model that computes the color of a surface point by summing ambient, diffuse, and specular components based on the surface normal, light direction, and view direction [S1, S2, S4]. In the context of volume rendering, it is applied to sample points along rays, often using the gradient of the scalar field as the normal vector [S3, S4]. It differs from [Blinn-Phong Shading Model](2239-blinn-phong-shading-model.md) by using the reflected light vector for specular calculation rather than the halfway vector, and from [Phong Shading Model for Volume Data](1353-phong-shading-model-for-volume-data.md) by typically relying on pre-computed reflectance maps or explicit vector math rather than specialized volume-specific modulation tables [S3, S4].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Surface position $x$ (or sample point in volume).
*   Surface normal $n$ (unit vector). For implicit surfaces or volumes, $n$ is often the normalized gradient of the scalar field $f(x)$ [S3, S4].
*   Light direction $l$ (unit vector, pointing from surface to light).
*   View direction $v$ (unit vector, pointing from surface to camera).
*   Material parameters: ambient reflectance $k_a$, diffuse reflectance $k_d$, specular reflectance $k_s$, emissive coefficient $k_e$, shininess exponent $\alpha$ (or $shininess$) [S1, S3, S4].
*   Light parameters: ambient light color $I_a$, diffuse light color $I_d$, specular light color $I_s$ [S1, S4].
*   Optional: Color lookup table (LUT) values for volume data [S3].

**Outputs:**
*   Final color $C(x)$ or $I(x)$ at the surface point [S1, S4].

### Data Structures
*   **Reflectance Maps (VolumePro specific):** Pre-computed tables mapping gradient vectors (for diffuse) and reflection vectors (for specular) to illumination values. Mapped onto six sides of a cube, indexed by the vector components, using bi-linear interpolation [S3].
*   **Registers (VolumePro specific):** $k_e, k_d, k_s, \text{specularColor}$ [S3].
*   **Flags:** $\text{Gmim}_d, \text{Gmim}_s$ (Gradient Magnitude Illumination Modulation flags for diffuse/specular) [S3].

### Steps
1.  **Normalize Vectors:** Ensure $n$, $l$, and $v$ are unit vectors [S1].
2.  **Compute Ambient Term:**
    $$ \text{ambient} = k_a(x) I_a $$
    where $k_a(x)$ is the ambient reflectance and $I_a$ is the global ambient light color [S4].
3.  **Compute Diffuse Term:**
    $$ \text{diffuse} = k_d(x) I_d \max(n \cdot l, 0) $$
    where $k_d(x)$ is the diffuse color/reflectance and $I_d$ is the incoming diffuse light color [S4].
    *   *Volume Variant:* $I_d$ may be looked up from a reflectance map indexed by the unnormalized gradient vector, modulated by a gradient magnitude LUT if $\text{Gmim}_d=1$ [S3].
4.  **Compute Specular Term:**
    *   Calculate the reflected light direction $r$:
        $$ r = 2(n \cdot l)n - l $$
        [S1]
    *   Calculate the specular coefficient:
        $$ \text{specular\_coeff} = \max(r \cdot v, 0)^{\text{shininess}} $$
        [S1]
    *   *Alternative (Blinn-Phong, not used here):* Uses halfway vector $h$. Phong uses $r$ [S4].
    *   *Volume Variant:* The specular illumination value $I_s$ may be looked up from a reflectance map indexed by the unnormalized reflection vector (computed from gradient and eye vector) [S3].
    *   Calculate facing parameter:
        $$ \text{facing} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } n \cdot l > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        [S4]
    *   Final specular contribution:
        $$ \text{specular} = \text{facing} \cdot k_s(x) I_s \text{specular\_coeff} $$
        [S4]
5.  **Compute Emissive Term (Optional/Volume):**
    $$ \text{emissive} = k_e \cdot \text{colorLUT}[sampleValue] $$
    [S3]
6.  **Combine:**
    $$ I(x) = \text{ambient} + \text{diffuse} + \text{specular} + \text{emissive} $$
    [S1, S3, S4]
    *   Note: Some implementations exclude emissive [S4].
    *   Clamp result to $[0, 1]$ [S1].

### Formulas
**Standard Phong (Implicit Surfaces) [S4]:**
$$ I(x) = k_a I_a + k_d I_d \max(n \cdot l, 0) + k_s I_s \max(r \cdot v, 0)^{\alpha} \cdot \text{facing} $$
Where $r = 2(n \cdot l)n - l$ and $\text{facing} = \text{step}(n \cdot l, 0)$.

**VolumePro Phong [S3]:**
$$ \text{sampleColor} = \left( (k_e + (I_d \cdot G_d \cdot k_d)) \cdot \text{colorLUT}[sampleValue] \right) + (I_s \cdot G_s \cdot k_s \cdot \text{specularColor}) $$
Where:
*   $I_d = \text{diffuseReflectanceMap}[\text{gradient Vector}]$
*   $I_s = \text{specularReflectanceMap}[\text{reflection Vector}]$
*   $G_{[d,s]} = \begin{cases} \text{GmLUT}[\text{Gradient}] & \text{if } \text{Gmim}_{[d,s]} = 1 \\ 1 & \text{if } \text{Gmim}_{[d,s]} = 0 \end{cases}$

### Parameters
*   **Shininess ($\alpha$ or $shininess$):** Exponent for specular highlight. Default 64.0 [S1].
*   **Ambient Color ($I_a$):** Default white (1.0, 1.0, 1.0) [S1].
*   **Diffuse Color ($I_d$):** Default white (1.0, 1.0, 1.0) [S1].
*   **Specular Color ($I_s$):** Default white (1.0, 1.0, 1.0) [S1].
*   **Material Ambient ($k_a$):** Default 0.2 plus random noise [S1].
*   **Material Diffuse ($k_d$):** Default 0.7 plus random noise [S1].
*   **Material Specular ($k_s$):** Default 0.1 [S1].
*   **Emissive Coefficient ($k_e$):** Register value [S3].
*   **Light Source:** Single directional light is common in examples [S4].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Facing Culling:** The specular term should only appear on front-facing surfaces relative to the light ($n \cdot l > 0$) to avoid artifacts on back faces [S4].
*   **Reflectance Map Reloads:** In hardware implementations like VolumePro, reflectance maps must be reloaded if the number or positions of light sources change. They do *not* need reloading if the eye vector changes [S3].
*   **Gradient Normalization:** When using gradients as normals, ensure they are normalized before dot product calculations to maintain correct lighting intensity [S1, S4].
*   **Clamping:** Final color values must be clamped to the valid range (e.g., 0.0 to 1.0) to prevent overflow artifacts [S1].

### Pseudocode
```python
def phong_shading(position, normal, light_dir, view_dir, material, light):
    """
    Computes Phong shading for a single point.
    
    Args:
        position: (3,) array, surface position.
        normal: (3,) array, unit normal vector.
        light_dir: (3,) array, unit vector from surface to light.
        view_dir: (3,) array, unit vector from surface to camera.
        material: Object with attributes ka, kd, ks, ke, shininess, ambient_color, diffuse_color, specular_color.
        light: Object with attributes Ia, Id, Is.
        
    Returns:
        color: (3,) array, final RGB color.
    """
    # 1. Normalize inputs (ensure unit vectors)
    n = normal / np.linalg.norm(normal)
    l = light_dir / np.linalg.norm(light_dir)
    v = view_dir / np.linalg.norm(view_dir)
    
    # 2. Ambient Term
    ambient = material.ka * light.Ia
    
    # 3. Diffuse Term
    # max(n . l, 0)
    diff_dot = np.dot(n, l)
    diffuse_factor = max(diff_dot, 0.0)
    diffuse = material.kd * light.Id * diffuse_factor
    
    # 4. Specular Term
    # Calculate reflected vector r = 2(n . l)n - l
    r = 2.0 * n * diff_dot - l
    
    # Calculate specular coefficient: max(r . v, 0)^shininess
    spec_dot = np.dot(r, v)
    spec_factor = max(spec_dot, 0.0) ** material.shininess
    
    # Facing parameter: 1 if n . l > 0, else 0
    facing = 1.0 if diff_dot > 0.0 else 0.0
    
    specular = facing * material.ks * light.Is * spec_factor
    
    # 5. Emissive Term (Optional, included if ke > 0)
    emissive = material.ke * material.emissive_color if hasattr(material, 'ke') else np.zeros(3)
    
    # 6. Combine
    color = ambient + diffuse + specular + emissive
    
    # Clamp to [0, 1]
    color = np.clip(color, 0.0, 1.0)
    
    return color
```

## Subtopics

### [Blinn-Phong Shading Model](2239-blinn-phong-shading-model.md)
Uses the halfway vector instead of the reflected vector for specular calculation. Faster to compute (no reflection vector calculation) and often produces smoother highlights. Choose when performance is critical or smoother highlights are desired.

### [Phong Shading Model for Volume Data](1353-phong-shading-model-for-volume-data.md)
Adapts Phong shading specifically for volume rendering using gradient-based normals and potentially specialized lookup tables for illumination modulation. Choose when rendering scalar fields where gradient magnitude affects appearance.

### [Specular Reflections via Polynomial Expansion](1172-specular-reflections-via-polynomial-expansion.md)
Approximates specular highlights using polynomial expansions. Choose when analytical integration of specular terms is required or for specific optimization paths.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample point. Requires a few dot products, a power operation, and vector arithmetic.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for standard implementation. VolumePro variant requires memory for reflectance maps (cube-mapped tables) [S3].
*   **Performance:** Very fast, suitable for real-time rendering. The power operation is the most expensive part but is hardware-accelerated.
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement, visually pleasing for glossy surfaces, widely supported.
*   **Limitations:** Not physically based (energy conservation not guaranteed), specular highlights can be sharp or unrealistic depending on shininess, does not account for view-dependent diffuse effects (BRDF).
*   **When to use:** General-purpose shading for implicit surfaces or volume data where physical accuracy is not paramount.
*   **When not to use:** Physically based rendering (PBR) pipelines, or when view-dependent diffuse effects are needed.

## Background
The Phong shading model was introduced by Bui Tuong Phong in 1975 [S4, implied by FK03 reference]. It is a local illumination model that approximates the reflection of light on surfaces. It has been widely adopted in computer graphics for its simplicity and visual quality. In volume rendering, it has been implemented in hardware systems like VolumePro, which uses pre-computed reflectance maps to accelerate the calculation of diffuse and specular contributions based on gradient and reflection vectors [S3].
