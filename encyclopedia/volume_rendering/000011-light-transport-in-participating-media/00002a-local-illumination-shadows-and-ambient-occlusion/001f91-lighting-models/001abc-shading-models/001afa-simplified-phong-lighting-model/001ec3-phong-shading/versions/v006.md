# Phong Shading

Also called: Phong Illumination, Phong Illumination Model, Phong Lighting Model, Phong Model, Phong Reflection Model, Phong Shading Model

## Overview

Phong Shading is a local illumination model that approximates the direct lighting of a surface by summing ambient, diffuse, and specular components [S2][S3, S7, unofficial]. It solves the problem of rendering smooth, shiny surfaces by interpolating surface normals across a polygon and evaluating the lighting equation per fragment, rather than per vertex [S3, S11, unofficial]. It differs from its siblings by using the angle between the view direction and the perfect reflection vector (or the half-vector in Blinn-Phong) to compute specular highlights, whereas [Blinn-Phong Shading Model](2239-blinn-phong-shading-model.md) uses the half-vector for computational efficiency, and [Phong Shading Model for Volume Data](1353-phong-shading-model-for-volume-data.md) adapts this to volumetric gradient-based shading [S2][S9, unofficial].

## How it works

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Surface position $\vec{p}$, surface normal $\vec{N}$, view direction $\vec{V}$, light direction $\vec{L}$, material properties ($k_a, k_d, k_s, \alpha$), and light properties ($I_a, I_d, I_s$).
*   **Outputs:** Fragment color $C_{final}$.

**Data Structures:**
The material is defined by a structure containing:
*   `k_a`: Ambient reflection coefficient (scalar or vec3).
*   `k_d`: Diffuse reflection coefficient (scalar or vec3).
*   `k_s`: Specular reflection coefficient (scalar or vec3).
*   `alpha` (or `shininess`): Specular exponent (scalar).
*   `color_a`: Ambient color multiplier.
*   `color_d`: Diffuse color.
*   `color_s`: Specular color.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Vector Normalization:** Ensure $\vec{N}$, $\vec{V}$, and $\vec{L}$ are unit vectors.
    $$ \hat{N} = \frac{\vec{N}}{|\vec{N}|}, \quad \hat{V} = \frac{\vec{V}}{|\vec{V}|}, \quad \hat{L} = \frac{\vec{L}}{|\vec{L}|} $$
    [S2][S3, S5, unofficial]

2.  **Ambient Component:** Calculate the ambient contribution, assuming constant illumination from all directions.
    $$ C_{ambient} = k_a \cdot I_a \cdot \text{color}_a $$
    [S2][S7, S10, unofficial]

3.  **Diffuse Component:** Calculate the Lambertian reflection. If the surface faces away from the light, the contribution is zero.
    $$ \cos \theta_d = \max(\hat{N} \cdot \hat{L}, 0.0) $$
    $$ C_{diffuse} = k_d \cdot I_d \cdot \text{color}_d \cdot \cos \theta_d $$
    [S2][S3, S7, S12, unofficial]

4.  **Specular Component:** Calculate the specular highlight. Two common variants exist:
    *   **Original Phong (Reflection Vector):** Compute the reflection vector $\vec{R}$ of the light direction about the normal.
        $$ \vec{R} = 2(\hat{N} \cdot \hat{L})\hat{N} - \hat{L} $$
        $$ \cos \theta_s = \max(\hat{R} \cdot \hat{V}, 0.0) $$
        $$ C_{specular} = k_s \cdot I_s \cdot \text{color}_s \cdot (\cos \theta_s)^{\alpha} $$
        [S2][S3, S7, unofficial]
    *   **Blinn-Phong (Half-Vector):** Compute the halfway vector $\vec{H}$ between $\hat{L}$ and $\hat{V}$.
        $$ \vec{H} = \frac{\hat{L} + \hat{V}}{|\hat{L} + \hat{V}|} $$
        $$ \cos \theta_h = \max(\hat{N} \cdot \vec{H}, 0.0) $$
        $$ C_{specular} = k_s \cdot I_s \cdot \text{color}_s \cdot (\cos \theta_h)^{\alpha} $$
        [S2][S8, S12, unofficial]
    *Note: The original Phong model uses the reflection vector [S2][S3, unofficial], while Blinn-Phong uses the half-vector [S8, S12, unofficial]. This article describes the original Phong method as the primary definition, with Blinn-Phong as a variant.*

5.  **Combine:** Sum the components.
    $$ C_{final} = C_{ambient} + C_{diffuse} + C_{specular} $$
    [S2][S7, S12, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def phong_shading(position, normal, view_direction, light_direction, 
                  material, light):
    """
    Computes the Phong shading color for a single light source.
    
    Args:
        position: vec3, world-space position of the fragment.
        normal: vec3, world-space surface normal (assumed normalized).
        view_direction: vec3, world-space direction from fragment to camera (assumed normalized).
        light_direction: vec3, world-space direction from fragment to light source (assumed normalized).
        material: dict with keys 'ka', 'kd', 'ks', 'shininess', 'color_a', 'color_d', 'color_s'.
        light: dict with keys 'ambient_color', 'diffuse_color', 'specular_color'.
        
    Returns:
        vec3, the final fragment color.
    """
    # 1. Ambient Component
    ambient = material['ka'] * light['ambient_color'] * material['color_a']
    
    # 2. Diffuse Component
    # Clamp dot product to [0, 1] to handle back-facing surfaces
    diff_dot = max(dot(normal, light_direction), 0.0)
    diffuse = material['kd'] * light['diffuse_color'] * material['color_d'] * diff_dot
    
    # 3. Specular Component (Original Phong: Reflection Vector)
    # Calculate reflection vector R = 2 * (N . L) * N - L
    n_dot_l = dot(normal, light_direction)
    reflection = 2.0 * n_dot_l * normal - light_direction
    
    # View vector is already normalized
    # Calculate dot product between reflection and view direction
    spec_dot = max(dot(reflection, view_direction), 0.0)
    
    # Raise to shininess power
    spec_intensity = pow(spec_dot, material['shininess'])
    specular = material['ks'] * light['specular_color'] * material['color_s'] * spec_intensity
    
    # 4. Combine
    color = ambient + diffuse + specular
    
    return color
```

**Parameters with Typical Values:**
*   `shininess` ($\alpha$): Controls the size of the specular highlight. Higher values produce smaller, sharper highlights. Typical values range from 10 to 128 [S5, S8, S11, unofficial].
*   `ka`, `kd`, `ks`: Reflection coefficients. Typically sum to $\le 1$ for energy conservation, though Phong is not physically based [S2][S7, unofficial].
*   `color_a`, `color_d`, `color_s`: Material colors. Often `color_a` is a dimmed version of `color_d` [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-Physical:** The model is an approximation and does not conserve energy or strictly follow the rendering equation [S3, S7, unofficial].
*   **Glossy Term Discard:** The original derivation discards the $\cos \theta_s$ factor for the specular term because specular reflections are unaffected by projected surface area [S3, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The specular term involves a power function; very high shininess values can lead to precision issues if the dot product is slightly less than 1.0 [G].
*   **Back-facing:** The diffuse term must be clamped to 0 for back-facing surfaces to avoid incorrect lighting [S7, S12, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Reflectance Map Shading](3606-reflectance-map-shading.md)
A local illumination optimization that evaluates the Blinn-Phong shading model per-pixel using cube map dependent texture reads, thereby avoiding the computational cost of vector normalization. It differs from standard Phong shading by replacing per-pixel dot-product calculations and normalization with a direct lookup into a pre-computed reflectance map organized as six $n \times n$ tables.

### [Phong Shading with Exponential Fog](4998-phong-shading-with-exponential-fog.md)
A fragment shading model that combines the standard Phong lighting components (ambient, diffuse, specular) with exponential distance fog to blend the computed surface color with a fog color based on the fragment's distance from the camera. It differs from standard Phong shading by adding a distance-based attenuation factor that simulates atmospheric depth, and from linear fog models by using an exponential decay function which provides a smoother, more natural falloff.

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per fragment per light source. The cost is dominated by a few dot products, a power function, and vector arithmetic.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for material and light parameters.
*   **Measured Performance:** On modern GPUs, Phong shading is extremely fast, often limited by memory bandwidth or fragment throughput rather than lighting calculations [S3, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Scales linearly with the number of lights. For many lights, performance degrades unless optimized (e.g., using reflectance maps or deferred shading) [S9, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement, computationally efficient, produces visually pleasing results for shiny surfaces [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Not physically based; does not account for shadows, occlusion, or interreflection [S3, unofficial]. The specular highlight can appear "off" if the reflection vector is not accurately computed or if the surface normal is interpolated poorly [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering where performance is critical and physical accuracy is not required [S3, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Physically based rendering (PBR) pipelines, scenes requiring accurate shadowing or global illumination [S3, unofficial].

## Background

Phong shading was developed by Bui Tuong Phong in 1975 [S3, unofficial]. It was introduced as a method to generate higher quality images with lighting by interpolating normals across polygons and evaluating the lighting model per fragment, rather than per vertex (Gouraud shading) [S1, S3, unofficial]. It became the de facto baseline for local illumination in computer graphics [S3, unofficial]. The model is based on empirical observations of how light reflects off surfaces, combining ambient, diffuse, and specular components [S2][S7, unofficial].
