# Whitted-Style Shading with Diffuse/Glossy Materials

Also called: Diffuse Reflection, Lambertian Shading with Shadow Rays, Lambertian reflection, Volume Shading with Shadow Rays, Whitted-Style Shading

## Overview
Whitted-Style Shading with Diffuse/Glossy Materials evaluates illumination at a surface point by combining ambient lighting with direct illumination from light sources, using shadow rays to determine visibility [S2, unofficial]. It differs from its siblings by explicitly separating the computation of diffuse (Lambertian) and glossy (specular) reflection components, whereas [Shading models](837-shading-models.md) provides a broader taxonomy, [Shading for Semi-Transparent Volumes](2144-shading-for-semi-transparent-volumes.md) handles volume attenuation, and [Cook-Torrance BRDF](2509-cook-torrance-brdf.md) uses a more complex microfacet model for speculars.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$.
- Scene geometry (objects with materials) and light sources.
- Material properties: diffuse coefficient $k_d$, glossy coefficient $k_s$, albedo $\mathbf{C}_{albedo}$, shininess exponent $\alpha$.

**Outputs:**
- Final color $\mathbf{C}_{final}$ for the ray.

**Data Structures:**
- `Ray`: Contains origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$, min/max $t$ bounds, and depth [S2, unofficial].
- `HitRecord`: Contains intersection point $\mathbf{p}$, surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, material reference, and distance $t$ [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Intersection Test:** Find the closest intersection between the input ray and the scene geometry. If no intersection is found, return the background color [S2, S3, unofficial].
   - If the hit is invalid, return a default background color, e.g., $\{0.2, 0.6, 1.0\}$ [S3, unofficial].
2. **Transform to World Space:** If the intersection is in object space, transform the normal $\mathbf{n}_{obj}$ and position $\mathbf{p}_{obj}$ to world space using the object-to-world matrix $\mathbf{M}_{obj}$ [S3, unofficial]:
   $$ \mathbf{n} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{M}_{obj} \mathbf{n}_{obj}) $$
   $$ \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{M}_{obj} \mathbf{p}_{obj} $$
3. **Ambient Contribution:** Calculate the ambient term. This is often a constant factor multiplied by the diffuse coefficient and albedo [S2, S3, unofficial]:
   $$ \mathbf{C}_{ambient} = \mathbf{C}_{ambient\_const} \cdot k_d \cdot \mathbf{C}_{albedo} $$
   Typical values: $\mathbf{C}_{ambient\_const} = \{0.25, 0.25, 0.25\}$ [S2, unofficial] or a small constant like $0.003$ [S3, unofficial].
4. **Direct Illumination Loop:** For each light source in the scene:
   a. **Light Direction:** Compute the vector $\mathbf{L}$ from the hit point $\mathbf{p}$ to the light position $\mathbf{p}_L$:
      $$ \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{p}_L - \mathbf{p} $$
      Compute the inverse squared distance:
      $$ d^{-2} = \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{L}\|^2} $$
      Normalize the direction:
      $$ \mathbf{l} = \frac{\mathbf{L}}{\|\mathbf{L}\|} $$
   b. **Shadow Ray:** Cast a shadow ray from $\mathbf{p}$ towards the light. To prevent self-intersection (shadow acne), offset the origin slightly along the normal:
      $$ \mathbf{o}_{shadow} = \mathbf{p} + \epsilon \mathbf{n}, \quad \epsilon \approx 10^{-4} $$
      The shadow ray direction is $\mathbf{l}$. The maximum $t$ is the distance to the light $\|\mathbf{L}\|$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
   c. **Visibility Check:** Trace the shadow ray. If it hits an object before reaching the light, the point is in shadow ($\mathbf{C}_{direct} = \mathbf{0}$) [S3, unofficial].
   d. **Diffuse Calculation:** If not occluded, compute the Lambertian diffuse reflection. The BRDF $f_r$ is constant for diffuse surfaces, related to reflectance $\rho$ by $\rho = \pi f_r$ [S1, unofficial]. The reflected radiance is:
      $$ \mathbf{C}_{diffuse} = k_d \cdot \mathbf{C}_{albedo} \cdot \max(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}, 0) \cdot I_L \cdot d^{-2} $$
      where $I_L$ is the light emission intensity [S3, unofficial].
   e. **Glossy Calculation:** If the material has a glossy component, compute the specular highlight. First, compute the perfect reflection vector $\mathbf{R}$:
      $$ \mathbf{R} = 2(\mathbf{n} \cdot (-\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{n} - (-\mathbf{v}) $$
      where $\mathbf{v}$ is the view direction (from $\mathbf{p}$ to camera). The glossy term is:
      $$ \mathbf{C}_{glossy} = k_s \cdot I_L \cdot d^{-2} \cdot \max(\mathbf{R} \cdot \mathbf{v}, 0)^\alpha $$
      where $\alpha$ is the shininess exponent [S1, unofficial].
5. **Accumulate:** Sum the ambient, diffuse, and glossy contributions:
   $$ \mathbf{C}_{final} = \mathbf{C}_{ambient} + \sum_{lights} (\mathbf{C}_{diffuse} + \mathbf{C}_{glossy}) $$

**Parameters:**
- $\alpha$ (shininess): Controls the sharpness of glossy highlights. Higher values yield narrower highlights [S1, unofficial].
- $\epsilon$ (shadow offset): Prevents self-intersection artifacts. Typical value $10^{-4}$ [S3, unofficial].
- Light position: Typical value $\{-3.0, 2.0, 5.0\}$ [S3, unofficial].
- Light emission: Typical value $20.0$ [S3, unofficial].
- Albedo: Typical value $\{1.0, 1.0, 1.0\}$ [S3, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
- **Self-Intersection:** Without the offset $\epsilon$, shadow rays may immediately hit the surface they originated from, causing incorrect shadows.
- **Zero Distance:** If the light is at the hit point, $d^{-2}$ becomes infinite. Clamp or handle light positions carefully.
- **Back-facing Normals:** Ensure $\max(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}, 0)$ handles cases where the light is behind the surface.

## Subtopics

### [Color Ramp Modulation](14791-color-ramp-modulation.md)
Alters diffuse/specular colors via a 1D texture based on light intensity, allowing stylized color shifts.

### [Inter-reflection Modeling](15367-inter-reflection-modeling.md)
Approximates indirect illumination by tracing a single ray to a secondary intersection, suitable for non-collocated light/camera setups.

### Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays
A simple shading model that returns emissive radiance or diffuse reflectance based on material properties, using shadow rays to determine visibility [S2, unofficial].

### Glossy Reflection
Models reflections on rough surfaces by distributing outgoing rays around the perfect mirror reflection direction, controlled by a shininess exponent $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial].

### Ray Reflection Tracing
Traces reflected rays to simulate mirror-like reflections, limited to a maximum recursion depth (e.g., 1 bounce) [S4, unofficial].

### Water Reflection
Water reflects the sky it is under, rather than a static authored color, making low sun angles optimal for visuals [S5, unofficial].

### Pre-computed Normal Shading
An alternative lighting mode using quantized, pre-computed normals stored in a second Mixture Graph [S6].

### Phong Shading for Hybrid Surfaces
Computes per-pixel normals for hybrid surfaces (isosurface + cut surface) using a Phong fragment shader [S7, unofficial].

### Monte Carlo Shading
Approximates the rendering equation via stochastic estimation, targeting mirror-like objects with high computational cost and variance [S8, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot L)$ per pixel, where $N$ is the number of lights and $L$ is the cost of shadow ray tracing (intersection tests).
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per pixel, plus scene storage.
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement, fast for scenes with few lights, physically plausible for diffuse surfaces.
- **Limitations:** No indirect illumination (global illumination), noisy for rough surfaces if using MC variants, limited recursion depth for reflections [S4, S8, unofficial].
- **When to use:** Real-time rendering with static lighting, simple materials, or as a base for more complex models.
- **When not to use:** Scenes requiring soft shadows, global illumination, or highly complex material interactions.

## Background
Originated from Whitted's 1980 paper "An Improved Illumination Model for Shaded Display" [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is a foundational technique in ray tracing, extending the basic ray-object intersection to include reflection and refraction. In volume rendering, it is adapted to handle local illumination on volume boundaries or implicit surfaces [S3, unofficial].
