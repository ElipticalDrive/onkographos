# Whitted-Style Shading with Diffuse/Glossy Materials

Also called: Diffuse Reflection, Lambertian Shading with Shadow Rays, Lambertian reflection, Volume Shading with Shadow Rays, Whitted-Style Shading

## Overview
Whitted-style shading with diffuse/glossy materials evaluates local illumination at a surface or volume boundary point by combining ambient lighting with direct light contributions, using shadow rays to determine visibility [S2, S3, unofficial]. It differs from full path tracing by limiting recursion to direct lighting and simple reflections, and from pure volume scattering by treating the interaction as a local surface-like reflection model [S1, S2, unofficial]. This method is chosen when real-time performance is required for scenes with opaque or semi-transparent surfaces where approximate shadows and basic specular highlights are sufficient [S2, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$.
*   Scene geometry or volume data structure supporting intersection tests.
*   Material properties: albedo $\mathbf{k}_d$ (diffuse color), shininess $\alpha$ (glossy exponent), specular coefficient $\mathbf{k}_s$ (optional, for glossy).
*   Light sources: position $\mathbf{l}_{pos}$, emission intensity $I_{light}$, and type (point/directional).
*   Camera/View direction $\mathbf{v}$ (for glossy reflection calculation).

**Outputs:**
*   Radiance color $\mathbf{C}_{out}$ at the intersection point.

**Data Structures:**
*   `HitRecord`: Contains intersection point $\mathbf{p}$, surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, material reference, and distance $t$.
*   `Ray`: Origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$, min/max $t$ bounds.

**Steps:**

1.  **Intersection Test:** Trace the primary ray against the scene. Find the closest intersection $\mathbf{p}$ and normal $\mathbf{n}$. If no intersection is found, return the background color [S2, S3, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* If the scene is a volume, the "surface" is often defined by an isosurface or the first density threshold hit. Normals are derived from the volume gradient $\nabla V$ [G].

2.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Transform the hit point $\mathbf{p}$ and normal $\mathbf{n}$ to world space if they are stored in object space [S3, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{world} = M_{obj} \cdot \mathbf{p}_{obj} $$
    $$ \mathbf{n}_{world} = \text{normalize}(M_{obj}^{-T} \cdot \mathbf{n}_{obj}) $$
    where $M_{obj}$ is the model matrix [S3, unofficial].

3.  **Ambient Contribution:** Calculate the ambient term. This is a constant approximation of indirect light [S2, S3, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{ambient} = \mathbf{k}_d \cdot C_{ambient\_const} $$
    Typical value for $C_{ambient\_const}$ is $0.003$ to $0.25$ depending on implementation [S2, S3, unofficial].

4.  **Direct Lighting Loop:** For each light source in the scene:
    a.  **Light Vector:** Compute the direction to the light $\mathbf{l}$ and the inverse squared distance $1/r^2$ [S3, unofficial].
        $$ \mathbf{L}_{vec} = \mathbf{l}_{pos} - \mathbf{p}_{world} $$
        $$ r = \|\mathbf{L}_{vec}\| $$
        $$ \mathbf{l} = \mathbf{L}_{vec} / r $$
        $$ inv\_dd = 1.0 / r^2 $$
    b.  **Shadow Ray:** Cast a shadow ray from $\mathbf{p}_{world}$ towards $\mathbf{l}$ to check for occlusion. To prevent self-intersection (shadow acne), offset the origin slightly along the normal [S3, unofficial].
        $$ \mathbf{o}_{shadow} = \mathbf{p}_{world} + \epsilon \cdot \mathbf{n}_{world}, \quad \epsilon \approx 10^{-4} $$
        $$ \mathbf{d}_{shadow} = \mathbf{l} $$
        If the shadow ray hits any geometry before reaching the light, the point is in shadow ($\mathbf{C}_{diffuse} = \mathbf{0}$). Otherwise, calculate the diffuse contribution [S3, unofficial].
    c.  **Diffuse Calculation:** If not occluded, compute the Lambertian diffuse term [S1, S3, unofficial].
        $$ \mathbf{C}_{diffuse} = \mathbf{k}_d \cdot \frac{\rho}{\pi} \cdot (\mathbf{n}_{world} \cdot \mathbf{l}) \cdot I_{light} \cdot inv\_dd $$
        Where $\rho$ is the reflectance fraction. For a perfect Lambertian surface, $\rho = \|\mathbf{k}_d\|$ (assuming albedo represents reflectance) and the BRDF is constant $f_r = \rho/\pi$ [S1, unofficial]. The term $\max(\mathbf{n}_{world} \cdot \mathbf{l}, 0)$ ensures back-face lighting is zero [S3, unofficial].
    d.  **Glossy/Specular Calculation (Optional):** If the material has a glossy component, calculate the specular highlight [S1, S2, unofficial].
        i.  Compute the perfect reflection vector $\mathbf{R}$:
            $$ \mathbf{R} = 2(\mathbf{n}_{world} \cdot -\mathbf{v}) \mathbf{n}_{world} + \mathbf{v} $$
            (Note: $\mathbf{v}$ is the view direction, typically $-\mathbf{d}$).
        ii. Calculate the dot product between $\mathbf{R}$ and the view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (or the direction to the eye). In Whitted-style, this is often simplified to the dot product between the reflection vector and the light vector for the specular lobe, or the reflection vector and the view vector for ray tracing reflections. Based on [S1, unofficial], the glossy term uses the dot product of the reflection vector and the view direction raised to the shininess exponent:
            $$ \text{specular\_term} = (\max(\mathbf{R} \cdot \mathbf{v}, 0))^\alpha $$
        iii. Multiply by the specular coefficient and light intensity:
            $$ \mathbf{C}_{specular} = \mathbf{k}_s \cdot \text{specular\_term} \cdot I_{light} \cdot inv\_dd $$
        *Note:* Some implementations [S2, unofficial] simply add a constant ambient or use a simplified Phong model. The Whitted-style glossy reflection often involves spawning a new ray in direction $\mathbf{R}$ [S4, unofficial].

5.  **Reflection Tracing (Whitted-Style Extension):** If the material supports glossy/mirror reflections, spawn a new ray in direction $\mathbf{R}$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   Recursively call the shading function for this new ray, limited by a maximum recursion depth (e.g., 1 bounce) [S4, unofficial].
    *   Add the returned radiance weighted by the material's specular reflectivity [S4, unofficial].

6.  **Accumulation:** Sum the ambient, diffuse, specular, and reflected contributions [S2, S3, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{out} = \mathbf{C}_{ambient} + \sum_{lights} (\mathbf{C}_{diffuse} + \mathbf{C}_{specular}) + \mathbf{C}_{reflection} $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def whitted_shade(ray, scene, depth, max_depth):
    # 1. Find intersection
    hit = scene.closest_intersection(ray)
    
    # 2. Handle no hit
    if not hit.valid:
        return scene.background_color

    # 3. Transform to world space
    p_world = hit.object.transform_point(hit.p)
    n_world = normalize(hit.object.transform_normal(hit.ng))
    v_world = normalize(-ray.d) # View direction is opposite to ray direction

    # 4. Ambient term
    albedo = hit.material.albedo
    ambient = albedo * 0.003 # Typical ambient constant [S3, unofficial]

    # 5. Direct lighting
    direct_light = vec3(0.0)
    for light in scene.lights:
        # Light vector and distance
        l_vec = light.position - p_world
        dist = length(l_vec)
        l_dir = normalize(l_vec)
        inv_dd = 1.0 / (dist * dist)

        # Shadow ray
        shadow_origin = p_world + 1e-4 * n_world # Prevent self-intersection [S3, unofficial]
        shadow_ray = Ray(shadow_origin, l_dir)
        occluded = scene.trace_any(shadow_ray)

        if not occluded:
            # Diffuse term (Lambertian)
            # rho/pi * (n . l) * I_light * inv_dd
            # Assuming albedo is rho, and BRDF is rho/pi [S1, unofficial]
            diff_factor = max(dot(n_world, l_dir), 0.0)
            light_intensity = light.emission * inv_dd
            diffuse = albedo * (diff_factor * light_intensity) # Simplified: rho/pi integrated out or absorbed in constant? 
            # Strictly: L_r = (rho/pi) * E. E = L_i * cos(theta). 
            # So diffuse = albedo * (1.0/pi) * diff_factor * light_intensity
            # However, [S3, unofficial] uses: glm::max(glm::dot(L, ng), 0.0f) * light_emission * inv_dd * albedo
            # This implies the 1/pi factor is either ignored or absorbed into light_emission/albedo scaling.
            # We follow [S3, unofficial] implementation detail for consistency with provided notes.
            diffuse = albedo * diff_factor * light_intensity
            
            # Glossy/Specular term (if applicable)
            specular = vec3(0.0)
            if hit.material.has_glossy:
                # Reflection vector
                r_vec = reflect(-l_dir, n_world) # Reflect light direction? Or view?
                # [S1, unofficial] uses R (perfect mirror reflection) and view direction.
                # For direct lighting specular highlight, we usually reflect the LIGHT vector around the normal
                # and dot with VIEW. Or reflect VIEW and dot with LIGHT.
                # Standard Phong/Blinn-Phong is different. Whitted glossy reflection usually traces a ray.
                # If tracing a ray:
                if depth < max_depth:
                    refl_ray = Ray(p_world + 1e-4 * n_world, reflect(-v_world, n_world))
                    refl_color = whitted_shade(refl_ray, scene, depth + 1, max_depth)
                    specular = hit.material.specular_coeff * refl_color
                else:
                    specular = vec3(0.0)
            
            direct_light += diffuse + specular

    return ambient + direct_light
```

**Parameters:**
*   `light_position`: Typical value `{-3.0, 2.0, 5.0}` [S3, unofficial].
*   `light_emission`: Typical value `20.0` [S3, unofficial].
*   `albedo`: Surface reflectance color, typical value `{1.0, 1.0, 1.0}` [S3, unofficial].
*   `shadow_offset`: `1e-4` times normal magnitude [S3, unofficial].
*   `ambient_constant`: `0.003` [S3, unofficial] or `0.25` [S2, unofficial].
*   `max_depth`: Typically 1 for basic Whitted shading [S4, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Self-Intersection:** Shadow rays originating from the surface can hit the surface itself. Mitigated by offsetting the origin along the normal [S3, unofficial].
*   **Back-Facing Normals:** Ensure $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l} \ge 0$ to avoid negative lighting [S3, unofficial].
*   **Recursion Depth:** Unbounded recursion leads to infinite loops. Enforce a maximum depth [S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Whitted-Style Shading with Diffuse/Glossy Materials — Direct Illumination with Shadow Rays
A simplified variant that computes only diffuse reflectance and ambient lighting, using shadow rays for visibility but omitting recursive reflection tracing [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen for performance-critical applications where only direct lighting is needed. It returns emissive radiance if the material is emissive, otherwise diffuse reflectance [S2, unofficial].

### Glossy Reflection
Models reflections on rough surfaces by distributing outgoing rays around the perfect mirror reflection direction, controlled by a shininess exponent $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when a sharp but not perfect mirror reflection is desired. The intensity is calculated as $(\max(\mathbf{R} \cdot \mathbf{v}, 0))^\alpha$ [S1, unofficial].

### Ray Reflection Tracing
Traces reflected rays to simulate mirror-like reflections, limited to a maximum recursion depth [S4, unofficial]. It is chosen for scenes requiring accurate mirror reflections. It is limited to 1 bounce in the provided implementation [S4, unofficial].

### Water Reflection
A specific application where water reflects the sky it is under, rather than a static color, making low sun angles optimal for visuals [S5, unofficial]. It is chosen for aquatic environments. Swimming underwater limits sight distance to 34 meters and removes red light [S5, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot L \cdot D)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels, $L$ is the number of lights, and $D$ is the maximum recursion depth (typically 1) [S2, S4, unofficial]. Shadow ray intersection tests dominate the cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per pixel for shading, plus scene storage. No large buffers required compared to screen-space methods [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Fast for small scenes with few lights. Performance degrades linearly with the number of lights and scene complexity due to shadow ray casting [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; produces sharp shadows and specular highlights; real-time capable [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** No soft shadows (unless jittered); no indirect illumination (global illumination); aliasing on reflections; limited to surface-like interactions [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering, games, interactive applications where direct lighting and sharp shadows are sufficient [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring soft shadows, global illumination, or complex light transport [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Whitted-style shading originated from the Whitted ray tracing algorithm [S1, S2, unofficial]. It was adapted for real-time rendering and volume shading contexts, often combining Lambertian diffuse models with shadow rays for visibility [S1, S3, unofficial]. The method is widely used in educational ray tracers and real-time engines for basic surface shading [S2, S3, unofficial].
