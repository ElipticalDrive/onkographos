# Voxel-Space Ambient Occlusion

Also called: VSAO

## Overview
Voxel-Space Ambient Occlusion (VSAO) is a multiresolution, screen-space technique that computes ambient occlusion using a coarse voxel representation of scene geometry [S1, unofficial]. It improves upon standard G-buffer based techniques by avoiding artifacts such as haloing and is scalable to the level-of-detail desired by the user [S1, unofficial]. Unlike screen-space techniques that rely on depth comparisons or sphere approximations, VSAO assumes voxels are small objects and computes occlusion using an area rendering equation formulation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Screen-space geometry (G-buffer data).
*   Camera parameters (up, right, at vectors; near and far planes).
*   User-defined parameters:
    *   `screen_space_radius`: Radius in world space around the pixel for sampling [S1, unofficial].
    *   `num_samples`: Number of samples within a cosine-weighted hemisphere [S1, unofficial].
    *   `num_radii_steps`: Number of times each sample is used at different radii (if multiple-radii sampling is enabled) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Ambient occlusion factor per pixel.

### Data Structures
The technique uses a voxel buffer to store occupancy information.
*   **Buffer Layout:** Four 128-bit integer buffers are used over the depth range [S1, unofficial].
*   **Bins:** This results in 512 bins between the near and far planes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Content:** Each bin represents a boolean value: `1` indicates the presence of geometry, `0` indicates absence [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Geometry:** Voxels are generated within the viewing frustum, resulting in non-uniform voxels that mimic the shape of the frustum. They are typically "stretched out" in the Z-direction (depth) compared to X and Y [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Voxelization:** Voxelize screen-space geometry using a single-pass technique by Eisemann and Decorat [ED08] to generate a coarse representation of scene geometry [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Coordinate System Orientation:** Orient the voxel's coordinate system such that:
    *   Camera's up vector is co-linear with the voxel's y-direction.
    *   Camera's right vector is co-linear with the voxel's x-direction.
    *   Camera's at vector is co-linear with the voxel's z-direction [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Voxel Size Calculation:** Compute the world size of the front and back faces of the voxel by performing an inverse perspective transform on the pixel coordinates [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Side Area Calculation:** Compute the areas of the sides oriented along the z-axis of the voxel's coordinate system. This is done by dividing the region between the near and far plane into 512 equal bins [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Sample Generation:** Generate `num_samples` points within a cosine-weighted hemisphere centered at the surface point [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Projection:** Multiply the sample directions by the `screen_space_radius` and project them back to screen-space, noting the depth at each sample location [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Occupancy Query:** Query the voxel buffer to check for occupancy at each sample location [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Occlusion Contribution:** If a voxel is present at a sample location, calculate its contribution to ambient occlusion using the area formulation (Equation 8 below) [S1, unofficial].
9.  **Multiple-Radii Sampling (Optional):** If enabled, each cosine-weighted hemisphere sample is used multiple times at different radii (number of times set by user). Voxels closer to the point take higher precedence; samples at larger radii are ignored if a voxel is occupied at a smaller radius location for the same sample direction [S1, unofficial].
10. **Multiresolution Rendering:** Use a stencil-based screen-space multiresolution buffer to refine and render the final image. Curvature and depth discontinuity metrics are used to identify appropriate sampling rates, with higher detail applied to crevices [S1, unofficial].

### Formula
The ambient occlusion contribution is derived from the area representation of the rendering equation. The total ambient occlusion $AO$ is computed as [S1, unofficial]:

$$
AO = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \frac{4\beta}{|x-y|^2} \frac{(N_x \cdot \theta_i) (N_y \cdot -\theta_i)}{V_p}
$$

Where:
*   $N$ is the number of samples [S1, unofficial].
*   $\beta$ is a constant [S1, unofficial].
*   $|x-y|$ is the distance between the point and the sample location [S1, unofficial].
*   $N_x$ and $N_y$ are the normals of the voxel surfaces contributing to occlusion [S1, unofficial].
*   $\theta_i$ is the direction of the $i$-th sample [S1, unofficial].
*   $V_p$ is the visibility of a point on the object [S1, unofficial].

*Note: The sources provide the formula in a summation form in Equation 8, but the text also references a simplified fraction form in the notes. The summation form is the standard Monte Carlo integration result derived in the source [S1, unofficial].*

def compute_voxel_space_ao(surface_point, surface_normal, camera, voxel_buffer, 
                           screen_space_radius, num_samples, num_radii_steps):
    """
    Computes ambient occlusion for a surface point using voxel-space sampling.
    
    Args:
        surface_point: World space position of the point.
        surface_normal: Normal vector at the surface point.
        camera: Object containing up, right, at vectors and projection info.
        voxel_buffer: 512-bin occupancy buffer (4x128-bit integers).
        screen_space_radius: World space radius for sampling.
        num_samples: Number of hemisphere samples.
        num_radii_steps: Number of radii steps for multiple-radius sampling.
        
    Returns:
        float: Ambient occlusion factor.
    """
    total_occlusion = 0.0
    N = num_samples
    
    # Generate cosine-weighted hemisphere samples
    samples = generate_cosine_weighted_hemisphere_samples(surface_normal, N)
    
    # Determine radii steps
    if num_radii_steps > 1:
        radii = generate_radii_steps(screen_space_radius, num_radii_steps)
    else:
        radii = [screen_space_radius]
        
    # For each sample direction
    for sample_dir in samples:
        # Track if this sample direction has been occluded at a closer radius
        occluded = False
        
        # Iterate through radii from smallest to largest
        for radius in radii:
            if occluded:
                break
                
            # Project sample point to world space
            sample_offset = sample_dir * radius
            sample_world_pos = surface_point + sample_offset
            
            # Project sample_world_pos to screen space and get depth
            screen_pos, sample_depth = project_to_screen(sample_world_pos, camera)
            
            # Query voxel buffer for occupancy at this screen location and depth bin
            # The depth bin is determined by mapping sample_depth to the 512 bins
            bin_index = map_depth_to_bin(sample_depth, camera.near_plane, camera.far_plane, num_bins=512)
            
            # Check occupancy in the voxel buffer
            # The buffer is 4x128-bit integers, so we need to determine which integer and bit
            occupied = check_voxel_occupancy(voxel_buffer, screen_pos, bin_index)
            
            if occupied:
                # Calculate contribution of this voxel to AO
                # Note: The formula requires voxel normals and distance.
                # In a real implementation, the voxel buffer might store more info,
                # or we approximate using the surface normal and distance.
                # The source equation uses N_x, N_y (voxel normals) and V_p (visibility).
                # For simplicity, we assume the voxel contributes based on distance and 
                # a simplified visibility term if detailed voxel normals are not stored.
                
                distance_sq = length_squared(sample_world_pos - surface_point)
                
                # Simplified contribution based on source Equation 8 structure
                # Assuming beta is a constant, and visibility V_p is 1 if occupied
                # The term (N_x . theta_i) (N_y . -theta_i) requires voxel normals.
                # Since the source does not specify how voxel normals are stored in the 
                # 128-bit buffer, we assume a placeholder for voxel normal calculation.
                voxel_normal = get_voxel_normal_at(screen_pos, bin_index, camera) # [G]
                
                # Dot products for the area formulation
                dot1 = dot(voxel_normal, sample_dir)
                dot2 = dot(voxel_normal, -sample_dir) # Assuming symmetry or specific voxel orientation
                
                # Contribution
                # Note: The source formula has 4*beta / |x-y|^2 * ... / V_p
                # We assume V_p = 1 for occupied voxels.
                contribution = (4.0 * 1.0) / distance_sq * dot1 * dot2 # [G] beta=1 assumed if not specified
                
                total_occlusion += contribution
                occluded = True # Closer voxel takes precedence
            else:
                # If not occupied at this radius, continue to next radius
                pass
                
    # Average over N samples
    ao_factor = total_occlusion / N
    
    return ao_factor

def generate_cosine_weighted_hemisphere_samples(normal, count):
    """Generates count samples on a cosine-weighted hemisphere."""
    samples = []
    for _ in range(count):
        # Standard cosine-weighted hemisphere sampling
        u = random_float()
        v = random_float()
        r = sqrt(u)
        theta = 2.0 * pi * v
        x = r * cos(theta)
        y = r * sin(theta)
        z = sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - u))
        
        # Construct basis vectors
        if abs(normal.z) < 0.999:
            up = vec3(0, 0, 1)
        else:
            up = vec3(1, 0, 0)
            
        tangent = normalize(cross(up, normal))
        bitangent = cross(normal, tangent)
        
        sample = tangent * x + bitangent * y + normal * z
        samples.append(sample)
    return samples

def map_depth_to_bin(depth, near, far, num_bins):
    """Maps depth to one of 512 bins."""
    # Linear mapping from depth range to bin index
    # Note: Depth is usually non-linear in screen space, but the source 
    # mentions dividing the region between near and far plane into 512 equal bins.
    # This implies linear depth bins in world space or normalized depth.
    normalized_depth = (depth - near) / (far - near)
    bin_index = int(normalized_depth * num_bins)
    return max(0, min(num_bins - 1, bin_index))

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Non-uniform Voxels:** Voxels are generated within the viewing frustum, resulting in non-uniform voxels that mimic the shape of the frustum. They are usually "stretched out" in the Z-direction, having more depth than width or height. This affects how ambient occlusion is calculated, as individual sides of voxels need to be projected onto the hemisphere [S1, unofficial].
*   **Buffer Size:** A single 128-bit integer buffer is insufficient for high-quality ambient occlusion. Four 128-bit integer buffers (512 bins) are required [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precedence:** In multiple-radii sampling, voxels closer to the point take higher precedence. Samples at larger radii are ignored if a voxel is occupied at a smaller radius location for the same sample direction [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of samples (`num_samples`) and the number of radii steps (`num_radii_steps`). The voxelization is a single pass [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires four 128-bit integer buffers for the voxel representation, resulting in 512 bins [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The technique offers a significant quality increase with only a small performance hit compared to using fewer bins [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scalability:** The technique is scalable to the level-of-detail desired by the user via the number of samples and radii steps [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Avoids haloing artifacts common in G-buffer based techniques. Coarse geometry representation suffices due to the low frequency nature of ambient occlusion [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Non-uniform voxels due to frustum shape may affect calculation accuracy. Requires careful tuning of sampling rates using curvature and depth discontinuities [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Voxel-Space Ambient Occlusion was presented by Rajeev Penmat and Chris Wyman from the University of Iowa in a technical report (UICS-12-01) dated February 1, 2012 [S1, unofficial]. It extends the stencil-based multi-resolution technique by Nichols et al. by using curvature and depth discontinuities, as opposed to normal and depth discontinuities [S1, unofficial]. The voxelization step uses a technique by Eisemann and Decorat [ED08]. The multiresolution approach is similar to that by Nichols and Wyman [NW0...] [S1, unofficial].
