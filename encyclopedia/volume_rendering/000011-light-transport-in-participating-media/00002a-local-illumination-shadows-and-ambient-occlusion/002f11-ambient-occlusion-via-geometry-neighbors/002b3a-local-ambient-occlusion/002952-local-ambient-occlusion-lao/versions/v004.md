# Local Ambient Occlusion (LAO)

Also called: LAO Renderer, Local Ambient Occlusion (LAO) in DVR, Volumetric Ambient Occlusion

## Overview
Local Ambient Occlusion (LAO) is a technique to estimate local occlusion by sampling the volume along random directions around the light vector, weighted by distance, to simulate ambient occlusion effects in volume rendering [S2, unofficial]. It differs from global illumination methods by approximating indirect lighting and self-shadowing through incremental, per-pixel ray casting into the volume, rather than computing full light transport paths [S3, unofficial]. It builds on [Local Ambient Occlusion](4977-local-ambient-occlusion.md) by providing a specific GPU-based implementation for volumetric data.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Volume data (scalar field), Transfer Functions for Color ($TF_c$) and Emission ($TF_E$), light direction $\mathbf{l}$, and screen coordinates $(x, y)$.
*   **Output:** A Local Ambient Occlusion (LAO) map stored in a Frame Buffer Object (FBO), where planes correspond to screen coordinates and store intensity/light computation results [S3, unofficial]. This map is subsequently used in the final ray-casting pass [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **LAO Map:** Stored in an FBO. The pipeline uses indices $V \to P$ (Volume to Pixel) and $P \to V$ (Pixel to Volume) to map packed coordinates to volume coordinates [S3, unofficial].
*   **Multiresolution Volume:** Voxels are organized into a multiresolution representation (e.g., flat multiresolution blocking) to optimize image quality and reduce computation [S3, unofficial]. Empty space skipping is applied if a volume block is smaller than $4^3$ [S3, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $uLocalAmbientOcclusion$: Boolean flag to enable/disable LAO calculation. Default: `true` [S2, unofficial].
*   $uNumLAOSamples$: Number of LAO samples per pixel. Default: `1` [S2, unofficial].
*   $uLAOStepSize$: Step size for LAO ray marching. Default: `0.05` [S2, unofficial].
*   $uLAOWeight$: Weight applied to the LAO contribution when mixing into the final color. Default: `0.69` [S2, unofficial].
*   $uLightRadious$: Radius of the light source used for LAO sampling. Default: `0.19` [S2, unofficial].
*   $uLightCoeficient$: Coefficient used to normalize LAO accumulation. Default: `1.0` [S2, unofficial].
*   $c_E(s)$: Colored light emission parameter at sample point $s$, controlled by the emissive component in the transfer function [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The method estimates light from $K$ number of directions, updated incrementally with one ray per frame [S3, unofficial].

1.  **Initialize Accumulator:**
    Set `accumuLAOContribution` to `vec4(0.0)` [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Outer Loop (Directions):**
    Loop $K$ times (e.g., 1 or 8 directions) [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Generate Random Direction:**
        Compute a random direction vector:
        $$ \mathbf{d}_{rand} = \text{normalize}((-1.0 + 2.0 \cdot \mathbf{v}_{rand1}) \cdot \mathbf{v}_{rand2}) $$
        where $\mathbf{v}_{rand1}$ and $\mathbf{v}_{rand2}$ are random vectors in $[-1, 1]^3$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Compute LAO Half Vector:**
        For each sample $t$ in the inner loop, compute the sampling direction:
        $$ \mathbf{h} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{l} + \mathbf{d}_{rand} \cdot \text{mix}(0.0, uLightRadious, t) - \mathbf{p}) $$
        where $\mathbf{l}$ is the light vector, $\mathbf{p}$ is the current position, and $t$ is the normalized distance [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Inner Loop (Ray Marching):**
        Loop $uNumLAOSamples$ times. Inside, loop $t$ from $0.001$ to $1.0$ with step $uLAOStepSize$ [S2, unofficial].
        *   **Sample Volume:**
            Sample the volume at $\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{h} \cdot t$. Let this sample be $S$ [S2, unofficial].
        *   **Accumulate Contribution:**
            Add to the accumulator weighted by $(1.0 - t)^2$:
            $$ \text{accumuLAOContribution} \leftarrow \text{accumuLAOContribution} + S \cdot (1.0 - t)^2 $$
            [S2, unofficial]. Light is contributed at each sample point (or only at the boundary depending on configuration) [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Normalize Direction Accumulation:**
        After the inner loop, divide the accumulated contribution by $uLightCoeficient$, clamp to $[0, 1]$, and add to `LAOContribution` [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Final Normalization:**
    After the outer loop, divide `LAOContribution` by $uNumLAOSamples$ [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Final Ray Casting:**
    Use the computed LAO map in the final ray-casting shader. The shader applies $TF_c$ for Color and $TF_E$ for Emission. The RGB channel stores the emissive color, and the Alpha channel stores the Ambient Occlusion [S3, unofficial]. The incident light equation is modified to include colored light emission $c_E(s)$:
    $$ I_k(x) = \frac{\int_{a}^{R_\Omega} (1 + c_E(s)) \exp\left(-\int_{a}^{s} \tau(u) du\right) ds}{R_\Omega - a} $$
    [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_lao_map(volume, light_vector, screen_coords, params):
    """
    Computes the Local Ambient Occlusion map for a given screen coordinate.
    volume: Function to sample volume data at (x,y,z).
    light_vector: Normalized light direction vector.
    screen_coords: (x, y) screen coordinates.
    params: Dictionary containing uNumLAOSamples, uLAOStepSize, uLightRadious, etc.
    """
    # Initialize accumulator
    accumu_lao = vec4(0.0)
    
    # Convert screen coords to volume position p
    p = screen_to_volume(screen_coords)
    
    # Outer loop: K directions
    for k in range(params['K']):
        # Generate random direction
        rand1 = vec3(random_float(), random_float(), random_float())
        rand2 = random_float()
        random_dir = normalize((-1.0 + 2.0 * rand1) * rand2)
        
        # Compute half vector based on light and random direction
        # Note: The formula in [S2, unofficial] mixes light and random direction scaled by radius
        lao_half_vector = normalize(light_vector + random_dir * mix(0.0, params['uLightRadious'], 0.5) - p)
        
        # Inner loop: Ray marching
        t = 0.001
        while t <= 1.0:
            # Sample volume at position + half_vector * t
            sample_pos = p + lao_half_vector * t
            sample_value = volume(sample_pos)
            
            # Accumulate weighted by (1-t)^2
            weight = pow(1.0 - t, 2.0)
            accumu_lao += sample_value * weight
            
            t += params['uLAOStepSize']
        
        # Normalize by light coefficient and clamp
        contribution = clamp(accumu_lao / params['uLightCoeficient'], 0.0, 1.0)
        accumu_lao = contribution # Reset or accumulate? [S2, unofficial] says "add to LAOContribution", implying accumulation across K
        
    # Final normalization by number of samples
    final_lao = accumu_lao / params['uNumLAOSamples']
    
    return final_lao
```
*Note: The pseudocode above simplifies the accumulation logic described in [S2, unofficial]. Specifically, [S2, unofficial] states "After inner loop, divide by uLightCoeficient... and add to LAOContribution" and "After outer loop, divide LAOContribution by uNumLAOSamples". The exact accumulation structure across K directions is not fully detailed in the provided notes, so the pseudocode assumes a standard accumulation pattern [G].*

## Subtopics

### In-scattering / Emittance Integral
A discretized compositing scheme for computing in-scattered light using an emittance integral, implemented via progressive refinement with one direction per pass [S3, unofficial]. It is similar to Local Ambient Occlusion (LAO) but applied to in-scattered/emissive light contributions rather than just shadowing [S3, unofficial].
*   **Steps:** Discretize the emittance integral into a compositing scheme. Apply progressive refinement, computing one light direction per rendering pass [S3, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** $I_{bias}$: Minimum light value clamped for a volume sample to prevent dark artifacts. Typical value: $0.0$ to $0.2$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Translucency effect parameters: $R_\Omega$ (ray count) = $16$ or $48$, $I_{bias} = 0$ or $0.2$. Higher $R_\Omega$ and $I_{bias}$ increase brightness and reduce noise [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method updates incrementally, adding one ray per frame [S3, unofficial]. Performance on an NVIDIA GF8800 Ultra with 768 MB graphics texture memory was 51 ms/frame to update one ray in the LAO map, resulting in 27 FPS [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires an FBO to store the LAO map [S3, unofficial]. Multiresolution blocking reduces the number of voxels to compute, offering super-linear performance increases for large volumes (e.g., $512^3$) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Avoids superfluous sampling in areas of low occlusion contribution through multiresolution approaches [S3, unofficial]. Enhances luminosity effects by incorporating emissive components [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Incremental update requires multiple frames to converge to a stable result [S3, unofficial]. Performance is sensitive to the number of directions $K$ and the volume resolution [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When real-time or near-real-time ambient occlusion is needed for volumetric data, and full global illumination is too expensive [S3, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When instantaneous, frame-accurate occlusion is required without temporal accumulation [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Local Ambient Occlusion for volume rendering was developed by Patric Ljung at Siemens Corporate Research, Princeton, USA [S3, unofficial]. It was presented in the paper "Advanced Illumination Techniques for GPU-Based Volume Raycasting" (2008) [S3, unofficial]. The technique builds on earlier work in flat multiresolution blocking [LLYM04] [S1]. It is used in GPU-based volume raycasting to improve the visual quality of volumetric data by adding self-shadowing and indirect lighting effects [S3, unofficial].
