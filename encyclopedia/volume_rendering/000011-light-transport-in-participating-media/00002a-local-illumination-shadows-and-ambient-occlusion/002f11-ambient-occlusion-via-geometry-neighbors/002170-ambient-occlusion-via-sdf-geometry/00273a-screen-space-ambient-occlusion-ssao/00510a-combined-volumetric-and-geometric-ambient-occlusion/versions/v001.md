# Combined Volumetric and Geometric Ambient Occlusion

Also called: Combined Occlusion Effects for Combined Volumes and Tubular Geometry, Combined Volumetric and Geometric Occlusion Shading, Combined surface and volumetric occlusion shading, DOS extension for tubes, DOS shading, Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) with Volumetric and Geometric Occlusion, Extended DOS Method

## Overview
This method computes ambient occlusion for visualizations combining volumetric scalar fields and tube-shaped geometric primitives (e.g., streamlines, DTI fibers) by considering mutual occlusion between the volume and the geometry [S1]. It addresses the lack of depth discrimination in separate volumetric and geometric occlusion methods by integrating shadows cast by geometry into volume, volume onto geometry, and geometry onto geometry [S1]. Unlike screen-space ambient occlusion (SSAO) which approximates low-frequency occlusion with spheres, this method captures low-frequency occlusion effects in a volumetric occlusion buffer using Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS), while using a vicinity occlusion term based on geometry depths for high-frequency local geometric structures [S1].

## How it works
The algorithm extends the Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) model to allow rendering of geometric shapes in combination with a 3D volume context, considering mutual occlusion between structures represented by a volume or geometry [S1]. It is based on incremental filtering, used to approximate integration for computing advanced volumetric scattering and shading effects [S1]. The method integrates into a slice-based volume renderer [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   3D scalar volume data.
*   Geometric structures (e.g., stream tubes, DTI fiber tracts) represented as triangle meshes.
*   Transfer function mapping scalar values and gradients to scattering ($\sigma_s$) and extinction ($\sigma_t$) coefficients.
*   Slice distance $d$.
*   DOS cone opening angle $\theta$.
*   User-specified `geometry_opacity` (high opacity value).
*   `depth_bias` offset for vicinity occlusion.
*   Vicinity occlusion extent $R_t$.

**Outputs:**
*   Final composited image in `eye_buffer`.

### Data Structures
*   **G-Buffer:** A set of 2D images storing per-pixel: color, view space position, view space normal, and view space distance (depth) [S1].
*   **volume_occl_buf:** A 2D buffer storing the volumetric occlusion factor. It is double-buffered (`volume_occl_bufprev` and `volume_occl_bufnext`) and swapped after each slice's volumetric occlusion update [S1].
*   **partial_occl_buffer:** A 2D buffer storing the shadows cast by the volume onto the geometry. It is updated by interpolating between volumetric occlusion values of adjacent slices based on the surface distance [S1].
*   **eye_buffer:** The final color buffer where the composited volume and occlusion effects are rendered [S1].
*   **Stencil Buffer:** Used to mask fragments covered by streamlines geometry to optimize partial occlusion buffer updates [S1].

### Parameters
*   `geometry_opacity`: A user-specified high opacity value used to replace the transfer function opacity when a volume sample is behind a geometric structure [S1].
*   `depth_bias`: An offset added to the surface depth value to avoid self-occlusion in the vicinity occlusion term [S1].
*   `slice_distance` ($d$): The distance between adjacent volume slices [S1].
*   $\theta$: The opening angle of the DOS cone [S1].
*   $R_t$: The vicinity occlusion extent [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Preprocessing (G-Buffer):** Render geometric structures before slice traversal to capture colors, positions, normals, and depth values into the G-Buffer. Use the stencil buffer to mask fragments covered by streamlines geometry [S1].
2.  **Initialization:** Initialize `eye_buffer` to background color. Initialize `volume_occl_bufprev` and `volume_occl_bufnext` to 1. Initialize `partial_occl_buffer` to 1 [S1].
3.  **Slice Loop:** For each slice $s$ in all slices:
    a.  Call `UpdateEyeBuffer(s)`.
    b.  Call `UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s)`.
    c.  Call `UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s)`.
4.  **Compositing:** Call `CompositeBuffers()` to combine the eye buffer, partial occlusion buffer, and G-Buffer for final shading [S1].
5.  **Vicinity Occlusion:** Compute the vicinity occlusion term using the depth buffer of the geometric structures to capture high-frequency occlusion effects [S1].

    G_buffer = ComputeGBuffer(geometry)
    
    # 2. Initialization
    eye_buffer = background_color
    volume_occl_bufprev = 1.0
    volume_occl_bufnext = 1.0
    partial_occl_buffer = 1.0
    
    # 3. Slice Loop
    for s in all_slices:
        UpdateEyeBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, slice_distance)
        UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s, volume_occl_bufprev, volume_occl_bufnext, geometry, slice_distance)
        UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, geometry_opacity)
        
        # Swap buffers for next iteration
        volume_occl_bufprev, volume_occl_bufnext = volume_occl_bufnext, volume_occl_bufprev
        
    # 4. Compositing
    CompositeBuffers(eye_buffer, partial_occl_buffer, G_buffer)
    
    # 5. Vicinity Occlusion (can be done in parallel or after)
    vicinity_occl = VicinityOcclusion(geometry, depth_bias, R_t)
    
    return eye_buffer, vicinity_occl

        'position': Image2D(),
        'normal': Image2D(),
        'depth': Image2D(),
        'linear_depth': Image2D(),
        'stencil': StencilBuffer()
    }

def UpdateEyeBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, slice_distance):
    """
    Updates the eye buffer for the current slice, incorporating volumetric occlusion
    and geometric occlusion (if volume is behind geometry).
    """
    SetRenderTarget(eye_buffer, geometry.geom_depth_stencil)
    EnableDepthTest(PassIfLess, DoNotWriteDepth)
    EnableBlending(OneMinusDestinationAlpha, One)
    
    for fragment f of s:
        # Sample volume
        x, grad_mag = Texture3D(volume, f)
        sigma_s, sigma_t = Texture2D(transfer_function, (x, grad_mag))
        
        # Sample volumetric occlusion from previous buffer
        occlusion = Texture2D(volume_occl_bufprev, f.clip)
        
        # Check geometric occlusion
        # delta is the distance to the nearest geometric surface along the view ray
        delta = abs(f.viewz - Texture2D(geometry.geom_linear_depth, f.clip))
        
        # Sampling distance is limited by slice distance or distance to geometry
        sampling_distance = min(slice_distance, delta)
        
        # Compute alpha
        alpha = 1.0 - exp(-sigma_t * sampling_distance)
        
        # Output color and alpha
        frame_buffer = (f.La * sigma_s * occlusion * alpha, alpha)

def UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, geometry_opacity):
    """
    Computes the volumetric occlusion factor for the current slice.
    Incorporates occlusion from geometry into the volume.
    """
    SetRenderTarget(volume_occl_bufnext)
    DisableBlending()
    
    for fragment f of s:
        # Sample volume
        x, grad_mag = Texture3D(volume, f)
        sigma_t = Texture2D(transfer_function, (x, grad_mag))
        
        # Check if volume sample is behind geometric structure
        if f.depth < Texture2D(geometry.geom_depth, f.clip):
            sigma_t = geometry_opacity
            
        # Compute DOS occlusion
        occlusion = ComputeDOS(volume_occl_bufprev, sigma_t)
        
        frame_buffer = occlusion

def UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s, volume_occl_bufprev, volume_occl_bufnext, geometry, slice_distance):
    """
    Computes the partial occlusion buffer, storing shadows cast by volume onto geometry.
    Interpolates between volumetric occlusion of previous and current slices.
    """
    SetRenderTarget(partial_occl_buffer, geometry.geom_depth_stencil)
    DisableBlending()
    EnableDepthTest(PassIfLess, DoNotWriteDepth)
    EnableStencilTest(StencilFunc(Equal, 1, ~0), StencilOp(Keep, Decrease, Keep))
    
    # Render previous slice s-1 because current slice s is occluded by surface
    for fragment f of s-1:
        z_surface = Texture2D(geometry.geom_linear_depth, f.clip)
        z_slice = f.viewz
        z_delta = abs(z_surface - z_slice)
        
        # Clamp z_delta by slice distance
        z_ratio = min(z_delta, slice_distance) / slice_distance
        
        occl_prev = Texture2D(volume_occl_bufprev, f.clip)
        occl_next = Texture2D(volume_occl_bufnext, f.clip)
        
        # Interpolate occlusion
        partial_occl = occl_prev * (1.0 - z_ratio) + occl_next * z_ratio
        
        frame_buffer = partial_occl

def VicinityOcclusion(geometry, depth_bias, R_t):

    Computes high-frequency geometric occlusion using screen-space depth sampling.
    """

    for sample p_i within vicinity occlusion extent R_t:
        # Project p_i into texture space

        depth_sample = Texture2D(geometry.geom_linear_depth, t_i)

### Formulas
*   $z_\Delta = |z_{slice} - z_{surface}|$ [S1]
*   $z_{ratio} = \frac{\min(z_\Delta, d)}{d}$, where $d$ is `slice_distance` [S1]
*   $partial\_occl_n = volumetric\_occl_{n-1} \cdot (1 - z_{ratio}) + volumetric\_occl_n \cdot z_{ratio}$ [S1]

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Partial Occlusion Buffer Update:** The current slice $s_n$ is discarded by the depth test because it is occluded by the surface. Therefore, the previous slice $s_{n-1}$ must be rendered to update the buffer for the surface located between $s_{n-1}$ and $s_n$ [S1].
*   **Clamping:** The difference $z_\Delta$ between surface and slice distance is clamped by the slice distance $d$ to correctly handle cases where there is no surface between two subsequent slices [S1].
*   **Self-Occlusion:** Self-occlusion in the vicinity occlusion term is avoided by offsetting the depth value with a `depth_bias` [S1].
*   **Performance Bottleneck:** The performance bottleneck is the incremental filtering inherent to the DOS method, not the vicinity occlusion term computation. The vicinity occlusion term alone performs at interactive frame rates despite high sample counts [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance scaling may be limited by internal memory layout of anisotropic 3D textures rather than triangle count. For example, the mixing pipe dataset did not scale on the GTX 580, likely due to the texture layout [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Vicinity Occlusion](14394-vicinity-occlusion.md)
An approximate geometric occlusion term for high-frequency geometric details, determined by rendering a full screen quad and comparing the depth of each pixel with neighboring pixels in the geometric depth buffer.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The method is integrated into a slice-based volume renderer. The performance bottleneck is the incremental filtering inherent to the DOS method [S1]. The vicinity occlusion term computation is not the limiting factor [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 580 GPU, the combined shading method is reduced by about 25% compared to volumetric occlusion shading alone, 86% compared to emission/absorption, and 71% compared to combined Phong shading [S1].
    *   Mixing pipe (137k triangles, 574 slices, 1800x121x121 resolution): 45° DOS angle, 2x2 volumetric samples, 118 vicinity samples, 768x128 image resolution.
    *   DTI fibers (720k triangles, 512 slices, 96x96x81 resolution): 45° DOS angle, 4x4 volumetric samples, 81 vicinity samples, 1153x256 image resolution.
    *   Astrophysical (7.9M triangles, 961 slices, 256x512x512 resolution): 65° DOS angle, 2x2 volumetric samples, 81 vicinity samples, 512x512 image resolution [S1].
*   **Strengths and Limitations:**
    *   **Strengths:** Captures mutual occlusion between volume and geometry (volume-to-volume, geometry-to-geometry, geometry-to-volume, volume-to-geometry). Independent of volumetric gradient, avoiding artifacts in homogeneous or noisy regions where gradient estimation is problematic [S1].
    *   **Limitations:** Performance scaling may be limited by internal memory layout of anisotropic 3D textures [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Interactive direct volume rendering of combined volumetric and geometric primitives where depth perception and context between the two data types are important [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When performance is critical and the scene has complex anisotropic 3D textures that do not scale well on the target hardware [S1].

## Background
The method was presented by Schott et al. in 2012 [S1]. It extends the Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) model presented by Schott et al. [24] and is based on incremental filtering, which has been successfully used in the past to approximate integration for computing advanced volumetric scattering and shading effects [11], [18], [23], [24], [31] [S1]. The method is based on work presented previously [22] and encompasses additions including more algorithmic details and an effective stream tube interpolation scheme [S1].
