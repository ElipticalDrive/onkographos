# Combined Volumetric and Geometric Ambient Occlusion

Also called: Combined Occlusion Effects for Combined Volumes and Tubular Geometry, Combined Volumetric and Geometric Occlusion Shading, Combined surface and volumetric occlusion shading, DOS extension for tubes, DOS shading, Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) with Volumetric and Geometric Occlusion, Extended DOS Method

## Overview
This method computes ambient occlusion for visualizations combining volumetric data (scalar fields) and tube-shaped geometric primitives (e.g., streamlines, DTI fiber tracts) by considering mutual occlusion between the volume and the geometry [S1]. It extends the Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) model to integrate shadows cast by geometry into the volume, volume onto geometry, and geometry onto geometry, addressing the lack of depth discrimination in separate volumetric and geometric occlusion methods [S1]. Unlike naive methods that compute occlusion independently, this approach provides consistent shading across both data types, enhancing depth perception [S1]. It differs from Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO) [3980-screen-space-ambient-occlusion-ssao.md] by capturing low-frequency occlusion in a volumetric buffer rather than approximating it with spheres, and from [Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion for Volumes](16107-screen-space-ambient-occlusion-for-volumes.md) by explicitly handling the interaction between distinct geometric and volumetric entities [S1].

## How it works
The algorithm is integrated into a slice-based volume renderer. It uses incremental filtering to approximate integration for advanced volumetric scattering and shading effects [S1]. The method consists of preprocessing to create a G-Buffer, iterative slice traversal to update occlusion buffers, and a final compositing step.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Volume Data:** 3D scalar field $V$.
*   **Transfer Function:** Maps $(x, |\nabla x|)$ to scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$ and extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ [S1].
*   **Geometric Structures:** Tube-shaped primitives (e.g., streamlines) defined by triangles [S1].
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $d$: Slice distance (constant between slices) [S1].
    *   $\theta$: Opening angle of the DOS cone [S1].
    *   $R_t$: Vicinity occlusion extent radius [S1].
    *   $depth\_bias$: Offset to avoid self-occlusion in vicinity term [S1].
    *   $geometry\_opacity$: User-specified high opacity value for geometry [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   **Eye Buffer:** Final composited color and alpha.
*   **Occlusion Buffers:** Intermediate buffers for volumetric and partial occlusion.

### Data Structures
*   **G-Buffer:** Set of 2D images storing per-pixel: color, view space position, view space normal, and view space distance (depth) [S1].
*   **volume_occl_buf:** 2D buffer storing the volumetric occlusion factor. Double-buffered as `volume_occl_bufprev` and `volume_occl_bufnext` [S1].
*   **partial_occl_buffer:** 2D buffer storing shadows cast by the volume onto the geometry [S1].
*   **eye_buffer:** Final color buffer [S1].
*   **geom_depth:** 2D texture storing the depth of geometric structures [S1].
*   **geom_linear_depth:** 2D texture storing linearized depth of geometric structures [S1].
*   **Stencil Buffer:** Used to mask fragments covered by streamlines geometry to optimize partial occlusion buffer updates [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Preprocessing (ComputeGBuffer):**
    *   Render geometric structures to the G-Buffer to capture colors, positions, normals, and depth values [S1].
    *   Use the stencil buffer to mark pixels covered by geometry (set to 1) to optimize subsequent updates [S1].
    *   Initialize `volume_occl_bufprev` and `volume_occl_bufnext` to 1 (no occlusion) [S1].
    *   Initialize `partial_occl_buffer` to 1 [S1].
    *   Initialize `eye_buffer` to background color [S1].

2.  **Slice Traversal Loop:**
    For each slice $s$ in the volume:
    *   **UpdateEyeBuffer(s):** Render the current slice to the `eye_buffer`, incorporating volumetric occlusion and geometric depth [S1].
    *   **UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s):** Compute shadows cast by the volume onto the geometry using the previous slice's data [S1].
    *   **UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s):** Compute volumetric occlusion for the current slice, considering geometry as occluders [S1].

3.  **Compositing (CompositeBuffers):**
    *   Combine the `eye_buffer`, `partial_occl_buffer`, and G-Buffer data to shade the geometric surfaces with the final combined occlusion term [S1].

4.  **Vicinity Occlusion (Post-Processing):**
    *   Compute high-frequency geometric occlusion using the G-Buffer depth [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def CombinedOcclusionShading(volume, transfer_function, geometry, slice_distance, theta, R_t, depth_bias, geometry_opacity):
    # 1. Preprocessing
    g_buffer = ComputeGBuffer(geometry)
    eye_buffer = background_color
    volume_occl_bufprev = 1.0
    volume_occl_bufnext = 1.0
    partial_occl_buffer = 1.0
    
    # 2. Slice Traversal
    for slice s in all_slices:
        UpdateEyeBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, slice_distance)
        UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s, volume_occl_bufprev, volume_occl_bufnext, geometry, slice_distance)
        UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, geometry_opacity)
        
        # Swap buffers for next iteration
        volume_occl_bufprev = volume_occl_bufnext

    # 3. Compositing
    CompositeBuffers(eye_buffer, partial_occl_buffer, g_buffer)
    
    # 4. Vicinity Occlusion (Can be done in parallel or post-process)
    vicinity_occlusion = VicinityOcclusion(geometry.linear_depth, R_t, depth_bias)
    ApplyVicinityOcclusion(g_buffer, vicinity_occlusion)

def UpdateEyeBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, slice_distance):
    # Bind textures: volume_occl_bufprev, geom_linear_depth
    # Set render target: eye_buffer
    # Enable Depth Test: PassIfLess, DoNotWriteDepth
    # Enable Blending: OneMinusDestinationAlpha, One
    
    for fragment f of slice s:
        # Sample volume data
        x, grad_magnitude = Texture3D(volume, f.position)
        
        # Sample transfer function
        sigma_s, sigma_t = Texture2D(transfer_function, (x, grad_magnitude))
        
        # Sample previous volumetric occlusion
        occlusion = Texture2D(volume_occl_bufprev, f.clip_coords)
        
        # Calculate distance to nearest geometry
        geom_depth_val = Texture2D(geometry.linear_depth, f.clip_coords)
        delta = abs(f.view_z - geom_depth_val)
        
        # Clamp sampling distance to slice distance
        sampling_distance = min(slice_distance, delta)
        
        # Compute alpha
        alpha = 1.0 - exp(-sigma_t * sampling_distance)
        
        # Output color and alpha
        # La is ambient light, assumed constant or pre-calculated
        frame_buffer_color = La * sigma_s * occlusion * alpha
        frame_buffer_alpha = alpha
        
        WriteToRenderTarget(frame_buffer_color, frame_buffer_alpha)

def UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s, volume, transfer_function, volume_occl_bufprev, geometry, geometry_opacity):
    # Bind textures: volume_occl_bufprev, geom_depth
    # Set render target: volume_occl_bufnext
    # Disable Blending
    
    for fragment f of slice s:
        # Sample volume data
        x, grad_magnitude = Texture3D(volume, f.position)
        
        # Sample transfer function
        sigma_t = Texture2D(transfer_function, (x, grad_magnitude))
        
        # Check if behind geometry
        geom_depth_val = Texture2D(geometry.depth, f.clip_coords)
        
        if f.depth < geom_depth_val:
            sigma_t = geometry_opacity
            
        # Compute DOS occlusion
        # ComputeDOS performs incremental filtering based on sigma_t and previous buffer
        occlusion = ComputeDOS(volume_occl_bufprev, sigma_t)
        
        WriteToRenderTarget(occlusion)
        
    # Swap buffers
    volume_occl_bufprev = volume_occl_bufnext

def UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s, volume_occl_bufprev, volume_occl_bufnext, geometry, slice_distance):
    # Bind textures: volume_occl_bufprev, volume_occl_bufnext, geom_linear_depth
    # Set render target: partial_occl_buffer
    # Disable Blending
    # Enable Depth Test: PassIfLess, DoNotWriteDepth
    # Enable Stencil Test: StencilFunc(Equal, 1, ~0), StencilOp(Keep, Decrease, Keep)
    
    # Render previous slice s-1
    for fragment f of slice (s-1):
        z_surface = Texture2D(geometry.linear_depth, f.clip_coords)
        z_slice = f.view_z
        
        # Calculate difference
        z_delta = abs(z_slice - z_surface)
        
        # Clamp by slice distance
        z_ratio = min(z_delta, slice_distance) / slice_distance
        
        # Sample occlusion values
        occl_prev = Texture2D(volume_occl_bufprev, f.clip_coords)
        occl_next = Texture2D(volume_occl_bufnext, f.clip_coords)
        
        # Interpolate
        partial_occl = occl_prev * (1.0 - z_ratio) + occl_next * z_ratio
        
        WriteToRenderTarget(partial_occl)

def VicinityOcclusion(geom_linear_depth, R_t, depth_bias):
    occlusion_count = 0
    total_samples = 0
    
    # Render full screen quad
    for sample p_i within vicinity occlusion extent R_t:
        # Get surface depth at current pixel
        z_surface = Texture2D(geom_linear_depth, p_i)
        
        # Apply bias to avoid self-occlusion
        biased_depth = z_surface + depth_bias
        
        # Project sample into texture space
        t_i = project(p_i, z_surface)
        
        # Sample depth at neighbor
        depth_sample = Texture2D(geom_linear_depth, t_i)
        
        # Count occluded neighbors
        if depth_sample > 0 and depth_sample < biased_depth:
            occlusion_count += 1
            
        total_samples += 1
        
    # Return approximate occlusion term
    return 1.0 - (occlusion_count / total_samples)
```

### Formulas
*   **Sampling Distance:** $\text{sampling\_distance} = \min(\text{slice\_distance}, \delta)$, where $\delta = |f_{\text{viewz}} - \text{Texture2D}(\text{geom\_linear\_depth}, f_{\text{clip}})|$ [S1].
*   **Alpha:** $\alpha = 1 - e^{-\sigma_t \cdot \text{sampling\_distance}}$ [S1].
*   **Partial Occlusion Difference:** $z_\Delta = |z_{\text{slice}} - z_{\text{surface}}|$ [S1].
*   **Partial Occlusion Ratio:** $z_{\text{ratio}} = \frac{\min(z_\Delta, d)}{d}$, where $d$ is `slice_distance` [S1].
*   **Partial Occlusion Value:** $\text{partial\_occl}_n = \text{volumetric\_occl}_{n-1} \cdot (1 - z_{\text{ratio}}) + \text{volumetric\_occl}_n \cdot z_{\text{ratio}}$ [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Slice Discarding:** When computing the partial occlusion buffer, the current slice $s_n$ is discarded by the depth test because it is occluded by the surface. Therefore, the previous slice $s_{n-1}$ must be rendered to update the buffer for the surface located between $s_{n-1}$ and $s_n$ [S1].
*   **Clamping:** The difference $z_\Delta$ between surface and slice distance is clamped by the slice distance $d$ to correctly handle cases where there is no surface between two subsequent slices [S1].
*   **Self-Occlusion:** Self-occlusion in the vicinity occlusion term is avoided by offsetting the depth value with a `depth_bias` [S1].
*   **Performance Bottleneck:** The performance bottleneck is the incremental filtering inherent to the DOS method, not the vicinity occlusion term computation [S1].
*   **Texture Layout:** Performance scaling may be limited by the internal memory layout of anisotropic 3D textures rather than triangle count [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Vicinity Occlusion](14394-vicinity-occlusion.md)
Vicinity occlusion is an approximate geometric ambient occlusion term designed to capture high-frequency surface-to-surface occlusion effects on tubular geometric structures (such as DTI fiber tracts) within a volume rendering pipeline. It addresses the lack of depth discrimination in separate volumetric and geometric occlusion methods by integrating shadows cast by geometry onto itself, thereby enhancing depth perception. Unlike standard Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO) techniques that cover the entire screen, this method is computed only for pixels containing geometric structures, leveraging the sparsity of the geometry to minimize performance impact.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method is based on incremental filtering, which is efficient for slice-based rendering. The vicinity occlusion term performs at interactive frame rates despite high sample counts [S1]. The bottleneck is the DOS incremental filtering, not the vicinity term [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for G-Buffer (color, position, normal, depth), double-buffered volumetric occlusion buffer, partial occlusion buffer, and depth textures [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 580 GPU, the combined shading method is reduced by about 25% compared to volumetric occlusion shading alone, 86% compared to emission/absorption, and 71% compared to combined Phong shading [S1].
    *   Mixing pipe (137k triangles, 1800x121x121 volume): Interactive rates maintained [S1].
    *   DTI fibers (720k triangles, 96x96x81 volume): Interactive rates maintained [S1].
    *   Astrophysical (7.9M triangles, 256x512x512 volume): Renders at half speed compared to DTI, but still interactive [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Captures mutual occlusion between volume and geometry (volume-to-volume, geometry-to-geometry, geometry-to-volume, volume-to-geometry) [S1].
    *   Independent of volumetric gradient estimation, avoiding artifacts in homogeneous or noisy regions [S1].
    *   High-frequency geometric occlusion is captured efficiently via vicinity occlusion [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Performance may be limited by 3D texture memory layout (anisotropic textures) [S1].
    *   Requires pre-computation of G-Buffer and stencil masks [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Interactive visualization of combined volumetric and tubular geometric data (e.g., DTI fiber tracts in brain imaging, streamlines in fluid dynamics) where depth perception and mutual occlusion are critical [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes without geometric primitives, or where gradient-based shading is preferred and data is not noisy/homogeneous [S1].

## Background
The method extends the Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) model presented by Schott et al. [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of naive methods that compute volumetric and geometric occlusion independently, which miss interactions such as shadows cast from geometry into volume and volume onto geometry [S1]. The approach builds on incremental filtering techniques used for advanced volumetric scattering and shading [S1]. It is particularly relevant for scientific visualization involving DTI fiber tractography or streamline tracing [S1].
