# Combined Volumetric and Geometric Ambient Occlusion

Also called: Combined Occlusion Effects for Combined Volumes and Tubular Geometry, Combined Volumetric and Geometric Occlusion Shading, Combined surface and volumetric occlusion shading, DOS extension for tubes, DOS shading, Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) with Volumetric and Geometric Occlusion, Extended DOS Method

## Overview
This method computes ambient occlusion for interactive direct volume rendering that combines scalar volumetric data with tube-shaped geometric primitives (e.g., streamlines, DTI fiber tracts) [S1]. It solves the problem of depth perception loss in mixed media by calculating mutual occlusion between the volume and geometry, including volume-to-volume, geometry-to-geometry, geometry-to-volume, and volume-to-geometry interactions [S1]. Unlike naive approaches that compute these effects independently, this method integrates shadows cast by geometry into the volume and volume onto the geometry, using Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) for low-frequency effects and a screen-space vicinity term for high-frequency geometric details [S1].

## How it works
The algorithm extends the Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) model [S1] to support geometric primitives within a slice-based volume rendering pipeline [S1]. It relies on incremental filtering to approximate integration for shading [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   3D scalar volume data $V$ [S1].
*   Geometric primitives (tubes/streams) defined by vertices [S1].
*   Transfer function $TF(x, |\nabla x|) \rightarrow (\sigma_s, \sigma_t)$ mapping value and gradient magnitude to scattering and extinction coefficients [S1].
*   Slice plane parameters for volume traversal [S1].
*   Parameters: $geometry\_opacity$ (high opacity value for geometric occlusion), $depth\_bias$ (offset for vicinity occlusion), $R_t$ (vicinity occlusion extent radius), $\theta$ (DOS cone opening angle) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Final color buffer ($eye\_buffer$) containing the composited volume, geometry, and combined occlusion [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **G-Buffer:** A set of 2D images storing per-pixel data for geometric structures: color, view-space position, view-space normal, and view-space depth ($geom\_linear\_depth$) [S1].
*   **volume\_occl\_buf:** A 2D buffer storing the volumetric occlusion factor. It is double-buffered as $volume\_occl\_bufprev$ and $volume\_occl\_bufnext$ [S1].
*   **partial\_occl\_buffer:** A 2D buffer storing the shadows cast by the volume onto the geometry [S1].
*   **eye\_buffer:** The final color buffer [S1].
*   **Stencil Buffer:** Used to mask fragments covered by streamlines geometry to optimize partial occlusion buffer updates [S1].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Preprocessing (G-Buffer):** Render geometric structures to populate the G-Buffer [S1]. Store colors, positions, normals, and depth [S1]. Use the stencil buffer to mark pixels covered by geometry [S1].
2.  **Initialization:** Set $eye\_buffer$ to background color [S1]. Initialize $volume\_occl\_bufprev$ and $volume\_occl\_bufnext$ to 1 [S1]. Initialize $partial\_occl\_buffer$ to 1 [S1].
3.  **Slice Traversal:** For each slice $s$ in the volume:
    a.  Call `UpdateEyeBuffer(s)` [S1].
    b.  Call `UpdatePartialOcclusionBuffer(s)` [S1].
    c.  Call `UpdateVolumetricOcclusionBuffer(s)` [S1].
4.  **Compositing:** Call `CompositeBuffers()` to combine the final occlusion terms with the G-Buffer and render the geometry [S1].
5.  **Vicinity Occlusion:** Compute the high-frequency geometric occlusion term using `VicinityOcclusion(geom_linear_depth)` [S1].

### Formulas
*   **Distance to Geometry:** $\Delta z = |z_{slice} - z_{surface}|$ [S1].
*   **Interpolation Ratio:** $z_{ratio} = \frac{\min(\Delta z, d)}{d}$, where $d$ is $slice\_distance$ [S1].
*   **Partial Occlusion:** $partial\_occl_n = volumetric\_occl_{n-1} \cdot (1 - z_{ratio}) + volumetric\_occl_n \cdot z_{ratio}$ [S1].
*   **Vicinity Occlusion:** $O_{vic} = 1 - \frac{N_{occluded}}{N_{total}}$, where $N_{occluded}$ is the count of neighbors with depth $< z_{surface} + depth\_bias$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   $geometry\_opacity$: User-specified high opacity value replacing transfer function opacity when a volume sample is behind geometry [S1].
*   $depth\_bias$: Offset added to surface depth to avoid self-occlusion in vicinity term [S1].
*   $R_t$: Vicinity occlusion extent radius [S1].
*   $\theta$: DOS cone opening angle (e.g., $45^\circ$ or $65^\circ$) [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Partial Occlusion Buffer Update:** The current slice $s_n$ is discarded by the depth test because it is occluded by the surface. Therefore, the previous slice $s_{n-1}$ must be rendered to update the buffer for the surface located between $s_{n-1}$ and $s_n$ [S1].
*   **Clamping:** $\Delta z$ is clamped by $slice\_distance$ to handle cases where no surface exists between two subsequent slices [S1].
*   **Self-Occlusion:** Avoided in vicinity occlusion by applying $depth\_bias$ [S1].
*   **Performance Bottleneck:** The bottleneck is the incremental filtering of DOS, not the vicinity term. The vicinity term performs at interactive rates despite high sample counts [S1].
*   **Texture Layout:** Performance scaling may be limited by the internal memory layout of anisotropic 3D textures rather than triangle count [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Vicinity Occlusion](14394-vicinity-occlusion.md)
An approximate geometric ambient occlusion term for tubular structures, capturing high-frequency surface-to-surface occlusion. It is chosen when depth discrimination of geometric structures is required within a volume rendering pipeline, leveraging sparsity to minimize performance impact.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the incremental filtering of the DOS method [S1]. The vicinity occlusion term is efficient despite high sample counts [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for G-Buffer, double-buffered volumetric occlusion buffer, and partial occlusion buffer [S1].
*   **Performance:** On an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 580, the method is ~25% slower than volumetric occlusion shading alone, ~86% slower than emission/absorption, and ~71% slower than combined Phong shading [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Captures mutual occlusion (volume-to-geometry and geometry-to-volume) which naive methods miss. Independent of volumetric gradient, avoiding artifacts in homogeneous/noisy regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance may degrade with anisotropic 3D texture layouts. The method is designed for tube-like geometry; general complex geometry may require different handling [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Interactive visualization of mixed volumetric and streamline/tractography data where depth perception is critical [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with no geometric primitives, or where gradient-based shading is preferred and data is clean [S1].

## Background
The method extends the Directional Occlusion Shading (DOS) model [S1]. It builds on incremental filtering techniques used for volumetric scattering [S1]. It addresses limitations in screen-space ambient occlusion methods that approximate low-frequency occlusion with spheres or use summed area tables for global occlusion [S1].
