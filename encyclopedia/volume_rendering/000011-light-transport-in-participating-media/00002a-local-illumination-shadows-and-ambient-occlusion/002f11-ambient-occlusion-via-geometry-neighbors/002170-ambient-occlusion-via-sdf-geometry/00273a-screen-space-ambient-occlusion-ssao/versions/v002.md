# Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO)
Also called: Crytek's Screen-Space AO, SSAO (Original), Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO) Pass

## Overview
Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO) is a post-processing technique that estimates ambient occlusion by sampling points in a hemisphere around each pixel's surface position and checking if those points are occluded by geometry in the depth buffer [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It is the first screen-space method for ambient occlusion, using only the Z-Buffer to compute AO at full resolution [S2, unofficial]. Unlike SDF-based methods which rely on global geometric representations, SSAO relies exclusively on screen-space data (depth and normals), meaning its performance cost is independent of scene complexity and depends only on output resolution [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   `hitPosTex`: Texture containing the world-space position of the fragment hit by the primary ray [S1, unofficial].
    *   `hitNormTex`: Texture containing the world-space normal of the fragment [S1, unofficial].
    *   `depthTex`: Texture containing the depth buffer information and cell size parameters [S1, unofficial].
    *   `noiseTex`: Texture containing random vectors used for tangent space generation to break up rotational symmetry [S1, unofficial].
    *   `projMat`: Projection matrix [S1, unofficial].
    *   `viewMat`: View matrix [S1, unofficial].
    *   `radius`: The radius of the hemisphere used for sampling, determining the scale of occlusion [S1, unofficial].
    *   `NUM_SAMPLES`: The number of random samples taken per pixel for the occlusion estimation [S1, unofficial].
    *   `camPos`: Camera position in world space [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:**
    *   `outVisibility`: A float texture at location 0, storing the final AO factor (1.0 = fully lit, 0.0 = fully occluded) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **TBN Matrix:** A 3x3 matrix constructed from the tangent, bitangent, and normal vectors, used to rotate sample vectors from tangent space to world space [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sample Vectors:** A pre-generated array of `NUM_SAMPLES` random vectors in tangent space, typically distributed on a hemisphere or sphere [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Fetch Surface Data:** For the current fragment coordinate, fetch the hit position and normal from their respective textures [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p} = \text{texelFetch}(\text{hitPosTex}, \text{coord}, 0).xyz $$
    $$ \mathbf{n} = \text{texelFetch}(\text{hitNormTex}, \text{coord}, 0).xyz $$
2.  **Discard Background:** If the hit position is zero (indicating no geometry was hit), discard the fragment [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Offset Position:** Offset the hit position slightly along the normal to avoid self-intersection or z-fighting, using depth cell size [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{offset} = \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{n} \times \frac{\text{hitDepth\_cellSize}.y}{5.0} $$
4.  **Construct TBN Matrix:** Generate a tangent vector using a random vector from `noiseTex` and the normal, then compute the bitangent to form the TBN matrix [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{t} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{n} \times (\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{n})) $$
    $$ \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{n} \times \mathbf{t} $$
    $$ \text{TBN} = [\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{n}] $$
    where $\mathbf{r}$ is the random vector fetched from `noiseTex` [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Sample Hemisphere:** Loop through `NUM_SAMPLES` [S1, unofficial]. For each sample index $i$:
    a. Retrieve the sample vector $\mathbf{s}_{tan}$ from the pre-generated sample array [S1, unofficial].
    b. Transform $\mathbf{s}_{tan}$ into world space using the TBN matrix and scale by `radius` [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{s}_{world} = \text{TBN} \times \mathbf{s}_{tan} \times \text{radius} $$
    c. Compute the sample position in world space [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{samp} = \mathbf{p}_{offset} + \mathbf{s}_{world} $$
6.  **Project to Screen Space:** Project the sample position $\mathbf{p}_{samp}$ into clip space and then to normalized device coordinates (NDC) [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{v}_{clip} = \text{projMat} \times \text{viewMat} \times [\mathbf{p}_{samp}, 1.0]^T $$
    $$ \mathbf{v}_{ndc} = \frac{\mathbf{v}_{clip}.xyz}{\mathbf{v}_{clip}.w} $$
    $$ \mathbf{v}_{screen} = \mathbf{v}_{ndc} \times 0.5 + 0.5 $$
7.  **Check Screen Bounds:** Verify if the projected sample lies within the screen bounds $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Accumulate Occlusion:** If within bounds, perform a range check and depth comparison [S1, unofficial].
    a. Fetch the depth value at the projected screen coordinate [S1, unofficial].
    b. Check if the sample is within the radius range [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \text{rangeCheck} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{if } |\text{hitDepth\_cellSize}.x - d_{samp}| < \text{radius} \\ 0.0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    c. Check if the sample is occluded (closer to the camera than the hit point) [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \text{isOccluded} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{if } d_{samp} < \text{length}(\mathbf{p}_{cam} - \mathbf{p}_{samp}) \\ 0.0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    d. Add to occlusion accumulator [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \text{occlusion} += \text{isOccluded} \times \text{rangeCheck} $$
9.  **Calculate Final Visibility:** Average the occlusion count [S1, unofficial].
    $$ AO = \begin{cases} 1.0 - \frac{\text{occlusion}}{\text{count}} & \text{if } \text{count} > 0 \\ 1.0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where `count` is the number of valid samples within the radius [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# SSAO Fragment Shader Pseudocode
# Inputs: hitPosTex, hitNormTex, depthTex, noiseTex, projMat, viewMat, radius, NUM_SAMPLES, camPos
# Output: outVisibility

def texel_fetch(tex, coord):
    return tex[coord]  # Simplified texture fetch

def main():
    coord = (gl_FragCoord.x / resolution.x, gl_FragCoord.y / resolution.y)
    hitPos = texel_fetch(hitPosTex, coord)
    hitNorm = texel_fetch(hitNormTex, coord)

    if hitPos == vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0):
        return  # discard

    cellSizeY = texel_fetch(depthTex, coord).y
    hitPos = hitPos + hitNorm * (cellSizeY / 5.0)

    randVec = texel_fetch(noiseTex, coord)
    tangent = normalize(randVec - hitNorm * dot(randVec, hitNorm))
    bitangent = cross(hitNorm, tangent)
    tbn = mat3(tangent, bitangent, hitNorm)

    occlusion = 0.0
    count = 0

    for i in range(NUM_SAMPLES):
        samp = tbn @ samples[i]
        samp = hitPos + samp * radius

        offset = vec4(samp, 1.0)
        offset = projMat @ viewMat @ offset
        offset = vec4(offset.x / offset.w, offset.y / offset.w, offset.z / offset.w, 1.0)
        offset = offset * 0.5 + 0.5

        if all(lessThanEqual(offset.xy, vec2(1.0))) and all(greaterThanEqual(offset.xy, vec2(0.0))):
            screenCoord = offset.xy * resolution
            sampleDepth = texel_fetch(depthTex, screenCoord).x
            hitDepth_cell_size_x = texel_fetch(depthTex, coord).x

            rangeCheck = 1.0 if abs(hitDepth_cell_size_x - sampleDepth) < radius else 0.0
            isOccluded = 1.0 if sampleDepth < length(camPos - samp) else 0.0

            occlusion += isOccluded * rangeCheck
            count += 1

    outVisibility = 1.0 - occlusion / count if count > 0 else 1.0
    return outVisibility
```

**Parameters:**
*   `NUM_SAMPLES`: The number of random samples taken per pixel for the occlusion estimation [S1, unofficial].
*   `radius`: The radius of the hemisphere used for sampling, determining the scale of occlusion [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Halo Effect:** Because the method is not cosine-weighted, it accounts for occlusion that should not be accounted for, leading to blackened edges around objects [S2, unofficial].
*   **Self-Intersection:** Without proper offsetting, samples may intersect the surface they are sampling from, causing incorrect occlusion values [S1, unofficial].
*   **Screen Edges:** Samples near the edge of the screen may project outside the visible area, requiring bounds checking [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot R)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $R$ is the number of samples per pixel. The cost is independent of scene geometry complexity [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing the input textures (position, normal, depth) and output texture.
*   **Performance:** Performance depends only on output resolution, not scene complexity [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time performance on modern hardware; no need for complex geometric data structures like SDFs or BVHs [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to visible geometry (screen-space); suffers from the halo effect due to lack of cosine weighting [S2, unofficial]; artifacts at screen edges and near depth discontinuities.

## Background
SSAO was introduced by Crytek as the first screen-space method for ambient occlusion, utilizing only the Z-Buffer to compute AO at full resolution [S2, unofficial]. It was later expanded upon by Loos and Sloan, who formulated it as a Monte Carlo integration and named it Volumetric Obscurance [S2, unofficial].
