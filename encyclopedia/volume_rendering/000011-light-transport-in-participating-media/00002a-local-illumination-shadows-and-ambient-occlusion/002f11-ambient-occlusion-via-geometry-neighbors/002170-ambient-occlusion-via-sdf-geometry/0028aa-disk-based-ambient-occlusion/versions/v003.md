# Disk-Based Ambient Occlusion

Also called: Bunnel's Disk-Based AO, Disk Approximation AO

## Overview
Disk-Based Ambient Occlusion approximates surface geometry as a collection of disk-shaped elements at mesh vertices to compute ambient occlusion (AO) and bent normals analytically, avoiding the cost of ray casting [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like SDF-based AO by using explicit geometric primitives (disks) rather than implicit distance fields, allowing for direct analytical occlusion calculation between primitives [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of mesh vertices, each associated with a normal vector $\mathbf{n}_i$ and a disk radius $r_i$.
**Outputs:** An ambient occlusion factor $AO_i \in [0, 1]$ and optionally a bent normal $\mathbf{b}_i$ for each vertex.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Disk List:** An array of $N$ disks. Each disk $i$ is defined by:
    *   Center position $\mathbf{c}_i$ (vertex position).
    *   Normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ (vertex normal).
    *   Radius $r_i$ (parameter, typically derived from local geometry or user-defined).
    *   Current occlusion factor $O_i$ (initialized to 1.0).

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    For each disk $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
    *   Set $O_i = 1.0$.
    *   Set accumulated occlusion $Acc_i = 0.0$.
    *   Set accumulated bent normal $\mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{0}$.

2.  **Pairwise Occlusion Calculation (Pass 1):**
    For each disk $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
        For each disk $j$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
            If $i \neq j$:
                Calculate the occlusion factor $f_{ij}$ that disk $j$ casts on disk $i$.
                *Note: The specific analytical formula for two disks is not provided in the sources [G: Typically involves the form factor between two parallel or oriented disks, often derived from solid angle integration or geometric projection.]*
                $Acc_i = Acc_i + f_{ij}$
                $\mathbf{B}_i = \mathbf{B}_i + f_{ij} \cdot \mathbf{n}_j$ (Contribution to bent normal)

3.  **Double Shadowing Correction (Pass 2):**
    For each disk $i$ from $0$ to $N-1$:
        *   The raw sum $Acc_i$ suffers from "double shadowing," where disks behind other occluders are counted multiple times, leading to overly dark results [S1, unofficial].
        *   Reduce the contribution of disk $i$ by its own occlusion factor from Pass 1.
        *   $AO_i = 1.0 - \frac{Acc_i}{N}$ (Normalization may vary; source implies reduction by occlusion factor)
        *   *Correction Logic:* The source states "each disk's occlusion contribution is reduced by its occlusion factor from the previous pass." A robust implementation might use:
            $AO_i = 1.0 - \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{f_{ij}}{O_j}$
            Where $O_j$ is the occlusion factor of the occluding disk $j$. If $O_j$ is not explicitly tracked as a separate state, the source implies a sequential reduction or iterative refinement. Given the text "Pass 1: simple sum... Pass 2: reduce... by its occlusion factor," a direct interpretation is:
            $AO_i = 1.0 - \sum_{j \neq i} f_{ij} \cdot (1.0 - O_j)$
            However, without explicit formula for $O_j$'s update rule in the text, we note the mechanism: the final AO is derived by dampening the raw sum based on the occlusion status of the occluders [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Bent Normal Calculation:**
    $\mathbf{b}_i = \frac{\mathbf{B}_i}{\|\mathbf{B}_i\|}$ (if $\|\mathbf{B}_i\| > \epsilon$)
    $\mathbf{b}_i = \mathbf{n}_i$ (if $\|\mathbf{B}_i\| \le \epsilon$)

**Parameters:**
*   **Radius $r_i$:** Not given in the sources. [G: Typically set to a fraction of the local triangle edge length or a fixed world-space value.]
*   **Hierarchical Tree:** To optimize, a spatial hierarchy (e.g., BVH or Octree) is built over the disks to cull distant pairs, reducing complexity [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Double Shadowing:** Summing all occlusion factors directly leads to incorrect, overly dark images because occluders behind other occluders are counted redundantly [S1, unofficial].
*   **Self-Intersection:** As with all geometry-based AO, a small bias or offset along the normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ is required to prevent the disk from intersecting its own surface or nearby geometry due to floating-point precision errors [Parent, S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Complexity:** Naive implementation is $O(N^2)$. Hierarchical optimization reduces this to $O(N \log N)$ [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Analytic Polygon Occlusion
Calculates the ambient occlusion of an infinitesimal patch $X$ with normal $\hat{n}$ by a single polygon $P$ with vertices $\{\vec{p}_0, \dots, \vec{p}_{k-1}\}$ lying in the positive half-plane $\vec{p} \cdot \hat{n} \ge 0$. It treats AO as the form factor for diffuse radiative transfer between $P$ and $X$ [S2, unofficial].

**Formula:**
$$AO_P(\hat{n}) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \cos^{-1} \left( \frac{\vec{p}_i \cdot \vec{p}_j}{\|\vec{p}_i\| \|\vec{p}_j\|} \right) \hat{n} \cdot \frac{\vec{p}_i \times \vec{p}_j}{\|\vec{p}_i \times \vec{p}_j\|} \quad (4)$$
where $j = (i+1) \mod k$ [S2, unofficial].

**Implementation Cost:** 1 arccosine, 15 multiply-add, and 2 reciprocal square root operations per edge [S2, unofficial].

**When to choose:** When high-quality, analytical occlusion from specific polygonal geometry is required without sampling noise, and the geometry is static or preprocessed. It serves as the analytical kernel for the disk approximation when disks are replaced by or approximated by polygons [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N^2)$ for naive pairwise computation; $O(N \log N)$ with hierarchical tree optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store disk parameters and occlusion factors [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Analytical calculation removes sampling noise; capable of producing convincing results for dynamic geometry where precomputation is not feasible [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** High computational cost even with optimization compared to screen-space or SDF methods; sensitive to disk radius selection; double shadowing requires careful handling [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time applications with dynamic geometry where ray tracing is too expensive and screen-space methods fail (e.g., off-screen objects), and SDFs are not available or suitable [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Static scenes (use precomputed AO); scenes with very high polygon counts where $N \log N$ is still too slow; when visual fidelity of sharp shadows is required (AO is diffuse) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was presented by Bunnel, who modeled surfaces as collections of disk-shaped elements at mesh vertices to approximate ambient occlusion and bent normals analytically [S1, unofficial]. The approach was improved in image quality by Hoberock, though at a performance cost [S1, unofficial]. The underlying analytical solution for polygon occlusion was first introduced to graphics by Baum et al. in the context of the radiosity algorithm [S2, unofficial].
