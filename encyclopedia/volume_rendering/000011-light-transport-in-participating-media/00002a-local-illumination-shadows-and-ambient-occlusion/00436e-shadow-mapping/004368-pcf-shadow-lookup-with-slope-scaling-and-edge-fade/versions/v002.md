# PCF Shadow Lookup with Slope Scaling and Edge Fade

Also called: PCF with Slope Scaling and Edge Fade

## Overview
This method computes sun shadow occlusion for volume rendering by sampling a shadow map with a dynamic Percentage-Closer Filtering (PCF) kernel, applying a slope-scaled depth bias to prevent peter-panning, and using distance-based edge fading to soften shadow boundaries [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard shadow mapping by offsetting the sample position along the surface normal rather than the light direction, ensuring contact points remain stable, and by explicitly fading occlusion at the edges of the shadow map slab to avoid hard black walls [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `world_pos`: The 3D world position of the point being shaded.
*   `normal`: The surface normal at `world_pos`, normalized.
*   `light_dir`: The direction from the point to the light source (normalized).
*   `scene.light_view_proj`: The 4x4 view-projection matrix transforming world coordinates to light clip space.
*   `scene.shadow_params`: A uniform vector containing:
    *   `x`: Texel size in UV space.
    *   `y`: Base normal offset magnitude.
    *   `z`: Shadow strength multiplier (0 disables shadows).
    *   `w`: Kernel radius in texels (dynamically adjusted).
*   `shadow_map`: A `sampler2DShadow` comparison sampler [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `occlusion`: A float in $[0, 1]$ representing the shadow factor. 0.0 is fully lit, 1.0 is fully shadowed.

**Data Structures:**
*   `shadow_map`: A 2D depth texture accessed via `texture(shadow_map, vec3(u, v, depth))`. The comparison sampler returns 1.0 if the stored depth is less than or equal to the provided depth, and 0.0 otherwise. In PCF, this is sampled multiple times and averaged.

**Parameters:**
*   `SHADOW_PCF_MAX_RADIUS`: Integer constant, typically 4. Limits the kernel half-width to a maximum of 9x9 samples [S1, unofficial].
*   `SHADOW_SLOPE_OFFSET`: Float constant, typically 2.0. Controls the magnitude of the slope-scaled bias [S1, unofficial].
*   `SHADOW_FADE_START`: Float constant, typically 0.85. The fraction of the map half-width where edge fading begins [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Normal-Light Dot Product:**
    Calculate the alignment between the surface normal and the light direction.
    $$ n\_dot\_l = \text{clamp}(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}, 0.0, 1.0) $$

2.  **Slope-Scaled Offset:**
    Compute a normal offset to prevent peter-panning. The offset magnitude increases as the surface becomes more perpendicular to the light (lower $n\_dot\_l$).
    $$ \text{offset} = \text{scene.shadow\_params.y} \times (1.0 + \text{SHADOW\_SLOPE\_OFFSET} \times (1.0 - n\_dot\_l)) $$
    *Note: The offset is applied along the surface normal, not the light direction, to keep contact points fixed [S1, unofficial].*

3.  **Transform to Light Clip Space:**
    Apply the offset to the world position and transform to light clip space.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{offset} = \mathbf{world\_pos} + \mathbf{n} \times \text{offset} $$
    $$ \mathbf{light\_clip} = \text{scene.light\_view\_proj} \times \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_{offset} \\ 1.0 \end{bmatrix} $$

4.  **Convert to NDC:**
    Perform perspective division.
    $$ \mathbf{ndc} = \frac{\mathbf{light\_clip.xyz}}{\mathbf{light\_clip.w}} $$
    **Edge Case:** If $\mathbf{ndc}.z \le 0.0$ or $\mathbf{ndc}.z \ge 1.0$, the point is outside the light's view frustum. Return 0.0 (fully lit) [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Compute UV Coordinates:**
    Map NDC to texture UV space $[0, 1]$.
    $$ \mathbf{uv} = \mathbf{ndc.xy} \times 0.5 + 0.5 $$
    **Edge Case:** If $\mathbf{uv}.x < 0.0$, $\mathbf{uv}.x > 1.0$, $\mathbf{uv}.y < 0.0$, or $\mathbf{uv}.y > 1.0$, the point is outside the shadow map. Return 0.0 (fully lit) to avoid black walls at volume edges [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Initialize PCF Kernel:**
    Get texel size and clamp the radius.
    $$ \text{texel} = \text{scene.shadow\_params.x} $$
    $$ \text{radius} = \text{clamp}(\lfloor \text{scene.shadow\_params.w} + 0.5 \rfloor, 0, \text{SHADOW\_PCF\_MAX\_RADIUS}) $$

7.  **Accumulate Occlusion:**
    Sample the shadow map in a square kernel centered at `uv`.
    $$ \text{occlusion} = 0.0 $$
    For $y$ from $-\text{radius}$ to $\text{radius}$:
        For $x$ from $-\text{radius}$ to $\text{radius}$:
            $$ \mathbf{tap} = \mathbf{uv} + \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \times \text{texel} $$
            $$ \text{sample} = \text{texture}(\text{shadow\_map}, \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{tap} \\ \mathbf{ndc}.z \end{bmatrix}) $$
            $$ \text{occlusion} += 1.0 - \text{sample} $$
    *Note: The comparison sampler returns 1.0 if the depth is occluded. We subtract from 1.0 to get "lit" contribution, then sum "occluded" contribution.*

8.  **Average Occlusion:**
    $$ \text{occlusion} = \frac{\text{occlusion}}{(2 \times \text{radius} + 1)^2} $$

9.  **Edge Fade:**
    Compute distance from the center of the shadow map to fade out shadows at the edges.
    $$ \text{edge} = \max(|\mathbf{uv}.x - 0.5|, |\mathbf{uv}.y - 0.5|) \times 2.0 $$
    $$ \text{fade} = 1.0 - \text{smoothstep}(\text{SHADOW\_FADE\_START}, 1.0, \text{edge}) $$

10. **Final Result:**
    $$ \text{result} = \text{occlusion} \times \text{fade} \times \text{scene.shadow\_params.z} $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def pcf_shadow_lookup(world_pos, normal, light_dir, scene, shadow_map):
    # 1. Normal-Light Dot Product
    n_dot_l = max(dot(normal, light_dir), 0.0)
    
    # 2. Slope-Scaled Offset
    offset = scene.shadow_params.y * (1.0 + SHADOW_SLOPE_OFFSET * (1.0 - n_dot_l))
    
    # 3. Transform to Light Clip Space
    p_offset = world_pos + normal * offset
    light_clip = scene.light_view_proj * vec4(p_offset, 1.0)
    
    # 4. Convert to NDC
    ndc = light_clip.xyz / light_clip.w
    
    # Edge case: Outside light frustum
    if ndc.z <= 0.0 or ndc.z >= 1.0:
        return 0.0
    
    # 5. Compute UV Coordinates
    uv = ndc.xy * 0.5 + 0.5
    
    # Edge case: Outside shadow map
    if uv.x < 0.0 or uv.x > 1.0 or uv.y < 0.0 or uv.y > 1.0:
        return 0.0
    
    # 6. Initialize PCF Kernel
    texel = scene.shadow_params.x
    radius = clamp(int(scene.shadow_params.w + 0.5), 0, SHADOW_PCF_MAX_RADIUS)
    
    occlusion = 0.0
    kernel_size = 2 * radius + 1
    
    # 7. Accumulate Occlusion
    for y in range(-radius, radius + 1):
        for x in range(-radius, radius + 1):
            tap = uv + vec2(x, y) * texel
            # Comparison sampler returns 1.0 if depth <= ndc.z, else 0.0
            sample = texture(shadow_map, vec3(tap, ndc.z))
            occlusion += 1.0 - sample
            
    # 8. Average Occlusion
    occlusion /= float(kernel_size * kernel_size)
    
    # 9. Edge Fade
    edge = max(abs(uv.x - 0.5), abs(uv.y - 0.5)) * 2.0
    fade = 1.0 - smoothstep(SHADOW_FADE_START, 1.0, edge)
    
    # 10. Final Result
    return occlusion * fade * scene.shadow_params.z
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(R^2)$ per pixel, where $R$ is the PCF radius. With $R \le 4$, this is a constant 81 samples maximum.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory per pixel, plus the shadow map texture.
*   **Strengths:** The slope-scaled offset effectively reduces peter-panning artifacts without requiring complex depth bias calculations. The edge fade prevents harsh discontinuities at the boundaries of the shadow map, which is particularly useful for large volumes or distant lights.
*   **Limitations:** The method relies on a pre-computed shadow map, which may not capture dynamic self-shadowing within the volume accurately if the volume geometry changes frequently. The linear interpolation in PCF can cause soft shadows that may not match the physical attenuation of participating media.
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for static or slowly changing volumes where performance is critical and soft shadows are desired. Not ideal for highly dynamic scenes requiring precise self-shadowing.

## Background
This method is derived from techniques used in real-time rendering for terrain and voxel-based environments. It combines standard PCF shadow mapping with specific adjustments for normal-based biasing and edge handling, as seen in implementations like `VoxelPhase` [S1, unofficial]. The approach addresses common artifacts in volume shadowing, such as peter-panning and hard edges, by leveraging normal information and spatial fading.
