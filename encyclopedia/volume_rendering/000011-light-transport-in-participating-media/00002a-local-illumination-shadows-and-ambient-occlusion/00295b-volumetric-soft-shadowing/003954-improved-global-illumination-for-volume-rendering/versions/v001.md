# Improved Global Illumination for Volume Rendering

Also called: Improved light model, Soft shadow global illumination

## Overview
A global illumination technique that simulates light transmission as continuous slices to produce realistic global illumination and real-time interactivity, but modifies the base method to generate soft shadows instead of hard shadows, thereby preserving local details [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [Volumetric Soft Shadowing](4507-volumetric-soft-shadowing.md) by operating in volume space using a light volume generated slice-by-slice, and from [ZM13](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12188) by avoiding back-lighting which fails with high-opacity data and lacks directional depth perception [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Volume dataset (density, opacity, color).
- Transfer function (for visualization mapping).
- Light source position (user-adjustable "Key Light").
- Light volume from [RDRS10] (simulating light transmission as continuous slices) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Final rendered image with soft shadows and enhanced global illumination.

**Data Structures:**
- **Light Volume:** A 3D texture or volume grid storing light intensity values. Generated by [RDRS10], it records light transmission as continuous slices. The exact layout is not specified beyond being a volume space representation [S1, unofficial].
- **Volume Data:** Standard 3D grid of voxels with density, opacity, and color attributes.

**Steps:**
1. **Generate Light Volume:** Use the method from [RDRS10] to simulate light transmission as continuous slices and record the result in a light volume [S1, unofficial].
2. **Combine Global Illumination:** Integrate the light volume with the volume rendering process. Do not separate global illumination into indirect lighting and direct lighting, as this separation produces harder shadows [S1, unofficial].
3. **Apply Blurring for Chromaticity:** Use blurring operations on the light volume or color channels to simulate multi-scattering effects for chromaticity [S1, unofficial].
4. **Modify Light Propagation:** In the light propagation step, remove the shadowing propagation involving the voxel in the neighboring slice between the current voxel and the light source. This modification is key to generating soft shadows instead of hard shadows [S1, unofficial].
5. **Emphasize Scattering Propagation:** Emphasize the scattering propagation that takes into account several voxels when light travels from the light source through the volume slice-by-slice [S1, unofficial].
6. **Adjust Key Light:** Allow the user to adjust the position of the "Key Light" to produce depth information arriving from different directions. For example, if an object has a hole from left to right, apply global light at either left or right to produce depth information [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Hard Shadows:** The base method [RDRS10] generates hard shadows which are not physically correct and hide local details. The improved method addresses this by modifying light propagation [S1, unofficial].
- **High-Opacity Data:** Unlike [ZM13] which applies global illumination at the back of the object, this method does not strongly depend on the transfer function and works appropriately with substantial high-opacity data at the back [S1, unofficial].
- **Depth Perception:** The method produces depth perception from different directions by allowing user adjustment of the Key Light, unlike [ZM13] which does not [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Real-time interactivity is claimed due to calculation in volume space using continuous slices [S1, unofficial]. Exact complexity not given.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the volume data and the light volume. Exact memory usage not given [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Produces realistic global illumination, real-time interactivity, soft shadows, preserves local details, and provides depth perception from different directions [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Loses local shape and detail information compared to surface display methods using normals, though this is mitigated by the soft shadow approach [S1, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** When realistic global illumination and soft shadows are needed in volume rendering with real-time requirements, and when depth perception from different directions is important [S1, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** When hard shadows are desired, or when local shape details are more critical than global illumination effects [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originated from the work described in "Local Detail Enhancement for Volume Rendering under Global Illumination" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [RDRS10] which simulates light transmission as continuous slices. The method was developed to address the limitations of [RDRS10] (hard shadows) and [ZM13] (back-lighting issues) [S1, unofficial].
