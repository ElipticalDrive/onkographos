# Noise-based 2D Fire

Also called: 2D Fire Texture Generation, Voronoi-Perlin Fire Noise

## Overview
This method generates real-time procedural volumetric fire by combining a 2D fire profile texture with procedural noise functions (specifically Improved Perlin Noise and Voronoi Noise) to create micro-level flame details [S1, S4][S5, unofficial]. It solves the problem of rendering visually convincing 3D fire in real-time without the computational costs of physical simulation or the artifacts of sprite-based systems [S4]. It sets itself apart from pure particle systems by using a lattice-based volume representation driven by noise for micro-interactivity, and from pure volumetric ray-marching by relying on a pre-defined 2D texture for color and shape definition [S1, S4].

## How it works
The method operates in two main phases: the construction of a parametric lattice around a central curve, and the pixel-shader-based rendering of that lattice using noise-modulated texture coordinates.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  **Central Curve:** A 4D curve (3D space + time) defining the macro shape and motion of the fire [S4]. This can be pre-scripted or driven by a particle simulation accounting for wind, buoyancy, and dispersion [S4].
2.  **Fire Profile Texture:** A 2D texture defining the base color, shape, and intensity of the fire [S1, S4].
3.  **Noise Functions:** Improved Perlin Noise and/or Voronoi Noise [S2, S3, unofficial][S4].
4.  **Parameters:**
    *   `radius(t)`: The radius of the fire at time `t` [S2, unofficial].
    *   `length(t)`: The length of the fire segment at time `t` [S2, unofficial].
    *   `frequency`: An application-specific scalar to adjust the spatial frequency of micro-details [S2, unofficial].
    *   `burning_speed`: A parameter controlling the temporal evolution of the noise [S4].
    *   `instance_offset`: A constant offset added to noise positions to differentiate multiple fire instances [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A rendered 3D volumetric fire effect with macro-shape deformation and micro-flicker [S4].

### Data Structures
1.  **Unit Fire Volume:** A canonical coordinate system for the fire geometry.
    *   X and Y axes: Range $[-1, 1]$ [S1].
    *   Z axis: Range $[0, 1]$ [S1].
2.  **Lattice:** A set of quadrilateral planar polygons constructed around the central curve [S4].
    *   Generated by creating four corner splines at a distance of $2 \cdot r(t)$ from the center spline [S4].
    *   Corners are determined by sampling the center spline by length to find corresponding positions on the corner splines [S4].
3.  **Noise Space:** A 4D coordinate space $(x, y, z, t)$ used for sampling noise [S2, unofficial].

### Steps

1.  **Lattice Construction:**
    *   Define the central curve $\mathbf{C}(t)$ based on particle simulation or script [S4].
    *   Generate four corner splines offset by $2 \cdot r(t)$ from $\mathbf{C}(t)$ [S4].
    *   Slice the volume between these splines into quadrilateral polygons [S4].

2.  **Noise Space Scaling (Artifact Prevention):**
    *   To prevent stretching artifacts when the fire radius or length changes, scale the noise space coordinates [S2, unofficial].
    *   Correct each noise function to have a base frequency of 1 [S2, unofficial].
    *   Calculate the scaled noise position $\mathbf{P}_{noise}$ for a fragment:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_{noise} = \left( \frac{x_{unit}}{r(t)} \cdot \text{frequency}, \frac{y_{unit}}{r(t)} \cdot \text{frequency}, \frac{z_{unit}}{length(t)} \cdot \text{frequency}, t \cdot \text{burning\_speed} + \text{instance\_offset} \right) $$
        *   Note: The vertical axis ($z$) is often offset by $-t$ to create an upward flow effect [S2, unofficial].
        *   $x_{unit}, y_{unit}, z_{unit}$ are the texture coordinates within the unit fire volume [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Noise Evaluation (Micro Details):**
    *   Sample the noise functions using $\mathbf{P}_{noise}$ [S4].
    *   **Variant A (Perlin/Voronoi Product):** Multiply Voronoi noise and Perlin noise textures [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Variant B (M-Noise/Improved Perlin):** Use 3D or 4D Improved Perlin Noise or M-Noise (GPU-optimized Perlin avoiding chained table lookups) to generate turbulent detail [S5, unofficial].
    *   Interpolate the product of the noises with a standard UV texture to create a modified UV coordinate [S3, unofficial].

4.  **Texture Mapping:**
    *   Map the 3D position in the unit fire volume to the 2D fire profile texture [S1].
    *   Calculate the 2D UV vector $\vec{uv}$ from the 3D unit volume coordinates $(tex_x, tex_y, tex_z)$:
        $$ \vec{uv} = \left( \sqrt{tex_x^2 + tex_y^2}, tex_z \right) $$
        [S1]
    *   Sample the fire profile texture:
        $$ \text{color} = \text{FireProfileTexture}(\vec{uv}) $$
        [S1]
    *   Apply a color gradient (e.g., orange) to the result [S3, unofficial].

5.  **Rendering:**
    *   Render the lattice polygons with the calculated texture coordinates into the unit fire volume [S4].
    *   Use hardware acceleration to render fragments, allowing implicit interpolation of color properties for realistic merging of flame elements [S5, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def render_fire_fragment(unit_coords, time, radius, length, frequency, instance_offset):
    """
    Renders a single fragment of the procedural fire.
    
    Args:
        unit_coords: Tuple (x, y, z) in range [-1, 1] x [-1, 1] x [0, 1]
        time: Current simulation time
        radius: Current fire radius r(t)
        length: Current fire length l(t)
        frequency: Spatial frequency multiplier
        instance_offset: Constant offset for this fire instance
        
    Returns:
        Color value
    """
    x, y, z = unit_coords
    
    # 1. Scale noise space to prevent stretching artifacts
    # Correct base frequency to 1, then apply application-specific frequency
    # Vertical axis offset by -time for upward flow
    noise_x = (x / radius) * frequency
    noise_y = (y / radius) * frequency
    noise_z = (z / length) * frequency
    noise_t = time * burning_speed - z + instance_offset # -z for upward flow, + instance_offset
    
    # 2. Evaluate Noise
    # Option A: Product of Voronoi and Perlin
    # noise_val = voronoi(noise_x, noise_y, noise_z) * perlin(noise_x, noise_y, noise_z, noise_t)
    
    # Option B: Improved Perlin or M-Noise (4D)
    # noise_val = improved_perlin_4d(noise_x, noise_y, noise_z, noise_t)
    
    # Interpolate product with standard UV if using Option A
    # modified_uv = lerp(noise_val, standard_uv, weight)
    
    # 3. Map to 2D Fire Profile Texture
    # Calculate radial distance for x-y plane
    radial_dist = sqrt(x*x + y*y)
    
    # Create 2D UV for the profile texture
    uv_2d = (radial_dist, z)
    
    # 4. Sample Color
    base_color = sample_fire_profile_texture(uv_2d)
    
    # 5. Apply Noise Modulation (Micro details)
    # The noise value modulates the intensity or color of the base texture
    final_color = base_color * (1.0 + noise_val * intensity_factor)
    
    # 6. Apply Color Gradient (e.g., Orange)
    final_color = apply_color_gradient(final_color)
    
    return final_color
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Stretching Artifacts:** If noise space is not scaled by the current radius and length, enlarging the fire will stretch the noise patterns [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Chained table lookups in standard Perlin Noise can cause GPU bottlenecks; M-Noise is recommended for hardware acceleration [S5, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The unit fire volume has specific ranges ($x,y \in [-1,1], z \in [0,1]$); sampling outside these bounds requires clamping or wrapping logic depending on the texture setup [S1].
*   **Collision Detection:** The lattice structure supports efficient collision detection, but the particle simulation driving the central curve must be sufficiently intelligent to handle bi-directional interaction without excessive computational cost [S4].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Real-time performance is achieved via hardware acceleration in the pixel shader [S4]. The complexity is dominated by the noise evaluation per fragment, which is $O(1)$ per pixel but depends on the noise implementation (M-Noise is faster than standard Perlin on GPU) [S5, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low memory footprint as it relies on 2D textures and simple lattice structures rather than large 3D voxel grids or particle buffers [S4][S5, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Achieves real-time results on hardware such as NVIDIA 2060 (in related voxel-based comparisons, though this specific method is shader-based) [S3, unofficial]. The method avoids the ~3 minutes per frame cost of physical simulations like [Nguyen et al. 2002] [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Visually convincing 3D fire without sprite artifacts [S4].
    *   Artist-controllable via 2D texture [S1].
    *   Supports real-time bi-directional environmental interaction (wind, collision) [S4].
    *   No stretching artifacts when scaling [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on a pre-defined profile texture; purely procedural color variation is limited compared to full physical simulations [S1].
    *   Macro-shape is constrained by the central curve and lattice structure [S4].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time applications (games, VR) requiring interactive fire with artistic control [S4].
*   **When Not to Use:** Offline cinematic rendering requiring maximum physical accuracy (use fluid simulation) [S1].

## Background
The method was developed by A. Fuller, H. Krishnan, K. Mahrous, H. Hamann, and J. Joy at UC Davis [S4]. It was presented in 2006/2007 as a solution for real-time procedural volumetric fire [S4][S2, unofficial]. It builds upon earlier work on Improved Perlin Noise [S4] and integrates concepts from particle simulations for macro-motion [S4]. The approach is distinct from voxel-based fire simulations (like those using Navier-Stokes equations) by focusing on a parametric lattice and noise-driven micro-details [S3, unofficial][S4].
