# Plenoxels Interpolation Methods

Also called: Plenoxel Grid with Trilinear Interpolation, Plenoxel Trilinear Interpolation, Trilinear Plenoxel, Trilinear Plenoxel Interpolation

## Overview
Plenoxels Interpolation Methods define a continuous plenoptic function throughout a sparse 3D voxel grid by trilinearly interpolating density and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients stored at voxel centers [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Trilinear Interpolation](1531-trilinear-interpolation.md) which handles general rectilinear grids, this method specifically targets the optimization stability and effective resolution of radiance field representations [S1][S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from [Multi-Texture Trilinear Estimation](1164-multi-texture-trilinear-estimation.md) and [Semi-Analytical Volume Rendering with Trilinear Interpolation](1312-semi-analytical-volume-rendering-with-trilinear-interpolatio.md) by integrating SH-based view-dependent color directly into the interpolation weights, and from [Radial Basis Function Interpolation](16091-radial-basis-function-interpolation.md) by using a structured grid rather than scattered points [S1][S2, unofficial]. The primary advantage is that trilinear interpolation produces a continuous function approximation critical for stable gradient-based optimization, outperforming nearest-neighbor approaches even at lower resolutions [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A sparse 3D voxel grid containing optimized parameters; a ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$; the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ for SH evaluation.
*   **Output:** Transmittance $T$, density $\sigma$, and color $\mathbf{c}$ at sample points along the ray.

**Data Structures:**
The grid stores values at voxel centers. For each active voxel, the following data is stored:
1.  **Density ($\sigma$):** A single scalar value.
2.  **Spherical Harmonic Coefficients:** For each of the 3 color channels (RGB), 9 coefficients are stored (Degree 2 SH). Total: 27 coefficients per voxel.
    *   Layout: A flat array of 28 floats per voxel (1 density + 27 SH coefficients).
    *   Bit packing: Not specified in sources; typically stored as standard 32-bit floats [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Spherical Harmonics Degree:** 2. This requires 9 coefficients per color channel, totaling 27 harmonic coefficients per voxel [S1][S2, unofficial]. Higher orders confer minimal benefit [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Grid Resolution:** Variable (e.g., $128^3$, $256^3$). Coarse-to-fine strategies may start at lower resolutions and upsample [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Marching Setup:** Define sample points along the ray.
2.  **Voxel Lookup:** For a sample point $\mathbf{p}$, identify the 8 nearest voxel centers surrounding $\mathbf{p}$ in the 3D grid.
3.  **Barycentric Weights:** Compute the trilinear interpolation weights based on the relative position of $\mathbf{p}$ within the unit cube defined by the 8 voxels. Let $\mathbf{w} = (w_x, w_y, w_z)$ be the fractional coordinates of $\mathbf{p}$ within the voxel cell, where $0 \le w_i < 1$. The weight for a corner voxel at offset $(i, j, k)$ where $i,j,k \in \{0,1\}$ is:
    $$
    W_{ijk} = \begin{cases} 
    (1-w_x)(1-w_y)(1-w_z) & \text{if } i=0, j=0, k=0 \\
    w_x(1-w_y)(1-w_z) & \text{if } i=1, j=0, k=0 \\
    (1-w_x)w_y(1-w_z) & \text{if } i=0, j=1, k=0 \\
    w_x w_y (1-w_z) & \text{if } i=1, j=1, k=0 \\
    (1-w_x)(1-w_y)w_z & \text{if } i=0, j=0, k=1 \\
    w_x(1-w_y)w_z & \text{if } i=1, j=0, k=1 \\
    (1-w_x)w_y w_z & \text{if } i=0, j=1, k=1 \\
    w_x w_y w_z & \text{if } i=1, j=1, k=1 
    \end{cases}
    $$
4.  **Interpolate Density:** Compute the density $\sigma(\mathbf{p})$ by summing the weighted density values $\sigma_v$ of the 8 neighbors:
    $$
    \sigma(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{v \in \text{neighbors}} W_v \cdot \sigma_v
    $$
5.  **Interpolate SH Coefficients:** For each color channel $c \in \{R, G, B\}$, compute the interpolated SH coefficients $\mathbf{a}_{c}$ by summing the weighted SH coefficient vectors $\mathbf{a}_{c,v}$ of the 8 neighbors:
    $$
    \mathbf{a}_{c}(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{v \in \text{neighbors}} W_v \cdot \mathbf{a}_{c,v}
    $$
    where $\mathbf{a}_{c,v}$ is the vector of 9 SH coefficients for channel $c$ at voxel $v$.
6.  **Evaluate Color:** Compute the color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{p})$ by evaluating the spherical harmonic basis functions $\mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{d})$ for the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ and dotting with the interpolated coefficients:
    $$
    \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{c \in \{R,G,B\}} \left( \mathbf{a}_{c}(\mathbf{p}) \cdot \mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{d}) \right) \hat{e}_c
    $$
    where $\hat{e}_c$ is the basis vector for the color channel.
7.  **Volume Rendering:** Use $\sigma(\mathbf{p})$ and $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{p})$ in the standard volume rendering integral (e.g., front-to-back compositing) to compute the final pixel color.

import numpy as np

def evaluate_plenoxel_trilinear(voxel_grid, ray_origin, ray_direction, sample_points):
    """
    Evaluates the plenoptic function at sample points along a ray using trilinear interpolation.
    
    Args:
        voxel_grid: Sparse grid structure. 
                    grid.values: array of shape (N, 28), where N is number of active voxels.
                    grid.coords: array of shape (N, 3), voxel center coordinates.
                    grid.active: boolean mask or list of active indices.
        ray_origin: (3,) array, ray origin.
        ray_direction: (3,) array, ray direction.
        sample_points: (M, 3) array, M sample points along the ray.
        
    Returns:
        densities: (M,) array of interpolated densities.
        colors: (M, 3) array of interpolated colors.
    """
    # Precompute SH basis functions for the viewing direction
    # Degree 2 SH has 9 basis functions. 
    # Y(d) is a vector of shape (9,)
    Y_d = compute_sh_basis_degree_2(ray_direction)
    
    densities = np.zeros(len(sample_points))
    colors = np.zeros((len(sample_points), 3))
    
    for i, p in enumerate(sample_points):
        # 1. Find the 8 nearest voxel centers surrounding point p
        # In a dense grid, this is O(1) via integer division. 
        # In a sparse grid, a spatial hash or k-d tree is needed.
        # Assuming we have a function to get the 8 neighbors and their weights:
        neighbors, weights = get_trilinear_neighbors(p, voxel_grid)
        
        if not neighbors:
            densities[i] = 0.0
            colors[i] = 0.0
            continue
            
        # 2. Extract data from neighbors
        # Each neighbor has 28 values: 1 density + 27 SH coeffs (9 per channel)
        neighbor_data = np.array([voxel_grid.values[v] for v in neighbors])
        
        # 3. Interpolate Density
        # neighbor_data[:, 0] is the density column
        densities[i] = np.dot(weights, neighbor_data[:, 0])
        
        # 4. Interpolate SH Coefficients
        # neighbor_data[:, 1:28] contains the 27 SH coefficients
        # Reshape to (8 neighbors, 3 channels, 9 coeffs)
        sh_coeffs = neighbor_data[:, 1:].reshape(-1, 3, 9)
        
        # Interpolate weights for each channel
        # weights is shape (8,)
        # interpolated_sh is shape (3, 9)
        interpolated_sh = np.einsum('n,ncm->cm', weights, sh_coeffs)
        
        # 5. Evaluate Color
        # Dot product of interpolated SH coeffs with SH basis Y_d
        # Y_d is shape (9,)
        # color_channel = sum(interpolated_sh[c, :] * Y_d)
        colors[i, 0] = np.dot(interpolated_sh[0, :], Y_d)
        colors[i, 1] = np.dot(interpolated_sh[1, :], Y_d)
        colors[i, 2] = np.dot(interpolated_sh[2, :], Y_d)
        
    return densities, colors

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Nearest Neighbor Instability:** Using nearest neighbor interpolation creates a discontinuous model that is difficult to optimize and less stable with respect to learning rate variations [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Tricubic Cost:** Tricubic interpolation offers continuous and smooth approximation but incurs substantial computation with negligible quality gain compared to trilinear [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The method assumes a bounded grid. Handling unbounded scenes requires extensions like NDC or Multisphere Images (see Subtopics) [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Memory:** Scales linearly with the number of active voxels. Each voxel stores 28 floats (1 density + 27 SH coefficients) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Time:** Per sample point, the cost is dominated by the lookup of 8 neighbors and the dot product of 9-element vectors. It is $O(1)$ per sample point given efficient spatial indexing [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
*   On 8 NeRF synthetic scenes, Trilinear Plenoxels at $128^3$ resolution achieves PSNR 28.46, SSIM 0.926, LPIPS 0.100 [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Trilinear at $256^3$ achieves PSNR 30.57, SSIM 0.950, LPIPS 0.065 [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Nearest Neighbor at $256^3$ achieves PSNR 27.17, SSIM 0.914, LPIPS 0.119 [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Nearest Neighbor at $128^3$ achieves PSNR 23.73, SSIM 0.866, LPIPS 0.176 [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Trilinear interpolation at $128^3$ outperforms Nearest Neighbor at $256^3$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Increases effective resolution by representing sub-voxel variations; produces a continuous function approximation critical for successful optimization; more stable with respect to learning rate variations than nearest neighbor [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Tricubic interpolation, while smoother, is computationally expensive with negligible quality gain [S1]. Requires careful handling of unbounded scenes [S1][S2, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
*   Use when optimizing a radiance field representation and stability/convergence is a concern.
*   Use when higher effective resolution is needed without increasing the grid size significantly.
*   Do not use if computational resources are extremely limited and nearest neighbor is acceptable (though quality will suffer).

## Background
Plenoxels were introduced by Fridovich-Keil et al. in 2022 [S1][S2, unofficial]. The method was developed as a sparse voxel-based alternative to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), aiming to eliminate the need for neural networks in radiance field representation [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of PlenOctrees but uses a regular grid with trilinear interpolation to define a continuous plenoptic function, contrasting with PlenOctrees which assumes constant values inside each voxel [S1][S2, unofficial]. The use of Spherical Harmonics (SH) for view-dependent color was inspired by PlenOctrees, but the trilinear interpolation of SH coefficients is a key innovation of Plenoxels [S1][S2, unofficial].
