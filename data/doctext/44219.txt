volume-cartographer: opt-in GPU sampling for vc_render_tifxyz (scripts/render_gpu)
In one sentence: A new opt-in tool renders tifxyz surfaces with the sampling step on a CUDA or ROCm GPU; on 20 PHerc0800 wraps the output zarr was byte-identical to vc_render_tifxyz at all six pyramid levels.
One real example: Rendering 10 PHerc0800 wraps (volume 20250521135224, 8.640 µm, 116 keV; w030–w075, surfaces up to 12,080 px wide, -n 31 --flip-normals), render_gpu.py on a Radeon AI PRO R9700 took 28–47 s against 64–82 s for the stock CPU render, and wrote 60 of 60 pyramid levels byte-identical to it.
Before: Most of vc_render_tifxyz's time goes to sampleTileSlices, which samples every output tile at every depth layer on the CPU.
After this PR: Nothing changes unless you use the new tool in volume-cartographer/scripts/render_gpu/.
render_shim.so is loaded into the unchanged vc_render_tifxyz with LD_PRELOAD. It records each tile's sample positions instead of sampling them.
render_gpu.py then does the sampling on the GPU, keeping volume chunks in GPU memory across surfaces, and writes levels 0–5 into the zarr the C++ run created.
render_band.sh LIST VOLUME runs both for a list of surfaces. Without RENDER_GPU=1, or without PyTorch and a GPU, or with a remote volume, it runs the normal CPU render.
Proof:
Parity: 20 of 20 PHerc0800 wraps (w030–w125, every 5th) were byte-identical to the unchanged CPU vc_render_tifxyz at all six levels (L0–L5), sampled on an AMD Instinct MI50 (ROCm). Two of them (w030, w035) were re-checked later, again identical at L0–L5.
End to end: RENDER_GPU=1 render_band.sh on the R9700 (ROCm) for the 10 wraps above: 60/60 levels byte-identical to RENDER_GPU=0. An earlier run over 20 wraps gave 120/120.
At the head of this PR (the rows above used an earlier version, before chunk reads were overlapped with uploads):
Repeat parity: 20 further rounds of the 10 wraps on an MI50, each against a fresh CPU render: 1,200 of 1,200 levels byte-identical.
CUDA: on an RTX 3080, 150 tiles of w030 were byte-identical to the CPU render at L0 (a spot check; the pyramid levels were not compared on CUDA).
Last commit (device selection and kernel launch checks): the same 150 tiles re-run on the RTX 3080 and on an MI50, byte-identical at L0 on both.
Speed: the 10 wraps, 16 CPU cores, CPU and GPU runs interleaved, on a shared machine (load average 11–34). The GPU column is render_gpu.py alone; the dump step before it took 0.4–1.3 s per wrap and is not included.

On the 3080 the widest wraps (060–075) gain little. The R9700's first GPU round includes the one-time kernel build. On a heavily loaded MI50 the same runs were byte-identical (60/60 ×3) but the times were not clean enough to report.
Why / where this is useful:
Rendering surface layers is the step between a fitted surface and ink detection, and it has to be redone for every wrap whenever a surface changes, so it adds up across a scroll. The CPU render spends most of its time sampling the volume. Doing that sampling on the GPU gave byte-identical output in every check I ran, on NVIDIA and AMD cards, and finished the same 10 wraps in about half the time on an R9700 and in about three quarters of the time on an MI50. It's a separate tool you opt into, so the normal vc_render_tifxyz path doesn't change.
I personally verified that the example and proof above were produced by this PR on the stated data.
Details
How parity was checked, per wrap:
```
cd volume-cartographer/scripts/render_gpu
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -shared -fPIC render_shim.cpp -o render_shim.so $(pkg-config --cflags opencv4) -ldl
RENDER_GPU=0 ./render_band.sh cpu_list.txt VOLUME     # lines: "SURFACE_DIR CPU_OUT.zarr"
mkdir -p GPU_OUT.zarr.rgpu_dump
LD_PRELOAD=$PWD/render_shim.so RENDER_SHIM_DIR=GPU_OUT.zarr.rgpu_dump \
  vc_render_tifxyz -s SURFACE_DIR -v VOLUME -g 0 --scale 1 -n 31 --flip-normals --zarr-output GPU_OUT.zarr --cache-gb 4
python3 render_gpu.py GPU_OUT.zarr.rgpu_dump GPU_OUT.zarr VOLUME/0
for l in 0 1 2 3 4 5; do python3 cmp_zarr.py CPU_OUT.zarr GPU_OUT.zarr $l; done   # "identical": true
```
These are the two steps render_band.sh runs with RENDER_GPU=1; the combined path was also run end to end (above).
Limits:
Local OME-Zarr volume with 128³ u8 chunks, -g 0, u8 output, no composite, no accumulate offsets.
The shim works by LD_PRELOAD interposition of two C++ symbols (sampleTileSlices<u8> and VcDataset::writeChunkSkipEmpty), matched by mangled name, so vc_core must be a shared library (the default).
If their signatures change, the dump comes out empty and render_gpu.py stops with an error. It does not write a wrong render.
A native hook in vc_render_tifxyz would be cleaner and is a natural follow-up.
render_gpu.py needs PyTorch with CUDA or ROCm. The kernel is built on first use.
