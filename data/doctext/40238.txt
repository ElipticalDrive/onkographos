We propose an inverse rendering pipeline that simultaneously reconstructs scene geometry, lighting, and spatially-varying material
from a set of multi-view images. Specifically, the proposed pipeline
involves volume and physics-based rendering, which are performed
separately in two steps: exploration and exploitation. During the
exploration step, our method utilizes the compactness of neural
radiance fields and a flexible differentiable volume rendering technique to learn an initial volumetric field. Here, we introduce a novel
cascaded tensorial radiance field method on top of the Canonical
Polyadic (CP) decomposition to boost model compactness beyond
conventional methods. In the exploitation step, a shading pass that
incorporates a differentiable physics-based shading method is applied to jointly optimize the scene’s geometry, spatially-varying
materials, and lighting, using image reconstruction loss. Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed inverse rendering
pipeline, IRCasTRF, outperforms prior works in inverse rendering
quality. The final output is highly compatible with downstream
applications like scene editing and advanced simulations. Further
details are available on the project page: https://ircasrf.github.io/.

INTRODUCTION
Novel view synthesis has been a long-standing research problem in
computer vision. Given a set of posed multi-view images, it aims to
learn a scene representation and synthesize novel views. Early studies utilize Light Field techniques [56, 57] and Multi-Plane Images
(MPI) [54, 55] to record the scene. Recent advancements in neural scene representations [32, 36, 53], which utilize differentiable
rendering techniques, have led to the development of continuous
volumetric fields. However, the learned volumetric fields suffer
from entangled appearance and environmental lighting, making
them unsuitable for direct use in downstream applications. Learning separate specifications for geometry, materials, and lighting
from images, known as inverse rendering, has emerged as an intersection between computer vision and graphics. Despite significant
progress, inverse rendering remains challenging due to its inherently underconstrained nature [64]. Because optimizing both the
geometry and spatially-varying material simultaneously can lead to
local minima, adversely impacting the quality of reconstruction. To
address this challenge, neural pipelines that employ separate stages
for exploration and exploitation have emerged as a novel trend in inverse rendering research [38, 60]. This approach helps improve the
efficiency and stability of the inherently underconstrained learning
process.
We introduce a novel hybrid pipeline called IRCasTRF to solve
the challenging problem of inverse rendering. Our pipeline consists
of two stages: exploration and exploitation, utilizing both volumetric field and explicit mesh representations’ strengths. Specifically,
in the exploration stage, IRCasTRF first leverages the advantages in
compactness and efficiency of our proposed novel volumetric field
representation – Cascaded Canonical Polyadic Tensorial Radiance
Fields (CasCPRF). The proposed CasCPRF leverage inspirations
from TensoRF [9] and Hierarchical Tucker (H-Tucker) [16], which

2 RELATED WORKS
2.1 Neural Scene Representation
Neural scene representation represents the scene as a volumetric
function with both geometry and appearance optimized through
differentiable rendering techniques. NeRF [36] and its variants
[11, 23, 44, 51, 59, 62–64] are generative methods of converting
spatial coordinates into density and colors for volume rendering.
However, NeRF lacks direct surface regularization and is slow in inference. An alternative approach, NeuS [50], learns a neural signed
distance function (SDF) using differentiable volume rendering, and
the surface can be extracted by finding the zero-level set of SDF
values. Additionally, NeuS improves surface smoothness via eikonal
loss [17]. Apart from learning SDF through volume rendering techniques, there is another class of surface-based neural scene representations that learn and render disentangled surfaces and appearance. For instance, IDR [58] uses MLPs to acquire disentangled
surfaces and appearance for rendering. Meanwhile, PhySG [61]
learns geometry as SDF and approximates BRDFs and lighting
using Spherical Gaussians (SGs) [22]. IRON [60] places a strong
emphasis on improving the accuracy of geometric features, such as
edges. However, conventional surface-based methods employ MLPs
as their network backbones, which can considerably slow down
their computational efficiency. In contrast, Nvdiffrec [37] takes an
alternative approach by explicitly learning and evolving a mesh
from scratch using DMTet [42] in conjunction with differentiable
mesh rendering gradients. Nonetheless, this technique may fall
prey to local minima and learn implausible surfaces.
2.2
METHOD
In this paper, we present a hybrid pipeline that first reconstructs
volumetric fields by tensor decomposition, which is efficient, compact, and can easily adapt to complex shapes, thus producing a
good topology initialization for the second stage. Next, we estimate
albedo, roughness, environment lighting, and surface normal using
physics-based shading methods [20, 28, 37], building on the preliminary topology generated in the previous stage. The primary
contribution of our approach is the CasCPRF method, a compact
and efficient volumetric field representation built upon tensor decomposition. By explicitly extracting the mesh from the CasCPRF,
we initialize the geometry for shading in the second stage. This
leads to better training results than previous inverse rendering
techniques [37, 60]. Figure 1 shows an overview of the pipeline.

3.3
The CasCPRF (cascaded CPD) method is a novel approach to effectively approximating a high-order radiance field feature volume
𝒱. An illustration of the overall CasCPRF pipeline can be seen in
Figure 2. Our approach first draws inspiration from the hierarchical
tucker decomposition (HTD) [18] that decomposes a tensor volume
into a tree structure, the vectors in a higher level are estimated by
multiplying vectors from the lower level. We also draw inspiration
from Bacon’s [31] band-limited coordinate networks which manipulate the frequency of each layer to attain an analytical Fourier
spectrum for multi-scale scene representation. By utilizing a cascaded structure and differentiable learning process, our proposed
method CasCPRF also serves as a multi-scale scene representation.
By employing a cascaded scheme, our approach enables the expansion of the high-order radiance field feature volumes across
multiple modes and hierarchical levels. This results in a multi-scale
scene representation that implicitly controls the frequency band of
learned contents in each scale, thereby facilitating a more compact
tensor approximation. As a result, CasCPRF can produce superior reconstruction quality compared to its single-scale counterpart while
utilizing the same model size budget. Empirical experiments reveal
that the coarsest scale, which has a low spatial resolution, primarily
learns specular patterns characterized by low spatial frequency but
high angular frequency. On the other hand, the finer scale, with
higher spatial resolution, focuses on learning texture details characterized by high spatial frequency but low angular frequency. This
separation of frequencies in different scales of cascaded structure
leads to a compact scene representation.

3.4.3 Texturing. Surface texture parameterization has been a longstanding research issue in computer graphics. To ensure that the
texture seamlessly varies with changing topology during reconstruction, approaches such as Nvdiffrec [37] and PhySG [61] learn
a volumetric texture that is indexed by world-space positions and
modeled compactly using an MLP network. However, we found
that this approach tends to converge slowly and produce overlysmoothed results. Consequently, we adopted a different strategy
[37]: initializing the 2D texture using learnable parameters and
generating the index from the input mesh in Blender. To simplify
the problem, we fixed the indexes between the vertices and the 2D
texture map during the optimization.

CONCLUSION
We propose a hybrid inverse rendering pipeline that leverages the
benefits of both neural rendering and physics-based shading. Specifically, in the exploration stage, we introduce a novel compact neural
scene representation known as CasCPRF, which compactly approximates a high-order radiance field feature volume by employing
tensor decomposition to expand varying frequency contents across
multiple hierarchical levels and modes. In the second exploitation
stage, an explicit mesh is extracted and processed through the
shading pass, which learns decomposed scene elements such as geometry, spatially-varying materials, and lighting. Our experiments
demonstrate that our proposed method surpasses other state-ofthe-art inverse rendering approaches.
5.4.2 Exploitation. To validate the importance of the per-vertex
deformation vectors Δv and global movement vector Δm in our
pipeline, we conduct an ablation study by removing them from
the reconstruction process. Our quantitative results presented in
Table 3 indisputably demonstrate that both vectors play a crucial
role in achieving high-quality reconstruction. Figure 8 presents the
visualization of our ablation results for per-vertex optimization. We
observe that in the absence of Δv, our reconstructed object contains
floating noisy points, resulting in impaired lighting.
