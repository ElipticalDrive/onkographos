OpenCL, short for Open Computing Language, is a standard of
GPGPU that allows programmers to do cross-platform C/C++
programming without any deep understanding in assemble
languages among different calculation hardware. This feature
enables us to implement parallel programming with diverse
processors, which are nowadays prevailingly advantaged in our
desktop PCs and Macs.
ABSTRACT
3-D interactive volume rendering can be rather complicated
through conventional ray casting with simple sampling and
texture mapping. Owing to the limitation of hardware resources,
volume rendering algorithms are considerably time-consuming.
Therefore, adaptive sampling technique is proposed to tackle the
problem of excessive computational cost. In this paper,
considering an optimization in parallelized ray-casting algorithms
of volume rendering, we propose an adaptive sampling method,
which mainly reduces the number of sampling points through
non-linear sampling function. This new method can be rather
effective while making a trade-off between performance and
rendering quality. Our experimental results demonstrate that the
proposed adaptive sampling method can result in high efficiency
in computation, and produce high-quality image based on the
MSE and SSIM metrics.
Ray-casting [2] algorithm, shown in Figure 1, is one of the
rendering methods that are easily parallelizable, and directly
implementable through OpenCL. For every pixel on the image
plane, there is a ray that goes through the whole box of 3-D
volumetric data at some specific direction from the view point.
Through weighted sum and transfer function lookup, a pixel of
result is accumulated.
Performance of volume rendering through ray-casting can be
limited by the computational speed and the onboard memory of
computational hardware. While the approach to do optimization
by sampling view rays, or doing sampling on the image plane, is
hard to implement in OpenCL, it is still possible to skip rays that
are superfluous. This optimization is called adaptive sampling.
Since it avoids scanning all details of the volumetric data, it is
crucial that the method of implementation will not compromise
the quality of rendering result. MSE (mean square error) and
SSIM [3] metrics are used to compute the qualitive losses between
our implementation and the source image, therefore define the
feasibility and the degree of optimization in our rendering
algorithm.

1. INTRODUCTION
Volume Rendering [1] is a set of specific methods or techniques,
which comprise extracting constructive information from a 3-D
volumetric data and accumulating the information to a displayable
result, to draw a 2-D projection out of a 3-D volumetric data.
There are many methods proposed towards solving this issue,
however, complexity for such methods are relatively high.
Although programmable processors on a single desktop PC is
abundant currently, it is still messy to take this superiority, since
the structure of GPGPU determines that a computation task can be
efficaciously accelerated only if the task is implemented in
parallel.

The sampling method used in ray-casting volume rendering is
crucial to the computational performance. Some useful and easily
implementable methods can be found published by Huawei Wang
et al. [9]. When a ray hits nothing, or the alpha value of frontal
opacity is already saturated, there will be no demand to do the
intermediate sum anymore. Simultaneously, it is essential to
terminate when the alpha value reaches top opacity-level. The
method to decide whether a ray hits can be executed in CPU by a
simple intersection processing. However, doing optimization on
the choice of pixels can be hard to implement in parallel. Another
problem is that, the GPU must additionally compute the
intersection of the rear plane, which results in a larger time
complexity of the whole rendering system.

Ray-casting algorithm [2], which projects 3-D volume data into a
2-D image, is implemented in the OpenCL kernel and runs on the
GPU. There’re three steps to implement the ray-casting algorithm:

There are a variety of methods that do optimization on sampling

then do parallelized computation through topological sorting,

method which samples through creating priority queue with treelike algorithm can be found published by Yuchi Huo et al. [11].
Those algorithms leave us an intuition to do sampling in every
workgroup or tree and then sum the result up. However, owing to
the memory architectural feature of the OpenCL standard, it is
hard to do topological sorting among threads of workgroups
without increasing the time complexity. Finally, the idea to utilize
the 3-D index space of architecture is abandoned.

●

The homogeneous coordinates [16] is generally used in various
fields including OpenGL coordinate system. Therefore, it is
convenient to find the ray direction and the distance between any
point of 3-D volume to any pixel on the image plane through
vector calculations. The definition of the casting vector which
starts from the view point of image plane and ends at the sampling
target point is shown in Eq. (2) [16] (1).
Compared with sampling on the image plane, adaptive sampling
in a view ray is easier to implement. Theoretically, adaptive
sampling is to sample only the key points, according to the work
of Huawei Wang et al. [12]. Reducing redundant sampling points
in a view ray is an optimization of rendering. Nevertheless, the
algorithm used to implement the computation on identifying the
sampling points become another problem to increase the time
complexity and memory usage. Skipping sampling points through
a specific function or algorithm is another kind of adaptive
sampling method. One similar and distinct method can be found
published by Martin Kraus et al. [13], which does sampling in
three directions, skipping slices of sampling points that are too far
from the view plane of every view ray, and then do pyramidal
synthesis on every level of slices to skip sampling points.

The algorithm to determine the position that a ray hits is the raybox intersection. The entire 3-D volumetric data is analogically
put into a cubical box, whose side lengths are scaled to a degree
that the longest one is 2. E.g., a 3-D volumetric cubical size of
256*256*128 will be scaled into a cube size of 2*2*1. Then
several calculations including the comparison of distances
between the eye and the hit voxel are implemented for the
subsequent sampling and texture loading.
In linear sampling, the parameter tstep is fixed. Therefore, the
implementation of linear sampling method confronts a dilemma,
whether to provide a high-quality rendering result or to achieve a
high efficiency at relatively low rendering quality. The proposed
sampling algorithm is inspired by the method described in [15],
where a specific monotonically decreasing function is used. When
the independent variable x is near zero, the dependent variable
should be large enough. The goal of our approach is to simulate
photographing, where objects too far away are vague and those
close enough are clear. In reality, it follows the simple Eq. (4),
where f(x) is the clarity (in volume rendering, the density of
sampling points) and x is the distance between the eye and the
object. In the algorithm [15], tstep is not chosen to be necessarily
nonlinear, instead, it gradually exponential increases in power of 2
in the whole range, even fixed in the local region of sampling
intervals. Differently, in the proposed sampling algorithm, tstep
changes all the time along the tracing ray resulting in nonlinear
sampling.
3.2 Unnormalized Coordinate
Similar to the section 3.1, an unnormalized coordinate is utilized
to load and read image texture from the image buffer. Instead, the
length of the longest edge will practically be scaled into 1. E.g., a
volumetric cube size of 128*128*64 will be scaled into a cubical
box that size of 1*1*0.5. To scale the longest edge to 1 instead of
2 helps in calculating texture coordinate without changing the
center of the world coordinate into the cubical box. Since there is
no negative coordinate exists, the texture loading method is
uniformed and optimized. Comparing to ray-box intersection, the
use of unnormalized coordinate is rather indispensable. Since the
length of three dimensions in a volumetric data is dissimilar, the
hastily use of normalized coordinate may cause an out-of-bound
buffer reading. Nevertheless, the selection of unnormalized
coordinate is optional, for reaching a voxel that is out-of-bound
will not seriously affect the finally result of one ray.
Inspired by the linear sampling algorithm, our algorithm takes
every step of sampling density as the prior knowledge to the next
sampling point, in other words, every continuous two sampling
points have the same difference in the sampling density. Since the
mathematical function is nonlinear, even though the distance of
sample density preserves identical, tstep differs, resulting in
ignoring relatively more redundant sampling points compared
with the linear sampling algorithm.

5. CONCLUSION
The proposed adaptive sampling algorithm is efficient in
computational complexity, while achieve satisfying quality result.
Our approach of sampling algorithm outperformed uniformed
linear sampling algorithm by a great margin in MSE. In our
approach, when k value is set to near 4, both the MSE and SSIM
come to an acceptable satisfactory. A higher k value may increase
the SSIM value, but the MSE value will simultaneously increase,
too.
