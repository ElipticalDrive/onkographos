ABSTRACT
In this paper, we propose a modeling method of clouds from a single photograph. To synthesize realistic images of outdoor scenes,
modeling of 3D clouds is necessary. However, synthesizing realistic clouds is still a difficult task. To address this problem, we
propose an example-based volume texture synthesis to model nonhomogeneous density volumes. Our method prepares an example
cloud volume data using a physically-based fluid simulator. Then
the output cloud volume is automatically synthesized from the
example cloud volume data so that its rendered image becomes
similar to the clouds in the input photograph. In contrast to the previous texture synthesis methods, our method synthesizes density
volumes taking into account scattering and transmittance of light.
We show several cloud volumes whose rendered images are similar
to the input photograph.

INTRODUCTION
Textures are important elements to enhance the realism of computer
graphics and widely used in many applications including movies
and games. The creation of 2D textures is relatively easy by taking
photographs of real objects. Three-dimensional volume textures, on
the other hand, are difficult to acquire just by taking photographs,
and thus the creation of 3D textures is quite difficult.
Clouds play an important role in creating synthetic images of
outdoor scenes. To represent realistic clouds, the creation of volume
texture that stores the density distribution of clouds is necessary.
Many methods have been developed for this purpose.

There are two major approaches to modeling clouds: a procedural approach and a physically-based approach. The procedural
approach can synthesize realistic clouds with a low computational
cost [6]. However, in order to create the desired types of cloud,
many parameters have to be determined manually by trial and error processes. The physically-based approach, on the other hand,
can generate clouds with realistic shapes by simulating the physical
process of cloud formation [2]. However, the rendering results of
simulated clouds are not always similar to the clouds in the photograph. Moreover, it is quite difficult to simulate clouds whose
rendered images are similar to those in the photograph due to the
expensive computational costs.
There is also another approach for modeling clouds, an imagebased approach, where synthetic clouds are generated from photographs. Dobashi et al. proposed a method for modeling largescale clouds from satellite images [3]. Dobashi et al. also proposed
a method for modeling clouds from photographs taken from the
ground [4]. Although these methods can create realistic clouds,
these are based on heuristic ideas and the shading of clouds in the
photographs is not taken into account.
In this paper, we propose a new image-based modeling method
for clouds using a single photograph and an example volumetric
cloud dataset. The goal of our method is to synthesize clouds so that
the rendered images of the clouds are similar to those in the input
photograph as shown in Fig. 1. Unlike the previous image-based
methods, we take into account the shading of the clouds in the photograph. To solve this problem, we employ the optimization-based
texture synthesis method [11]. Our method synthesizes density
volumes taking into account the scattering and the transmittance
of light. To make the volume texture synthesis feasible, our method
employs a single scattering model and the multiple scattering of
light is approximated with a constant ambient term. By specifying
the direction of the sunlight, our method estimates the radiances
of the sunlight, the skylight, and the ambient term from the input
photograph. We show that the rendering result of synthesized volume resembles the input photograph. Moreover, our method can
be extended to synthesize a cloud volume from two orthogonal
images.

RELATED WORK
Procedural modeling: Many methods have been proposed to
model clouds using procedural modeling such as fractals [20], textured ellipsoids [7], spectral synthesis [17], and metaballs [5]. Schpok et al. have developed a real-time system for the procedural
modeling of clouds [18]. A more detailed explanation of the procedural modeling of clouds can be found in [6]. These methods can
generate realistic clouds, but many parameters have to be adjusted
by trial and error processes.

Image-based modeling: Dobashi et al. proposed image-based
methods for modeling clouds [3, 4]. In [3], large-scale clouds are
generated from an infrared satellite images so that the accumulated
density of the synthetic clouds becomes the same as the intensity
of the satellite image. In [4], clouds are generated so that the opacity of the clouds is similar to the clouds in the photograph. This
method can generate three types of clouds, cirrus, altocumulus,
and cumulus. Both methods do not take into account the shading
of the clouds and therefore the images obtained by rendering the
synthetic clouds are not similar to the clouds in the photographs.
Yuan et al. proposed an estimation method of cloud shapes from single images [21]. Although this method can model cumulus clouds
similar to input photographs, this method is restricted to uniform
densities inside clouds. Okabe et al. proposed a modeling method
of fluids from a sparse set of images [16]. Although this method can
model smokes, fires, and water, this method is limited to emissive
media and does not consider scattering of light. Recently, Levis et
al. estimated the distribution of extinction coefficients inside clouds
based on computational tomography [12]. This method, however,
requires a large number of photographs captured from a high altitude airplane.
Texture synthesis: Heeger and Bergen proposed a histogrambased approach for creating volume textures from 2D textures [8].
Although this method produces good-looking results, the method
cannot reproduce structural information of the input textures. Kopf
et al. addressed this problem by using a nonparametric approach [10]
. This method successfully synthesizes a wide range of volume textures. More recently, Shu et al. proposed a very efficient method
for synthesizing volume textures of aggregate solid materials [19].

OVERVIEW OF OUR METHOD
Our goal is to synthesize a cloud volume V that stores the extinction
coefficient σt at each voxel, so that its rendered image is similar
to an input photograph Iph provided by the user (see Fig. 2). We
achieve this goal with the help of an example-based texture synthesis technique [11]. In addition to the input photograph, we prepare
an example cloud volume database, Vex = {V1ex , · · · , VNex }, which
is created by fluid simulation [2].

We first search for a set of voxels Cex , whose rendered image Bex
is similar to Bph (p), from the database. Since Bph (p) and Bex are
similar, then the unknown voxel values Cph (p) in the output volume
V should be similar to Cex . We therefore copy Cex into Cph (p). By
repeating these processes, V with its rendered image similar to the
input photograph should be created. We formulate this idea as an
optimization problem, following the work proposed by Kwatra et
al. [11].
Our method consists of the preprocess and the synthesis process.
In the preprocess, we prepare a set of voxels Cex from the example
volume database Vex . To match the rendered images of Cex with
Bph (p), rendering parameters (e.g. the radiance of the sun) are estimated. For the rendering, our method employs a single scattering
model. The synthesis process is to minimize the energy function
that measures the difference of pixels between the rendered image
of V and the input photograph Iph , and of voxels (the differences
between the voxel values of V and those derived from the example
cloud volume database Vex ).

CLOUD VOLUME RENDERING
In rendering the cloud volume, we use the parallel projection and we
consider a local coordinate system such that the viewing direction
is parallel to the z axis and the image plane is on xy plane as
shown in Fig. 3. We then generate an axis-aligned grid with this
coordinate system and resample the volume using this grid with
N x × Ny × Nz voxels, where N x and Ny are equal to the resolution
of the input photograph Iph , and Nz is specified by the user. To
simplify the explanation, the length of the voxel is assumed to be 1,
and each voxel is indexed by the pair of corresponding pixel p and
z coordinate, (p, z), as shown in Fig. 3.
Let us define a pixel block B(p) that represents a set of neighborhood pixels centered around pixel p with a user-specified width w.
We also define a voxel block C which consists of w × w × Nz voxels,
and voxel block whose projected area onto the image plane is equal
to B(p) is explicitly denoted as C(p).

RESULTS
Fig. 5 shows the rendering results of cloud volumes synthesized
by using our method. All results are synthesized on a desktop PC
with an Intel Core-i7 4790 3.60 GHz CPU. The example cloud volume database consists of 137 cloud volumes created by using fluid
simulation method [2]. The resolutions of original example cloud
volumes range from 30 to 60. The computational time for the preprocess is about 4 min. Fig. 5(a) shows a synthesized cloud volume
(the resolution of the volume is 64 × 51 × 20). Fig. 5(b) shows the
rendering result of side view of (a). The input photograph is shown
in the inset, and the width w of the voxel block for Figs. 5(a)(b) is set
to 3. The computational time for synthesizing the cloud volume in
Figs. 5(a)(b) is 18s. In Figs. 5(c)(d), the resolution of the synthesized
cloud volume is 60 × 48 × 30, and the width of the voxel block is 3.
The computational time for Figs. 5(c)(d) is 16s. In Figs. 5(e)(f), the
resolution of the cloud volume is 120 × 60 × 82, and voxel blocks
whose width is 5 are used. The computational time for Figs. 5(e)(f)
is 67s. As shown in these results, our method can model cloud
volumes automatically whose rendered images are similar to the
input photographs shown in the insets. Since our method extracts
voxel blocks from the realistic cloud volumes [2], rendering results
viewed from different directions look natural.

7.1
Discussion
Although our method can model cloud volumes whose rendered
images are similar to the input photograph, our method has several
limitations. Our rendering model for voxel blocks approximates
the multiple scattering of light with a constant ambient term La .
Calculating multiple scattering of light from other voxel blocks
can improve the visual appearance of synthesized cloud volume.
However, the calculation of multiple scattering of light is computationally expensive, and considering scattering light from all other
voxel blocks makes the synthesis process infeasible.
Currently, our method can handle relatively low resolution volumes due to the large data size of the example cloud volume database. For example, the data size of the example cloud volume database for Fig. 6 is about 3.5GB. Compressing example cloud volumes

CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we have proposed a volume texture synthesis method
for clouds. Our method synthesizes the cloud volume so that the
rendered image is similar to the input photograph, by using the
voxel block extracted from the example cloud volume database. In
contrast to the previous texture synthesis methods, our method
takes into account the scattering of light within volume. The synthesis process is formulated by minimizing the differences of the voxel
