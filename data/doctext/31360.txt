Abstract
In recent years, 3D Gaussian splatting has achieved significant
progress as an efficient rasterization rendering method in real-time
3D reconstruction and rendering. However, existing methods still
face limitations in terms of geometric accuracy, material realism,
and adaptability to complex scenes, particularly in balancing the
representation of planar and thin structures, achieving global geometric consistency, and optimizing specular reflection performance.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a high-precision
3D Gaussian splatting rendering method that integrates hybrid
Gaussian representations with geometry-guided techniques. The
method adaptively selects 2D or 3D Gaussian splatting within a
unified rendering pipeline to enhance the representation efficiency
of planar and volumetric structures. It combines TensoSDF geometry guidance to achieve efficient implicit surface constraints
and global geometric consistency through tensor decomposition.
Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms the
original 3DGS in terms of rendering quality and geometric accuracy across multiple benchmark datasets, while maintaining high
training and rendering efficiency. It is applicable to diverse scenarios, including architectural reconstruction, vegetation rendering,
human modeling, and real-time interaction.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid development of virtual reality, augmented reality, digital
twins, film special effects, and gaming has significantly increased
the demand for 3D visual content production and rendering. Efficient rendering that maintains geometric accuracy and photorealism is now a central research objective in computer graphics and
visual computing. Traditional mesh- and voxel-based 3D representations, although well-studied, often exhibit high storage redundancy,
low rendering efficiency, and limited geometric expressiveness in
complex scenes. Recent methods such as Neural Implicit Representations (NIR) and Point-based Rendering (PBR) have addressed
some of these limitations. Notably, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
has emerged as a leading approach for novel view synthesis and
high-quality 3D reconstruction, offering advantages in spatially
continuous representation and rasterization efficiency. [1] [2] [3]
[4]
3DGS represents scene geometry and appearance using parameterized 3D Gaussian distributions, which are projected onto screen
space via a rasterization pipeline for efficient rendering. Compared
to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), 3DGS provides superior rendering efficiency and real-time performance. However, current 3DGS
methods exhibit notable limitations. When modeling planar or thinlayer structures, Gaussian spheres or ellipsoids require numerous
redundant instances to approximate geometry, resulting in inefficient storage and computation. Additionally, 3DGS lacks explicit
global geometric constraints, leading to geometric inconsistencies
or local distortions in complex scenes. [5] [6] [7]
To overcome these limitations, this study introduces a 3DGS rendering framework that integrates hybrid Gaussian representations

METHOD OVERVIEW
As shown in Figure 1, we introduce a hybrid Gaussian representation and a geometry-guided high-precision 3D Gaussian splatter
rendering method designed to unify the optimization objectives
of geometric accuracy, material realism, and rendering efficiency.
Architecturally, the method employs a hybrid Gaussian representation system that adaptively selects between 2D and 3D Gaussians
based on local geometric features. This approach reduces parameter
redundancy in planar and thin regions while preserving comprehensive 3D Gaussian representation in volumetric complex structures,
achieving a balance between geometric fidelity and computational
efficiency. The adaptive mechanism utilizes tangent plane parameterization and local curvature analysis to ensure optimal representation across diverse geometric surfaces.

4 EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS ANALYSIS
4.1 Experimental Setup
Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with
CUDA 11.3, using a PyTorch 1.12-based training framework. We
selected publicly available multi-view 3D reconstruction datasets,
including indoor scenes, complex geometric objects, and real-world
scenes with high-frequency textured materials. Comparison methods include original 3D Gaussian splatter, Mip-NeRF360, InstantNGP, Plenoxels, and an ablation control without the specular module (w/o SM). All methods were trained and tested under identical
data preprocessing, image resolution, and iteration counts.

CONCLUSION
The proposed 3DGS enhancement framework effectively overcomes
the limitations of the original 3DGS method in key technical dimensions by integrating hybrid Gaussian representations with tensor
SDF geometry-guided rendering. Specifically, the geometry-guided
hybrid Gaussian representation significantly reduces redundancy
in modeling planar and thin-layer structures through adaptive selection of 2D/3D Gaussian instances. TensoSDF’s global geometric constraints substantially enhance geometric consistency and
modeling accuracy in complex scenes. The synergistic interaction
between these two components collectively improves geometric
modeling precision, material realism, and scene adaptability.
Cross-dataset experiments further validate the method’s effectiveness: Compared to the original 3DGS and other representative
reconstruction-rendering benchmarks, the proposed framework
achieves significant improvements in geometric consistency metrics
and visual fidelity for complex materials. Simultaneously, through
Gaussian redundancy optimization and parallel processing, its rendering efficiency matches that of the original 3DGS, successfully
balancing “accuracy-realism-efficiency.” This provides a viable technical solution for high-quality 3D visual content demands in fields
such as virtual reality and digital twins.
Despite significant progress, further optimization remains possible. On one hand, the current hybrid Gaussian representation and
tensor SDF-guided mechanism may introduce additional storage
overhead. On the other hand, hybrid Gaussians exhibit suboptimal
performance in reconstructing certain special scenarios, such as
highly transparent environments. Addressing these two aspects
will be our key focus in subsequent work.
