Abstract
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) enable high-fidelity 3D reconstruction but suffer from per-scene optimization and long training times. Tensorial Radiance Fields (TensoRF) improve efficiency but still require per-scene training. We introduce Generalized Tensorial Radiance Fields (GTensoRF), a framework that learns multiple scenes simultaneously, achieving scalability and efficiency. GTensoRF trains on 100 scenes in 60 minutes on a single GPU, whereas NeRF requires 24+ hours per scene. Additionally, GTensoRF enables view-consistent, semantically meaningful 3D editing through optimized tensor components and density-based resampling. Evaluated on the Photoshapes dataset, GTensoRF significantly improves multi-scene generalization and efficiency over existing methods, including EditNeRF, which requires 48 hours per scene.
Overview of the GTensoRF pipeline. Stage 1: Learn scene-specific geometry (VMσ) and appearance (VMc) representations. Stage 2: These representations allow for novel scene edits by combining color from one scene and geometry from another. Stage 3: Semantic correspondences across scenes guides view-consistent 3D renderings.
1 Introduction
NeRF has revolutionized 3D scene reconstruction but is hindered by per-scene optimization, making it impractical for large-scale applications like AR/VR and interactive 3D modeling. GTensoRF overcomes these limitations by shifting from scene-specific to generalizable radiance fields, leveraging higher-order tensor decomposition to drastically reduce training time.
Where NeRF needs 24+ hours per scene, GTensoRF learns 100 scenes in under 60 minutes, enabling rapid deployment in real-world scenarios. Key contributions include:
•
Generalizable Radiance Fields – Optimizes multiple scenes concurrently, dramatically reducing training time.
•
View-Consistent 3D Editing – Ensures coherent object manipulation using semantic correspondences.
•
Optimized Resampling Strategy – Aligns density peaks for spatial and color consistency.
•
Selective Fine-Tuning – Updates appearance while preserving geometry for high-fidelity edits.
2 Methodology
This section outlines our technical contributions and provides implementation details necessary for reproducing our work.
2.1 Preliminaries
We build upon Tensorial Radiance Fields (TensoRF) [Chen et al. 2022], which uses tensor decomposition to model 3D scenes efficiently. Unlike traditional Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [Mildenhall et al. 2021], which rely on a single deep neural network, TensoRF employs low-rank tensor factorization, representing 3D coordinates (x, y, z) and view directions (θ, ϕ) through a combination of vectors and matrices. This decomposition enables faster and more compact scene representation:
σ,c=Gσ(x),S(Gc(x),d)
(1)
Illustration of our resampling strategies for scene alignment: (a) Uniform Sampling: Points are sampled evenly along the ray, which can lead to misalignment. (b) Strategy 2: Points are valid only if density exceeds a threshold in both scenes, without resampling. (c) Density-based Resampling: Points are adjusted based on density peaks in both scenes, improving consistency by aligning initial object intersections.
2.2 Generalized Tensorial Radiance Fields
We extend TensoRF to support multi-scene learning by initializing scene-specific low-rank tensors. This design allows the model to handle multiple environments in a single training session. Each scene’s tensors are optimized using a customized Adam optimizer, enabling parallelized learning and reducing computational costs compared to sequential scene training.
2.3 View-consistent 3D Editing
Our method decouples density and radiance components, enabling view-consistent generative editing. By recombining scene elements like geometry and appearance across different scenes, we can create new assets with consistent geometry and texture. For example, combining a density tensor Gσ(scene1) for the geometry of a chair from Scene 1 with a radiance tensor Gc(scene2) for its color from Scene 2 produces a new chair with coherent structural and visual properties:
σ,c=Gσ(scene1)(x),S(Gc(scene2)(x),d)
(2)
Our method ensures realistic multi-view consistency, which is challenging for traditional generative models. Additionally, it handles variability within object categories by recombining compatible density and radiance components, maintaining structural fidelity while enabling new visual styles.
Our 3D editing results before/after finetuning stage.
2.4 Mapping Strategies
To map pixels between two scenes, we experiment with three strategies: identity mapping, semantic correspondence-based mapping, and co-segmentation-based mapping. Identity mapping assumes direct pixel correspondence but lacks spatial alignment accuracy. Semantic correspondence-based mapping improves alignment using Vision Transformers (ViT) [Dosovitskiy 2020] and DINO [Amir et al. 2021], extracting dense semantic features to improve spatial matching. Co-segmentation-based mapping further refines alignment by segmenting images into semantically meaningful parts using DINO’s clustering, yielding finer correspondence and maintaining semantic boundaries.
2.5 Resampling Techniques
To align geometry and color between scenes, we implement three sampling strategies along corresponding rays (Figure 2). Initial uniform sampling fails due to misalignment, so we introduce a density-based resampling strategy. By identifying density peaks along each ray, we adjust sampling to align key points in both scenes, ensuring improved color-geometry consistency and more realistic fusion of visual attributes.
3 Results & Conclusion
Unlike generative models that struggle with structured scene manipulation, GTensoRF utilizes dense semantic correspondences and controlled appearance fine-tuning for efficient multi-scene learning and interactive 3D editing, as seen in Figure 3. This work lays the foundation for more efficient 3D scene manipulation techniques, potentially revolutionizing fields such as AR/VR, and interactive media production. For future work, we plan to perform extensive quantitative evaluations against state-of-the-art methods [Liu et al. 2021].
