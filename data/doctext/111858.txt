The GPU path of the gridless isosurface extraction (md_gto_iso_gpu), optimized over rounds r1–r34.
Each round was measured with viamd's Profiler on an RTX 3080 (Vulkan) and an M4 Pro (Metal). In r32 the
variants that lost were removed, so the branch now carries a single path. The full history and numbers
are in the commit messages.
The pipeline
Octree: tested from level 2.
Kernels: iso_octree; iso_octree_small for up to 4 functions; iso_octree_dform for densities as
phi^T D phi.
Kept nodes are emitted in Morton order through a prefix sum.
Brick slots and the brick hash are built on the device.
Corners: from a point list, with shared high-face corners. The kernel depends on the set:
iso_corners_list_f1v4: one function.
Tiled GEMM iso_gcorners_f16/32/64.
Wide GEMM w128/w192/y256: canonical sets of more than 64 functions.
iso_dcorners: the D form.
Flood (approximate): only for densities whose maxima are at the atoms
(md_gto_iso_field_set_maxima_at_atoms), with all weights ≥ 0. Seeds are the bricks whose corners
straddle the iso value, plus the bricks holding an atom. It matched the exact mesh in every measured case.
Mesh and projection:
Surface Nets cells and quads.
Relaxation on the lattice surface, then a Newton projection.
One orbital runs the whole projection in one launch (iso_project_list_f1).
A step whose remaining error is predicted far below the tolerance ends the vertex.
Output is regrouped per field and sheet.
Non-blocking job API: md_gto_iso_gpu_job_begin/poll/progress/end.
Three host waits per extraction, plus one per flood round.
Long jobs launch the corners and the vertices in chunks, each followed by a progress marker.
md_gto_iso_gpu_extract is the blocking form.
Switches left: AO cutoff (GTO_ISO_EVAL_EPS, default 1e-6), D form (GTO_ISO_DFORM, default off),
flood (GTO_ISO_FLOOD, default 1). Also md_gto_iso_gpu_switches_set.
Profiling: md_gto_iso_gpu_profile_set/print gives per-step times, counts and kernel statistics.
md_gpu: md_gpu_kernel_info_t.stats reports the driver's statistics for a compiled kernel: Vulkan
pipeline executable statistics, Metal threadgroup memory.

Densities beat the dense evaluation. The dense column is the evaluation only, without a mesh.
Orbitals at h = 0.2 stay slower than dense. The octree's bounds are loose for them because the AO terms
cancel: of 6,592 bricks kept for the mm HOMO, 704 have surface. Flooding is not safe for orbitals.
Flooding every field loses small lobes; that is measured in the Profiler series and in the
flood_sweep test.
Tests (lavapipe)
gto_iso_gpu_lib passes: matches_cpu_reference, fields_batch_like_alone, job_matches_extract,
repeated_field_shares_functions and flood_sweep.
It also passes with GTO_ISO_DFORM=1, GTO_ISO_CHUNKS=5 and GTO_ISO_FLOOD=0.
flood_sweep: the default differs from the exact mesh in 0 cases.
Metal was checked by the M4 Pro Profiler series. The meshes there match the 3080's vertex and triangle
counts.
For review
Kernels after shutdown: md_gto_iso_gpu_shutdown must be called before the device is destroyed. A
later md_gto_iso_gpu_initialize with a new device otherwise reuses the old kernels. One test tripped on
this. A guard in initialize (drop the kernels when the device changes) would be small.
Pipeline statistics: Vulkan now captures pipeline statistics for every kernel when the driver
supports it. This is useful for profiling, but you may prefer it behind a device-creation flag.
Experiment tests: the tests gated behind environment variables (taylor_brick_*, flood_experiment,
benchmark) are analysis tools from the experiments. They are kept but skipped by default.
Merge: master is merged into the branch (b5949ae), without conflicts. The build and the
gto_iso_gpu_lib tests pass on the merged tree. Merging this PR with a merge commit (not a squash)
keeps b5949ae on master, which is the commit viamd#174's ext/mdlib points at.
The viamd side is scanberg/viamd#174: the non-blocking extraction in iso_meshes, with a progress bar.

Octree: the shells' ranges over a node are made once per pass for up to 4
tiles of 64 functions (each lane owns a function of every tile) instead of
once per tile, and only for the shells that reach the node. A dropped shell
has every AO below 1e-10 over the node; fn_tail (2e-10 times the column's

Corners: the Cartesian monomials of a shell made at once per l in md_gto's
order, the AO normalization folded into the staged rows of C, those read as
float4; a single function accumulates the shell's polynomial and multiplies
by the radial part once. The groupshared rows are sized per F, so a narrow
kernel keeps its occupancy.

iso_eval: the radial part and its derivative once per (shell, vertex), not
once per AO. iso_surface_box: the padding found by doubling and bisection
over the monotone tails instead of 0.05 bohr steps (on the CPU and GPU
paths alike).

The register-form kernels (corners per brick, corners from the point list,
vertex values) read every shell of a chunk from groupshared memory: its
centre, cutoff, l, AO count and primitives are staged with its rows of C
(k_load_chunk, chunks packed to at most 256 primitives), so the inner loop
no longer waits on dependent global loads per shell. Each point skips the
shells out of its reach in all three.

Canonical terms (GTO_ISO_CANON_TERMS, default 1): when every term of every
field is w a, w a^2 or w a b with a even and b = a + 1 (orbitals, density
matrices, products of pairs), the set keeps them as three weights per field
and column. The epilogues then stage a tile's weights and every thread
applies them to its own accumulators: no rounds of 64 points over the
term list. The vertex kernel puts a vertex's four channels on neighbouring
lanes and shuffles the value channel to the gradient channels. The corner
values accumulate in memory over the tiles instead of in registers.

md_gpu: md_gpu_kernel_info reports the driver's statistics of a kernel
(Vulkan: VK_KHR_pipeline_executable_properties, captured where the driver
offers it; Metal: thread limit and threadgroup memory). The extraction
profile lists them for the octree, corner and vertex kernels it ran.

The set refuses shells of more than 64 primitives.

iso_gcorners_f{16,32,64} and iso_geval_f{16,32,64} (GTO_ISO_GEMM, default 1):
128 rows per group (corner points of the list; or 32 vertices x value and

functions bound each node with a thread per shell, its AOs' sums straight

iso_dcorners and iso_deval (GTO_ISO_DFORM, default 1, sets of densities
only): rho = phi^T D phi over the AOs that reach each group's box, whatever
the factorization's rank. A group (128 rows: corner points of the list, or
32 vertices x value and gradient channels) lists the shells reaching its
box, then over tiles J of 64 local AOs accumulates T_J = sum over chunks
I <= J of 16 AOs of Phi_I D'_IJ (D' = 2 D before the diagonal block: the
upper block triangle) as the GEMM of r8 does, and adds Phi_J . T_J with the
tile's AO values made again per column group; the gradient is
dPhi_J . T_J + Phi_J . dT_J. About A^2 / 2 multiply-adds per row for A local
AOs, against A x r in the factored form.

D is built at set creation from the fields' terms (with the AO
normalization folded in), one per distinct field, when every field is a
density of products and the basis has at most 1024 shells (the local list
in groupshared memory). The octree still bounds the factored form, so the
set's limit of 1024 functions remains.

iso_octree_dform (GTO_ISO_DFORM_OCTREE, default 1, with the D form): over
the AOs a node's box reaches, with pc their values at the centre and dev
bounds of their deviation from them over the box,
rho = pc^T D pc + 2 g . delta + delta^T D delta (g = D pc, |delta| <= dev),
so rho lies within rho_c +- (2 sum |g| dev + sum dev |D| dev), intersected
with |rho| <= m^T |D| m (m: the AOs' magnitude bounds; rho >= -slack for a
positive semidefinite D), plus what the dropped AOs (each below eps over the
box) can add: 2 (2 eps) sum m_mu sum_nu |D_mu nu| / |nrm_nu| and
(2 eps)^2 sum |D|. Rounding is covered as in the factored bound. Per node
four matrix-vector products over the local list, in blocks of 256 AOs. A
local list of more than 2048 shells keeps the node.

On amide (h 0.25) against the factored bound: 638 bricks instead of 660
(SCF density), 450 instead of 436 (transition), 603 instead of 479
(difference density: indefinite, where the factored form's per-function
intersection is tighter). Every crossing cell is still in a brick.

Sets of densities with more than 1024 functions no longer fail: they are
made without functions (factorless) and run the D form only. A field made
from a density matrix keeps the matrix (float), and the D form uses it
instead of rebuilding it from the factors. The local lists hold 2048 shells
(16-bit indices); a group of points whose list overflows fails the
extraction with a message.

TEST: GTO_ISO_FN_CAP lowers the 1024 (factorless sets on small systems).
The CPU comparison skips the rejected siblings the point list keeps.

Coefficient screening (GTO_ISO_COEF_SCREEN, default 1, sets of orbitals): a
shell is dropped where its largest coefficient (over the set's functions)
times its AOs is below eps, as the volume path screens, rather than where
its AOs alone are; a shell without coefficients is never listed. The bound's
tail per function becomes 2 eps n_AO. mol HOMO at h 0.2: 89 AOs reach a
brick instead of 112, 897 bricks instead of 887; on amide the corners stay
within 3.4e-6 of the CPU reference.

The D-form octree no longer needs the D-form evaluation: it bounds the
matrix's density, which the slack in the bound relates to the factored
one. On C60 (GTX 1060) the D-form octree took 12.7 ms against 50 ms and kept
fewer bricks, while the D-form evaluation was 3.6 times slower than the
factored GEMM, so its default is now off (sets beyond 1024 functions use it
regardless). Its shells read their primitives from groupshared memory only:
a select between that and the global copy issued the global load as well.

New D-form kernels (GTO_ISO_DFORM_KERNEL: 1 iso_dcorners2/iso_deval2, the
default; 2 iso_dcorners3/iso_deval3; 0 those of r9), for sets of densities
with GTO_ISO_DFORM and for every factorless set:

- D' is upper triangular by chunks of 16 AOs (2 D before a column's chunk,
  D on its diagonal block, 0 after), so the T of a column chunk is complete
  at its own row chunk and Phi . T is taken then from the AO values already
  in g_phi: a tile's AO values are no longer made a second time.
- The column chunks are waves (cg = tid / 32): a wave skips the row chunks
  after its own and a column chunk past the list's end (about half the
  multiply-adds where the list spans two tiles or less).
- Each chunk's first shell comes from a table made once per group, and the
  next chunk is staged while the current one is evaluated (two buffers):
  two barriers per chunk instead of five, no searches per chunk.
- The tile's D' is loaded into registers before the AO values are made;
  Phi . T is summed over the tiles in registers and reduced once.
- Phi . T reads the AO values again after a groupshared fence: without it
  the compiler kept the MAC loop's 16 float4 loads alive for it (ACO: 154
  VGPRs before scheduling, 112 with the fence).

Kernel 2 takes tiles of 128 columns in groups of 256 threads, two threads
per row making its AO values (the chunk's shells split by parity): half the
chunk steps per row for long lists, but 34 KB of groupshared memory (two
groups per GTX 1060 SM, one if the driver gives it more than 128
registers) against 30 KB (three).

On lavapipe, mol SCF density with the D form (min of 3 runs): corners 458
-> 406 (kernel 1) / 390 (kernel 2) ms at h 0.25, and 477 -> 290 / 277 ms
with the AO cutoff at 1e-10 (126 AOs per brick); vertex values 460 -> 302 /
293 and 495 -> 285 / 270 ms. The same meshes; on amide the corners stay
within 3.1e-5 of the CPU reference (max |f| 46), factorless sets too.

The chunk table holds 1024 chunks: more than 16384 AOs reaching a group
fail the extraction, as more than 2048 shells do.

… and shell (r33)

The vertex evaluation was 42% of the M4 Pro's mm density extraction (8.3 of 19.7 ms) and wasted work.
The GEMM kernels give each vertex four threads, one per channel (value, d/dx, d/dy, d/dz). Each of
those threads made every shell's radial part (its exponentials) and monomials for its own channel,
so three of every four exponentials were redundant. The corners have no such redundancy: each of
their threads is a point of its own.

VERT 2 (iso_geval_*_sr): one thread makes a vertex's four channels of a shell, so the radial part is
made once per vertex and shell. The monomials are made once, and each axis's derivatives in turn
(the registers held stay those of the per-channel form).
  - iso_geval_f*_sr: the vertex's four threads take every fourth shell of a chunk (all of them before).
  - iso_geval_w*_sr, iso_geval_y256_sr: the vertex's 16 threads take every 16th shell (before: every
    fourth, per channel).
The products and the epilogues are unchanged. Each value is the same expression as before, made by
another thread.

GTO_ISO_VSHARE=0|1 (md_gto_iso_gpu_switches_t.vshare, default 1) switches back to the per-channel
kernels, for the Profiler's comparison. The D form's vertices (iso_deval) are not changed.

Test vshare_matches: the per-channel and shared forms give the same triangles, to the bit, through
f32 (amide density), f64 (mol), w128 (amide + virtuals, 100 functions), w192 (170) and y256 (220).
