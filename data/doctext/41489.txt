We propose a novel technique for modeling and rendering a 3D
point cloud obtained from a set of photographs of a real 3D scene
as a set of textured elliptical splats. We first obtain the base splat
model by calculating, for each point of the cloud, an ellipse approximating locally the underlying surface. We then refine the
base model by removing redundant splats to minimize overlaps, and
merging splats covering flat regions of the point cloud into larger ellipses. We later apply a multi-texturing process to generate a single
texture atlas from the set of photographs, by blending information
from multiple cameras for every splat. Finally, we render this multitextured, splat-based 3D model with an efficient implementation of
OpenGL ES 2.0 vertex and fragment shaders which guarantees its
fluid display on handheld devices.
Turning these clouds into polygonal meshes through triangulation
techniques can alleviate these drawbacks, but the results are not,
in general, very realistic, due to the limited information available.
Consequently, splat-based solutions [Zwicker et al. 2001] have been
explored as an extension to conventional point clouds, capable of
preserving their flexibility while enhancing the visual feeling of the
models. A splat is an arbitrary-shaped (typically elliptical) patch
which approximates locally the underlying surface, and which is
sent to the renderer as a single graphic primitive. Unlike regular
point clouds, where every point is displayed as a single pixel or a
simple square (no matter the viewpoint), splat-based models reduce
the empty space among points and approximate better the surface,
hence lessening the aforementioned weaknesses.

Related Work and System Overview
The idea of rendering point clouds as surface patches instead of
simple pixels was introduced, among others, by the QSplat system [Rusinkiewicz and Levoy 2000] and the surface splatting technique [Zwicker et al. 2001], which both tried to reconstruct the
scene geometry using elliptical splats in object space. However,
they were only applied to very dense models with several million
points obtained with laser scanners or by manual modeling, that is,
with reasonably accurate 3D positions and colors.
3
Model Generation
3.1
Base Splat Model
The first approximation of the splat model is obtained by replacing each point (which is a mere isolated 3D sample) with a surface
patch that adapts better to the shape of the original object. In order
to infer information on the surface from which the point was sampled, neighboring points are considered. A fixed-radius neighbor
search was chosen since it also filters out noisy samples that are not
surrounded by enough nearby points. A speed-up of this procedure
is attained with the discretization of the 3D space into voxels, thus
narrowing down the number of candidate points to those located
inside the same voxel and the 26 adjacent ones.
Soon after, simplification methods started to arise to mitigate the
issues derived from having too many primitives: Pauly et al. [2002]
proposed a hierarchical clustering technique, but it did not take into
account the whole shape of the splats, only their locations; Wu and
Kobbelt [2004; 2005] first devised an optimized point cloud subsampling scheme, which did consider the splat shape to minimize
the number of primitives through a global hole-free optimization,
and later introduced the progressive splatting concept, where again
the full splat geometry was used to obtain different levels of detail
of the 3D model. Our system brings the added value of replacing
several small splats tiling (with overlaps) a “planar-ish” area with
a single larger splat, thus improving the model compactness and
rendering quality, particularly when multi-texturing is applied.
For each point pi ∈ IR3 in the input cloud (0 ≤ i < M , M being the total number of points), its neighbors within a distance r,
Nr (pi ), are identified and undergo Principal Component Analysis (PCA) [Jolliffe 1986] to estimate the normal and tangential directions of the local surface. The results include the average point
pi and three eigenvalues λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ λ3 associated to three eigenvectors w1 , w2 , w3 , which are the principal directions along the
point set [Pauly et al. 2002]. The plane yielding the best approximation of the local surface in the least-squares sense is hence found,
given by w1 and w2 , and the normal vector is defined as ni = w3 .

Regarding improvements on splat rendering, one of the first proposals was Phong splatting [Botsch et al. 2004], which similarly
to Phong shading associates to each splat a varying normal field,
instead of a single vector, to enhance the lighting result. Some
approaches to textured splats have also been published: Yang et
al. [2006] propose a view-dependent method where each splat has
several textures which are dynamically selected and blended as the
viewpoint changes; in contrast, Sibbing et al. [2013] assign to each
splat a single texture extracted from one of the 2D images and
perform a three-pass rendering to reduce color transitions through
blending. In our proposal, a single multi-texture atlas is generated
by blending the contribution of several images for each splat, hence
reducing potential inaccuracies due to misalignments or irregular
lighting conditions.

Splat Decimation and Fusion
The base splat model can be directly textured and rendered, but
probably not in mobile devices, or at least not smoothly, given its
high number of splats, most of which in fact overlap. Therefore,
it must be simplified, but in such a way that sharp details are preserved and the number of new holes is minimized. Our proposal
is partially based on the merging operators of progressive splatting [Wu et al. 2005], but we follow a two-stage decimation/fusion
process to reduce the computational cost of the algorithm and use
fewer larger (vs. many small) splats in planar regions, since texturing is then easier and provides better visual results. A coarse
simplification stage focusing on the splat centers is first carried out
to eliminate quickly as many nearby ellipses as possible, and then a
more detailed merging stage is applied to merge several ellipses.
For the cases where ellipses i and j have been found to overlap,
a minimum enclosing ellipsoid algorithm [Khachiyan 1996] is applied using, as usual, eight samples from each ellipse as the input
set of points. This method estimates the center and the semiaxes
of the minimum-area ellipse enclosing all the given points, with a
predefined error tolerance. We keep this center as the splat location
and scale the semiaxes with the flatness coefficient fi , since this
approach trims the resulting splats in the less planar areas of the
model, thus reducing potential inaccuracies.

Multi-texturing
Once the shape and location of the refined splat model is computed,
a multi-texturing process is applied to every splat to obtain a realistic rendering. This process is based on a simplified version of the
approach developed by Pagés et al. [2015], which merges the color
information provided by several cameras to produce a seamless textured model. Although originally designed for triangular meshes,
this system works as well for splats, even when some partially occlude each other because they cover the same region of the model,
and produces fine results with seamless transitions across surfaces.

Rendering in Mobile Devices
In terms of rendering, using splats instead of points not only gets
rid of the unrealistic billboard effect caused by typical square points
(which remain square regardless of the viewpoint), but also tends
to fill the holes due to sub-sampling. However, displaying splats
without meshing them previously is impossible in standard graphic
pipelines, which is why a specific implementation based on point
primitives has been developed with the support of the OpenGL ES
2.0 rendering library. This involved the design of specific GLSL
vertex and fragment shaders, whose details are summarized next.

A novel method to generate lightweight textured splat-based models from sparse 3D point clouds has been proposed. After a base
splat model is obtained through PCA of the neighborhood of each
point (which is built quickly thanks to voxelization), it is simplified
by removing overlapping splats and merging nearby ones covering the same (roughly) planar regions. A multi-texturing algorithm
is then run to generate a single texture atlas and texture coordinates for all splats so that, at rendering time, splats may be consistently textured, instead of uniformly colored each. Our system
manages to greatly improve the photo-realism of the rendered point
clouds, while reducing significantly their modeling size, which in
turn eases again the rendering in handheld devices, which typically
cannot handle smoothly heavy models.
