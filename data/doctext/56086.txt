Zixiang Xiong*, Senior Member, IEEE, Xiaolin Wu, Senior Member, IEEE, Samuel Cheng, Student Member, IEEE,

Abstract—We study lossy-to-lossless compression of medical
volumetric data using three-dimensional (3-D) integer wavelet
transforms. To achieve good lossy coding performance, it is
important to have transforms that are unitary. In addition to the
lifting approach, we first introduce a general 3-D integer wavelet
packet transform structure that allows implicit bit shifting of
wavelet coefficients to approximate a 3-D unitary transformation.
We then focus on context modeling for efficient arithmetic coding
of wavelet coefficients. Two state-of-the-art 3-D wavelet video
coding techniques, namely, 3-D set partitioning in hierarchical
trees (Kim et al., 2000) and 3-D embedded subband coding with
optimal truncation (Xu et al., 2001), are modified and applied
to compression of medical volumetric data, achieving the best
performance published so far in the literature—both in terms of
lossy and lossless compression.
Index Terms—3-D ESCOT, 3-D SPIHT, bit shifting, context
modeling, lossy-to-lossless compression, medical volumetric data.

The efficacy and elegance of wavelet-based embedded
image coding was first manifested by Shapiro’s embedded
zerotree wavelet (EZW) [5]. Later work by Said and Pearlman
on set partitioning in hierarchical trees (SPIHT) improved
upon EZW coding and applied successfully to both lossy [6]
and lossless compression [7] of images. The new JPEG-2000
standard is based on a similar scheme called embedded block
coding with optimal truncation (EBCOT) [8]. Inspired by
the success of wavelet image coding, several authors have
extended the EZW framework to three-dimensional (3-D)
medical volumetric data compression [9], [10]. One motivation
for these 3-D based approaches is to exploit the correlation that
exists among neighboring image slices for better compression.
Because of this, results in [9] and [10] are better than those
from both two-dimensional (2-D) approaches (e.g., transform
coding [3] and predictive coding [11]) and early work on
3-D wavelet-based medical image compression [12]. Another
reason for 3-D based approaches is that, with an integer wavelet
transform, one can generate a single embedded bitstream that
allows progressive lossy-to-lossless compression.
Recently, Kim et al. considered very low bit rate embedded
video coding in a 3-D SPIHT approach [1]; Xu et al. published
embedded video coding results better than MPEG-4s using
3-D embedded subband coding with optimal truncation (3-D
EBCOT) [2]. Building upon these works and those in [13], this
paper presents a comprehensive treatment of lossy-to-lossless
compression of medical volumetric data using 3-D integer
wavelet transforms. Two important issues—3-D integer wavelet
transform and efficient arithmetic coding [14], [15] of bit planes
of wavelet coefficients—are addressed. A unique and novel
result of this paper is a general 3-D integer wavelet packet
transform structure that allows implicit bit shifting of wavelet
coefficients to approximate a 3-D unitary transformation. This
new transform structure is used in our experiments whenever
it is necessary (e.g., when filters in [16] are used). While bit
plane coding of wavelet coefficients facilitates lossly to lossless
3-D compression, it is context modeling of wavelet coefficients
that ultimately determines the efficiency of arithmetic coding.
Because of this, a main thrust of this paper is on context
modeling in arithmetic coding. We present two 3-D context
modeling techniques. One is 3-D SPIHT [1], which is generalized from the celebrated SPIHT image coding algorithm [6];
the other is a modified version of the 3-D ESCOT technique
[2], which is a 3-D extension of EBCOT [8]. These techniques

lossy and lossless compression.
There are several other works related to ours. For example,
Dragotti et al. applied 3-D SPIHT for compression of multispectral images [17]; Kim and Pearlman used stripe-based
SPIHT for volumetric medical data compression with low
memory [18]; Schelkens et al. worked on compression of
volumetric medical data with cube-splitting [19]. In addition,
Lu, Kim and Pearlman studied compression of ECG signals
with one-dimensional (1-D) SPIHT [20]. However, the most
relevant work seems to be [21], in which the authors proposed a
3-D context-based EZW (3-D CB-EZW) algorithm for medical
image compression. Since 3-D integer wavelet transforms
are used, progressive compression becomes possible in 3-D
CB-EZW. However, the problem of efficient progressive lossy
compression is not treated there. One issue with integer wavelet
transforms is that they might not be unitary. The quantization
error in the wavelet domain is, thus, not equal to the mean
squared error (MSE) in the time or spatial domain. This
makes it difficult to keep track of the final coding error in the
quantizer. It is, therefore, desirable to scale the integer wavelet
coefficients to make the transform approximately unitary
before quantization. Otherwise the lossy coding performance
will be compromised. For example, our experiments show that
3-D SPIHT and 3-D ESCOT outperform 3-D CB-EZW in the
lossy mode by 2–3 dB and 3–4 dB on average, respectively.
This is partially due to the fact that the transforms we use
are approximately unitary. In addition, our lossless coding
performance is about 10% better than those reported in [21]
because our 3-D context modeling technique is more effective
than that in 3-D CB-EZW.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Lifting-based
integer wavelet transform is covered in Section II, in which we
introduce a general 3-D integer wavelet packet transform structure that allows implicit bit shifting of wavelet coefficients to approximate a 3-D unitary transformation. Section III is devoted to
context modeling of 3-D wavelet coefficients, and in particular
its application to wavelet coding of volumetric medical data. Experimental results, in both lossy and lossless cases, are given in
Section IV, and conclusions drawn in Section V.

The same wavelet filters are used in all three dimensions to

III. EMBEDDED CODING OF INTEGER WAVELET COEFFICIENTS
A. Three-Dimensional SPIHT
The 2-D SPIHT algorithm [6], like the EZW coding algorithm
[5], views wavelet coefficients as a collection of spatial orientation trees, with each tree consisting of coefficients from all subbands that correspond to the same spatial location in an image
[see Fig. 4(a)]. It uses multipass “zerotree” coding to transmit
the largest wavelet coefficients (in magnitude) first. A set of tree
coefficients is significant if the largest coefficient magnitude
in the set is greater than or equal to a certain threshold (e.g.,
a power of two); otherwise, it is insignificant. Similarly, a coefficient is significant if its magnitude is greater than or equal
to the threshold; otherwise, it is insignificant. In each pass the
significance of a larger set in the tree is tested first: if the set
is insignificant, a binary “zerotree” bit is used to set all coefficients in the set to zero; otherwise, the set is partitioned into
subsets (or child sets) for further significance tests. After all coefficients are tested in one pass, the threshold is halved before
the next pass.
The underlying assumption of SPIHT coding is that most images can be modeled as having decaying power spectral densities. That is: if a parent node in the wavelet coefficient tree is
insignificant, it is very likely that its descendents are also insignificant. The zerotree symbol is used very efficiently in this
case to signify a spatial subtree of zeros.
When the thresholds are powers of two, SPIHT coding can
be thought of as a bit plane coding scheme. It encodes one bit

use 3-D SPIHT as an entropy coder. The main modification

bitstream is generated after coding all bit planes of all integer
wavelet coefficients. Decoding any truncated version of this
lossless bitstream gives a lossy version of the original volumetric data with quality commensurate with the bit rate.

Borrowing ideas from the EBCOT image coding algorithm
[8], the original 3-D ESCOT algorithm was designed to achieve
better coding performance for scalable wavelet video compression. Rate scalability is accomplished by independently
coding wavelet coefficients in different subbands using bit
plane coding. The main contribution in extending the 2-D
EBCOT algorithm to 3-D ESCOT lies in 3-D context formation
and modeling for arithmetic coding. In the following, we
provide a high-level summary of the 3-D ESCOT algorithm and
highlight its difference from the 3-D SPIHT algorithm. Readers
interested in the algorithmic details are referred to [24].
While the basic components of bit plane coding, namely, significance coding, sign coding and magnitude refinement, are the
same in 3-D ESCOT and 3-D SPIHT, major differences between
the two are as follows.

magnitude refinement and normalization—to process

pass, which involves significance coding and sign coding,
processes coefficients that are highly likely to become
significant in the current bit plane. It typically achieves
a higher R-D ratio than the magnitude refinement pass.
The normalization pass finishes coding bits left over by
the first two passes. This processing order of the three
fractional bit planes, thus, follows the decreasing order of
their perceived R-D significance levels. It ensures an R-D
optimized embedded bitstream for each subband. Note
that with context models built differently for significance
coding, sign coding and magnitude refinement, processing significance coding and magnitude refinement
in different fractional bit planes comes naturally in 3-D
ESCOT.

to save in medical image databases different sets of regression
coefficients and quantizer parameters that are optimized for
specific image classes. These customized class-dependent
codec settings can be synchronized between encoder and
decoder by file header information.

In this paper, we have proposed a coding system for lossy to
lossless compression of medical volumetric data. The system
uses a general 3-D integer wavelet packet transform structure
to approximate a 3-D unitary transformation so that bit plane
coding leads to good R-D performance. The front end of the
system adopts a memory-constrained integer wavelet transform
implementation. At the back end, two state-of-the-art entropy

coding techniques for 3-D wavelet video compression, 3-D
SPIHT and 3-D ESCOT, are modified to suit 3-D wavelet
coding of volumetric medical data. The new techniques were
demonstrated to have both better lossly and lossless performances than previously reported.
Our work has advanced the art in [21] and made following
progress: our proposed 3-D integer wavelet transform structure,
followed by proper bit shifts, improves the lossy compression performance; the memory-constrained integer wavelet
transform implementation eliminates PSNR drops at GOP
boundaries; the 3-D ESCOT entropy coder achieves the best
lossy and lossless compression of medical volumetric data
so far.
As seen from the compression performance comparison in
Table X, we have achieved the goals of lossy compression (reduced storage space or transmission time) while simultaneously
allowing lossless compression. With DICOM expecting to adopt
JPEG-2000 as the standard for medical image compression in
the near future, we hope that our work will stimulate interests in
standardizing compression method for medical volumetric data
based on 3-D integer wavelet transforms.
