Sorting-free Pre-integrated Projected Tetrahedra
Naohisa Sakamoto1, Takuma Kawamura2, Hiroshi Kuwano2 and Koji Koyamada1

ABSTRACT
Conventionally, researchers have used surface-based
visualization techniques to understand such a large-scale irregular
volume using sectional slices or boundary geometry, since current
irregular volume renderers cannot handle it. Although the
visualization results can provide with useful information, they are
limited to the surface. Before an adequate surface can be
determined, it is often necessary to grasp the spatial distribution of
the related physical quantities. Although a volume rendering
technique is desirable for such purposes, it is difficult for a
conventional volume rendering technique to deal with a largescale irregular-volume dataset. This difficulty arises since the
techniques require the entire volume dataset to be stored in a
single computational node in order to calculate the visibility of the
mesh cell at each viewpoint. It is apparent that such a requirement
is impractical, since the whole computational space is divided into
multiple sub-volumes when a large scale simulation model is
calculated using multiple computing nodes.
In this paper, we present a sorting-free technique for volume
rendering of an irregular volume dataset. Although the importance
of our particle-based volume rendering is widely recognized, its
low image quality has been pointed out. Especially when the
viewing point is located near the volume dataset, the footprint of
the particle may be visible. To solve the problem, we develop a
sorting-free technique which does not use particles and employs a
projected tetrahedra with a pre-integration. In general, a projection
technique requires the visibility sorting at each viewing point. To
make the technique sorting-free, we regard the volume rendering
as a stochastic process in which a sampling point occurs along a
viewing ray at the probability of the opacity. Thus, we develop a
sorting-free approach that controls the fragment rendering by
using the opacity value to calculate a rendered image. Through
this approach, a projected tetrahedra technique can be successfully
made sorting-free. We applied our technique to large-scale
irregular volumes in order to confirm its effectiveness.
There exists a strategy in which each sub-volume is rendered
and the resulting sub-images are composited in visibility order.
However, this strategy will fail when the shape of the sub-volume
is concave, since in that case the visibility order cannot be defined
Thus, a sorting-free irregular volume rendering technique would
be a good candidate for this purpose and would be well suited to
cell-by-cell processing, which does not require entire cells to be
stored in a single memory space.

General Terms
In 2005, the Particle-based Volume Rendering (PBVR)
technique, which is an efficient approach for rendering volume
datasets without the need for visibility sorting, was proposed [2].
The technique represents a given volume dataset as a set of
particles and can be employed efficiently to render an irregular
volume dataset [3]. Although it shows good scalability for
handling a large-scale irregular volume, the current drawback of
PBVR is the image quality. Especially, the particle’s foot print
may appear when the viewing point is located inside the volume.
Moreover, it is necessary to generate a large number of particles
in order to obtain the high resolution image.

1. INTRODUCTION
To achieve a practical and effective solution to a large-scale
simulation model, an advanced general-purpose computational
mechanics system for a large-scale finite element method (FEM)
simulation, such as "ADVENTURE", was previously developed
[1]. Due to its excellent performance in solving ultra-large scale
mechanics problems and its open source license, the simulation
system is currently used by a variety of industrial and academic
groups. It is also becoming popular in computational fluid
dynamics (CFD) and computational structural mechanics (CSM).
This trend has created a new challenge in irregular volume
rendering, since a large-scale FEM simulation model can now be
calculated in a distributed computing environment. In this case, an
irregular volume dataset is composed of several sub-volumes,
since the full simulation model cannot be fitted into a single
computing node.

The development of techniques for rendering irregular volumes
has remained a major challenge in the visualization community.

Such datasets consist mainly of scalar data defined on collections
of irregularly-ordered cells, whose shapes are not necessarily

Roettger et al. [9] pointed out that the memory bandwidth
required for visibility sorting becomes the limiting factor, so they
proposed an algorithm that requires no visibility sorting for cells
of irregular volume. Since their optical model considers only
emissions, however, its application is limited to the visualization
of gaseous phenomena. Csebfalvi proposed a sorting-free volume
rendering technique [10][11] that can be categorized as an X-ray
volume rendering approach, although their optical model
considers only absorption. Zhou et al. proposed a sorting-free
rendering technique implemented with additional terms that help
to provide enhanced depth cues without visibility sorting [12].
Although it has achieved 20 fps for 17.6 million tetrahedra, their
optical model did not consider absorption effects.
Irregular volume rendering can involve an image-order or
object-order algorithm. The image-order algorithm requires the
entire volume dataset, including the cell adjacency information, to
be stored in the main memory. This requirement can become a
disadvantage when a large-scale volume dataset is processed. The
object-order algorithm is more promising, since the cell-by-cell
approach can be easily implemented. In general, a volume
rendering technique uses the density emitter model, in which the
3D scalar field is characterized as a varying density emitter with a
single level of scattering. This model was proposed by Sabella [4]
and is related to a particle system in which the particles are
sufficiently small and of low albedo. A conventional volume
rendering technique models the density of particles, not the
particles themselves, which makes the visibility sorting of volume
cells indispensable in the rendering algorithm.
To solve the above problems recognized in previous sortingfree volume rendering techniques, we returned to the density
emitter model and presented a basic idea for this approach. The
proposed particle-based volume rendering (PBVR) technique
represents the 3D scalar fields as a set of particles, and considers
both emission and absorption effects [2]. The particle density is
derived from a user-specified transfer function and is used to
estimate the number of particles to be generated in a given volume
dataset. Since the particles can be considered fully opaque, no
visibility sorting processing is required during the rendering
process; this is advantageous from a distributed processing
perspective. The first version of PBVR limits the locations of the
particles to a regular grid [13]. This limitation results in the poor
image quality. The second version introduces a more precise
model of particles to improve the image quality [3]. It
successfully generated an image that is equivalent to a ray-caster’s
image. Thereafter, we proposed a distributed PBVR by using a PC
cluster system for large-scale irregular volume datasets [14] and a
hardware accelerated PBVR using the NVIDIA CUDA
architecture [15]. In [16], we proposed a dynamic LOD control
technique based on ensemble averaging for large-scale irregular
volume dataset and the image quality can be improved by
applying the metropolis sampling in a cell. In these versions, since
the particle density field is linearly interpolated in a cell by using
density values defined at each cell vertex, the particle generation
can be incorrect when the distribution of the transfer function
changes drastically. To overcome this problem, we proposed a
layered sampling method that generates particles at each interval
of a piece-wise linear transfer function in a volume dataset [17].
Although, currently, PBVR can efficiently generate an improved
image from a large-scale irregular volume dataset, the footprint of
particle may be visible when the viewing point is located inside
the volume. To complement the drawback, we need to develop a
sorting-free technique which does not use particles.
After Shirley et al. [5] proposed a projected tetrahedra (PT)
algorithm for generating a volume-rendered image by using
tetrahedral cells, many irregular volume rendering techniques
have been proposed based on the object-order approach. In the PT
algorithm, each tetrahedral cell is projected onto the screen in
visibility order, from back to front, to build up a semitransparent
image. This approach may generate a low-quality image when a
transfer function becomes non-linear. To solve the problem of
image quality, Engel et al. [6] proposed a novel texture-based
volume rendering approach that achieves the image quality of the
best post-shading approaches by integrating non-linear transfer
functions in a preprocessing step. This approach is suitable for
new flexible consumer graphics hardware and provides high
image quality even for a non-linear transfer function with high
frequencies, without the performance overhead caused by
rendering additional sampling points.
Although the above approach can contribute to highperformance calculation and high-quality image generation, the
visibility sorting of irregular volume cells remains indispensable.
Currently, two of the major directions in irregular volume
rendering involve relaxing the processing requirement for the
visibility sorting and developing a technique that does not use
sorting.
To address the former possibility, Callahan et al. [7] developed
an integrated visibility sorting technique called HAVS (HardwareAssisted Visibility Sorting). In this technique, the centroids of the
cell faces are first sorted in order to generate a rough visibility
sorting; pixel fragments generated from rasterized faces are used
along with the K-buffer in order to increase the accuracy.
Although HAVS achieved 1.3 fps for 1.4 million tetrahedra, it
generates some artifacts when the k-buffer is not sufficiently
large. It is difficult to determine the optimal value for k. The
memory space for sorting cell faces is about twice that for
tetrahedral cells.

In this section, we detail the implementation of our sorting-free
projected tetrahedral technique based on pre-integration.
4.1 Pre-integration
We describe a sorting-free projected tetrahedral technique that
employs pre-integration classification for high-quality rendering.
In order to achieve high image quality without sacrificing much
performance, pre-integration classification [16] is applied to the
rendering stage. The pre-integration computes the lookup tables
mapping three integration parameters (scalar value at the front: sf,
scalar value at the back: sb, and length of the segment: d) to the
pre-integrated colors C and opacity α. This table is calculated
according to the transfer function and stored in the form of a 3D
texture that provides the mappings C=C(sf,sb,d) and α=α(sf,sb,d)
(see Figure 2). In this implementation, the pre-integrated color has
been divided by the corresponding opacity since it is opacityweighted.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed a sorting-free approach for irregular
volume rendering and applied it to a projected tetrahedral
technique with pre-integration. We applied our technique to CFD
simulation results of a size that has never been visualized before
by using a volume rendering technique. As we demonstrated, SPT
becomes a feasible choice for irregular volume rendering when
the number of particles becomes large or when particle generation
becomes frequent in PBVR. We also conducted a thorough
experimental measurement in order to construct the performance
model. From this model, we could verify that SPT and PBVR can
handle a large-scale volume dataset in comparison with HAVS. In
future studies, we are planning to improve the performance and
the scalability of the SPT using a distributed rendering
environment.
The irregular volume dataset of the CSM simulation, which is
hereinafter referred to as “Pump”, is the result of an elastostatic
analysis of weight with one hundred million degrees of freedom
using a PC cluster system. The Pump dataset is composed of
26,289,770 tetrahedral cells with 4,697,743 nodes, and is
generated from the CSM computation by a finite element method
solver developed by the ADVENTURE project [1].
The CFD data, which are hereinafter referred to as “Oral”, are
composed of hexahedral cells and represent the results of an oral
airflow simulation. In the simulation model, the oral cavity shape
of the dental fricative was obtained by a Cone Beam CT scanner
[18]. Using volume datasets derived from image slices, the oral
cavity can be extracted using two threshold CT values; the
required hexahedral cells are constructed for a large eddy CFD
simulation. The original Oral dataset is composed of 2,646,268
hexahedral cells with 2,725,232 nodes. Since SPT currently
supports only a tetrahedral cell, we subdivide a hexahedral cell
into five tetrahedral cells in a cell-by-cell manner. As a result, we
used the Oral dataset composed of 2,646,268 x 5 = 13,231,340
tetrahedral cells in our experiment.
