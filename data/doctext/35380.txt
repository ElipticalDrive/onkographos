What
Phase 2c: blocks are drawn. Every block is a beveled cube with its own panel, a dark groove where it meets its neighbors and ambient occlusion in the corners, in four material tiers (Scrap, Composite, Alloy, Energy-Infused) with smooth, detailed procedural tiles. Chunks re-mesh nearest the camera first inside a 3 ms budget.
Nothing places a block yet (that is 2d), so two demo switches raise a structure before the first frame: /?demo=outpost and /?demo=swatch. Slabs, wedges and the lighting pass against references 04 and 05 are 2e.
Headless Chromium on software GL at 1280 by 720, Balanced. Each reproduces from its URL (the table is in docs/art-direction.md), for example /?demo=outpost&shot=outpost&quality=balanced&hud=0&stats=0&freeze=1.
What changed
The mesher (src/world/voxels/mesher.ts, pure, no three.js). A block is up to four kinds of piece: a panel per exposed face, a strip per exposed edge, a triangle per exposed corner, and a small cap where four blocks meet along an edge. Faces and chamfers a neighbor hides are dropped, so a wall is one closed surface with a groove between blocks. No greedy merging, as decided. The caps were found by the tests: without them four chamfered blocks around an edge leave a diamond shaft open. The tests weld the vertices of random worlds and check that every edge is used exactly twice in opposite directions, across chunk borders too. The hot path allocates nothing.
Tier materials (src/engine/glsl/structure-bake.glsl.ts, texture-bake.ts). The tiles are baked on the GPU, the first time a block is meshed, into array textures: per tier, four tile layouts of albedo with height, and gloss, metal and glow. Each face of each block picks a layout from its cell hash and turns it one of eight ways, so a wall never reads as one stamp repeated. A block's material id picks its tile, so a Phase 5 upgrade changes one number and the block re-meshes. No image files, nothing in CREDITS.md.
The renderer (src/world/render/voxel-render.ts, structure-material.ts, src/world/voxels/remesh-queue.ts). One material and one draw call per chunk that holds a block. The dirty chunks go into a RemeshQueue, nearest the camera first, and are meshed until 3 ms of the frame are spent, always at least one chunk.
Plants give way to blocks. Grass rooted inside or on top of a block, and a tree whose trunk a block stands in, are skipped when the vegetation is assembled (rootBuried, trunkBlocked in scatter.ts). It is redone when the camera moves a meter or VoxelWorld.revision says a block changed, so 2d only has to place blocks.
?demo=outpost and ?demo=swatch, and four viewpoints for them (outpost, gate, swatch, closeup). Each demo places its blocks one at a time through checkPlace, so it also exercises the placement rules (the unit tests replay the edits and count no refusals).
Worth a look
Steel at golden hour. The deep orange key turned Alloy tan, and a wall of it read as wooden crates in the game's default light. A metallic tile's diffuse term now takes the key only part of the way to its grey, and glints keep the full color. Composite still reads olive at golden hour. Whether that and the tile style suit references 04 and 05 is the 2e lighting pass.
Frame budget. The outpost is 4 chunks and 10 thousand triangles. The outpost view is 37 draws and 200k triangles on Low, 114 and 879k on Balanced, 153 and 1.87M on Ultra, against the valley's 111 and 855k on Balanced. A chunk is a draw in every pass, shadows included. There is no GPU in the sandbox, so the frame rate with blocks is unmeasured; /?bench=1&demo=outpost is the run to repeat on a real machine.
Tile memory and the first block. The baked tiles are 16 layers at 512 by 512 (256 on Low), two arrays with mipmaps: about 43 MB of video memory at Balanced and Ultra and 11 MB on Low. They are baked the first time a block is meshed (Stage.structure, VoxelRenderer.warmUp), so a world with no blocks pays nothing; baking them at start-up added 9 seconds to every page load under software rendering. On a real GPU the bake is a few milliseconds, but the first draw also compiles the shader, so 2d calls warmUp when the player enters Build Mode.
Meshing is on the main thread. A typical chunk takes 0.3 to 1.3 ms in the sandbox; a very dense one (a hollow room of 28 thousand triangles) takes about 7 ms, and one chunk is always meshed per frame, so it can cost a frame. The mesher is pure and can move to a Worker if Phase 6 needs it.
Decisions are in docs/decisions.md under "Phase 2c"; the budget table and known gaps are in docs/art-direction.md.
Checks
npm run check: typecheck, ESLint with the layer rules, Prettier, 344 unit tests and the build all pass. New tests cover mesher geometry (closed surface, winding, ambient occlusion, hidden faces, tile layouts), the remesh queue order, the demo structures, hiding plants, and the new ?demo switch.
npx playwright test --workers=1 against the built site (the CI setting): 31 tests, about 7 minutes under software rendering, including five new ones: the outpost is meshed, drawn and free of shader errors; an unknown demo name builds nothing; a bare ?demo raises the outpost; a placed block is meshed and a broken one is dropped; swapping the world rebuilds the demo without leaking meshes. They also check that a page with no blocks has not baked the tiles.
