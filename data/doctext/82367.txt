Note
This is a working branch, not the final diff intended for upstream Bevy. It still contains debugging and validation features (shader debug defines, a reference path tracer, GPU timing and readback dumps, and environment-variable switches in the example) that would be removed or split out before a PR to bevyengine/bevy main.
Summary
Volumetric clouds for the atmosphere plugin, combining several published techniques:
Atmosphere and lighting: Hillaire 2020 (sky-view/aerial-perspective LUTs; its multiple-scattering transfer fraction, applied to clouds through a per-voxel escape probability). Frostbite (Hillaire 2016) energy-conserving integration and temporal reprojection.
Shaping: Nubis Evolved (Schneider 2022) dimensional profile and Perlin-Worley shape noise; Nubis Cubed (2023) alligator detail noise and distance-adaptive stepping.
Phase: cloud droplet Mie phase from Jendersie & d'Eon 2023.
Shadows: UE5-style cloud shadow map (front depth, mean extinction, max optical depth), shared by the sky, ground and mesh lighting.
Cirrus: a thin layer after Hogan & Kew 2005 (−5/3 fBm, log-normal ice water, fall streaks).
API: VolumetricCloudLayer and ThinCloudLayer components on the atmosphere entity, with an AtmosphereWind profile.
References
S. Hillaire, A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique, EGSR 2020.
S. Hillaire, Physically Based Sky, Atmosphere and Cloud Rendering in Frostbite, SIGGRAPH 2016 Physically Based Shading course.
S. Hillaire, Physically Based and Scalable Atmospheres in Unreal Engine, SIGGRAPH 2020 Physically Based Shading course; Unreal Engine volumetric cloud shadow map.
A. Schneider, Nubis, Evolved: Real-Time Volumetric Clouds for Skies, Environments, and VFX, SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances in Real-Time Rendering.
A. Schneider, Nubis³: Methods (and madness) to model and render immersive real-time voxel-based clouds, SIGGRAPH 2023 Advances in Real-Time Rendering.

Cloud shadow map: history that holds a static map exactly (audit 1 va…
…
465ea42
```
…lidation)

Validating the fixed shadow quadrature (audit 1) against a dense trace (512 samples) in a frozen
scene found the shipped map far from its own trace at the default interleave of 4: transmittance
0.145 too low on average, 0.155 mean error, against 0.025 for the trace itself. Three causes:

- The reprojection of untraced texels interpolated optical depth linearly. Cumulus depths are
  ~50, so a few percent of weight from a cloud texel darkened a clear neighbour, every frame
  until its next trace. It now interpolates transmittance (mean-preserving).
- The history blend mixed depths linearly too (darker than the mixed transmittances); it blends
  transmittance as well, which also recovers faster from a history reset.
- A static history still moved: the inverse warp's GPU sinh/asinh error shifted texels near
  the rim by up to a tenth of a texel per frame, and the round trip through absolute positions
  at planetary radius lost ~0.5 m. The reprojection is now anchor-relative and differential
  (uv plus the change of the inverse warp), so a static map maps to itself.

Converged (frame 1200), fixed quadrature: mean error 0.040, bias +0.02, flicker 0.0006.
ATM_DBG_SHADOW_JITTER rotates the sample phases per texel and frame and blends optical depth
(the unbiased average of noisy depth estimates): mean error 0.019 and 40 % of the edge error,
with 0.02 frame-to-frame noise in edge transmittance. The fixed phases stay the default.
```

Clouds: physical detail at every distance (multi-scale thermals, foot…
…
e6346bf
```
…print closure)

The physical profiles' detail was Nubis's up-res with Nubis's art constants on top of the
two-octave bubble scheme: it removed ~10 % of the cloud fraction, raised the mean liquid water by
~22 % (pow 0.6), and added almost no surface roughness (D_s 2.09 vs 2.32).

The scheme's moisture fluctuation is now Y = g + r (b1 + s b2 + s^2 b3): g the Gaussianized
shape noise, b nine octaves of thermal bubbles (cells 1/8 .. 1/2048 of the shape tile) with
amplitude size^(2/3), so level sets have dimension 3 - H = 7/3 (Siebesma & Jonker 2000). A baked
128^3 texture (FbmNoiseTexture::scheme_noise, binding 35) holds g, its variance, b and its
variance; its mips (moments downsample) hold the variance a footprint leaves unresolved, and the
shader samples it at the tile and at 1/8 and 1/64 of it. Each sample stands for its pixel
footprint: the Gaussian closure of the statistical scheme (Sommeria & Deardorff 1977),
E[(mu + sigma Z - t)+], gives the footprint's mean liquid water; near the camera sigma -> 0 and
it is the resolved threshold. The scheme table integrates the measured bubble distribution
against g. Nubis and envelope profiles keep Nubis's up-res.

Validation (GPU-baked texture, .clouds-handoff/noise_study/physical_detail):
  cloud fraction vs the presence lookup within 1-3 %, mean excess within 1 %;
  footprint-mean liquid water at 24-384 m: bias <= 2.5 %, rms <= 4 % (re-normalizing and
  thresholding the filtered field: up to 51 % rms);
  40 m grid, 12.8 km domain: D_s 2.19-2.40 (LES 2.32), size density -1.77..-1.80 (LES -1.70,
  observed -1.9..-2.0), dominant size 460-840 m (BOMEX ~700 m), holes < 1 %;
  projected / maximum cloud fraction 1.68-1.76 (BOMEX LES 1.98-2.54: still too vertically
  coherent).
Cost (Medium, M5): clouds_raymarch hero 4.7 -> 5.9 ms, in the layer 7.0 -> 7.3 ms; cloud_field
1.1 -> 0.8 ms; with ~1.5x the screen cloud cover. The finer taps are fetched only near the
cloud surface; the closure is zero beyond 3 sigma below the threshold.

detail_noise_scale no longer affects the physical profiles; detail_strength scales the bubbles.
Known: edges close up are softer than Nubis's sharpened ones (liquid water linear in the
excess); needs an observed edge profile.
```

Clouds: mixed-shell liquid water at cumulus edges, fitted to aircraft…
…
ec420fc
```
… composites

Liquid water linear in the scheme's excess made a tent across each cloud: along horizontal
legs through clouds at least 200 m wide (the selection of Wang, Geerts & French 2009), it reached
0.41 of the interior value at x* = -0.2 and 0.25 at -0.1, against 0.88 and 0.80 in their
composite of 1624 aircraft penetrations of cumulus (J. Atmos. Sci. 66, 3660, Fig. 5a: flat in the
interior, dropping within ~10 % of the radius of the edge).

Liquid water is now min(Y - t, c)+ / c times a magnitude: linear in the excess over a mixed
shell of depth c, flat in the core. c = 0.5 x the mean excess, fitted to the composite: rms 0.050
over x* in [-0.3, 0] at cloud fractions 0.05-0.2 (0.414 for linear; 1 - exp(-e/e_s) best 0.079,
tanh 0.067). The footprint closure stays exact: E[min(e, c)+] = E[(e)+] - E[(e - c)+]. The
scheme row now holds (t, c, 1 / E[min(Y - t, c) / c], 1), so the in-cloud mean liquid water
stays the lookup's (0.996-1.003 on the baked texture); the bubble moments move to the
distribution rows. Core samples beyond the shell skip the finer taps.

Close up, the look changes little: the remaining contrast to Nubis's sharpened density is mostly
lighting (flatter shaded sides) and a fringe of tens of metres, as the composite has.
GPU timing not re-measured (GPU shared with other work).
```

```
CloudLayerOf(atmosphere) / CloudLayers (linked spawn): VolumetricCloudLayer and ThinCloudLayer
on any entity related to an atmosphere are its layers; layer components on the atmosphere
entity itself still count, so single-layer scenes are unchanged. Reverses the Oct 16 decision

  Compositing loops over the thin layers lowest first (above the camera: behind, nearest first;
  below: in front, farthest first); the shadow map's alpha sums their similarity-reduced optical
  depths; the generating field is shared, decorrelated per layer by an offset.
- Wind: the atmosphere's AtmosphereWind for every layer (the layer kinds no longer require their
  own, which would have shadowed it); advance_cloud_wind follows CloudLayerOf.
- Temporal histories reproject thin-layer pixels with the lowest thin layer's motion.
- Example: ATMOSPHERE_SECOND_THIN=1 adds a cirrostratus layer entity at 11.5 km.
```

```
A CloudInfluence on an entity with a Transform edits the weather around its position: a
rectangle or ellipse (local x/z, yaw from the transform) with a feathered edge, a blend mode
(Normal, Add, Subtract, Multiply, Max, Min; mix(base, mode(base, value), opacity x mask)), an
order, and optional channel values: art (coverage, type, density scale) and physical
(cloud-fraction scale, air mass, droplet number, base offset). Influences are anchored in the
world: the compose pass maps each window texel back from the weather map's rotated, advected
frame to the world point it feeds, so the procedural weather drifts with the wind underneath a
fixed hole or region. Up to 16 per view (sorted by order, warn beyond), extracted into
ExtractedCloudInfluences and packed per view into the compose pass's uniform in atmosphere space.
Everything that samples the weather (clouds, shadow map, cloud field, LUTs, probes) sees them
inside the 64 km window.
```

```
With the physical profiles every density function (fine, coarse bound, the lean's base) reads
one column weather (cloud_column_weather): the weather window's physical channels scale the
```

```
Multiply scattered sunlight in thick cloud from a diffusion solve on the cloud field grid
(128 x 128 x 32): D = 1 / (3 sigma'), source (1 + g') sigma' T'_sun, Marshak flux at cloud
```

```
The field follows the camera in 375 m blocks. The warm-started diffusion solution and its

in the SDF's own grid, which switches three frames after the extinction.
```
