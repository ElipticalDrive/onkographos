I. INTRODUCTION
Explosion and impact problems have important practical engineering value in the safety protection field; the phenomena of shock
waves, jets, penetration, and fragmentation generated by explosions
and impacts are the focus of researchers. However, such problems
usually involve multiple scales, large deformations, fragmentation,
and material nonlinearity. Explosive experiments are not only very
dangerous but also produce a lot of smoke and fire, and in practice, it
is difficult to observe and record the experimental process. Both theoretical and experimental studies are very difficult. These seriously
affect researchers’ ability to observe and analyze jets, shock waves,
projectiles, and targets during explosions and impacts. Therefore,
these problems are usually studied by using numerical simulation
methods.1–8 With the development of numerical simulation technology, in the study of explosion and impact problems, increasing

attention has been given to the shape and position of solid substances
such as fragments, jets, steel bars, and concrete. Such detail requires
that postprocessing programs for big data visualization must have
the ability to clearly and correctly determine the physical material
interface and to express the fine structure therein. The visualization
of big data is an interdisciplinary subject of human–computer interaction, graphics, image science, statistical analysis, and other disciplines. It integrates various knowledge and skills, such as data processing, algorithm design, software development, human–computer
interaction, animation, and other forms, to display data, to interpret the relationships and trends between data, and to improve the
efficiency of reading and understanding data. Utilizing the knowledge of these subjects to develop better visualization procedures has
become a real problem in recent research.

To observe the deformation and the failure process of solid objects in explosion and impact simulations, the clarity and accuracy of simulation
image representations should be ensured. However, in the results of three-dimensional (3D) explosion field rendering, surface confusion and
blurred images of the microstructure often lead to misunderstandings of the simulation results. In this work, based on the ray casting method
and the Blinn–Phong lighting model, a volume rendering method is proposed that can adapt to the characteristics of step changes in the
explosion field data and satisfies the requirements of observing the microstructure. The Blinn–Phong lighting model is applied to the light
and shade calculation of the resampling point, and the gradient of the resampling point is obtained via interpolation calculations; then, it is

ray casting synthesis formula. In addition, several groups of examples of visual effects are analyzed from the subjective point of view of human
visual effects and the objective points of view of image entropy, the mean gradient, image mean, and standard deviation. The results show
that the new method addresses the problem of interface color confusion, improves the accuracy of microstructure rendering in solid media,
increases the spatial sense of visual images, and satisfies the visualization requirements for explosion products and shock waves. At the same
time, this approach effectively shortens the time for volume rendering of the explosion field.

II. RELATED WORK
Limited by theoretical solutions and experimental studies,
many researchers have developed relevant simulation calculation
programs for explosions and shocks. Rajasekar et al.9 studied the
visualization of shock waves generated by underwater explosions.
Hopson et al.10 of the US Naval Surface Weapons Center used the
Euler algorithm impact physics program CTH that was developed by
the Sandia National Laboratory in the United States to numerically
simulate the crushing process of AerMet100 alloy cylindrical shells
under internal explosion loading. Matsumoto et al.11 studied the
method of visualizing the shock wave generated by the explosion of
the bridge wires, and Murphy et al.12 studied the method of visualizing the stress wave during the explosion process, which clarified the
dynamic behavior of the stress wave during the explosion process.
Guo et al.13 established smooth particle hydrodynamics (SPH) models based on impact tests of a buffer layer. Their models simulated the
dynamic response of foam concrete under low-velocity impact reasonably well. The mechanism of impact damage was investigated,
and the characteristics of layer-by-layer damage of foam concrete
were demonstrated. After long-term research, Ning et al.14,15 developed a multisubstance Euler-type fluid dynamics numerical method
and the corresponding explosion simulation program PMMIC-3D.
The accuracy of the numerical simulation results of explosions and
shock waves was verified through experiments; blasts were simulated
in air; shaped charge jets were simulated by using the PMMIC3D hydrocode; and empirical and experimental comparisons were
performed.

The data obtained from numerical simulations are a large
number of figures, very specialized, and difficult to understand
intuitively. Visualization efforts can help researchers understand
physical problems quickly and clearly. The resulting file contains a
large amount of information about physical quantities, such as time,
velocity, pressure, displacement, and spatial coordinates. Sometimes
the data information is also stored in the file in binary form to save
storage space. Due to the limitations of the human visual system,
data in a four-dimensional or higher-dimensional space cannot be
observed directly. Visualizing such data requires projecting the data
into a lower-dimensional space that is perceptible to humans.16
Considerable research has been conducted on the visualization of multivariate data to obtain high-quality visualization results.
Fuchs and Hauser17 summarize the types of multivariate data fields,
the corresponding visualization processes, and typical visualization
techniques and point out that the appropriate visualization method
should be determined according to specific data fields and visualization requirements. Wu et al.18 used automatic analysis technology
to achieve more efficient human–computer interactions and better
visualization quality. Sundén et al.19 proposed a multilight source
lighting algorithm suitable for multivariable data fields. Such an
approach enhances the spatial sense of the visualized results. Volume rendering is one of the core algorithms for realizing 3D data
field visualization.20–23 Rendering in this manner can directly and
effectively display the physical phenomena inside the data field,
and it has very strong expressive power. To achieve high-quality
rendering, traditional methods rely on high-resolution sampling
of volume data and complex transfer functions.24 High-resolution
sampling can directly improve the rendering quality, but it greatly
increases the amount of calculation involved in volume rendering.
The other common approach is to improve the transfer function
for volume rendering. This class of methods is used to design new
transfer functions, such as gradient,25 curvature,26 size,27 shape,28
and texture,29 by incorporating physical features in the data field
into one-dimensional transfer functions. However, the design of a
multidimensional transfer function is generally difficult and timeconsuming, and the rendering quality is also highly uncertain. To
better address these problems, researchers have proposed automatic
and semiautomatic transfer functions. These functions use the Gaussian mixture model,30–32 kernel probability density estimation,33
visibility histogram,34,35 visibility of volumetric features,32,36 information disparity,37 etc. However, such transfer functions are often
not universal, and they can be used only in specific visualization
processes.
The large body of literature mentioned earlier indicates that due
to its own diversity and the diversity of visualization purposes, there
is no unified visualization algorithm for multivariate data fields. Specific problems should be analyzed in detail. The three-dimensional
explosion field is a special case of the multivariable data field. The
explosion and shock problem is a transient dynamic problem under
extreme conditions such as high speed, high strain rate, large deformation, and high pressure; its numerical value often varies in the
range of zero to tens of megapascals. The medium type variables
have a clear material interface that has nothing to do with the parameters, which is the greatest difference from other data fields. Moreover, the explosion process changes with the development of time,
and the numerical simulation result is often an instantaneous state
for a series of discrete time points, which makes the visualization

C. Imaging visualization via the ray casting method
The ray casting method is a typical direct volume rendering
method that has the advantage of highly defined rendering results.
Figure 1 shows the basic principle of the ray casting method: Starting
from each pixel on the projection screen, a ray passing through the
three-dimensional data field along the line of sight is emitted; several
equally spaced resampling points on the ray are selected; their opacity and color values are calculated; and then they are synthesized in
the order from front to back or from back to front to obtain the color
value of the pixel. By analogy, the final image is obtained.20 In this

on the visualization of explosion fields and few reports in the literature. Ning et al.38,39 adopted the fuzzy interface processing method
to simulate an air explosion and a formed jet. Zhang et al. presented a
streamline-based method to visualize 3D explosion fields.40,41 However, the image effect of the explosion field is not intuitive enough,
and the observer must have a good level of professional knowledge
to understand the image information. In terms of the visualization
requirements for the three-dimensional explosion field, on the one
hand, the focus is on the extraction and enhanced display of physical features such as shock waves; on the other hand, the visualization
results must be able to clearly and accurately visualize the material
interface, microstructure, etc. Wang developed ViSC-3D visualization software by using the volume rendering method, achieved good
visualization results, and successfully displayed the shock wave.42
However, the strong discontinuity and high strain rate characteristics of the explosion field were not considered, and the Phong
lighting model was applied directly to the color solution of the mesh
vertices. When the volume rendering method is applied to draw
solid substances in the explosion field, interface color confusion
and an unclear microstructure of solid substances often occur. This
occurrence seriously affects the understanding and judgment of the
numerical simulation results by researchers.
Good visualization methods provide not only better results but
also faster processing speeds, which increases research efficiency.
The amount of data saved in the resulting files of 3D explosion
fields is particularly large, so the efficiency of volume rendering is
also a major problem that hinders visualization processing. Generally, the following two methods are used to improve the rendering
speed: One is to introduce parallel processing technology and to
develop parallel volume rendering algorithms;43 the other is to use
GPU hardware acceleration to speed up sampling and compositing
calculations in the rendering process to improve the volume rendering efficiency.44–47 Both of these methods set greater demands on the
performance of the computer.
To address the problems and difficulties mentioned earlier and
to better satisfy the need for visualization of explosion and impact
problems, the basic principles and visualization process of the explosion field volume rendering method are introduced. Then, based on
the ray casting method and the Blinn–Phong lighting model, starting
from the light synthesis formula, the color weighted interpolation
and normal vector interpolation methods are introduced to improve
the calculation of the resampling point information. In addition,
no new multidimensional transfer function is used. This approach
avoids possible uncertainty in the visualization process and ensures
rendering quality stability. Moreover, the problem of interface color
confusion is solved, and the accuracy of microstructure rendering in
physical media is improved. Moreover, by changing the visual process, the computational burden greatly decreases, and the efficiency
is improved without the use of parallel algorithms or GPU hardware
acceleration.

cannot accurately represent the specular reflected light that should

greater the influence. In addition, this effect will continue to
the resampling point color interpolation calculation so that
the resampling point color is closer to the average of different color scalar values, resulting in the “distortion” of the
resampling point color calculation.
In the new method, Eq. (15) first calculates the material color
of the voxel points in an opacity-weighted way so that the
material color of the voxel points is close to the real value;
then, it uses trilinear interpolation to obtain the corrected
color value and opacity of the resampling point material. This
processing avoids the errors23 caused when color and opacity
are interpolated separately, which can make the color of the
resampled point closer to the original color.
The purpose of using the ray casting method in the volume
rendering of the explosive field is to improve the rendering
accuracy of the image as much as possible. In the current

method, first solve the normal vector of the voxel, substitute it into the light model to solve the voxel color, and

In the whole data field visualization process, both methods
include three parts: “transfer function calculation,” “lighting
model calculation,” and “interpolation calculation,” and the
lighting model calculation is the most resource-consuming
part of computing. The current method first performs a
normalization calculation to obtain the normal vector of
the voxel point. Next, the lighting model is calculated for
the voxel points; each voxel needs to be calculated eight
times of the lighting model. After four interpolation times,
the material color and opacity of the resampling point are
determined. The new method obtains the normal vector
of the resampling point after three interpolation operations
and two normalization calculations. Then, four interpolation calculations are performed to obtain the opacity and
material color of the resampling point. Finally, a lighting
model calculation is performed on the resampling point.
After a total of seven difference calculations, two gradient
calculations, and one lighting model calculation are performed. Compared with the two methods, the new method
adds three interpolation calculations and one normalized
normal vector calculation. However, the lighting model calculation, which consumes the most calculations, reduces the
calculation amount by seven times, saves 85% of the calculation amount, and finally effectively improves the calculation
efficiency.
Compared to the current method, the new approach
improves the visualization process of the explosion field,
adjusts the application position of the lighting model, and
does not introduce new computational models and parallel algorithms; no new multidimensional transfer function
is used. This approach avoids possible uncertainty in the
visualization process and ensures rendering quality stability.

evaluation method, is more widely used because it is difficult to
obtain an ideal image in general, so this quality evaluation method,
which is completely detached from the dependence on an ideal reference image, is more widely used. The reference-free method is
generally based on image statistical properties; this method usually

The test results show that the explosion field visualization program based on the new method has good rendering capabilities for
solid and semitransparent media on the basis of high-definition rendering. The program can mine 3D data in many aspects in depth

In this work, according to the characteristics of the explosion field, a more suitable visualization method is explored by
researchers. A volume rendering method for the explosion field
based on color weighted interpolation and normal vector interpolation is proposed. Subjectively, the visual effect of the human eye is
used as a reference, and four evaluation indicators—image mean U,
standard deviation Std, image entropy H, and mean gradient G—are
objectively introduced to quantitatively analyze the visual image.
The experimental results show that the new method corrects the
interface color confusion problem and improves the display accuracy of the microstructure in the explosion field without affecting the
rendering of translucent media such as shock waves and detonation
products. At the same time, by improving the visualization process,
the computational burden is greatly reduced, and the efficiency is
improved without the use of parallel algorithms or GPU hardware
acceleration. The new method is able to stably volume render the
data field of tens of millions of meshes at a lower usage of computer
memory and graphics cards, demonstrating the potential for scalability to larger datasets and having good practical applicability. It
can help explosion domain scientists better understand and analyze
explosion events and their effects.
The new method greatly improves the efficiency of volume rendering, but in the visualization of dynamic numerical simulation, the
data field at each moment usually needs to be visualized to show the
simulation process in the form of animation. The new method takes
hundreds of seconds for the rendering time of a single file when
dealing with data fields of ten million grids, which still has a large
room for improvement in the continuous body rendering of multiple data files, and the next step needs to make improvements on how
to further improve the visualization efficiency.
