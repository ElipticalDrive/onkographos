Mitigates #76 (does not close it — see below).
Problem
A non-uniform scale multiplies the child distance field by min(sx,sy,sz) — the correct 1-Lipschitz correction — so a scaled subtree under-reports distance by up to max(scale)/min(scale). The fixed-budget marcher in SdfMesh.ts (256 steps, stepping t += abs(d) * 0.7) then needs that many times the usual step count, and rays that exhaust the budget fall through to discard: valid geometry silently vanishes at grazing angles and reappears as you orbit back.
Change
Enhanced sphere tracing (Keinert et al. 2014): over-relax the step by omega = 1.6, and retreat when the over-relaxed step overshoots the field's safe radius. The backtrack lands back inside the last known safe sphere, so a surface is never skipped — at least as robust as plain sphere tracing, while converging in far fewer steps through conservative fields.
Its precondition is that the field never overstates distance, which is exactly what #75 pinned down and property-tests. The two changes fit together: over-relaxation is only sound on a conservative field.

A non-uniform `scale` multiplies the child distance field by `min(sx,sy,sz)` —
the correct 1-Lipschitz correction — so a scaled subtree under-reports distance
by up to `max(scale)/min(scale)`. The fixed-budget marcher (256 steps, stepping
`t += abs(d) * 0.7`) then needs that many times the usual step count, and rays
that exhaust the budget fall through to `discard`: valid geometry silently
vanishes at grazing angles and reappears as you orbit back (#76).

step by omega = 1.6, and retreat when the over-relaxed step overshoots the
field's safe radius. The backtrack lands back inside the last known safe

plain sphere tracing while converging in far fewer steps through conservative
fields. Its precondition is that the field never *overstates* distance, which
is exactly what #75 pinned down and property-tests; the two changes fit
together, since over-relaxation is only sound on a conservative field.

Fourth CI failure on this branch, and the pattern across the four runs identifies
the cause: `sdf-parity`'s `page.goto` times out only when the marcher spec runs
to completion alongside it. When the marcher spec died early instead, its
neighbour passed. Raising the per-test timeout from 30s to 60s did not help —
`page.goto` blew through 60s too.

Every spec in this suite drives a WebGL context, which CI renders in software.
Two Playwright workers on a two-core runner therefore oversubscribe it: whichever
test is marching pixels starves the other, and the failure surfaces on the victim
rather than the cause. That makes it look like a flaky `page.goto` when it is
really CPU exhaustion.

Serial execution costs little wall clock here, because one file dominates:
`app.spec.ts` is ~9 of the suite's ~10 minutes, so the other specs were never
buying much from parallelism. The 20 min global cap leaves room.

Scoped to CI via `process.env.CI`, so local runs keep Playwright's default worker
count and stay fast.

#96)

Tier 2 of #89, plus its outline-kernel item.

`calcNormal` used central differences on three axes: six full sdf()
evaluations per hit pixel. The tetrahedron technique gets the same
gradient in four. On a shape filling the viewport that is a third of the
shading cost.

Its epsilon was `length(u_bbMax - u_bbMin) * 0.0002`, and u_bbMin/u_bbMax
are the diagonal-expanded cube the mesh is drawn as, not the model's box
— so it was ~1.7x larger than it reads as intending. Being tied to the
scene's size, it also did not shrink when you zoomed into a small feature
of a large model, which is exactly where an oversized epsilon smooths
detail away. It is now a fraction of the marched distance.

The hit threshold was `length(u_cameraPos) * 0.00005` — the camera's
distance from the world origin, which no ray-surface intersection depends
on. It is now half a pixel at the marched distance: there is nothing to
resolve below that and it costs steps to try.

The outline kernel swept -4..4 and culled past r = 3.0, so 32 of its 81
taps did a sqrt and two depth linearisations only to be discarded. -3..3
is the smallest box containing the disc, and the radius is a compile-time
constant rather than a uniform.

Item 5 in the issue is already done and its text is stale: the marcher
has used Keinert over-relaxation since #79, not `t += abs(d) * 0.7`.

The image changes, and only where it should. Five of six golden scenes
are unchanged; box-minus-sphere differs by 169 pixels, confined to the
crease edges, which is where a corrected normal epsilon shows up and
where the creases now read slightly crisper. The diff was inspected
before the reference was regenerated.

Also added: an invariance test rendering the same model at the origin and
700mm away and comparing the two directly. It passes under the old
formulation too — the old threshold was wrong in kind but stayed under a
pixel in practice, so it wasted steps rather than producing a wrong image
— so it is a regression guard, not a demonstration. Stated as such in the
test.

Refs #89.
