Vision
Current rendering treats every gas particle as a discrete halo (Stefan-Boltzmann sized, Planck colour). Up close this looks like "dots with glow," not "diffuse gas cloud." Real cosmology renders (Illustris-TNG, ENZO visualisations, NASA HST imagery) use volumetric ray-marching — for each screen pixel, march a ray through the gas density field and accumulate emission + extinction along the path.
This produces the actual visual look of:
Pillars of Creation: dense dark dust columns with edges illuminated by nearby stars
The Orion Nebula: glowing emission gas with structure at every scale
Galactic dust lanes: foreground dust hiding stars behind it
Molecular clouds: cold dark regions where stars form
It's the gold standard for cosmology renders — and it's the reason those renders look so much better than naive particle-based ones.
Real-physics reference
Radiative transfer equation (Rybicki & Lightman, Radiative Processes in Astrophysics §1):
dI/ds = j(s) − κ(s) · I(s)
where I is the specific intensity, j is the emission coefficient, κ is the absorption coefficient, s is path length.
SPH visualisation (smoothed-particle hydrodynamics): particles become Gaussian density blobs; ray marches accumulate from these.
Existing tools: SPLASH (open-source SPH viz), VTK volume rendering, Blender's volume shader.
Scope
Phase A — Density field construction (~1-2 weeks)
For each particle, define a Gaussian kernel W(r, h) with smoothing length h (e.g. proportional to local inter-particle distance)
The density field at any point: ρ(p) = Σ_i m_i · W(|p − p_i|, h_i)
Emission field: j(p) = Σ_i L_i · W(|p − p_i|, h_i) (Stefan-Boltzmann luminosity per particle)
Absorption field: κ(p) = Σ_i σ_i · W(|p − p_i|, h_i) (geometric cross-section)
Phase B — Ray marching on the GPU (~2-3 weeks)
For each screen pixel, cast a ray from camera through the volume
March along the ray in fixed steps (e.g. 200 samples per ray)
At each sample, evaluate ρ, j, κ from the particle distribution (or a pre-computed 3D grid for speed)
Accumulate: I_out = ∫ j(s) · exp(−∫ κ(s') ds') ds (the radiative-transfer integral)
This must run on the GPU via custom GLSL shader to be real-time
Phase C — Performance optimisation (~1-2 weeks)
Bake particle distribution into a 3D texture grid each frame (or every N frames)
Use mipmapping for hierarchical ray marching
Adaptive step size (more samples in dense regions)
What this does NOT need to model in v1
Full multi-frequency radiative transfer (use a single greyscale channel + colour from underlying particles)
Anisotropic scattering (Mie/Rayleigh phase functions)
Self-consistent radiation pressure on gas (we have explicit photons for that)
Acceptance criteria
Switching from current rendering to volumetric (toggle) shows the same physics but with continuous gas appearance
A bright source behind a dense cold region is dimmed/reddened by the gas (real extinction, already in scope, now properly volumetric)
A diffuse warm region glows with continuous structure rather than dots
At least 30 fps at N=1000 particles, 1280×720 resolution, on a recent laptop GPU
Tests in tests/test_volumetric.py verify ray-march correctness on simple geometries (one source, one dense blob, etc.)
Toggle key (V?) switches between particle-halo and volumetric modes for comparison
Suggested files
New: sim/volumetric.py — Python-side density field construction
New: GLSL shaders in sim/shaders/ — volume rendering
Major update: sim/viewer.py — switching renderers
New: tests/test_volumetric.py
Estimated effort
4–8 weeks for Phases A+B+C with reasonable performance. Production-quality volumetric rendering (e.g. matching Illustris) is years of GPU-shader work; we target the educational / demo level.
Known risks / hard parts
GPU shader complexity: writing volume shaders in vispy is unfamiliar territory. May need to evaluate alternative renderers (moderngl, PyOpenGL custom, or even Three.js front-end via web export).
Performance: naive ray-marching is O(pixels × samples × particles per sample). For 1280×720 with 200 samples and the world's 1000 particles, that's 1.8e11 evaluations per frame — impossible without spatial indexing.
Spatial indexing: pre-baked 3D grid (e.g. 128³ voxels = 2M cells) needs efficient particle → grid splatting.
Sampling artefacts: stepping along rays at finite samples produces banding/striping. Need dithering or jittered sampling.
Memory bandwidth: 3D textures + per-step lookups stress GPU bandwidth. Will benchmark carefully.
Could be a separate web-based renderer (Three.js volume rendering) reading exported simulator snapshots, which may be the lowest-effort path to a stunning demo.
Related to existing photon/extinction work — extends issue #12.
