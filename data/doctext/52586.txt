This restyles rust/kansei-wasm/examples/voxel-gi-particles toward Hector Arellano's "indirect lighting on particles" (miaumiau.cat, p=1476). It also adds the article's ray-traced bonus. The PR #105 scene is still there with scene=cornell.
Side by side
Left: the article (images linked from miaumiau.cat). Right: this example in headless Chrome, 1600×800, Medium quality, cropped.
Article
This PR
Top row: the default "lightbox" scene. Bottom row: rt=on.
The lightbox (new default)
Room: a closed, pale green-grey room on a black background. The only light is an emissive panel in the ceiling. The camera looks in from the front.
The walls get the panel's light from Lambert's polygon formula (the irradiance from a rectangle), shadowed by one cone through the voxel volume toward the panel.
Five hemisphere cones bring the walls the pile's glow and bounce light. Five short cones (aoDistance) darken the corners and the area around the pile.
Particles: sphere impostors with a per-particle radius. They are charcoal to brown, and 25% glow orange to yellow.
Each sphere gets the light the GI gathered for it, plus the panel's light. Its visibility of the panel comes from the GI's narrow cone, which points up. Each sphere also gets a soft reflection of the panel.
The glow reaches neighbouring particles and the walls through the volume.
Simulation: a damped, viscous SPH rain (damping 0.994, viscosity 3) that settles into a pile.
The fluid stops 1.5 m short of the open front, so a strip of floor shows in front of the pile.
Default 12288 particles (6144 on phones).
Reflective floor: each wall and the particles are drawn a second time, mirrored under the box and faded with distance.
Kansei's PlanarReflection belongs to the GBuffer path. This example renders forward with its own materials, so a mirrored redraw is simpler, and exact for a plane under everything.
How the volume holds the room:
The walls go in as GiBoxes whose emission is their panel-lit radiance (the panel's mean irradiance over each face).
The volume stops at the ceiling. The sky gradient stands in for the ceiling and for the open front.
There is no sun term and no gi core change.
Ray traced (rt=on): the article's bonus
Materials: 30% of the particles are glossy black mirrors and 35% are glass (IOR 1.5, Fresnel). The glass refracts through the sphere and out of its far side. The rest stay matte grey.
Ray walk: reflected and refracted rays walk the fluid's own neighbour grid (sorted positions, sorted indices, cell offsets) with a 3D DDA over its dual cells. Each step tests the 2×2×2 grid cells around a dual cell with ray-sphere tests. Since a radius is under half a cell, that finds every hit inside the dual cell.
Misses: a ray that hits no sphere meets the room. The panel, or a wall lit by the panel plus one voxel cone. The open front is black.
Bounces: 1 or 2 (set_rt_bounces, default 2). The pane has the mirror and glass shares and the bounce count.
Defaults: 8192 larger particles, with the glow off. It can be turned back on in the pane.
Core change (additive): FluidSimulation gains sorted_positions_buffer, sorted_indices_buffer, cell_offsets_buffer, cell_size and grid_origin. Nothing is restructured. No gi file is touched.
Impostor depth
The spheres are ray-cast on camera-facing quads placed at the sphere's nearest point. They do not write frag_depth, because writing it runs the fragment shader before the depth test.
A pile is dozens of spheres deep, so with frag_depth every layer ran the full shading, ray tracing included.
With the quads at the nearest point and particles ordered front to back (the initial lattice fills the front first, and indices are stable), rt=on went from about 92 ms per frame to the 16.7 ms vsync cap in headless Chrome.
That came from the page's own frame average while checking that it ran, not a performance run.
The cost: overlapping spheres meet along flat lines instead of curves. At this scale that is hard to see.
AGENTS.md gains this as a sharp edge.
URL parameters and controls
scene=lightbox|cornell, rt=on, plus the existing gi=, view=indirect, quality=, particles= and stats=1.
The Tweakpane shows controls for the active scene:
lightbox: ceiling light, floor reflection, wall occlusion and panel cone;
Cornell: the sun as before.
New setters on window.kansei: set_panel, set_reflectivity, set_ao, set_rt, set_rt_mix, set_rt_bounces.
Tests
cargo test in the example (new, native):
all four composed shaders validate with naga under Capabilities::empty();
SceneParams, Grid, Particles and Surface match their Rust sizes;
the CPU panel-irradiance formula behaves as expected.
start is gated to wasm32, so the crate compiles natively.
cargo test -p kansei-core: all pass.
Headless Chrome (chrome-devtools-axi, WebGPU, local server): no console errors in any of these:
lightbox, rt=on, gi=off, view=indirect;
quality=low and quality=high;
scene=cornell and scene=cornell&gi=off;
rt=on&gi=off.
There were no deploys.
Differences from the reference, and follow-ups
Floating particles: the article's particles hang in the air above the pile. The spec asked for a pile that settles, so the SPH rain lands, and its top is flatter than the article's granular heap.
Glow on the floor: the glow patches on the floor's front strip are fainter than the article's. The camera sits low, so the strip shows thin.
Overlapping spheres: two spheres that overlap show the nearer whole, as described under "Impostor depth".
