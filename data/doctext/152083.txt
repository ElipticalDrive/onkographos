# Direct Volume Rendering

---

## Absorption Only

### Assumptions

1. The medium consists of perfectly black particles that all absorb light perfectly

2. Each particle has area $A = \rho r^2$

3. $ \rho \doteq $ Number of particles per unit volume

4. The unit volume can be approximated as a small cylindrical slab with base area $ E $ and thickness $ \Delta s $ 

   <img src="C:\Users\hendra11\Downloads\000 - PyVista Failing Image Regression Tests\Absorption_Model.png" alt="Absorption_Model" style="zoom:50%;" />
$$
\text{Total Number of Particles} = E \Delta s \rho
$$

$$
\text{Total Area Occluded by Particles} = A E \Delta s \rho
$$

$$
\text{Fraction of Occluded Area} = A E \Delta s \rho / E = A \Delta s \rho
$$

then the intensity of light $ I $ absorbed is given by the ODE
$$
\frac{d I\left( s \right)}{ds} = - I\left( s \right) \frac{A \Delta s \rho \left( s \right)}{\Delta s} = -A \rho \left( s \right) I \left( s \right)
$$
whose solution is
$$
I \left( s \right) = I_0 e^{-\int_0^s \rho \left( t \right) A dt} = I_0 e^{-\int_0^s \tau \left( t \right) dt}
$$
where
$$
\rho \left( t \right) A = \tau \left( t \right)
$$
is the **extinction coefficient** and $ I_0 $ is the **initial light intensity**. The term
$$
e^{-\int_0^s \tau \left( t \right) dt}
$$


can be seen as **transparency** (i.e. $ 1 - \alpha $ , where $\alpha$ is **opacity**) from $ 0 $ to $ s $.





## Emission Only

### Assumptions

1. The medium consists of perfectly glowing diffuse particles with intensity $ C $.
2. Each particle has area $A = \rho r^2$
3. $ \rho \doteq $ Number of particles per unit volume
4. The unit volume can be approximated as a small cylindrical slab with base area $ E $ and thickness $ \Delta s $ 

<img src="C:\Users\hendra11\Downloads\000 - PyVista Failing Image Regression Tests\Emission_Model.png" alt="Emission_Model" style="zoom:50%;" />
$$
\text{ Glow Flux } = C A E \Delta s \rho
$$

$$
\text{ Glow per Unit Area } = C A E \Delta s \rho / E = C A \Delta s \rho
$$

then the change in light emission over the slab is given by the ODE
$$
\frac{ dI }{ ds } = C \left( s \right) A \rho \left( s \right) = C \left( s \right) \tau \left( s \right) = g \left( s \right)
$$
whose solution is
$$
I(s) = I_0 + \int_0^s g\left( t \right) dt = I_0 + \int_0^s C \left( t \right) \tau \left( t \right) dt
$$

## Emission plus Absorption

Hence, adding absorption and emission together
$$
\frac{ dI }{ ds } = C\left( s \right) \tau \left( s \right) - A \rho \left( s \right) I \left( s \right) = g \left( s \right) - \tau \left( s \right) I \left( s \right)
$$
whose solution is
$$
I \left( D \right) = I_0 e^{-\int_0^D \tau \left( t \right) dt} + \int_0^D g \left( s \right) e^{-\int_s^D \tau \left( t \right) dt} ds
$$
where $ D $ is the distance from $ s = 0 $.

# Discrete Implementation

---

Recall
$$
1 - e^{-\int_0^D \tau \left( t \right) dt} = \alpha \implies e^{-\int_0^D \tau \left( t \right) dt} = 1 - \alpha
$$
and since
$$
e^{-\int_0^D \tau \left( t \right) dt} \approx e^{-\sum_{i=1}^n \tau \left( t_i \right) \Delta t} = \prod_{i=1}^n e^{-\tau \left( i \Delta x \right) \Delta x}
$$
we can write
$$
e^{-\int_0^D \tau \left( t \right) dt} \approx \prod_{i=1}^n \left( 1 - \alpha_i \right)
$$
and therefore
$$
I(D) = I_0 \prod_{i=1}^n \left( 1 - \alpha_i \right) + \sum_{i=1}^n g_i \prod_{j=1+1}^n \left( 1 - \alpha_i \right)
$$
<img src="C:\Users\hendra11\AppData\Roaming\Typora\typora-user-images\image-20220815154338342.png" alt="image-20220815154338342" style="zoom:50%;" />

That is, the amount of light reaching the viewer is equal to the total absorption of the background light (first term) plus the emission from each particle less the amount each particle's emission is occluded by the particles in front of it (second term).

Often, this is computed using **back-to-front compositing**, in which we shoot a ray from the viewer to the light source and curry ("chain") calls together, since it is more computationally efficient:
