Requested by Adrien · project thread
Before: light only interacted with surfaces, so scenes could not have haze, smoke or visible light beams.
After: a scene can fill a sphere or box with fog of constant density (medium), or fill the whole scene (fog). A volume material sets its color and how much it scatters forward (Henyey-Greenstein g). scenes/cornell_fog.scene shows light shafts coming through a window into a hazy Cornell box (renders/11-fog-shafts.png).
How: constant_medium::hit samples a free-flight distance -ln(1-ξ)/density inside the boundary and reports a scattering point if it comes before the exit or the next surface. phase_material samples and evaluates the phase function through the usual pdf/eval interface, so ray_color does NEE + MIS at scatter points without any changes. A shadow ray that scatters in the fog counts as blocked, which gives an unbiased estimate of transmittance. Transforms apply to the boundary, so density stays in world units.
There's also a separate bug fix: sample_light sent shadow rays along the unnormalized vector to the light, so the 0.001 self-hit offset skipped about one scene unit. With a light outside a closed room, that made bright lines leak along every wall seam. The shadow direction is now normalized.
Tests: the Henyey-Greenstein pdf integrates to 1 and matches its sampling for g in {0, 0.5, -0.7, 0.9}. A white medium (g = 0 and g = 0.7) disappears in the furnace test. The build has no warnings.
