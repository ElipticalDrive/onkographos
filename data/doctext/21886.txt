AMR gas (currently Ramses) can now be rendered as what it is: piecewise-constant cubic cells, rather than SPH particles. This covers slices, projections, perspective images and healpix maps. It is opt-in, through a config option or a per-call argument; the default is still SPH rendering. Rendering moves from pynbody.sph to a new pynbody.imaging package, with the old names kept working. Two bugs in SPH perspective rendering are also fixed.

AMR rendering
Each cell is a cube of side smooth, volume mass/rho, with edges along the original simulation axes. A snapshot declares its AMR families through SimSnap._amr_cell_families, which RamsesSnap sets to gas.
Opting in. AMR rendering is off by default, and can be switched on at two levels:
Config file: use-true-amr in [sph], False by default. If it is set to True, make_render_pipeline (and so plot.image etc.) uses an AMR renderer whenever the snapshot consists only of AMR cells, unless a kernel is given or the target is 'volume'.
Per call: use_true_amr on make_render_pipeline, plot.image, velocity_image and render_spherical_image.
None (the default) follows the config file.
False always uses SPH.
True forces AMR rendering. It raises ValueError if the snapshot's format does not declare AMR cells, if the snapshot mixes families, or if a kernel or target='volume' is given.
Images (AMRImageRenderer):
Slices give the exact pixel-averaged cross-section. Aligned cells use separable overlaps; rotated cells use polygon clipping. A slice lying exactly along cell faces consistently takes the +z cells.
Aligned projections (including after 90° rotations) are exact separable overlaps.
Rotated projections, by default (approximate_fast=True): cells up to 16 px across interpolate a tabulated corner function $F(X,Y)$, the volume of the cell with $x\le X$, $y\le Y$. This is mass-exact, with pixel errors of typically 1e-5 and at most 1e-3.
Rotated projections, exact: larger cells, cells cut by the depth limits, or all cells with approximate_fast=False use an exact row-difference method, whose cost scales with the cell's perimeter. The exact corner volumes come from the divergence theorem.
Perspective projections give $\int q,dl$ along each ray from the camera.
Under the perspective map each face stays planar, so the depth where a ray crosses it is $K/L(X,Y)$ with $L$ affine. A pixel's integral is a sum of $\int 1/L$ over convex polygons. These use series from polygon moments where they converge, otherwise exact Hermite–Genocchi formulas on triangles.
The $\sec\theta$ path-length factor is integrated to second order per pixel.
Shared faces cancel, so cells tile exactly.
With approximate_fast=True, cells whose perspective image differs from their scaled orthographic image by less than perspective_tolerance (0.1 px) at every corner use the orthographic tables, with exact totals. In images of small cells this is several times faster, with r.m.s. errors around 1e-4 of the peak.
Perspective slices are orthographic slices of the plane, rescaled.
Orientation follows rotations applied to the snapshot, via the new Transformable.net_rotation_matrix. Copy-on-access snapshots inherit their base's rotation and AMR families. There is an explicit orientation= / set_orientation override for positions rotated outside pynbody's transformations.
No kernel or SPH approximation is used: set_kernel accepts only None, and denoising is off unless smooth_floor makes cells overlap.
Healpix maps (AMRHealpixRenderer):
Projections: each pixel gets $qV(\text{cell}\cap\text{cone}_p)/\Omega_p$, from exact clipping of each cube to the pixel's cone. The curved sides of healpix pixels are corrected separately. Mass is conserved to round-off: $2\times10^{-8}$ on the Ramses test data, against $5\times10^{-3}$ for SPH.
Thin shells (shell_distance) sample sub-pixels, with exact tangent-plane fractions for sub-pixels at cell edges.
Pipeline:
approximate_fast now means "approximations are allowed for speed", interpreted per renderer: multi-resolution rendering for SPH, tables for AMR, and none for healpix.
with_approximate() raises for renderers that can't approximate.
velocity_image renders its vectors the same way as its image.
Timings
Wall-clock seconds for make_render_pipeline(...).render() with the default settings (4 threads, and approximate_fast from the default config, i.e. True). Each is the better of two runs on a shared cloud machine, so expect variations of about ±30%.

Orthographic images: comparable to SPH, and faster for slices. The exception is views dominated by cells hundreds of pixels across, such as the 1 Mpc view above, where the exact large-cell path costs a few times more.
Perspective projections: usually slower than SPH, because every face/pixel overlap of a cell is a separate exact integral. The approximate mode helps mainly when cells are about a pixel or smaller; for larger cells it falls back to the exact path.
Healpix: subagent measurements found AMR as fast as SPH or faster at nside ≥ 64, up to 4× faster at nside 256, and 4–7× slower at nside 4–8.
pynbody.imaging package
Moves: renderers, render_image, render_3d_grid and render_spherical_image move from pynbody.sph to pynbody.imaging, along with the Cython cores.
Deprecated forwarding: the old names still work through module __getattr__ forwarding, with a DeprecationWarning.
Plotting alias: pynbody.plot.sph becomes pynbody.plot.imaging; the old name is kept as a silent alias.
What stays in pynbody.sph: kernels and the smooth/rho derived arrays.
Bug fixes (SPH perspective, z_camera)
Projections: the kernel was integrated along $z$ rather than along the oblique ray, so off-axis particles were too faint by $\cos\theta$ and not elongated radially. It is now evaluated at the impact parameter perpendicular to the ray.
Slices: pixels were placed at each particle's depth rather than the depth of the slice plane.

Docs: versionadded/versionchanged 2.9.0 notes on the affected functions, an overhaul note in the pynbody.imaging docstring, and an AMR note in the images tutorial explaining how to opt in. The release notes should repeat the SPH perspective fixes and the new opt-in AMR rendering.

They cover brute-force ray-sampling references, mass conservation, tiling of subdivided cells, orthographic limits, renderer selection (including SPH remaining the default) and the Ramses test data. The AMR test modules switch use-true-amr on with an autouse fixture.

Render AMR cells as exact cubes rather than SPH kernels

```
For AMR gas (currently Ramses), 2D images now default to a new CellKernel,
which treats each cell as a cube of side 'smooth' aligned with the original
simulation axes. Slices give the exact cross-section of each cell and
projections the exact chord length through it, averaged over each pixel, so
the image is an exact rendering of the piecewise-constant field and no
denoising is needed.

- Axis-aligned cells (including after 90-degree rotations) use exact,
  separable pixel overlaps.
- Slices through rotated cells use exact polygon clipping against pixels.
- Projections of rotated cells are exact wherever the chord length is linear
  over the pixel (identified by the entry/exit faces at the pixel corners);
  pixels straddling projected edges are supersampled on a ray grid common to
  all cells, so the cells still tile exactly.
- Cell orientation follows rotations applied to the snapshot, via the new
  Transformable.net_rotation_matrix / Transformation.rotation_matrix.
- Perspective (z_camera) images, volume and healpix targets keep the SPH
  kernel; pass kernel='CubicSpline' to recover the old behaviour.
```

Speed up projections of rotated AMR cells
…
a149599
```
Projections of rotated cells were 5-10x slower than the SPH renderer, mainly
because cells at or below pixel scale paid for a convex hull, corner labels
and up to 16 sub-rays per pixel. Sub-sampling is now replaced entirely:

- The integral of a cell's column over a pixel is a difference of
  F(X, Y) = vol(cell with x <= X, y <= Y) across the pixel corners. Since all
  cells share one orientation, F is a rescaled copy of a single function,
  which is tabulated exactly once per orientation (cached) and bilinearly
  interpolated for cells up to 16 pixels. Being monotonic, the table conserves
  each cell's mass exactly and never produces negative pixels; errors are
  typically 1e-5 and at most 1e-3 per pixel.
- Cells covering at most 2x2 pixels need only three table lookups.
- Larger cells are accumulated as second differences along each pixel row,
  which vanish except near the cell's 12 projected edges, so the cost scales
  with perimeter rather than area; the image is recovered by cumulative sums.
  Pixels crossed by a single edge are integrated exactly as two convex pieces
  with linear column; the remainder use F evaluated exactly.
- Exact F uses the divergence theorem about a point on both clipping planes,
  so only the six clipped cube faces contribute, with closed-form areas where
  a face is cut by one plane.
- Very large cells handled exactly (e.g. truncated by the depth range) fill
  blocks of pixels where the column is linear, rather than pixel by pixel.

Rotated projections now cost 0.1-1x the SPH renderer (without its
approximate mode) for cells larger than half a pixel, and 1.5-1.9x for much
smaller cells. Tests compare each fast path with the exact one.
```

```
Treating AMR cells as a 'kernel' was misleading: the cell renderer shares no
algorithm with SPH interpolation, and needed special cases throughout the
kernel machinery. Instead:

- CellKernel is removed; AMRImageRenderer (a subclass of ImageRenderer, for
  its array handling, units and periodic wrapping) renders cells without any
  kernel, and rejects attempts to set one.
- The Cython code moves from _render.pyx (now back to its original state) to
  a separate extension, _render_amr.pyx.
- Renderers declare whether they benefit from denoising by default and
  whether they support approximation, through hooks which multipass wrappers
  delegate to their sub-renderers, replacing checks on the kernel type.
- make_render_pipeline and plot.sph.image gain an 'amr' parameter. By default
  AMR gas (e.g. Ramses) uses AMRImageRenderer, unless a kernel or z_camera is
  given or the target is not an image, so most users see no interface change.
```

```
Cells truncated by the depth range of a projection, and the unit cube used
to build interpolation tables, were projected by a separate per-pixel exact
path. They now use the row-difference projection like other large cells,
which removes that path and its supporting code (block fill, convex hulls,
entry/exit face labels):

- The row projection accepts a z range. The cross-sections of the cube at
  its limits add up to 12 further lines along which the column's gradient
  changes; chords are limited to the range.
- The volume of a cube within a quadrant and a z range now applies the
  divergence theorem about a point on three of the four clipping planes,
  so only the clipped cube faces and one cross-section contribute. This is
  over twice as fast as the general clipped-polytope calculation it
  replaces, which is removed.
- Quadrant limits are clamped to the cube's extent before use, avoiding
  cancellation when called with effectively infinite limits.
- The row_threshold parameter is removed: rotated cells use a table where
  one covers their size and they lie within the z range, and are otherwise
  projected exactly.

Depth-limited projections are 2-20x faster; others are unchanged, within
timing noise, on both synthetic lattices and the Ramses test data. A new
test compares single rotated cells, with and without depth limits, against
a brute-force evaluation of the exact volume at every pixel corner.
```

Render perspective images of AMR cells exactly
…
b3a9289
```
AMR cells were previously rendered as SPH particles whenever z_camera was set. Perspective
images are now rendered by AMRImageRenderer:

- A slice is the orthographic slice of the plane, rescaled to the view from the camera.
- A projection gives the pixel average of the integral of the quantity along each ray from
  the camera, computed in a new extension, pynbody.imaging._render_amr_perspective. Under the
  perspective map each face of a cell stays planar, so the depth at which a ray crosses it is
  K / L(X, Y) with L affine in the image coordinates; the integral over a pixel is a sum over
  faces of integrals of 1/L over convex polygons. These are evaluated from polygon moments with
  a series expansion of 1/L where it converges rapidly, and otherwise exactly (by the
  Hermite-Genocchi formula on triangles). Pixels entirely within a face use a closed form,
  cells within a single pixel a series for their whole volume, and the secant converting depth
  to path length is integrated to second order within each pixel. Since neighbouring cells
  share faces, they tile without gaps or overlaps.

Tests compare against brute-force ray sampling (agreement at the 1e-5 level of the reference),
check tiling of subdivided cells, the orthographic limit, depth limits and the pipeline.
```

```
Add AMRHealpixRenderer, the healpix counterpart of AMRImageRenderer, with
its core in the new Cython module pynbody.imaging._render_amr_healpix.
make_render_pipeline now uses it by default for AMR gas (e.g. Ramses) when
target='healpix', and accepts amr=True with target='healpix'.

Projections are exact: each pixel receives the volume of each cube lying
within the pixel's cone, found by clipping the cube's faces to the planes
through the pixel's corners, plus corrections for the slivers between
healpix pixel sides and great circles (pixel areas correct to 3e-5). The
volume of every cell is shared exactly between pixels, so mass is conserved
to round-off and cells that tile a region give the same map as one cell.
Cells lying within a single pixel deposit their volume directly.

Thin shells sample cells on equal-area healpix sub-pixels, accepting
sub-pixels wholly inside or outside a cell and computing the fraction
inside at the edges using the tangent plane of the shell.
```

```
Projections integrated each kernel along z rather than along the oblique ray from the camera:
the projected kernel was evaluated at the offset from the ray in the plane of the particle,
rather than at the impact parameter perpendicular to the ray. Away from the centre of the image,
particles were therefore too faint by cos(theta) and insufficiently elongated radially. The

they now show the plane z = z0 as seen from the camera (and are empty if it is behind the camera).

rendering of SPH, and notes on the fixes and on the AMR overhaul in the docs.
```

```
With approximate_fast (the default), a cell whose perspective image differs from its
orthographic image, scaled to its distance from the camera and centred on the projection of its
centre, by less than AMRImageRenderer.perspective_tolerance pixels (0.1 by default) at every

the camera, which were not recognised as edge-on when the plane's offset was itself near zero.
```
