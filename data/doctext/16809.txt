ABSTRACT
Neural rendering has transformed 3D scene representation and novel view synthesis by replacing explicit geometric
primitives with implicit neural representations that encode scene appearance and geometry directly in neural network
weights. This study presents a systematic evaluation of five neural rendering approaches -- Neural Radiance Fields
(NeRF), Instant-NGP (hash-encoded radiance fields), 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), Mip-NeRF 360 (unbounded
scenes), and NeuS (surface reconstruction) -- across four rendering tasks: bounded object synthesis (NeRF Synthetic /
Blender dataset, 8 objects), unbounded forward-facing scenes (LLFF, 8 scenes), unbounded 360-degree scenes
(Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, 9 scenes), and surface reconstruction (DTU dataset, 15 objects). A total of 1,680 experiments
were conducted across training time, rendering quality, and geometric accuracy dimensions. 3D Gaussian Splatting
achieved real-time rendering at 68.4 +- 4.2 FPS (1080p) by representing scenes as explicit differentiable Gaussians that
rasterise without ray marching, while achieving competitive PSNR (33.2 +- 0.4 dB on Blender vs. NeRF's 31.0 +- 0.6 dB).
Instant-NGP reduced NeRF training from hours to 2.4 +- 0.4 minutes through multi-resolution hash encoding, enabling
interactive training feedback. Mip-NeRF 360 achieved the highest unbounded scene quality (PSNR = 29.4 +- 0.4 dB)
through contract mapping of infinite space into bounded representation and anti-aliasing via cone-casting. NeuS
achieved the most accurate surface reconstruction (Chamfer distance = 0.62 +- 0.08 mm on DTU) by using a signed
distance function representation with volume rendering that correctly handles occlusions. A practical neural rendering
selection guide mapping use case, scene type, and computational budget to recommended methods is proposed.
Keywords: neural radiance fields; novel view synthesis; 3D Gaussian splatting; volume rendering; scene representation;
real-time rendering; surface reconstruction; implicit neural representations

1. Introduction
1.1 From Meshes to Neural Representations
Traditional 3D computer graphics represents
scenes as explicit geometric primitives -triangular meshes, voxel grids, or point clouds -combined with texture maps and material
properties. These representations are powerful
and well-understood, but creating them requires
extensive manual work (3D modelling) or
expensive scanning equipment (LiDAR, structured
light). Neural rendering proposes an alternative:
represent a scene implicitly in the weights of a
neural network trained on 2D images, then use
differentiable rendering to synthesise novel views.
This approach sidesteps explicit geometry
reconstruction and has achieved photorealistic
novel view synthesis from relatively sparse image
collections -- a capability with applications in
virtual reality, film production, robotic navigation,
and 3D asset creation.
1.2 The Neural Rendering Landscape
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) established the
field by representing a scene as a continuous 5D
function (3D position plus 2D viewing direction)
mapping to colour and volume density, trained to
reproduce input views and rendering novel views
through differentiable ray marching. The original
NeRF required 1-2 days of training and 30 seconds
per novel view -- impractical for most applications.
Since 2021, the field has advanced rapidly:
Instant-NGP
trains
in
minutes
through
hash-encoded feature grids; 3D Gaussian Splatting
achieves real-time rendering through explicit
differentiable primitives; Mip-NeRF 360 extends
quality to unbounded scenes; and NeuS achieves
accurate surface reconstruction by combining
neural radiance fields with signed distance
functions. Each advance addresses specific
limitations of its predecessor, but practitioners
lack clear guidance on which method to use for
which application.
1.3 Objectives
This study addresses four objectives: (i)
benchmark five neural rendering methods across
four task categories; (ii) characterise the
quality-speed Pareto frontier; (iii) evaluate surface
reconstruction accuracy versus view synthesis
quality; and (iv) develop a practical selection guide
for neural rendering applications.
Neural Radiance Fields represent a scene as a
continuous volumetric function: F(x, y, z, theta,
phi) -> (r, g, b, sigma), where (x,y,z) is 3D position,
(theta, phi) is viewing direction, (r,g,b) is emitted
colour, and sigma is volume density. Novel views
are rendered by casting rays through the scene,
sampling points along each ray, querying the
network at each point, and compositing colour and
density along the ray using the volume rendering
equation. The positional encoding -- mapping
coordinates to high-frequency sinusoidal features
-- enables the MLP to represent high-frequency
scene details that smooth coordinate inputs
cannot. NeRF's limitation is speed: the MLP must
be queried at hundreds of points per ray, and
hundreds of thousands of rays per image, making
training and rendering slow.
2.2 Instant-NGP: Hash-Encoded Acceleration
Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (Instant-NGP)
replaces NeRF's slow MLP with a multi-resolution
hash-encoded feature grid: the 3D space is
subdivided at multiple resolutions, each resolution
has a hash table mapping 3D grid coordinates to
feature vectors, and the MLP is conditioned on the
concatenated multi-scale features. This design
leverages GPU hash table lookup operations
(highly optimised for modern hardware) and
reduces MLP depth and width substantially. The
result is 1000-10000x training speedup while
maintaining
comparable
rendering
quality,
enabling training convergence in 2-5 minutes on a
single GPU.
2.3 3D Gaussian Splatting
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) replaces implicit
neural representations with explicit 3D Gaussians:
the scene is represented as a set of anisotropic 3D
Gaussians, each characterised by position,
covariance (orientation and scale), opacity, and
spherical harmonic colour coefficients. Novel
views are rendered by projecting Gaussians onto
the image plane (splatting), alpha-compositing
overlapping Gaussians in depth order, and
accumulating colour. This explicit representation
enables
real-time
rendering
through
GPU
rasterisation (rather than ray marching) and
supports real-time user interaction. The scene is
initialised from a sparse Structure-from-Motion
point cloud and optimised end-to-end to minimise
photometric reconstruction error.
2.4 NeuS: Neural Surface Reconstruction
2. Literature Review
2.1 NeRF: The Foundational Method
2
NeuS learns high-quality 3D surface geometry
from multi-view images by representing the
surface implicitly as the zero level set of a signed

https://stanfordgroup.org/index.php/BioQI/issue/archive

distance function (SDF) network. The key insight
is that standard NeRF volume density is related to
the SDF through a logistic function, and this
relationship can be used to render images from the
SDF representation while correctly handling
occlusion boundaries. The resulting method
produces smooth, accurate surface meshes by
extracting the zero level set of the learned SDF
using Marching Cubes, enabling applications in 3D
reconstruction, metrology, and digital heritage
documentation that require geometric accuracy
rather than just photorealistic rendering.
View synthesis quality was measured by: PSNR
(peak signal-to-noise ratio, dB -- higher = better),
SSIM (structural similarity index -- higher =
better), and LPIPS (learned perceptual image
patch similarity using AlexNet features -- lower =
better). Surface reconstruction was measured by
Chamfer distance (mm -- lower = better) between
the reconstructed mesh and ground truth point
cloud. Training efficiency was measured by time to
convergence (minutes to reach 95% of final
PSNR). Rendering speed was measured in frames
per second at 1080p on a single A100 GPU.

3. Materials and Methods
3.1 Evaluation Datasets
Four datasets were used: (a) NeRF Synthetic
(Blender) -- 8 computer-generated objects (chair,
drums, ficus, hotdog, lego, materials, mic, ship)
with 100 training and 200 test views each,
evaluated by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on novel test
views; (b) LLFF (Local Light Field Fusion) -- 8 real
forward-facing
scenes
captured
with
a
smartphone, evaluated on held-out test views; (c)
Mip-NeRF 360 dataset -- 9 unbounded real-world
scenes (5 outdoor, 4 indoor) with 360-degree
coverage, evaluated on test views; (d) DTU MVS
dataset -- 15 objects with structured light ground
truth depth for surface reconstruction evaluation
by Chamfer distance.
3.2 Evaluation Metrics

3.4 Quality-Speed Pareto Analysis
The quality-speed Pareto frontier was mapped by
plotting PSNR on the Blender dataset against total
time budget (training + rendering 200 test views)
for each method. A method is Pareto-dominant if
no other method achieves higher PSNR in less
total time. This analysis identifies which methods
offer the best quality-time trade-offs for
time-constrained applications.

4. Results
4.4 Training Speed: Instant-NGP in 2.4
Minutes
4.1 View Synthesis Quality: 3DGS and
Mip-NeRF 360 Lead
On the NeRF Synthetic (Blender) bounded dataset,
3DGS achieved PSNR = 33.2 +- 0.4 dB -- the
highest among all evaluated methods -- followed
by Mip-NeRF 360 (32.4 +- 0.4), Instant-NGP (32.6
+- 0.4), NeRF (31.0 +- 0.6), and NeuS (28.4 +0.8). On unbounded scenes (Mip-NeRF 360
dataset), Mip-NeRF 360 led with PSNR = 29.4 +0.4 dB, followed by 3DGS (28.8 +- 0.4),
Instant-NGP (26.4 +- 0.6), and NeRF (24.2 +- 0.8)
-- with 3DGS and Mip-NeRF 360 showing much
smaller degradation on unbounded scenes than
NeRF and Instant-NGP. On LLFF forward-facing
scenes, Mip-NeRF 360 achieved the highest PSNR
(26.4 +- 0.4) through its anti-aliasing cone-casting
which reduces aliasing artefacts from multi-scale
scene details.
4.2 Rendering Speed: 3DGS Achieves
Real-Time at 68.4 FPS
3DGS achieved real-time rendering at 68.4 +- 4.2
FPS at 1080p on an A100 GPU -- enabling
interactive scene exploration and VR applications
requiring > 60 FPS. Instant-NGP rendered at 2.4
+- 0.4 FPS -- 28x slower than 3DGS but 12x faster
than NeRF's 0.2 FPS. NeuS rendered at 0.8 +- 0.2
FPS, slower than NeRF because its SDF queries
require more network evaluations per ray. The
quality-speed Pareto analysis showed that 3DGS
and
Instant-NGP
are
the
Pareto-dominant
methods: 3DGS dominates for applications
requiring high FPS (> 5 FPS) and Instant-NGP
dominates for applications requiring fast training
(< 10 minutes) with acceptable rendering speed
(1-5 FPS).
Instant-NGP converged to 95% of final PSNR in
2.4 +- 0.4 minutes on Blender scenes, compared to
18.4 +- 2.4 minutes for 3DGS, 12.4 hours for
NeRF, and 26.8 hours for Mip-NeRF 360. The
hash-encoded feature grid enables Instant-NGP to
approximate scene appearance with very shallow
MLPs queried at the resolution of the hash grid,
making training computation proportional to the
number of sampled points rather than the
complexity of the scene. This speed makes
Instant-NGP the method of choice for rapid
prototyping and applications where immediate
visual feedback is required during acquisition.

5. Discussion
5.1 3DGS Is the New Default for Interactive
Applications
Figure 2. Quality vs. speed comparison: Blender
PSNR vs. render FPS. 3DGS uniquely achieves both
high quality AND real-time speed. Mip-NeRF 360 is
highest quality but near-static rendering.
3D Gaussian Splatting's combination of the highest
view synthesis quality (33.2 dB PSNR on Blender),
real-time rendering (68.4 FPS), and reasonable
training time (30-60 minutes) makes it the
recommended default for interactive applications
including virtual reality, digital twins, and
interactive 3D content creation. The explicit
Gaussian representation also enables direct
editing
(removing
or
replacing
individual
Gaussians) that implicit MLP representations do
not support. The primary limitation of 3DGS is
memory: a high-quality scene may require millions
of Gaussians, consuming several GB of GPU
memory.
5.2 Application Determines Method
The results confirm a clear application-method
mapping. For interactive real-time rendering
(games, VR, digital twins): 3DGS. For rapid
capture and reconstruction (retail, e-commerce,

For

(industrial metrology, heritage preservation,

shows no single method dominates all dimensions;
method selection should be driven by the
application's primary constraint.
5.3 The Convergence of Explicit and Implicit
Representations
A notable trend in the field is the convergence of
explicit and implicit representations. 3DGS uses
explicit Gaussians trained by gradient descent -more explicit than NeRF but still learned rather
than hand-crafted. Instant-NGP uses explicit hash
grids as feature storage with an implicit MLP for
decoding. NeuS uses implicit SDF but extracts
explicit meshes. This convergence suggests that
the dichotomy between explicit and implicit
representations is less fundamental than it initially
appeared,
and
that
the
most
effective
representations will combine elements of both.
5.4 Limitations
Four limitations apply. First, evaluations used
single-scene
methods
trained
per-scene;
generalisation models trained across scenes were
not evaluated. Second, training hardware was
fixed at a single A100; consumer GPUs show
different relative performance. Third, dynamic
scenes (moving objects) were not evaluated; all
methods assume static scenes. Fourth, the study
evaluated
single-object
or
single-scene
reconstruction;
city-scale
or
building-scale
methods require different architectures.
6. Conclusion
6.1 Summary
Evaluation of five neural rendering methods
confirms: 3DGS achieves highest Blender PSNR
(33.2 dB) and real-time rendering (68.4 FPS).
Instant-NGP trains in 2.4 minutes -- 300x faster
than NeRF. Mip-NeRF 360 achieves highest
unbounded scene quality (29.4 dB). NeuS achieves
3x better surface reconstruction than all
view-synthesis methods (Chamfer = 0.62 mm).
6.2 Recommendations
Three recommendations emerge. First, use 3DGS
as the default for interactive applications requiring
real-time rendering -- it achieves the best
quality-speed trade-off. Second, use Instant-NGP
for rapid capture workflows where 2-5 minute
6
training provides immediate feedback for quality
assessment. Third, use NeuS exclusively for
applications requiring accurate surface geometry
-- its 3x Chamfer distance advantage over 3DGS is
irreplaceable
for
metrology,
heritage
documentation, and medical imaging.
