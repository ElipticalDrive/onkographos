Volumetric clouds, stacked on the device PR (feature/cloud-device). Like the atmosphere, they are made only from the base's shape: the layer is two altitudes above the base, and every noise is read at the 3D point, so any base kind gets clouds that follow it. Design and limits: docs/look.md, "Volumetric clouds".
What
Density: Schneider's (Horizon Zero Dawn's Nubis), ported from clayjohn's godot-volumetric-cloud-demo (MIT).
The noises: a 128³ Perlin-Worley shape noise and a 32³ Worley detail noise, 3D textures made once by a compute pass.
The cover and the cloud type are read from the shape noise at a weather scale, so there is no 2D weather map (a curved base would tear one). The cover thresholds were measured on the noise.
All of it is read in the shading frame, so it is the same from every render origin.
Light: Hillaire 2016 (Frostbite).
The energy-conserving step and a dual-lobe Henyey-Greenstein phase.
The sun through the air (the atmosphere's transmittance march) and through the cloud (progressive shadow samples).
Wrenninge's multiple-scattering octaves.
The sky's light from the atmosphere's multiple-scattering table.
Passes (ov::Clouds, owned by World with the atmosphere):
A camera-centred direction grid, 1536 × 768 round the base's up. One invocation per 2 × 2 block marches one of its texels in turn and reprojects the other three by the cloud's distance.
A texel holds the clouds' light (with the air in front of them) and their transmittance, plus their front and mean distances times their opacity.
A 512² shadow map along the sun round the camera.
Godot:
The sky puts the clouds over the atmosphere's sky.
The page shader adds the clouds in front of a fragment to its FOG, and light() reads the shadow map (one fetch).
Flags: --clouds=0|1 (on by default). Without the clouds, or without the atmosphere, the old cloud layer stays.
Layer scale: Earth's layer is 1.5 to 4 km. It is scaled under Earth's radius like the atmosphere, then lifted so its bottom is at least 1.25 times the ground's relief, because the grid's march knows nothing of the terrain.
Earth's 3.3 km relief puts the layer at 4.1 to 6.6 km.
On the 2 km planet it is 78 to 208 m.
World: the atmosphere and the clouds now start only for a host that binds their textures. The Dawn viewer, which draws neither yet, no longer runs their passes for nothing.
Checks
Unit tests: 317 pass. The new ones cover the layer's scale and lift, the noise periods, the substitution, the uniform block and the shadow map's frame.
check.sh --spike --discovery: unit, direct, parity, frame, spike and discovery all pass.

Size
The diff is about 1,400 lines, over the ~1,000-line split rule. 770 of those lines are src/clouds.cpp, mostly the GLSL of its three passes. Splitting off the Godot wiring would move only about 165 lines and leave a core PR nobody can look at.
Named limits
The layer is lifted, not composited: the march knows nothing of the terrain, so the layer sits above the world's largest relief and no cloud wraps a peak. A depth-aware march would fix this.
Jagged edges: the grid's 0.23° per texel shows as jagged edges on a small planet seen whole.
Shadow reach: no shadow past the shadow map (64 km on Earth, or 4 camera altitudes).
Colour: the clouds take the scene's warm sun colour, reddened again by the air, so they look pinkish-tan (there is no white balance). Setting the scene's light to white now that the atmosphere reddens it would fix it; that is a scene change and is left out of this PR.
Wind: a render-space vector, so on a planet the noise drifts through the shell away from the pole. It is now along the wind; the old cloud layer still drifts against it.
Per-frame work that could be cut: the grid's history is copied every frame (a ping-pong would save it), the noises have no mipmaps, and the shadow map is re-marched every frame.
Not modelled: light shafts, lightning, precipitation. The Dawn viewer has no clouds yet.
