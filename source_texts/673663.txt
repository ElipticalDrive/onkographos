Description
Light entering a surface, scattering inside it, and leaving somewhere else. Skin, marble, wax, milk, leaves, jade. It reads as a soft terminator with no hard shadow edge, and as ears glowing red when backlit.
Notes
Fields: subsurface_weight, subsurface_color, subsurface_radius (per-channel, since red travels furthest in skin), subsurface_scale. Weight 0 by default.
Unlike the other lobes in this milestone, this is not purely local shading. Light arrives at one point and leaves at another, so a correct result blurs radiance across the surface — which is a screen-space pass over a separated diffuse buffer, not a line in the fragment shader. That is why it depends on the HDR target: the blur has to happen in linear light, before tonemapping.
There is a cheap tier worth considering first — pre-integrated scattering, a lookup table indexed by curvature and N·L. Entirely local, no extra pass, and it captures the soft terminator that carries most of the visual effect. It does not capture translucency (backlit ears), which needs thickness.
Has no KHR_materials_* extension of its own, so nothing arrives via import. KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission covers the thin translucent case only and is a separate decision.
Decide at the gate which tier ships. If it is the cheap one, the issue says so plainly rather than claiming subsurface scattering generally.
Definition of done
A material with subsurface weight above 0 renders with a visibly softened terminator that reddens with a skin-like radius, and the effect scales smoothly to nothing at weight 0. Cost measured A/B at 1080p and 1440p; blank-scene gate unmoved.
