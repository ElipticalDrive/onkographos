Owner
A camera inside a volume saw none of its absorption, so its renders came out too bright. This restores what flex-cuda-pt
did before 9747462, as a fix-forward on the rewritten main (one commit).
Cause
9747462 (homogeneous scattering media) replaced the per-segment Beer–Lambert rule, which attenuated every segment ending
at a volume's back face, the camera's own included, with media tracked along the path. That tracking starts as "none at
the camera" (walk, renderer.cu:997 and renderer.slang:1050). walk also starts at the camera's first hit, so nothing
ever attenuated the segment from the camera to it.
Measured with an absorbing slab (IOR-1 glass, 4 × 4 × 1, Volume Absorption (0.5, 0.8, 1), density 1, white world,
camera 0.75 below its top face looking up; .agent/volprobe.py):
build
camera inside, exact 0.687 / 0.861 / 1
7de0339 (before 9747462)
0.687 / 0.861 / 1
9747462 and main
1 / 1 / 1
From outside the slab both are exact.
Fix
Export: start() keeps a BVH of the world-space triangles of each instance whose material has a linked Volume
output, and refreshes it for moved instances on updates.
Request: trace() finds the volume containing the camera by ray parity (inside(): a ray up from the camera
crosses that mesh an odd number of times). It sends that instance's first triangle of a material filling the volume
(a linked Volume output) as the new Settings.medium, or NONE. This runs per request, so a moving viewport camera is covered.
Renderer, both backends:
walk starts with that volume's medium, shaded from the triangle, so a textured volume works too.
It multiplies the throughput by the camera segment's absorption, exp((scatter − extinction) · distance) =
exp(−σa · distance). The first hit's emission (seen) takes it as well.
RECORD_STRIDE 544 → 560 on both backends: Settings grows by one field, and the SBT record by 16 bytes.
Known approximations
Camera-segment scattering is not sampled. The camera segment is attenuated by absorption alone: out-scattering is
not removed and in-scattering is not added there. That's exact for absorbing media and in a furnace, where the two
cancel. Outside a furnace, fog seen from inside shows neither its haze nor its glow along that segment. Main has no
camera medium at all, so this is strictly closer to the truth. A single-scattering estimator for the camera segment
(lit fog from inside) is a separate decision for Bendik. Sampling it the way walk does after each hit would move the path's first vertex
off the ReSTIR domain's hit (dom.hit), which the reuse passes are built on. That's out of scope for this fix-forward.
Segments after the first hit scatter as before.
One material stands for the volume. The instance's first triangle of a volume-filling material stands for its
volume. An instance with several different volume materials reads the first one's medium; nested volumes aren't
tracked, as before.
Tests (make test)
camera in a volume: the camera inside the slab sees the sky at exp(color − 1)^0.25.
camera in a volume around an emitter: an emitter 0.15 below the camera, inside the slab, reads exp(color − 1)^0.15.
This is the case where the camera's first hit is not a volume face.
camera in a volume of a later slot: the slab's first face takes a copy of its glass without a volume, the camera
inside still reads exp(color − 1)^0.25. An earlier revision sent the instance's first triangle and read no absorption.
camera in a scattering volume: the slab with a white Volume Scatter instead, under the white world, a furnace: mean
within 2% of 1. Attenuating by the full extinction, as an earlier revision did, loses light here.

Where it comes from: a build of abbb6cc with only walk()'s camera lines reverted renders occluder and cornell bit-identical to main. Those are the Medium medium = … line, the throughput = exponential(…) line and seen = throughput * surface.emissive. The Settings field, the stride and the exporter are not involved.
Why it isn't behaviour: with no camera medium, these lines compute expf(0). Under --use_fast_math I measured that as exactly 1 (0x3f800000) on this GPU. Vulkan is bit-identical, so the values are the same and only nvcc's code for them differs.
For the manager: decide whether ulp-level differences on OptiX are acceptable. If bit-identity is required, the same lines would need to be skipped when settings.medium == NONE.
ABBA (OptiX, 1280 × 720, 20 frames, ReSTIR, denoise and decorrelation off, one hold per scene)
the showcase scene: median 76.5 ms on the head against 80.4 ms on main.
tokyo: median 154.0 ms against 156.2 ms.
No regression. The small speedup probably comes from the same change in compiled code.
The stated limits, against the code before 9747462
Scattering on the camera segment: not sampled, so out-scattering is taken to be balanced by in-scattering. This is the same as before 9747462, which had absorption only. Exact in the white furnace above.
One triangle stands for the volume: worse than before 9747462 when the instance has several material slots.
Instances are flagged as volumes if any slot's material has a Volume output. The exporter then sends the instance's first triangle, whatever its material.
Test case: the absorbing slab above, with its first face (a side face the camera never sees) given the same glass without a volume. It reads 0.7998 grey on abbb6cc and on main, on both backends; the exact value is 0.5496 / 0.6888 / 0.8.
Before 9747462, the camera segment took the material of the face it left through: here the absorbing top face, so the result was correct.
Fix: send the first triangle whose slot's material fills the volume. In the exporter's loop, that is the first index entry whose material has a linked Volume output, instead of self.slots[i][0]; about 3 lines. Add a test 'camera in a volume whose first face has none', which fails on abbb6cc.
Nested volumes: the first containing instance in instance order is used, not the innermost. Before 9747462, the face the ray left through decided. Narrow, but worth stating in the body.
