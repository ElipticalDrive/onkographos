The metal and dielectric specular lobes perturb reflect() with a uniform-cube fuzz scaled by roughness² — not a normalized BRDF sample. Two costs: high variance on rough reflections, and wasted samples (fuzz below the horizon kills the whole path). This is the main reason reflections converge slowly.
Fix (one shader session):
GGX VNDF sampling (Heitz 2018, JCGT): sample the visible-normal distribution; the sample weight is just G2/G1 (near-constant variance), no below-horizon waste. Apply to the metal lobe and the dielectric reflect/refract half-vector. α = roughness² (Disney convention), clamped ≥ 1e-3. Typical 2–5x variance reduction on glossy lobes plus an energy-correctness upgrade.
Roughness regularization (Kaplanyan/Dachsbacher path-space regularization): after the first diffuse bounce, roughness = max(roughness, k) with k ≈ 0.25. Glossy chains converge instead of sparkling; mirror-in-mirror stays sharp because regularization only kicks in after the path went diffuse. Same accepted-bias philosophy as the existing blurred-env-mip and firefly clamp.
