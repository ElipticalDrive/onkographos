Summary
rayTriangleHit (the naive Möller-Trumbore test used by swtrace.comp,
swpathtrace.comp, and — since M45-F05 PBI-350 — sw_bvh_traverse.glsl, shared by
swpathtrace_realistic.comp) has a floating-point precision gap for a ray that lands
exactly on the shared edge between two triangles of a flat quad.
Reproduction: quad = two triangles (-5,-5,0),(5,-5,0),(5,5,0) and
(-5,-5,0),(5,5,0),(-5,5,0) (the shared diagonal is y=x). A ray from (0,0,10)
toward (0.30458447, 0.30458447, -0.9024725) hits the quad at (3.375, 3.375, 0) —
exactly on the diagonal seam. Verified in float64 by hand that BOTH triangles' own
Möller-Trumbore u/v (u=0, v=0.8375 and u=0.8375, v=0) legitimately accept the
hit at that precision. But SWScene.Trace (the actual float32 GPU compute shader)
reports a MISS — the float32 rounding of u/v for this exact-boundary case
apparently lands just outside [0,1] on BOTH triangles simultaneously, a classic
non-watertight-intersection crack (see Woop et al. 2013, "Watertight Ray/Triangle
Intersection").
The hardware RT backend (RTScene.Trace, same ray) correctly reports a hit — its
built-in intersection unit does not have this gap.
Impact
Any ray that happens to land within float32 epsilon of a shared triangle edge can
report a false miss through the software backend only, causing a hairline dark seam
(or, worse for a shadow ray, a false unoccluded result) exactly along internal
triangle edges of any tessellated mesh — most visible on adjacent-triangle diagonals
of a coarsely tessellated flat face. Confirmed via M45-F05 PBI-350's live per-pixel
image tests (TestSWSceneRealisticImageMatchesCPUOracle,
TestRealisticImageBackendParity) — both had to avoid a camera setup whose per-pixel
ray grid lands exactly on a test fixture's triangle diagonal to pass, which is a
workaround for THIS bug, not a fix.
Fix
Replace rayTriangleHit (duplicated today in swtrace.comp's own copy and in the
shared sw_bvh_traverse.glsl) with a watertight ray-triangle test (Woop et al. 2013)
that guarantees no missed hits at shared edges regardless of floating-point rounding.
Verify against the exact reproduction above as a regression test, plus re-run every
existing head/internal/native RT/SW test to confirm no behavior change on the
already-passing cases.
Pre-existing since PBI-334 (swtrace.comp's original rayTriangleHit); not introduced
by PBI-350, only newly exercised by it.
