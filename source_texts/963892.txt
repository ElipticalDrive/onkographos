Summary
Forward-fix the analytical-primitive routing inside TrimmedCSGStump. Per ADR-0019 every analytical primitive (plane, cylinder, sphere, cone, torus) contributes its raw untrimmed half-space SDF to the CSG-Stump DNF, recovering bit-equivalence with DifferentiableCSGStump on analytical-only models.
ADR-0018's Marschner signed blend is preserved for two distinct use cases: standalone trimmed-face distance queries (the *_face_sdf wrappers in brep/trim_frame.py, unaffected) and the future BSpline-patch path inside the composition.
New regression benchmark tests/benchmarks/test_trim_baseline.py extended with a sdf_trim column to lock the bit-equivalence invariant on four analytical fixtures.
Why
The previous wiring (PR #76) substituted the Marschner blend for every curved analytical primitive inside the DNF. Measurement on the new baseline benchmark showed:

Plane-only fixtures were bit-identical between the two paths but box_with_holes collapsed from +0.10% direct error to -3.30% under trim-aware. Isolation experiments narrowed the cause to a category error: the CSG-Stump DNF treats each primitive's SDF as a half-space ingredient, but the Marschner formula returns the signed distance to a finite trimmed face patch which collapses to a non-negative boundary distance outside the trim parameter range. Substituted into the DNF this collapses entire half-spaces to thin shells. The asymmetry that the prior wiring documented for plane primitives generalises to every analytical surface type.
ADR-0019 records the scope correction; ADR-0018 stands as the formula's mathematical specification and is not superseded.

Per ADR-0019, every analytical primitive (plane, cylinder, sphere,
cone, torus) contributes its raw untrimmed half-space SDF to the
DNF composition.  The Marschner trim-aware blend from ADR-0018 is
preserved for two distinct use cases: standalone trimmed-face
distance queries (the *_face_sdf wrappers, unaffected) and the
future BSpline-patch path inside the composition.

Substituting the Marschner blend for analytical primitives inside
the DNF was a category error.  CSG-Stump's DNF treats each
primitive's signed distance as a half-space ingredient; a closed
solid is exactly the intersection of half-spaces.  The Marschner
formula returns the signed distance to a finite trimmed face
patch, which collapses to a non-negative boundary distance outside
the trim parameter range.  Substituted into the DNF this collapses
entire half-spaces to thin shells.  Measured on box_with_holes the
trim-aware composite drifted from +0.10% (untrimmed) to -3.30% at
res=64; the asymmetry that the prior wiring documented for plane
primitives generalises to every analytical surface type.

Changes:
- TrimmedCSGStump now stores both primitives and frames; sdf
  composes via primitive.sdf for every slot, identical to the
  untrimmed composite.
- enrich_with_trim_frames no longer flips intersection-matrix
  columns based on face orientation; the original matrix is used.
- Module docstring rewritten to explain the analytical-vs-BSpline
  asymmetry and its connection to ADR-0019.
- Unit tests updated to pin the bit-equivalence invariant on
  sample_box and box_with_holes; the gradient regression flows
  through primitive parameters.
- test_trim_baseline.py extended with a sdf_trim column to lock
  the same invariant on four analytical fixtures
  (sample_box, sample_cylinder, box_with_holes, l_bracket).
