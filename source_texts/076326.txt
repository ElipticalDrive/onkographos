Real froxel volumetric fog and light shafts for URP. Cinematic, art-directable, and cheap.
URP still ships without true volumetric fog. You get flat exponential fog, or you fake beams with billboard cards that ignore your lights and shadows entirely. No god-rays, no dusty air, no light actually scattering through the scene. And the real thing has usually meant an expensive, hard-to-ship setup.
This asset is froxel volumetric lighting, inspired by HDRP's and adapted and expanded for URP.
How it works
It builds a froxel (3D voxel) fog volume that light scatters through, so your directional, point and spot lights cast real, shadow-aware shafts, all driven from a Volume.
Performance
Compute-based froxel integration on the RenderGraph path, with quality tiers that scale froxel resolution and step count. Noise, phase and sampling are all optimized; in our own scenes it's genuinely hard to even spot in the profiler. Developed and tested on Unity 6+ / URP 17+ (RenderGraph).
Features
Light shafts and god-rays from sun, point and spot lights.
Shadow-aware beams. Soft 4-tap PCF, so geometry blocks the fog correctly.
Animated 3D noise for drifting, living fog.
Height fog and distance fog.
Per-light overrides: side scatter, beam reach, shaft rolloff, tint, intensity.
Decoupled fog intensity vs ambient, plus a GI dimmer.
Quality tiers that scale resolution and step count.
Temporal reprojection with neighbourhood clamping for a stable, low-noise image.
One Render Feature plus a Volume. No scene rework.
RenderGraph native. Full source, no DLLs.
Easy Setup
1. Add the Render Feature to your Universal Renderer.
2. Add the Volume override and dial in the look.
3. (Optional) Drop per-light overrides on the lights you want to art-direct.
Good to know
Unity 6+ / URP 17+ with RenderGraph only. It uses the modern compute + RenderGraph path.
Needs a compute-capable GPU (no GLES2).
It's a froxel system. Very long-range fog detail is bounded by the froxel range / resolution you choose, and higher tiers cost more.
Hard camera cuts can show a touch of temporal softening (clamped, rarely noticeable).
Battle-Tested in Production
Built for our own game, not a tech demo. This fog exists because Silent Strike, our in-development tactical shooter, needed real atmosphere: light shafts through windows, dust hanging in interiors, haze over the exteriors, without a cost it couldn't pay. It's been hardened in real scenes and real player builds, in actual gameplay.
