TGA3D: Texture-Geometry Admixture with
Explicit 3D for Single-Image Novel View Synthesis
Hang Zhang, Ruituo Wu, Chao Zhang, Xinyu Lin, Member IEEE,
Frederic Dufaux, Fellow IEEE, Ce Zhu, Fellow IEEE, and Yipeng Liu, Senior Member IEEE
Abstract—Single-image Novel View Synthesis (NVS) aims to
generate photorealistic multi-view renderings of objects from a
single image based on image-generative frameworks or implicit
3D neural representations. However, a fundamental trade-off
persists. Image-based methods often sacrifice geometric fidelity
due to insufficient 3D consistency, whereas implicit 3D representations of complex point clouds exhibit limited capacity to recover
high-fidelity textures. We formulate this challenge as a dualobjective optimization problem, where preserving fine-grained
textures conflicts with reconstructing precise 3D geometry. To reconcile this imbalance, we propose TGA3D, a Texture-Geometry
Admixture framework that admixtures explicit 3D geometry
reconstruction and detail-preserving texture alignment through
frequency-aware disentanglement. Image contents are decomposed based on a frequency edge into low-frequency structural
priors and high-frequency texture details via Pixel-Conditioned
Prompts. In the Geometry-Aware Module, to iteratively refine
geometric reconstructions and colour adjustments conditioned on
structure information from low-frequency prompts, 3D-consistent
geometric reconstructions integrate two-phase cross-attention
layers. In the Texture-Align Module, to align fine surface details
across synthesized views, multi-level feature mixtures fuse image
features with high-frequency prompts guidance. A fusion module
then amalgamates outputs from both branches, balancing geometric precision and textural fidelity. During training, to mimic
the hierarchical integration of geometric priors and textural
statistics observed in human cognition, TGA3D integrates pixellevel MSE optimization with geometric-aware KL divergence loss.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that TGA3D achieves stateof-the-art performance.
Index Terms—Single Image Novel View Synthesis, Gaussian
Splatting, Explicit 3D Reconstruction, Geometry Awareness

Texture fidelity demands precise preservation of surface
characteristics under varying viewpoints, such as material
patterns, specular highlights, and edge sharpness [13], [14].
Current approaches leveraging 2D generative models [15], [16]
synthesize plausible per-frame details but suffer from crossview inconsistencies. Referring to a single image, synthesized
images with missing textures or occluded spatial information
may result in inherent ambiguities showing incomplete reconstructions. These methods rely on diffusion-based priors
[17] that lack explicit 3D constraints, leading to artifacts like
misaligned textures, blurred edges, and implausible lighting
transitions [18]. Such failures stem from an over-reliance on
2D image statistics, which cannot resolve inherent ambiguities
in occluded regions or disambiguate view-dependent appearance variations [19]. Consequently, synthesized textures often
violate underlying geometric principles, particularly for complex materials or heavily occluded objects [20], [21]. The issue
is particularly pronounced in scenes with complex materials
or severe occlusions. And view-dependent discontinuities or
abrupt texture changes could further degrade the realism of
synthesized images [20]–[22].
Geometric precision requires maintaining structural integrity across synthesized views, avoiding distortions during
viewpoint extrapolation [23]. While implicit neural representations (NeRFs) [24], [25] encode scenes as continuous
volumetric functions to resolve depth uncertainty and model
occluded regions, they struggle to enforce explicit shape
constraints from single-view inputs within continuous function
approximations. Lacking explicit shape constraints could result
in unstable extrapolations, which are manifested as distorted
silhouettes, implausible surface deformations, or discontinuous
depth transitions [26]. The challenge is exacerbated in more
recent methods, which infer full 3D geometry of various
objects from limited perspective cues, amplifying errors in
occluded regions [27], [28]. Post-hoc geometry refinement
strategies [29] further compound these issues by decoupling
structure optimization from the synthesis process, rather than
embedding geometric priors at initialization. The root cause
of geometric instability is underestimated, where a robust 3D
foundation could guide a robust reconstruction to reduce the
absence of reliable geometric constraints.

The core challenge lies in optimizing the balance between
texture and geometry objectives within a unified framework.
Generative models prioritize local detail synthesis at the
expense of global coherence, while NeRF-based methods
over-regularize geometry, suppressing high-frequency textures.
Recent explicit 3D representations like Gaussian Splatting [30]
offer improved efficiency but falter in single-view scenarios
due to under-constrained occlusions [31]. This reveals a critical
need for disentangling geometric reconstruction from texture
alignment—a principle observed in human visual perception
during sculptural arts, where structural modelling precedes
surface detailing.
Inspired by pottery craftsmanship, we posit that the core
issue lies in the lack of explicit geometric disentanglement
during synthesis as shown in Fig. 1. When reconstructing from
a single reference view, clay modelling and decorative painting
are treated as two distinct stages. If processed before building a
stable 3D structural foundation, texture optimization becomes
ill-posed. Depth ambiguities in occluded regions could cause
gradient noise in 2D-3D correspondences. Also, geometric
priors that over-regularize surfaces would compensate for
missing multi-view supervision.
Building on the above insights, we propose TGA, a texturegeometry admixture framework that decouples and fuses
geometry and texture synthesis via image feature prompts
processing. Our key innovations are the Geometry Explicit 3D
reconstruction (TGA3D) and the Pixel-Conditioned Prompt
(PCP). TGA3D introduces an explicit, editable 3D representation using primitives-optimized Gaussian Splatting, addressing
the critical limitations of implicit representations in singleview scenarios. Unlike its render applications, TGA3D enforces 3D positional embeddings of the objects’ sub-geometry.
Each Gaussian primitive is parametrized by position and covariance. This explicitness resolves the over-smoothing and instability of implicit methods by disentangling shape attributes
(scale, rotation) from colour ones (colour, opacity). PixelConditioned Prompts (PCP) solve the ambiguity of unified
guidance in prior works by conditioning pixel-level separation
of the input image to generate two distinct guidance. A lowfrequency structural mask that highlights coarse geometric
cues and a high-frequency detail map encodes fine textures of
material properties. As integrated into the TGA framework, the
Geometry-Aware Module (GAM) leverages low-frequency
PCP to enable TGA3D. Concurrently, the Texture Alignment
Module (TAM) utilizes high-frequency prompts to align texture consistency without geometric interference. The training
combines pixel-level MSE reconstruction with probabilistic
KL regularization.
In summary, this paper makes the following key contributions:
1) We systematically analyze the texture-geometry imbalance in single-image NVS, identifying limitations in joint
optimization frameworks.
2) We propose TGA3D, the first framework to decouple
explicit Gaussian-based geometry reconstruction (GAM)
from frequency-guided texture alignment (TAM), enabled
by our Pixel-Conditioned Prompt mechanism.
2
3) We introduce a hybrid training objective combining pixellevel reconstruction with distributional KL regularization,
aligning with human perceptual hierarchies.
4) We demonstrate that TGA3D achieves state-of-the-art
performance and synthesizes pleasure views.

A. Related Work
1) Image-based Generative Methods: Approaches to
single-image Neural View Synthesis (NVS) predominantly
employed image-to-image translation models for novel view
inference [1], [8], [32], [33]. Representative works like Zero1-to-3 [1] and its scaled variants such as Zero123-XL [1] and
Zero123++ [34] utilize 2D diffusion-based transformations but
lack explicit 3D geometric constraints, resulting in perspective
distortions and cross-view geometric inconsistencies [35]–
[38]. Subsequent methods such as Wonder3D [39], Consistent123 [37] and DreamCraft3D++ [40] introduced singleimage spatial constraints to improve view alignment, yet
they still face challenges in achieving fine-grained depth
accuracy and texture consistency across viewpoints. Recent
advancements in generative models enhance high-fidelity view
synthesis through diffusion techniques: MVDiffusion [41]
and DreamGaussian [42] employ pre-trained diffusion priors
to infer occluded viewpoints, but these approaches often
generate spurious high-frequency details, leading to texture
artifacts and surface misalignments [11], [43]–[45]. Methods
like Free3D [8] and Magic123 [46] enforce view consistency
by integrating implicit 3D modelling frameworks, but they
still face difficulties in decoupling texture and geometry information in challenging scenarios. While Trellis [47] directly
generating 3D assets from images, its high-cost feed-forward
generating may lead unnatural structure without detailed text
prompts.
2) Implicit Neural 3D Representation Methods: The introduction of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [24] marked a
pivotal shift toward implicit 3D scene representations. PixelNeRF [25] and CodeNeRF [48] extended this framework by
conditioning neural radiance functions on image features, enabling few-shot novel view synthesis with sparse input views.
VisionNeRF [14] advanced this line of work by integrating
large-scale visual pretraining to bridge the gap between 2D
image understanding and 3D neural rendering, while NeRFDiff [49] introduced differentiable volume rendering to enforce
cross-view consistency through learnable radiance fields. Other
approaches directly exploit image priors, utilizing dense view
pixels and latent space embeddings to enable shape-aware
novel view generation [23], [49]–[52]. Recent innovations like
SplatterImage [9] demonstrate real-time performance by combining 2D image priors with implicit neural representations,
while DIG3D [53] integrates diffusion-based generative priors
into the NeRF.
3) Gaussian Splatting Methods: Gaussian splatting [30] has
emerged as a promising paradigm for 3D modeling, representing scenes through a collection of Gaussian primitives that
encode both geometric and appearance properties [9], [10].

A. Pixel-Conditioned Prompt
As shown in Fig. 2, we introduce the Pixel-Conditioned
Prompt (PCP) to enhance the model’s ability to synthesize
novel views from a single image by performing pixel-level
frequency-aware decomposition.
Text prompts generally treat the image content as a single
entity, guiding the model to synthesize the image based on
a unified textual description. While this approach is effective
for certain tasks, it lacks the control needed to independently
optimize geometry and texture details. This often results in
compromises in either geometric accuracy or texture fidelity,
especially when dealing with complex objects or significant
occlusions. In contrast to text-based prompts used in methods
like TextureDreamer [56] and Dreambooth [57], which rely
on textual descriptions to guide the generation process, PCP
separates the input image into low-frequency structural cues
and high-frequency texture details. This separation allows for
more precise and independent optimization of geometry and
texture. It ensures that the geometry optimization process is not
influenced by texture information, and that texture refinement
does not interfere with maintaining geometric consistency. The
result is a more stable and interpretable model capable of
synthesizing novel views that preserve both geometric integrity
and high-frequency textural details.

IV. E XPERIMENTS
In this section, we apply TGA3D on two single-image
NVS scenarios and conduct comprehensive qualitative and
quantitative comparisons against state-of-the-art baselines. We
validate and analyze the impact of critical modules or designs
by systematic ablation studies, quantifying their contributions.
A. Implementation Details
Following the standard settings for image synthesis
pipelines [1], [8], [9], the process of generating novel views
from a single image is conditioned on both the camera intrinsics and the extrinsic of views. Camera parameters encode the
camera positions used to capture the object in the real world.
The images are centre-cropped to ensure that the primary
object remains in focus out of the blank background. For the
pipeline, the model is trained on 8 NVIDIA 3080-Ti GPUs.
The Adam optimizer is adopted to minimize the MSE loss and
KL divergence loss.
TGA3D is trained on three individual single-image NVS
experiments: 1) rendered Objaverse-v1.0 [58], comprising
over 1,000 objects, each rendered from 11 viewpoints, and
Google Scanned Objects (GSO) [59] for evaluation; 2) standard splits in ShapeNet-SRN [60]; and 3) standard splits in
Objaverse-XL [61]. To quantitatively evaluate the performance
of TGA3D, we utilize the following metrics to measure novel
views synthesized from a single image. Peak Signal-to-Noise
Ratio (PSNR) measures pixel-level accuracy. Structural Similarity (SSIM) [62] evaluates structural similarity in image patterns. Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) [63]
assesses perceptual similarity by comparing deep features
between images. Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) [64] results
in the overall quality and realism of the generated images.
B. Main Results on Single-Image NVS
We conduct both qualitative and quantitative evaluations,
benchmarking TGA3D against state-of-the-art methods 1 .
TGA3D is evaluated in three independent experimental sets.
To check key differences in geometry and texture, we present
the comparisons of different datasets and tasks and highlight
TGA3D’s generalizability and effectiveness across scenarios. As an idea from separate structure rebuilds and detail
attachments, TGA3D preserves precise geometry and highfidelity textures across novel views, even when generating
unseen objects from a single image. Compared to existing
methods, our approach produces more precise object shapes
and finer details while also reducing artefacts and colour
inaccuracies. The divided selection of baselines ensures a
fair and comprehensive evaluation of TGA3D against wellestablished benchmarks in each task. While other promising
works have been excluded due to unavailable code at the time
of submission, our comparisons are focused on methods that
are well-represented in the community.
We also evaluate TGA3D free views. Building on the
promising results from the public dataset, we extend our evaluation to real-world objects, capturing random single images
to assess performance under free camera views.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we introduced TGA3D, a novel single-view
novel view synthesis (NVS) approach that combined explicit
3D Gaussian attention and texture alignment to effectively
separate structural and textural features. By leveraging pixelconditioned prompts, TGA3D captured low-frequency structure and high-frequency detail information, ensuring precise
geometry reconstruction and high-fidelity texture generation.
This approach addressed key challenges in NVS, including viewpoint variability and texture inconsistency. In the
Geometry-Aware Module, the 3D shape and colour information were processed in two distinct phases, ensuring a
clear separation between geometry reconstruction and texture
refinement. Our method outperformed existing state-of-theart models on benchmarks such as GSO and ShapeNetSRN. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrated that
Explicit3D is highly robust in handling challenging viewpoints
and occlusions, establishing a new benchmark for single-view
NVS.
