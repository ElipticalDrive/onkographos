Goal
Bring to 3-D volumes (FITS cubes, VBT volumes) the rendering techniques built for particles in #101: light accumulated in floating point and graded with a tone curve, colour from a second quantity, and projected maps. Volumes keep their analysis rendering (Composite, MIP, MinIP, threshold, colour bar); this is a presentation style the user switches on.
Why
The cube viewer's 3-D view renders with VTK's GPU ray caster. It already integrates emission and absorption along each ray, the same model as the ray-traced particles, but:
colour and opacity come from transfer functions with a limited range, so a cube with a large dynamic range either saturates its cores or loses its faint diffuse emission, and the opacity curve has to be tuned by hand;
one quantity sets both colour and opacity; there is no way to show, for example, a simulation's density as brightness and its temperature as colour;
there is no projected view (the density summed along the line of sight, from any direction) for spatial grids.
What
HDR light and tone mapping. Emission accumulated in floating point along each ray, then graded as the particle renderer grades its frames: exposure in stops over an automatic one, asinh / logarithmic / exponential / linear curves, gamma, saturation. Optional grey absorption, given as the optical depth where the light is brightest.
Two-field colour table. Brightness from one quantity, colour from another:
simulation grids (VBT volumes, or two fields of one): density as brightness, temperature (or any field) as colour;
spectral cubes (RA, Dec, velocity): intensity as brightness, velocity as colour, in 3-D from any viewpoint.
Projected map. For spatial grids: the column along the line of sight, without perspective, optionally within a slab, brightness from its logarithm and colour from the density or from the density-weighted mean of a second field. For spectral cubes this only has a physical meaning along the velocity axis, where the moment maps already cover it.
How (proposal)
Interactive view on the desktop's GPU: the cube as a 3-D texture, ray marched in a shader into a 32-bit float buffer, toned by the same pass as the particles' GPU view (ParticleGLView). A third style in the cube viewer's 3-D view, next to Composite and MIP, with its settings in the sidebar as the particles' "Ray-traced" shape has them.
Stills and movies on the backend, as for particles (/v1/tasks/particle_render): the same model on the CPU (numba), so the result does not depend on the desktop's GPU, and runs on any HPC node.
Large cubes: GPU memory is the limit (a 512³ float cube is 512 MB). Use half precision, or the levels of detail the cube viewer already builds for out-of-core cubes.
Remote: the same view in the backend's render session on EGL nodes, for cubes that do not fit the desktop.
Order
Simulation grids (VBT volumes): HDR light, second field as colour, projected map.
Spectral cubes: velocity as colour.
Remote rendering on EGL nodes.
Related
Cinematic rendering: lit volumes (SSAO, scattering) and Splotch-style particle rendering #101: cinematic rendering (lit volumes with ambient occlusion; ray-traced particles, whose renderer, tone curves and GPU view this reuses).
