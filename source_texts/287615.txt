Working title
Three ways to see a scene: comparing NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, and neural surface reconstruction
Category
learning (tutorial, educational content)
Description
An interactive comparison of the three dominant radiance-field / reconstruction paradigms, with at least one in-browser interactive visualization per method, all driven from a single captured scene.
The angle that makes this more than a survey: the three families differ not just in quality but in how easily they ship to the web, and that asymmetry is the comparison. 3DGS and NeuS produce explicit primitives that ship as static assets; NeRFs do not. Rather than hide that, the post makes it the through-line.
Per-method visualization plan:
3DGS-like (splatfacto-mcmc): export PLY, compress to SPZ, embed with Spark or mkkellogg/GaussianSplats3D. Free camera, no backend. Easy.
NeuS-like (Neuralangelo or 2DGS): extract mesh, ship glTF, view with <model-viewer>. Free camera, no backend. Easy.
NeRF-like (nerfacto): no static-asset story. The honest option is a pre-rendered orbit (image strip or sprite sheet, draggable). The post should annotate the limitation ("you can't freely fly a NeRF in the browser without a baking pipeline") rather than fake it. Baking routes (MobileNeRF, SMERF) get a mention but are out of scope as the live demo: SMERF needs roughly 16-24h on 16x A100 per scene and has no Nerfstudio integration, so it is the wrong tool for a self-funded blog post.
Suggested pipeline: capture one scene, train all three, define one shared orbit camera path, render each at matched resolution, host everything as static files. Add per-method static figures alongside the interactive ones (density slice, gaussian count, mesh wireframe).
The post should be honest about the asymmetry in viewer maturity: there is a SuperSplat for splats, nothing equivalent for NeRFs, and that is a concrete reason NeRFs lost the "default representation" slot in 2025-2026 despite competitive render quality.
Possible sidebars or a companion post (a lot of reusable depth came out of the planning conversation):
How a 3DGS PLY actually encodes a scene (~62 float properties per Gaussian, values stored in raw optimization space: sigmoid(opacity), exp(scale), normalized quat, SH-to-RGB on the DC term). Explains why MeshLab shows wrong colors.
splatfacto variants decision tree: mcmc (better Gaussian placement via SGLD) vs big (config/budget knob) vs w (in-the-wild appearance embedding). These are orthogonal axes, not a quality ladder.
Splat cleanup best practice: opacity then scale then anisotropy then SOR, finished in SuperSplat. Plain position-based SOR alone misses big single floaters and erodes thin structures.
The splatfacto-w web-deployment gotcha: the appearance MLP plus per-image embeddings do not survive into a standard viewer, so you have to bake at a chosen (or mean) embedding before export.
