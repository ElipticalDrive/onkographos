The rapid increase in 3D data, particularly from LiDAR point clouds, poses significant challenges for storage, transmission, and processing. Compression techniques offer a means to manage this data, however, the challenge lies in reducing the data size while preserving essential details that ensure usability in practical applications. This study assesses both classical and deep learning-based compression methodologies, revealing that compression performance is closely related to point cloud density characteristics. Deep learning methods demonstrate efficacy with dense data but encounter difficulties in sparse regions. Geometry-based approaches are more suitable to handle sparse areas but are less effective in dense regions. To mitigate these limitations, we introduce a hybrid compression approach that classifies the point cloud based on density and applies deep learning for dense regions and geometry-based compression for sparse regions. This leads to enhanced RD performance at bitrates below 4 bits per point, a range that reflects realistic constraints in practical applications.
