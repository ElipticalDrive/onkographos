Asked whether a grid should be drawn as small cubes, with a comment proposing marching cubes or dual contouring. Directionally right — and wrong on three specifics, which was worth checking before writing anything.
What the check found
The algorithm is not missing. clay_voxel_mesh_smooth is surface nets — the dual method the comment recommends — and has been in ClayCore all along. Nothing needed writing here or in the engine.
It is not a replacement. The engine's own header: the boxy picture is "correct for hard-surface voxel work and for export, and the wrong picture of an organic sculpt", and the choice is kept an argument rather than grid state so "one host can show both pictures of one sculpt without mutating it". A display setting, which is also what 3DCoat offers.
It is not dual contouring. A vertex sits at the centroid of its cell's edge crossings, which is what smooths — so corners round. Dual contouring fits the vertex by least squares to hermite data and keeps a sharp corner sharp. The comment's "preserves sharp features" is not what this gives, and getting it would be an engine change.

The two facts that shaped the wiring
Both the engine's, not preferences.
The smooth mesh carries no normals. Colour blends across it — a vertex sits between up to eight voxels and averages the occupied ones, there being no facet to hold one palette entry — but a normal is the host's to work out. Without them the surface renders as a flat silhouette, which is exactly what an earlier attempt at this looked like and why it was dropped. They are computed area-weighted in the bridge, with a test that would have caught it: the rendered surface must show more than twenty distinct tones.
It cannot be meshed a chunk at a time. clay_voxel_mesh_chunks is the greedy mesher alone, and the engine explains why — greedy quads are axis-aligned and exact, so clamping their merge to a chunk boundary emits more, smaller quads over the identical surface and never a crack, while surface nets place a vertex from a cell's neighbourhood and would tear.
So the smooth picture is a settle, rebuilt when a gesture ends beside the brick surface's own: the incremental boxy path is 3.3 ms a dab against 309 ms for a whole-grid re-mesh. It counts toward mesh_revision, or a settle that rebuilt it without touching a cell would never reach the viewport.
Saying what the filtering costs
Suavização is the engine's blur, in passes of a 3×3×3 box over occupancy, and its trade is real in both directions. At 0 nothing is filtered and nothing can be lost, but the surface still terraces. At 1 it reads as clay, and an isolated voxel sits near 0.3 occupancy — under the isolevel, and gone; thin features go the same way.
The default is 0, in the engine's own words: "a default that silently deletes a sculptor's detail is the wrong default however good it looks." SmoothBlur::can_lose_detail is asked by the interface, which says so in the accent colour where it is true — rather than leaving a sculptor to find out from a missing finger.
Tests
clayspace-engine/tests/voxel_display.rs, six:
the boxes are what a grid draws until it is asked otherwise
the smooth picture is a different surface over the same cells — and going back gives the boxes exactly
the smooth surface carries normals, and they do not all point one way (the assertion that would have caught the flat silhouette)
blurring smooths further and its setting reports that it can cost detail
a dab made while the smooth picture is up reaches it
changing the picture records no history entry
clayspace-model gains three on the vocabulary — the defaults are the ones that lose nothing, only the boxes mesh incrementally, and the blur clamps to what the engine describes.
clayspace-app/tests/visual_voxel_brushes.rs gains one: the two pictures are different frames, blur changes the frame again, and the smooth surface is shaded rather than flat.
Docs
docs/features.md gains Boxes or a surface under voxel layers, with the measurements, both engine facts, the blur's trade in its own words, and the surface-nets-not-dual-contouring note. The OpenSpec change gains a requirement with three scenarios and a task group.
