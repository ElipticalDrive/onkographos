I developed an instancing technique for voxels. I cannot break them yet and i only finished scripting the top face of my voxel level generator (Edit: i succeeded in scripting all 6 faces). But it’s working. 495++ fps (for a map 100 chunks in width x 100 chunks in depth x 10 chunks in height) running in a threaded app or running on the UI thread when only the top face is generated. With all faces generated and a level just as big, the fps varies between 120 to 170 depending on the number of chunks spawned.
Also, my instancing voxel project is making use of my triangle/vertex reducer technique, in order to enlarge faces to cover more area. This reduces even further the cost of spawning more voxel square faces. It’s hard to see without any gridlike overlay on the voxels themselves, but i can assure you that i was able to develop this and those screenshots all are from my voxel instancing trig/vert reduced.
I barely read about “how is it called” what i developed myself. It seems that the proper name is “chunking instanced voxels”. Well, Craig Perko in his first minecraft tutorial on youtube was using the chunking technique without instancing so that’s where i learned to remove faces that are outside of the view spectrum/inner faces. I developed my own instancing technique on top of it and incorporated my own vertex triangle reducer technique. There is still work left to do as i feel the ram/memory usage is quite high when spawning a big level, but on the cpu side i am amazed compared to when i spawn individual meshes using deferred rendering or not. Currently i didn’t implement deferred rendering on my instanced voxel reduced example, and i hope i will have the time to code that soon.
Thank you for reading me,
Steve Chassé
