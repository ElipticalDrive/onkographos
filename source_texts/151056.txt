A BSTRACT
Scalable 3D Gaussian Splatting allows one scene representation to serve different
Gaussian-count budgets, yet its quality–budget curve may lag behind models optimized separately for each budget. Reducing the number of Gaussians changes
both the selected members and the reconstruction tasks carried by the survivors;
parameters inherited from a high-budget model may fit a low-budget representation poorly. We propose ParetoGS, which describes member changes and parameter evolution with a small set of adapted budget anchors and decodes intermediate
budgets without separate optimization at every requested count. ParetoGS alternates pruning and fixed-member adaptation to build nested anchors with persistent identities, and encodes their parameter changes relative to the full model in a
compact residual atlas. At decoding, it interpolates the states of shared members
and gradually introduces the rendering contributions of new members, connecting
sparse anchors into a continuous-budget representation. Across 13 scenes, ParetoGS attains better average PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS with fewer Gaussians than
the compared prefix methods at 10% of each method’s own full model. From
a shared full model, it also outperforms FlexGS-imp at its trained 1%, 5%, and
10% budgets and NanoGS-imp at the 1% and 0.1% budgets. On Garden, its nonanchor quality approaches an empirical reference from independently optimized
GNS models at higher retention ratios and exceeds it at some lower-budget points.
Across 13 scenes, encoding reduces mean additional state from 390.0 to 85.7 MB
with an average PSNR change of 0.01 dB.

I NTRODUCTION
The explicit primitives of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al., 2023) make Gaussian count
a direct control on rendering cost. Selecting a smaller subset from a full model, however, need
not yield a high-quality representation at that count. Pruning and re-optimizing for each budget
gives strong individual models but requires repeated training and multiple assets. Scalable methods
instead use member orderings, hierarchies, or budget-conditioned transformations to serve many
counts from one asset. Their quality–budget curves can still lag behind models optimized separately
at each count. We ask whether one representation can decode any Gaussian-count budget while
approaching this empirical per-budget quality reference.
Count reduction changes both membership and the reconstruction tasks of the survivors. After other
Gaussians are removed, retained members must adjust their geometry, opacity, and appearance to
fit the reduced capacity. Even a suitable subset can perform poorly with parameters inherited from
the full model. Its adapted state also provides a new starting point for the next pruning decision.
ParetoGS therefore contracts from previously adapted anchors and describes both member changes
and parameter evolution.
Persistent identities retained through progressive contraction align the same Gaussian across neighboring budgets. Budget evolution then has two parts: discrete member entries and exits, described

We propose ParetoGS (Figure 1), which alternates structural contraction and fixed-member adaptation to build strictly nested anchors. It preserves identities and deletion order, encodes each anchor’s
changes relative to the full model in a layered residual atlas, and decodes intermediate budgets by
interpolating shared-member states and gradually activating entering members. An approximately
doubling schedule places anchors from 0.1% of Full to the full count, yielding a single asset that
serves every integer count in this range without further optimization.
Across 13 scenes, ParetoGS outperforms CLOD-3DGS and MGS at 10% of each method’s own
full model while using fewer Gaussians. From a shared full model, it improves mean quality over
FlexGS-imp at its trained 1%, 5%, and 10% budgets and over NanoGS-imp at 1% and 0.1%. On
Garden, non-anchor quality approaches independently optimized GNS results at higher retention
and exceeds them at some lower budgets; qualitative results also show better low-budget structure.
Across 13 scenes, encoding reduces mean additional state from 390.0 to 85.7 MB.
Our contributions are:
• A representation of continuous-budget 3DGS as a joint trajectory of nested membership
and budget-specific parameter states, organized by progressively adapted anchors.
• ParetoGS, which connects those anchors through persistent identities, aligned residual encoding, and piecewise decoding to generate any intermediate count without separate optimization.
• Thirteen-scene multi-budget comparisons, non-anchor evaluation, and ablations showing
the value of adapted states and interval decoding, together with compact storage of the
anchor family.

R ELATED W ORK
3DGS representation and reconstruction. 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) combines explicit
Gaussians with differentiable rasterization. Subsequent work extends primitives and dynamic
scenes (Huang et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024), improves surface reconstruction (Yu et al., 2024b;
Chen et al., 2025), visibility and filtering (Radl et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024a), rendering and training
efficiency (Hanson et al., 2025a; Mallick et al., 2024), and initialization and capacity allocation (Kotovenko et al., 2026; Deng et al., 2026; Kheradmand et al., 2024). Wild-GS, PPISP, and Deblurring
3D-GS address appearance changes, camera photometry, and blur in real captures (Xu et al., 2024;
Deutsch et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2024a). We focus on organizing an already trained scene into an
asset decodable at arbitrary Gaussian budgets.
Compact and multilevel representations. Pruning reduces membership, whereas compression
encodes attributes more compactly. HAC, SOGS, CompGS, and Compact-3DGS explore context
coding, regular grids, vector quantization, and compact parameterization (Chen et al., 2024; Morgenstern et al., 2025; Navaneet et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2024b). LightGaussian, PUP 3D-GS, and
Trimming the Fat use contribution or sensitivity for pruning (Fan et al., 2024; Hanson et al., 2025b;
Ali et al., 2024); Mini-Splatting and GHAP use sampling or mixture reduction (Fang & Wang, 2024;
Wang et al., 2025). MaskGaussian and GNS integrate member selection with adaptation (Liu et al.,
2025c; Deng et al., 2025). Scale and spatial hierarchies organize models at different resolutions
or capacities (Yan et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b; Kerbl et al., 2024; Kulhanek
et al., 2025; Seo et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2026). H3DGS also interpolates
optimized parent–child attributes (Kerbl et al., 2024). Contribution orderings and layered decompositions yield cumulative representations (Zoomers et al., 2025; Tsai et al., 2025; Lavi et al., 2026),
sometimes with progressive compression or attribute precision (Di Sario et al., 2025; Chen et al.,
2026). ParetoGS uses GNS to build budget anchors and connects their aligned parameter states.
Arbitrary-budget decoding and adaptation. CLOD-3DGS fine-tunes random prefixes, MGS
jointly trains a prefix and the full set on each view, and Differentiable Hierarchies learns a counttruncatable ordering (Milef et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2026; Pan et al., 2026). These approaches reuse
the same Gaussian parameters across prefix budgets. RAVE reorders incremental members between
neighboring bitrate levels to connect intermediate rates and counts (Tran et al., 2026). LapisGS arranges content into cumulative resolution layers and interpolates readapted old-layer opacity while
retaining old members’ geometry and appearance (Shi et al., 2025). SCAR-GS progressively refines neural anchor features with residual vector quantization (Revilla et al., 2026); its residuals
approximate features rather than record optimized states at different budgets. NanoGS progressively merges local pairs to produce a compact model at a target count without image-supervised
optimization (Xiong et al., 2026). FlexGS learns both budget-conditioned selection and geometry
transformation; its paper optimizes these modules after 15K pretraining (Liu et al., 2025a). ParetoGS
instead stores a few explicitly optimized nested anchors, continues pruning from each adapted state,
and connects the geometry, opacity, and appearance of corresponding members. Experiments assess
the resulting quality at equal own-Full fractions, from a shared Full, and at non-anchor budgets.

3.2
S PARSE A DAPTED A NCHORS AND M EMBER C ORRESPONDENCE
We explicitly optimize a few budgets, adapting survivors at each anchor before starting the next
contraction.
Let M anchors have budgets Kmin = K1 < K2 < · · · < KM = N . From Full, each stage
contracts to the next target while optimizing parameters, then holds membership fixed and continues
optimizing pixel L1 and SSIM reconstruction losses. The adapted state is a decoding endpoint and
the next stage’s starting point. The chain deletes existing Gaussians without further densification;
persistent IDs maintain correspondence as parameters change.
When contracting upper anchor U to lower anchor L, we record deletions and reverse them to obtain
the entry order πL→U as budget grows. For L < K < U , decoding retains every member of the
lower anchor and admits the first K − L entries:
S(K) = S(L) ∪ πL→U [1 : K − L],
L < K < U.
(3)
Concatenating the smallest anchor with these entry sequences yields a global nested order, indexed
so budget K uses the first K rows.
Our approximately doubling schedule starts at 0.1% of the actual full count and ends at the full
budget, giving 11 anchors for every scene. The target counts are Kj = round(N ρj ) for ρj =
0.001, 0.002, . . . , 0.512, 1. When building downward from Full, each stage inherits the previous
anchor’s Gaussian parameters, optimizer state, and persistent IDs. It resets the iteration label to
14,999, uses GNS (Deng et al., 2025) to reach Kj during 15K–18K, and continues fixed-member
adaptation through 25K. GNS adjusts regularization in response to a prescribed linear opacity-decay
trajectory at the budget boundary, advances removal within the selection window, and uses its budget
safeguard to retain exactly Kj members. The Full model uses ImprovedGS (Deng et al., 2026)
densification; subsequent anchor construction follows GNS parameter optimization and pruning
with densification disabled.

L AYERED R ESIDUAL ATLAS
The layered residual atlas encodes all anchor changes by member layer and parameter field, with
Full as a common reference; decoding a request restores only its neighboring endpoints.
Let ΘKj be the parameter matrix at anchor Kj , one Gaussian per row, and order the full matrix ΘN
by the global member sequence. For an anchor, we store its changes relative to the corresponding
rows of the full model:
ΘKj = ΘN [1 : Kj ] + ∆j .

Rows of Bsf are bases, and rows of Csf are anchor coefficients. We retain all state components,

a shared channel basis that reconstructs every channel. These coefficients and other fields’ bases

E XPERIMENTS
We evaluate cross-budget reconstruction quality, budget-specific parameter states, interval-decoding
components, and additional storage to test whether sparse adapted anchors form a high-quality family of budgeted models.
4.1
E XPERIMENTAL S ETUP
Datasets and metrics. We report PSNR, SSIM (Wang et al., 2004), and LPIPS (Zhang et al.,
2018) on nine Mip-NeRF 360 (Barron et al., 2022) scenes, Train and Truck from Tanks and Temples (Knapitsch et al., 2017), and Playroom and DrJohnson from Deep Blending (Hedman et al.,
2018). The outdoor and indoor Mip-NeRF 360 scenes use the 4× and 2× downsampled images
5
provided by 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023); the other datasets use its provided versions. After sorting
image filenames, every eighth view is held out for testing and the rest are used for training. All
methods use the same test views and image sizes, with the three metrics computed by the 3DGS
evaluation implementation. The main tables and component and encoding ablations weight the 13
scenes equally; ablations first average ten interval midpoints within each scene.
Implementation and comparison protocol. We train a full model for 30K iterations with ImprovedGS (Deng et al., 2026), then progressively contract and adapt it with GNS (Deng et al., 2025),
retaining 11 budget anchors. The anchors span 0.1% to 100% of each scene’s full count; each interval uses ten training views to calibrate p, q. Appendix A gives the member-order, state-encoding,
and interval-calibration procedures. All experiments use an RTX 4090 GPU with data device
set to GPU.
We use the comparison methods’ official source code in two protocols. First, CLOD-3DGS (Milef
et al., 2025), MGS (Guo et al., 2026), and ParetoGS each use their own full model; we compare equal
retention ratios and report actual Gaussian counts. CLOD-3DGS trains down to 100K Gaussians.
Second, FlexGS-imp and NanoGS-imp receive the same ImprovedGS full anchor as ParetoGS, and
we compare equal requested budgets. FlexGS-imp runs the public FlexGS (Liu et al., 2025a) code’s
35K-iteration budget-adaptation stage, replacing the output of its standard 3DGS coarse stage with
the shared full model; this stage samples 1%, 5%, 10%, and 15%. Table 2 also queries 0.1% and
30% outside that sampled range. NanoGS-imp uses its official implementation to simplify the same
full model offline (Xiong et al., 2026). Table headers report 13-scene mean offline time: ParetoGS
includes full-model and anchor training, encoding, calibration, and packaging, while FlexGS-imp
includes the shared full-model construction and its 35K adaptation stage. Appendix B provides
per-scene quality, counts, and time.

Across 13 scenes, the atlas reduces mean additional state from 390.0 MB (FP32) to 85.7 MB (atlas),

C ONCLUSION
ParetoGS describes both Gaussian membership and budget-specific parameter states with sparse
adapted anchors. Nested member sets and persistent identities establish a cross-budget member
order, while shared-state interpolation and an entering-member gate connect discrete anchors into a
family of models decodable at requested counts. A residual atlas compresses the additional adapted
states.
Across 13 scenes, ParetoGS achieves better mean quality than the compared prefix methods with
fewer Gaussians at 10% retention. From a shared full model, it also improves over FlexGS-imp
at its sampled 1%, 5%, and 10% budgets and over NanoGS-imp at 1% and 0.1%. Continuousbudget curves and component ablations across 13 scenes further demonstrate the value of adapted
parameters and interval decoding. The compressed atlas reduces mean additional state from 390.0
to 85.7 MB with little average quality change. Organizing budget-specific states into one asset thus
provides higher-quality multi-budget decoding from a single scene representation. In a three-scene
asset comparison, ParetoGS uses about 17% more total storage than FlexGS-imp; its mean offline
construction time across 13 scenes is shorter than those of CLOD-3DGS, MGS, and FlexGS-imp.

S TATE E NCODING , C ALIBRATION , AND B UDGET D ECODING
This section specifies the member ordering, residual encoding, interval calibration, and decoding
rules used by the main method. Given constructed nested adapted anchors and the full model, the
runtime procedure returns parameters for exactly K Gaussians at any valid integer budget.
A.1
M EMBER O RDERING AND R ESIDUAL ATLAS
When contracting an upper anchor U to a lower anchor L, we record deleted IDs. Reversing each
deletion trace yields the order in which members enter as budget increases. Starting with the smallest
anchor and concatenating these entry sequences gives the global nested ordering used in the paper;
full-model parameters are reordered accordingly. Anchors are aligned to this ordering by ID, and
their residuals relative to Full are computed in raw parameter coordinates. The six fields are position,
log scale, quaternion, opacity logit, DC color, and higher-order SH coefficients, giving 59 scalar
components per Gaussian at SH degree three.
Non-full anchor residuals are converted to FP16 before stacking by member layer and parameter
field. Each layer undergoes an orthogonal decomposition along the anchor-state dimension. The
maximum supplied state-axis rank is ten; all 11 − s available components of layer s are retained,
with no truncation along that dimension. The 45 higher-order SH channels use a rank-36 channel subspace, while decoding still outputs all 45 channels. For non-SH state bases and the perGaussian SH-channel coefficients, we apply symmetric INT8 quantization independently to each
channel within blocks of 64 Gaussian rows:

B UDGET-S WITCH PARAMETER C ONSTRUCTION T IME
We measure the time required to construct Gaussian parameters for a requested budget after the
model has been loaded. The four methods use the same 13 scenes and an RTX 4090 GPU, running
serially in separate processes. Before timing, CLOD-3DGS and MGS have their Full parameters
and prefix orders resident on the GPU; FlexGS-imp has its Full parameters and selector; ParetoGS
has all anchor fields predecoded and resident. At a switch, CLOD-3DGS and MGS construct rendering parameters for the requested prefix, FlexGS-imp selects members with its native threshold
and applies deformation, and ParetoGS interpolates endpoint states and gates entering members. A
Full request to ParetoGS reuses the resident model.
We synchronize CUDA and read host wall time t0 before a request, then synchronize after target
parameters are constructed and read t1 . The reported time is Tswitch = t1 − t0 . After each timed
request, we render the first test view of that scene before the next request. The trajectory covers 16
ratios from 0.1% through 100%, including forward, reverse, and cross-interval changes. After three
repetitions and removing requests with no change in ratio, each method contributes 108 switches
per scene and 1,404 across 13 scenes.
