The quest for realistic computer synthesized images began in the mid sixties. To generate images which realistically simulate an actual scene, the behavior and perception of visible light must be modeled. This involves both physics and psychology in a challenge that has inspired some of the most analytically innovative computer graphics research. Generally, the utilization of a particular illumination model represents a compromise between image precision and computing cost. Dramatic increases in computing capabilities have promoted the search for increasingly accurate illumination models. Future computing platforms promise a continuance of research effort into modeling techniques providing even greater image realism. This paper is a tutorial which presents a technical-level chronology of illumination model development. Included are treatments of Lambertian (constant) shading, Gouraud shading, Phong shading, the Torrence-Sparrow illumination model, Whitted’s original ray-tracing based illumination model, and inter-reflections between diffuse surfaces (radiosity)
