Summary
A planar viewport of the generic architecture (PLANAR_NEXT) draws the acquisition plane from
the volume texture when the data has a volume. When the GPU capability profile reduces that
texture along the slice axis, each displayed slice is an average of several acquired slices.
The fidelity indicator then shows the acquisition plane as lossy.
The viewport already has an image path that draws the original frame. This issue asks the
viewport to use that path in the acquisition plane when the volume texture is reduced along
the slice axis.
Current behaviour
The example is a CT of 512 × 512 × 2464 under the profile medium (texture edge 2048). The
volume texture is 512 × 512 × 1232, with the reduction factors [1, 1, 2].

The cause is isVolumeBackedDataSet in PlanarRenderPathDecisionService.ts. The function
returns true when the data set has a volumeId, and it does not look at
isAcquisitionPath. OHIF gives a volume to each MPR viewport, so the axial viewport of the
OHIF MPR layout always uses the volume path. OHIF/Viewers#6295 shows this with
?useNextViewports=true.
User requirements
AQP-U-1. In the acquisition plane, the viewport shows the acquired slice at full
resolution when the volume texture is reduced only for the GPU. The fidelity indicator
does not show the acquisition plane as lossy for that reason.
AQP-U-2. The position of the image, the measurements, the annotations and the
segmentations do not change when the viewport changes between the two paths.
AQP-U-3. A slab or a MIP of more than one acquired slice keeps its current result.
Implementation requirements
Edited on 2026-11-03. The first version of this section described a remount between the volume
path and the image path. #3018 now keeps both render paths mounted and switches their
visibility. The user requirements above do not change.
These requirements give the recommended approach. They can change when a better approach
appears.
AQP-I-1. The decision service always selects the volume render mode for a data set with
a volume. A change of orientation does not remount the data. getCurrentMode() gives
'volume', and setViewReference uses the volume branch in every orientation.
AQP-I-2. The volume slice path of the source keeps a second render path mounted: the
image path (VtkImageMapperRenderPath) for the acquired frames. Both actors stay in the
renderer. The render path changes only the visibility of the two actors. It does not add
or remove an actor for a change of orientation.
AQP-I-3. The frame actor is visible when all of these conditions are true. Otherwise the
volume slice actor is visible.
The view is in the acquisition plane: the tilt of the view plane normal from the scan
axis moves the plane by less than half a slice across the in-plane extent of the volume
(utilities.isAcquisitionPlaneNormal). Either direction of the scan axis counts. The 0.99
tolerance of VolumeViewport.isInAcquisitionPlane still decides only whether a reference
to an image counts as the view.
canDrawAcquiredFrames is true: the data set has a cached volume that is not dynamic,
final downsampling with the active GPU settings reduces the volume texture on at least
one axis, the volume path draws from a GPU texture, the slab is not thicker than one
acquired slice, and the blend mode is the composite blend. Use
volumeTextureReductionFactors. Do not add a copy of that arithmetic.
The frame of the current plane has loaded.
The volume navigation (the camera focal point) gives the frame. The frame index is
round(worldToIndex(focalPoint)[2]) on the full-resolution volume grid. A labelmap that
reslices the same grid rounds in the same way, so the frame and the labelmap stay on one
plane, also between two frames.
AQP-I-4. The frame actor moves along the view plane normal onto the plane of the focal
point, so that an overlay at that plane stays in front of the frame. This translation is for
the rendering only. The frame index, the events and the view references keep the nearest
frame.
AQP-I-5. The frame uses the presentation of the volume: the VOI range, the colormap, the
invert and the interpolation. The default VOI is the default VOI of the volume, not the
window of each frame. The frame uses the linear VOI function of the volume slice, also when
the frame or the presentation states a sigmoid. Limit: the frame does not apply the opacity
table of a colormap.
AQP-I-6. A volume overlay does not stop the frames. The frames show with a volume
labelmap of a segmentation and with a fusion overlay. The actor of an overlay stays above
both source actors. An overlay keeps the render backend of the source (vtk.js or CPU).
AQP-I-7. The fidelity indicator follows the visible actor. The hidden rendering sets
excludeFromFidelity, and the viewport reads each rendering of the binding. While the
reduced slice shows, the indicator shows the reduction. While the frame shows, the
indicator shows the quality of the frame.
AQP-I-8. Until the frame of the current plane arrives, the volume slice shows.
AQP-I-9. Each slice change sends one VOLUME_NEW_IMAGE and one STACK_NEW_IMAGE, also
while the frame shows. The STACK_NEW_IMAGE of a volume source has no image.
AQP-I-10. Legacy viewports do not change. The legacy adapter of the planar viewport
does not draw the frames.
Tests
API (jest):
For a reduced volume in the acquisition plane, the decision service selects the volume
render mode, and canDrawAcquiredFrames is true. For a slab of more than one slice, for a
MIP, and with no reduction, canDrawAcquiredFrames is false.
utilities.isAcquisitionPlaneNormal accepts both directions of the scan axis, and
refuses a tilt of 5 degrees.
User experience (jest):
In the acquisition plane, getCurrentMode() gives 'volume'. The reduced slice shows
until the frame loads, and then the frame shows. A scroll sends one event of each type.
A change from axial to sagittal and back loads the data one time, switches the visible
actor, and keeps the camera position and the image index.
A sagittal view reference with a volumeId turns an axial viewport sagittal.
The frame uses the VOI range and the linear VOI function of the volume.
Between two frames, the frame actor moves onto the focal plane.
With a volume labelmap, the frames show, and both source actors are below the labelmap.
User experience (browser, tests/vitest-browser/renderPathParity.browser.test.ts, mode
vtkVolumeSliceFrames):
In the acquisition plane, the frame draws the same pixels as the stack, and the viewport
is not lossy.
A tilt of 5 degrees shows the reduced slice, and the viewport is lossy. A change to
sagittal hides the frame, and the return to axial shows the same frame actor.
Out of scope
Legacy viewports. Their fix needs a full-resolution slab texture that follows the current
slice. MutableVolumeTextureSlab in VolumeTextureSet.ts gives the storage, but no
strategy provisions such a slab yet.
A change to the default viewports of OHIF.
