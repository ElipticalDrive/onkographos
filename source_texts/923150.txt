Summary
Ambient occlusion darkens areas where surfaces are close together — corners, crevices, the base of walls — approximating the way indirect light is blocked by nearby geometry. It adds significant perceived depth and realism to indoor scenes with little or no artistic effort. Screen-Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO) is the standard real-time approach.
Behavior
A depth buffer and view-space normals are sampled for the current frame
For each pixel, a hemisphere of sample points is tested against the depth buffer to estimate how occluded that point is
The occlusion factor is blurred (separable Gaussian or bilateral) to reduce noise
The blurred occlusion map is multiplied into the ambient lighting term of the final scene composite
Tunable parameters:
Radius — world-space hemisphere radius for sample testing (controls how far-reaching the occlusion is)
Bias — depth offset to prevent self-occlusion artifacts on flat surfaces
Intensity — strength of the darkening effect
Runtime Requirement
SSAO must be togglable on/off at runtime from a Graphics settings menu. Radius, bias, and intensity should also be runtime-adjustable. When disabled, the occlusion pass is fully skipped.
Acceptance Criteria
SSAO can be enabled and disabled at runtime with no restart
Radius, bias, and intensity are runtime-tunable
Corners and surface contacts are visibly darker when SSAO is enabled
Toggling SSAO off produces no visible change to the scene other than removing the occlusion darkening
No significant depth artifacts (halos, self-shadowing on flat surfaces) at default settings
Notes
SSAO requires access to a view-space depth buffer and normals, which may require rendering to a G-buffer or adding a normals render target. This is the most architecturally involved effect in Tier 2 and should be planned alongside the intermediate FBO pipeline.
