Abstract\n\nLine Integral Convolution (LIC), introduced in 1993 by Cabral and Leedom [1], is a powerful texture-based \ntechnique for vector field visualization. Based upon blurring an input texture locally along field lines \nof a given vector field, it generates images that convey the directional information of the field. Due to \nthe computational expense of generating the 3D textures and the difficulties of effectively displaying \nthe resulting output textures, the application of LIC has most commonly been to depict vector fields in \n2D or over a surface in 3D. We propose and study methods and strategies for more efficient visualization \nof three-dimensional vector fields with LIC.\n\nFor large 3D data sets (512^3 or greater), even the Fast LIC algorithm proposed by Stalling and Hege [2] \nis very computationally intensive. We present a new method for computing 3D LIC textures called Seed LIC.\nThis method exploits the sparsity of the input texture by calculating field lines and computing the \nconvolution starting from a set of distributed points (the seed points) only. The seed points can be \nchosen using certain properties of the field to be visualized, e.g. the vector magnitude. Sparse input \ntextures have been used together with LIC to reveal the interior structures of the field [3]. The Seed \nLIC algorithm can be faster than the Fast LIC algorithm by more than an order of magnitude, and can thus \nbe used in an interactive setting. Instead of a computation time of several hours, the computation time \nfor generating the LIC textures can be reduced to a few minutes or even seconds. Earlier, 3D LIC have \nbeen thought of as a tool only to visualize vector fields in those instances where high image quality is\ndesired and the ability to generate images at an interactive rate is not required.\n\nTo reveal the depth relation among the eld lines, a shading, or halo, technique called limb darkening \nis used. To emphasize the halo effect and to reduce aliasing effects due to the use of voxels to represent \nthe field lines, the textures are oversampled by a factor of 2 to 4 in each direction and then convolved \nwith an isotropic 3*3*3 filter. This approach smears the field lines outwards, making the strokes in the \noutput texture thicker and the 3D shape more clear.\n\nFinally, we show how direct volume rendering applied to 3D LIC can be used to display additional \ninformation about related scalar quantities when visualizing vector elds. This includes the presentation \nof a new two-field visualization technique, which we have called candy cane visualization. We use this \ntechnique to depict the directional structures of the vorticity field inside vortices obtained from a \nsimulation of strati ed shear turbulence by Werne and Fritts [4].\n\nReferences\n\n[1] B.
