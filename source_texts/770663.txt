Part of #415.
Several kernels need prefix sums:
the slot offsets of the model's pair list (ML1 step 3: the model term's pair list from MDIR's neighbor structures (D[mlip-pairlist]) #386, D277);
the bin offsets of tiled spreading (PME on a device: spreading through tiles of bins, chosen by the size of the grid (D[pme-tiles]) #410 avoided a scan with fixed-capacity runs and an overflow list);
the counts and offsets of the neighbor builds and pruning;
the incidence rows of tuples (docs/device-tuples.md).
CUB's device scan is single-pass by decoupled look-back (device/device_scan.cuh, and agent/single_pass_scan_operators.cuh; Merrill and Garland, "Single-pass Parallel Prefix Scan with Decoupled Look-back", NVIDIA Technical Report NVR-2016-002). Its block scans have raking and warp-scan variants (block/block_scan.cuh).
Work
Inventory the scans in MDIR's device code: how each is done today (one block, Hillis–Steele, several kernels, or on the host), its length, and its time per step on the Amber suite.
Where a scan is on the critical path, implement a single-pass scan as a lowering of an md_exec scan op (or a runtime kernel), with the old path behind a switch.
Revisit PME on a device: spreading through tiles of bins, chosen by the size of the grid (D[pme-tiles]) #410's fixed-capacity runs: does an exact scan beat the overflow list?
Integer scans give the same results in any order, so the deterministic mode can use them too.
