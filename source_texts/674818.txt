Abstract
Three-dimensional Gaussian splashing technology provides a new representation
paradigm that combines high fidelity and computational efficiency for dynamic reconstruction and real-time interaction of facial expressions in virtual portraits. This
article systematically discusses the mechanism of virtual portrait expression modeling and interactive implementation based on a 3D Gaussian model. At the theoretical level, the geometric mathematical representation, dynamic properties, and
differentiable rendering support of Gaussian primitives were elucidated, and the
modeling requirements for spatiotemporal continuity of facial expression motion
were analyzed. At the methodological level, a mapping architecture from the expression parameter space to Gaussian attributes was constructed, and deformation
fields based on temporal continuity, high-frequency micro-expression adaptive refinement strategies, and geometric texture fidelity constraints were proposed. At
the interactive level, real-time inference paths, multimodal coupling frameworks,
anti-drift mechanisms, and sparse computing collaborative optimization schemes
were designed. Theoretical analysis and methodological verification indicate that
this framework can effectively support low-latency and high-stability immersive
virtual portrait interaction.
Keywords
3D Gaussian splash; virtual portrait; dynamic reconstruction of facial expressions;
real-time interaction
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of virtual reality, digital humans, and human-computer interaction technology, how to
achieve high fidelity, low latency dynamic reconstruction, and natural interaction of virtual portrait expressions has
become a research hotspot in the fields of computer graphics and multimedia. Traditional methods based on grid
deformation or neural radiation fields have inherent limitations in terms of expression detail, real-time rendering
efficiency, and interactive stability. 3D Gaussian splashing, as an emerging scene representation technology, provides
a new solution for facial expression modeling of virtual portraits with its explicit geometric modeling, differentiable
rendering, and dynamic attribute extension capabilities [1]. This article systematically discusses the dynamic reconstruction and interactive implementation of virtual portrait expressions based on a three-dimensional Gaussian model.
Firstly, the theoretical basis and representation mechanism of the three-dimensional Gaussian model are explained.
Then, key methods for dynamic reconstruction of expressions are proposed, and the implementation mechanism for
real-time interaction is further explored. Finally, the advantages and future research directions of this technology path
are summarized.

2. Theoretical Basis and Characterization Mechanism of the Three-dimensional Gaussian Model
2.1 Geometric and Mathematical Representation of Three-dimensional Gaussian Splashing
Three-dimensional Gaussian splashing, as an emerging scene representation method, relies on parameterized modeling of continuous geometric and appearance information in space using a three-dimensional Gaussian distribution
function with a covariance matrix. Each Gaussian primitive is defined by parameters such as center position, covariance matrix, spherical harmonic coefficient, and opacity, and achieves flexible approximation of complex surface
shapes through the orientation and stretching of ellipsoids in three-dimensional space. Unlike traditional implicit
representations such as neural radiation fields, Gaussian splashing does not require dense sampling in volume rendering, but directly projects these explicit Gaussian primitives onto a two-dimensional image plane and uses splashing
operations to blend opacity pixel by pixel [2]. Mathematical representation of this geometry has obvious advantages,
as it retains the editability of explicit representation, and can also achieve end-to-end parameter optimization through
differentiable rendering. In modeling of virtual portraits, 3D Gaussian splatting can use a set of Gaussian primitives
to cover different regions of the face, such as “eyes, lips, and cheeks”, providing a geometric foundation for subsequent dynamic reconstruction of facial expressions [3]. The learnability of the covariance matrix enables Gaussian
primitives to adaptively adjust their shapes, thereby depicting local geometric features such as wrinkles and dimples,
laying the mathematical foundation for fine modeling of facial expressions.
2.2 Dynamic Properties and Differentiable Rendering Support of Gaussian Primitives
Dynamic attributes of Gaussian primitives are key features that distinguish them from static scene representations,
and each Gaussian primitive not only contains static geometric and appearance parameters, but can also introduce
dynamic components that vary with time or expression control signals, such as position offsets, covariance matrix
changes, and spherical harmonics modulation parameters, which constitute the core that distinguishes them from
static representations. This dynamic attribute enables three-dimensional Gaussian models to respond to different facial action units, and also realizes transformation from static measurements to spatiotemporal deformation [4], with
supporting this dynamic process being the technique of differentiable rendering, which projects three-dimensional
Gaussian primitives onto plane of two-dimensional images and calculates accumulated color of each pixel, and rendering pipeline can output predicted results with same resolution as input images, thereby constructing loss functions
with real expression images. Because the entire forward process possesses differentiability, the network can backpropagate gradients to update dynamic parameters of Gaussian primitives, and for reconstruction of virtual portrait
expressions, differentiable rendering not only provides the feasibility of end-to-end training, but also ensures consistency between geometric deformation and appearance changes during expression changes, and more importantly,
its efficiency makes real-time expression driving possible, thus providing technical support for interactive scenes.
3. Dynamic Reconstruction Method for Virtual Portrait Expressions
3.1 Mapping Architecture from Expression Parameter Space to Gaussian Attributes
The primary step in achieving dynamic reconstruction of virtual portrait expressions is to establish a differentiable
mapping relationship between the expression parameter space and Gaussian primitive attributes. The expression parameter space is usually composed of several action units or low-dimensional semantic control vectors defined by
the facial action encoding system, and each parameter independently controls the expression intensity of a certain
local area. The core of mapping architecture lies in designing a function form that can convert the discrete or continuous control signals into Gaussian primitive position, covariance, color, opacity, and other attribute changes [5]. An
effective solution is to use a lightweight multi-layer perceptron as the mapping network, with facial expression parameters as inputs and Gaussian attribute offsets as outputs, and to achieve end-to-end learning through differentiable
rendering error supervision during the training phase. This architecture also needs to consider the differences in receptive fields of different Gaussian primitives in the facial region: the Gaussian in the mouth region is sensitive to
the mouth action unit, while the Gaussian in the eye region mainly responds to signals such as blinking and eyebrow
raising.

3.2 Construction of Expression Deformation Field Based on Temporal Continuity
Single-frame expression mapping is difficult to ensure the coherence of expression motion between adjacent frames,
and it is necessary to explicitly construct a deformation field with temporal constraints in three-dimensional Gaussian
space. Deformation field is essentially a function of spacetime, which receives expression parameters of current frame,
also receives information of historical states, and outputs position offset of each Gaussian primitive at current moment
as well as gradient of deformation, in order to ensure temporal continuity, recurrent neural units and temporal selfattention mechanisms can be introduced into architecture of mapping, thus making update of Gaussian attributes of
current frame dependent on output of hidden state of previous frame [6]. This design can effectively suppress the
jumping phenomenon, and this phenomenon originates from the sudden change of facial expression parameters, while
it can also simulate the process of elastic natural recovery of facial muscles. The deformation field should also distinguish different types of facial expressions. For rapid transient expressions such as blinking, it needs to maintain a
higher response speed, but allows short temporal discontinuities. For slowly changing expressions, such as transitioning from a neutral expression to a smile, it should emphasize smooth transitions, with learnable temporal filtering
parameters enabling the deformation field to adaptively adjust the response speed of different action units.
3.3 Gaussian Adaptive Refinement Strategy for High-frequency Micro-expressions
Real human facial expressions contain a large amount of high-frequency micro-expression information, such as the
dynamic display of fine lines at the corners of the eyes, slight trembling of the lips, or instantaneous twitching of the
eyebrows. These details are crucial for enhancing the realism and credibility of virtual portraits. However, a fixed
number of Gaussian primitives is difficult to simultaneously meet the modeling requirements of low-frequency large
deformations and high-frequency micro expressions on a global scale. The core of the Gaussian adaptive refinement
strategy lies in dynamically adjusting the spatial distribution and quantity of Gaussian primitives: increasing density
in the micro expression occurrence area and maintaining sparsity in the static area [7]. In a specific implementation,
the second-order degree of the rendering error map or deformation field can be used as a refinement criterion. When
the rendering error of a local area continues to exceed the threshold or the deformation changes dramatically, the
Gaussian primitives in that area can be split or copied. When splitting, it is necessary to initialize the position and
covariance of the sub-Gaussian reasonably to avoid introducing rendering artifacts caused by abrupt changes. Meanwhile, the refinement operation should be compatible with the differentiable rendering process, meaning that the
refined Gaussian set still needs to support end-to-end gradient feedback [8]. In addition, to control computational
overhead, a periodic coarsening mechanism can be introduced to merge or remove redundant Gaussian primitives
when high-frequency micro-expressions fade away.
3.4 Geometric and Texture Fidelity Constraints in Dynamic Reconstruction
In the process of dynamic reconstruction of facial expressions, geometric deformation and texture appearance must
maintain inherent consistency; there will be inaccurate phenomena such as “geometric distortion while texture remains unchanged” or “texture misalignment while geometric correctness”. Geometric fidelity constraints require that
the position and covariance changes of Gaussian primitives should not compromise the integrity of facial topology.
For example, when the mouth is opened, the stretching amplitude of the corners of the mouth should be coordinated
with the deformation gradient of the surrounding area to avoid penetration or overlap [9]. The texture fidelity constraint requires the spherical harmonic coefficients of Gaussian primitives to maintain adjustments corresponding to
geometric deformations during expression changes. For example, when facial skin is stretched, the directionality of
specular reflections should change rather than remain unchanged. To achieve dual fidelity constraints, geometric
regularization terms and texture consistency regularization terms can be designed in the loss function. The geometric
regularization term can be defined as the deformation consistency loss of the relative position between adjacent
Gaussian primitives, while the texture consistency regularization term can use the optical flow field to constrain the
amplitude of the spherical harmonic coefficient change at the same spatial point before and after the expression
change.

4. Interactive Implementation Mechanism Based on the Gaussian Model
4.1 Gaussian Update and Inference Path Driven by Real-time Facial Expressions
In interactive scenarios, virtual portraits need to respond to external expression driving signals within millisecondlevel delays, which imposes strict computational efficiency requirements on the parameter updates and inference
paths of Gaussian models. The traditional forward propagation method requires traversing all Gaussian primitives
and sequentially calculating projection and mixing operations, making it difficult to achieve real-time frame rates
with limited hardware resources. For this purpose, a hierarchical reasoning path can be designed: dividing Gaussian
primitives into independent subsets based on facial regions, and updating and rendering only the Gaussian of relevant
regions based on the spatial range of the current driving signal [10]. Meanwhile, the update of Gaussian parameters
should not rely on complete forward-backward iterations, but should use a pre-trained mapping network for a single
feedforward calculation, avoiding online optimization processes. To further reduce latency, an incremental update
strategy can be introduced, which only caches the Gaussian attributes of the previous frame and calculates only the
attribute offset caused by the change in expression parameters in the current frame, rather than regenerating all parameters. The inference path should also be parallelized at the data flow level: the projection, depth sorting, and
splashing operations of Gaussian primitives are all executed concurrently in blocks on the graphics processing unit.
4.2 Coupling Framework Between Multimodal Input (Speech/Visual) and Facial Expression Response
In natural human-computer interaction, the signal sources that drive facial expressions are often not single-dimensional control parameters, but contain multimodal information such as speech semantics, speech emotions, facial
capture data, and eye movements. The expression response mechanism of the Gaussian model requires spatiotemporal alignment and coupling of these heterogeneous signals to generate coordinated and consistent expression outputs. The core of the coupling framework is a multimodal fusion module that receives input features from different
modalities and maps them to a shared expression latent space. The speech modality can extract phoneme content and
prosodic features, driving mouth shape and emotional expression respectively; the visual modality obtains the user’s
expression coefficient through real-time facial keypoint detection, which is used to drive the imitation response of
virtual portraits [11]. The fusion module also needs to solve the problem of modal asynchrony: speech information
is about tens of milliseconds ahead of lip movement, while visual capture is close to real-time, requiring the introduction of timing alignment units to synchronize control signals from different sources. On this basis, the frame shall
support dynamic adjustment of modal weight, and automatically degenerate into a response mode dominated by other
modes when a certain mode is unreliable or missing.
4.3 Stability and Anti-drift Design of the Gaussian Model During the Interaction Process
During long-term interaction, if the dynamic parameters of Gaussian primitives lack effective constraint mechanisms,
there may be a “drift” phenomenon caused by accumulated errors, manifested as the facial reference posture gradually
deviating from the original neutral face, and local areas experiencing geometric collapse or excessive stretching due
to continuous deformation. Anti-drift design first requires the introduction of regularization constraints at the parameter update level, such as designing a differentiable “reset” path, so that Gaussian primitives can accurately restore
their initial geometric and appearance states when detecting expression parameters return to zero, rather than being
stuck in historical cumulative positions [12]. Secondly, a periodic anchoring mechanism should be set up in the temporal sequence, using pre-stored neutral face Gaussian parameters to perform soft calibration on the current state
every fixed interaction time to prevent long-term error accumulation. Stability is also reflected in the ability to tolerate
abnormal inputs: when there is instantaneous noise or extreme values in the driving signal (such as large displacement
caused by false detection of facial keypoints), the Gaussian model should not produce severe nonphysical jumps.
4.4 Collaborative Optimization of Low-latency Rendering and Gaussian Sparse Computation
Rendering delay is a key bottleneck that restricts the immersive experience of virtual portrait interaction, and the
computational load of Gaussian splashing is linearly related to the number of primitives, which must be reduced
while ensuring image quality. The core of Gaussian sparse computing lies in utilizing visual redundancy and motion
sparsity: not all Gaussian primitives make a perceptible contribution to the rendering results of each frame. A cropping

5. Conclusion
In summary, the dynamic reconstruction and interactive implementation of virtual portrait expressions based on 3D
Gaussian models effectively support end-to-end mapping from expression parameters to dynamic deformations
through the explicit geometric representation and differentiable rendering mechanism of Gaussian primitives. At the
reconstruction level, the temporal continuous deformation field and adaptive refinement strategy ensure the naturalness of facial expressions and the realism of micro expressions. At the interactive level, the real-time inference path,
multimodal coupling framework, anti-drift design, and sparse computing collaborative optimization jointly achieve
a low latency and high stability immersive experience. This framework provides a technical path that combines theoretical completeness and engineering feasibility for the emotional expression of virtual digital humans and natural
human-computer interaction.
