ABSTRACT
We have developed a display system that achieves to render palmsized volumetric contents in real space. The system consisted of
two optical paths for laser rendering, in which femtosecond-laserexcited emission points were scanned by a liquid-crystal spatial
light modulator and a 3D laser scanner. A single volume was formed
by the cooperative dual-path laser rendering based on the optimal
design of the optical system and scanning path. Volumetric contents
such as volumetric graphics and animations with well-filled voxels
were demonstrated in a centimeter-sized volume.

INTRODUCTION
Volumetric display allows multiple people to view a 3D graphics that
naturally satisfies human depth perception simultaneously with
the naked eye. By contrast, this technique is still in the research
phase due to the difficulty of generating and arranging voxels in
physical space.
Optical generation of voxels is promising method for rendering
natural volumetric graphics with a wide viewing angle due to creating the rendering volume without physical wiring or high-speed
scanning of screen. Focused irradiation of ultrashort-pulse laser
has been demonstrated to be capable of generating voxels in the
air [Kimura et al. 2006], and the applying femtosecond lasers and
computer-generated holograms (CGHs) has indicated potential for
improving the number of voxels [Kumagai et al. 2021; Ochiai et al.
2016]. Particle levitation based on photophoretic traps has used
to generate light scattering voxels that can represent any color

two optical paths. A Yb: KGW femtosecond laser (Pharos series
PH1SP-10-1M, Light Conversion) with a center wavelength of 1030
nm, a repetition frequency of 10 kHz, and a pulse duration of < 155
fs was split into two optical paths which have pulse energy of 480
µJ each using a half-wave plate and a polarizing beam splitter, and
then focused by an F𝜃 lens with a focal length of 100 mm onto the
rendering space to form an emission point based on multiphoton
excitation. Before focusing, the phase of laser was modulated by
CGHs displayed on a LCSLM (IL-WU070, JVCKENWOOD) with
a pixel pitch of 8 µm and a display area of 1920 × 1200 pixels.
Phase modulation by CGH makes it possible to control the number
and position of focal points, thus enabling rendering a graphics or
increasing the number of voxels per frame. A two-axis galvanometer
scanners (GVS312, Thorlabs) and a varifocal lens (TL-16-40-TC,
Optotune) change the deflection angle and spread angle of the laser
incident on the F𝜃 lens, respectively, to perform a volumetric 3D
scan of the emission points. A 100 × 100 × 100 mm3 glass cell filled
with Xenon gas (750 Torr) was placed in the rendering space to
reduce the threshold energy for excitation to form voxels and to
increase the brightness intensity. A concave lens with a focal length
of -568 mm placed immediately after the varifocal lens was inserted
to avoid damage of the optical elements due to reflected light from
the glass cell surface.

2.2
Rendering
Rendering was performed by obtaining the scanning data to be
given to the galvanometer scanner and varifocal lens on the two
optical paths from the 3D point cloud data. The target 3D point
cloud data was divided into two volumes along a 𝑧 direction that
same with the optical axis for optical path 1 and optical path 2.
The divided point data were each sliced in the 𝑧 direction, and
was sorted so that the scanning path was the shortest. A graphics
were formed by stacking these 2D slices in the 𝑧 direction. The 3D
scanning data was converted to a voltage signal via a D/A converter
and apply to the galvanometer scanner and the varifocal lens.

4
CONCLUSIONS
We have proposed the display system with dual-path which render
volumetric graphics using CGHs and a 3D laser scanning. Palmsized volumetric graphics and animations that were able to be
viewed in real time and real space have been demonstrated by
cooperative rendering in two optical paths.
