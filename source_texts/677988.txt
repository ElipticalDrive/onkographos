Abstract
With the rapid development of Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR), 3D avatar reconstruction has garnered
extensive attention from researchers. However, challenges remain in multi-view-based avatar reconstruction, particularly in
accurately aligning features such as hair, clothing, and facial details. Additionally, limitations in avatar representation can lead
to unrealistic outcomes under varying lighting conditions. This paper addresses these challenges by introducing a novel tilebased rasterization method that enhances the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework for avatar reconstruction. Our approach
utilizes Gaussian ellipsoids initialized through Patch Match Stereo (PMS) and employs both active and passive optimization
strategies to refine Gaussian internal parameters and perform effective density filtering. This innovation results in high-fidelity
reconstructions without visual artifacts, eliminating the need for facial parameter models and background sampling guidance.
Furthermore, our method enables the generation of avatar radiation fields using only monocular video input, simplifying the
reconstruction process and reducing execution costs. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional
techniques and existing avatar radiation field methods, showcasing its flexibility and lightweight pipeline architecture. This
work achieved a competitive results in avatar rendering performance metrics, providing a streamlined solution for high-quality
avatar representation.

Related work
Our approach is an end-to-end radiance field avatar reconstruction pipeline based on GS (Gaussian Splatting) technology. Compared to existing point-based rendering methods, it
does not require MLP layers that consume significant time
for optimization, nor does it rely on low-dimensional morphable models as priors or guidance. We primarily build
upon the Differentiable Tile Rasterizer from 3DGS [10]
as the key technology for avatar reconstruction, and combine it with Patch Match Stereo(PMS) [13, 14] to generate
high-dimensional feature representations for avatars. The
following section will provide an analysis of these related
technologies.
Reconstruction based on a morphable model
The 3D Morphable Model (3DMM) [1] and the Basel Face
Model (BFM) [15] have long been favored by researchers
as low-dimensional yet efficient facial coefficient models.
These models are commonly used as priors to guide training networks in most Avatar Reconstruction tasks [16–18].
However, the facial meshes generated by these methods often
fail to fully capture high-dimensional texture information
and realistic surface structures. Some approaches[19–22]
focus on addressing these challenges. Additionally, the highdimensional and editable facial model FLAME [23, 24] has
garnered significant attention in the Avatar Reconstruction
community. In our method, we no longer rely on such models for auxiliary learning.
Human avatar reconstruction
Augmented Reality (AR) technology holds substantial application and commercial value in the digital human industry.
The advent of 3DMM [1] in the 1990 s marked the beginning of 3D face reconstruction methods based on statistical
models, laying the foundation for subsequent research. FaceWareHouse [2] provided crucial resources for data-driven
3D face reconstruction methods, fueling the development
of both statistical and machine learning approaches. Today,
a range of deep learning-based methods [16, 17, 25–30]
has elevated AR technology to new heights. Notably, these
methods are non-end-to-end pipelines based on morphable
models [23, 24], with most relying on mesh representations.
Some specialized methods [31] use deep neural networks to
represent the entire head mesh. However, due to geometric
estimation errors, mesh-based avatars are often affected by
blurry textures. With the advent of Neural Radiance Fields
(NeRF) [11], meshless representation of avatars has become
a new research focus. Some attempts have been made to
directly establish NeRF on the avatar mesh template, offering potential solutions to the limitations of mesh-based avatar

Point-based radiance field rendering
Points, as discrete and unstructured representations in a
scene, can effectively fit geometries with arbitrary topologies [32]. Coupled with differentiable rendering strategies
and the learning of point intrinsic parameters, they can produce excellent view rendering results, as well as new view
synthesis and 3D radiance field reconstruction. NeRF [11],
with its high computational cost and low model training efficiency, has been outperformed by 3DGS [10]. While 3DGS
excels over NeRF in view rendering metrics, when applied
to the more refined task of Avatar Reconstruction, it heavily
depends on the reasonable initialization of Structure-fromMotion (SfM) [33] points due to simpler density control
strategies. Several outstanding works have integrated GS
technology into Avatar Reconstruction tasks, but still rely
on 3DMM [1] facial coefficients as guidance. For instance,
GaussianAvatars [7] directly defines Gaussian ellipsoids on
a FLAME-based mesh to achieve high-precision 4D avatar
reconstruction, while NPGA [34] uses Neural Parametric
Head Models(NPHM) [35] to represent dynamic features of
the head. These methods have achieved impressive results in
reconstructing dynamic avatars but rely on massive inputs,
such as 16 camera positions and over 4700 2k images. In
contrast, when the number of views for avatar representation
is extremely sparse, we propose strengthening the input with
multi-view stereo without using parameter models to guide
training. A static head model in our method can be trained
in approximately 10 min to produce excellent reconstruction
results, using only a monocular video as input, without any
background masks or other guidance. This leads to a simple,
efficient 3D avatar reconstruction solution.

region. In the initial phase, we use Structure-from-Motion
[33] to obtain calibrated camera parameters and SfM points.

and matching feature point clouds based on random sampling and propagation mechanisms.) [13, 14] techniques are
employed to supplement low-density features and densify
the surface point cloud. In point cloud enhanced by PMS,

Active optimization
Following the PMS-based matching process, the initialized
Gaussians exhibit a robust density foundation. Subsequent
optimization efforts focus on both active and passive refinement of Gaussian parameters and the filtering of redundant
Gaussians. Additionally, we propose constructing the avatar
using smaller-scaled unit Gaussians, enabling a more effective representation of low-frequency features on the avatar’s
surface. However, due to the repeated multi-view feature
matching during the PM process, excessive stacking of Gaussians in high-frequency regions is inevitable. This excessive
stacking leads to an increase in the number of Gaussians
participating in the rasterized rendering process, resulting
in avatars with an unrealistic appearance, suboptimal Gaussian color optimization, and excessive model size. To address
this issue, we employ global filtering criteria φ O and φ S to
remove Gaussians with low opacity or large scaling factors.

Passive optimization
We adopted the view-space position gradient control strategy
proposed by 3DGS to determine whether a Split operation
should be performed while avoiding the Clone operation.
This decision was primarily made to capitalize on the density advantage of our initial Gaussian distribution while
effectively managing the Gaussian density. To improve the
robustness of this strategy for our specific task, we propose
modifying the original Split optimization after 7000 epochs
of training. Specifically, instead of splitting a larger Gaussian
into two components, we directly reduce its scaling factor to
5/8 of its original value. This adjustment helps control the
rendered output while preventing an unnecessary increase
in Gaussian density. Furthermore, we innovatively introduce
a pixel gradient evaluation strategy to complement the split

which can occur due to excessively large Gaussian scaling,
especially in the reconstruction of fine features or thin structures. It should be noted that our passive optimization strategy
shares some similarities with the adaptive density control
strategy in 3DGS. However, the key difference lies in the
optimization process: ours can be summarized as a Gaussian point cloud refinement process, while 3DGS focuses on
densification. I utilize PMS streamlines to obtain an initial
point cloud with clear surface structure and good density.
During the passive optimization process, the collaboration
between pruning and Gaussian splitting ensures an effective
balance of Gaussian density, thereby effectively controlling
computational overhead. Furthermore, the filtering strategy
in active optimization further enhances this advantage. Under
the premise that the initial point density is guaranteed, pruning and filtering out low-quality points does not significantly
sacrifice rendering details.
In our optimization strategy, the execution of passive optimization will directly participate in the optimization process
of the internal parameters of the Gaussian model after gradient calculation during training. Further data removal and
optimization will be performed on the updated data returned
by the optimizer, followed by a global update. The differentiated Gaussian point cloud optimized in this way can
effectively prevent local overfitting in view rendering. The
active optimization strategy is designed to operate outside the
training process, performing global filtering within specific
epochs. Its primary purpose is to lightweight scene data and
reduce redundant stacking along the normal direction. The
integration of both strategies achieves high-fidelity avatar
reconstruction in the perspective.

Experimental setting
Datasets
In this section, to ensure consistency with experimental
data from other avatar reconstruction methods and enable
a meaningful comparison of relevant metrics, we selected
portions of head capture sequences from the NeRSemble
dataset [38]. These sequences were used as subsets of frames,
akin to monocular video, for head representation during our
reconstruction task. The NeRSemble dataset features multiexpression head captures of several subjects from 16 different
camera viewpoints. Each viewpoint is recorded as a video
segment, and the dataset also includes background masks,
depth maps, and camera parameters for all captured heads.
Implementation details
The framework is developed by Pytorch integration, and
the MVS points are directly obtained by the existing PMS
open source framework in conjunction with data from existing datasets, more detail. All experimental results were
obtained based on calculations performed on an NVIDIA
3090ti(24 G) GPU, the training process was based on Python,
C++ (rasterization) and implemented using PyCharm, and
the visualization of the experimental results was based on
the Unity software for the representation of the Gaussian
Splatting radiation field. In addition, we have 16 G of shared
GPU memory and our CPU is an Intel i5 13600kf.
