ATV: Thunder Ridge Riders is a racing video game developed for the Game Boy Advance in 2006 and for the Nintendo DS in 2007.
Gameplay
[edit]
In ATV: Thunder Ridge Riders, players maneuver all-terrain vehicles through a desert wasteland environment while competing against three computer-controlled opponents. The game features three distinct styles of play: "Quick Game", "Career", and "Timed Checkpoint". Throughout the game, players can earn performance upgrades for their vehicles to improve their engines, shocks, and tires.[1]
Development and release
[edit]
The Game Boy Advance version was developed by Skyworks Technologies, published by DSI Games for North-America and by Zoo Digital Publishing for Europe. The game was developed with the voxel engine.[2] Prior to its release, a schedule in the German magazine N-Zone mistakenly listed Capcom as the game's publisher.[3]
In 2007, a port was prepared for the Nintendo DS, co-developed by Apex Design and Skyworks Interactive. This version was not sold as a standalone release, but was exclusively packaged as a double-pack compilation bundle with Monster Truck Mayhem.
On December 6, 2007, Nintendo issued an official press release announcing the line-up of titles scheduled for the Wii console throughout 2008, which included ATV Thunder Ridge Riders & Monster Trucks for March 7, 2008, to be published by Zoo Digital Publishing.[4] This release schedule was also reflected in the industry press at the time, appearing in the upcoming release lists of magazines such as Hobby Consolas.[5] However, the game was never released for the Wii, and the project was subsequently cancelled without further official notification.

The Game Boy Advance version of ATV: Thunder Ridge Riders it was featured in the Brazilian magazine Nintendo World, which found the multiplayer mode fun, but noted that the gameplay felt not very sensitive.[11] The magazine Retro Gamer included the title, among the 3D games that challenged Nintendo's vision for hardware, thanks to the voxel engine implemented by the developers in the game.[2] The version was also reviewed in a 3D racing game roundup by the German magazine Mobile Gamer, which awarded it a score of 6 out of 10 for its graphics engine, but criticized its gameplay for being clunky.[7]
Nintendo Gamer noted that the game "works" but in a predictable way due to its opponent AI and identical-looking tracks.[10]
The Nintendo DS double-pack version was heavily panned by critics. Steven Woodward of GameZone awarded the bundle a 4/10, citing poor overall quality.[8] Jack Devries of IGN rated the compilation a 2/10, describing the package as a poorly executed release.[6]
