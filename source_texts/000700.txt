Abstract
Shell mapping is a method of representing microstructures on surfaces by mapping a three-dimensional texture space bijectively
into a shell space. This paper introduces a volumetric texture to
the texture space for the purpose of representing implicit surfaces
and translucent materials. However, conventional approaches often generate artifacts at the boundaries of neighboring tetrahedra
due to piecewise-linear approximate mapping. To achieve smooth
mapping, we present an approach of mapping rays nonlinearly into
the texture space, and we also address efficient sampling by using
hierarchical volumetric textures.

Introduction
To enhance the visual quality of three-dimensional (3D) models,
various techniques, such as texture mapping, normal mapping, and
displacement mapping, have been widely used. Moreover, it is also
possible to take advantage of hardware accelerations by applying
to simple polygons textures that represent details [Hasselgren et al.
2021]. However, these are inadequate for representing the complex
microstructures of surfaces, such as animal hairs or clothing fibers.
As such, shell mapping [Porumbescu et al. 2005] defines complex
shapes as microstructures by mapping a 3D texture space bijectively into a shell space, which is obtained by extruding an existing
triangular polygon (base polygon) along normals at vertices of the
base polygon.
In this paper, we introduce volumetric textures to the texture
space for the purpose of representing implicit surfaces and translucent materials. One method involves dividing a prism into three
tetrahedra and mapping the textures into the shell space using
piecewise-linear approximation. Although this method is straightforward, it results in artifacts at the boundaries of neighboring tetrahedra. Meanwhile, nonlinear ray tracing [Ogaki 2023] avoids the
tetrahedral decomposition by mapping rays in the shell space into

2
Related Work
Shell mapping [Porumbescu et al. 2005] divides a prism into three
tetrahedra and maps the texture space into the shell space using
the barycentric coordinates of each tetrahedron. The effect of light
absorption in participating media can even be simulated by defining
the texture space as translucent volumetric textures [Dufort et al.
2005]. With these methods, however, rays in the texture space
become C0 continuous at the boundaries of neighboring tetrahedra,
leading to artifacts. This issue becomes particularly serious when
the normal at each vertex of a base polygon significantly differs
from that of the base polygon or when a base polygon is extruded
to a considerable height.
One approach [Jeschke et al. 2007], which solves a cubic equation
for the correspondence between the texture space and the shell
space at each sampling, eliminates the tetrahedral decomposition,
making rays in the texture space C1 continuous. This could solve
the conventional problem of artifacts, but representing the accurate
geometry would take much sampling.
Rays mapped into the texture space are nonlinear, and they are
formulated as rational quadratic functions [Ogaki 2023]. We extend
this method because we can avoid many of the cubic equations
required for sampling, as well as the artifacts caused by tetrahedral
decomposition.

Acceleration structure
Similar to previous attempts, we adopted a two-level Bounding
Volume Hierarchy (BVH) [Clark 1976] as an acceleration structure
to locate intersections of rays and prisms. Each leaf node of the
Bottom Level Acceleration Structures in the BVH contains an AxisAligned minimum Bounding Box (AABB) with respect to a prism.
Figure 1 (b) shows how to find intersections of a ray and a prism,
composed of five faces when intersecting the AABB. Regarding
sides that may be non-planar quadrangles, bilinear patches defined
by four interpolated points are introduced.

4.3
Volume rendering
Figure 1 (a) shows volume rendering of every shell that a single ray
intersects. First, the ray range is narrowed by closest-hit shaders,
and then, in any-hit shaders, transmittance and radiance are computed using HDDA. In this paper, we consider only absorption,
out-scattering, and emission, except for in-scattering.

Quality evaluation
First, we compared our method with the tetrahedral decomposition
method using the volumetric texture in [JangaFX Software 2024].
Shell spaces composed of two prisms are shown in Fig. 4 (a) and
(b). It was observed that the distortion shown in shell spaces (a)
caused by tetrahedral decomposition disappeared in our method
(b). Figure 5 (a) and (b) show an example of mapping the volumetric
texture to complex shell spaces. It can be confirmed that mapping is
smooth, even when sides are non-planar quadrangles. In addition,
Fig. 1 (e) is a case of instancing along torus surfaces, accounting
for both heterogeneous transmission and emission in participating
media. As examples of application, a furry Stanford Bunny and a
fuzzy peach [printable_models 2024] are demonstrated in Fig. 5 (c)
and (d), respectively. See Section 5 of the supplemental material for
magnified views of these examples.
