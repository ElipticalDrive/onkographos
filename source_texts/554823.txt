What
A first version of our own volume viewer, as an option beside NiiVue. The volume pane's bar has a viewer select (NiiVue / bricks (ours)), or use ?vol=bricks. NiiVue stays the default and is unchanged.
Server (baillarger.viewer.bricks, routes in server.py):
The volume is a pyramid of levels. Level L+1 is the mean of 2×2×2 blocks of level L; at the far edge of an odd axis a block has fewer voxels, and the mean is over those. Shapes are ceil(n/2), and the levels stop at the first one that fits in one brick. Each level is cut into 32³ bricks; the bricks at the far edges are smaller.
Level L's affine is A0 @ [[s,0,0,(s-1)/2],…] with s = 2^L: each coarse voxel is the centre of its block.
Values: integer images stay int16 (coarse levels round ties to even); anything else is float32. Brick bytes are little-endian, x fastest (the 3D-texture order), with no header.
GET /volume (the name, without reading the volume), /volume/meta (each level's shape and affine, the brick size, the dtype, and a default window: the 0.5–99.5 percentiles of the finest level with ≤ 2²² voxels), /volume/bricks?level=L&b=i,j,k;… (a batch of bricks; gzip when the page accepts it, 31% smaller on the hemisphere).
The pyramid is built on first use. In each mode the source is: the column volume's box, the label review's box, or with --white the whole echo 2. The hemisphere (700×384×1148 int16, 7 levels) builds in 6.8 s with a 1.4 GB peak, measured under memguard.
BrickSource (levels, read(level, b)) is the interface that a NIfTI-Zarr store with its own multi-resolution chunks can implement in place of ArrayPyramid.
Page (index.html, WebGL2 in the style of the other panes):
Axial, coronal and sagittal slices (one view, or the 2×2 grid; the fourth tile, 3D, comes later), in world mm, with nearest-neighbour voxels.
A fragment shader samples one 3D texture of brick slots (the atlas: 2048 slots, 128 MB for int16) through a page table of all levels. It shows the finest level loaded at each point, starting from the level the zoom asks for.
The zoom asks for the level whose voxels are at least one CSS pixel. When that level's bricks do not fit the atlas, the next coarser level is used. Bricks are loaded in batches of 64, coarse levels first, and the least recently used bricks that the view does not need are evicted.
The crosshair is the shared CROSS/share one: a click or drag moves it, and a crosshair from any other pane or window moves the slices (a tile that does not show it centres on it). Shift-click sets the WM selection at the nearest white-surface point and leaves the crosshair in place, as in the NiiVue pane.
Trackpad: a pinch (a wheel event with ctrlKey) zooms about the cursor by exp(-0.01·deltaY). |deltaY| is capped at 50 per event, so a mouse notch zooms ×1.65, not ×2.7. Safari's gesturestart/gesturechange (e.scale) also zoom, and a ctrl-wheel during a gesture is ignored. A two-finger scroll pans, and so does a middle-drag. ↑/↓ step the slice under the pointer by one voxel, and Page Up/Down by ten.
With --white, ?vol=bricks adds the pane on the whole hemisphere. The white mode's default view is unchanged, to stay clear of feat: the volume pane in --white mode, a box round the WM selection #82, which puts NiiVue on a box there; a one-line conflict in the surface_only block is expected, and feat: the volume pane in --white mode, a box round the WM selection #82's logic plus the ?vol=bricks line is the resolution.
Checks
tests/test_viewer_bricks.py, all with exact answers:
voxel↔mm on levels 0, 1 and 2, including an oblique affine;
level shapes, and block means: a full block, edge blocks, a one-voxel corner, ties to even;
bricks inside, at the edges and on coarse levels, with their byte order;
the crop affine;
the routes: lazy build, gzip, 400 for bad bricks, 404 without a volume.
scripts/check --changed-since origin/main: lint, format and mypy clean; 44 passed, 1 skipped.
Browser, hemisphere (--white … ?vol=bricks):
At levels 3, 1 and 0, every sampled pixel (342, 400 and 420 samples) equals the value read directly from /volume/bricks through the window.
Zoom exactly ×e^3.5 after 70 pinch events of deltaY −5; ×2 from gesture events.
The arrow and page keys move exactly −9 voxels (−1.44251 mm).
A click reaches /state; a crosshair from another window moves the slices; shift-click sets the selection and leaves the crosshair.
Browser, the V1V2 box in column mode: NiiVue loads as before. Ours puts the crosshair on the same voxel (52, 70, 66) with the same orientation as NiiVue in the coronal and sagittal views.
Not in this PR
Mesh outlines, windowing controls, labels and overlays, the 3D tile, and linear interpolation (which needs a one-voxel apron on each brick).
