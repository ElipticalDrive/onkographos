Problem description
As demonstrated by the animated GIF in the Wikipedia article https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouraud_shading, per-vertex lighting looks terrible on curved shiny surfaces unless the vertex density is very high, typically higher than you'd need for the shape to look right or export for 3D printing etc..
The same geometry with per-pixel lighting (also known as Phong Shading, which is a separate thing to the Phong Reflectance Model that FreeCAD already uses) typically looks significantly better. Instead of interpolating the lit colour between vertices, the normal is interpolated instead, which works better as while a specular highlight in the middle of a face can be completely missed by interpolating colours, interpolated normals would still capture the highlight.
It looks like this has been an out-of-the-box feature of Coin3D for a long time, but I'm not familiar with how FreeCAD uses their API, so don't know if this would be a five-minute one-line fix, or whether it's blocked by a mountain of other changes that'd have to happen first. Hopefully, this terrible-looking spring is enough to inspire someone:
