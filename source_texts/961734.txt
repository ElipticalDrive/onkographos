Owner
Adds 2D-SuGaR's DBSCAN largest-cluster prune (Divyam et al., EG'26) as a Stage-1 primitive-reduction baseline to contrast against single-layer (N_eff) ironing for inverse rendering.
What
RefGaussianModel.cluster_prune() — faithful port of gaussian_splatting_2d/scene/gaussian_model.py::cluster_gaussians + the train.py invocation: DBSCAN over surfel centers, keep only the largest connected cluster, prune all other clusters + noise. eps auto-estimated from the knn-percentile of k-th-NN distances (defaults min_samples=knn_k=4, percentile=98, matching the paper).
scene/ref_gaussian_model.py: cluster_prune() next to visibility_prune; reuses prune_points + reset_contribution_stats; no-op guards.
arguments/refgs.py: --cluster_prune_{eps,min_samples,knn_k,knn_percentile,min_cluster_size}.
train_init.py: --cluster_prune_iterations (int nargs list on the main parser, attached to opt) + call site after visibility_prune, outside the densify guard so the reduction sticks in the handed-off checkpoint.
Why
A hard spatial-truncation pruning baseline (cf. SpikingGS = hard opacity truncation) to show single-layer ironing compresses geometry more effectively than plain gaussian pruning. Runs through the same Stage-2 relight backend; only the Stage-1 reduction lever differs.
Safety / compat
Off by default (empty cluster_prune_iterations) → stock/ironed runs byte-for-byte unchanged. Validated end-to-end on TensoIR/hotdog (prune fires, checkpoint saves, Stage-2 restore_from_refgs loads the pruned geometry). Requires scikit-learn (present in the radiogs env).

gaussian_splatting_2d cluster_gaussians + train.py) into RefGaussianModel: run DBSCAN

  values stay int-typed; attached to the opt namespace) + call site after visibility_prune,
