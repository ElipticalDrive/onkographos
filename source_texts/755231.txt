Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) are widely used in collision detection, contact modeling, and differentiable simulation due to their sign and gradient semantics. However, articulated objects are often assembled from rigid parts with internal truncation interfaces introduced by joint design, creating non-physical boundaries. Learning per-part SDFs and composing them via implicit min/max operations can yield spurious zero level set, distance bias, and gradient artifacts near truncations and junctions, destabilizing gradient-based penetration correction. We propose CC-IDF, a differentiable global distance-field framework with consistent physical semantics for truncated subregions. For each subregion, we learn complementary local fields: LSDF defines signed distance to the union of the real outer surface and truncation interfaces to retain a consistent sign/normal convention, while LBDF encodes a negated closest-point distance only to the real outer surface and excludes truncation interfaces as targets. We obtain global GSDF/GBDF via differentiable fusion and learn a pointwise confidence field to adaptively couple them, mitigating semantic mixing and gradient artifacts near truncations and junctions.
