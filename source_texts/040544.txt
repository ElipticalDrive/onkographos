Summary
napari-js has no clipping planes and no plane rendering (napari's depiction: 'plane' with plane.position and plane.normal). SIV's volume split view needs three orthogonal slice planes in the 3D view, each dragged along its axis by a handle. SIV builds all of this itself.
What SIV does today
napari-volume-slice-planes.ts (258 lines):
Each plane is a translucent two-triangle addSurface quad, coloured with tintColormap over constant values. It is removed and re-added on every move, because a surface's geometry can't be updated in place.
The handles are DOM elements over the canvas, placed with projectPoint(camera3d.viewProjection(...)) and re-placed on camera3d.changed and on resize.
A handle drag re-implements gizmo math: the cursor ray (see feat: surface (mesh) picking: SurfaceLayer.pick / MeshPicker #7), then the closest point between that ray and the plane's axis line.
Known problem: it sizes the viewport from host.clientWidth/Height instead of viewer.viewportSize(), which is the overlay offset hazard that projectPoints warns about.
Proposal
In order of value:
Plane rendering for VolumeLayer: depiction: 'volume' | 'plane' with plane: { position, normal, thickness? }, as napari does. This shows the actual slice of data in 3D, not a tinted quad.
Clipping planes on VolumeLayer and SurfaceLayer: clippingPlanes: { position, normal, enabled }[].
An orthogonal slice-plane widget (an overlay or a layer): three axis-aligned planes with drag handles, colours from AxesLayer, and a changed event giving { axis, value }.
Updating a surface's geometry in place: SurfaceLayer.setData(vertices, faces, values?) without remove and re-add.
Done when
SIV's planes are napari-js planes (or widgets), moved in place, and the drag math is gone from SIV.
Handles stay aligned with their planes when the canvas and host sizes differ.
