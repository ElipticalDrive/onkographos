Round 6: path-traced spin
Asked: make the initial view better and keep it that good at every moment, including while switching views or moving around; a loading screen of about 30 s is acceptable. The owner chose a path-traced spin on eight L40S GPUs with a one-minute idle shut-off. ?spin=0 keeps the previous behaviour.
What it does
Every view is a path-traced frame (src/render/spin.ts).
The exterior is a ring of 72 frames (every 5°) at three distances, like a 360° product spin. Dragging lands on the nearest frame; an arrow press moves one step.
The interior is one 360° panorama, so looking around inside stays path traced at every angle.
Once a view is rendered, the real-time image is not shown.
Loading screen (SpinLoading in src/App.tsx): on opening, it stays up until the visitor's view is at full quality and its ring is ready as previews, or for a minute at most. Frames wait for the scanned textures, so a design renders once, complete. A view's first visit shows it again only if nothing of that view is ready.
Order of work:
the visitor's frame, as a preview and then at full quality;
its ring as previews, nearest first, then the other distances;
the panorama preview;
full quality for everything, then the full panorama.
A design change keeps the previous frame up ("Path tracing your changes") until the new one crossfades in.
Server side:
Frames are finished on the server (exposure, the browser's bloom, AgX, dither; fury_render/finishing.py) and sent as WebP: 0.2 MB at 1200×827 instead of 4 MB.
They are cached on the volume by the SHA-256 of everything that determines them, together with their exposure reading.
One Blender process per GPU: whole frames render eight at a time behind the shared priority queue. Splitting each 1-megapixel frame across eight GPUs would mostly idle them.
Exposure: metered once per lighting look and per view, then held, like a camera on manual exposure. The real-time frame and a 16-sample traced frame are compared at the same camera and size; the interior's is measured while the visitor is still outside.
Cost guards:
rendering pauses while the tab is hidden;
a busy server (429/503) is asked again after 2 s;
after three failures in a row the spin stays off for that page.
Gateway render limit: now 600 a minute (burst 240), to fit twelve open frame requests per visitor.
Bugs found while testing
Windows were dark mirrors from inside (predates this PR). Cycles' Fresnel node treats a back face as light leaving glass, so it reflected totally beyond 41°. A 3 cm pane passed 8% of the light seen through it at 60°.
The converter now uses Schlick's Fresnel on |cos θ|, which also brightens the view into the gym from outside, ?spin=0 included.
A server test fails without the fix.
Exposure metering read wrong three ways, each fixed and covered by tests:
the meter render's camera was taken before the view was framed;
the scene was captured before the textures arrived (white walls read bright);
a 128-pixel meter reads brighter than the full frame.
Mipmaps blurred the panorama and would have drawn a seam where it wraps; they are off for spin frames.

Round 4: pre-rendered orbit views
Asked: reach full-resolution traced images without re-rendering on every slight move. A traced image is only valid for the exact pose it was rendered from, so the owner chose to pre-render the views around the resting one (over more GPUs per render, or warping the traced image while moving). Since Round 6 this is the ?spin=0 behaviour.
What it does
Resting views sit on a grid (src/render/orbit.ts).
The grid has 48 azimuths (every 7.5°), 5° elevation steps, and 6% zoom steps.
When the exterior camera stops, it eases onto the nearest pose in 280 ms. That is a correction of at most 3.75°.
An arrow key or short drag always advances at least one step; it is never snapped back.
Snapping applies only with a cloud renderer, in the exterior view, and not while auto-rotating.
The ring is pre-rendered (CloudSession in src/render/cloud.ts).
Once the visitor's view is shown, the other 47 azimuths at that elevation and zoom render one at a time, nearest first, at the same full resolution.
Orbiting then lands on finished images: no render request, and no local tracing.
The badge reads "Path traced · 12/48 angles ready".
The visitor comes first.
Pre-renders use their own server session, so neither drops the other.
They wait while the visitor's own view is in flight.
A view the visitor lands on while it is being pre-rendered is joined, not rendered twice.
When it stops: a design edit stops the ring. It pauses while the tab is hidden or after 3 minutes without input, and stops after three failures in a row.
Images stay compressed in memory.
The server can now send the image as a gzip file ("transfer": "file") instead of gzip transfer encoding, which the browser would inflate.
A 320 MB cache keeps about 60 views at 1920×1200. Decoding one takes about 30–50 ms.
Gateway render limit: raised from 12 to 40 a minute (burst 8), so a visitor plus their pre-renders fit.
Cost: a full ring is 47 extra renders while someone is using the page. I estimate a few seconds of L40S time each, but it is not measured.

Round 3: cloud GPU rendering
A laptop GPU can't reach 1024 spp at full resolution in seconds. A server GPU with hardware ray tracing (OptiX) and GPU denoising can.
What it does
render-server/: Blender Cycles (bpy 4.5) behind a FastAPI app, in a child process that owns bpy.
modal_app.py ran at most one L40S, switched off after 5 idle minutes (now eight L40S and one minute, see Round 6). OptiX kernel caches are kept on a Modal volume.
Since Protect cloud GPU rendering with a server-verified PIN #11, the public endpoint is a CPU-only gateway (fury_render/gateway.py). It validates and rate-limits requests before calling the private GPU method. Its PIN has since been removed (above).
Scene transport (src/render/cloud.ts):
The browser sends the same prepared path-trace scene the local tracer captures, as JSON. Geometry, instance matrices, the sky map and texture files go as content-hashed blobs, uploaded only when the server answers 409.
Since Protect cloud GPU rendering with a server-verified PIN #11, the browser uploads texture bytes itself; the server never fetches URLs.
A camera move or finish change sends just the JSON. Cycles' persistent data re-syncs only what changed.
Matching the local tracer: light units were calibrated against three-gpu-pathtracer:
emission and sky radiance carry over 1:1;
sun strength is irradiance;
point and spot power is 4π × intensity;
area power is π × area × radiance.
Spot cones fade over the same angles, glass is thin-walled, and one-sided faces are culled except for shadow rays.
Image format: linear half floats, packed losslessly and gzip-compressed. The browser decodes 1920×1200 in about 50 ms and applies the usual exposure match, bloom and tone mapping.
Hybrid with local tracing (src/render/session.ts):
A server that answered within 4 minutes gets a 6 s head start. Otherwise the local tracer starts at once, and the cloud image crossfades over it.
On a failure, tracing continues locally.
Lite devices and WebGL-only browsers leave tracing to the cloud.
Limits:
one GPU at most;
a session's superseded requests are dropped;
15 s of sampling per render;
gateway rate limits;
CORS restricted to explicitly configured origins.
Modal workspace spending controls are the hard cap.
Fixes after #11 (measured on the merged code)
Images were inflated in transit. The private method read the renderer's response with httpx, which decompresses gzip, so a 1920×1200 view crossed the gateway and the internet as 18.4 MB instead of 4.9 MB.
fury_render/rpc.py now passes the raw, still-compressed body through with its Content-Encoding.
A gateway test checks the wire length; with the real renderer, the same view now crosses as 4.9 MB.
Denoising had moved to the CPU. Protect cloud GPU rendering with a server-verified PIN #11 found that OptiX's denoiser fails on Modal and switched to OIDN, but also turned off OIDN's GPU mode. CPU OIDN with the accurate prefilter took about 12.5 s per 1920×1200 frame on 4 vCPUs.
OIDN now runs on the GPU; it has its own CUDA kernels, independent of OptiX.
If GPU denoising fails at warm-up, at start or mid-render, the renderer falls back to CPU OIDN with the fast prefilter (about 3.5 s on the same machine).
FURY_DENOISE=cpu forces the CPU. /health, X-Denoise and ?traceStats=1 report which is in use.
GPU denoising was later confirmed on Modal (X-Denoise: gpu, Round 5).
To turn it on (owner)
See render-server/README.md: deploy with FURY_ALLOWED_ORIGINS, then set VITE_CLOUD_RENDER_URL to the gateway URL on a preview branch.

Keep path-traced samples when the camera moves

ab531c5
```
Every camera move reset the accumulation, so each new angle re-climbed
to 1024 samples from zero. When tracing stops, the view's accumulation,
sample counts, and rasterized positions, normals, and albedo are now
kept, and the next view of the same scene is seeded from them by a
compute pass that reprojects each pixel to the same surface point in
the old view.

A pixel inherits old samples only where that point was visible and
only as far as its look is view-independent: none for glass, mirrors,
metals, clear coats, or glossy finishes; most for rough dielectrics,
less as the viewing angle turns, when zooming in, and across shadow
edges. Light (color over albedo) is carried over and re-multiplied by
the new view's own albedo, so textures stay sharp. At least a quarter of
every pixel's samples stay fresh, and the tracer spends its time on
what the old view did not show. Seeded views are first denoised at 2
samples.

The denoiser's albedo/normal raster pass becomes this G-buffer,
rendered when tracing starts.

On SwiftShader WebGPU, a 6-degree orbit reaches 32 spp 1.84x sooner
and is closer to a 256-spp reference than a fresh render (4.15% vs
4.78% mean per-pixel error), with no brightness bias.

Cancel GPU calls the gateway stops waiting for
…
896a6a3
```
While the renderer container sat "scheduling" on Modal, frame requests
from minutes earlier were still pending: the gateway gives up after 180
seconds and answers 503, but a `.remote()` call stays queued, and the
browser asks again after every 503. Once a GPU started, it would render
each of them for no one.

The gateway now spawns the call and cancels it when the wait ends
without an answer (timeout or disconnect). Cancelling reaches the
renderer, so a request still waiting in its priority queue is dropped.

before its request ends, keeping the worker's turn and output file until
then (released sooner, the next render would share the worker's pipe),
and a finished spin frame still goes into the frame cache.
