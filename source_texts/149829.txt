Mittwoch, 1. Juli 2026, 13:00 Uhr - Maximilian Palm - A Configurable Synthetic Data Pipeline for Multi-View Industrial Vision
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Maximilian Palm:
"A Configurable Synthetic Data Pipeline for Multi-View Industrial Vision"
Betreuung: Prof. Dr. Jürgen Hesser
Die Präsentation findet am Mittwoch, den 1. Juli 2026, um 13:00 Uhr
online über heiCONF statt.
Abstract
Training modern computer-vision models requires large amounts of labelled image data, and a single dataset rarely provides every modality a downstream task needs: bounding boxes, segmentation masks, depth, and disparity.
This thesis presents a configurable synthetic data generation pipeline, built on the open-source BlenderProc framework, that derives all of these modalities from a single physics-simulated scene.
A custom camera module renders a configurable multicamera array, a cross layout that mirrors the LumiScanX capture hardware or a generic matrix layout, with depth and disparity-ready output; further modules add occlusion-aware top-layer annotation, V-HACD compound collision shapes for concave objects, multi-exposure HDR rendering, and a graphical interface that exposes every parameter to users who do not program.
The pipeline is evaluated on an industrial bin-picking task, syringe detection, developed in cooperation with HD Vision Systems GmbH. An object detector trained on purely synthetic images exceeds an equal-size real-only baseline on recall, and the multi-view output is shown to be geometrically consistent to sub-pixel precision by an independent stereo-matching cross-check.

Mittwoch, 03.06.2026 - Denisa Radu - Incorporating Time-Series Data into GNN-TARnet for Individual Treatment Effect Estimation
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Denisa Radu:
"Incorporating Time-Series Data into GNN-TARnet for Individual Treatment Effect Estimation"
Betreuung: Prof. Dr. Jürgen Hesser
Die Präsentation findet am Mittwoch, den 03. Juni 2026, um 15:30 Uhr online über HeiCONF statt.
Abstract
Clinical studies are widely adopted as the main methodology for assessing the efficacy of new classes of drugs (e.g., chemotherapy), where the effects of the drug are estimated by the statistical differences in key defined outcomes between participants and controls. A classical problem that arises, both out of financial and ethical considerations, is how to assess what effect a particular drug would have had on a given participant. Individual Treatment Effect (ITE) estimation draws upon Causal Inference literature and aims to solve this problem by estimating the effect of potential treatments at an individual level, by using the observed effects of another participant who received the target medicine. This thesis investigates how time-aware features based on patient-reported outcome measures influence the ITE estimation when using the GNN-TARnet, a graph neural network extension of TARnet. We propose two temporal feature regimes: a fixed-window aggregation over periods with identical treatment and derived physical-functioning features. Based on observational data collected during a lung cancer clinical trial in which patients completed the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire over multiple visits, semi-synthetic data, and fully synthetic data, we evaluate the performance of the model while using two different graph generation techniques. Established ITE metrics, PEHE and ATE, are computed for the semi-synthetic and fully synthetic datasets. Overall, the performance of the model is primarily driven by the data regime and outcome noise, rather than the graph generation methods. Fully synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets validate our implementation at low noise, whereas performance on the observational data is limited.

Freitag, 29.05.2026 - Paul Horn - LLMs for textual plan space explanation in PlanPilot
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Paul Horn:
"LLMs for textual plan space explanation in PlanPilot"
Betreuung: -
Die Präsentation findet am Freitag, den 29. Mai 2026, um 14:00 Uhr in Raum SR5 im Mathematikon (INF 205) statt.
Abstract
Planning is a crucial component of processes in many domains, including production
lines, general problem-solving, optimization, or many others. However,
planning as a research field often remains difficult to understand and inaccessible
to non-experts. To address this issue, the PlanningCopilot is introduced. It
combines the PlanPilot planning system with a large language model (LLM) to
bridge the gap between complex planning systems and users of varying levels
of expertise. The goal is to allow for an easier usage of PlanPilot and, in doing
so, making planning as a whole more accessible to everyone.
To evaluate whether this objective was achieved, a user study was conducted
and analyzed. The results show that the PlanningCopilot successfully enables
users to explore the plan and facet spaces, review the plans, and adapt them to
their individual needs. Test participants reported a significantly improved user
experience and considerably easier usability compared to PlanPilot. Furthermore,
the integration of an LLM also enables new functionalities such as swap detection.

Montag, 8. Mai 2026, 10:00 Uhr - Paul Kling - Graph-Aware Follow-Up Question Generation for German Medical QA
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Paul Kling:
"Graph-Aware Follow-Up Question Generation for German Medical QA"
Betreuung: Prof. Dr. Michael Gertz
Die Präsentation findet am Montag, den 8. Mai 2026, um 10:00 Uhr
online statt.
Abstract
Digital chatbot assistants have become the default starting point for many people seeking information, even in sensitive domains such as medicine. Large language models (LLMs) can answer such queries smoothly, but their outputs cannot be traced back to their sources, and they can hallucinate false statements with high confidence, making them risky in a medical context.
To address this problem, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems have been developed, recently often in combination with knowledge graphs. However, most users cannot fully exploit such systems, since they often do not know what follow-up questions to ask in order to explore a domain thoroughly.
This thesis addresses this gap by generating follow-up questions that stay on topic, introduce new information not covered in the original answer, and explore different directions within a German-language medical corpus on diabetes.
The first contribution is a German diabetes knowledge graph built from unstructured web pages using a domain-specific schema. Unlike traditional knowledge graphs, it also includes source documents and text chunks as nodes, directly connecting corpus content with conceptual structure.
The second contribution is a follow-up question generation framework that leverages three signals of concept relatedness: typed graph relations, sequential chunk order, and hyperlink structure. Candidate concepts are filtered against already mentioned content and diversified using embedding-based spacing.
Evaluation against two baselines using proxy metrics and an independent LLM judge shows strong improvements in novelty and diversity, while relevance slightly decreases as a trade-off for exploration. Overall, the system successfully balances topical coherence with the introduction of new exploratory directions.
Donnerstag, 07.05.2026 - Felix Schäfer - Automated Feature Selection Strategies for Machine Learning Models in Demand Forecasting
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Felix Schäfer:
"Automated Feature Selection Strategies for Machine Learning Models in Demand Forecasting"
Betreuung: Prof. Dr. Georgia Koppe
Die Präsentation findet am Donnerstag, den 07. Mai 2026, um 13:15 Uhr in Raum 4/414 im Mathematikon (INF 205) statt.
Abstract
Demand forecasting models rely on diverse input features such as product attributes, calendar information and historical sales patterns. In practice, feature sets are often curated manually by domain experts, a process that is time-consuming, offers no guarantee of consistent quality and does not scale well. We present a systematic empirical comparison of six automated feature selection methods from the filter, embedded and wrapper families on six industrial demand forecasting datasets on a LightGBM model. All methods are evaluated within a nested crossvalidation framework using expanding-window backtesting. This design avoids the optimization bias of flat cross-validation, which we show overestimates performance by 1.5% on average. Including all available features without selection reduces forecast accuracy by 2.5% RMSE on average compared to expert-curated baselines, confirming that indiscriminate feature inclusion degrades performance. Among the automated methods, Mutual Information-based selection achieves the strongest aggregate performance with an average RMSE improvement of 2.7% over the expert baseline across all six datasets, ranking first on four. Embedded importance methods match expert-level performance. However, per-item analysis reveals weak cross-item agreement (Kendall’s W < 0.10 on five of six datasets), indicating that no single method dominates across all items within a dataset. On two datasets with 30 synthetic noise features added on top of the real ones, MI-based selection improves upon clean-baseline accuracy. A broader experiment across all six datasets shows that noise does not always degrade performance, with directions varying by dataset. Given its consistently negative aggregate RMSE change on five of six datasets, we recommend Mutual Information-based selection as a practical default for demand forecasting pipelines with tree-based models, noting that both per-dataset and per-item variability remain high.
Donnerstag, 07.05.2026 - Priti Kamat - AHEAD Retrospective Data Analysis Pipeline
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Priti Kamat:
"AHEAD Retrospective Data Analysis Pipeline"
Betreuung: Prof. Dr. Jürgen Hesser
Die Präsentation findet am Donnerstag, den 07.05. Mai 2026, um 10:00 Uhr online über HeiCONF statt.
Abstract
The increasing availability of structured clinical data has enabled the development of data-driven methods for clinical risk prediction. However, such datasets are characterized by heterogeneity, missing values, high-dimensional categorical codes, and class imbalance, which limit the effectiveness of direct machine learning applications. This thesis addresses these challenges through the design and implementation of a structured and modular data science pipeline for predicting high-risk patients from retrospective clinical data.
The proposed approach integrates multiple stages, including data preprocessing, domain-specific feature engineering, feature selection, and machine learning-based prediction. Clinical diagnosis codes are transformed into a comorbidity score using a standardized model, while procedural data is converted into a numerical severity score through a rule-based mapping approach. These transformations enable the representation of complex clinical information in a compact and meaningful form. The processed dataset is used to train an Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) classifier, with stratified cross-validation and synthetic oversampling techniques applied to ensure robustness and address class imbalance.
The main contribution of this work lies in the development of an end-to-end pipeline that systematically handles clinical data challenges while maintaining interpretability and modularity. Experimental results demonstrate stable predictive performance across multiple validation settings and improved detection of high-risk cases. The proposed framework provides a reliable and extensible foundation

Freitag, 22.09.2023, 10:00 - Mara-Eliana Popescu - Induced Matchings in Graphs of Maximum Degree 4
Präsentation der Bachelorarbeit von Frau Mara-Eliana Popescu:
"Induced Matchings in Graphs of Maximum Degree 4"
Betreuer: Prof. Dr. Felix Joos
Die Präsentation findet am Freitag, den 22. September 2023, um 10 Uhr im Raum 2/414 (Mathematikon) statt.
Zusammenfassung: Ein induziertes Matching eines Graphen G ist eine Teilmenge M seiner Kanten, so dass beliebige zwei Knoten, die inzident zu verschiedenen Kanten von M sind, mindestens Abstand 2 in G haben. Das klassische Matchingproblem ist in polynomialer Zeit lösbar, aber es wurde bereits gezeigt, dass das Finden eines maximal induziertes Matchings in beliebigen Graphen ein NP-vollständiges Problem ist. Präsentation der Masterarbeit von Sophia Heck:
"Approximation Algorithms for the Minimum (Sliding Window) Temporal Vertex Cover Problem"
Betreuer: Prof. Dr. Christian Schulz
Die Präsentation findet am 27.7. um 10Uhr online über HeiCONF statt. Bitte melden Sie sich, falls Interesse besteht, bei christian.schulz@informatik.uni-heidelberg.de.
Abstract:
Modern real life networks are often highly dynamic. Temporal graphs represent these changes in the network configuration through discrete edge appearances on a fixed set of vertices. In a temporal graph all these changes are known in advance until a maximal timestep, the lifetime of the graph. The classical problem of Vertex Cover aims to find a set of vertices such that all edges are covered by one of their endpoints. This can be naturally extended in the time changing setting to the Temporal Vertex Cover (TVC) and Sliding Window Temporal Vertex Cover (∆-TVC). In the TVC every edge is covered once over the whole lifetime, while in the ∆-TVC every appearing edge is covered once in every window of ∆ consecutive timesteps. Both extensions are known to be NP-hard. In this thesis, known (approximation) algorithms for these extensions are implemented and experimentally evaluated. In particular, we consider two approximation algorithms and one non-polynomial exact algorithm. The approximations have approximation ratios bounded by the maximal degree d of any subgraph in at a certain timestep, leading to a ratio of d and d − 1. Moreover, two new approximation algorithms for the restricted case of always star temporal graphs are presented, leading to a 2∆ − 1 and a ∆ − 1 approximation ratio, where ∆ is the sliding window size. For the experiments a temporal graph generator for certain temporal graph classes and a framework for solving the (∆-)TVC are introduced. The experiments verify the stated runtime and approximation ratios of the known algorithm and even provide some improvements made through the implementations. Further, we compare the computation of the ∆-TVC on restricted inputs of always star temporal graphs through the known approximation algorithms with the here presented new ones. We show that the new approximations outperform the known d − 1-approximation in shorter runtime even in some cases where ∆ > d.

Präsentation der Master-Abschlussarbeit von Herrn Peter Hügel:
"Improving the Accuracy of Neural Networks when Predicting the Forward Propagation of Acoustic Waves"
Betreuer: Prof. Dr. Jürgen Hesser
Die Präsentation findet am Donnerstag, den 26. Januar 2023 um 16 Uhr im Besprechungsraum 1.414 (Mathematikon 1. OG) statt.
Abstract:
The acoustic wave equation is an important part of state-of-the-art imaging methods. This
presentation explores the limitations of using deep learning to solve the forward propagation of
acoustic waves in heterogeneous media within the frequency domain. With the intention of
using the Trefftz method to solve the acoustic wavefield in a large domain, small subregions
are solved with a deep convolutional neural network. For this reason, the task is designed
so that the values at the boundaries of the patches are assumed to be known. With the goal
of predicting local Green’s functions within these smaller patches, networks are trained with
different configurations on a variety of datasets, taking into account the sound speed values
inside the patches. The accuracy is significantly improved by exploring different avenues.
Limiting factors are identified and possible future solutions to further reduce the error are
discussed.

Montag, 5.12.2022, 10:00 - Roman Erhardt - Engineering Algorithms for the Weighted Maximum Clique Problem
Präsentation der Masterarbeit von Herrn Roman Erhardt:
"Engineering Algorithms for the Weighted Maximum Clique Problem"
Betreuer: Prof. Dr. Christian Schulz
Die Präsentation findet am 5.12.2022 10Uhr online über HeiCONF statt. Bitte melden Sie sich, falls Interesse besteht (bei christian.schulz@informatik.uni-heidelberg.de).
Abstract:
The maximum weighted clique problem (MWC) is a well-known problem in graph theory with many applications. In this work, both exact and heuristic algorithms, which interleave successful techniques from related work with novel graph reduction rules are proposed. While graph reductions based on upper bounds have been used for MWC in the past, we present reduction rules, that make use of local graph structures in order to identify and remove vertices and edges without reducing the optimal solution. A set of exact reduction rules is employed in an exact algorithm called MWCRedu, while heuristic reduction techniques based on machine learning models such as MLP, Deepset and GNN are explored in the heuristic framework MWCPeel. Experiments on a broad range of graphs show, that MWCRedu outperforms the current state-of-the-art exact solver TSM-MWC [25] for most inputs. Specifically for naturally weighted, medium-sized street network graphs and random hyperbolic graphs, which are considered to model real-world graphs well, MWCRedu is faster by orders of magnitudes. The heuristic solver MWCPeel also outperforms its competitors FastWCLq [12] and SCCWalk4l [47] on these instances, but is slightly less effective on extremely dense or large instances. It is planned to publish the result.
