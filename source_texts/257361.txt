Adds physically calibrated surface and volume lights, passive bounce lighting and the preparation behind them, and extends +TF texture filtering from pigments to whole textures, perturbed normals and the sky.
Lights
Surface lights. Any object can carry light { ... }: one-sided outward Lambertian emission over the object's actual geometry, in nits or lumens, from an explicit pigment or the material's own emission. Every light uses one finite-source connection law with a continuous directional sampler, so there is no point-light substitution and no switch by distance.
Volume lights. interior { light { ... } } with the same calibration; scale_lumens(value, metres) converts units in both scene languages.
Units as measured quantities. A light in nits has that mean radiance: over the area for a surface light, at the container's boundary for a volume light, whatever the pigment's colour or pattern. A unitless brightness stays a plain multiplier. Nits or lumens over an input whose luminance is zero are an error.
Passive bounce. light { emission bounce { passes N } } makes receiving surfaces and volumes sources of the next order. Each pass reads an immutable copy of the previous one, the primary is counted once and nothing is renormalised. A light block with bounce and no pigment or emission only re-emits what it receives. Subsurface materials take part through a diffusion of each node's entering light over its neighbours, with no extra rays.
Light in varying media. Prepared method 4 media with a varying density take the light of volume and surface lights along the camera ray at sqrt(N) samples per cell width of ray length, where N is the light's samples; each sample has its own position and its own draw on the light. A new light_samples M in a finish or media block scales what that receiver takes from every light: N × M draws per surface hit, sqrt(N) × M samples per cell width in media. Volume bounce gathers each order into directional lobes per node and re-emits it for the next order.
Prepared source layouts for volume emitters: Cartesian or cubemap-and-radial charts with exact cell volumes, positive-mass proposals with exact integer Bernoulli draws, and shared incident fields per phase.
Parallel, deterministic preparation. Bounce orders, subsurface bounce nodes, media, refraction and light grids prepare on every work thread with byte-identical results at any thread count, and preparation reports its progress (Preparation plan: / Preparation: <phase> <done>/<total> <elapsed>s, with finished and stalled lines).
Texture filtering (+TF)
With +TF, camera rays carry how their origin and direction change from one pixel to the next, and each texture layer they hit, pigment and normal, is filtered over the area the pixel covers there. This PR extends that filter:
Whole textures and bumped normals. A filtered normal is evaluated at the pigment filter's taps, and everything that reads the perturbed normal sees that spread: diffuse light is the mean of what each tap's normal receives; specular and phong highlights widen by the normals' spread and keep their total brightness; irid, Fresnel and falloff reflection, metallic, the Fresnel loss of ambient, emission and radiosity, conserve_energy and faked caustics average over the taps; aoi and slope see each tap's own normal. Bumps finer than a pixel shade as their average instead of speckling.
Reflected and refracted rays leave along the taps' mean normal, and their footprint widens by the normals' spread, so what they hit is filtered over the blur the bumps cause.
uv mapping. A uv_mapping texture or normal, or a texture on a uv-mapped object, is filtered over the footprint carried into uv.
Sky. A ray that leaves the scene averages the sky_sphere pigments over the directions its footprint spans; a reflected ray's directions that dip below the surface it left are folded back above it.
Texture_Filter_Pigments=off / Texture_Filter_Normals=off opt a kind out; Texture_Filter_Taps=3 starts with three taps and adds the rest only where they disagree.
+TF never casts extra secondary rays. Every filtered term comes from taps of the texture at the one hit, or from the footprint a single ray already carries. A footprint can therefore reach texture that something else hides, such as a pattern clipped by another solid or a sky_sphere gradient that wraps below the horizon behind the ground, and the filter blends that texture in; make the texture continuous there, or leave +TF off for that render. A light is tested for shadow where the taps' mean normal faces it, so ray counts can differ slightly from -TF because the filtered normals differ. Where a footprint's mean normal would reflect totally while some of its taps transmit, the hit takes the usual reflection and refraction pair, the refraction about the transmitting taps, each at its filtered Fresnel share.
Fixes
Surface lights on unions dimmed themselves. The per-thread shadow cache kept the part of an object that blocked a ray and later tested it alone, so the light's endpoint on that part no longer matched and the part shadowed its own light; the cache now also keeps the object the roots were admitted as. A sample point now moves to its part's own root on the connecting ray, and preparation draws further area-sample rounds where a union's parts fill little of its bounds. A cylinder lathe in a union read 0.88 of the same cylinder, a cylinder in a union with a small far sphere 0.70; both now read within 1%.
A volume light in a standalone lathe, sor, blob, superellipsoid, height_field or julia_fractal container, or an isosurface cut short by max_trace, measured its chords from the first crossing only and emitted too little (a lathe cylinder gave 0.70 of its stated intensity). Every crossing of the container now counts; ordinary tracing of those objects is unchanged.
Subsurface single scattering from a light touching the surface (unbounded variance), a squared scattering coefficient in strided media lighting, surface-light photon weighting, entry index resolution on meshes and thin shells, compound-CSG bounce coverage, shadow traversal through coincident CSG/mesh/media boundaries, and SimpleVector::insert reading past the end (present since the initial import).
Scenes and manual
The media, haze, refraction, light and camera sample scenes state their lights in real units and show their options side by side in one image (four quarters or rows of a matrix) instead of behind Declare switches; the manual's images and descriptions follow them.
Compatibility
Scenes of version 3.7 and earlier render as before; new features work at every scene version and in both languages. Superseded unreleased syntax is removed rather than kept. Ray is 336 bytes on x86-64 GCC (208 on performance); the guard in ray.h and the "Ray size" section of doc/PERF.md record why and how to measure it.
The texture filtering changes nothing without +TF. slopemt_37.pov was rewritten in #113 to make its patchy vegetation deliberately, since #110 corrected the normal its slope pattern reads.
Evidence
light_clouds.pov at 640×480, samples 16, no anti-aliasing, user-space instructions: performance 6.10T (its own scene version, media emitters), this branch 10.68T. The storm tower's scattered light is free of the speckle an earlier per-ray estimator showed, and its noise on the tower is slightly below performance's. light_samples 0.25 renders in 3.64T and light_samples 4 in 38.81T; noise halves for each fourfold increase.
Media cost fixes in the shadow walk, volume-light chords and the shadow march cut that scene from 11.32T to 8.04T with pixel-identical output before the sampling change.
Render suites: all 45 render suites under tests/render that cover media, bounce, volume and surface lights, subsurface, shadows, texture filtering and the 4.0 parser pass, among them surface_light (union cases), volume_light (lathe and sor containers) and media_stride_scattering (light_samples scaling). Every commit builds. Renders at 1 and 3 threads have identical pixels.

Volume lights: an interior light block with one radiance law
…
d61fdcc
```
A light block moves from media to the interior: interior { media { ... }
light { ... } }. It states radiance as its pigment times brightness, in
nits or as a total in lumens. Per cell the emission coefficient is the
radiance times the media's density-weighted extinction, so the
thick-limit radiance is the stated one for any density field; a medium
without extinction falls back to its container's mean chord. Falloff
follows the emitter's geometry: near-field draws take their own shadow
rays, the far field keeps the emitter's angular extent, and a chord
keeps its last crossing.

Finite-source connections are shared between surfaces and media, keep
boundary media on the sampled connection, trace visibility to the
sampled endpoint, and are stratified over source power in
low-discrepancy strata.

The block is read by the classic parser, #default, the 4.0 schema, the
tree-sitter grammar, the editor tables and the codemod. The manual and
doc/volume-light-calibration.md describe the law; media_light,
volume_light, volume_scattering, volume_transport, volume_visibility and
volume_field_support test it, and the stock media scenes state their
lights as interior lights.

```
Prepared method 4 media separate their fixed coefficients from lighting,
publish their density fields before tracing, and sample the camera
distance and the volume light jointly. Constant media and prepared
modifiers stay on fixed integration, the prepared policy holds across
camera, shadow, photon and subsurface paths, and photon segments pass
their bounds to the field selection. Interior light fields are immutable
RGB fields with normalised spatial proposals on shared prepared
coordinates (preparedfield.h, preparedlightaxis.h, preparedtransfer.h,
interiorfield.h).

Physical phases are normalised and scattering mixtures weighted by their
coefficients; Henyey-Greenstein eccentricity must lie strictly between
-1 and 1, with a warning for 3.7 and older scenes. A medium's appearance
emission stays out of indirect light, and subsurface transport keeps
finite volume light samples consistent.

Render statistics report volume connection work by path. Tests cover
joint media, mixed prepared media, publication, prepared photons,
infinite joint media, the emission and indirect split, phases and the
subsurface volume emitter.

Surface lights
…
1bac2de
```
Objects accept a surface light { ... } block beside the interior volume
light. It shares the pigment or emission input, brightness in nits or
lumens, samples and transport flags, and emits outward with a Lambert
distribution from a surface of finite, nonzero area. Emission is
evaluated from finite parts with angular guides. Portal bodies stay out
of sampling, a receiver does not draw its own root, and each surface
part may state its own accuracy. Light preparation is safe to run from
several threads.

This also adds the bounce-order fields that surface and volume lights
share (bouncefield.h, bouncegrid.h, bounceincident.h, bounceorders.h,
bounceprepare.h) and the prepared source layouts and selectors. The
manual, grammar, editor tables and language reference describe the
block; surface_light and the standalone guide, bounce and source tests
cover it, and distribution/scenes/lights/surface_light.pov shows it.

27aef7b

Method 2 ballistic light takes Fresnel transmission. Single-scattering

…
16744ec
```
A shadow walk steps past any boundary it returns and finds a nearer root
again instead of stepping past it; a coincident-surface probe starts at
least twice the minimum hit depth back. A shadow ray's last stretch is
dimmed by the prepared or classic media of the containers the light is

Bounce: surface and volume bounce orders with adaptive surface nodes
…
8e4366f
```
Light blocks accept bounce and bounce { passes N }. Each order's surface
bounce field holds the light arriving at adaptive nodes, seeded over the
scene's bounce area and refined from what each node measures; a lookup
takes the nearest nodes of the surface and interpolates across
components a CSG chart misses. Subsurface surfaces diffuse their bounce
light between the nodes at the profile's own scale, within each
material. A volume bounce node takes a light's falloff over its cell and
holds each distinct beam once. Work whose result is already known is
skipped.

Preparation states its plan and reports each phase as it goes (parse,
meshes, media, atmosphere, lights, bounce, photons) with work done
against its total, elapsed seconds and stalls, in lines that fit the
console. Bounce orders and every final media grid are prepared on the
scene's work threads, and ParallelFor's failures and thread use are
predictable, so results do not depend on the thread count; doc/PERF.md
gives the format.

The manual documents surface lights, bounce, units and preparation;
bounce_adaptive, bounce_preparation, media_tested_density_threads and
the bounce and subsurface patch source tests cover it.

…

```

Light units: measured nits and lumens, attenuation cutoff
…
db8db0d
```
Nits are the mean radiance of the light over its surface or at its
container's boundary, for emission and pigment inputs alike; lumens are
the generated flux; a point emitter follows the inverse-square law and
every emitter light weights its photons by its own path factor. Nonzero
units over an input with zero luminance are an error. A bounce block
without an input of its own is bounce-only. Volume light normalisation
counts every boundary crossing, and a surface light on a union does not
dim itself.

attenuation { cutoff C } leaves a light out where its prepared power
falls below C on the output scale (default 0.000004; 0 turns it off),
for surface, volume and bounce lights.

The stock media and light scenes state their lights in lumens or nits
and their method 4 scattering to keep their look; the manual describes
them.

…
33de178
```
Camera rays in varying prepared method 4 media take volume and surface
lights at each integration step, or once per stride, each with one draw
stratified along the ray and its own shadow ray. A volume or surface

+TF: filter whole textures over the pixel footprint
…
e116440
```
+TF filters a texture over each hit's pixel footprint: pigments, and the
perturbed normal over a tap lattice that keeps an isotropic footprint,
with Toksvig-style gloss, iridescence, Fresnel and conserve_energy
reading the filtered normals. +TN filters normals alone. +TF casts no
extra rays; footprint tolerances are bounded by the scene's own lengths,
and a light is tested from a +TF hit only where the taps' mean normal
faces it, shadow rays converging on a sampled emitter point.

The manual, povray.1 and doc/PERF.md describe the switches, their
opt-outs, measured error and cost; texture_filter tests them.
```

ray's sky fold survives an unbent transmission. texture_filter tests
what a filtered sky layer passes.
