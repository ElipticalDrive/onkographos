COW-23 Phases 1 and 2: the AuthoredVoids protection index, and the one
policy-aware guard that consumes it. The toggle is not flipped —
cromatolis_v0_procedural_layers.ron still has caves: false, so this ships
dead in production and every test drives the query functions directly.
What this is
Authored underground geometry and the purely-procedural cave lattice both write
into the same block volumes and neither can see the other. Today the only way to
stop a hash-derived tunnel punching through a hand-authored vault is to switch
the whole procedural layer off for the region — which is what Cromatolis does, at
the cost of its entire procedural underground (cave biomes, cave fauna, and every
Iron/Coal/Cobalt/Silver and gem deposit in the game, all of which come from that
one layer).
world/src/layer/authored_voids.rs is the narrow alternative: an exact-shape
protection index the procedural layers consult at one choke point.
Exact shape, not a bound. A disc for a chamber or room; a capsule following
the real bowed quadratic spline for a tunnel, connection or water feature.
The bow is load-bearing — authored tunnels leave their rooms along a control
offset that can sit ~60° off the chord, so a chord-based capsule would protect
substantially the wrong volume.
A capsule knows which end its z anchors. An air passage is floor-anchored
and the surface cap lowers only its ceiling; a liquid body is surface-anchored
and the cap lowers the whole band. Modelling water the first way under-protects
it exactly where the terrain is shallowest.
Every shape carries a ProceduralContact. Seal dilates by 16 blocks
(plus the carve's own edge slack) in x, y and z and cuts the tunnel back at
that column. Connect has no margin — structurally, not as a tuning
choice: the clip's unit is a column, so any dilation of a connected void would
delete a ring of tunnel outside the chamber and strand the rest of it as a
sealed air pocket inside. Seal wins ties, because a promise beats a
permission.
Connect needs no blend or join code. apply_caves_to runs before the
authored carve, and the authored carve is an unconditional overwrite of its own
whole footprint. The join is produced by pass ordering; what survives is the
tunnel's own cross-section, which tapers to zero at its lateral edge, so the
opening always reads as a lens-shaped mouth rather than a rectangular punch.
The guard
One edit, in tunnel_bounds_at_from and nowhere else. That is the single
function apply_caves_to, tree.rs and shrub.rs all read tunnel z-ranges
through, so carving and vegetation suppression stay consistent by
construction under both policies — any other placement recreates COW-15's
"treeless corridor over a tunnel that is not there", scoped to authored
footprints.
Vegetation suppression deliberately does not track authored footprints: an
authored ceiling is capped four blocks below the surface and can never reach the
bands tree.rs/shrub.rs test, so suppressing there would clear trees over
intact ground — the same bug with the sign flipped.
Layering
The index imports no authored layer. world/src/layer/authored_regions.rs is the
one content-aware site that decides which authored layers contribute shapes; the
authored layers expose their resolved geometry through narrow accessors and know
nothing about the index. A future authored region inherits the index, the choke
point, the tie-break, the margin and the tests, and touches only the registration
site. procedural_contact is authored content — a RON enum literal,
#[serde(default)] to Seal, so an asset predating the field (including the one
shipped today) is protected rather than exposed.
Interiors hard-code Seal and get no RON field: they already author real surface
accesses, so they never needed a procedural tunnel to be reachable, and their
shape accessors deliberately over-approximate the carve — only sound for a shape
that gets dilated.
Cost
Per column: one OnceLock load, one bucket hash, then a bounding-box reject per
candidate shape before any spline solve. The bucket grid is chunk-granular over
a bowed segment's box, so most shapes a bucket hands back are not actually over
the column; without that reject the cubic solve would dominate exactly inside
authored footprints.
EDGE_SOFTNESS and SURFACE_MARGIN now have one definition each, read by both
carve sites, because the index is defined as the volume those carves produce.
For the same reason a unit test pins the three copies of the spline sample
against each other.

New tests: 19 unit tests on synthetic shapes (both policies, the margin, the

Known and bounded, recorded rather than fixed
A Connect void that sits inside a neighbouring Seal void's 19-block shell is
re-sealed, silently. That is the tie-break working as designed; the real-asset
test now reports the count (0 today) so the intent-loss is a number.
An interior water feature's narrow waterfall column is not indexed; a drop
deeper than the margin would leave its lower part unguarded. No authored
waterfall is that deep.
The index builds lazily inside a OnceLock on first query, so the first wave of
chunk-gen workers can convoy on it once. Same shape as the two existing
Cromatolis caches; worth warming eagerly before caves: true ships.

Adds `world/src/layer/authored_voids.rs`: an engine-general index of every
block volume an authored feature carves, plus the policy a purely-procedural
layer must obey when it meets one, and `world/src/layer/authored_regions.rs`:
the one content-aware site that decides which authored layers contribute
shapes. The index imports no authored layer, so a future authored region
inherits the whole mechanism and touches only the registration site.

Each authored shape is registered exactly as it is carved -- a disc for a
chamber or room, a capsule following the real bowed quadratic spline for a
tunnel, connection or water feature -- so the protected volume is the carved
volume dilated by one margin rather than a bounding circle several times too
big. The bow matters: authored tunnels leave their rooms along a control
offset that can sit ~60 degrees off the chord, so a chord-based capsule would
protect substantially the wrong volume. A capsule also records whether its
endpoint z anchors its floor (an air passage; the surface cap lowers only the
ceiling) or its surface (a liquid body; the cap lowers the whole band), so a
shallow water feature is not under-protected.

Every shape carries a `ProceduralContact`:

- `Seal` dilates by 16 blocks (plus the carve's own edge slack) in x, y and z,
  and cuts the procedural tunnel back at that column, leaving a solid plug.
- `Connect` has *no* margin, structurally rather than as a tuning choice: the
  clip's unit is a column, so any dilation of a connected void would delete a
  ring of tunnel outside the chamber and strand the rest of it as a sealed air
  pocket inside. It is tested against its exact carved volume and is a total
  no-op at the clip site -- the authored carve runs afterwards and
  unconditionally overwrites its own footprint, so the mouth the player sees
  is produced by pass ordering alone, with no blend or join code.

Where several shapes claim a column the strongest policy wins: `Seal` is a
promise, `Connect` only a permission.

The guard goes in `tunnel_bounds_at_from` and nowhere else. That is the single
function `apply_caves_to`, `tree.rs` and `shrub.rs` all read tunnel z-ranges
through, so carving and vegetation suppression stay consistent by
construction under both policies. Vegetation suppression deliberately does
*not* track authored footprints: an authored ceiling is capped four blocks
below the surface and can never reach the bands those two layers test, so
suppressing there would clear trees over intact ground.

Per column the guard is one `OnceLock` load, one bucket hash, and then a
bounding-box reject per candidate shape before any spline solve -- the bucket
grid is chunk-granular over a bowed segment's box, so most shapes it hands
back are not actually over the column. `EDGE_SOFTNESS` and `SURFACE_MARGIN`
now have one definition each, read by both carve sites, because the index is
defined as the volume those carves produce; a unit test pins the three copies
of the spline sample against each other for the same reason.

Cached on `Index` as a per-world `OnceLock`, `None` when no authored region is
loaded -- which makes every consumer a no-op, and generation bit-identical,
for a purely procedural world.

Measured on the real authored region: 1411 indexed shapes (1363 from 279
caves, 48 from the two interiors) across 12372 chunk buckets, largest bucket
19; 17 sealed caves breached by a procedural tunnel before the guard, 0 after,
over 43019 sampled authored columns; 150 surface cave markers unchanged;
+0.20% on the per-column tunnel query over a uniform 20000-column sweep and
+0.75% over 20000 columns drawn from the authored caves' own footprints;
worst interior spline-vs-bounds deficit -25.4 blocks.

The toggle stays off: the guard ships dead until `caves: true`.
