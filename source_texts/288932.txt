Many modern learning algorithms try to solve a JPEG compression artifact removal (CAR) problem in pixel domain by mapping a low-quality compressed image to the corresponding high-quality image. Although JPEG artifacts arise from quantizing discrete cosine transform (DCT) coefficients of nonoverlapping image blocks, researchers have utilized a transform domain as auxiliary information at most. On the other hand, it is well known and approved that extracting image blocks with overlap improves decompression performance. Inspired by these observations, we propose novel and fully transformed domain convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for the problem. We choose the discrete cosine transform (DCT), discrete sine transform (DST), and discrete Hartley transform (DHT)—another effective DCT-like transform in terms of energy compactness—as the transforms to be utilized and refer to them as harmonic transforms. We perform harmonic transforms on small overlapping blocks of compressed images in the first layer of the proposed networks and create spectral feature maps by properly ordering their harmonic transform coefficients with the same frequency. After a series of convolution blocks, we take the inverse harmonic transform of the corresponding image blocks at the end of the network and put the resulting decompressed blocks back in their place. We show that forward and inverse transform layers of our harmonic networks are efficiently implemented with fast convolution and deconvolution layers by using 2D harmonic basis images as convolution kernels with a mathematical justification. Experimental study indicates that although our harmonic networks have a simple network topology and much fewer parameters than compared state-of-the art deep networks, they are effective and efficient to suppress compression artifacts and they give comparable results.
