Final Project Report - DE-FG02-06ER25733
Efficient Feature-Driven Visualization of Large-Scale Scientific Data
Aidong Lu
aidong.lu@uncc.edu
University of North Carolina at Charlotte
Summary
Very large, complex scientific data acquired in many research areas creates critical
challenges for scientists to understand, analyze, and organize their data. The objective
of this project is to expand the feature extraction and analysis capabilities to develop
powerful and accurate visualization tools that can assist domain scientists with their
requirements in multiple phases of scientific discovery. We have recently developed
several feature-driven visualization methods for extracting different data characteristics
of volumetric datasets. Our results verify the hypothesis in the proposal and will be used
to develop additional prototype systems.
Results and Achievements
Here I summarize the main results of this project.
1. We have developed a time-line visualization approach, which is a highly abstraction of
various time-varying 3D simulations. The time line approach is flexible to adopt data
features from different scientific domains and be applied to a number of visualization
tasks.
Figure 1. The time line of a storm surge dataset demonstrates the effects of Hurricane
Isabel to Outer Banks, North Carolina. The left portion (blue to yellow) corresponds to
the periodicity of tidal elevations that influence every 12.4 hours. The right portion
(orange to red) indicates significant data changes of ocean surface from its normal
distribution during the hurricane. The circle in the snapshots indicates the user specified
regions.
2. We have developed an animated visualization approach, which takes the popularity of
animations in time-varying scientific simulations into account. The concept of event
graph is proposed to characterize data features from different attributes in a time-varying
dataset to a hierarchical tree structure. Different from previous animation approaches,
we generate animations through finding a storyline on the event graph. The results are
user-friendly to domain scientists, as they do not need to know the technologies to adjust
the rendering effect. Instead, they can choose their interested data features to generate
animations for representation and analysis.
3. We have worked closely with several groups on different scientific applications. Three
graduate students and one undergraduate student were supported through this project.
Two PhD students will graduate in December 2012 and May 2013 respectively. One
PhD student (previously supported as the undergraduate) will take his proposal exam in
Spring 2013.
