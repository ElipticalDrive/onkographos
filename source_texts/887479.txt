Description :
Bloodborne's native PS4 post-processing Anti-Aliasing relies on an early, low-pass spatial FXAA filter built for 1080p console displays (similar to the washed-out, blur-heavy AA implementation in GTA IV).
When rendered on modern PC monitors at 1080p, 1440p, or scaled resolutions, the current AA pass exhibits severe visual drawbacks:
Severe Temporal Shimmering & Edge Fuzzing:
Moving the camera causes high-frequency geometry (such as Central Yharnam's iron fences, Cathedral Ward railings, gravestones, and foliage) to pixelate and shimmer violently.
Loss of Sub-Pixel Detail & Texture Smearing:
The spatial filter treats fine texture detail (character hair, Hunter coat fabrics, blood splatter) as aliased noise, blurring the image without effectively smoothing jagged geometry edges.
Incompatibility with High Refresh Rates / Resolution Scaling:
Unlocking frame rates or running upscaling passes makes the stock AA filter breakdown more noticeable, forcing users to rely on external ReShade injections.
