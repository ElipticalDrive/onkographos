Tomographic reconstruction techniques have been employed in a variety of medical applications. An attempt at a general approach to tomographic reconstruction is presented in this chapter, followed by a brief discussion of some of the special problems in nuclear medicine, specifically attenuation and scatter correction. As a complete alternative to such iterative techniques, a number of so-called analytical reconstruction methods have been proposed. In tomographic reconstruction, it is important to limit the high frequency components before the reconstruction (ramp) filter is applied. A number of other reconstruction methods are also becoming of increasing importance, now that hardware suitable for implementing the corresponding algorithms is becoming available. In particular, the maximum entropy images from a single-slice ring system have shown striking improvement over conventional filtered backprojection reconstruction, since such a reconstruction process is less sensitive to errors caused by the angular undersampling of such systems.
