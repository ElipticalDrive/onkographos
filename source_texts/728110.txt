A BSDF makes two claims in different code. sample draws a direction and
pdf says how likely that draw was, and bsdf.hpp spends a page arguing the
second must exist separately from the first. The second half of that argument
arrives here: two independent claims about one distribution can be checked
against each other by a machine.
A million draws, histogrammed over 2048 cells of the whole sphere, against the
density integrated over each cell by a midpoint quadrature that never calls
the sampler. If the reference came from drawing more samples the test would
compare a histogram against a histogram of the same thing and pass whatever
either one did, so the two halves share no code.
The cells are pooled rather than fixed. Pearson's statistic needs about five
expected draws per cell to be chi-squared at all, a cosine-weighted hemisphere
puts almost nothing near the horizon, and v0.7's specular lobe will put almost
nothing anywhere but one cell. So cells close when their expectation reaches
five, and the degrees of freedom are counted from what is left — 49 cells at
256 draws, 1024 at a million. Half the grid has zero density because a
reflector scatters into one hemisphere; those bins are absorbed rather than
contributing a division by zero, and a draw landing in one is reported as a
bug rather than as a large residual.
The chi-squared distribution is two functions — a series and a continued
fraction for the regularised upper incomplete gamma, Abramowitz and Stegun 6.5
— because house rule 4 leaves nowhere to get it from.
Two exact facts sit under the table, because both would otherwise show up as
suspiciously equal chi-squareds and that is not a measurement. The three
alternatives of Bsdf share Lambert's sampling routine, so from one seed they
must draw one direction: over 2^20 draws, compared bit for bit, 0 differ. And
a wo below the horizon draws the mirror of the one above, because lambert.hpp
negates a z rather than sampling again: 0 of 2^20 differ from the negated draw.
And the calibration, which is the part worth keeping. A test that has never
failed is a test nobody has checked, so two dishonest samplers are run through
the same grid. Both draw exactly what Lambert draws and then misreport the
density. The gross one claims a flat hemisphere and dies at p = 0. The subtle
one claims cos^1.02 — two percent too steep — and returns a chi2/dof of 1.2,
which looks fine, at p = 5.7e-05.
That is the result. A two-percent error in a density is invisible in an image,
conserves energy exactly, passes the furnace, and looks like a chi2/dof of
1.2. It is also a question of how hard you look: the same liar is passed at
2^19 draws with p = 0.04 and caught at 2^20 with p = 6e-05. The power curve is
printed rather than described, and it is why verify runs the liars at four
times the draws it runs the honest samplers at.
Wired into ./cornell verify in short form, so a disagreement breaks a build
rather than waiting for somebody to run an instrument, and into CI whole,
because an instrument's printing path is code too.

CI caught something clang cannot. `0x9e37'79b9'7f4a'7c15ULL * uint64_t(n)` is a
-Wconversion error under g++-14 and silent under clang: on Linux uint64_t is
unsigned long and ULL is unsigned long long, the same width and a different
type. There is no gcc on this machine — /usr/bin/g++ is clang under another
name — so the two-compiler matrix is the only thing standing between this
project and a class of warning it cannot see locally, and it earned its place
on the first pull request that gave it something to find. The seeds are
uint64_t constants now, with a note saying why.
