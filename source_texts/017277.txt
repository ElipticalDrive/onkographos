Photorealistic rendering is the process of image generation by simulating the physical interaction of light in an environment. Specifically, the simulation involves how light from light sources is reflected and scattered throughout the environment. The environment, or scene, as it is sometimes called, consists of a collection of mathematically described geometric objects. The shape description of the objects is known as the geometric model of the environment; however, this is only part of the complete model needed for rendering. Surface properties, such as color and reflection characteristics, as well as volume elements such as smoke and fog, affect the light propagation and are therefore a necessary part of the model. These elements, and a model for the light propagation itself all require a mathematical representation that can be incorporated into the rendering simulation.
