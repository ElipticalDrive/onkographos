Two fixes that let a surface model and the atmosphere exchange the same radiative fluxes. Both surfaced in NumericalEarth's coupling of a land model to Breeze's RadiativeTransferModel.
Gray optics reflects the direct beam. The non-scattering gray shortwave solve carried no upwelling shortwave, so surface_albedo had no effect. A coupled surface that absorbs (1 - α) of the direct beam then dropped the reflected α of it from the budget: 224.6 W/m², 20% of the surface shortwave, at noon in NumericalEarth's test column. The surface now reflects direct_surface_albedo of the direct beam diffusely. The reflected beam climbs the column attenuated by exp(-D τ) per layer, with D ≈ 1.64 fixed at the diffusivity of the gray longwave solve's default quadrature (Gauss-Jacobi-5, one angle). The atmosphere absorbs its share, and the rest leaves through the top. The docs no longer say that gray optics ignores the albedo.
Fluxes are updated between solves, following Hogan and Bozzo (2015) as in the IFS and ecRad. Between two solves the surface temperature changes and the sun moves. The surface model sees both every step, while the atmosphere's heating stays frozen at the last solve: a surface that warms from 298 to 308 K between solves loses about 60 W/m² that nothing absorbs. With any schedule other than every iteration, every state update between solves now updates the fluxes:
Longwave. Each solve repeats the longwave solve with the surface 1 K warmer. That gives the response of every face's up- and downwelling fluxes to T₀⁴. Each update shifts the longwave fluxes by that response times the change in T₀⁴. The column absorbs its share of a change in surface emission, so surface and atmosphere agree on the boundary flux. For gray optics the update is exact, because the fluxes are linear in the surface emission. A constant surface_temperature skips the second solve. Full-spectrum RRTMGP clamps the surface temperature into its Planck tables (160 to 355 K): within 1 K of the top the second solve cools the surface instead, and above 355 K the updates hold the longwave fluxes, as a solve would.
Shortwave. Each solve uses μ̄₀, the cosine of the solar zenith angle averaged over the sunlit part of its radiation window (Hogan and Hirahara 2015, ECMWF TM 758; ecRad's cos_sza). The average is an exact time integral clipped at sunrise and sunset, per calendar day for the apparent sun. The window runs from the start of the solving time step, since SSP RK3's third stage returns to tⁿ + Δt/2, to the start of the step that solves next. Each update scales the solved shortwave fluxes by μ₀ / μ̄₀. The incoming flux at the top then tracks the current sun, and a sun that rises between solves lights the column from sunrise.
Re-solves. Within its window the sun stays below 4 μ̄₀ (the bound is approached only by a sun skimming the horizon around a short night). An update that finds μ₀ > 4 μ̄₀ solves again without advancing the schedule, since μ̄₀'s slant path would deposit the beam far too high in the column. This covers daylight after a night-time solve, a sun climbing far past a solve with no known interval, and an IterationInterval whose time steps grew past its estimate. A correctly estimated window never triggers it, including along the terminator of a global grid (tested).
One mechanism, ApproximateRadiationUpdates in src/AtmosphereModels/approximate_radiation_updates.jl, serves gray, full-spectrum RRTMGP and ecCKD. Each backend only supplies its perturbed-surface longwave solve, reusing its own flux copy. The update state is checkpointed, so a restart between solves continues bitwise. With the default IterationInterval(1) there are no steps between solves: approximate_updates is nothing, and behavior and cost are unchanged.
Cost and memory. One extra longwave solve per radiation call, when the surface temperature is not constant. Four extra ZFaceFields per model: the two longwave responses and the shortwave fluxes as solved. Per state update, a column kernel, the flux-divergence kernel, and one fused reduction whose result the host reads to decide on a re-solve.
Behavior changes.
Gray shortwave now reflects.
Models solving radiation on TimeInterval or IterationInterval(N > 1) schedules now get the updates, solve with μ̄₀ instead of the instantaneous μ₀, and can solve off-schedule when sunlight falls outside the window of the last solve.
RadiativeTransferModel has two new trailing fields and type parameters, approximate_updates and cache_valid.
Limitations.
Under Reactant the traced conditional only solves; fluxes are not updated between solves (documented).
The all-sky RRTMGP response relies on the extra longwave solve drawing the same McICA cloud sample. With RRTMGP 1.1 the sample is keyed on cloud_state.mcica_key, which this direct solve_lw! path leaves fixed, so the two solves agree; the cloudy all-sky test checks this. RRTMGP 0.22 and 1.0 draw the sample from the global RNG, so there the two solves sample different clouds. Raising the RRTMGP compat floor to 1.1 would close that.
The response comes from a finite difference. A solver-provided surface Jacobian, as in ecRad's lw_derivatives or RTE+RRTMGP's flux_up_Jac, would remove the extra solve. RRTMGP.jl and NumericalRadiation.jl do not provide one yet.
With no known interval, a re-solve fires each time the sun climbs fourfold, so the slant path can be up to four times too long between them.
Tests.
New test/approximate_radiation_updates.jl, Float32 and Float64:
longwave update against a re-solve at the new surface temperature, for gray (exact) and for clear-sky, cloudy all-sky and ecCKD (changes within 2%), and for clear-sky at and above RRTMGP's 355 K ceiling;
top-of-atmosphere shortwave equal to -S₀ μ₀ after the solve and after an update;
a sun rising between solves, and daylight at either end of the window;
re-solves for a sun climbing past a solve with no known interval, and for an underestimated IterationInterval;
a solve in the time step of sunset averaging from the start of the step, with no re-solve at SSP RK3's third stage;
a 24×12 latitude-longitude grid solving only on schedule over two hours;
time stepping between solves, with the column budget closing, checkpoint restarts, and every Runge-Kutta stage following the sun.
New test/mean_solar_zenith_angle.jl checks the sunlit means against sampled ones.
test/gray_radiative_transfer.jl now checks the reflected beam analytically.
Run locally on CPU, all passing:
approximate_radiation_updates.jl, mean_solar_zenith_angle.jl and gray_radiative_transfer.jl, in Float32 and Float64;
checkpointing.jl, including the gray bitwise-restart case whose checkpoint falls between two solves;
radiation_scheduling.jl, solar_zenith_angle.jl, clear_sky_radiative_transfer.jl, all_sky_radiative_transfer.jl and ecckd_radiative_transfer.jl;
numerical_radiation_{column_round_trip,gray_column,overlying_atmosphere,storage}.jl;
the ExplicitImports checks, for Breeze and its RRTMGP, NumericalRadiation and CloudMicrophysics extensions.
Not run locally: GPU, Reactant, Aqua, and the docs build.

The radiation window now begins at the start of the solving time step,
since SSP RK3's third stage returns to tⁿ + Δt/2, and for TimeInterval
schedules extends to the step that solves next. A solve in the step of
sunset no longer finds daylight outside its window and solves again,
which on a global grid had doubled the radiation cost.

An update solves again when μ₀ > 4 μ̄₀. A correctly estimated window
never exceeds this ratio, so the rule fires only for daylight after a
night-time solve, a sun climbing far past a solve with no known interval,
or an IterationInterval whose time steps outgrew its estimate. The check
is one fused reduction and no longer writes into the sunlit duration.

Full-spectrum RRTMGP clamps the surface temperature to its Planck table
(160 to 355 K). The updates now use the same clamped temperature, and
within 1 K of the top the perturbed longwave solve cools the surface
instead. The batched solve restores the surface temperature from the
field rather than subtracting the increment.

The reflected-beam diffusivity of the gray shortwave is a fixed constant
instead of being read from the longwave solver's quadrature. The legacy
NaN checkpoint marker, which no committed format wrote, is removed.

🟡 (optional) Reactant users compiling a radiation run now trace a full radiation solve into every update_state! of a step, not only the scheduled first stage. On a traced clock radiation_update_due still returns false off stage 2, but OR-ing it with !radiation_cache_is_valid(rtm) turns it into a traced Bool. That emits a @ trace if around _update_radiation! at every stage. Fix: keep the traced validity check to one place per compiled step, such as the stage-2 conditional or prepare_first_time_step!. That keeps restart recovery without copying the solve into later stages and the end-of-step update.
Why this was flagged
Under ReactantState, rtm.cache_valid is a traced array (radiation_cache_validity(::ReactantState) = on_architecture(arch, [false]), ext/BreezeReactantExt/Timesteppers.jl:78), and radiation_cache_is_valid(valid::AbstractArray) = @ allowscalar only(valid) returns a TracedRNumber{Bool} while tracing. update_radiation! On the base branch only the stage-2 update_state! carried that branch. Each compiled time step now holds several copies of the radiation graph, so compile time and program size grow for every Reactant radiation run. 🟡 (optional) Runs with a fixed sun and a constant surface temperature now do per-stage work that never changes the fluxes, and blocking host syncs, whenever radiation is scheduled. Examples are FixedCosineZenith with surface_temperature = 300 on IterationInterval(N>1) or a TimeInterval. approximate_radiation_updates returns nothing only for IterationInterval(1), so these models allocate six ZFaceFields. Every update_state! then runs a column kernel, the divergence kernel and a fused reduction whose result the host waits for. Fix: return nothing when the sun cannot move (FixedCosineZenith) and surface_temperature is a ConstantField.
Why this was flagged
approximate_radiation_updates (approximate_radiation_updates.jl:72-73) checks only schedule isa IterationInterval && schedule.interval == 1. With FixedCosineZenith, update_cos_zenith! is a no-op (solar_position.jl:280), and average_solve_cos_zenith! falls back to doing nothing, so μ₀ / μ̄₀ == 1 at every update. With a constant surface temperature, solves_longwave_response is false and ΔT⁴ == 0 in _update_radiation_between_solves!. Every update therefore rewrites the fluxes with their solved values. Even so, each non-solving update_state! calls update_radiation_between_solves!, which runs update_cos_zenith!, a mapreduce the host waits on (mapreduce(identity, |, daylight_solves; init=false)), _update_radiation_between_solves! and compute_radiation_flux_divergence!. On the base branch these steps did no radiation work between solves and allocated nothing extra. Users pick a sparse schedule to make radiation cheap, so the cost lands exactly where they wanted the savings, and it hurts most on GPU, where each reduction forces a device sync.
Verification: approximate_radiation_updates.jl:73 returns nothing only for schedule isa IterationInterval && schedule.interval == 1. On the base branch, steps between solves did no radiation work. Now every RK stage pays one column kernel, one divergence kernel and one reduction with a blocking host sync, all with no change in results.
