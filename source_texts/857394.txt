Closes #22.
What
lib/fc_sky.ps gets a computed sky: single scattering of sunlight in a clear atmosphere (Rayleigh air, a thin aerosol layer, an ozone layer, over a curved earth), with a cheap multiple-scattering pass. The sun's direction alone now sets the time of day:
Sun colour is white sunlight through the air along the sun's path (Chapman-style air mass, continued below the horizon), so it reddens as the sun drops: alpenglow on the peaks while the valleys, lit by the sky, stay blue.
Sky per direction from an eight-band integral along the view ray (closed-form transmittance per band; within a few percent of a fine march at every sun height). The aerosol's forward lobe is the glow round the sun; the ozone layer tints the twilight.
Haze keeps the same closed-form height-fog integral, but its colour is the model's horizon in the ray's direction (so haze toward the sun carries the phase term and goes orange at dusk), and HazeChroma fades each channel at the air's own rate: distant ground goes blue before it goes pale.
SunCol, Zenith, Horizon, SkyAmb, AmbTop, AmbBot are optional: derived from the model unless the piece sets them. A piece that sets Zenith or Horizon gets the old blend sky, unchanged.
Night: a moon (MoonElev/MoonAzim or MoonDir) lights the sky through the same scattering and, once it outshines the sun, takes over SunDir/SunCol for the ground and clouds (day-for-night; Exposure is the eye). Stars from a hashed cell grid, faded under a bright sky.
SunElev/SunAzim set the sun by angles.
Time-of-day sweep of gallery/mountains.ps (only the sun, a moon after dark and the exposure change between tiles): docs/prs/22-sky-model/sweep.png, made by sweep.sh + sheet.py there.
Look change (deliberate)
mountains and alpine_forest leave the colours unset, so they move to the model; evening_lake sets SunCol/Zenith/Horizon/SkyAmb and is pixel-identical. The model is calibrated so the sun at the pieces' heights (25° and 35°) lands near the old hand-picked palette: sun (1.00, 0.92, 0.76) vs (1.00, 0.93, 0.80), ambient (0.59, 0.71, 0.90) vs (0.58, 0.66, 0.80). What changes: the zenith is a less saturated, more physical blue, the horizon whiter, the sunlight a touch warmer, and far ridges a little bluer (the haze colour is the model's horizon, and blue fades slightly sooner).
Before / after at 320×200: docs/prs/22-sky-model/mountains-before.png, mountains-after.png, alpine_forest-before.png, alpine_forest-after.png. References in tests/regress/linux/ regenerated with scripts/regress.sh --docker --update. After the rebase onto #50, charted_range (which leaves the colours unset too) also moves to the model; its reference changed very little (charted_range-ref-before.png / -after.png, 160×100), and evening_lake's stayed byte-identical.
Decisions
Model: a single-scattering integral (Nishita 1993 physics, Hillaire 2020's per-segment closed form) rather than Preetham or Hosek–Wilkie. Preetham's fit goes wrong at low sun and needs CIE conversions; Hosek–Wilkie needs a 3×1080-coefficient table, impractical in a hand-written PostScript library. Eight bands cost about 12% on a mountains render (160 s → 180 s at 320×200).
Defaults: the model is on whenever a piece leaves Zenith and Horizon unset (the issue's "optional"), so the two gallery pieces that never picked colours change; the one that did keeps them.
Calibration: Rayleigh coefficients (5.2, 12, 35)·10⁻⁵ per 10 m (an sRGB fit near Bruneton's), SkySat 1.2, SkyGain 14, AmbGain 2.3 — chosen so the derived colours at the gallery's sun heights sit near the old palette.
Multiple scattering: the once-scattered sky's hemispheric mean scattered again (one extra pass per sun position, 32 samples at load). Without it the horizon is yellow-green at mid sun.
Moon takes over SunDir: the surface libraries read SunDir/SunCol directly and are read-only for this issue, so moonlight reaches the ground by fc-sun-update swapping them when the moon is brighter at the camera; the true sun is kept in SkSunDir for the next update.
Twilight is a dim purple-grey with an orange band toward the sun; the deep blue of real civil twilight needs multiple scattering at altitude that the cheap pass doesn't give. Noted as a limit.
Not in this PR
Sun shafts through cloud gaps are Sun shafts through cloud gaps (follow-up to #22) #49: lib/fc_clouds.ps was being changed by Cloud types and layered skies (stratus, altocumulus, cirrus, cumulus, nimbostratus) #14 at the same time, so no cloud-shadow probe was added to the haze here; fc-haze keeps its closed form.
Review fixes (second round)
Disc in the haze colour (blocker): fc-haze-colour now samples fc-sky-light (no discs, no stars) when the model is on; the blend sky keeps fc-sky for identity. Confirmed on mountains with SunElev 2 SunAzim 10: no column under the sun (docs/prs/22-sky-model/lowsun-2deg.png).
Saturation clip: SkySat is limited per sample to L / (L − min channel), so no channel clips; daytime samples are unchanged (their limit is above 1.2).
Teal foreground in alpine_forest: the cause was not the ambient (changing it moved the conifers by one unit) but HazeChroma: it used the clear model air's channel ratio (blue 2×) for a haze that the piece's HazeDist says is seven times thicker than clear air, i.e. mostly aerosol. The weights are now Rayleigh air plus enough neutral aerosol to reach HazeDist in green: blue 12–15% faster, red 4% slower. Foreground conifers are back near the old colour. The derived ambient is also pulled further toward grey (65%, AmbGain 2.1), landing at (0.61, 0.67, 0.79) sRGB against the old hand-picked (0.58, 0.66, 0.80).
Golden hour: sweep gains tiles at 5° and 10° with the sun in frame, and 5° at evening; sunrise/sunset exposure 3. The "valleys stay blue" claim is softened in techniques.html §9 and here: with haze, a low sun warms sky, haze and ambient together, so the result is a golden wash with the warmest light on the snow rather than textbook pink-on-blue alpenglow.
Stars: multiplied by the view ray's transmittance (SkTa), the faint end of the brightness distribution lowered (0.03 instead of 0.1), and faded by a gaussian in the sky's luminance, e^(−(L/0.002)²): at the civil-twilight zenith (L 0.003) a tenth remains, so only the brightest few show through an exposure of 6; a moonlit sky (0.005 and up) hides them, as a full moon does, so the moon tiles are now starless and the star field needs a dark sky.
Cost: mountains 320×200 re-measured after the fixes: 187 s against 160 s on main, both with three renders sharing the machine (+17%; the earlier uncontended figure was +12%). fc-sky-scatter is in ProfileProcs for /Profile true.
Foreground check on alpine_forest (mean sRGB of the bottom 50 rows): main (72, 80, 84), first PR (70, 91, 114), now (76, 91, 94).
Scratch names SkSb/SkSs in the stars renamed (SkStb, SkSsg); check_names.sh allows SkyAmb/AmbTop/AmbBot, which fc_sky derives for fc_terrain/fc_clouds by design.
Codex review
codex exec -s read-only -c model_reasoning_effort=high reviewed the diff; fixes are in their own commit (24fbe67):
the haze colour sample skipped the stars (a star in it painted a bright column down the whole frame; visible in the first dusk tiles);
each band now asks for the sun at its own local elevation down the curved ray (Bruneton's geometry; dusk horizons were symmetric);
SunPower scales the scattered sky and the moon as well as the disc (SkyGain 12.7 keeps the look); multiple scattering only in the sun's pass (the moon pass was adding a second copy);
the moon takes over SunDir even when a piece set SunCol; the blend sky keeps its old SunCol default (a piece setting only Zenith/Horizon stayed as on main); light directions are never exactly level (fc_terrain's cloud shadow divides by the light's y: SunElev 0 crashed);
ambient colours encoded with 1 2.2 div, the exact inverse of fc-colour;
docs: sin² in the below-horizon air mass, the real haze ratio (red 1.5× not 2×), the twilight accuracy caveat.
Not changed (noted as limits in the docs or here): derived colours use the camera height at load (CamY 0; scenes are under 500 m so it does not matter, and fc-sun-update after setup recomputes), a fc-sun-update between loading fc_sky and fc_terrain would double-convert the ambient (no piece does this), and twilight overestimates the zenith with eight bands.
Pixel regression: scripts/regress.sh --ref main on the Mac shows evening_lake unchanged and the other two pieces changed everywhere by small amounts (max 55/255); --docker --update regenerated the references (evening_lake.png came back byte-identical).
