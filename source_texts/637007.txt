Owner
Problem
The owner asked for a way to inspect a fin's sections in 3D: a moving section plane through a see-through fin, horizontal and vertical, with controls that stay reachable while the view is rotated. Further analysis tools may follow.
Solution
In the 3D view
Analysis (top left, aqua when on) turns the fin see-through and shows Vertical and Horizontal check boxes and Reset view. Vertical is on by default; both can be on.
Vertical: an upright plane square to the base. Its slider runs along the bottom of the view, oriented the way the fin lies in the starting view (nose on the left for a left or centre fin, on the right for a right fin). Readout: distance back from the leading edge, the blade's height there, and its greatest thickness.
Horizontal: a plane at a height up the blade, showing the foil there. Its slider runs up the right edge. Readout: height, chord and thickness.
Planes are translucent orange; each cut is drawn in yellow with depthTest off, so it reads from any angle. Vertical cuts include the tab's section.
Reset view re-frames the camera to the starting side view. Rotate, pan and zoom work as before.
Sliders sit on the view's edges rather than as 3D handles, so they stay in reach however the fin is turned, and work by touch and keyboard.
Kernel (mesh-slice.ts, pure): sliceMesh(mesh, axis, at) returns one segment per crossed triangle; measureSlice(segments, axis) returns the cut's length (chord, or the blade's height above the tab top) and its greatest width across.
render3d: FinPreview3D takes sections (planes with their cuts): the blade and tab turn translucent, and each plane is drawn with its cut. sectionPlaneCorners sizes a plane to the scene's bounds plus a margin; FinScene now carries its bounds.
App (SectionAnalysis.tsx): analysis state lives in the page (not saved, not undoable). While analysis is open, the cuts use a mesh about 3× finer than the preview's (120 rows × 64 stations), built once per settled edit; moving a slider only re-cuts it. Planes stop 0.001 mm inside the blade's ends, so a plane on a row of mesh points still cuts. Widths are multiplied by cos(cant) to read square to the blade (the fin's own cant; a slice with a different cant reads slightly off above it).
Checked in the built app with software WebGL: correct planes, cuts and readouts; a slider move costs well under 20 ms.
The help page has a new "Section analysis" section.
