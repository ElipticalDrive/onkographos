Destination
A ranked technique catalogue — one GitHub issue, shaped like the vertical-slice spec — of client-side optimizations for BhayanakVoxel's current brick / LOD-ring / path-traced architecture that cut frame time and hitches without a visible change: conservative visibility and culling, LOD selection, residency and streaming, prefetch and eviction, scheduling, and dynamic content.
The catalogue carries tiers (must-have / high-value / conditional / rejected), a short sheet per technique, and 3–5 bhayanak bench experiments for the 4090. It ranks frame time first, then hitches, then VRAM. This effort produces the catalogue; choosing and scheduling the architecture is a follow-up effort.
Catalogue: Technique catalogue: extreme optimization without losing fidelity — settled in the grilling. The residency decision is ADR 0004 (on main).
Notes
Domain: a hobby/tech-demo voxel game on one fully voxel, editable, 16 km-radius Planet of 10 cm Voxels. Sparse 8³ bricks, camera-centred all-voxel LOD rings to orbit, path-traced lighting with a radiance cache, half-res tracing plus TAAU, continuous Zoom from first person to ~20–50 R. Budgets: 1440p60 on a 4090, ≈ 8.6 ms GPU, ~2 GB client cache, 50 m/s flight. Vocabulary lives in CONTEXT.md; architecture in docs/adr/.
Standing preferences:
Client-side only. Server interest management, replication policy and simulation budgets stay in M2–M4.
Current architecture only, but the open contracts may be refined. Keep the brick store, LOD rings and path tracer; the catalogue may reshape ring centring and radii, culling hooks, prefetch policy, scene layout and scheduling in LOD rings on the GPU #48, Streaming generation #49, Hardware RT trace() #50 and Half-res tracing and TAAU upscale #51. No wholesale replacement.
Content is everything — terrain, vegetation, Debris and Fluids — but only how the client culls, LODs, streams and renders them; replication effects may be noted, server policy is out.
Fidelity is invisible by construction. Culling is conservative (never removes a surface that could contribute a pixel); LOD error stays under ~1 output pixel; no popping, holes or stale Edits. The image-diff gate against the reference path tracer is the referee.
Include and classify. Techniques that can change the image are still catalogued, in a rejected/watchlist section with the reason, not silently dropped.
Output is one GitHub issue, like the vertical-slice spec: tiers, per-technique sheets, rejected list, experiments.
Evidence is estimates plus experiments. The agent machine has no GPU, so numbers are ranges with a confidence level, from literature and first principles. The top picks each get a concrete bhayanak bench experiment for the dev's 4090.
Complexity is labelled. Every technique carries an agent-week cost; the recommended shortlist favours roughly 1–2 weeks each, and multi-week bets stay in the appendix.
Agent proposes tiers, the dev reviews and re-ranks.
Plain language. The dev is a graphics-programming beginner: explain trade-offs without jargon and give a recommendation.
Skills: research tickets call research; the synthesis ticket calls grilling + domain-modeling; use codebase-design when module seams come up.
Prior art in this repo: Vertical-slice spec, ADR 0002, tickets Generate bricks directly at any LOD level #47, LOD rings on the GPU #48, Streaming generation #49, Hardware RT trace() #50, Half-res tracing and TAAU upscale #51 and M1a bench path and budgets #64.
Cross-ticket dependency: #79 LOD Material: prefer the exposed surface is mandatory for LOD fidelity. Geometry transitions are clean; coarse Materials flip to underground rock without it. It is an M1a ticket, not a ticket on this map.
Cave target: #46 Caves and overhangs now carries 8 m maximum cave width and ~1 km lateral reach; the horizon cap applies to level 7+ (12.8 m cells), so the cap is orbit-first.
ADR: 0004 Residency is a 360° shell (on main).
Decisions so far
Research: measurement and fidelity gates: the bench can time passes but cannot see hitches — offscreen frames block on readback, so frame_time is draw + readback and a mean/p95 gate misses a 1.6 s repack. Defines hitch p99/p99.9, resident bytes, generation throughput, upload (steady vs event), culling false positives/negatives via a brick-id buffer, prefetch hit/coverage, LOD error in output pixels, and MSE + FLIP image diff. Gates ordered strongest-first: exact brick-id equality, CPU hole oracle, analytic 1-px LOD bound + FLIP, pinned-Mesa lavapipe goldens, path tracer as shading referee. Five experiments with 4090 commands; CI can gate fidelity and throughput, only the 4090 can gate time, VRAM, hitches, real streaming and TAAU quality.
Research: vegetation, Debris and Fluids on the client: unedited vegetation and the ocean are as regenerable as terrain; the genuinely unrecoverable set is diverged bricks (Edits, settled Fluids, merged Debris) plus live Debris and active Fluid — one diverged-brick set gives all three the same residency, persistence and invalidation policy. Vegetation stays Voxels, but majority downsampling deletes a forest, so generate it per LOD level and compose remake per child (+60–130% near-ring bricks for +15–40% generation work). Debris is a hitch problem, not VRAM (~5 MB for 1,000): cap/batch BLAS builds, one-frame static-to-instance handoff, bricks before instance on merge, interpolate poses; per-object culling rejected because the BVH already culls and culling changes shadows/GI. Fluids: 20 Hz deltas through the append-only Scene::update cross the compaction threshold every 0.5–2 s, so use stable per-brick slots patched in place; settling is a persistence event; model the ocean as a BrickSource plus an exact surface, removing 25–50% of ring bricks.
Research: scheduling and upload hitches: the 1.6 s rebuild is a full repack from the BrickStore, not compaction — 1.25–1.37 s for 2.63 M bricks, of which 139 ms is sorting and the rest BTreeMap grouping plus repacking at 0.50 µs/brick. A 1 m dig's patch is 20–90 µs and ~5 KB, so editing itself is already hitch-free; the rebuild triggers on a missing region (constant once rings stream) and on garbage over len/16 + 4096 (~2 K digs). Compaction can be designed away with per-region ref runs and brick-data free lists that rewrite in place, and uploads should grow only the overflowed buffer (not all five, 136 MB) and bound each frame to 4 MB / 64 writes through one StagingBelt. Keep the one-thread renderer; a world thread plus N−2 workers, priority classes with age/reserved slots/epoch cancellation, and the frame thread applying pre-packed bytes. Bench gaps: it never digs or streams and reports p95. Cross-ticket: the 2 s spawn ring implies ≈200 K bricks (a 120 m full-crust ring is 9–32 s today), a 1 m dig changes no coarse brick at any of 19 levels, and sim::apply at the 2 m brush costs 4–10 ms on the frame thread.
Research: conservative visibility and culling: culling cannot cut rays in this renderer — trace() is a DDA that only visits what a ray crosses, so a per-ray frustum test is added work and a depth prepass duplicates the primary pass. Only ~2.0 of the 8.6 ms frame is addressable at all; the honest frame-time win is 0.05–0.25 ms (1–3%). The wins are hitches and VRAM. Frustum alone keeps 7.7% of surface bricks at eye height while 1.7e-5 are visible (4,650× waste; 1.7e7× looking 60° down, because the cone takes the far side of the Planet). The horizon cap fixes it at 3.7 ns/node, view-independent and with no look-around risk: 2.0–2.1× the coarse set at 20–50 R (the visible fraction never exceeds ½), 4× at 1 R, 30–3,000× between 10 m and 1 km; with the frustum it leaves 1.0–2.1× the visible set. Occlusion does not pay — Hi-Z is circular for camera rays and wrong-facing for shadow rays; conservative occupancy mips cannot be both cheap and exact, and the only exact occluders are the outer sphere and Core. Culling belongs in CPU scene selection on a camera change (12.5 ns/node measured), and Scene's lack of per-node bounds is the one real contract gap. Proof gates: bit-exact on/off PNGs, a CPU ray-incidence oracle (visited ⊆ retained), and a no-hole path including a cave dive. Blocker: Caves and overhangs #46 never states cave width or lateral reach, so the horizon cap's cell-size gate has nothing to compare against.
Research: residency, prefetch and eviction: the guarantee is a complete 360° shell, not faster prefetch. A 180°/s turn sweeps the horizon at ~800 m/s — 16× the 50 m/s flight speed — so frustum-only residency shows holes (or coarse pops) no matter how fast the loader is. The working set is small; generation is the bottleneck: ~27–52 MB of bricks (53–105 MB at k = 2) at one Voxel per output pixel, peaking at 50–100 m altitude, plus a 20 MB always-resident guard at levels 8–13. Horizontal churn at 50 m/s is ~37 K bricks/s against ~130 K/s on eight cores. Sizing follows ring geometry: r0 = 118 m, level L has 0.1·2^L m Voxels and 0.8·2^L m bricks, each ring one octave and ~40–50 K bricks. Take-off is the worst case, not flight: a 50 m/s vertical climb moves the horizon at ~4.5 km/s and needs ~55 K new bricks in the first 0.16 s; a 20 m vertical margin removes the spike for ~19 MB. Evict by distance band, then LRU inside it (Cesium's pattern), with 1.25× / 0.5 s hysteresis, overflow rather than holes, and pinning of the guard, required set, in-flight requests and live-Edit chain. Edits are journaled first, epoch-stamped and patched before submit: ~370 bricks / ~75 KB for a 2 m Brush across all resident levels, zero stale frames; the disk cache stores only unedited bricks. What changes in Streaming generation #49: nearest first at all azimuths, per-level sparse Scenes, Generate bricks directly at any LOD level #47 as a hard dependency, guard-first startup, and zero hole/stale/soft-miss counters.
Research: LOD selection and transition fidelity: select one level-L Voxel per output pixel, switching at d = 102.8·2^L m. Measured surface displacement from majority downsampling at those distances is 0.27 / 0.55 / 0.87 output px (mean / p95 / max); using one internal pixel instead doubles that for a 4× cut in bricks, so it is a bias knob. Rings are per-brick distance bands with 5% hysteresis, camera-centred shells, 13 levels (0–12), at most 6 rings populated (peak at low altitude), one at 20–50 R; a ring is 3πf²/64 ≈ 156 K bricks, bounded by output resolution rather than radius. Peak ≈ 0.93 M bricks ≈ 130 MB; the orbital Planet is a few hundred bricks. Cracks are impossible by construction (closed volumes plus an exact partition); 0/512 rays missed at coarse levels, and the seam is a ≤ 1 px step, so no stitching, dithering or geomorphing is needed. The real blocker is Materials, not geometry: the top Voxel flips SOIL→ROCK on 51% / 85% / 99% of columns at levels 3 / 4 / 5, so #79 is mandatory and must be consistent across levels; FAR_BLEND and voxel_tint must be removed. Generate bricks directly at any LOD level #47 is the biggest win: today's bottom-up pyramid needs ~30 M level-0 bricks (~4 GB) for an orbital view, while a level-agnostic BrickSource makes it ~150–300 bricks. Test tolerance should be surface displacement ≤ ~0.5 coarse Voxel (p95), not a Voxel fraction. Five 4090 experiments are specified.
Task: synthesize and rank the technique catalogue: produced the catalogue. Settled LOD-first (culling is 1–3% of frame time), the 360° shell plus always-resident guard, strict 1 px LOD, a 40–50 m full-crust spawn ring, an orbit-first horizon cap with Caves and overhangs #46 at 8 m caves / ~1 km reach, the M1a-1 walk / M1a-2 fly-orbit split, ocean Voxels until M4, a CPU prefetch simulator before Streaming generation #49, and X1 → X3 → X2. ADR 0004 is on main.

Out of scope
Replacing the engine, the renderer, or the brick/LOD architecture (GPU-driven scene, virtual geometry, mesh fallback, a different acceleration structure).
Server-side interest management, replication policy and simulation budgets (M2–M4).
Techniques whose only win is lower fidelity — impostors, checkerboard rendering, baked GI, foveation. They are catalogued in the rejected/watchlist section, not pursued here.
Multiple planets, console/mobile/web targets, gameplay systems.
