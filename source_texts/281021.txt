M4's last unbuilt piece, shipped as a first slice behind voxel.GI.Enabled (default off). The mechanism verifiably works; the visual quality is rough and there is one defect I could not explain. Both are stated plainly below rather than buried.
Approach — surface-voxelized cone tracing (Crassin et al. 2011), CPU-side
Three choices, each forced by a real constraint:
Surface, not solid, voxelization. Cones stop at the first surface, so interiors never matter — which is fortunate, because the only voxel data reachable without touching VoxelWorldSubsystem's PImpl (owned by another agent) is the greedy-mesher quad list already flowing through SetChunkQuads — itself a surface description, and one that already fires on stream-in and every edit remesh.
Field aligned 1:1 with level-0 chunks (8³ cells × 40 UU = ChunkEdgeUU), so brick lifetime, dirtying, and eviction follow chunk streaming exactly.
Scalar into VertexColor.G, the channel M_VoxelTerrain already multiplies into BaseColor. No material-asset edit, no global shader, no render-pass integration — reviewable CPU code. With GI off the emitted FColor is byte-identical.
Six 90° cones per surface-adjacent air cell → an ambient cube, recombined per-vertex with max(0, N·D). Mips are MAX-aggregated, biasing toward too-dark rather than light leaking through thin walls. Mesher AO is kept and multiplied in — it covers contact scale, the cone covers large scale.
Edit response
SetChunkQuads is the entire mechanism — no deterministic path is read or written. An edited chunk's brick is re-voxelized (clear-then-fill) and its 5×5×5 neighbourhood queued for re-solve. One bounce is progressive: cones pick up the previously-solved irradiance of what they hit, so nothing has to converge within a frame.
Budget
Per-frame caps: 16 voxelizes, 8 brick solves (blocking ParallelFor), 2 round-robin refreshes, 4 proxy rebuilds, 4096 bricks. At 1,999 resident bricks: 8.2 MB, 0.5–0.6 ms tick. A big explosion lengthens the queue, never the frame. Only level-0 chunks feed or sample the field; GI fades back to plain AO over 4800–6400 UU.

Screenshots — docs/images/m4-gi/
03/04-roofed-* is the convincing pair: the enclosed wall face goes bright cream → dark grey. 05/06-breach-* shows cut faces correctly dark after holing the roof. 01/02-outdoor-* is weak — GI deepens crevice occlusion but the framing is mostly ocean.
⚠️ Honest assessment — read this before judging the screenshots
The lighting is rough. It's monochrome; it modulates albedo rather than the ambient term (which only reads acceptably because the material is flat and untextured); resolution is vertex-rate.
One unresolved defect: in 04-roofed-gi-on.png the roof slab underside renders fully lit even though the field probes 0.00 there and the chunk's vertex data is dark. The agent could not explain it and flagged it rather than overselling the shot. Filed as the top follow-up.
And a finding that matters beyond GI: an underground screenshot could not be produced at all. The streaming footprint only meshes a band around the terrain surface, so a camera 9 m down sees open sky beneath a thin crust regardless of GI. That took three runs to discover and is why the enclosure fixture is above ground. This is a real limitation of the current streaming, not of GI — and it will block caves (M4), mining depth, and any underground gameplay until addressed.
Instrumentation confirms the field itself is correct: voxel.GI.Debug 2 shows open terrain solving to 0.65–0.70, buried/enclosed chunks to 0.001–0.02, ~95% vertex hit rate.
Follow-ups
Resolve the roof-underside discrepancy; move tracing to GPU (the CPU per-vertex sampling is what costs the GI-on ms); coloured bounce; sun-visibility cone; underground streaming.

Surface-voxelized voxel cone tracing (Crassin et al.), CPU-side, outside
the determinism boundary. Default OFF via voxel.GI.Enabled.

- VoxelLightField.{h,cpp}: sparse world-space field aligned 1:1 with
  level-0 render chunks (8^3 cells of 40 UU == one 320 UU chunk), built
  by surface-voxelizing the greedy-mesher quads. 4 in-brick mips +
  3 sparse global levels, MAX-aggregated to bias against light leaking.
  Six 90-degree cones per air-cell adjacent to a surface -> an ambient
  cube; progressive one-bounce gather that converges over re-solves.
- VoxelGI.{h,cpp}: UVoxelGISubsystem owning budgets, dirty queues and
  16 voxel.GI.* cvars. Every stage is capped per frame, so a large
  explosion lengthens the queue rather than the frame.
- VoxelChunkComponent: the scene proxy samples the field per vertex and
  folds the result into VertexColor.G, the channel M_VoxelTerrain
  already multiplies into BaseColor -- no material asset change needed.
  SetChunkQuads notifies the subsystem, which is the entire edit
  responsiveness mechanism (it already fires on every edit remesh).
  With GI off the emitted FColor is byte-identical to before.
- VoxelEarthGameMode: one anchored, self-contained test-switch block
  (-VoxelGIOn, -VoxelGITest, -VoxelGIBreach).

M1 60fps gate re-run (-VoxelPerfRun=60, seed 20260719, min-spec proxy):
GI off p95 4.82 / 4.85 ms -- matches the published 4.79-4.93 ms band,
unchanged. GI on p95 7.80 ms, 0 post-warmup hitches, still inside the
16.6 ms bar. Screenshots and an honest assessment of what is still
missing are in docs/status.md.
