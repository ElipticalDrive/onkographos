RUNE v10 — targeted empirical improvement over v7; HOLD for merge
Draft research PR stacked on RUNE v7 PR #2. NO-GO v8/v9 not integrated; main and proprietary LICENSE unchanged. Do not merge automatically.
Mechanism
Adds full-width 7-pass 10-bit LSD stable radix (six 10-bit digits and a 4-bit final digit), 7×1024 counters ~56KiB; scratch N×8 bytes.
Guarded at N≥524288, high-entropy 64-element sample (≥20 descents, ≥60 varying bits), and [40,180] observed exact duplicate events before 4K-key bounded-frequency rejection.
No correctness reliance on the sample. Both radix variants sort the complete uint64 domain, so wrong path selection affects performance only.
Frozen v7 frequency tiers and other sorting paths are unchanged in this distinct candidate header.
Full, completed verification
GitHub full CMake differential and adversarial tests: passed.
First five-seed v10 versus v7 benchmark: both ARM64 and x86 passed all exact-output checks.
Independent five-additional-seed, nine-sorter benchmark: both ARM64 and x86 passed all exact-output checks. Pinned Google Highway VQSort, IPS4o, Boost pdqsort/spreadsort, std::sort and frozen v5/v6/v7.
17 workloads at N=100K and N=1M. Five independent held-out seeds; 4 measured repetitions after 1 warmup. Shared inputs, randomized sorter call order, copy/reference validation excluded from timed area.

x86 runner: AMD EPYC 7763. ARM64 runner: Neoverse-N2. The first direct x86 test had a different Intel Xeon CPU; do not pool raw times across CPU models.
New path improved v7 on the target repeated-key band, typically 1–5% ARM and ~10–20% x86, but Highway VQSort still beats v10 substantially on x86 above ~81K keys.
Negative findings: ARM random32 11.96ms v10 vs 11.39ms v7 and sparse16 18.85 vs 18.49; x86 sparse16 5.73 vs 5.48. These off-target losses may reflect code layout / VM noise; they remain unresolved.
Among 17 one-million-key workloads: v10 outright fastest in 9 on ARM64 and 5 on x86; VQSort won 9 on x86. x86 N=100K VQSort won 13/17.
