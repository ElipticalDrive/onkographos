Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting has emerged as a novel explicit representation for 3D scenes, achieving high-fidelity view synthesis through differentiable rasterization of anisotropic Gaussian ellipsoids. However, web-based deployment using legacy WebGL suffers from severe CPU-GPU synchronization bottlenecks, limited parallel computing, and suboptimal rendering efficiency for large-scale models. This paper presents a high-performance rendering framework built natively on WebGPU. We first design a WebGPU-adapted 3DGS data organization paradigm that optimizes memory and VRAM layout for efficient transmission. We then propose a complete optimization pipeline featuring asynchronous PLY preprocessing, octree-based frustum culling, GPU-parallel bitonic sorting, vertex pulling, rasterized shading, a three-level caching mechanism, and scene roaming. Experimental results on four benchmark scenes (235k–876k Gaussians) demonstrate that the proposed method achieves real-time rendering performance ranging from 493.0 to 1494.9 FPS, while maintaining GPU sorting time within 2.5–3.5 ms. Compared with the state-of-the-art WebAssembly-based baseline SuperSplat, our system achieves overall performance improvements ranging from 1.13× to 3.87× across different scene scales. In dynamic interaction and scene roaming scenarios, the peak frame-rate acceleration reaches up to 9× while maintaining visually comparable rendering quality. The proposed framework provides an efficient and scalable solution for next-generation web-based 3D visualization.
Keywords: 3D Gaussian Splatting, WebGPU, 3D Visualization, Rendering Optimization
