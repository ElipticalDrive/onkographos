View Video Presentation: https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2023-3241.vid Two different gradient reconstruction techniques are analyzed in the context of a cell-centered, finite volume formulation for the discrete solution of the gas dynamics equations. Attention is given to the usage of hybrid meshes. The quality of the results is assessed by considering two different test cases of interest to the aerospace industry. The first one is the two-dimensional, subsonic NASA high-lift Common Research Model multielement airfoil. The second case is the three-dimensional, transonic ONERA M6 wing. All cases are simulated using the compressible Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes equations. Turbulence effects are taken into account by making use of the negative Spalart-Allmaras turbulence model. Spatial discretization uses the Roe second-order, total variation diminishing, numerical flux for the definition of the cell-interface inviscid flux vectors. Viscous flux vectors are discretized by applying a standard centered scheme. Further comparisons are made with experimental and numerical data available in the literature. Very simple approaches may lead to numerical instabilities. More robust formulations are able to circumvent this problem and achieve comparable results, with no meaningful differences for the cases herein considered. Very good agreement is observed with experimental data and numerical results reported in the literature.
