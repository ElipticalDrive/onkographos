Why
Haze is by distance only. Valleys filled with mist, low fog over a swamp, and the warm glow towards the sun at sunset all need fog that thins with height and lights up towards the sun.
How other engines do it
Unreal Exponential Height Fog has density that falls off with height, integrated analytically along the view ray. It adds directional inscattering (a coloured glow towards the sun) and a start distance.
Unity HDRP fog has a base height, a maximum height, a mean free path and an optional colour from the sky.
Both let materials (for example skybox elements) opt out of fog.
Proposed approach
Add view.Fog(new FogSettings { Density, HeightFalloff, BaseHeight, Color, SunGlow, SunGlowColor, StartDistance, ColorFromSky }).
Integrate the exponential height density analytically along each pixel's ray in the lighting pass (or a cheap fullscreen pass), and blend towards the fog colour. The colour can be taken from the sky in the view direction.
Add sun inscattering with a phase function (Henyey–Greenstein) on the first sun.
Apply it to glass and particles in their own passes.
Decide how the existing HazeStart/HazeEnd maps onto this (keep it as a simple case, or replace it).
This is the base layer that Volumetric fog and fog volumes #17 builds on.
Done when
A valley mist screenshot and a sunset glow screenshot.
Glass and particles are fogged consistently.
rendering.md is updated.
