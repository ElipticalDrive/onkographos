Flow is NVIDIA's sparse voxel fluid simulation, the standard choice for real-time fire and smoke that reacts to the world rather than looping a sprite sheet. Sparse grids mean cost tracks the occupied volume rather than a fixed box, which is what makes it viable in an open scene.
Integration shape:
An emitter component (position, shape, temperature, fuel, velocity) and a simulation volume that allocates sparse blocks around active emitters. Gameplay pokes emitters; the engine owns stepping the simulation on the GPU.
Rendering is the interesting half: Flow produces a density and temperature volume that wants raymarching, ideally composited against scene depth so smoke occludes correctly and receives shadowing from the directional light. That means it slots in with the volumetric work already on the tracker, and sharing the raymarch infrastructure with volumetric fog and light shafts is the sane approach rather than three separate marchers.
Backend reality: Flow targets D3D12 and Vulkan compute. GL and DX11 do not get it, which makes this the first feature that is genuinely backend-gated rather than merely unported, and the feature capability system needs to express that honestly.
Cost control through simulation resolution, block budget and step rate as quality tier settings, since this is the kind of effect that eats an entire frame budget if unattended.
Verify current licensing terms before vendoring, since the NVIDIA library licenses differ per package and have changed over time.
Done: a fire emitter produces rising smoke that curls around geometry, receives shadow, composites correctly against scene depth, and stays inside a declared millisecond budget at the medium tier.
