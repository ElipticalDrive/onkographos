Replace screen-space ambient occlusion (GTAO) with world-space occlusion built for how Halo is lit.
Why: Halo's level lighting is Bungie's baked radiosity, which already holds the level's own occlusion. What's missing is only between dynamic things and the world. Objects are lit by a single ambient sample, so a Spartan in a dark corridor or a Warthog under a bridge looks too bright and "floats". Screen-space AO only sees what's on screen. That means:
off-screen occluders don't count;
it shimmers and pops at screen edges;
it needs the stencil/mark bookkeeping that caused Ambient occlusion: stop objects behind walls showing through them #545;
it's too costly for the Steam Deck, mobile and VR.
Design:
Static world distance field, baked at map load. Build a signed distance field (or occlusion volume) of the structure BSP from the player's own map, once per map. Cache it like the menu pictures, never shipped. Objects sample it to get correct enclosure and occlusion from walls, overhangs and ceilings, on or off screen.
Analytic proxies for dynamic objects. A few capsules, spheres or boxes per object, from its skeleton or collision (a Spartan's limbs, a Warthog's body). These give exact, soft contact occlusion on the level and on other objects.
Applied only to the ambient term, through the per-surface ambient slot added in Ambient occlusion: darken the ambient light alone, not the flashlight and dynamic lights #574. The level's baked occlusion isn't darkened by itself; dynamic lights, the flashlight, self-illumination and the first-person weapon are untouched.
Stable (view-independent, no noise or denoiser), deterministic and render-only. Cheap enough for Vulkan and Metal on the Deck, mobile and VR.
Keep "off" pixel-identical. Retire GTAO once the new one is at least as good, or keep it as a fallback if useful.
Groundwork toward ray/path tracing later (worth doing while we're in here, so a future RT path plugs in instead of needing a rewrite):
A world-space scene representation: static level geometry and per-object meshes in GPU buffers with stable IDs and transforms, built once per map and updated per tick/frame. The distance-field bake uses it now; RT acceleration structures (BLAS/TLAS) would use it later.
Separated lighting terms in every shader: ambient/indirect (done in Ambient occlusion: darken the ambient light alone, not the flashlight and dynamic lights #574), direct dynamic lights, specular/reflections and emissive each in their own slot, so RT shadows, RT AO, GI or reflections can replace any one of them.
Per-pixel normals, material IDs and motion vectors. Motion vectors also unlock temporal anti-aliasing and modern upscalers. We already interpolate render state between ticks, so the previous-frame transforms exist.
A world-space light list (the per-pixel light grid is most of the way there), linear HDR throughout (have it), and support for sub-pixel camera jitter.
A "Classic" look switch that always bypasses all of this.
