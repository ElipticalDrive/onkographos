The user asked: "How much more complicated can shapes be and still perform well?"
Why the shapes are limited today
A ray-marched SDF costs pixels × march steps × the cost of one distance evaluation. Every extra primitive or blend in the shape formula is paid at every step of every pixel. That is why 23 and 25 sit near the #88 budget with fairly simple shapes (25: march ≈ 11 ms on the box's 780M).
Inigo Quilez describes the same limit: "often times I embrace the limitation and design my scenes and world accordingly, which means I often create simple shapes rather than the complex ones I'd love to do." (SDF bounding volumes)
The fix: bake each shape into a 3D texture
How it works: sample the shape's signed distance once into a 3D grid (a 3D texture). While marching, each step is then one trilinear texture fetch, whatever the shape is.
A knotted teapot, a genus-5 pretzel or a scanned sculpture costs the same per step as a sphere.
Cost then depends only on pixels × steps.
Size: 128³ R16F is 4 MB per shape. Keep the current and next shape resident, 8 MB in all.
Tektite: "A 128³ grid holds about 2 million samples, while a 256³ grid holds over 16 million." (baked SDFs)
Where shapes come from:
(a) any analytic SDF, however expensive, baked in the browser over ~8 frames (tiled, like the Paint Pour bake);
(b) any closed mesh (Utah teapot, trefoil knot, Klein-bottle immersion, cup with a handle, scanned objects), baked offline by a small node script into assets/shapes/*.sdf16 and loaded at start.
Morphs that can change genus: draw d = mix(dA, dB, s).
A convex mix of two 1-Lipschitz fields is still 1-Lipschitz, so sphere tracing stays safe.
The surface passes smoothly through topology changes (sphere → torus → pretzel), which is exactly what the Poincaré scenes want.
Safety: the trilinear value can over-estimate between samples. Tektite: "Ray marchers treat a sampled field as a conservative lower bound, widen their safety margins, and use a smaller step multiplier." So use a step × 0.8, and ray–box clip first so rays outside the volume skip straight to it.
Limits
Details thinner than about one voxel vanish and sharp corners round off. "features thinner than a voxel disappear from the field entirely." At 128³ over a unit box that's ~0.8 % of the shape's size.
Use 192³ (13.5 MB) only for shapes that need it.
Add surface-level detail as an analytic displacement on top (bumps on the beat), which stays cheap.
The bake must not drop frames: it is tiled over ≥ 8 frames, and the next shape is pre-baked during the current hold.
Other levers (smaller wins)
Bounding volumes around expensive parts. Quilez got an 8× speed-up on a complex character this way.
Over-relaxed stepping (Keinert et al., Enhanced Sphere Tracing), already in 25 per 23/25: declared render scales for the #88 ray-march gate; 25 cheaper march #104.
Complex-looking, cheap formulas: gyroid and other triply periodic minimal surfaces, domain repetition, twists. These give high genus for a few ALU ops.
Render scale (QA gates for every scene: flash limit (WCAG 2.3.1) + blended-performance budget #88 soloScale/blendScale) stays the last resort.
Plan for v3 (23 first, then 25)
shared/sdfvol.js: load a .sdf16 (header: dims, bounds, genus) and upload it as a TEXTURE_3D R16F. Bake from an analytic function in tiles. Provide march(volA, volB, s).
tools/bake-sdf.mjs (offline, node): mesh (OBJ) → 128³ signed distance, using a winding-number sign so small mesh defects don't flip the inside. It writes assets/shapes/<name>.sdf16 plus the genus taken from the mesh's Euler characteristic.
Shape library v1, at least 12 shapes across genus 0–5:
genus 0: teapot (no handle), bunny
genus 1: torus knot (2,3), cup with a handle, kettle
genus 2: double torus, pretzel
genus 3: triple torus, 3-holed pretzel
genus 5: gyroid cell clipped to a sphere
plus two scanned sculptures from a free-licence set
The existing look stays: bumps on the beat, K-colour, the camera flying through the holes, and shapeRate on macro1 as in 16b.
QA truths
Cost is flat in shape complexity: GPU p95 for the teapot, the pretzel and a 2-sphere differs by ≤ 10 %, at the same pixels, steps and scale.
Budget (QA gates for every scene: flash limit (WCAG 2.3.1) + blended-performance budget #88): solo p95 ≤ 12 ms at soloScale and blend p95 ≤ 7 ms at blendScale on the box, including a bake in the 60 s window.
Bake accuracy: for the analytic shapes, the max |baked − exact| at random points within 2 voxels of the surface is ≤ 0.5 voxel.
Genus stays true: the check-23 Gauss–Bonnet gate (23 Shape Shifter (Poincaré v2): mug ↔ kettle ↔ doughnut, surgery on the drop #56 ruling), with |∫K − 4π(1−g)| ≤ 0.05·∫|K| on resolved frames, holds on every settled shape. The header genus matches the measured one.
No frame drops while baking: the rAF p99 during a bake is ≤ 17.5 ms.
Morph safety: the step count stays bounded, with no holes or speckle, at s = 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 for each pair in the library (pixels of the miss-colour ≤ 0.1 %).
