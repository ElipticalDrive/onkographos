Description
Note: I'm adding this to 0.9.2 because I think this is highest priority given we got feedback on this removal prior to even the release of 0.9.0 and we should decide on this quickly.
This PR adds "nD" projection to the points layer as a strict Slice-only replacement for out_of_slice_display deprecation. As discussed in #9449, the present rescale_linear does not properly replace out_of_slice_display (previously called n_dimensional) because it only captures points whose position is within the slice. This is because the rescale modes introduced by #8786 do not treat the points with "ndimensional extents equivalent to their point size" but rather only checks for whether the point's position is within the thick slice (can I call this slab? I'm going to)
So, this PR re-introduces what out_of_slice_display actually did which is to treat the extent (i.e diameter) of the point as within the slice.
I created examples/points_projection_modes.py to show this, here with dims.thickness = (0,0,0,0)
And here with z thickness of 10:
There are pros/cons to each mode (especially play with the 4D-ness of the example), but importantly now we have the option to return to treating a points extent as within the slice, rather than just its position. Then, we return the point-disc equivalent to the sphere intersecting the slab and use either the linear fade (rescale_linear_nd) or the sphere fad (rescale_sphere_nd). While an argument could be made that these are "wrong", I think it's best understood that a point is just a position, so any visualization we do is precisely that, a visualization, and each has benefits to each other. Consider how there are even the sphere visual and not just disc for the points!
For this reason, I also now redirect layer.out_of_slice_display = True to rescale_linear_nd because it exactly reproduces the behavior from <0.9 but now also allows introducing slice thickness
This was extensively discussed last night with @pnewstein, @DragaDoncila, and @carlosmariorr at the community meeting, and they helped me round this out to the PR it is now 😁
Would also love to see feedback from @hanjinliu if this does work for your use case. Your diagrams were incredibly helpful! And then followed up by @brisvag
With this we recover projection mode none and out_of_slice true as rescale_linear_nd and spherical goes even further to return points with positions outside the slice and becomes rescale_spherical_nd I think this is actually what @pnewstein and @hanjinliu really want, which is different to what @brisvag thought through. If needed, we just need more projection modes 😉
Alternatives
Alternatives were discussed in #9449, including just returning out_of_slice_display, but because that goes through the layer rather than the slicing code path, I figure this is hopefully a defensible way to reintroduce the visualization.
Not reintroducing this because of some... I dunno principle? @jni gave an unclear reply of why we can't do this, but I think as a projection mode it does seem possible.

Mmm, this is exactly the kind of thing we were trying to avoid with the refactor, and the reason why I ended up not having projection affect which points are in the slice.

Thank you both, I thought about not including it as a projection mode, and returning something like out_of_slice_display as a boolean. The main conceptual blocker for me with that is that, as a refactor, I assumed that #8786 would be moving towards the slicing style that we want to use, namely I observe two things:
_get_slice_data returns both visible and size -- the latter being a "rendering-only" value
both None and rescale_spherical (https://github.com/napari/napari/pull/9534/changes#diff-e7120404911a55169b36a3a8932f63113d838b8b814c6fcf2031b5404296f49eL163) both change the slice fetch (membership) -- so projection mode is already integrated into the slicing machinery
Therefore, to me, pulling out not only an _ND-like rendering only makes sense if the slicing does not already have projection-mode branching, but our recent refactor stuck it there. Thus, we'd need some kind of separation of the membership and projection mode, and then that will also have degenerate combos which I think makes for a poorer user experience.
To me, at least, I have a hard time visualizing how #8786's refactor doesn't lead to putting the necessary fetch and size/fade pathways inside slicing without another refactor.
Also, this is again just to say that from my perspective, our slicing machinery is about "what data should be included in the slice" which doesn't necessitate that it's position is within the slice, given that there is interest in treating the rendering of points as an object-with-extent within the slice -- all seeming, to me, to point nicely to putting it in the slice machinery.
Edit: I think for me to be persuaded away from this I would need something more concrete to visualize the architecture than what has been recommended so far.

To add a bit more concreteness about why I'd like to take another release cycle to discuss this, and in particular to @brisvag's "we want to avoid removing and readding things multiple times":
If we are going to go down the route of the projection-mode can affect what is sliced (and I think probably that is ok to do, as long as it happens at slicing time, and not in a separate fetch), then maybe we don't actually need _nd, and we can just switch rescale_spherical and rescale_linear to the proposed behaviour. I actually don't love the fact that a large blob can fall out of view if its radius exceeds the slice thickness (the rightmost blob in the top-right panel in the figure on your OP), but we decided to punt on that in the hackathon — reasoning that a user can always increase the slice thickness. Which, if I understand correctly, is the workaround @pnewstein has implemented for now and works, even if a bit cumbersome. The fact that this workaround exists is why I'm willing to wait a bit longer — I do hope both @pnewstein and @hanjinliu can manage their work with the large-thick-slice workaround?
Even if we decide to keep separate modes, I don't think _ND is the right suffix, because the other modes are also n-dimensional anyway. Maybe _sized, or _extended? I don't know, but I do know _nd ain't it. Again, something to discuss before release so we don't have to change it again in the future.
Regarding slicing (and another reason why we decided to punt on dealing with extents at the hackathon), in the future, we hope to use tree indices (such as a KD-Tree or R-tree) for the points, which should massively increase performance for many points, but which makes it harder to account for size — we'd need to keep track of the bounding box of each point instead of just point coordinates. I think that's doable, but it's something in the back of my mind when looking at the points slicing code and how it interacts with projection mode. Again, I think it's doable, but we need to make sure that slicing happens only once, in the async path, and that it can (theoretically) be made to work with a tree search instead of a linear search.

Ok, so we talked extensively about this at the community meeting and came up with a plan. TLDR: let's go with projections only.
Here's an updated diagram with all the options we came up with:
Colors: red = full size, yellow = rescaled somehow
none: only points whose coordinate is on the slice center will be shown, full size
spherical: only points whose sperical extent intersect the slice center will be shown, rescaled to the intersection (regardless of slice thickness!)
all: all points whose coordinate is inside the thick slice will be shown, full size
linear: all points whose coordinate is inside the thick slice will be shown, rescaled by their distance from the center
sperical thick (name TBD): all points whose spherical extent intersects the thick slice will be shown, rescaled to the largest diameter of the intersecting spherical cap
@hanjinliu @pnewstein how does this look like to you? I assume spherical covers your use case. Do you think spherical thick would also be useful? And do you have any suggestion about naming?
