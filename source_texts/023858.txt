Abstract
A layered rendering method for generating computer-generated hologram (CGH) of 3D objects based on view volume transformation is presented. The shape distortion and the size deviation of the 3D reconstructed image existing in conventional layer-based methods are avoided, and the reconstructed 3D image can preserve the real size of the original model with high quality. Real-time interaction with the digitally reconstructed holographic image with clear depth cues is experimentally demonstrated.

Introduction
Since holograms can record all the information of the 3D object, holographic display shows unique advantages and great flexibility in 3D display, which can provide complete optical wave fields of 3D objects as well as clearly depth cues [1]. Traditional optical holographic recording requires a strict recording environment and a complex process. Computer-generated hologram (CGH) only requires the mathematical description of the light wave coded in the computer. CGH can not only fully record the amplitude and phase of the actual light wave, but also can synthesize the wave front of objects that do not exist in the world.
Main methods for generating CGHs are classified as point-based, polygon-based, image-based and layer-based methods. The point-based method is the traditional methods, which takes the 3D object as the combination of a large number of points. Each point emits a separate spherical wave. These spherical waves are superimposed on the holographic plane to calculate the CGH of the 3D object at a specific distance. Due to the large number of points, the calculation process is time-consuming. Some improved methods were proposed to improve calculation speed, such as look-up-table(LUT) and wavefront recording method [2], [3], [4], [5]. Polygon methods use the polygon as the unit, and the holographic information for each polygon is calculated and then placed into the corresponding position. Due to the reduction of units, the load of computation also decreases [6], [7], [8]. The Image-based method renders projected images of the 3D object from different views. Holograms are loaded on the display system to form 3D effect [9], [10]. As the calculation process can be accelerated by the Fourier algorithm, the calculation speed is fast.
The layer-based method divides the projection images of 3D object into multiple layers according to the depth cue. Therefore, all layers are the same size. Since fast Fourier Transform can be used in the calculation of holograms [11], [12], the computation speed can be accelerated. However, the sampling pitch in the Fresnel diffraction calculation is proportional to the reconstruction distance, which results in inconsistent sizes for different reconstructed layers. Distortion and the size deviation occur for the reconstructed 3D image. Some improved methods were proposed, such as angular spectral methods, Band-limited Double-step Fresnel diffraction (DSF) and improved Fresnel-transform methods. Angular spectral methods keeps the sampling pitch constant. There is overlap between reconstructed images since the physical size of the reconstructed image is smaller than the physical size of the reconstructed object. In order to improve this problem, it needs to expand the source and destination planes to be at least four times as large as the original ones. It makes the computational load increase significantly. The total computing time is one two-dimensional FFT together with one two-dimensional IFFT. The complexity is proportional to. 4N2log22N, where N is the number of sampling points on the source plane [13], [14]. Band-limited Double-step Fresnel diffraction (DSF) calculates the hologram faster than the angular spectral method. Besides, it improves the problem of change in sampling interval. The light propagation between the source plane and the destination plane is calculated by two SSFs, via a virtual plane. The first SSF calculates the light propagation between the source plane and the virtual plane at z1. The second SSF calculates the light propagation between the virtual plane and the destination plane at z2. The total propagation distance is z=z1+z2. Unlike the angular spectrum method, there is no zero-padding during the calculation process. The time complexity is T=TSSFz1+TSSFz2, where TSSF. is N2log2N

It can generate a reconstructed image having the same pixel size by a hologram recorded with same wavelengths at different reconstruction distances or a hologram recorded with different wavelengths at the same distance. Zero-padding is required during the calculation. Although this method has a relatively small computational load compared to the angular spectral method, zero-padding undoubtedly increases the computation time of the hologram [16].
Here, a layered real-time CGH rendering method based on view volume transformation is presented, which can provide the reconstructed image that matches the real size of the 3D object. The layered rendering technology is used to divide the 3D object into layers [17]. Calculation process takes full advantage of GPU parallelism and the Fast Fourier Transform library (cuFFT) [18]. Experimental results show that the proposed method can provide the real size optical reconstruction of 3D object with high quality in real time (see Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3).

Experimental results show that the reconstructed image realize a real-size reconstruction of the 3D object with clear depth cues. An optimized method of dual spatial light modulators holographic display is proposed in accordance with wavefront frequency decomposition. The wavefront frequency decomposition is conducted on the object light wave to generate two frequency images. The low frequency image is determined by amplitude computer-generated hologram (CGH) and then loaded on an amplitude spatial light modulator (SLM). The high frequency image is decided by phase CGH and loaded on a phase SLM. The mentioned two sub-holograms are reconstructed at the identical location and integrated superposed as the final result, so the pixel-to-pixel alignment between two spatial light modulators is not required to be considered. Moreover, the numerical analysis of image evaluation functions demonstrates that the proposed method is valid. As opposed to the conventional methods, the amplitude and phase information are enhanced. The quality of reconstructed images is optimized effectively (the peak signal-to-noise ratio by 3%, the image structure similarity by 3%, the speckle contrast by 30%, and the Michelson contrast by 15%). Optical experimental results show that the reconstruction details are more vivid.
