Using Sparse Voxel Octrees in a Level-of-Detail Pipeline for Rio 2
Sean Palmer
Eric Maurer
Blue Sky Studios
Rio 2 challenged Blue Sky Studios to populate, animate, and raytrace dynamic shots containing thousands of intricate characters
with reasonable memory use and render times. Complex bird rigs
with multiple variations of models, feathers, and materials were
animated by different departments and tool sets to create elaborate
scenes including a choreographed flying dance and a macaw
soccer match. This led us to develop a unified level-of-detail
(LOD) pipeline using our in-house sparse-voxel-octree (SVO)
geometry cache to support all assets through multiple work flows
with consistent and efficient throughput to lighting and rendering.
Sharing Technology for Asset and Shot Setup
Our production assets can become extremely complex and often
contain nested geometry hierarchies, thousands of rig joints, and
procedurally generated geometry, fur, and materials. We updated
our asset pipeline to generate consistent LOD representations for
all types and variations of characters, props, and vegetation. This
included a light proxy format for visualization, a simplified binary
file of the geometry, a partial voxel representation for fine detail
(e.g. the leaves of a tree), and a fully voxelized SVO. An SVO is
made by sampling geometry into a stored octree and contains
positions, normals, UVs, and other surface data such as local
offsets. Material procedures are baked into channels for color,
specular, roughness, etc. along with control signals and alternate
material descriptions for variations. It is analogous to SVO data
structures used in various real-time ray-tracing applications.
The Set Dressing, Animation, Crowds, and Fur departments at
Blue Sky all have unique tools and processes for populating and
animating complex shots. To support these divergent work flows,
we consolidated common processes into shared library functions
that could load assets, share them across procedural systems, build
all available LODs, and choose a level to use for an instance
based on screen size or artist choice. The system outputs an object
description used by our in-house render tool, the modular
generator, to place, deform, and voxelize assets into a single
render object. Loading all assets from a shared process with
consistent input to our renderer significantly reduced processing
times and memory use through added render-time instancing, and
ensured consistent render results regardless of the department
authoring the work. Test shots with 40 assets saved 74% in load
time (339s to 89s) and 56% in RAM (4253 MB to 1189 MB). The
benefit scaled with scene size: rendering 400 assets saved 89% in
load (3451s to 374s) and 82% in RAM (15262 MB to 2816 MB).

To further optimize the asset publish process, we only generated a
single, static SVO of each asset. At render time, the SVO's stored
surface and UV data were used to look up corresponding positions
of posed geometry cycles in order to animate each sample. Once
the posed SVO was sampled into a voxel grid, the geometry could
be purged from memory. Animating SVOs of birds by evaluating
their complex rigs would have required emulating a variety of
deformers for thousands of joints. For speed and ease of use, we
consolidated a range of deformations into blend shapes that were
procedurally selected and triggered. Deforming the bird SVOs
exposed several issues. Overlapping feathers in the static SVO did
not separate cleanly once animated. This was improved by
regenerating SVOs with high resolution and optimal posing,
splaying feathers and other areas apart as much as possible to
increase fidelity. Wings that were colored uniquely on each side
also rendered incorrectly, as their material required the worldspace positions of the feathers to correctly encode the side of the
wing facing camera. This was resolved by storing local-space
vectors in the SVO that could be transposed by the generator.
Results and Future Work
The complexity of characters and scenes in Rio 2 prompted the
creation of a unified pipeline to generate LOD resolutions for all
assets to support flexible shot design using multiple work flows.
We are continuing to refine this platform, consolidating
department tools for streamlined support and re-organizing scene
data at render time for increased efficiency. This work enabled
richer asset detail, movement and integration in more elaborately
designed shots throughout Rio 2, blurring the lines between hand
animation, procedural scene dressing, and crowd simulation.
