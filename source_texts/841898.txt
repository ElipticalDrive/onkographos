Part of #212.
ParticleSystem3D integrates constant velocity — a particle spawns with a direction and speed and travels in a straight line until it expires. There is no force model of any kind, which rules out most of what particles are usually for:
Fire needs buoyancy: flame accelerates upward as it rises, and slows and spreads as it cools.
Smoke needs drag and a wind bias, or it flies off at spawn speed forever.
Sparks and embers need gravity to arc back down.
Everything organic needs some turbulence, or the plume is visibly a cone of straight lines.
Proposed change
Add to ParticleEmitter3D:
Acceleration (a Vector3) applied every step — gravity for sparks, +Y buoyancy for flame, a horizontal bias for wind. One vector covers all three cases.
Drag — velocity damping per second, so particles settle toward terminal velocity instead of coasting.
Turbulence — a small amount of coherent noise displacement or acceleration, scaled by a frequency. Value noise sampled on position and time is enough; the goal is to break up straight-line motion, not to simulate fluid.
Defaults of zero everywhere, so existing emitters keep constant-velocity motion exactly.
Notes
Integration order matters for reproducibility — pick semi-implicit Euler and document it, since a seeded run must stay deterministic across framerates as far as the fixed step allows. Worth checking whether the pool's per-particle state needs to grow (turbulence phase, for instance) and what that costs, given ParticlePool3D's fixed-size no-allocation contract.
The noise function should be a pure static in Yaeger.Core alongside the rest of the simulation, unit-tested for range and continuity, rather than an opaque helper inside the system.
Acceptance criteria
A flame emitter with upward acceleration and drag produces a plume that rises, spreads, and slows rather than a straight cone
A spark emitter with downward acceleration arcs
Turbulence visibly perturbs paths without particles jumping discontinuously between frames
A seeded run is reproducible; a zero-force emitter behaves exactly as today
No per-frame allocation is introduced into the simulation step
