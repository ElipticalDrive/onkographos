First of three render-core PRs for the 3-D cottage sprint (F2). Small and early, so the language changes landing in render can rebase over it.
What changes
One lighting convention for both materials, written in a new Slang module renderframe.slang that lit.slang and pbr.slang both import: the light's colour is irradiance (lux in physical units), ambient, sky and emissive colours are radiance (nits). Blinn-Phong's diffuse and highlight are now divided by pi as the physically based material's are; the browser's PHYSICAL_GAIN hack is no longer needed.
Emissive: Material.emissive, PbrMaterial.emissive and emissiveStrength (glTF's KHR_materials_emissive_strength), and glowing(color, strength) on both.
Pre-exposure: Renderer.exposure (default 1, so scenes in display units are unchanged) multiplies everything drawn, the sky and the clear colour included. exposureFromEv100(ev) = 1 / (1.2 x 2^EV100) (Lagarde and de Rousiers 2014).
Callers drawing with Blinn-Phong alone multiply their light by pi and look the same (spin, pipevk, vfx draw test, offscreen test). The starship's glowing balls are emissive. pipepbr and sdf's horn put a Blinn-Phong twin under the same light as a physical surface, so the twin is now pi dimmer: the comparison is like for like.
The AgX coefficients were checked: the polynomial and EV range against three.js r160 and Filament, and the matrices are inverses of each other.

The Blinn-Phong material and the physically based one lit a surface
differently: the physical one takes the light's colour as an irradiance
and divides its diffuse light by pi, the Blinn-Phong one did not, so a
program mixing them (the browser) multiplied its physical colours by pi.
Now every material keeps one convention, written down in a new Slang
module both shaders import, renderframe.slang: the light's colour is an
irradiance (lux in physical units), the ambient light, the sky and an
emissive colour are radiance (nits), so a white diffuse surface facing a
light of pi is 1 bright. Lit's diffuse and highlight are divided by pi;
its ambient term is unchanged.

Both materials gain an emissive colour, unlit and added after the
lighting: Material.emissive, and PbrMaterial.emissive with
emissiveStrength (glTF's KHR_materials_emissive_strength); 'glowing' sets
either. The draw's tint multiplies it as it does the base colour.

The renderer gains 'exposure', which everything it draws (materials, the
sky, the cleared background) is multiplied by as it is written, so a scene
in physical units keeps the half-float frame near 1 (pre-exposure, as
Frostbite does). exposureFromEv100 gives a camera's exposure at an EV100,
1 / (1.2 x 2^EV100), checked against Lagarde and de Rousiers 2014 as
Unity's PhysicalCamera.hlsl and colour-hdri carry it. The default, 1,
changes nothing for scenes in display units.

The programs drawing with Blinn-Phong alone (frame's spin, render's
pipevk, vfx's draw test, render's offscreen test) multiply their light by
pi and look as they did; the offscreen test gains a case for the emissive
colour and the exposure (80 checks). The starship's glowing balls are
black and emissive rather than lit with huge colours. pipepbr and sdf's
horn, which set a Blinn-Phong twin beside a physical surface under one
light, now light the twin by the same convention, so it is pi dimmer
than before: the comparison they make is now like for like.

The AgX tone mapper's coefficients were checked: the polynomial and the
EV range against three.js r160 and Filament (both citing Wrensch's
minimal AgX), and the inset and outset matrices are each other's inverse
to 1e-16.
