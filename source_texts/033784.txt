Abstract
Recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods have enabled effective
multi-view 3D reconstruction by predicting Gaussian primitives in a single forward
pass. However, existing approaches predominantly adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm
that predicts one Gaussian per input pixel, coupling the Gaussian count to the
input resolution and number of views. This also leads to redundant primitives in
textureless regions and insufficient coverage in geometrically complex areas. We
present Anchor3DGS, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that replaces
pixel-aligned prediction with a compact anchor-based representation. Guided
by information entropy, our method places a set of 3D anchors in the scene,
concentrating them in regions of higher visual complexity while maintaining broad
spatial coverage. Each anchor performs occlusion-aware aggregation of multiview image features, exchanges information with other anchors, and is decoded
into a set of local Gaussians. A lightweight feature enhancement module further
refines the coarse RGB output from Gaussian splatting and improves rendering
fidelity. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that
our Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis quality while only
using an order of magnitude fewer Gaussian primitives.

Introduction
Reconstructing 3D scenes and synthesizing novel views from multiple 2D captures is a fundamental
problem in computer vision and graphics, with broad applications in robotics, autonomous driving,
gaming, and virtual reality. The pioneering NeRF [19] represents scenes as an implicit radiance field
optimized via volume rendering, while subsequent 3DGS [13] employs differentiable rasterization of
anisotropic 3D Gaussians to achieve real-time, high-fidelity rendering. Despite its success, standard
3DGS requires a time-consuming per-scene optimization process. To address this limitation, a
growing line of work has explored feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction [4, 6, 34, 7, 32], which trains
generalizable models on large-scale datasets to directly predict 3D Gaussians from input views in
a single forward pass. Recent advances have further eliminated the dependency on pre-computed
camera poses [38, 11, 9], enabling truly end-to-end reconstruction from uncalibrated images.
However, current feed-forward approaches adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm in which one Gaussian
is predicted for each input pixel. While conceptually straightforward, this design suffers from
three key limitations: (1) Redundancy—textureless or homogeneous regions consume as many
Gaussians as detail-rich areas, yielding redundant primitives and wasted capacity; (2) Insufficient

In this work, we present Anchor3DGS, a feed-forward framework that takes uncalibrated multi-view
images as input and outputs 3D Gaussians of the scene (see Fig. 1). Our key insight is that a scene’s
3D structure has no intrinsic connection to the number of input views or pixels that happen to observe
it. We therefore propose to lift Gaussian allocation into 3D space: we represent the scene with a set
of anchors whose spatial distribution is guided by scene content, while the total number of anchors
is a hyperparameter, independent of image resolution and view count.
Our framework consists of several key components: (i) Entropy-guided anchor sampling. Our 3D
anchor sampling strategy simultaneously ensures broad spatial coverage of the scene and adaptively
allocates more anchors to regions exhibiting higher visual complexity, as identified by local information entropy. Entropy automatically guides 3D anchors to where they are needed most: few anchors
suffice for low-complexity regions (e.g., white walls or sky), while many anchors concentrate on
high-complexity regions (e.g., intricate structures or rich textures). (ii) Multi-view anchor aggregation
and local decoding. Each 3D anchor aggregates visual features from all input views under occlusionaware weighting, and is decoded into a small set of neighboring Gaussians that represent its local
3D region. Anchors also exchange information with each other, allowing those in poorly observed
regions to leverage the context from well-observed neighbors and still produce plausible appearances.
In contrast, pixel-aligned methods often leave visible holes in such regions. (iii) Feature enhancement.
Beyond standard attributes, each Gaussian is also assigned a feature vector. These features are later
splatted and decoded by a lightweight network, which compensates for the high-frequency details
missing from the coarse splatted RGB image.
Extensive experiments across benchmark datasets validate the effectiveness of our method. In sparseview settings (3 and 6 input views), Anchor3DGS significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art
baselines. In dense-view settings (32 input views), Anchor3DGS achieves comparable rendering
quality while requiring an order of magnitude fewer Gaussians. This demonstrates that a compact
anchor-based representation can match the fidelity of pixel-aligned approaches with greater efficiency.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
• We propose Anchor3DGS, a novel anchor-based feed-forward 3DGS framework that
replaces the pixel-aligned paradigm with a compact anchor-based representation, decoupling
the Gaussian count from input image resolution and the number of input views.
• We introduce three key designs—entropy-guided anchor sampling, occlusion-aware anchor
aggregation and local decoding, and feature enhancement—which together enable highfidelity novel view synthesis with fine details.
• We achieve state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmark datasets with substantially
fewer Gaussians than existing methods, demonstrating that our method yields a more
compact yet equally effective representation.

Related Work
Per-Scene Novel View Synthesis. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [19] pioneered novel view
synthesis by representing scenes as continuous density and radiance fields, and optimizing them
via differentiable volume rendering. Despite subsequent improvements [27, 17, 2, 20, 41, 12], the
expensive ray sampling in volume rendering still prevents real-time performance. 3D Gaussian
Splatting (3DGS) [13, 33] instead represents scenes as explicit anisotropic 3D Gaussians and exploits
differentiable tile-based rasterization, achieving real-time speed with superior quality. However, both
NeRF- and 3DGS-based methods require accurate camera poses, typically from a costly Structurefrom-Motion (SfM) pipeline such as COLMAP [24]. To remove this dependency, several works [3,
10, 14, 8] attempt to jointly optimize the camera poses and the scene representation. Nevertheless, all
these methods still rely on tedious per-scene optimization. In contrast, our Anchor3DGS directly
predicts camera poses and 3D Gaussians in a feed-forward pass within seconds.
Feed-Forward Novel View Synthesis. Feed-forward novel view synthesis avoids per-scene optimization by learning generalizable priors from large-scale datasets. Early works [40, 29, 5] condition
the NeRF representation on image features aggregated from input views, enabling cross-scene generalization without per-scene retraining. Following the success of 3DGS, subsequent feed-forward
works [26, 4, 6, 7, 34, 32, 31, 42] replace NeRF with explicit Gaussian primitives for higher rendering
quality and faster speed. As representative examples, pixelSplat [4] employs epipolar attention to
infer a dense depth probability distribution for predicting 3D Gaussians from image pairs, whereas
MVSplat [6] incorporates multi-view stereo principles by constructing explicit cost volumes for more
accurate geometric reconstruction. A recent line of pose-free feed-forward methods [38, 44, 37, 11]
further eliminates the reliance on known camera parameters. By leveraging robust geometric priors
from 3D foundation models [30, 28], FLARE [44] and AnySplat [11] can simultaneously infer the
camera poses of input images and the matching geometry-aligned 3D Gaussians. Despite these
advances, all aforementioned feed-forward 3DGS methods typically predict Gaussians in a dense,
pixel-aligned manner, resulting in massive redundant Gaussians. Although AnySplat [11] applies
post-hoc voxelization for Gaussian reduction, this fails to address the root cause, and the remaining
Gaussians are still far from compact. Instead, we decouple Gaussian allocation from the pixel-aligned
paradigm and model the scene using a compact set of 3D anchors. A few concurrent works [1, 22] also
attempt to explore compact representations using learnable tokens, whereas we explicitly leverage
3D scene structure and visual complexity to guide Gaussian allocation.
3
Method
The key idea of our method is to represent the scene with a compact set of 3D anchors, rather than
being strictly bound to the input image pixels. Each anchor represents a local 3D region: it is placed
on a geometry-aware scene scaffold, gathers information from multiple views, exchanges information
with other anchors, and is finally decoded into a set of local Gaussians. In this way, the scene
representation is organized directly in 3D space, with its size controlled by the anchor budget.
Concretely, given a set of uncalibrated input images I = {Iv }Vv=1 , we first build a multi-view
geometric scaffold using a VGGT-based pose-free geometry backbone [28], which predicts camera
parameters C = {Cv }Vv=1 and per-view depth maps D = {Dv }Vv=1 . By unprojecting the predicted
depth maps with the predicted cameras, we obtain scene points P in 3D space, from which we
sample a fixed number of anchors guided by local image entropy. Each anchor is then encoded into a
latent token zn through occlusion-aware multi-view feature aggregation and cross-anchor interaction.
Finally, each token is decoded into K local Gaussian primitives, yielding the final Gaussian set

representation is built as a fixed-size set of 3D anchors with features in scene space.

Training
We train Anchor3DGS on five public multi-view datasets: DL3DV [16], ScanNet++ [39], Hypersim [23], BlendedMVS [36], and Waymo [18]. These datasets cover synthetic and real-world
captures, indoor and outdoor scenes, and object-scale to street-scale settings, which enhances the
model’s generalization ability to unseen scenarios. For each training scene, we sample a set of context
views as input. For datasets with sequentially ordered captures (e.g., DL3DV [16]), we adopt an
index-based sampler that selects two boundary frames at a chosen index gap, and samples additional
context views from intermediate frame indices between them. For datasets without a sequential
ordering (e.g., ScanNet++ [39]), we instead use a ranking-based sampler that ranks all views by the
camera-pose distance (rotation angle and translation) relative to a randomly chosen reference, and
then selects context views from this distance-sorted list. In both cases, the extent covered by the
selected context views (an index gap or a rank window) is randomized across iterations, producing a
mixture of small- and wide-baseline configurations during training.
Our model is trained end-to-end by minimizing a combination of photometric and geometric losses.

5.1
Experimental Setup
(10)
Evaluation Datasets & Baselines. Following AnySplat [11], we evaluate the novel view synthesis
quality on VR-NeRF [35] and MipNeRF360 [2] datasets. We also test on a held-out subset of
DL3DV [16] that is disjoint from training. Together, these benchmarks jointly cover object-centric,
indoor, and outdoor scenes, providing a comprehensive evaluation.
We compare Anchor3DGS against recent pose-free feed-forward 3DGS methods: NoPoSplat [38],
FLARE [44], Depth Anything 3 (DA3) [15], and AnySplat [11]. Among them, NoPoSplat and
FLARE are tailored for sparse-view inputs and do not scale to dense-view inputs, so we only include
them in the sparse-view comparison (3 and 6 views). In the dense-view setting (32 views), we also
report VGGT+3DGS as a per-scene optimization reference. We compute PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS
between the rendered images and the ground-truth images. To further reflect the compactness of our
representation, we also report the number of Gaussian primitives (#GS) for each scene.
Implementation Details. We set the anchor budget to N = 32,768 and decode K = 8 Gaussians
per anchor, yielding roughly 0.26M Gaussians per scene independent of input resolution and view
count. The dimension of each anchor embedding zn is 128, and the dimension of per-Gaussian feature
vector h (used for rendering refinement) is 16. We initialize the geometry backbone of Anchor3DGS
with the pretrained VGGT [28] weights and fine-tune it jointly with the rest of the network using a
0.1× smaller learning rate, while keeping the patch-embedding layer frozen. The model is trained on
8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs for 30,000 iterations (about 2–3 days). We use the AdamW optimizer with
a peak learning rate of 2 × 10−4 and 1,000 warmup steps. During training, the number of context
views per scene is randomly sampled between 2 and 16, and the maximum input resolution is set to
448 pixels on the longer side. At inference time, all images are resized to 448 × 448 resolution. For
the training loss weights, we set λmse = 1.0, λlpips = 0.05, and λdepth = 0.1.

million Gaussians with no corresponding quality gain. The VGGT+3DGS also provides a strong
reference but represents a different paradigm—it requires minutes of optimization per scene, whereas
Anchor3DGS directly predicts the 3DGS representation from the input views in a feed-forward pass
within seconds. Despite this efficiency gap, our method still achieves on-par rendering quality.

5.4
Ablation Study
We conduct the ablation study to validate each key design of Anchor3DGS. Tab. 3 reports results on
VR-NeRF [35] under both sparse-view (3 views) and dense-view (32 views) settings. Removing any
component causes a quality drop, confirming that all four designs contribute to the final performance.
(i) w/o enhancement removes the enhancement UNet for refinement, which leads to consistent
degradation as the splatted RGB alone struggles to recover fine high-frequency details. (ii) w/o
self-attention disables cross-anchor interaction so each anchor is encoded independently, and (iii)
w/o occ-aggregation replaces occlusion-aware multi-view aggregation with plain mean pooling.
Both of them impair the encoded anchor features and thus degrade rendering performance. (iv) w/o
entropy-sample replaces entropy-guided sampling with uniform grid subsampling, which causes
noticeable PSNR drop (−1.53 dB at 3 views). This indicates that our strategy allocates the limited
anchor budget more reasonably. We further examine how performance varies with anchor count
N and decoded Gaussians per anchor K in Fig. 5. The results confirm that our default setting
(N = 32,768 and K=8) effectively balances rendering quality and Gaussian compactness.
6
Conclusion
We present Anchor3DGS, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that replaces the prevailing
pixel-aligned paradigm with a compact anchor-based paradigm. By placing a fixed number of 3D
anchors on a geometry-aware scaffold, our method decouples the Gaussian count from the input
resolution and view number, and instead allocates representation capacity according to scene content.
Three key designs—entropy-guided anchor sampling, occlusion-aware multi-view aggregation, and
feature-based rendering refinement—together enable high-fidelity novel view synthesis. Extensive
experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate that Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art ren9
dering quality in sparse-view settings. In dense-view settings, Anchor3DGS attains comparable
quality while using an order of magnitude fewer Gaussians and lower storage cost, validating the
effectiveness and efficiency of our compact anchor-based representation. Beyond view synthesis,
such a compact anchor-based representation naturally serves as a global geometric memory [46],
offering a promising substrate for future video world models.
Despite the advancements, Anchor3DGS still has limitations. The performance of our method relies
on the prediction quality of the geometry backbone. When the backbone produces inaccurate poses
or noisy depth maps, the resulting anchor placement may degrade and harm the final rendering. In
addition, since the anchor budget is fixed regardless of scene scale, very large or unbounded scenes
may benefit from a scene-adaptive budget, which we leave as future work.

Appendix
A
More Implementation Details
Model Architecture. Our geometry backbone is a VGGT-based [28] model with a ViT architecture.
The backbone takes V uncalibrated images as input and produces multi-scale tokens, from which
we take the intermediate features at layers {4, 11, 17, 23} and fuse them through a DPT-style head
into per-view feature maps Fv ∈ R128×H×W used for anchor feature aggregation. For anchor
encoding, we first apply Fourier positional encoding with L=6 frequencies (yielding 3 + 6×3×2=39
dimensions) on the anchor coordinates and concatenate log dn as an additional density signal. After
multi-view aggregation, each anchor feature is refined by 4 self-attention blocks, where each block
uses RMSNorm pre-normalization, multi-head self-attention with head_dim=128 (4 heads), and an
MLP layer with expansion ratio 4. The feature enhancement module is a 3-level UNet with channel
widths {64, 128, 256} from shallow to deep. Downsampling uses 3×3 convolutions with stride 2,
upsampling uses 4×4 transposed convolutions followed by a 3×3 convolution, and skip connections
concatenate encoder and decoder features at each level. A final 1 × 1 convolution produces the
3-channel RGB residual ∆I. The weights and biases of the final convolution are zero-initialized, so
the network behaves as an identity mapping at the start of training.
Datasets. We train on five public multi-view datasets covering diverse scene types: DL3DV [16]
(real-world indoor and outdoor scenes), ScanNet++ [39] (real-world indoor scenes), Hypersim [23]
(synthetic indoor scenes), BlendedMVS [36] (object-centric and aerial scenes), and Waymo [18]
(outdoor driving scenes). These datasets are mixed with sampling probabilities 0.4, 0.2, 0.2, 0.1, 0.1
respectively. All training images are resized so that the longer side equals 448 pixels, with intrinsics
rescaled accordingly. For evaluation, following AnySplat [11], we report results on three benchmarks:
VR-NeRF [35] (4 scenes), MipNeRF360 [2] (4 scenes), and DL3DV [16] (10 held-out scenes).
Testing Protocol. Because our task takes uncalibrated images as input, evaluation must rely on
camera poses predicted by the model itself. We follow the testing protocol of AnySplat [11]. Given a
test scene with context views and held-out target views, we run the model twice: (i) On context views
alone, we obtain the predicted context poses C (1) and the scene’s 3D Gaussians G. (ii) On the union
of context and target views, we obtain a second set of poses C (2) that includes the target views. Since
the two passes can produce slightly different context poses, we apply a Procrustes alignment between
the context poses in C (1) and C (2) , and use the resulting transform to map the target poses in C (2)
into the coordinate frame of G. We then render G at the aligned target poses and compute metrics
against ground-truth images. For NoPoSplat [38], which does not predict input camera poses, we
follow the original protocol and recover target camera poses via a 200-step test-time optimization
that minimizes the MSE between the rendered image and the ground-truth image.

The results isolate two effects. (i) The backbone contribution — comparing our backbone + pixel
decoder against AnySplat [11] — is small: +0.28 / +0.03 / +0.19 dB on VR-NeRF [35] dataset and
+0.15 / +0.39 / +0.26 dB on DL3DV [16] dataset at 3 / 6 / 32 views, confirming that fine-tuning
the backbone alone does not explain our rendering improvement. (ii) The anchor contribution —
comparing Anchor3DGS against our backbone + pixel decoder — is substantial: +2.10 dB (VRNeRF 3 views) and +3.33 dB (DL3DV 3 views), narrowing to +0.32 / +0.50 dB at 32 views. The
performance gain therefore primarily comes from the anchor-based representation rather than the finetuned backbone. Unlike pixel-aligned decoders that predict one Gaussian per pixel and leave holes in
poorly-observed regions, our anchors aggregate multi-view context through attention mechanism and
concentrate capacity in high-entropy regions via entropy-guided sampling.
C
Broader Social Impacts
Anchor3DGS is a general-purpose feed-forward 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis method,
and we expect its social impact to be generally positive. By predicting a compact set of 3D Gaussians
from uncalibrated images in a single forward pass, our method substantially reduces the per-scene
optimization cost of conventional 3DGS pipelines. This efficiency lowers the computational and
memory footprint of 3D content creation, and also makes high-quality 3D reconstruction more
accessible to downstream applications such as virtual and augmented reality, robotics, autonomous
driving simulation, and immersive telepresence. On the other hand, like other photorealistic novel
view synthesis techniques, our method could be misused. Realistic renderings of reconstructed scenes
might be repurposed to fabricate misleading visual content, and unconstrained reconstruction from
publicly available imagery raises potential privacy concerns regarding individuals or private spaces
captured in the input views. These risks can be mitigated through standard practices, including
responsible data curation, watermarking or provenance tracking of synthesized content, and obtaining
proper consent before reconstructing scenes that contain private property.
