Before: eleven cubes were drawn as GPU instances from a hand-written position list.
After: the same arrangement lives in a 16x16x16 Chunk of blocks and renders as one prebuilt chunk mesh. Faces between touching blocks aren't generated, so the scene looks identical but draws fewer triangles.
How:
chunk.rs: Chunk owns a boxed, flat array of Block (just Air/Solid until block types arrive in step 6), indexed Y-major, with get/set. Reads outside the chunk count as air.
mesh.rs: build_chunk_mesh walks the chunk and emits a face only when its neighbour is air. It replaces cube.rs and the instancing path. Indices are now u32, because a chunk can exceed 65k vertices.
The renderer uploads one mesh and draws it, and the shader has no per-instance input any more.
Two unit tests cover culling, and CI now runs cargo test.
This is the first slice of the Sodium-style pipeline from your PR #3 comment: prebuilt chunk meshes with hidden faces removed. Batched multi-draw, region buffers, background mesh workers and per-vertex AO will come in as later steps need them. One choice for you: chunks are 16³ cubes, like Minecraft sections. Step 5 can stack them vertically or make them taller, whichever you prefer. Faces on a chunk's border are always emitted for now, and culling across chunk borders comes once step 5 has neighbouring chunks.

16³ cubes is the right structure, because that's better for frustrum culling. Without signing us up to build all of this right now, here is what I expect the terrain renderer to evolve into, when each piece is needed.
Stage
Implementation
Why
1
16³ voxel sections
Independent meshing and culling
2
Hidden-face elimination
Largest immediate geometry reduction
3
Asynchronous mesh generation
Prevents chunk updates blocking rendering
4
Packed vertex formats
Lower VRAM and memory bandwidth
5
Per-vertex AO and directional shading
Improves depth perception
6
Greedy meshing
Reduces large continuous surfaces
7
Frustum culling by section
Avoids unnecessary rendering
8
GPU buffer arenas and batched draws
Reduces allocations and submission overhead
9
Occlusion culling
Rejects hidden sections
10
GPU-driven submission and LOD
Scales to substantially larger view distances
Store that along-side a memory with terrain renderer goals. Another thing I missed is that we will likely need more than one renderer pass, e.g. for translucent terrain materials like liquids or portal or leaves.
This pass has been tested on my machine and works, merging, proceed to Step 5 with multiple chunks in x,y, and z.
