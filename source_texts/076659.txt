Quantitative and Qualitative Evaluation
Regression losses such as L1 and L2 have
achieved considerable success in training implicit
neural representations (INRs) for video, image,
and audio data. By contrast, few studies have
explored formulating INR training as a
classification task. In this work, we show that a
classification-based loss can match the
performance of traditional regression losses when
applied to scientific datasets.
PSNR vs Compression Ratio
We compare different solutions and their
performance in terms of PSNR versus
compression ratio. The comparison uses three
datasets of varying complexity. As the
compression ratio increases, both approaches
show reduced reconstruction performance. RT
denotes the Rayleigh–Taylor instability dataset,
Mag refers to a magnetic-reconnection
simulation, and HCCI stands for
homogeneous-charge-compression ignition.
Conclusion and Future Work
The performance of using classification loss to train implicit neural representation for scientific data has competitive
performance with using regression loss to train the same network. The neural network trained for classification has many nice
properties such as interpretability. In future, we would be interested to explore the latent space of INR under this type of
training framework.
