Mars cube foundation: ellipsoid and areoid conversion
Mars cube products previously rejected unequal radii, while the existing Mars pyramid treated planetocentric areoid heights as geodetic ellipsoid heights. This adds an explicit MOLA areoid-radius conversion, an ellipsoidal radial-lattice storage contract, and public CPU samples obtained by intersecting that lattice along the ellipsoid normal. CPU ray intersections stay independent of rendered residency, and ellipsoid horizon culling safely falls back to frustum culling. Ray queries discover incident closed tile/face footprints and hand rejected parent hits to finer owners. Actual ancestor outer-edge triangle support remains provisional when finer lattices have no intersection; superseded interiors remain rejected. Coordinate-scaled intersection tolerances preserve real edges at planetary coordinate magnitudes.
The bounded 78-tile Jezero product reuses the existing #50 fused field without refitting. Its frame, source conventions, input hashes, rounding bound, shared build revision, and measurements are recorded in docs/design/mars-cube-foundation.md and docs/benchmarks/mars-cube-foundation.json. An unlisted, registered local-only catalog and reusable builder/validator/capture interfaces make it reviewable without a production switch or upload. The experiment and its unpublished product are excluded from local-data and hosted deployment bundles.
Validation:
Build, test typecheck and lint passed. The physical-surface, cube-grid and Mars suites pass 28 tests, including offline regressions that fail the previous implementation. The local full run passed 2,238 tests with one existing CSPICE intermediate-progress callback failure; full GitHub CI passed on 2e4a0ad, with 2,440 tests, both terrain fixture suites and browser smoke green.
Independent synthetic 2:1 flattened, negative-height Cartesian fixtures passed; a new CI job runs the offline builder fixtures.
Existing lunar builder selftest and existing full lunar cube product validator passed.
All 96 targeted Mars boundary rays now hit. All 10 recorded former gaps are passing hit assertions independently checked against emitted surface-only triangles. Synthetic tests cover ancestor handoff, fine-hit priority, cube edges/corners, poles, antimeridian, missing coverage, and a 1 mm outside-triangle rejection.
All 12,480 tested Mars mixed-level edge samples have skirt coverage. 120 regular CPU picks agree with emitted float32 triangles within the precision budget (maximum ray discrepancy 0.131 m).
73 source-coordinate scalar controls retain maximum field discrepancy 0.0182 m; bounded triangles differ from the finest field by at most 2.92 m at those controls and 8.74 m at sampled blend points.
Two final-revision viewer captures settled without console errors in software WebGL. Near-surface rendered/CPU picks agree; orbital coarse rendered LOD differs from the finer CPU lattice and is recorded.
Limits: CPU grids exclude rendered skirts. Missing/unloaded coverage remains unavailable; finite query budgets, partial-product metadata and unknown accuracy prevent a certified miss or nearest-hit claim. Closed boundary support can report a coarser actual tile than half-open point coverage. Source/datum uncertainty remains unknown. The legacy flight builder's geodetic-coordinate assumption needs a separate provenance audit before the #244 production migrations. No full global build, upload, or mission trajectory switch was performed. Rendered-vs-CPU placement/clearance policy and mission/image coordinate audits remain #246 acceptance checks.
Refs #245; parent #244 remains separate.

Reviewed head c142dd1 against #245 and the #244 migration sequence. The core approach looks sound: the areoid-to-radius conversion is explicit, radial storage is separated from public ellipsoid-normal heights, CPU intersections use the emitted Cartesian lattice, the synthetic flattened/negative-height fixtures are meaningful, and this appropriately avoids switching production catalogs.
One fix before merge:
CI / managed dataset ownership (blocking). The new experiments/mars-terrain-cube-jezero.json references ../data/mars-terrain-cube-jezero/{tileset-measured.json,cpu-grids.json}, but that directory isn't registered in scripts/data-hosting/datasets.json. The full CI run failed in scripts/data-hosting/__tests__/data-hosting.test.mjs for exactly these two unmanaged paths (2325 tests passed, 1 failed; the new terrain-fixture job passed). Please register or otherwise correctly scope this unlisted/local experiment so the dataset ownership test passes without inadvertently requiring a not-yet-published product in hosted deployments. Rerun full CI.
Important follow-ups / acceptance checks for #246 (can stay separate from this bounded foundation):
Mixed-LOD CPU query gaps. The recorded benchmark has 10 unavailable/partial-coverage results among 96 deliberately targeted boundary queries, even though the rendered skirts cover the sampled seams. Keeping these unavailable rather than inventing a hit is right, but point elevation, pick, and navigation operations can still fail at boundaries; coverage() === 'finest' does not guarantee an intersection. Please capture those reproducible cases as a deterministic regression and track boundary/neighbor ownership improvements before production migration.
Rendered/CPU divergence during LOD changes. The boundary evidence reports up to ~291 m difference between a coarse rendered pair and the finer CPU geometry; the overview capture also documents a large offset. This is not necessarily a datum-conversion bug, but it matters for fly-to-point, clearance, and surface measurements while tiles refine. Before switching Ingenuity, explicitly test user-facing operations through loading/refinement and decide which surface governs placement/clearance (rather than checking only fully settled scenes).
The documented caveat about legacy flight coordinates is appropriate: auditing planetocentric vs planetographic mission placement belongs in #246/#244 and must not be skipped. Once CI is green and the two migration risks are explicitly tracked, this looks reasonable to merge as the #245 foundation, not as acceptance of a production Mars rollout.
