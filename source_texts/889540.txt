Ambient lighting is currently a constant plus image based lighting from a static environment, so indirect light does not respond to the scene at all: a red wall does not tint the floor, and a room with the door shut is as bright as one with it open.
RTXGI 2.0 offers two techniques worth evaluating separately rather than adopting as a bundle:
SHARC, a spatial hash radiance cache. World space hash cells accumulate radiance from rays, which are then reused across frames and across pixels. Comparatively cheap, resolution independent, and forgiving about geometry, which suits an open scene with streaming content.
NRC, a neural radiance cache that trains a small network online to predict radiance, which excels at multi-bounce and complex light transport but pulls in a tensor runtime dependency and hardware requirements that need thinking about.
Also worth weighing against both: probe based DDGI, which the earlier RTXGI generation used, is simpler to reason about and integrates with a light probe system that would be useful in the engine regardless.
Whichever technique wins, the engine-side work is the same shape: an indirect diffuse term computed per frame and consumed by the lighting pass in place of the constant ambient, a way to invalidate cached lighting when geometry or lights change, and a quality tier that scales ray counts and cache resolution.
Depends on the ray tracing foundation. Needs a non-RT fallback path, which today is the existing ambient plus SSAO, and that fallback must remain the default on backends without ray tracing.
Done: a room lit only through a doorway shows correct light falloff and color bleeding from nearby surfaces, updates within a few frames when a light moves, and costs a bounded, measured amount of frame time.
