The use of Gaussian Splats in computer graphics presents significant limitations regarding the dynamic management of the appearance and lighting of represented objects. In particular, the inability to dynamically modify parameters such as lighting and shading limits the independent use of a model, as it is strongly tied to the scene and environmental conditions under which it was trained. As a result, a Gaussian Splatting model cannot easily be detached from its original scene and reused in different environments with dynamic lighting. To address this limitation, we propose an approach based on the use of conventional three-dimensional models, represented as polygonal meshes, as a source of training data for Gaussian Splats. During the generation of the training data, the models are rendered without baked-in shading, allowing independent information about the object’s surface to be preserved. In addition to the base color (albedo), surface normal vectors (normals) and information regarding the material properties of the surface are also stored. Preserving these attributes enables lighting to be recalculated during rendering using any lighting model selected by the developer. In our implementation, the Cook-Torrance model is used to calculate the final appearance of the surface. In this way, the proposed technique enables independent and dynamic shading of Gaussian Splats, reducing their dependency on the conditions of the original scene. At the same time, it introduces the possibility of a new form of interaction between conventional polygonal models and Gaussian Splats, since both representation types can now be integrated within the same scene and subjected to dynamic lighting in a consistent manner.
