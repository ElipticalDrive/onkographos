METHODOLOGY
Two classes of digital images can be distinguished – analog
and digital images. Both types fall into non-temporal
multimedia type. Analog images are painted or created
through photographic process. During this process, the
image is captured by a camera on a film that becomes a
negative. We have a positive when the film is developed – no
processing is possible from this moment. When the
photography is made on a transparent medium then we are
dealing with a diapositive (slide). Analog images are
characterized by continuous, smooth transition of tones. This
means that between each two different points at the picture
there is an infinite number of tonal values. It is possible to
transform an analog image into digital. The digitization
process is usually caused by a need of digital processing. The
output of digitalization is a digital approximation of the input
analog image – the analog image is replaced by a set of pixels
(points organized in rows and columns) and every pixel has
a fixed, discrete tone value. Therefore, the image is not a
continuous tone of colors. The precision and accuracy of this
transformation depends on the size of a pixel – the larger
area of an analog image transformed into one pixel the less
precise approximation.
A. The key issues in FIC are proper selection of partitioning
