Roadmap change Q (castle-doom3): temporal anti-aliasing, like RBDOOM-3-BFG's TAA.
What it does
TCastleViewport.TemporalAntiAliasing (published, default false):
Jittered projection: ApplyProjection shifts the projection by a sub-pixel offset every frame. The offsets follow the Halton sequence (bases 2 and 3) over 8 frames.
Motion vectors: computed per pixel from the depth buffer plus the previous frame's view and projection (both without the jitter). Each pixel uses the nearest depth in its 3×3 neighborhood (Karis 2014), so pixels just outside a nearer object's edge move with it and leave no trail.
Depth layers: CGE draws the world with glDepthRange(0.1, 1) and the front layer (rlFront) with (0, 0.1). The shader decodes both. The front layer, like an FPS weapon, is assumed to move with the camera.
History: TCastleScreenEffects keeps 2 history textures (RGBA16F when float color buffers are available, otherwise RGBA8) and a framebuffer for them.
Clamping: the history is clamped to the mean ± standard deviation of the current 3×3 neighborhood, in YCoCg, then blended with feedback 0.9. This keeps moving objects and newly revealed areas free of trails.
Where it runs: right after the world, after the HighDynamicRange resolve. The result is copied back over the world with the depth and stencil kept, so the children UI and screen effects render on top and are not blended.
Ignored with multi-sampling, in transparent viewports, and without depth textures.
Tests (TTestCastleViewport)
TestTemporalAntiAliasingEdges: a rotated white box on black.
Edge pixels with intermediate colors go from 0 to ~240 after 24 frames.
The mean difference from the aliased image is 0.002, so the image does not shift.
Also checked with HighDynamicRange and with SSAO.
The projection matrix changes between frames only when TAA is on.
TestTemporalAntiAliasingCameraMotion: the camera pans about 6 px per frame, compared with renders without TAA.
0 pixels differ by more than 0.75.
With identity motion vectors the test fails (132 pixels).
TestTemporalAntiAliasingTransparent: a transparent viewport keeps what is behind it and is not jittered.
The full suite passes on desktop OpenGL (Mesa, Xvfb): all 600 tests, 5 aborted. On OpenGLES (-dOpenGLES), TTestCastleViewport.* passes.
Example
examples/viewport_and_scenes/temporal_anti_aliasing: a fence of thin poles and wires, diagonal bars, a fine floor grid and a spinning box.
T toggles TAA (on in the design).
O toggles the camera circling around the scene.
Without TAA, the wires and the far grid lines break into dashes.
castle-doom3
Console r_taa [0|1], set by name.
In the test map, the edges of walls and the ceiling are smoother, and the mean brightness is the same as without TAA.
The weapon (rlFront) and the HUD stay clean while walking.
Not done
Per-object motion vectors: objects moving by themselves are only clamped, so they get less anti-aliasing.
Sharpening: the bilinear history reads soften the image while the camera moves.
