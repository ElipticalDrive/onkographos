Abstract
Background
Physics-based differentiable rendering (PBDR) aims to propagate gradients from scene parameters to image pixels or in the reverse direction. The obtained physically correct gradients can be used in various applications including inverse rendering and machine learning. Currently, two categories of methods are prevalent in the PBDR community: reparameterization methods and boundary sampling methods. The state-of-the-art boundary sampling methods rely on a guiding structure to calculate the gradients efficiently. They utilize the rays generated in traditional path tracing methods and project them to the object silhouette boundary to initialize the guiding structure.
Methods
In this paper, we propose an augmentation to previous projective-sampling-based boundary sampling methods in a bidirectional manner. Specifically, not only do we utilize the rays spawned from sensors, but we also employ the rays emitted by emitters to initialize the guiding structure.
Results
To showcase the benefits of our technique, we perform a comparative analysis concerning both differentiable rendering and inverse-rendering performance. We utilize a range of synthetic scene examples and evaluate our method against state-of-the-art projective-sampling-based differentiable rendering methods.
Conclusions
The experiments show that our methods achieve lower variance gradients in the forward differentiable rendering process and better geometry reconstruction quality in the inverse-rendering results.
Keywords: Physics-based differentiable rendering, Global illumination, Inverse rendering
