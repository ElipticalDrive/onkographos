Goal
Give surfaces, terrain, and physical scene objects a coherent, lightweight atmospheric illumination model: attenuated direct sunlight plus diffuse sky irradiance. The existing #161 incident-light extinction is necessary but not sufficient. This must remain mission-visualization oriented and fast enough for the WebGL viewer.
Why
#161 (merged) attenuates sunlight through the atmosphere, but an atmosphere also redistributes light through the sky. Direct-only lighting can yield implausibly dark terrain and structures near twilight, inconsistent sky/ground color, and wrong material response. Takram's AtmosphereLight and sky-environment treatment offer a useful reference for the missing integration.
Deliverable / implementation
Audit how Cosmolabe currently lights globe, tiled terrain, DSK/irregular bodies, spacecraft and models: direct solar irradiance, material ambient/emissive values, other lights, ring/moon/eclipse attenuation, and sky/atmosphere contributions. Record current double-counting/omissions before editing.
Prototype a low-cost sky irradiance / directional or hemispherical ambient approximation derived from the existing atmosphere transmittance/multi-scatter/SkyView inputs, without expensive per-fragment scattering integrals. Consider a small directional basis or ambient probe only if measurements support it.
Apply consistently to supported material paths (including terrain/globe and representative physical meshes) without replacing Three.js materials globally. Keep sunlight, other lights, emission and physically unlit materials semantically separate.
Ensure changing Sun elevation, observer position, body and eclipse/ring shadow context updates lighting correctly; atmosphere-disabled or airless bodies must not get invented blue-sky fill.
Handle local directionality (surface up, oblate ellipsoids, tiled normals) and distinguish incoming sky radiance from camera-to-fragment aerial perspective. Do not approximate sky light by adding arbitrary haze to final color.
Define plausible behavior for occultation: no direct solar fill in umbra; any remaining diffuse ambient contribution must be physically qualified/approximately bounded. Do not promise radiometric or observation-instrument accuracy.
Support a predictable visual/physical mode or debug decomposition (direct, sky, total) for regression.
Acceptance
Deterministic Earth surface midday / sunset / night, Mars, Titan and airless Moon fixtures show smooth, explainable direct vs sky contributions.
Terrain and representative spacecraft/models use the same lighting conventions; no double-scattering or bloom/tonemapping double application.
Color/exposure remains stable while moving camera or playing time; shadowed and unshadowed comparisons make sense.
Baseline and after GPU frame time recorded on desktop and a constrained WebGL/mobile configuration; no noticeable multi-body penalty for off-screen/inactive atmospheres.
Existing physical Sun/eclipse/ring tests continue to pass.
Boundaries
Distinct from #134's optical transport correctness: this concerns illumination of real geometry by atmospheric light. #134 remains owner of density profiles, transmittance, sky LUTs and aerial perspective. Coordinate with the HDR/exposure follow-up; do not require WebGPU or full real-time global illumination.
