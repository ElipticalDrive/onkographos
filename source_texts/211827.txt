note: This is part of a larger stack tekton-dynamics#1 to support multi-resolution, sparse SDFs. But since this is a fundamental change, I wanted to put it up first
Motivation
tesseract::geometry::SignedDistanceField lets the contact managers check a robot against an environment described as a distance field rather than meshes. FCL already queries the field through the shared implicit SDF solver. Bullet instead copied every field into a btMiniSDF first: about 32 node indices and 7 doubles per grid sample, built through a hash map. On real scans of 2.9 M and 6.0 M samples that took 3.3 s and 7.1-7.8 s and 2.5 GB and 5.0 GB of extra memory, and it crashes above about 8.7 M samples (a 1 mm field of a single real part is 11-34 M samples). The copy added no accuracy: its cells are filled from the field's trilinear interpolant, which they reproduce exactly.
What changes
Bullet queries the field in place. Every SignedDistanceField (grid-backed or function-backed) becomes a TesseractSdfCollisionShape, a concave shape of Bullet's SDF type that holds the field and reports its scaled domain as its AABB. The convex-SDF and SDF-SDF algorithms collide it through the same implicit solver FCL uses (makeImplicitSDFShape). convertSDFToBt and the btMiniSDF copy are removed. On the scans above, adding a field now takes 0.2 ms with no extra memory, and queries run about 2x faster.
Normals only where needed. For hulls without an implicit form, Bullet samples the field at the hull's vertices. It now reads a vertex's distance first and its normal only when the vertex is within the contact threshold.
Scaling. Stock btSdfCollisionShape ignores local scaling, so a scaled field was collided unscaled. The shape now applies the field's own scale, and a non-uniform scale is rejected with std::invalid_argument when the field is added, as FCL already does.
The field's own gradient. SignedDistanceField gains getGradient() and getDistanceAndGradient(). A grid-backed field returns the exact gradient of its trilinear interpolant, from the same eight samples getDistance() reads; a function-backed field takes central differences of its function in one batched call. The implicit solver uses it instead of numerical differences of the distance, for both Bullet and FCL.
Docs. page_geometry.dox and the SignedDistanceField header now describe in-place queries, and list FCL alongside Bullet as a backend that consumes signed distance fields.
Behaviour changes
Bullet contacts can move slightly. On the scans above, 98.7% of probe distances are within 0.01 mm of before;
the rest moved, in both directions, by up to 1.4 mm where the solver converged on a different point.
Function-backed fields are not sampled by Bullet any more. A field made from a distance function
(createSignedDistanceField) used to be sampled onto a grid once when Bullet built its shape. It is now evaluated
at every query, as FCL already did. Results are exact, but an expensive function (for example, distance to a mesh)
can make Bullet much slower; sample it up front with createDiscreteSignedDistanceField instead. The docs say so.
A non-uniform scale is rejected when the field is added to a Bullet manager.
Tests
Bullet and FCL contacts for spheres, capsules, boxes, cylinders and cones against a field, SDF-SDF pairs, scaled
fields, and a convex hull through the per-vertex path.
The vertex path reads the normal only within the threshold and gives the same answer as a full query.
Bullet rejects a non-uniform scale, and collides a function-backed field without sampling it.
getGradient() matches finite differences of getDistance() and approximates the surface normal.

What changed in this PR
This PR replaces Bullet’s copied btMiniSDF representation with direct signed-distance-field queries through the shared collision solver used by FCL.
Changes:
Adds a Bullet SDF shape with scaled bounds and rejects non-uniform field scaling.
Adds field gradient APIs and skips unnecessary normal queries on the convex-hull vertex path.
Extends collision and gradient tests and documents in-place queries and function-backed field costs.
