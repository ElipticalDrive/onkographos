What this pull request does
A volume of many images does not always fit in one GPU texture. A series of 2464 images
exceeds the limit of 2048 voxels on one axis that every known device states. Before this
work, the allocation of that texture failed, and the viewer showed nothing.
This work gives a volume more than one representation of its voxels. One representation
holds the data that the loader delivered. Another holds a box average of that data on a
coarser grid. A viewport chooses the representation that its device can hold, and it draws
that representation.
This branch is the base of task T2 of
#2921. @mbellehumeur will
merge his later steps into this branch as he completes them.

The fill of a reduced texture
One render fills at most 16 reduced slices that hold data. The render also stops after 8 ms, but only after it fills one slice. rendering.reducedTextureFill sets the two limits. The texture then requests another render, and the next fill starts where the last fill stopped.
A reduced slice reads only the frames that arrived. Before this change, a frame that had not arrived became a frame of zeros, and the average became too low. A replicate counts only when no frame of the box arrived.
fillPlaneByAverage reads the target column from a table, and keeps one row of sums. An integer texture gets the rounded average. Before this change, the value was truncated.
The docs relax the rule "never a decimation" for a display. An interactive display or a lossy display of high-resolution data can use a decimation. A display of lower-resolution data uses the box average. The derivations on the CPU keep the box average.
The render still calculates the box average on the main thread. Issue #2966 proposes to move the reduction out of the render, and to pace the renders of a load.
The stack prefetch
A stack viewport queues a cache fill of its whole series, from the top down, with up to 25 prefetch slots. A switch to MPR did not cancel that fill. The interleaved stages of the volume use 5 thumbnail slots, so the stack fill took most of the bandwidth.
A volume that starts a progressive load removes the queued prefetch requests of other loaders for its images.
stackPrefetch and stackContextPrefetch queue nothing for a stack whose images a volume loads progressively.
cache.getVolumeContainingImageId skips a volume with no image ids. Before this change, the search stopped at that volume.
The images that the cache already holds
A stack viewport can load images of the series before a switch to MPR. Before this change, the volume did not know those images until its own stage requested them. A nearby fill also skipped those frames, because the cache already held a better image. The frames stayed empty until a late stage, and they showed gray.
When load() starts, the volume delivers each fully loaded cached image to itself.
The deliveries run in batches of at most 8 ms, so a volume whose images are all cached does not stall. A cancelled or a restarted load stops the batches.
A delivery only marks the texture slices. Each render fills them under the budget of rendering.reducedTextureFill.
The 64 stages of a large volume
Before this change, the coarse configuration had three stages of every 64th image, and then the stock stages of every 4th image. Each stock stage retrieves its images in index order, so the load swept the volume from one end to the other.
coarseInterleavedRetrieveStages now holds 64 stages of every 64th image.
The offsets follow the bit-reversed order 0, 32, 16, 48, 8, 40, and so on. Each level of stages halves the gap between the retrieved images over the whole volume: 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1.
Each image fills the frames up to half the gap of its level, and each image is retrieved once, at full resolution.
initialImages retrieves the middle image and the last image. The last image closes the tail of a volume whose length is not a multiple of 64.
This configuration has no lossy stage and no thumbnail stage. An HTJ2K server therefore gets no fast lossy first pass from it.
The sample distance of a volume mapper
The sample distance was half the mean voxel side of the full-resolution volume. A series of 2464 images then needed about 5575 samples on its longest ray, which is more than the 4000 of maximumSamplesPerRay. vtk.js does not enforce that maximum. It wrote a warning on each render, and it sampled the whole ray.
sampleDistanceOf uses half the mean voxel side of the grid that the texture holds. A reduced texture has a larger spacing, and a finer sample adds no detail.
The distance never falls below the diagonal of the volume divided by 4000, so one ray needs no more than 4000 samples.
The 3D render path sets the distance again when it binds a texture of another grid.

```
IVoxelManager was an alias of the class (`export type IVoxelManager<T> =
VoxelManager<T>`), so only a subclass could satisfy it. Replace the alias
with a structural interface, extracted from the public surface of the
class, and declare `implements IVoxelManager<T>` on the class so the
compiler keeps the two in agreement.

Four members are not methods. A factory installs each one on the instance
after the constructor returns, and only some of the factories install
them: createScalarVolumeVoxelManager and createImageVoxelManager install
none of the four. Mark all four optional. getRange carried no `?` before,
and getRange() throws a TypeError on the instances of those two
factories.

Add IVolumeVoxelManager, which marks the four required.
createImageVolumeVoxelManager returns it, because that factory installs
all four. createScalarDynamicVolumeVoxelManager installs getRange and
getCompleteScalarDataArray only, so that factory keeps IVoxelManager.

Nothing changes at run time. No call site of the four members gets a
guard, because tsconfig.base.json sets strictNullChecks to false.

Also:

- The history factories and the map, lazy and RLE factories now declare
  the interface instead of the class.
- One cast keeps the private backing store reachable inside the class.
- `_id` becomes private, and the interface declares `id` alone. `_id`
  carried no `private` keyword, and only the `id` getter reads it, so an
  implementer of the interface needs no such field.
- The stray `public static;` member is removed.
```

```
Commit 2 of the multi-resolution work of issue #2921. This commit adds
types and pure functions only. Nothing consumes them yet, and no
behaviour changes.

The grid descriptor, `VoxelGrid` in packages/core/src/types/VoxelGrid.ts,
identifies one representation of the voxel data. It carries an origin, a
direction, a spacing and a set of dimensions, and it carries nothing
else. The same type describes a sub-resolution grid, a sub-region grid
such as a brick or an oblique slab, and a level of a server side brick
store.

There is no level index. There is no `levelIndex` field, no convention
that "level 0 is the finest", and no array of levels. The spacing
already carries on three axes what an ordinal carries on one, and the
open question of the brick format can make the space of the levels
two-dimensional, that is an in-plane level and a k group. A descriptor
that holds no ordinal cannot be wrong either way.

A representation is identified by the pair (grid, statistic), and not by
the grid alone. The set of the statistics is OPEN, and it is not an
enum. It follows the pattern of `RenderBackends` and of `ViewportTypes`:

- `types/VoxelStatisticRegistry.ts` holds the augmentable interfaces
  `VoxelStatisticRegistry` and `VoxelStatisticConstants`, and the string
  union `VoxelStatistic` that each call site takes.
- `Enums.VoxelStatistics` holds the constants, with `Average` built in.
- `registerVoxelStatistic` adds the arithmetic of a statistic and, when
  the caller gives a `name`, a constant on `Enums.VoxelStatistics`.

An extension therefore adds a statistic with no change to a file of the
core package:

    declare module '@cornerstonejs/core' {
      interface VoxelStatisticRegistry {
        'myOrg:minimum': 'myOrg:minimum';
      }
      interface VoxelStatisticConstants {
        readonly MINIMUM: 'myOrg:minimum';
      }
    }

    registerVoxelStatistic({
      name: 'MINIMUM',
      statistic: 'myOrg:minimum',
      createAccumulator: () => ({ reset, add, getValue }),
    });

The core package registers `average`, and it is the only statistic whose
`defaultSelection` is true. A minimum or a maximum leaves that field
false, because a minimum grid at a high resolution would otherwise win a
selection that asks for the highest resolution, and a read would then
return minimum values in place of the data.

The reduction of the resolution is a box average, and it is never a
decimation. A decimation keeps the high spatial frequencies and folds
them into the signal as an alias, and a reformat then shows vertical
blur with stair steps on an oblique structure.

The new functions are in packages/core/src/utilities/voxelGrid/, and
`utilities.voxelGrid` exports them:

- `boxAverageReductionFactors` gives the box size of each axis that
  brings a grid inside a maximum edge, a maximum number of voxels, or
  both. The reduction is per axis. An edge of 2049 against a limit of
  2048 reduces one axis, and it leaves the other two axes alone.
- `reducedDimensions` rounds the count up, so the reduced grid covers
  the whole region and the last box of an axis can be partial. A
  decimation rounds down instead, and
  `loaders/decimatedVolumeModifiers/inPlaneDecimationModifier.ts` keeps
  that other rule. This commit reuses the arithmetic of that modifier,
  which already applies a separate factor to i and to j, and this commit
  does not change that file.
- `deriveBoxAverageGrid` gives the grid of the reduced data. The origin
  carries the sample offset: a decimation reports the corner voxel of a
  box, a box average reports the centre, and the two lie apart by
  `(factor - 1) / 2` source voxels on each axis. A consumer that
  transforms through the grid therefore gets the correct position with
  no new code. The function also describes a region of the source, so
  one brick is expressible.
- `reduceByBoxStatistic` and `boxStatisticAtIJK` compute the values of
  any registered statistic, and `reduceByBoxAverage` and
  `boxAverageAtIJK` name the average case. `IVoxelManager<number>` and
  `IVoxelManager<RGB>` satisfy the source shape and the target shape, so
  a caller passes a sub voxel manager directly.
- A VOXEL OF MORE THAN ONE COMPONENT GETS ONE ACCUMULATOR FOR EACH
  COMPONENT, and the components are independent. A definition of a
  statistic states the arithmetic of one component only, and the same
  definition serves a volume of one component and an RGB volume. The
  code reads the number of the components from the value, and not from
  `numberOfComponents` of the source, because a voxel manager of four
  components gives three components to a reader.
- The accumulators take only the values that are numbers, because a
  source that is not completely loaded returns `undefined` for a voxel
  that has not arrived, and one such voxel must not destroy the value of
  a box. One component with no value makes the whole voxel empty.
- `voxelGridKey` and `voxelGridsEqual` identify a representation. The
  key holds the statistic, the direction and the origin, so an oblique
  grid needs no second cache, and the odd slices and the even slices,
  which share a spacing, get two keys.
- `voxelCountOfGrid`, `maxEdgeOfGrid` and `voxelGridWithinLimits` count
  the cost of a grid before a caller allocates a texture.

The test packages/core/test/utilities/voxelGrid.jest.js covers each
function, with 35 tests. Acceptance criterion 4 of the issue is the test
that reduces exactly one axis for an edge of 2049 against a limit of
2048. Other tests register a statistic of a maximum, as an extension
does, and reduce one RGB voxel manager into another.

This commit does not add the approximation of a box average from a
decimation. That arithmetic belongs with the code that fills a
representation, and it arrives with the composite voxel manager.
```

```
Commit 3 of the multi-resolution work of issue #2921. A composite voxel
manager holds MORE THAN ONE REPRESENTATION OF THE SAME DATA AT THE SAME
TIME: a full-resolution volume, a reduced-resolution copy, a brick of a
server side store, or a tile of a whole slide image.

THIS COMMIT HAS NO CONSUMER, on purpose. Nothing in the render path, in
`ImageVolume` or in a loader changes.

`CompositeVoxelManager` implements `IVoxelManager`, and it is not a
subclass of `VoxelManager`. Every member of the existing API delegates to
the PRIMARY representation, so a viewport, a tool, a segmentation and an
annotation get the same geometry, the same number of slices and the same
positions that they get today. New code opts in to the new API.

THE SELECTION RULE takes three inputs, and not one: the region, the
statistic and the ceiling. The rule selects the representation of the
highest resolution that is not higher than the ceiling, among those that
match the statistic and cover the region. Without the ceiling, a request
that fills a texture at one eighth selects the full-resolution data and
reduces that data at every fill. When nothing sits at the ceiling, the
rule takes the coarsest candidate, which is the cheapest source of the
resolution that the caller asked for. A read from a tool passes no
ceiling, and the rule then degenerates to "the highest resolution
available", which is the behaviour of today.

THE SELECTION NEVER CREATES. `createRepresentation` derives a new
representation, and a caller decides whether a new representation is
worth its cost. A box size is a whole number of at least 1, so a
derivation always goes from a higher resolution to a lower one.

THE QUALITY RECORD IS PER DELIVERY, AND A DELIVERY IS NOT ALWAYS A FRAME.
A streaming volume delivers one frame, a brick store delivers a box, and
a whole slide image delivers a tile, so each entry of the record carries
the REGION that the delivery covered and the quality of that data.
`acceptData` takes `bounds` for a box and `frameIndex` for the case of
one frame. `getRegionQuality` aggregates the entries of a region into an
absolute record: the lowest and the highest quality, the number of
voxels, how many of them are missing, and whether that count is exact.
The record states no verdict, so two viewports over one volume can reach
a different answer from one record.

The record holds one entry for each delivery, and never one for each
voxel. A volume of 64 x 64 x 500 leaves 500 entries. A lower quality
never replaces a higher one, which is the rule that
`BaseStreamingImageVolume.updateTextureAndTriggerEvents` applies to
`cachedFrames`.

DELIVERIES CAN OVERLAP, and a brick store that delivers over a frame of a
progressive loader makes them overlap. Each voxel then carries the best
quality that any delivery gave it, and the record counts the union of the
deliveries exactly, so an overlap is never counted twice. An entry that a
better entry already holds does not enter the record, and an entry that
holds a no better entry replaces it.

A DERIVED REPRESENTATION HOLDS NO RECORD OF ITS OWN. One of its voxels is
a box of source voxels, so the quality of that voxel is the quality of
the worst source voxel of its box, and the voxel holds no data while any
source voxel of its box has not arrived. A derivation therefore freezes a
copy of the record of its source, and a query maps the region back into
the source. The copy is frozen, so the record never states more than the
data that the derivation really read.

Other members:

- `getRepresentation` addresses one representation directly, and no
  selection rule applies to that read. That is the only way to reach a
  statistic that a default selection does not consider.
- `coveringRepresentations` gives every representation that covers a
  region, from the finest to the coarsest.
- `fillGrid` fills a grid from the best sources. A FILL IS MANY TO ONE:
  several representations can cover one region between them, which is the
  brick case.
- `getAtIJK` returns the nearest value available, and it never returns
  `undefined` for a voxel that is in bounds while a representation covers
  the region. A region that no representation covers is a hole, and the
  coarsest representation is the backstop.

`utilities/voxelGrid/voxelGridTransforms.ts` holds the position
arithmetic: the world position of a voxel, the index of a world position,
the map of an index and of a region between two grids, and the test of
the coverage of a region. `gridCoversRegion` maps the eight corners, so
an oblique grid gives the right answer.

`mapBoundsBetweenGrids` WORKS ON THE EXTENT OF A VOXEL, and not on the
centre of a voxel. A rule that rounds a centre is wrong in both
directions: four slices of the full resolution meet ONE frame of a grid
whose k factor is 4, and one voxel of that grid meets the FOUR slices
that feed it.

Tests, 35 of them in
packages/core/test/utilities/CompositeVoxelManager.jest.js:

- A WSI-shaped composite from synthetic tiles, with per-level differing
  extents, non-uniform tiling, and levels that are not a clean pyramid of
  powers of two. Acceptance criterion 9 of the issue.
- A reduction of 2 on i, 3 on j and 4 on k, with a partial box on the j
  axis. The test reads the reduced data at the full resolution, and the
  full-resolution data at the reduced resolution, and it checks every

- Deliveries that overlap: the union, the better quality of an overlap,
  the refusal of a delivery that a better delivery already holds, and the

- The store of the record, which holds one entry for each delivery.
- A read over a hole, the selection rule, and the delegation of the
```

```
Commit 4 of the multi-resolution work of issue #2921, and the last commit
of the hand-over prefix. MR-API-VM-10 to MR-API-VM-14.

A QUALITY QUERY RETURNS AN ABSOLUTE RECORD, AND THE RECORD STATES NO
VERDICT. `VoxelQualityRecord` says which data exists, at which spacing,
with which loss, and how much is missing. A SEPARATE PURE FUNCTION,
`compareVoxelQuality`, compares that record against the requirement of
one reader and gives the verdict.

That split is what lets two viewports over one volume report differently,
and it is why a caller can cache a record and must not cache a verdict. A
record is a fact about the data. A verdict belongs to a pair: the data,
and the reader that uses the data.

THE KIND OF THE REDUCTION IS SEPARATE FROM THE SOURCE OF THE DATA,
because a box average and a decimation at one spacing have a different
loss:

- `reduction` states HOW the data reached its spacing. `Enums`
  `.VoxelReductions` holds `None`, `BoxAverage` and `Decimation`.
- `source` states WHERE the data came from. `Enums.VoxelDataSources`
  holds `ServerLevel`, `ClientDerived`, `DecoderSubResolution` and
  `DirectLoad`.

A verdict reads ONE property of the kind: whether that kind aliases. A
reduction that aliases folds the high spatial frequencies into the
signal, and no display resolution removes that error, so a view of such
data is lossy at every magnification. A reduction that does not alias is
lossless at a display resolution that its spacing can carry, which is
MR-U-5: the user does not get a warning that is not necessary.

The set of the kinds is OPEN, and it follows the pattern of the
statistics of commit 2. An extension augments `VoxelReductionRegistry`
and `VoxelReductionConstants`, and it calls `registerVoxelReduction`,
which states the one property that a verdict reads:

    registerVoxelReduction({
      name: 'WAVELET',
      reduction: 'myOrg:wavelet',
      aliases: false,
    });

A KIND THAT NOTHING REGISTERED COUNTS AS A KIND THAT ALIASES, so an
```

feat(core): choose a render strategy, and reduce a texture to fit the…

```
… device

A device cannot always hold the full-resolution texture of a volume. The code
asked for that texture at the full dimensions, the allocation failed inside the
driver, and the viewport showed black. This commit gives a volume render path a
set of strategies and lets it choose among them, which is MR-CODE-1 to
MR-CODE-3 and P37 to P39 of #2921.

A strategy states how a render gets its voxels, and the names of its texture
sets derive from it. The work splits in two, and the two costs are deliberately
different:

- a provider builds the strategies. It derives the voxels and allocates the
  textures, so it runs when the render path adds its actor, and again when the
  data finishes loading or the memory changes, but never during a render;
- a selection chooses one strategy for one render. Every strategy already
  exists, so the selection allocates nothing and cannot fail.

Both are parameters of the render path and never a branch inside it. A new
render type defines its own provider, and that is how a new render type defines
new texture sets. A render component that implements phases supplies its own
selection, and that is where "reduced for the first render, full resolution for
the lossless render" belongs. The phase of a render is not defined here,
because a phased render is tightly bound to how that render draws.

A strategy need not be backed by a texture: a ready strategy that offers no
binding states that the data is available by another means, such as the voxel
manager of a device that cannot hold the texture. A render may also choose no
strategy at all, which binds no texture, and a CPU render does that for its
lossless pass.

The capability profiles are a new exported module, because tasks T5 and T15
both consume them. An application states which profile applies, and nothing
probes a device. The speed of a profile is a relative index: 1 is a slow CPU
that emulates a GPU in software, 100 is a recent top-of-the-line GPU, and the
scale has no upper bound.

The reduction applies a separate factor to each axis. An edge of 2049 voxels
exceeds a limit of 2048 on one axis and by one voxel, so one axis reduces and
the other two do not.

vtkStreamingOpenGLVolumeMapper takes the dimensions of a texture from that
texture when the texture states a grid, so a reduced texture is no longer
reallocated at the full dimensions. It also counts the cost before it allocates,
and it refuses an allocation that the device cannot hold instead of letting the
driver fail. vtkStreamingOpenGLTexture fills a grid that is not the grid of the
volume from the composite voxel manager.

The render reports the quality, because the render is the only party that knows
which textures it used and how it used them. A render that reads a coarse
texture only to decide which fine textures to sample does not report the quality
of that coarse texture.

packages/docs holds the design, with a diagram of the pieces and the workflow,
the contracts that a consumer may rely on, and the implementation choices that
are free to change.
```

```
A derived representation follows the load: each frame that arrives redoes
the boxes of that frame. Three parts of that path repeated work, and one
part gave the wrong element type.

- One delivery can arrive twice. `BaseStreamingImageVolume` marks a frame
  from the delivery, and again from the event that the cache sends when
  the image of that frame arrives. The composite now records the quality
  at which a refresh last wrote voxels for a region, and it skips the
  second reduction of the same data. A mark that runs before the image
  arrives writes nothing and leaves no record, so it never blocks the
  mark that follows the image.
- `reduceRegionInto` returns the number of voxels that it wrote, so a
  refresh that wrote nothing does not claim the deliveries of its source.
- The record of the deliveries gets an index, by the region and by the k
  slice. A volume of 3720 frames records 3720 deliveries that no other
  delivery contains, so a scan of the record for each delivery was
  quadratic over a load.
- A slice whose image has not arrived is memoized. A reduction reads
  every voxel of a slice, so each of the 262144 reads of one slice of
  512 x 512 asked the cache for the same missing image again. The
  message for that image drops to the debug level, because a frame that
  has not arrived is the ordinary state of a streaming volume.

  `getConstructor` answers with the right type before the first image
  arrives. Without it a derivation that runs before the load allocates a
  Float32Array, which holds twice the bytes of the Int16Array of a CT.

`BaseStreamingImageVolume.invalidateVolume` clears the record of the
refresh, because a dynamic volume changes its dimension group and every
frame then holds other data at the same quality.
```

```
A derived representation allocated a typed array of its own, which no
cache counted, no memory pressure could evict and no second volume could
share. The image cache is the one store of the voxels, and a reduced
representation now holds one image of that cache for each slice of its
grid, exactly as a volume holds one image for each of its frames.

The image id of a reduced slice names the source, the size of the slice
and the statistic:

    reduced:<columns>x<rows>[x<frames>]:<statistic>:<sourceImageId>
    reduced:256x256:average:wadors:https://host/…/frames/1
    reduced:256x128x2:average:wadors:https://host/…/frames/1

`columns` and `rows` are the size of the reduced slice. `frames` states
how many source frames the slice covers, and it appears only when the k
axis reduces. The id names the FIRST of those frames, which identifies
the set, because the frames of a series are ordered. Two volumes that
share a frame therefore share the reduced slice that the frame produces,
and a `minimum` slice never collides with an `average` slice of the same
size.

THE SIZE MUST COME BEFORE THE STATISTIC. `imageIdToURI` strips one
scheme prefix when a second scheme-like prefix follows it, so
`reduced:average:wadors:…` would give the URI of the SOURCE image and
collide with it in `cache.getCachedImageBasedOnImageURI`. A size starts
with a digit, which no scheme does.

- `provideReducedImages` builds those images through
  `createAndCacheLocalImage`, with the reduced rows and columns, the
  spacing of the reduced grid, and the origin of each slice. The origin
  of the grid already carries the half-voxel offset of the box average,
  so a consumer that transforms through the image plane of a slice needs
  no new code. An image that the cache already holds comes back as it
  is, so a second viewport derives nothing again.
- The voxel manager over those images is a
  `createImageVolumeVoxelManager`, which is the kind of voxel manager
  that the volume itself uses, and it owns no array.
- Each image takes `sharedCacheKey`, which is the exemption from the
  eviction of the cache that the frames of a volume already take.
- `CompositeVoxelManager` takes a `createStorage` function and keeps the
  array as the fallback, so the composite decides nothing about where
  the voxels live and a test builds one without the cache. A
  representation states its `imageIds`, and `acceptData` takes them, so
  a loader that delivers a reduced level of a server store, or a brick,
  states where its voxels live.
- `ImageVolume` supplies that function for a derivation that reads the
  grid of the volume. Only then does a slice of the derived grid cover a
  whole number of frames that an image id can name.
- `vtkStreamingOpenGLTexture` reads one reduced slice from the image
  cache, which is the read that the full-resolution path already makes.
  The box factors are computed even when a derived representation
  exists, so a slice that the cache evicted falls back on its own
  instead of taking the whole texture with it.

An oblique grid is unchanged: it holds no representation, and
`fillGrid` fills its texture from the voxel managers.
```

feat(core): load a large volume in 64 stages of every 64th image
…
e85899e
```
The coarse configuration added three stages of every 64th image, and then
the stock stages of every 4th image. Each stock stage retrieves its images
in index order, so after the coarse stages the load swept the volume from
one end to the other.

`coarseInterleavedRetrieveStages` now holds 64 stages of every 64th image.
The offsets follow the bit-reversed order 0, 32, 16, 48, 8, ..., so each
level of stages halves the gap between the retrieved images over the whole
volume. Each image fills the frames up to half the gap of its level, and
each image is retrieved once, at full resolution.

`initialImages` retrieves the middle image and the last image. The last
image closes the tail of a volume whose length is not a multiple of 64.
```

fix(core): set the sample distance of a volume mapper from the textur…

18e8445

```
…e grid, within the maximum samples

The sample distance was half the mean voxel side of the full-resolution
volume. A long series, such as 2464 images, then needed more than the 4000
samples of `maximumSamplesPerRay` on its longest ray. vtk.js does not
enforce that maximum: it warned on every render and sampled the whole ray.
```
