Two commits on this branch. The first gives the beach real light; the second makes it a thing you can build in without fear.
1. Give the beach a sun and a sky
Nothing cast a shadow and nothing occluded anything, so every shape sat on the sand rather than in it. A moulded tower read as a sticker; a moat read as a darker circle painted on flat ground.
Both are now real, and both are cheap for the same reason: on a heightfield the occluder and the receiver are the same function. No shadow map, no second camera, no bias to tune, no resolution mismatch between what casts and what receives — you walk toward the sun and ask whether the ground has got above you yet.
src/shaders/light.js marches that walk and writes two terms into a small RGBA8 table over the playable square:
Sun visibility — directional, with a penumbra that widens with distance from the receiver, so a moulded lip stays crisp at its own foot and a headland goes soft across the bay.
Sky visibility — ambient, by horizon angle in eight directions. The one that can't be faked from a normal: at the bottom of a hole the normal points straight up at the sky it cannot see.
Baked to a table rather than evaluated per pixel so cost stops depending on pixel ratio — 256² is one 65k-fragment pass whatever the display is doing, against a terrain shader running over a million fragments at 2×. Re-baked every frame, because the thing casting the shadows is the thing you're digging. RGBA8 rather than float: eight bits is more than three-band shading can show, and it's the one format that's both colour-renderable and linearly filterable everywhere, so it's a single hardware tap where every other sampler here reimplements bilinear by hand.
Two decisions about how it's applied
The first draft got the second one wrong badly enough to be worth writing down.
Cast shadow folds into the lambert term before the banding. A quantised key light times a smooth shadow puts a fourth, ungraded value on the beach and the picture stops reading as flat colour — which is the whole look.
Occlusion goes into the shade colour, not on top of the result. AO describes sky light that never arrives, and the shade tone is where the sky light is. Laid over the top it double-darkens everything both turned away from the sun and enclosed — most of a sandcastle — and a moulded tower comes out looking like grey plastic.
The sea takes a gentle version of the sun term so a shadow crossing the waterline keeps going; props sample it at the foot so a bucket in a tower's shadow goes with the shadow. Props don't yet cast — that needs the prop list inside the bake, and a 192-entry loop per texel isn't the way.
2. Undo, dragged mould runs, and snap
The second and third turn out to be one feature seen from two sides: a wall is only a wall if it comes out straight.
Undo (↺, top left)
One entry per user action, taken before the action happens. A stroke is one undo, a dragged run of forty blocks is one undo, and so is New beach — until now the one action in this game with nothing to take it back.
The copy is blitFramebuffer, GPU to GPU. sim.snapshot() was already written and would have been the obvious call, and it's exactly wrong here: a readPixels of a megabyte and a half that stalls the pipeline, on every stroke, whether or not anybody ever presses the button. Blitting costs nothing on the strokes nobody undoes.
Depth is 8 on a phone, 14 on desktop (~10 MB of history on a phone). The button is greyed rather than hidden when empty — a control that comes and goes is a control you have to look for. Putting it top-left also gives the hint a symmetric band between the two corner buttons.
Dragging the mould lays a run
The stamp is a queue now rather than a single armed slot, drained one per substep, so a drag can lay shapes faster than a frame can turn them out.
Every shape in a run stands on the height the run started at. That's what makes a line of blocks come out as one wall with a level top instead of a row of lumps following the beach down to the sea — and it's what keeps the drag off surfaceAt, whose one-texel readback can't happen sixty times a second.
Blocks turn to face along the drag (the shader has always had a rotation input; nothing had ever passed anything but zero) and overlap heavily so the run reads as solid. Towers and cones stand further apart, so dragging those gives a row of turrets. The run walks the whole gap between pointer events rather than stamping one per event — same reason the brush is a swept segment.
Snap (⊞, beside the size slider)
The tool lands on a one-metre grid — fixed, not derived from the brush, because a grid that changes size when you move a slider is not a grid you can build a symmetry on. And a mould run or wall drag locks to one of eight compass headings, running dead straight from where it began.
The heading is latched once the drag is 1.5 m long, then held. The first cut re-derived it from the current point every event, which is much worse than it sounds: a hand wobbling across the boundary between two headings makes the far end of the wall teleport between two rays and the run fills in the gap each time. What comes out is not a wall, it's a ploughed field. Verified both ways — the same deliberately wobbly drag gives a serpentine wall with snap off and a straight one with it on.
Only the mould and the wall lock. A dig that can only travel on eight headings isn't a spade, it's a CAD program.
The cursor ring turns with the block and holds the heading after the drag ends, because the ring is a promise about the next tap rather than a report on the last one.
Verification
Headless Chromium throughout, scripted against merged main for the A/Bs.
Light — towers, blocks and the headland domes gain unmistakable ground shadows; the headlands go from flat cut-outs to shapes with volume. A dug pit reads concave rather than smudged. Both quality tiers compile and run (phone light table 256², desktop 320²), checked with and without mobile emulation since the tier comes from pointer: coarse. Median frame time moved ~3% on a CPU rasteriser, the worst case for an added full-table pass.
Undo — enables on the first action, empties to disabled, and is inert when clicked through the DOM at the bottom of the stack. A dragged wall appears and one press removes all of it.
Snap — same wobbly drag, serpentine off / straight on.
Layout — no console or page errors and nothing outside the safe area at 320, 375, 390 and 430 wide, plus landscape. The size row grew a button and measures 283 px inside a 320 px screen.
