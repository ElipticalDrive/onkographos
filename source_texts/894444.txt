Backface-culled square visible at scene center when camera is lowered below terrain surface
Context
When the camera is lowered below the terrain surface (using orbit/pan controls to look up from underneath the ground), a square-shaped artifact becomes visible at the center of the scene. This is the bottom face of the voxel-grid terrain mesh, which is rendered with side: THREE.FrontSide (src/renderer/TerrainMesh.ts:87), meaning the downward-facing triangles at the bottom of the terrain are backface-culled and invisible, but the geometry boundaries still form a visible square outline.
The terrain uses marching cubes on a VoxelGrid (default tutorial size: 24x24). When the camera goes below the surface, the FrontSide culling makes the downward-facing triangles disappear, revealing the square footprint of the voxel grid boundary.

Possible solutions
Double-side the terrain: Change side: THREE.FrontSide to side: THREE.DoubleSide in TerrainMesh.ts:87 -- renders both sides. Performance impact minimal for tutorial pit (24x24).
Clamp camera below terrain: Prevent orbit camera from moving below a minimum Y threshold (e.g., camera must stay above Y=1). See SceneManager.ts camera init (line 72) for orbit controls.
Bottom cap: Generate a single downward-facing quad at the base of the voxel grid to seal the bottom while keeping FrontSide rendering.
Selective DoubleSide: Apply DoubleSide only to horizontal faces while keeping vertical faces as FrontSide.
Recommended approach
Option 1 (DoubleSide) is simplest and most robust -- fixes visual bug regardless of camera angle. Performance cost negligible for tutorial-sized grids.
Acceptance criteria
Lowering camera below terrain no longer reveals a backface-culled square at scene center
Terrain looks correct from all camera angles (top, sides, below)
No regression in terrain rendering performance
No unexpected visual artifacts from DoubleSide rendering
