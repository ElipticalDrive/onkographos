Summary
Adds ClosestPoint: the point of a segment, triangle or tetrahedron nearest the origin, and the vertices of the feature it lies on, plus barycentric coordinates on a line and a plane. These are the simplex sub-algorithms GJK and EPA build on. They stay accurate for long, thin and nearly colinear triangles.
Closes #14
Changes
SimplexFeature: an option set with one bit per vertex, .a to .d. One bit means a vertex, two an edge, three a face, and four the inside of a tetrahedron.
ClosestPoint.barycentricCoordinates: for two points and for three. Each returns the coordinates and isDegenerate; the fallback coordinates are usable even when the input is degenerate.
A triangle is degenerate when |ab × ac|² is below 10⁻¹². Its coordinates are then those of its longest edge. Jolt's fallback compares only two of the three edges, so on degenerate input a different coordinate can be 0 than in Jolt. Both describe the same point.
ClosestPoint.onSegment, onTriangle and onTetrahedron: these follow Ericson's Real-Time Collision Detection, 5.1.
Triangle:
the normal is a precise cross product of the two shorter edges;
the face point is a projection along the normal;
a triangle under 10⁻¹⁰ in squared doubled area falls back to its vertices and edges.
Tetrahedron: the origin's side of each face comes from originOutsideFaces. Faces that rounding leaves disagreeing about the inside all count as outside.
includesC and includesD: these let GJK skip the features it knows can't be closest. Their docs state exactly what each one skips.
Speed:
The triangle's region tests run in Ericson's order on the exact negations of his dot products, four of them from one batch of multiplies and pairwise sums. A test pins every region, feature and point to his form, boundaries included.
The tetrahedron's face sides come from four precise normals in two vector passes.
Degenerate fallbacks stay out of line.
Benchmarks: ClosestPoint.onTriangle and ClosestPoint.onTetrahedron, with Jolt counterparts (GetClosestPointOnTriangle, GetClosestPointOnTetrahedron). Phase 1: Math and geometry #33 now uses these names.
Verification

Ported tests: Jolt's ClosestPointTests: the long triangle in all six vertex orders, the nearly colinear triangle, and the small triangle whose plane passes near the origin.
Regions: every Voronoi region of the segment, triangle and tetrahedron. Degenerate inputs include coincident and colinear points, a flat tetrahedron, and one whose faces disagree on the inside.
Random checks:
200,000 triangles, half on an integer grid so many origins sit on a region boundary, match Ericson's form exactly.
20,000 triangles and 20,000 tetrahedra match double-precision references, including an inside test.
