From Codex review thread on PR #924 (refs #921). Verified at 4293cd3523e.
Claim, verified structurally: the pre-upscale hook (render_forward_clustered.cpp "Gaussian Splats Pre-Upscale" block) composites splat color into the internal buffer that TAA/FSR2/MetalFX-temporal then consume. gaussian_splat_renderer.cpp builds its render projection from the raw p_render_data->scene_data->cam_projection (copied at ~2361, used at ~2388 with only a flip_y adjustment) and never applies scene_data->taa_jitter; ordinary geometry renders with the jitter-corrected projection, and FSR2 is explicitly handed the jitter right below the hook (render_forward_clustered.cpp:2700). The temporal pass therefore receives splat pixels at unjittered positions while being told the frame is jittered — expected symptom: subpixel swimming/blur of splats relative to meshes under TAA/FSR2/MFX.
Relation to #924's named residual risk: same temporal-quality cluster as the missing motion vectors / reactive mask / depth write-back (deferred ladder steps). The FSR2 motion burst in the visual package (C:\Projects\godotGS-remediation\phase2\gpu001-visual\runs\gpu001fix__v3_fsr2_100_dtTrue) is the existing material where a human may already see it.
Remedy: render splats with the same jitter-corrected projection meshes use (apply taa_jitter to render_projection when running in the pre-upscale phase, mirroring RenderSceneDataRD::get_cam_projection()), before compositing into the temporal input.
Cost: renderer-math change — requires real-scan visual validation including motion capture on jitter-active configs; interacts with the sort/cull view state (verify the cull signature does not need the jitter — pure subpixel offset should not, but that must be checked, not assumed). Belongs with the temporal-quality follow-up work, not in #924 at disposition.

1.3 The attribution: the splat instability locks to the jitter phase exactly
The mesh control turned out to be saturated under FSR2 — a 2-px checker is near-Nyquist, the worst case for temporal reconstruction, so its ~2.2–2.4 delta is not a clean floor. The decisive evidence is not the ratio; it is the periodicity.
Per-pair splat-region delta over the 24-frame FSR2 @1.0 window:
```
1.17  0.84  1.31  0.98  0.92  1.68  0.70  1.14
1.17  0.84  1.31  0.98  0.92  1.69  0.70  1.14
1.17  0.84  1.30  0.98  0.92  1.68  0.70
```
The same eight values, three times, to ±0.01. Autocorrelation at lag 8 = +1.00 (next best: lag 11 at +0.57). The engine's jitter phase length at scaling_3d_scale = 1.0 is 8 * (target/render)² = 8 (servers/rendering/renderer_viewport.cpp:252-260).
At scale = 0.5 the phase is 32, longer than the 24-frame window, and — as that predicts — no period is detectable there (best lag 3 at only +0.37). The absence corroborates the model rather than contradicting it.
The chain that makes this an attribution rather than a correlation:
The splat raster output is frame-invariant: the none row is 0.000/0 across 23 consecutive pairs.
It is invariant to taa_frame_count too — the one other frame % phase quantity GS shaders read (shaders/includes/gs_lighting_common.glsl:72,116,148 → gs_directional_shadow.glsl), also period-8 under FSR2 and set at renderer_scene_cull.cpp:2696. Under TAA, taa_frame_count cycles 0–15 and the splat region still measures 0.002 mean / spread 0.001–0.002, i.e. bit-stable. So it is not the driver.
Therefore the FSR2 variation is introduced downstream of the splat raster, by the resolve, and it is locked to the FSR2 jitter phase. The only per-frame quantity FSR2 is handed is params.jitter (render_forward_clustered.cpp:2713).
That is taa_jitter, and nothing else.
1.4 Why FSR2 and not TAA
FSR2 is told the jitter and compensates for it, in two places:
thirdparty/amd-fsr2/shaders/ffx_fsr2_common.h:431-437, ComputeHrPosFromLrPos():
FfxFloat32x2 fSrcJitteredPos = FfxFloat32x2(iPxLrPos) + 0.5f - Jitter();
thirdparty/amd-fsr2/shaders/ffx_fsr2_accumulate.h:219-220, InitParams():
const FfxFloat32x2 fLrUvJittered = fHrUv + Jitter() / RenderSize(); — and that UV is what samples the input colour.
FSR2 assumes every low-res texel was sampled at pixel_centre − jitter and undoes that offset. Splat texels were not sampled there, so the splat layer is reconstructed displaced by exactly +Jitter() low-res pixels, changing every frame along the Halton sequence, while the meshes beside it are placed correctly.
Godot's TAA never receives the jitter at all. Its entire push constant is servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/effects/taa_resolve.glsl:50-55 → {vec2 resolution; float disocclusion_threshold; float disocclusion_scale;}. grep -n jitter taa_resolve.glsl returns exactly one hit, a comment at :329. Godot's TAA gets its antialiasing purely by accumulating jittered frames; it never un-jitters anything. With a constant input and zero velocity its blend converges to a fixed point — which is precisely the measured 0.002.

1.7 Jitter is one of four missing temporal inputs
modules/gaussian_splatting/shaders/viewport_blit.glsl reads the scene depth for its depth test (:120-135) and writes only u_destination_image — a single imageStore at :215, the file's only write. The painterly composite likewise declares one output, layout(location = 0) out vec4 out_color (shaders/painterly_composite.glsl:21), and draws into a colour-only framebuffer from framebuffer_cache->get_cache(scene_color) (interfaces/painterly_renderer.cpp:1332) — no depth attachment, no gl_FragDepth.
An exhaustive grep of modules/gaussian_splatting/ for velocity / motion_vector / reactive returns zero writes to any engine buffer (only streaming camera-speed heuristics, core/streaming_visibility_controller.cpp:1145-1166).
So of the four things render_forward_clustered.cpp:2683-2764 feeds the temporal stages — colour, depth, velocity, reactive mask — splats supply colour only, at an unjittered position.
docs/architecture/render-pipeline.md:149-153 already names three of the four:

On raster_output.depth, as it stands today rather than as older issues describe it: render_pipeline_stages.cpp:2782 assigns r_output.depth = renderer->get_painterly_depth_texture(), resolved at gaussian_splat_renderer.cpp:2064-2091 to the tile rasterizer's own depth texture. That is the GS-internal depth the composite tests against the scene depth — it is not written back into rb->get_depth_texture(). #1009-era work made it valid; it did not make it visible to FSR2.
2. Mechanism, with file:line at current master
Where the engine puts the jitter. servers/rendering/renderer_rd/storage_rd/render_scene_data_rd.cpp:39-45:
```
Projection RenderSceneDataRD::get_cam_projection() const {
    Projection correction;
    correction.set_depth_correction(flip_y);
    correction.add_jitter_offset(taa_jitter);
    return correction * cam_projection;
}
```
and the same three lines again at :60-65 (get_view_projection) and :82-86 (the UBO every ordinary shader reads, with taa_jitter mirrored at :112-113).
core/math/projection.cpp:932-935 — add_jitter_offset writes columns[3][0] += x; columns[3][1] += y; on the correction matrix, which left-multiplies, so the engine projection is J · D · cam_projection with J = identity plus that translation. Worked numerically against a Godot set_perspective matrix (75° fovy, 16:9, 0.05/200), the only non-zero entries of J·P − P are:
```
columns[2][0] delta = -jitter.x
columns[2][1] delta = -jitter.y
columns[0][0], columns[1][1], columns[2][3] : unchanged, bit-identical
resulting NDC offset at z = -0.5, -5.0, -100.0 : (+jitter.x, +jitter.y) in all three
```
So it is a constant NDC translation of exactly taa_jitter, independent of depth, leaving untouched the two diagonal entries the shaders derive focal length from. Both halves matter in §3.
Where it would have to enter for splats. gaussian_splat_renderer.cpp:2400-2402, immediately after the flip. Not via set_depth_correction — GS deliberately skips the engine's reverse-Z/remap-Z because the gaussian pipeline carries linear view-space depth (:2399 says so) — but as the jitter factor alone:
```
Projection jitter_correction;                              // identity
jitter_correction.add_jitter_offset(scene_data->taa_jitter);
render_projection = jitter_correction * render_projection; // == J · (flip · cam_projection)
```
Self-gating, for free: renderer_scene_cull.cpp:2681-2696 default-constructs Vector2 jitter; and only assigns it inside if (p_jitter_phase_count > 0), and renderer_viewport.cpp:252-260 leaves the phase count at 0 unless temporal scaling or TAA is on. So scene_data->taa_jitter is exactly (0,0) whenever no temporal stage is active and the expression is the identity in every other configuration. Source the value from scene_data->taa_jitter; do not generate an independent sequence — see §3.2.
What the temporal passes expect. render_forward_clustered.cpp:2683-2764, immediately after the GS hook at :2658-2681:
FSR2 (:2684-2729): params.color :2704, params.depth :2705, params.velocity :2706, params.reactive :2707, params.jitter :2713.
TAA (:2759-2763): the shader binds colour :44, depth :45, velocity :46, last-velocity :47, history :48.
What splats supply today: the colour texture only, unjittered.

3.1 The render cache — a correctness trap, but not a performance cost
render_pipeline_stages.cpp:2603 calls can_reuse_cached_render(..., p_input.projection, ...) — the unjittered one — and output_compositor.cpp:176 does an exact Projection compare on it; the signature is written back with the same unjittered value (update_render_cache_signature(..., raster_input.projection, ...) at :1909-1910, output_compositor.cpp:209).
So a jitter confined to render_projection would not invalidate the cache. Wherever reuse is live, the raster stage is skipped (render_pipeline_stages.cpp:1835, "Raster skipped: reused cached render" at :1861-1864) and the same texture — carrying one frozen jitter value — is re-composited every frame. The fix would silently do nothing, while still looking correct to any test that only inspects the projection matrix. Any jitter change must carry the jitter into the reuse key.
But the feared cost is not there. I expected losing static-camera reuse to be the price of Option B. It is not, at shipped defaults: _is_per_splat_clip_expected() (render_pipeline_stages.cpp:234-249) returns true whenever per_splat_depth_clip is on — its shipped default is true (core/gaussian_splat_manager.cpp:1002) — and there is a valid single-view scene depth, and :2598-2600 then refuses reuse outright for non-painterly frames. The code comment at :2590-2597 says so in as many words: "static-camera reuse is lost while the feature is on in scenes with a valid scene depth."
Measured, not inferred: all four configs in §1.2 reported raster_path = other, never cached. The static-camera render cache never engaged once, at shipped defaults, in any of them.
3.2 The composite depth test gets better, not worse
viewport_blit.glsl:120-135 compares GS depth against the engine scene depth at matching pixel coordinates; painterly_composite.glsl:76-80 and the per-splat clip in includes/tile_raster_common.glsl:62-69 do the same. Godot's opaque depth buffer is jittered whenever TAA/FSR2 is on (render_scene_data_rd.cpp:82-86). So today, with a temporal stage active, GS depth and scene depth already sit in different subpixel frames — up to half a pixel of silhouette misregistration in the shipped-default depth test. Applying the same taa_jitter aligns them. A differently-scaled or opposite-signed one would make it worse; this is the strongest reason to take the value verbatim.
3.3 A smaller one worth a look
shaders/tile_resolve.glsl:276 reconstructs a GS view position with params.inv_projection_matrix but an unjittered pixel-centre uv (:172, uv = (vec2(coord) + 0.5) / viewport_size), feeding lighting and shadow lookups (:211, :221, :231). With a jittered inverse that picks up a subpixel bias. Almost certainly invisible; not zero.
3.4 Screenshots, QA baselines and guards
tests/visual_baselines/ holds exactly one image (composite_hazard_256x256.png), used by a non-temporal case; no QA baseline is captured under TAA or FSR2, so none of them move. The only guard on this seam, tests/ci/check_gs_pre_upscale_hook.py, watches 7 files (:76-82) and gaussian_splat_renderer.cpp is not among them; it never mentions taa_jitter, cam_projection or render_projection. It would neither catch nor block this change — unless a change also renames one of its seven literal anchors in render_forward_clustered.cpp (:112-118), which its docstring (:62-65) requires be updated in the same commit rather than weakened.
4. Options
Option A — full temporal integration
Jitter + render-cache key + depth write-back into rb->get_depth_texture() + per-pixel motion vectors + FSR2 reactive-mask contribution.
Cost: large. Motion vectors need per-pixel world reconstruction from GS depth reprojected with the previous view-projection; the module caches no previous-frame camera state at all today. Depth and velocity write-back means new attachments/dispatches at the seam, i.e. edits in servers/rendering/renderer_rd/forward_clustered/render_forward_clustered.cpp → R3, ADR, two independent reviews, CODEOWNER.
Risk: high. Depth write-back changes what every later depth consumer sees; a wrong velocity is worse than none — it smears confidently.
User sees: splats behaving like geometry under TAA/FSR2. The disclosure disappears.
Option B — jitter, plus the cache key
Cost: small. ~3 lines at gaussian_splat_renderer.cpp:2400-2402 and the reuse-key change at render_pipeline_stages.cpp:2603 / output_compositor.cpp:176,209. R2. The R2 evidence bar ("Real-scan visual validation for rendering-math changes") is now reachable — the rig in §1.2 exists and is validated. Performance cost ≈ 0 at shipped defaults, because the cache it would invalidate is already disabled there (§3.1, measured).
Risk: low and mostly confined. No cull, sort, Jacobian, near/far or shadow impact (§3). It repairs a known misregistration (§3.2). Residual unknowns: tile_resolve.glsl:276 (§3.3), and the cache-key edit touches a path used by every frame.
User sees: on a static camera, FSR2 swimming gone and splats finally antialiased by TAA/FSR2 like the meshes. On a moving camera they still ghost — no motion vectors. The §8 disclosure is still required, shorter.
Option C — mark splats temporally excluded (reactive mask)
Cost: medium, and it has no TAA analogue — taa_resolve.glsl has no reactive input, so it fixes FSR2 and does nothing for TAA.
Risk: a quality trade, not a fix. A fully reactive region gets no temporal accumulation, so splats stay aliased by construction, permanently, and at FSR2's low internal resolution visibly softer than today.
User sees: no swimming, but splats permanently rougher than the meshes beside them, with no path to improvement. Rejected — it trades a fixable defect for an unfixable one.
Option D — disclose under §8 only
Cost: a §8.1 row, the missing fourth item in docs/architecture/render-pipeline.md:149-153, and re-deciding GPU-001 follow-up: splat projection is unjittered while feeding temporal consumers (TAA/FSR2/MetalFX) — subpixel mismatch vs meshes #929's §11 entry. R0.
Risk: we ship a measured, cheap-to-fix artefact and tell users not to hit it.
User sees: correct at Godot defaults (use_taa=false, scaling_3d_mode=bilinear, scale 1.0); a documented limitation with a workaround otherwise.
Recommendation: B and D together — land the jitter, and disclose the ghosting that remains. Not D alone, not A for the alpha.
I went into this expecting to recommend D alone, on the reasoning that B cannot remove the disclosure so it is not an alpha-shaped trade. The measurement changed that, in exactly the way I had written down in advance that it would:
The cost objection to B was the static-camera render cache. That cache is already disabled at shipped defaults, and all four measured configs confirm it never engaged (§3.1). B is close to free.
The artefact is no longer inferred. It is measured at 1.09–1.41 mean Δ with 104–136 peak single-channel swings, and attributed to the jitter specifically by a period-8 lock with autocorrelation +1.00 (§1.3), with taa_frame_count ruled out by the TAA control.
The R2 evidence bar for B was unreachable because nothing here could render FSR2. That rig now exists and is validated against a known-SHA optimized binary.
A three-line, self-gating change that removes a measured artefact at ~zero cost, with the evidence to prove it, should be made. It does not discharge the disclosure — moving-camera ghosting needs motion vectors, which do not exist — so D ships alongside it, scoped down to what actually remains. A stays as the post-alpha program.
What would change my mind:
If the maintainer puts TAA or FSR2 explicitly inside the §10.1 alpha envelope. §10.1 says "Forward+, single view" and is silent on temporal stages. If they are in-envelope, disclosure is not available and A becomes alpha work.
If the human visual pass says ~1 LSB mean is imperceptible on real-scan content. Then B is not urgent and D alone is right. That call is §1.6 and is not mine.
If the cache-key edit turns out to be invasive — if carrying the jitter into can_reuse_cached_render cannot be done without perturbing the non-temporal path, B loses its "close to free" property and the balance swings back to D.
If a moving-camera capture shows ghosting so severe that the static-camera fix is cosmetic next to it, B is rearranging deck chairs and the honest answer is D plus a prioritised A.
