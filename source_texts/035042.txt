A good tour starts with a clear scene. In this guide, we'll look at the splat's appearance settings, then remove the floating points above the park. Start with your tour open in the editor. Select 3D Splat on the right. This is the captured three-dimensional asset inside your tour. Position moves the asset along its axes. Scale changes its size. These controls change the scene itself, rather than simply moving your camera. The reveal effect controls how the splat appears when the scene opens. You can switch it on or off and choose an effect from the menu. This is separate from a camera animation, which moves the visitor through the scene. The color section lets you adjust the look of the capture. Presets such as Sunny and Cloudy provide starting points. Compare the appearance against your original scene, and keep the default if it already looks right. Here, we stay with the default. For unwanted geometry, open Edit Splat. The cloudy points above this park are part of the captured asset. Changing the sky background will not remove them. We need to select and delete those points first. Choose Volume box. Move the box into the unwanted region and adjust its dimensions. Look at where the box sits in three-dimensional space, so it surrounds the floating points without cutting through the park below. Choose Add to include the points inside the box in your selection. They turn yellow. Check the highlighted region before choosing Delete. The yellow points disappear, while the park remains. Selection and deletion are separate steps. For the remaining clutter, resize or move the box and repeat. A few controlled passes are easier to inspect than one oversized selection. Keep checking the subject from a useful angle as the space above it clears. When the cleanup looks right, choose Done. The edited asset needs to finish saving and uploading before you continue. Keep the tab open during that step. Once the editor returns, check that the clean scene is the one you expect. The distracting points are gone. Next, you can choose the sky and ground around the remaining capture. Those environment settings finish the surroundings without changing the splat's geometry.
