# An Accelerating Splatting Algorithm Based on Multi-texture Mapping for Volume Rendering

Han Xiao\* De-Gui Xiao**†**

School of Computer and Communication, Hunan University, Chang Sha, 410082, China

## Abstract

Texture-mapping hardware has been successfully exploited for volume rendering. In this paper, we combine splatting method with 2D texture mapping efficiently and propose an algorithm for footprint algorithm based volume rendering accelerated by multi texture mapping. First of all, a regular data set is divided by some texture slices along the primary viewing direction. Then the segmented data are projected to the texture slices plane. Lastly, all these texture slices compose the final image by blending. With our algorithm, the scaled volume data set can be rendered effectively and fast but not degrade the quality of image remarkably.

Keywords: Volume Render, Multi Texture Blending, Splatting, Footprint

## 1 Introduction

Volume visualization is a research area that deals with various techniques for extracting meaningful visual information from abstract and complex volume data. It has been successfully applied into several applications, including medical visualization, computational fluid dynamics, seismology, and natural phenomena simulation. One of the most frequently applied techniques for visualization is direct volume rendering (DVR), which produces 3D rendered images of high quality directly from volume data. DVR techniques obtain a synthetic image of the VoI (volume of interest) directly from the volume data, without any intermediate surface representation. Compared to surface rendering techniques, the major advantage of DVR is that all gray level information which has originally been acquired is kept during the rendering process. Furthermore, DVR allows a combined display of different aspects such as opaque and semitransparent surfaces, cuts and maximum intensity projections.

However, rendering speed has always been a major problem in direct volume rendering, because all regions of the volume may contribute to the image, and because new orientations generally require considerable re-computation. A number of methods offering different combinations of rendering speed versus image quality have been presented. Image-order algorithms like ray casting produce superior quality images, but have to traverse the data structure redundantly. Some object-order methods like footprint method [Westover 1990] and its accelerated algorithms were proposed one after the other. Shear-warp factorization methods have been developed for parallel volume rendering [Lacroute 1994]. Hardware-assisted method has been proposed in recent years and many researchers have developed this technology.

In this paper, by combining the advantages of hardware-assisted method with footprint algorithm, we present a new algorithm, which can achieve much faster rendering speed. It reduces time complexity of depth sorting by dividing volume data and composes the final image through hardware-assisted texture slices blending. These will be described in section 3.2 and 3.3 respectively. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we briefly review related work, and in Section 3 we describe the algorithm in detail. The method of this paper is compared with traditional footprint method and experimental results in Section 4. Finally, in Section 5 we end the paper with conclusion.

## 2 Related Work

Footprint method, introduced by Westover, voxels are traversed in either front-to-back or back-to-front order consistent with a given viewpoint. These algorithms access the volume data in storage order, in such a way that each voxel is visited only once, thus avoiding redundant computation. During the traversal, each voxel is classified and shaded by user-supplied opacity and material transfer functions. Then it is projected into the image plane, and its contribution is accumulated to an image buffer using a projected reconstruction kernel, called a footprint.

Over the years, many computer graphics researchers have developed accelerate splatting algorithms. [Laur and Hanrahan 1991] describe a hierarchical footprint method by using octree to exploit coherence of homogeneous region and to enable progressive refinement during rendering. [Insung 1995] developed an efficient data structure to skip voxels that do not contribution to the rendering image. [Daqing Xue 2005] developed a splatting algorithm that utilizes the new capabilities of vertex programs and the OpenGL extensions on a PC equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce4 display card.

General-purpose texture-mapping hardware has been used in direct volume rendering for several years. With the advances in graphics hardware, the rendering speed has dramatically increased as well as various features that improve image quality, such as lighting and tri-linear interpolation, have become available. Texture mapping is an established rendering technique used to enhance the realism of 3D models. In computer vision, 3D models are typically constructed using multiple images (and possibly range data); their textures are recovered using combinations of appropriately extracted parts of the source images.

## 3.3 Projecting and Blending

The main advantage of splatting(or footprint) method is the storage-order access to the data. In an orthographic view, the footprint of the projected reconstruction kernel for any sample is a constant except for a screen space offset. This allows us to build a footprint function table once and use the table for all samples. In practice, lookup tables for the filter weights avoid excessive arithmetic complexity. In our algorithm, the voxel is projected by using the improved splatting method which can avoid popping and splatting overlay. Compared with the traditional method, we projected the voxels onto the texture slice plane directly other than screen plane. For these purposes, we define the texture slice as follows (Figure 3):

- 1. Every point in texture has been set to be RGBA mode.
- 2. The originalα value of every point is zero.
- 3. Every texture slice parallels to the view plane.
- 4. The number of points in horizontal direction (x axis) and vertical direction (y axis) on all texture slices is equivalent respectively.

Figure 3: A texture slice

In order to get the translucent image, we need to include the opacity channel of every data sample, so we set the points in texture to be RGBA mode in definition 1. In definition 3, we let the texture plane parallel with the screen plane to avoid popping and splatting overlay. The definition 4 can ensure that the number of points on every texture slice is equal. With this definition, the

corresponding points in different slices are blended one by one correctly when blending textures. Projecting to the texture plane is similar to projecting to screen plane. According to the coordinate z(depth) of the voxels, we assume there are many layers which parallel the screen plane (Figure 4). The voxels which have the same belong to the same layer. The sample data in the same layer using footprint to computer the contributions to every point in texture slice. After all texture slices being made, texture blending is the last but key step which composes the final image. In this step, the textures must be mapped onto the polygon surface in the scene firstly. In texture mapping, each polygon vertex is given a point in the texture space, and the graphics system maps values from the texture map onto the polygon surface by interpolating texture coordinates. ## 5 Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, to speed up visualize the medium or large scale data set, we first analyze the time complexity of sorting segmented volume data and then project the voxels to the texture plane instead of screen plane. Lastly, we let the graphics system blend the textures with the help of hard-ware. The experiment expresses that the new splatting volume-rendering method can obviously accelerate speed of volume rendering.

There are several interesting areas for future work. Further experiments and code optimization are necessary for achieving even faster rendering rates. In particular, we hope that nextgeneration hardware will ease some of the current limitations. Another interesting area for future research is parallelization of the algorithm for segmented data as mentioned in section 3.2.
