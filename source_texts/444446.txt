Abstract
Voxel-based segmentation volumes often store a large number of labels and voxels, and the resulting amount of data can make storage, transfer, and interactive visualization difficult. We present a lossless compression technique which addresses these challenges. It processes individual small bricks of a segmentation volume and compactly encodes the labelled regions and their boundaries by an iterative refinement scheme. The result for each brick is a list of labels, and a sequence of operations to reconstruct the brick which is further compressed using rANS-entropy coding. As the relative frequencies of operations are very similar across bricks, the entropy coding can use global frequency tables for an entire data set which enables efficient and effective parallel (de)compression. Our technique achieves high throughput (up to gigabytes per second both for compression and decompression) and strong compression ratios of about 1% to 3% of the original data set size while being applicable to GPU-based rendering. We evaluate our method for various data sets from different fields and demonstrate GPU-based volume visualization with on-the-fly decompression, level-of-detail rendering (with optional on-demand streaming of detail coefficients to the GPU), and a caching strategy for decompressed bricks for further performance improvement.

Introduction
Scientific visualization faces an escalating challenge as datasets continue to grow exponentially in size and complexity. Voxel-based segmentation volumes, which assign discrete labels to individual voxels to represent distinct regions of interest, are fundamental to numerous scientific disciplines including materials science, connectomics, and computational biology. However, these datasets often contain billions of voxels and millions of unique labels, creating substantial bottlenecks in storage, transfer, and interactive visualization.
Three segmentation volumes demonstrating the method's effectiveness: cellular structures (left, 4 GB raw compressed to 112 MB), fiber data (top right, 5.694 GB to 51 MB), and massive cortex data (bottom, 927.7 GB to 13.5 GB), all maintaining interactive frame rates.
This paper presents "Fast Compressed Segmentation Volumes" (CSVs), a lossless compression technique specifically designed for segmentation data that achieves both exceptional compression ratios and real-time GPU-based decompression with adaptive level-of-detail rendering. The approach addresses the critical gap between existing methods that either prioritize compression efficiency at the expense of interactive performance, or provide fast access while sacrificing storage optimization.
Core Methodology
The CSV method operates on a brick-wise basis, partitioning input segmentation volumes into uniform b3b^3b3 bricks where b=2Nb = 2^Nb=2N. The key innovation lies in constructing a multi-resolution pyramid for each brick and encoding label assignments through a carefully designed sequence of operations that exploit spatial coherence and previously encoded information.
The encoding process: a 3D brick is converted into a multi-resolution pyramid, with nodes processed in Morton Z-curve order to generate an operation sequence stored alongside a palette of unique labels.
Multi-Resolution Pyramid Construction
For each brick, a resolution pyramid of log⁡2(b)+1\log_2(b) + 1log2​(b)+1 levels is constructed. The finest level L0L_0L0​ contains the original b3b^3b3 labels, while coarser levels LlL_lLl​ are generated by progressively halving the resolution along each axis. Each voxel in level LlL_lLl​ receives the most frequent label among the 8 corresponding voxels in level Ll−1L_{l-1}Ll−1​. This explicit pyramid generation requires approximately 14% additional temporary memory during encoding but enables efficient adaptive decoding.
Operation Sequence Encoding
The compression core relies on encoding each node's label through one of several operations that reference previously encoded information:
RpR_pRp​ (Parent Reuse): Assigns the parent node's label, highly effective due to pyramid construction
Rx,y,zR_{x,y,z}Rx,y,z​ (Neighbor Reuse): Assigns a label from an adjacent node on the current level
PaP_aPa​ (Palette Advance): Adds the current node's label to a local palette and assigns it
P0P_0P0​ (Palette Back Reference): Assigns the most recently used palette entry
PδP_\deltaPδ​ (Palette Back Reference with Offset): Assigns a palette entry at offset δ\deltaδ (0-15)
Neighbor reuse operations allow labels to be "pulled in" from adjacent nodes, effectively propagating region boundaries during the encoding traversal.
Each node also stores a stop bit indicating whether further operations are needed for its children in finer levels, enabling early termination for homogeneous sub-regions. The encoding prioritizes operations in a fixed order to optimize frequency distributions for subsequent entropy coding.
Entropy Coding with rANS
The sequence of operations (represented as 4-bit nibbles) undergoes further compression using range asymmetric numeral systems (rANS). The method employs two global static frequency tables per dataset: one for interior nodes (LNL_NLN​ to L1L_1L1​) and one for leaf nodes (L0L_0L0​). These tables are pre-computed during a fast pass over a subset of bricks, allowing highly efficient parallel decompression without per-brick overhead.
Interactive Visualization System
The visualization pipeline integrates GPU-based decompression with adaptive level-of-detail caching and optional CPU-to-GPU streaming for datasets exceeding GPU memory.
The visualization system performs brick-wise decompression directly on the GPU, with results cached in a sophisticated multi-resolution memory management system. The required level-of-detail for each brick is dynamically determined based on camera distance, ensuring optimal performance while maintaining visual quality.
For extremely large datasets like the 927.7 GB CORTEX volume, the finest detail level can be stored in CPU memory and streamed to the GPU on demand, allowing interactive exploration of datasets much larger than available GPU memory.
Performance Results
The method demonstrates exceptional performance across multiple metrics and datasets:
Compression Efficiency
The technique achieves outstanding lossless compression ratios:
FIBER dataset: 0.899% (1579×1092×1651 voxels, 16-bit labels)
CELLS dataset: 3.561% (1000³ voxels, high label count)
CORTEX dataset: 1.45% (6144×9216×4096 voxels, 927.7 GB uncompressed)
Operation frequency analysis reveals that parent reuse (RpR_pRp​) and neighbor reuse operations account for ~95% of all operations across datasets, validating the encoding strategy.
Rendering Performance
Interactive frame rates are maintained across all test datasets:
CELLS: 59 FPS (17 ms average frame time)
FIBER: 56 FPS (18 ms average frame time)
CORTEX: 25 FPS (40 ms average frame time) with CPU-GPU streaming
GPU decompression achieves throughput rates of 9.3-10.4 GB/s, enabling real-time brick decompression as needed during rendering.
Comparative Analysis
The method significantly outperforms existing approaches:
8-30× faster compression than Compresso+LZMA while achieving comparable compression ratios
Superior compression ratios compared to general-purpose methods (PNG, HDF5 with gzip)
Brick-wise adaptive decompression capability unavailable in global compression schemes like Compresso+LZMA
Stress test results showing frame time breakdown during fast camera movement with cold cache, demonstrating rapid stabilization after initial decompression peaks.
Significance and Applications
This research addresses a critical bottleneck in scientific visualization by enabling interactive exploration of petascale segmentation volumes on modest hardware. The method's significance extends across several domains:
Scientific Discovery: The ability to interactively explore massive biological structures, materials, and simulation outputs at full resolution accelerates hypothesis generation and pattern recognition in fields like connectomics and materials science.
Data Management: Compression ratios below 2% for typical datasets dramatically reduce storage and transfer requirements, making it more feasible to archive and share large scientific datasets across research collaborations.
Hardware Efficiency: The GPU-centric design leverages modern parallel processing capabilities while the flexible streaming architecture accommodates varying hardware configurations.
Foundation for Future Systems: The open-source release and modular design provide a foundation for next-generation visualization systems, with the brick-wise multi-resolution structure readily extensible to advanced rendering techniques.
The method successfully bridges the traditional trade-off between compression efficiency and interactive performance, offering a balanced solution that meets the stringent requirements of scientific visualization for both data fidelity and real-time exploration capabilities.
