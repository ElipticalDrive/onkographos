Advances in multimodal characterization methods fuel a
of increasing immense hyper-dimensional data sets. Color
5 mapping is employed for conveying higher dimensional data in two6 dimensional (2D) representations for human consumption without relying
7 on multiple projections. How one constructs these color maps, however,
8 critically aﬀects how accurately one perceives data. For simple scalar ﬁelds,
9 perceptually uniform color maps and color selection have been shown to
10 improve data readability and interpretation across research ﬁelds. Here we
11 review core concepts underlying the design of perceptually uniform color
12 maps and extend the concepts from scalar ﬁelds to two-dimensional vector
13 ﬁelds and three-component composition ﬁelds frequently found in
14 materials-chemistry research to enable high-ﬁdelity visualization. We
15 develop the software tools PAPUC and CMPUC to enable researchers to utilize these colorimetry principles and employ
16 perceptually uniform color spaces for rigorously meaningful color mapping of higher dimensional data representations. Last, we
17 demonstrate how these approaches deliver immediate improvements in data readability and interpretation in microscopies and
18 spectroscopies routinely used in discerning materials structure, chemistry, and properties.

170
171 common to use the HSV (hue, saturation, value) color space,
172 which is a mathematically simple transformation of sRGB. The
173 sRGB values for each pixel are determined by mapping the
174 magnitude onto a value, i.e., brightness and angle onto hue.
175 Small vectors appear dark and large vectors appear bright with

177 uniform representation presented here, the vector angle θ is

181 layout as the HSV color wheel (Figure 4b). When visualized in
182 CIELAB colorspace, input vector data are mapped onto the
183 surface of an inverted cone with an axis along L*, cone tip at
184 L* = 0, cone base at L* = 74, and radius of 40 which can be

CONCLUSIONS
336
Using colorimetry to design betterin a scientiﬁc ﬁdelity 337
rather than aesthetic sensedata representations for authentic 338
perception is gaining substantial traction. Yet many of the 339
current oﬀ-the-shelf tools may not cover the more complex 340
data representations with perceptually uniform use of color. 341
The two examples and their implementations in PAPUC and 342
CMPUC we highlighted are only marginally more complex 343
than normal data postprocessing as the software implementa- 344
tion of L*a*b*-sRGB color conversion is trivial. Yet for 345
minimal extra eﬀort, we demonstrate signiﬁcant improvements 346
in interpretability over the common HSV color mapping of 2D 347
vector ﬁelds. Most importantly, the ﬁxed lightness-magnitude 348
mapping no longer hides vectors mapped to blue, and we can 349
ensure uniform angle contrast. Last, within three-component 350
chemical mapping, we demonstrate a fair lightness-concen- 351
tration representation for all three components. We hope, 352
above all else, that our work encourages others to use 353
perceptually uniform color mappings since the community of 354
scientiﬁc researchers all strive for the accurate conveyance of 355
data.
