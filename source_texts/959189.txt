Contributor
Stacked on #246 (modern lighting): its branch is this PR's base. Adds hardware ray tracing through Bevy Solari behind the ray_tracing fidelity area (--ray-tracing modern, an F1 debug-menu checkbox greyed out without ray-query hardware; not part of --mode), with a denoiser of our own so it needs no NVIDIA SDK or DLSS Ray Reconstruction.
What it adds
fog/ray_tracing (native-only raytracing feature on cyrus-render, off for cyrus-web). Solari traces only plain StandardMaterial meshes and shades a deferred G-buffer, so while it runs each opaque world mesh gets stand-ins and the original draw is hidden through render layers. Flats, water, sky and chroma-keyed meshes keep their original shading.
Per source: one traced part (RaytracingMesh3d, never drawn, not emissive) and one drawn part per face brightness (fills the G-buffer). The original adds each face's brightness byte to its ambient light (preservation engine/model-lighting.md), so drawn parts give off albedo × brightness / 255, visible where seen but lighting nothing else. Solari picks lights uniformly among all emissive meshes, so making the stand-ins themselves emissive would starve the sun of samples.
Stand-ins are reconciled only for sources whose transform, mesh, visibility, layers or material changed. They follow a posed actor's swapped mesh asset.
Lights use feat(render): modern PBR lighting, effects, unfiltered textures and day/night #246's calibration (sun π/exposure, environment 1/exposure, tonemapping off). Object lights are emissive spheres of the raster point lights' power. The environment is scaled by 4 while traced, because traced sky light is occluded. feat(render): modern PBR lighting, effects, unfiltered textures and day/night #246's day/night cycle moves and tints the traced sun and ambient too.
Path-traced reference: an F1 checkbox under Ray tracing, or control ray_tracing_reference {enabled}, swaps the real-time lighting for Solari's path tracer, which converges while the camera is still.
Vendored bevy_solari (dev/vendor/bevy_solari via [patch.crates-io]) with a BLAS compaction fix (a replaced mesh was prepared twice) and the denoiser (SolariLighting::denoise, realtime/denoise.wesl). It divides the albedo out, accumulates against a reprojected history validated by the previous depth and normals (up to 48 frames), runs three edge-stopping à-trous passes (1, 2, 4 px) and multiplies the albedo back in. History is dropped on camera jumps (more than 5 units or 0.8 rad) and world changes.
Changes in #246's files (kept small)
modern_lighting/plugin.rs: lit stands down while Solari traces.
modern_lighting/lights.rs, day_night.rs: helpers and components made pub(in crate::fog), so the traced lights reuse them.
clipping.rs, water.rs: world-space geometry follows lighting Modern or active ray tracing (ray_tracing::world_space_geometry).
fog.rs, effects.rs, preparation.rs: the materials' modern-shader flag (lighting.x) uses the same world_space_geometry test, so keyed meshes (not traced, still drawn by their material) get the branch that fits the world-space geometry while Lighting is Faithful.
fog.rs: scene preparation skips the stand-ins.
modern_lighting/lights.rs (placed): object lights with a negative colour channel are kept, with that channel clamped to zero. The faithful lighting adds negative channels (subtracting them), so dropping the light was a bug; it left ISLAND's lanterns unlit in both modern tiers.
day_night.rs: the default speed is 1 in-game minute per real second (a 24-minute day) instead of 30 (a 48-second day).
modern_lighting.rs, modern_lighting/plugin.rs: a CameraHandover set pair (Teardown before Setup), so the raster camera's teardown and the traced camera's setup cannot drop Hdr in the same frame (crash when switching raster → traced).
water.rs, terrain.rs: modern waves stay in the original 16-cell wave window (fading over its outer 4 cells), and ticks inside an unchanged window rewrite only those vertices; terrain meshes no camera sees stay main-world only. feat(render): modern PBR lighting, effects, unfiltered textures and day/night #246's GPU waves (ray_tracing::shader_waves) stand down while traced, since Solari's stand-ins are built from the CPU terrain mesh.
animation.rs: posed meshes no camera layer sees are main-world only (PrivateMesh), so Bevy stops re-uploading them every frame.
Verification
GPU (RTX 4070, Vulkan, release build, --scene ISLAND --volume 0, 1600×900, fixed flycam view at the harbour; screenshots kept locally):
Solari and denoise.wesl compile and run with wgpu validation on and no errors.
Denoiser off vs on: mean frame-to-frame luminance change 2.4 → 0.3, high-frequency energy 9.2 → 3.5. The user's own screenshot shows a clean image whose shadows were too dark, which led to the ambient gain.
After the merge with feat(render): modern PBR lighting, effects, unfiltered textures and day/night #246's rework, shaded walls and street measure 20–47 with gain 4, against 24–43 for the raster tier.
Book menu and back, with a 12 s menu: brightness and flicker identical before and after. The earlier "noise after the menu" repro came from another session driving the same control port.
cargo clippy -D warnings for render (with and without raytracing), script, debug, control, mcp and cyrus.
Unit and behaviour tests: tier selection, world-space condition, stand-in layout, brightness grouping, emission, the stand-in sync, light power matching, view-jump reset, query disjointness.
publish-check and genmap.
Not verified yet
The last three changes have not run on a GPU: the self-lit drawn parts, change-detected reconciliation, and day/night following. The ambient gain may need lowering once the self-lit term is visible.
The path-traced reference has not been run.
Frame rates: the only measurement after the merge ran while other workloads shared the GPU (1–3 fps for every mode), so it says nothing. Before the merge, ray tracing cost about 25% over the faithful renderer at 1600×900.
Interior, torch-lit worlds have not been checked.
Fallback without ray-query hardware is covered by tests only. The area then selects the raster tier or faithful.
Not covered
The denoiser treats all light as diffuse, so there are no ray-traced reflections. Water keeps its original shader.
Animated meshes have no motion vectors, so they fall back to little history.
