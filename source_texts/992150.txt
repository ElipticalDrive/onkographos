Model: Opus for the surface shaders and the tuning of the grip; the models, where the look needs a shape, by Blender scripts (as the lamp, the fence and the gates: tools/blender/). The look of the new blocks needs the owner's approval before it goes into the game. Follows #55 (the review of the classic blocks: the road kit) and stands on #58 (the foundation: the procedural surface shader, the style of a block, the transitions and the grip of a surface).
What
A third graphic style of the track blocks, the off-road track: a dirt or field track with grass along its edges, in the place of the light-blue slab road with hazard stripes of the classic blocks (content/Levels/mod/, 2016).
The style has the collision shape, size, profile and turning of its classic block, so that any level can be played in either style and a level only changes how it looks and how the tyres grip. It has a much higher level of detail than the classic blocks. The machinery (the surface shader, the style per block, the transition mechanism, the grip of a surface) is built in #58; this issue is the look and the numbers of the off-road style.
The look
Ideas for the owner to choose from or replace (the models are shown to the owner before anything goes in):
Surface: packed earth in a few browns, with two wheel ruts, a lighter crown in the middle, puddles or dark damp patches, loose gravel and small stones, here and there a flat rock.
Edges: grass along both sides, with tufts and clumps, the grass thinning towards the track and the bare earth showing at the edge; a low bank or a ditch at the side instead of the dark slab of today. The side of a block that shows under the road (a slope, a jump, a step) is earth or a cut bank, not a black slab.
Slopes and jumps: the climbs and the lips as an earth ramp with deeper ruts and a worn crest; the stairs as a rocky step or a log.
Markings: none of the hazard stripes; the start and the finish as a field gate, a tape between two posts or a rope with flags, and the pits as real pits with earth edges.
Colours: earth, grass and stones must stay readable from the chase camera, which is far away, and the car (light colours, the palette of Garage: choose the colours of the cars (primary and secondary) and of the rims #46) must stand out against them; details thinner than a few pixels only cost triangles, so the detail has to be in big readable shapes and colour changes.
Needs
The off-road surfaces in the shader, on the foundation of Foundation for the track styles: procedural surface shader, style per block, transitions and surface grip #58 (the model parts name their surface by the material name in the MTL): the earth with its ruts and a berm beside them (as RoverTracks.fxh of BS3D), the gravel and stones, the grass (Grass.fxh). The ruts must run on from one block to the next. What a shader cannot give is a silhouette: tufts of grass and stones that stick out of the edge of the road are shapes. Say in the first renders which parts of the look the shader draws and which the geometry. Grass along a long road is then the heaviest part: it may be a shape repeated in rows, or part of the scenery props (content/Scenery/) instead of the block.
The road kit. The blocks that each style must have, the list from Review the track blocks: is the classic set complete enough for more varied levels? #55: the flat road, the slopes, the smooth slopes, the lips, the stairs, the pits, the start and the finish and whatever else Review the track blocks: is the classic set complete enough for more varied levels? #55 puts in. Blocks that only exist in the classic style (the tunnels, the decoration) stay as they are, and the editor and the game must work with a level that mixes them with the off-road track. An off-road block is the classic model with other materials, or a model of its own where the look needs a shape; each replaces nothing (the classic models stay), with a /build line in content/Content.mgcb; its collision is shared with the classic block (the test of Foundation for the track styles: procedural surface shader, style per block, transitions and surface grip #58), so the bumps, the stones and the grass must not change it.
Transition blocks of its pairs. The look of the transitions between the off-road and the classic style (the mechanism and the rules are in Foundation for the track styles: procedural surface shader, style per block, transitions and surface grip #58): the earth packs into the classic slab, the grass thins out and ends, the stripes begin; they turn, so one block serves both directions. The transition between the racing and the off-road style is made by whichever of Racing track style of the track blocks: detailed asphalt and edges, inspired by Formula 1 circuits #56 and Off-road track style of the track blocks: a dirt or field track with grass along its edges #57 finishes second.
The grip of the earth. The friction of the off-road surface (earth, gravel, grass) and the grip of each of the three wheels on it, tuned in test drives on the mechanism of Foundation for the track styles: procedural surface shader, style per block, transitions and surface grip #58: the Off-road and Rock crawler wheels are to win, the Rock crawler wheel (tall, wide and knobby) best on loose ground, and the Racing wheel slides but is not hopeless. The owner approves the numbers; docs/playing.md and docs/content.md describe them.
The editor and the catalog page: the previews and the pictures of the catalog page (docs/catalog/, tools/CatalogRender) show the off-road look, and docs/content.md, docs/levels.md and tools/levelgen/README.md describe the style.
Approval: before it goes in, show the owner renders of two or three variants of the earth and the grass from the camera of the game, by day, at sunset and at night, on a flat road, on a slope and at a jump, next to the classic block.
Done when
Style: every block of the road kit has an off-road look (a model, or the classic model with other materials) with the collision shape, size, profile and turning of its classic block, approved by the owner.
Transitions: a road passes from the classic style into the off-road one and back by transition blocks (flat at least) with no visible seam; the ruts run on through the transition.
Grip: the off-road surface has its own grip, every wheel grips differently on it, and the numbers are approved by the owner from test drives.
Playable: a shipped level drawn in the off-road style has the same collision as in the classic one, and the tests pass.
Frame rate: the levels with the longest roads keep it on the PC, on the phone and on the Raspberry Pi, with the surface shader at its default level.
Surfaces: the off-road surfaces work on every target (DesktopGL, Android, the Pi) with no seams between blocks and no shimmering in the distance.
Not in this issue
The machinery (#58), the racing style (#56), the tunnels, gates and decoration in an off-road look unless #55 puts them in the road kit, new block shapes (those are #40), a different sound or dust of the surface per style, and the scenery of the countryside (trees, fences, hay) beyond what the grass edge needs.
