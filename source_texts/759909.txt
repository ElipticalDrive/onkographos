Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time
photorealistic novel view synthesis. However, its training-time densiﬁcation remains largely heuristic, often resulting in redundant Gaussian proliferation and
inaccurate geometric reconstruction. Existing methods predominantly rely on ﬁrstorder screen-space gradients to trigger cloning and splitting, which fail to distinguish true geometric discontinuities from high-frequency textures or noisy residuals. To address this limitation, we propose ACD-GS, an Asymmetric Curvatureaware Densiﬁcation framework for compact and geometry-aware 3D Gaussian
optimization. Our key insight is that second-order depth responses provide a
more reliable structural prior for density control, enabling asymmetric regulation
of cloning and splitting to better capture geometric discontinuities. Building on
this, we introduce a multi-view photometric consistency constraint to suppress
transient single-view errors, together with a capacity-aware adaptive regulation
strategy that balances densiﬁcation and pruning during training. Unlike post-hoc
compression or quantization-based approaches, ACD-GS directly prevents redundant Gaussian generation during training, yielding inherently compact representations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method reduces nearly 60%
of Gaussian count and storage while maintaining competitive rendering ﬁdelity,
consistently outperforming existing methods across multiple datasets. Our code is
available in https://waveviewer.github.io/ACD-GS/.

Introduction
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [1] has rapidly emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time photorealistic novel view synthesis. Unlike implicit neural radiance ﬁelds, 3DGS represents a scene as an
explicit collection of anisotropic Gaussian primitives and renders them through differentiable rasterization, achieving both high rendering ﬁdelity and remarkable efﬁciency. Beneﬁting from its explicit
representation and fast optimization, 3DGS has inspired extensive advances in anti-aliasing [2–4],
surface reconstruction [5–9], dynamic scene modeling [10–14], compression [15–19], and hybrid
implicit-explicit representations [20–25]. Despite these advances, the efﬁciency and scalability of
3DGS still critically depend on how Gaussian primitives are created and evolved during optimization.
In standard 3DGS, densiﬁcation is mainly driven by accumulated screen-space positional gradients,
where Gaussians are cloned or split once the gradient magnitude exceeds predeﬁned thresholds.

While simple and effective in practice, this mechanism remains largely geometry-agnostic, as ﬁrstorder gradients primarily reﬂect local optimization pressure rather than underlying scene structure.
Consequently, regions with high-frequency textures, smoothly varying surfaces, and sharp geometric boundaries may all produce similarly large gradients despite requiring fundamentally different
density-control behaviors. A ﬂat textured wall, for example, should avoid excessive Gaussian proliferation, whereas thin structures and depth discontinuities require localized geometric reﬁnement.
This reveals an intrinsic ambiguity in gradient-driven densiﬁcation, where ﬁrst-order photometric
signals alone are insufﬁcient for reliable density control.
This ambiguity leads to a persistent trade-off in existing densiﬁcation strategies [26–30]. Aggressive
thresholds tend to trigger excessive Gaussian growth in textured regions, resulting in redundant
primitives, increased storage overhead, unstable optimization, and ﬂoating artifacts near foregroundbackground transitions. Conversely, conservative thresholds often fail to reconstruct ﬁne structures
and sharp depth discontinuities. Recent approaches attempt to improve densiﬁcation stability by
incorporating pixel-aware gradients [27], absolute gradient magnitudes [28], or gradient-direction
statistics [29, 30]. Although these methods alleviate optimization instability to some extent, they
still rely primarily on ﬁrst-order, single-view photometric signals and therefore remain insufﬁcient
for disentangling true geometric complexity from texture-induced residuals. As a result, existing
methods struggle to simultaneously achieve compact representations, geometric ﬁdelity, and stable
optimization. These limitations motivate the need for a geometry-aware density-control prior that
directly reﬂects scene structure rather than relying solely on photometric optimization cues.
In this work, we propose ACD-GS, an Asymmetric Curvature-aware Densiﬁcation framework that
reformulates Gaussian densiﬁcation as a geometry-aware optimization process. Our key insight is
that second-order depth responses provide a more reliable structural cue for identifying geometric discontinuities than ﬁrst-order screen-space gradients. Compared with gradient magnitude alone,
depth curvature is more sensitive to local geometric changes such as occlusion boundaries, thin structures, and high-curvature regions, while remaining less responsive to purely planar textured areas.
Based on this observation, ACD-GS introduces an asymmetric densiﬁcation mechanism that explicitly decouples cloning and splitting behaviors according to curvature-aware structural priors. Specifically, cloning is suppressed near depth discontinuities to avoid ambiguous foreground-background
duplication and ﬂoating artifacts, whereas splitting is promoted to reﬁne boundary-crossing Gaussians into ﬁner geometric primitives.
To further resolve the ambiguity between geometry and appearance, we introduce a multi-view photometric error gating strategy that enforces cross-view consistency instead of relying on volatile
single-view residuals. Furthermore, we design a capacity-constrained adaptive optimization scheme
that dynamically regulates primitive growth and lifecycle-aware pruning throughout training. Together, these designs establish a uniﬁed geometry-aware density-control framework that enables
native sparsiﬁcation, where redundant Gaussian primitives are prevented during training rather than
removed through post-hoc compression or quantization pipelines. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ACD-GS reduces nearly 60% of Gaussian primitives and storage while maintaining competitive rendering ﬁdelity across multiple datasets, consistently achieving a favorable quality and
compactness trade-off compared with existing methods.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:

Related Work
2.1
3D Gaussian Splatting Representation
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [1] represents a scene as anisotropic Gaussian primitives and renders
them through differentiable rasterization. Its explicit formulation has inspired extensions for antialiasing [2–4], surface reconstruction [5–9], dynamic scenes [10–14], and appearance modeling [31–
34]. Hybrid representations such as Scaffold-GS [20], SplatNet [23], and Octree-GS [21] further
organize primitives with learned features or hierarchical structures, while implicit ﬁelds [35–38]
provide complementary scene parameterizations. These methods mainly change representation or
rendering; the number and placement of Gaussians still depend on training-time density control,
which is the focus of this work. Because all of them retain a primitive-based rendering pipeline,
their efﬁciency and ﬁdelity are also affected by how many Gaussians remain after optimization.
2.2
Gradient-Driven Density Control
Standard 3DGS [1] densiﬁes Gaussians using accumulated screen-space positional gradients. PixelGS [27] accounts for pixel coverage, AbsGS [28] uses absolute gradients to reduce cancellation,
and GDAGS [29] incorporates gradient-direction information; other methods add external constraints [26, 30]. FastGS [39] instead estimates Gaussian importance from multi-view reconstruction
errors inside projected footprints and uses this score for both densiﬁcation and pruning. ACD-GS
shares the use of multi-view evidence but assigns it a different role: rendered-depth curvature modulates cloning and splitting, while center-projected RGB residuals only verify whether a candidate is
consistently under-reconstructed across views. This separates structural guidance from photometric
veriﬁcation and avoids using a footprint error score as the direct densiﬁcation criterion.
2.3
3DGS Compression
Post-hoc compression reduces Gaussian storage through signiﬁcance-based pruning and vector quantization [40, 41, 15, 42–44], while context models and entropy coding target rate–distortion efﬁciency [17, 18, 45–47]. Mini-Splatting [48] studies compact representations under constrained
primitive budgets. These approaches mainly operate after or around training and may require codebooks or ﬁne-tuning. ACD-GS instead reduces redundant primitive generation during optimization,
without a post-hoc quantization stage. Because it changes where capacity is allocated rather than
how parameters are encoded, it can be combined with downstream compression; this combination
is not evaluated here. This distinction also delimits the scope of our experiments, which measure
training-time sparsiﬁcation rather than gains from entropy coding or quantization.

4
Experiments
Experimental Setup. We evaluate ACD-GS on the Mip-NeRF 360 [49], Tanks & Temples [50], and
Deep Blending [51] datasets against vanilla 3DGS [1] and representative baselines including MipSplatting [2], Mini-Splatting [48], AbsGS [28], GDAGS [29], and Pixel-GS [27]. All models are
trained for 30K iterations on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. Our curvature-aware density control
operates every 100 steps from iteration 600 until training completion to strictly regulate primitive
proliferation. We report rendering metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and efﬁciency statistics (storage
size, Gaussian count, training time). Further implementation details are provided in the Appendix.

Trade-off Advantage. The key advantage of ACD-GS lies in its favorable quality and compactness trade-off. Recent density-control methods, such as Pixel-GS, often improve rendering quality by introducing excessive numbers of primitives, reaching 5.56M Gaussians on Mip-NeRF 360.

storage–quality plot in Fig. 1, ACD-GS occupies the most favorable region of the trade-off space,
achieving competitive ﬁdelity with substantially lower storage. This demonstrates that our method
effectively separates geometrically necessary densiﬁcation from texture-induced or view-speciﬁc
noise, leading to a compact yet expressive Gaussian representation.

Qualitative Comparison
Visual Rendering Fidelity. As shown in Fig. 3, ACD-GS achieves novel view synthesis quality
that is highly comparable to, and occasionally surpasses, the unconstrained vanilla 3DGS model.
Gradient-based baselines like AbsGS and GDAGS frequently suffer from texture degradation to
maintain a lower point count, our method consistently preserves high-frequency details. Crucially,
vanilla 3DGS is notoriously prone to generating severe ﬂoating artifacts in empty spaces (visibly
evident in the sky region of the Tree scene) due to its geometric blindness. In contrast, by rigorously
suppressing redundant densiﬁcation at its source, ACD-GS effectively eliminates these ﬂoaters and
reconstructs remarkably clean backgrounds and sharp object boundaries.

4.5
Sensitivity Analysis
We additionally evaluate the number of auxiliary views K and the candidate quota ratio µ on the
complete Deep Blending dataset. Increasing K from 4 to 8 improves PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, but
retains more Gaussians; increasing K to 12 provides no consistent quality beneﬁt and increases the
model size, so we use K = 8. For the quota ratio, µ = 0.004 yields a marginally smaller model,
whereas µ = 0.008 achieves higher PSNR and SSIM with the same LPIPS; µ = 0.012 provides no
additional quality improvement. We therefore use µ = 0.008 as the default.
5
Discussion and Limitations
While ACD-GS achieves signiﬁcant native sparsiﬁcation, extreme compression inevitably degrades
high-frequency texture ﬁdelity due to the inherent capacity limits of pure explicit representations. To
address this bottleneck, future work could explore hybrid architectures, replacing explicit spherical
harmonics with lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to maintain rendering quality under
stricter point budgets. Furthermore, because ACD-GS operates orthogonally to post-hoc compression pipelines, seamlessly integrating it with downstream quantization and entropy coding frameworks presents a promising direction to unlock ultra-compact assets for low-bandwidth streaming.
6
Conclusion
We present ACD-GS, an Asymmetric Curvature-aware Densiﬁcation framework for compact 3D
Gaussian Splatting. Second-order depth priors decouple cloning and splitting, suppressing unnecessary cloning in planar regions while promoting splitting along geometric boundaries. A multi-view
photometric gate ﬁlters transient noise, and adaptive thresholding with lifecycle-aware pruning regulates capacity. Experiments show that ACD-GS reduces Gaussian count and storage by nearly
60% across benchmarks while preserving competitive rendering ﬁdelity. By directly preventing redundancy during training, ACD-GS offers a practical, memory-efﬁcient foundation for scalable 3D
Gaussian representations.

Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis
We further evaluate the sensitivity of ACD-GS to three key hyperparameters, including the soft
threshold τ , the capacity-control parameters (α, β), and the rate of lifecycle-aware opacity pruning.
As shown in Tables A1–A3, ACD-GS exhibits stable rendering quality across different hyperparameter settings, while the representation size and the number of Gaussians vary more noticeably. For
the soft threshold τ , all tested settings achieve comparable average PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, indicating that the proposed soft modulation is not overly sensitive to the exact threshold value. The
default setting τ = 0.15 provides a balanced trade-off, maintaining competitive rendering quality
while yielding fewer Gaussians on average than the alternatives.
For the capacity-control parameters (α, β), the default conﬁguration (0.8, 1.5) consistently produces the most compact representations across almost all scenes. Compared with both smaller and
larger parameter settings, it substantially reduces the average storage and Gaussian count while
preserving the best average PSNR and SSIM. This suggests that moderate capacity regulation is
important: overly conservative control may retain redundant primitives, whereas overly aggressive
densiﬁcation-pruning schedules can increase representation size without clear quality gains.
Finally, varying the lifecycle-aware pruning rate leads to only minor changes in reconstruction quality. The average metrics remain very close across different rates, demonstrating that the pruning
schedule is robust within a reasonable range. Although a smaller rate can slightly reduce the average model size, the default rate achieves the best overall PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, indicating
a favorable balance between compactness and ﬁdelity. Overall, these results show that ACD-GS
is robust to hyperparameter variations and that the chosen default conﬁguration provides a stable
quality-efﬁciency trade-off across diverse scenes.
