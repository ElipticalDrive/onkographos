The digital revolution has introduced us to groundbreaking technologies that are reshaping how we interact with visual data. Among these innovations, 3D volumetric displays stand out as a futuristic marvel, promising immersive visual experiences that transcend traditional 2D screens. From entertainment and medicine to military and retail, these displays are steadily gaining traction. But what exactly are 3D volumetric displays? How are they being used, and how big is their market? Let’s delve into these questions.
What Are 3D Volumetric Displays?
3D volumetric displays create dynamic three-dimensional images in physical space, allowing viewers to see objects from any angle without requiring special glasses or VR headsets. Unlike flat screens that simulate 3D using perspective or shading, volumetric displays project real 3D visuals into a defined volume, enabling a more lifelike representation.
These displays work using techniques like:
Light Field Displays: Use light to project visuals in 3D space.
Digital Holography: Generate holographic images that seem to float in mid-air.
Rotating LED Arrays or Voxels: Build visuals using physical points in a 3D space.
Applications Across Industries
The versatility of 3D volumetric displays makes them a sought-after solution in multiple fields. Here’s how they are transforming industries:
1. Healthcare
One of the most impactful applications is in healthcare, where 3D displays enhance medical imaging. Surgeons can use these displays to visualize complex anatomical structures or plan surgeries with precision. For example:
EchoPixel’s True3D technology allows doctors to interact with CT and MRI scans as floating 3D models. This facilitates better pre-surgical planning and patient communication.
3D displays are also used in training, helping medical students understand intricate bodily systems more effectively.
2. Entertainment
From futuristic movies to gaming, volumetric displays are redefining entertainment:
In 2019, Voxon Photonics introduced volumetric displays for gaming, offering a “holographic arcade” experience. Players could interact with truly 3D games that move and evolve in real space.
Concerts and live performances have adopted 3D volumetric projections, as seen in events featuring holograms of artists like Tupac Shakur and Whitney Houston.
3. Retail and Advertising
Retailers are embracing 3D displays to captivate customers. Imagine walking into a store and seeing a product demo as a floating 3D hologram:
Hypervsn’s holographic displays are being used in malls and trade shows to create stunning, head-turning advertisements.
Jewelry stores, for instance, can showcase floating 3D models of high-value items, enabling customers to examine them virtually before purchase.
4. Military and Defense
Military applications are a high-growth area for volumetric displays:
Voxon Photonics has developed displays that visualize battlefield simulations in 3D, helping strategists make informed decisions in real-time.
These systems also assist in drone operations by projecting real-time 3D data of landscapes.
5. Automotive Design
Car manufacturers like BMW and Ford are using volumetric displays to design and review prototypes in real time. This reduces the time and cost associated with traditional modeling while ensuring design accuracy.
Market Size and Growth
The 3D volumetric display market is on an upward trajectory, driven by advancements in display technology, growing demand for immersive experiences, and applications across diverse industries.
Key Numbers
According to market reports, the global volumetric display market was valued at approximately US$ 867.4 million in 2024.
It is projected to grow at a CAGR of 13.8 %, reaching a market size of over US$ 1.89 billion by 2030

Drivers of Growth
Healthcare Advancements: The increasing use of 3D imaging in diagnostics and surgery.
Immersive Gaming and Entertainment: Consumer demand for enhanced experiences.
Military Investments: Adoption of volumetric displays in training and simulation.
Corporate R&D: Companies investing in next-gen product design using 3D displays.
Challenges
Despite their promise, volumetric displays face certain hurdles:
Cost: The technology is expensive, limiting accessibility for smaller businesses.
Limited Resolution: Current displays often struggle with fine details.
Power Consumption: High power requirements can make scaling a challenge.
Fragility: Advanced components are prone to damage, necessitating robust handling.
Future Trends
Looking ahead, the future of 3D volumetric displays is bright:
Miniaturization: Researchers are working on compact, portable volumetric displays.
Enhanced Resolution: Efforts to improve clarity and fidelity will open new possibilities in medical imaging and entertainment.
AI Integration: AI-driven interaction with 3D models could revolutionize fields like education and training.
Real-World Example: Voxon’s Impact
Voxon Photonics, an Australian company, is a pioneer in the field. Their volumetric displays are being used in schools to teach complex STEM concepts through interactive 3D visualization. A biology teacher, for instance, can show a heart beating in 3D, enabling students to explore it from all angles without needing VR headsets.
