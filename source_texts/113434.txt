Voxel planet: chunk trees, a fully destructible planet, and the lighting to show it
Goal: voxel terrain of 0.1–1 m cells on planets, planes and infinite planes. Three requirements:
No visible refinement from the ground to orbit, at any speed.
Any part of a world can be dug, up to the whole planet.
Terrain costs under 6 ms of GPU time at 1440p on an RTX 3060.
The design is in crates/passes/3d/helio-pass-voxel-planet/docs/chunk-trees.md. Why span columns were replaced, and the measurements behind it, are in docs/aaa-architecture.md.
Chunk trees (replace span columns)
Data: a planet-wide octree of chunk trees (64-trees of 4³ leaves). Leaves are stored in pages any chunk can use, with surface and subsurface materials, so there is no fragmentation.
Generation: on the GPU, once per chunk, within the frame's budget. The budget follows both the GPU and the CPU, and grows into what the frame leaves. Cells no cave can carve are skipped.
Residency: a screen-space cut of the octree that follows an eye from anywhere.
The drawn cut covers the whole world, so there are no checker holes.
Tiles out of view are a level coarser.
A camera dropped from orbit refines in a few frames.
A tile still refining reads as ground, not as boxes.
Motion-aware detail: where the eye's motion sweeps a tile's image by flow pixels a frame, its cells may cover 1 + flow / 16 pixels, up to 3. The view's centre, a still camera and turns keep one pixel.
Rendering:
Chunk boxes are rasterized and culled on the GPU into the seed's draw list. Rays run only inside one chunk.
A chunk's box takes the slope of its surface.
Levels crossfade into each other.
Rays continue across cube face edges.
From level 13 on, rays walk the grid's own cells (planes and spheres about the centre), so tiles and faces are watertight from orbit.
Camera-relative frames:
The frame's origin snaps to 1,024 m cells.
Motion vectors stay right when the origin moves.
On a host's shared device the pass never polls it.
Edits
Brushes run from one voxel to planet scale.
A big edit shows at once, coarse first, then refines.
Edits apply in layers: recent brushes exact, older small ones sealed into bricks, large ones analytic.
An edit tree keeps the surfaces edits leave, not their whole history.
Saves store the layers bit-exact, and memory stays bounded.
Blasts regenerate within the frame's budget.
Generation
Two-rate field: continent- and range-wide octaves are evaluated at lattice nodes and blended.
Column programs: each level's columns run a compiled column program instead of walking the octave table.
Speed: 41–43 µs per tile before, 32–34 after (docs/two-rate-field.md). Results stay bit-exact between CPU and GPU.
Erosion: erosion octaves step their corners and share steering.
Reproducible benchmark: GEN_BENCH gives deterministic generation timings.
Look
Shading at a distance: voxels under a pixel are lit by the heightfield's exact slope, which each leaf stores (leaf_normals). Contour stripes from orbit and bands on mountainsides are gone.
Material boundaries: where cells are a pixel or less, maria, snowlines and ejecta are filtered by a jittered material lookup that TSR averages.
Sun and sky: sun shadows and sky visibility are traced through the chunk trees at any distance. Rays rising over every solid cell stop there.
Lighting and post-processing (engine-wide)
Reversed-Z depth with no far plane: every pass reads and tests depth this way, and depth is precise to parts in 10⁸ of the distance.
Sky light from each surface's own sky: the atmosphere pass builds a sky irradiance table (Bruneton-style).
It covers 16 altitudes by 24 sun cosines, with 6 symmetric L2 harmonics per entry in the local frame.
It is published as the uniform atmosphere_sky_table, and deferred lighting looks up each pixel's own altitude and sun angle.
Ground seen from orbit keeps its sky fill, and terrain past the terminator is no longer lit by the eye's daylight. DeferredLight costs 0.60 ms.
Exposure: a 64-bin histogram of log luminance, which excludes sky and space. It averages the 10th to 90th percentiles.
Grading:
Contrast is applied in log2 around middle grey, and saturation around luminance.
The LUT applies after the tone map, so it no longer clips HDR.
White balance is Bradford from the Planckian colour temperature, and 6,500 K changes nothing.
World component (helio_component)
One description: VoxelTerrainComponent is the whole world, in five categories:
World: shape, radius or size, voxel size;
Generation: generator, seed, settings;
Environment: air;
Appearance;
Editing: editable, brush journal.
Old fields removed: the chunk-domain fields a world never used are gone. Old levels still load, and saving drops the fields.
Air: Custom, None, Earthlike, Thin or Dense, with density, sky tint and haze.
AtmosphereComponent::for_air derives the sky on the world's planet.
None means no sky, so a moon is airless.
Blueprints: every setting has a getter and a set_* method.
World cache: cached CPU worlds are keyed by scene and entity.
Merged with main
Main's lens pass and buffer view read depth the reversed way: helio_view_depth(proj, d), helio_is_sky, and helio_depth_nearest for a footprint's nearest texel.
The lens tests write the engine's own depth.
Main's removal of post-process depth of field and chromatic aberration wins.
The WGSL validation walk fills both of the fog shader's placeholders.
Leftovers deleted
helio-voxel-data: the chunk-batch inbox and publication worker, the generation worker and the generator registry. What remains is a voxel object's chunks, the brush journal, and the shared names (ids.rs).
Voxel planet:
span-column level windows (local_outer_radius, edited_top), column_kinds, FaceFrame, time_field, pixel_trace_walks, and the block slope stencil (CPU and WGSL);

Voxel edits: saved as layers, undoable seals, bounded memory
…
f8c3890
```
Persistence: a planet's edits snapshot as their layers (large brushes,
baked bricks of every level, recent brushes, history hash), keyed by grid
geometry since baked edits do not depend on the terrain under them. An
edit journal can start from such a base: saving a level folds all but its
latest 4096 edits into it, and loading builds the world from the base and
the listed tail instead of replaying every brush.

Undo: each of the latest 32 seals keeps the columns it replaced, so undo
reaches 2048 brushes past the recent window without rebuilding the world.

Memory: brush history, seal notices and undo data are bounded windows;
the baked store is keyed by column (a column's bricks are one lookup,
not a shard scan), and journals jump to a start index instead of walking.
Renderers sync baked changes from a bounded change list and fully
refresh only when they fell behind it.
```

Voxel terrain: a Cliffs layer of escarpments
…
024bf4c
```
Inside regions of `scale_km` covering `coverage`, the layers before it
step into terraces `height_m` tall (whole layers, so treads lie on cell
boundaries), joined by cliffs over the last `ratio` of each step. Integer
terracing in 32 bits, identical on CPU (`landform::terrace`) and GPU
(`landform_terrace`); the riser share rides in the stack layer's kind
bits. Bounds: a terrace moves a height by less than a step and is not
Lipschitz, so each level's margin grows by two steps.

Tests: CPU and GPU fields bit-identical with cliffs on a sphere and both
planes; on land columns of an Earth with cliffs ~88% of heights sit on a
tread with steep risers between, within the checked bounds; the terrace
function is flat, monotonic and within a step, also below zero. The
editor's layer list offers Cliffs.
```

Voxel edits: a shared brush table, sealing sooner
…
a3e8bf5
```
Heavily sculpted areas vanished from a few hundred metres up and stopped
taking edits. Each column's edit block held its brushes inline (12 words
each), so where hundreds of recent brushes overlapped, every column
repeated them all and the edit buffer filled: columns waited for blocks
forever, and the edit pressure drove the LOD coarser, past the levels
recent brushes draw at. Blocks now reference a shared face brush table
(one word per brush; distinct brushes stored once, counted per block,
freed with their last block; the table grows to a 12 MB budget). Recent
brushes draw only where their cells are finer than them, so the recent
window shrinks to 64 brushes, sealed 16 at a time into baked cells whose
coarse levels show accumulated sculpting from any height; 128 seals stay
undoable (2,112 brushes).

Test: 1,200 spiral digs seen from 60 m to 2.5 km show the pit at every
height, with nothing pending and bounded edit data.
```

Voxel planet: span columns, a fully destructible planet
…
5760707
```
Phases 2-5 of docs/span-columns.md. A column (an 8x8 footprint of one
level) no longer stores one band of bricks with solid below and air
above. Its vertical content is an ordered list of spans: AIR, SOLID,
LANES (each lane uniform: a crater's wall), TOPS (each lane solid below
its own top: a dug floor), NATURAL (the generated ground) and BRICKS
(arbitrary occupancy). Open terrain is one NATURAL span with no table,
as the heightfield columns were.

Generation evaluates cells only in candidate intervals where some lane
can change: around each lane's terrain top and evaluated volume, where
each brush's surface crosses each lane (boxes exactly; balls solved per
lane along the exact slope of its volume points, with an f32 error
margin) and at each baked brick. Between them one exact cell per lane
classifies each gap. The interior of a 2 km dig evaluates its floor,
not thousands of cells of air: that column evaluation was the 25x
generation cost, and the band's air bricks were stepped one by one.

Rays cross an AIR span as one box and a lane span lane by lane (one
radial solve per lane however tall), so carved volume costs like sky.

The natural tops live in the header over a column base (16 bits when
they spread past a byte), independent of the spans: material depth
counts from the generated ground at any depth. A dig 400 m deep showed
grass on its floor near the eye, where the band-relative byte could not
hold the top.

Each level describes a window of 2048 cells around the eye's layer (a
3D clipmap); the coarsest level is never clipped, and columns begin at
the planet's centre (below it a lane's line runs out the antipode).

column_view::ColumnView decodes records as the shaders read them.
Tests: resident_columns_hold_the_canonical_cells audits every fifth
resident column cell by cell against Planet::column_kinds on open
ground, in a pit, on a crater floor 3 km down, and from orbit over a
planet with a 0.8-radius bite (about 1.4 billion cells, none wrong);
destroyed_worlds_render_from_orbit checks the bite pixel by pixel and
a planet removed entirely draws only sky.
```

```
Each lane runs the brushes over its line in order as a union of solid
intervals (a Remove subtracts, an Add unites) and offers only the ends
of what remains, plus brush ends landing within two margins of the
set's boundaries (abutting boxes, slivers). Offering both crossings of
```

```
Every column under a long editor session received every brush over it:
hundreds of overlapping digs filled the GPU's edit words (admission stopped
with 80k columns pending), cost generation ~40x a column and made picks
walk hundreds of brushes per cell.

- EditLog's index is a per-face octree whose leaves keep only the brushes
  that can still change a cell in them: a Remove or Add holding a node
  replaces what came before (but larger brushes), a Remove or Paint inside
  space an earlier Remove emptied is left out. Containment is proven with
  an f64 copy of the volume map and its movement and bend bounds. 400
  overlapping 2 km digs leave ~20 brushes a column (was ~110).
- Columns with the same brush list share one edit block; a job's cost
  counts its brushes.
- Sealing a large brush over baked bricks fills or skips whole bricks.
- Picks return the drawn cell (Planet::drawn_hit); raycast_near is gone.
```

Voxel planet: voxels under a pixel are lit by the slope, at every level
…
b467881
```
Every level lit its cells by their faces, each step shadowing the next.
A coarse level's steps are a cell tall and their shadows long under a
low sun, so a slope read as stripes, and the stripes changed with every
switch between levels: the checkered "cardbox" blocks while climbing or
```

```
Since voxels under a pixel are lit by the slope, most distant hits take
the slope's normal. Where it could not be normalised (no slope across
the cell, or the face's normal opposite it at half weight) it was NaN,

(it only reached the fallback colour), and `=half` colours the left half
beside the shaded right, to line shading up with the cut.
```

```
The budget divided its milliseconds by each frame's average cost a
```

Voxel planet: a two-rate field and compiled column programs
…
cd7a707
```
Generation spent most of a tile on its columns, each walking the whole
octave table and evaluating every octave, the continent- and range-wide
ones included. Per tile (GENBENCH, RTX 3060): 41-43 us before, 32-34 us
now, bit-exact between CPU and GPU (docs/two-rate-field.md).

- Two-rate field: octaves 15 shifts or more above a column's level
  (erosion and craters excepted) are evaluated at nodes, the columns of
  the level whose indices are multiples of 16 on their face, and blended
  bilinearly with exact integer weights (high and low bytes summed
  apart). Ridges carry their noise and gradient; crests are made per
  column. Within 0.14 cells of the single-rate field at level 0, under
  0.1 cells elsewhere. TerrainField::height and surface take a Column.
- Tile node pass: each tile's 7x7 nodes once, in a buffer the tile's
  columns read; the program interface (terrain_node, terrain_node_word,
  terrain_column_nodes, generation_node_word) keeps the engine generic.
- Tile halo pass: the lean lattice's heights past a tile's edges, once a
  tile instead of once a chunk. Chunk columns no longer run the field;
  6.6 -> 4.8 us a chunk.
- Column programs: the table compiled per reference level into the ops
  its columns run, with their flags and the node words in use. CPU and
  GPU run the same plan; the per-column table walk was a sixth of a
  column's GPU time. Columns 23.6 -> 19 us a tile.
- mul_shr_signed rounds to nearest: one wide multiply, add and shift.
  Truncation's sign handling was a fifth of the column samples; rounding
  down biased the ridge envelope's statistics.
- GENBENCH reports the tile stages apart (nodes, columns, halos).
```

Sky light from each surface's own sky, not the eye's
…
710f0c6
```
Ambient light was one L2 harmonic probe integrated from the sky-view
table at the eye, and every pixel used it. Ground seen from orbit took
the sky of an eye in space, black above, so sunlit terrain lost its sky
fill and shadows went black. Terrain past the terminator, or at dusk
hundreds of kilometres away, took the eye's daylight.

(`sky_table_kernel`, Bruneton-style): over 16 altitudes by 24 sun
cosines, the sky and the lit ground below the horizon, through the air,

cosine-convolved, of which the six symmetric about the sun's vertical
plane are kept, in the local frame. It is published as

its texture limit. Deferred lighting looks up each pixel's own altitude
and sun angle; above the atmosphere the planet's glow falls with its
solid angle. At the eye it matches the old probe: a ground view is
unchanged. The table costs about 0.05 ms in the atmosphere pass.
```

…

```
Auto exposure averaged the log luminance of every pixel, black space
included. A planet on black was metered as half void, pushed +4 EV and
blown out. Metering is now a 64-bin log2-luminance histogram (half an

UE's histogram metering does. Where nothing is metered (all sky) the

- Contrast was a plain multiplier; it is now log2 around middle grey.
- Saturation was a plain multiplier; it is now a mix with luminance.

  curve, and applied the hue shift and lift/gamma/gain twice around a
```

helio_component: a voxel world is one description, air included
…
66f1cdf
```
`VoxelTerrainComponent` is now the whole world, in five categories:
World (shape, radius or size, voxel size), Generation (generator, seed,
its settings), Environment (air), Appearance and Editing (editable, the
brush journal). The chunk-domain fields a world never used are gone:
`domain_mode`, the bounds, `chunk_edge_voxels`, `max_chunk_lod`,
`lod_scale`, `renderer_id`, `material_ids` and the live sample payload
store. Sample edits on a world were already brushes in its journal.
Levels saved with those fields still load, and saving drops the fields.

Air (`VoxelAir`) is Custom (the entity's `AtmosphereComponent` as
authored), None, Earthlike, Thin (Mars) or Dense (Titan), with density,
sky tint and haze. `AtmosphereComponent::for_air` derives a preset's
sky on the world's planet. The planet's placement, radius, ground and sun
are kept. None is no sky, so a moon is airless.

Every setting is a blueprint method, read and written: `world_shape`,
`planet_radius`, `plane_size`, `voxel_size`, `seed`, `air`, `air_density`,
`air_tint_*`, `air_haze` and `is_editable`, each with a `set_*`. Invalid
values are refused.

Cached CPU worlds are keyed by scene and entity, so worlds in different
scenes no longer evict each other. Tests running in parallel exposed the
collision; in the editor, its scene and a play session can share entity
ids.
```
