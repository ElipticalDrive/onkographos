Part 1 of 3 of the ray-traced reflections milestones from the cluster RT scout (its section 5, milestones 1-3).
Stack (merge in order; each PR targets the one before it):
this PR: the grid core (kansei_core::rt)
the GPU feed: the renderer gathers the grid from its renderables (InstanceCulling and cluster LOD) (to follow)
RtReflectionsEffect in outdoor-gi and gi-box (to follow)
Why
WebGPU has no hardware ray tracing, and voxels can't make a sharp reflection at any cost. The scout measured a near-field uniform grid of world triangles, rebuilt on the GPU, at 0.55 ms for sharp reflections of the forest (106k half-res rays at 1080p), about 2× faster than the best cluster BVH. This PR adds that grid as an engine module.
The technique combines Hector Arellano's two posts:
Triangles small enough to act as particles, with the big ones kept apart (miaumiau.cat/?p=1457).
What each voxel holds, walked by a 3D DDA and tested exactly (miaumiau.cat/?p=1476's ray tracing).
What
RtGrid: a box of cells (it follows the eye in snapped steps, or stays fixed for a room). Each rebuild runs on the GPU:
gather: sources append their world triangles. A source is a mesh placed once per record, by an RtPlacement (an InstanceTransform as cluster LOD reads it, or WGSL). Triangles whose box misses the grid are dropped, and each workgroup takes its survivors' slots with one atomic.
count, scan, fill: each triangle is listed in the cells it overlaps. It visits only the cells its plane crosses, column by column, then the separating-axis test confirms each cell.
Epsilon: every cell is widened by a small world-space epsilon (RtGridOptions::epsilon, a 1024th of a cell by default), so a triangle on a cell's face is listed on both sides. The scout lost 0.75% of its hits there.
Wide triangles: a triangle spanning more than 32 columns is scattered by a workgroup of its own, one column per thread in turn. One thread walking a wall's cells made the scout's gi-box build cost 9.4 ms.
Big triangles: room-sized ones (big_triangle_cells, 1024 columns) go to a short list that every ray tests, up to 64 of them as in the post. That saves their references and their build.
Capacities (triangles, references) grow from a readback of what a build needed (read_back, stats).
RT_GRID_WGSL: kansei_rt_trace(origin, dir, t_min, t_max, flags) returns the closest hit, or any hit with KANSEI_RT_ANY_HIT. It tests the big list, then walks the cells (Amanatides-Woo, leaving empty 4³ macro cells in one step).
It binds one uniform and two storage buffers (RtGrid::bindings_wgsl / layout_entries / bind_group_entries), well within WebGPU's 8 storage buffers a stage. The build binds four.
Alpha-tested triangles (RtSurface::with_alpha_layer) call the includer's kansei_rt_covered(layer, uv), so the caller samples its own foliage texture (there are no bindless textures). RT_OPAQUE_WGSL defines one that keeps every hit.
A hit gives its normal, barycentrics, uv, albedo, source and record.
RtScene / RtMesh: meshes placed on the CPU, for a grid with no renderer. The next PR feeds the grid from the renderer on the GPU and removes this CPU selection from the examples.
split_large_triangles: the post's midpoint split, at load.
Renderer::end_profiled_frame (doc-hidden): ends a profiled frame of offscreen work, so native tools can time passes.
docs/plans/2026-10-05-rt-grid-design.md: the design of all three milestones.

Tests
cargo test -p kansei-core --lib rt:::
naga validates every shader (the build, the gather with each kind of placement, the library as a caller uses it).
The uniforms' sizes match their WGSL structs.
Placements match the cluster cull's.
The half-float packing and the split are checked.
cargo test -p kansei-core --test rt_grid_gpu (real GPU, CPU reference in f64). The scene has unit cubes whose faces lie on cell faces (one turned a quarter turn), a soup of 800 small triangles, a sphere half out of the box, a ground too wide for the cells, an alpha-tested quad, and a cube out of the box.
Every gathered triangle is listed in every cell it exactly overlaps.
Closest and any hit match a brute-force reference for 5.4k rays: rays aimed at triangles, random rays, and rays running exactly along cell faces.
They also match without macro skipping, with a big list of one, with no big list, and with coarser cells.
After the buffers grow from an overflowing first build.
The shaders compile in Chrome (WebGPU) with no messages.
