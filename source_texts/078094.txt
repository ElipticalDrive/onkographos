Is your feature request related to a problem? Please describe.
Voxel terrain has exactly one mesher: Terrain/Voxel/MarchingCubes.{h,cpp}, which produces smooth isosurfaces. That is the right tool for organic terrain and the wrong one for blocky/cubic voxel worlds — marching cubes on binary voxel data gives you stair-stepped triangles at full density with no face merging, so a cubic world costs far more triangles and vertices than it should.
Describe the solution you'd like
A binary greedy mesher alongside marching cubes, selected per voxel volume:
Treat each axis-aligned face direction as a bitmask per column; merge contiguous visible faces into maximal quads with bitwise operations rather than per-voxel scanning.
Emit packed quads — local XYZ, width, height, face direction and colour/material packed into a compact per-quad record — and draw them instanced, so the vertex buffer is a fraction of a naive mesh.
Neighbour-aware padding so chunk-boundary faces are correct without meshing seams (the reference gathers a padded neighbourhood before meshing).
Describe alternatives you've considered
Keeping marching cubes for everything — wrong silhouette for cubic worlds. Naive per-face meshing — correct but wildly more geometry.
Additional context
Reference: D:\repos\VoxelEngine\VoxelEngine\voxel_mesher.h (padded neighbourhood gather in ChunkData::GatherData, greedy pass in VoxelMesher), voxel_math.h, and the unpacking vertex shader voxel_shader.vert.glsl. MIT, C++23, OpenGL 4.6 — same stack as ours. Their reported figures: 0.020 ms/chunk meshing at 8^3, 0.064 ms at 16^3. Note their documented limitation — the 2-byte quad encoding cannot express 32^3 chunks, so pick the encoding width deliberately.
Related: #113 (closed — procedural terrain), Terrain/Voxel/VoxelOverride.
Acceptance criteria
A cubic voxel scene meshes to substantially fewer triangles than the marching-cubes path at identical silhouette.
No seams or double-drawn faces at chunk boundaries; verified from several angles including looking along chunk borders.
Meshing runs on the task system without blocking the frame.
