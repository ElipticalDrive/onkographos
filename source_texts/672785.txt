ABSTRACT
Analytical and numerical tests were made of a number of possible methods
for scoring the energy exchange between radiation and matter in the implicit
Monte Carlo {IMC} radiation transport scheme of Fleck and Cummings.
The
interactions considered were effective absorption, elastic scattering, and
Compton scattering.
The scoring methods tested were limited to simple
combinations of analogue, linear expected value, and exponential expected
value scoring. Only two scoring methods were found that produced the same
results as a pure analogue method. These are a combination of exponential
expected value absorption and deposition and analogue Compton scattering of
the particle, with either linear expected value Compton deposition or analogue
Compton deposition.
In both methods, the collision distance is based on the
total scattering cross section.

INTRODUCTION
In Monte Carlo calculations, many methods may be used for determining how
p a r t i c l e energy weights sr.d energy deposition in the medium should be
determined, or scored, as a result of energy exchange and scattering
processes.
In the implicit Monte Carlo (IMC) radiation transport scheme of
Fleck and Cunnings,
these processes include emission, effective absorption,
effective e l a s t i c s c a t t e r i n g , and effective Compton s c a t t e r i n g .
A scoring
method must provide the correct expected values of p a r t i c l e energy, deposited
energy, and rates of energy transport, when applied over a sufficiently large
number of events, and must do so for arbitrary zone configurations, time step
sizes, and variations of cross sections or reaction rates with p a r t i c l e
frequency, position, and time.
If several scoring methods satisfy these requirements, one r.^y be chosen
on the basis of the variance in the r e s u l t s , the complexity of the
calculations, and the requirements for computation time and memory space.
The purpose of t h i s study was to evaluate several possible scoring
methods, to verify t h e i r accuracy and s u i t a b i l i t y .
Several types of evaluation were used:
(1) Various scoring methods were
evaluated for a single flight with a distance limit.
(2) The expected values
of the number of f l i g h t s , the p a r t i c l e energy and the energy deposition were
evaluated as a function of the total flight distance, for multiple f l i g h t s
with constant cross sections.
(3) Numerical calculations were made for a
model with multiple f l i g h t s , a distance l i m i t , and variable cross sections, to
find those scoring methods that agree with pure analogue scoring methods.
The results of these evaluations are described below.

Applicability to Multiple Flights
I t is not obvious that scoring methods that give the correct expected
values for single f l i g h t s are also correct when applied to multiple f l i g h t s .
A valid method must give the correct dependence of energy flux on both time
and position to do transport correctly.
The single-flight scoring methods
discussed above only give the same expected value of energy at the end of a
single f l i g h t , which may vary in average length, and therefore in elapsed
time, for different scoring methods.
For correct transport, the average
f l i g h t length must be balanced by the probability of scattering at the end of
the f l i g h t , so that a l l v a l i l methods produce equivalent results.
I f particles also have variable frequencies and cross sections, the
scoring method must also produce the correct frequency distribution in space
and time.
This is most simply determined by numerical tests, to find out
which of the simple methods described above are also appropriate for multiple
f l i g h t s with variable parameters.
I t is also possible that scoring methods
exist which do not give the correct expected value for a single f l i g h t , but do
give the correct expected value for a given f l i g h t time or total f l i g h t
distance.

V. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
Various energy scoring methods for IMC radiation transport were tested.
The tests included mathematical analyses of single-flight and multiple-flight
models with a distance l i m i t and constant cross sections, and numerical
analyses of a multiple-flight model with a distance limit and variable cross
sections.
The latter tests included tests of pure absorption, i m p l i c i t
absorption with effective scattering, e x p l i c i t Co-«pton scattering, implicit
Compton scattering, and a combination of implicit absorption and implicit
Compton scattering.
Several different forms of frequency-dependent cross
sections were used.
Several different scoring methods were found that give results in
agreement with pure analogue scoring methods. These include the methods:
(a) particle energy scoring based on an exponential expected value model of
absorption 'long each path, with collision distance based on the total
scattering cross section, and an analogue form of Compton energy exchange, and
(b) energy deposition scoring either complementary to the particle energy
scoring, or with Compton energy deposition along each path based on a linear
expected value model.
Method (a) has the advantage of an explicit energy
balance, and method (b) has the advantage of distributing the Compton energy
deposition smoothly over time and position.
