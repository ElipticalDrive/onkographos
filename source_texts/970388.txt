Owner
A standalone sky page, an optics laboratory: it opens directly at an observer's viewpoint and shows Rayleigh single scattering of a blackbody star by an explicit gas mixture. It is a visual prototype, labelled on the sky itself; it is not a validated sky colour.
Entry points
docs/sky.html: no Python needed. Double-click it, or open it in any browser. It is one offline file that starts on Earth, with a preset menu and every input editable. It is generated by python -m tools.sky_page and never edited by hand.
sky.write_sky_page(spec): a labelled hypothetical atmosphere from explicit composition, surface pressure, temperature profile, gravity, gas constant, planetary radius and star. It needs no run, prepared planet, terrain, archive or CUDA.
examples/sky_hypothetical.py: the scripted example. Its defaults are 0.1 bar N₂ under a 3200 K star, with every other input stated: 250 K, g = 9.80665 m s⁻², R from the composition, R = 6371 km, and the star's angular size set to give the Sun's bolometric irradiance at 1 au.
Simulation.sky(spec, time=…): one saved PE column above (lat, lon), reconstructed exactly on the CPU. The column is fixed in the page. Nothing is written into the run.
The page
Presets (sky.SKY_PRESETS, offered on every hypothetical page; a saved column is fixed and offers none). None is a validated reference atmosphere.
Earth: dry air at 1 atm, the US Standard Atmosphere 1976 troposphere (288.15 K, 6.5 K/km, isothermal from 216.65 K), the Sun at 1 au.
The 0.1 bar N₂ / 3200 K acceptance case.
Mars, gas only: NASA NSSDCA Mars Fact Sheet fractions, 6.36 hPa, 214 K, g = 3.73 m s⁻², R = 3389.5 km, the Sun at 1.52366 au. CO and traces (0.21 %) are added to CO₂, as its source says. The real Martian sky colour comes from dust, which is not modelled.
The menu reads custom once the atmosphere, composition or star spectrum differs from every preset.
Settings kept in the address (#key=value). Numbers are exact (view angles to 0.01°), so a reload or bookmark reopens the same sky. Unknown keys and unusable values are listed, never guessed. Save as page downloads the same file with the settings embedded. Reset returns to the settings the page was written with.
Edit the atmosphere: pressure, temperature/lapse/floor, gravity, radius. The gas constant is not an input: the composition already fixes it (R = R*/M̄), and the page shows it with M̄. A gas constant fixed from Python, for species without optical data such as Titan's CH₄, is shown read-only with a Use the composition's R/M* button.
Edit the composition (N₂, O₂, Ar, CO₂), with amounts as mole fractions, mass fractions or masses (kg).
Fractions must sum to 1 and are never renormalized; there is an explicit remainder button.
Mass fractions convert exactly to mole fractions.
Masses set the surface pressure, p_s = g·Σmᵢ/(4πR²). This thin-atmosphere relation ignores that the shell widens with height (about 2H/R). Gravity or radius edits keep the masses and move the pressure.
Edit the star: a Star menu of real stars; temperature; size as a radius (R☉) or luminosity (L☉); distance (au) or irradiance at the planet (W m⁻²). Whatever you give stays put: with a luminosity, a new temperature keeps L and the radius follows. Earth's irradiance places the star at 1361 W m⁻². Radius, luminosity, distance, irradiance and angular size are all shown. Azimuth and elevation (−90° to 90°) are set by hand.
The star disk is drawn at full screen resolution, with smooth edges, and set behind the ground below the horizon.
Edit the observer height (ground, altitude or pressure), the view, and the exposure.
Exposure AUTO: white is the brightest sky, so brightness changes are normalized away. Exposure FIXED: white is a held reference luminance, so brightness comparisons are meaningful. The active mode is shown.
Optical depth: Rayleigh τ at the zenith and towards the star, with caution (τ ≥ 0.1) and unreliable (τ ≥ 1) verdicts, also shown as a banner on the sky. Earth's 1 bar column gives τ(410 nm) = 0.320, equal to the analytic σ·p_s/(m̄g). The docs said 0.36 until this branch corrected it.
Globe pages unaffected
terrain_globe.html, terrain.py, atmosphere.py and PreparedPlanet.view are byte-identical to main. A test asserts that the globe template has no sky code and that neither globe writer takes a sky.
Optical data
N₂ and Ar refractive indices: Börzsönyi 2008. O₂: Zhang 2008. CO₂: Bideau-Mehu 1973. All transcribed via refractiveindex.info.
The King factors were checked against Bodhaine et al. (1999) as printed: eqs. 5–6 and the text after eq. 6, with the micrometre unit confirmed by its Fig. 1.
End-to-end check: the dry-air cross section agrees with Bodhaine's eq. 29 to within 0.094 % over 410–750 nm.
Colour matching: the CIE 1931 2° table, verbatim, with its SHA-256 checked.
Stars (spatial.optics.STARS), as blackbodies, read from each star's Wikipedia infobox on 2026-11-07 with the sources those cite:
Proxima Centauri, 2833 K and 0.162 R☉: Anugu et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.33373, a preprint.
ε Eridani and Procyon A: Soubiran et al. 2024, A&A 682, A145 (R and L).
Sirius A: the infobox's reference "bond-11" (R and L).
The Sun: IAU 2015 B3 nominal.
Where one paper gives R and L, T = (L/4πR²σ)^¼, within about 20 K of each infobox's own temperature.
Star constants: the IAU 2015 B3 nominal Sun (5772 K, 695 700 km, 3.828e26 W, 1361 W m⁻²) and the IAU 2012 au. σ comes from the exact SI constants.
Known limits
Single scattering only (hence the τ verdict).
Not modelled: aerosols, clouds, gas absorption, refraction, polarization.
No H₂, He or CH₄ optics.
The star is placed by hand (no orbit).
Tests
The page's float64 arithmetic matches the NumPy reference to a relative 1e-9: the column, the mixture σ, the radiance, the zenith and star-path columns, the star XYZ and the verdict. This holds for the acceptance case, after page edits, for every preset in docs/sky.html, after an address round trip, and for a saved column.
docs/sky.html stays current: tools.sky_page --check and a test compare the page text exactly and the embedded numbers to a relative 1e-12, so a last-bit NumPy difference between platforms isn't reported as drift.
Address and Save as page round trips restore every setting exactly. A bad address is reported, not obeyed.
The exposure modes, the warnings and the refusals are tested.
Gas constant: the derived value and the read-only fixed one are tested. Mass-fraction and kg amounts are checked against Composition.from_mass_fractions and the thin-atmosphere pressure. Every catalogue star placed at Earth's irradiance is checked against Star.of, along with the edits that hold the luminosity or the irradiance.
The star disk's white pixels match πr² to within 5 % and spread equally in x and y, at three view directions. The old 1/3-resolution disk changed shape as the view moved.
The example runs with CuPy, Matplotlib, temporal, run, the archive, diagnostics, terrain, transforms and the globe modules all blocked from import.
Full suite: 1570 passed locally at fa58996, and CI's full suite passed at 0486784. At 917e805 the sky, layout, published-layout, unused-function, architecture-tool and atmosphere-playback suites pass (136). Nothing outside the sky page imports spatial.optics, and CI runs the full suite. Architecture --check is clean.
The browser harness tolerates Windows locking DevToolsActivePort. OfflinePage.open(path, fragment=) can open a page at a #… address.

The sky page's inputs now follow the physics instead of exposing
redundant knobs, and the star disk is drawn round.

- Gas constant: no longer a page control. With the composition given,
  R = R*/M is already decided; the page shows it with M. A gas constant
  fixed from Python (for species without optical data, such as Titan's
  CH4) is shown read-only, with a "Use the composition's R*/M" button.
- Amounts as mole fractions, mass fractions or masses (kg). Mass
  fractions convert exactly, x_i = (w_i/M_i) / sum(w_j/M_j), and must
  sum to 1 (never renormalized). Masses set the surface pressure by the
  thin-atmosphere relation p_s = g sum(m_i) / (4 pi R^2), which ignores
  the shell widening with height (~2H/R); gravity or radius edits keep
  the masses. Composition.from_mass_fractions, .mass_fractions and
  thin_atmosphere_mass_kg / _surface_pressure_pa mirror the page.
- Star: temperature, size as a radius (R_sun) or a luminosity (L_sun),
  and distance (au) or irradiance (W m^-2); what is given stays put.
  sin(alpha) = R/d, L = 4 pi R^2 sigma T^4, S = sigma T^4 sin^2(alpha),
  with the IAU 2015 B3 nominal Sun and sigma from the exact SI
  constants. "Earth's irradiance" places a star at 1361 W m^-2.
  Star.of(T, R, d) and Star.sun_at keep radius and distance; an
  angle-only Star still works and is shown as its equivalent at 1 au.
- spatial.optics.STARS: Proxima Centauri (Anugu et al. 2026, arXiv
  preprint), epsilon Eridani and Procyon A (Soubiran et al. 2024, A&A
  682, A145), the Sun (IAU nominal), Sirius A (Wikipedia's "bond-11"
  reference), all as listed in the stars' Wikipedia infoboxes on
  2026-11-07. Where one paper gives R and L, T follows from them.
- Star disk: drawn at full screen resolution with 4x4 samples per pixel
  and hidden behind the ground. The sky image is 1/3 resolution, so a
  sub-pixel Sun used to be whichever coarse pixels fell inside it and
  changed shape as the view moved. The marker ring stays outside the
  disk.
- Elevation slider: -90 to 90 degrees (it stopped at -20).
- [hidden] now wins over .row's display:flex, which had kept rows meant
  to be hidden on screen.

Tests: browser tests check the derived and fixed gas constant, mass and
kg amounts against Composition.from_mass_fractions and the thin-
atmosphere pressure, every catalogue star at Earth's irradiance against
Star.of, the luminosity- and irradiance-holding edits, and the disk's
area (within 5 % of pi r^2) and roundness at three pointings. The sky,
layout, published-layout, unused-function, architecture-tool and
atmosphere-playback suites pass (136). Only sky.py imports
spatial.optics, so the full suite was not rerun locally; CI runs it.
docs/sky.html and the architecture snapshot are regenerated.
