Summary
On top of the exact rotation action (issue 1 of 2, #254 ), add the real-number layer: conversions between unit quaternions, axis-angle, Euler angles and rotation matrices, plus exp/log and slerp. This layer is RealQuaternion only and is tested with approximate equality.
Background
Every rotation of ℝ³ by angle θ about a unit axis n is q = cos(θ/2) + sin(θ/2)(n₁i + n₂j + n₃k), and ±q are the only two unit quaternions giving it. Euler angles are three successive rotations about coordinate axes; there are 12 axis sequences (6 Tait–Bryan such as ZYX, 6 proper Euler such as ZXZ), each in an intrinsic and an extrinsic reading, and all of them have a gimbal-lock locus where the conversion back to angles is not unique.
Scope
fromAxisAngle(axis, angle) and toAxisAngle(q); define the answer for the identity rotation (angle 0, a documented default axis).
exp of a pure quaternion and log of a unit quaternion; pow(q, t).
EulerSequence enum for the 12 sequences, and an explicit intrinsic/extrinsic flag. No unnamed default: the convention is always spelled at the call site.
fromEuler(sequence, a, b, c) as a product of three axis rotations (this direction is easy and convention-proof).
toEuler(sequence, q), with documented ranges and a documented choice at gimbal lock (set the third angle to 0).
fromRotationMatrix(m): the numerically stable branch on the largest of w², x², y², z² (Shepperd's method); IllegalArgumentException if m is not a rotation within tolerance.
slerp(q0, q1, t), taking the shorter arc, with the nearly-parallel fallback to normalized lerp.
README section, including one table of conventions (handedness, active vs passive, intrinsic vs extrinsic, angle ranges).
Tests
Round trips: axis-angle → quaternion → axis-angle; Euler → quaternion → Euler away from gimbal lock, for all 12 sequences; matrix → quaternion → matrix. Quaternion round trips hold up to sign.
fromEuler equals the product of the three axis rotations, and agrees with the product of the three elementary rotation matrices via rotationMatrix from issue 1.
Intrinsic XYZ equals extrinsic ZYX with the angles reversed.
At gimbal lock, toEuler followed by fromEuler still reproduces the rotation.
exp(log q) = q; slerp endpoints, constant angular speed, and slerp(q0, −q1, t) describing the same path of rotations.
Exact anchor points: the rational rotations of issue 1 at multiples of 90°.
Out of scope
Dual quaternions and rigid motions (own issue); squad and other spline interpolation; rotations in dimension 4.
Depends on
Quaternion rotations (1 of 2).
