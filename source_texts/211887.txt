Abstract
Transparent object reconstruction is crucial in various domains, including 3D modelling and robotic perception. However, accurately reconstructing nested transparent objects remains challenging due to complex light interactions. Recent advances like NU-NeRF have shown
promise but suffer from computational inefficiency. Here, we introduce Hash-NURF, a framework that integrates multi-resolution hash
encoding with neural implicit representations to accelerate reconstruction. Specifically, we utilize hash-based NeRF and SDF for efficient
optimization. To unbounded scenes, we added a contractive mapping
strategy. We also incorporated special sampling and regularization techniques to improve the model’s stability and help it converge faster.
Our method significantly reduces training time by up to three times
while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality. We validate our
approach on synthetic datasets, demonstrating its efficacy in both
outer and inner surface reconstruction of nested transparent objects.

Unfortunately, reconstructing transparent objects remains highly challenging. Unlike opaque surfaces, where light interactions are relatively simple,
transparent objects exhibit complex light propagation behaviors due to
refraction, reflection, and transmission. Accurately modeling these optical phenomena is inherently difficult. The situation becomes even more complicated
when multiple transparent objects are nested, as light rays undergo repeated
refraction and reflection through different interfaces. This introduces additional ambiguity in the inverse rendering process, making transparent object
reconstruction an ill-posed problem in computer vision and graphics [11].
Early approaches addressed this problem by leveraging specialized sensors,
such as polarization cameras [12] or tomographic systems [13], to capture additional physical cues. Although effective in controlled laboratory settings, these
methods rely heavily on custom hardware, limiting their applicability and scalability due to high cost and restricted deployment environments. To alleviate
this dependency, subsequent research has shifted towards using consumergrade imaging devices. Existing methods can generally be divided into two
categories. One category focuses on controlled capture environments [14, 15],
where modulated illumination or calibration targets are employed to simplify
light-path estimation. The other category aims at reconstruction in uncontrolled environments [16], typically by minimizing photometric errors between
rendered images and observed multi-view images.
For nested transparent objects, ReNeuS [17] first demonstrated reconstruction of objects enclosed within transparent shells under controlled uniform
lighting, combining ray tracing with NeuS [18]. More recently, Sun et al.
[11] introduced NU-NeRF, which integrates neural Signed Distance Field
(SDF) with a differentiable ray tracing framework to progressively reconstruct
background, outer, and inner structures. NU-NeRF achieved state-of-theart (SOTA) results without requiring specialized equipment, marking an
important step forward. However, a major drawback of NU-NeRF lies in
its computational inefficiency: the MLP-based SDF representation leads to
extremely slow optimization, often taking more than 12 hours to reconstruct
a single scene (see Figure 1). This heavy computational burden severely limits
its practical usability.

efficiency bottlenecks. By introducing a multi-resolution hash encoding strategy, Instant-NGP efficiently encodes spatial information at multiple scales
into trainable hash tables, while a lightweight MLP resolves hash collisions
and ensures smooth interpolation. Compared to conventional NeRF methods
[20], Instant-NGP achieves over three orders of magnitude speedup while preserving high fidelity, and has subsequently inspired extensions to neural SDF
representations [21, 22]. These studies demonstrate that multi-resolution hash

2 Related Works
2.1 Transparent Object Reconstruction
Reconstructing transparent objects is a challenging task due to the complex
reflection and refraction paths, which complicate the analysis of light propagation. Early methods relied on specialized capture devices to obtain surface
information of the object or details of light propagation. Notable techniques
include the use of polarization cameras [12], light field probes [26], and tomography methods [13]. However, these methods require specialized hardware,
limiting both their applicability and the scope of reconstruction targets. To
address this, recent research has focused on reducing reliance on specialized
hardware by building reconstruction frameworks that utilize consumer-grade
optical devices. One approach involves controlled capture environments that
often require modulated images or specific lighting conditions. For instance,
Wu et al. [3] reconstructed transparent objects by utilizing contour information and corresponding refraction paths. NeTO [14] uses modulated images
and a tracing method that incorporates self-occlusion to optimize the signed
distance field (SDF). Li et al. [27] use pre-measured environment maps and
design specialized rendering networks to reconstruct the geometry of transparent objects. To address this, recent research has focused on minimizing reliance
on specialized hardware, instead building reconstruction frameworks using
consumer-grade optical devices. One approach involves controlled capture environments, where methods often require modulated images or specific lighting
conditions. For example, Wu et al. [3] reconstructed transparent objects by
leveraging contour information and corresponding refraction paths. NeTO [14]
uses modulated images and a tracing method that incorporates self-occlusion
to optimize the signed distance field (SDF). Li et al. [27] utilize pre-measured
environment maps and design specialized rendering networks to reconstruct
the geometry of transparent objects.
In contrast, another group of methods targets reconstruction in uncontrolled environments. Gao et al. [16] place transparent objects on a patterned
plane and use a differentiable ray tracing method to optimize the SDF for
reconstruction. Chen et al. [28] decouple the geometry and appearance of transparent objects, utilizing masks for guidance during reconstruction. NU-NeRF
[11] proposes a novel framework based on SDFs and differentiable ray tracing techniques to reconstruct nested transparent structures, progressing from
outer to inner surfaces. However, their network architecture is complex, requiring multiple neural networks to predict geometry and shading parameters,
resulting in slower training time.

2.2 Neural Implicit Representation
Traditional 3D representation methods, such as voxel-based [29, 30], meshbased [15, 31], and point cloud representations [29, 32, 33], are widely used
in geometric processing and rendering. However, these explicit representations
encounter challenges during optimization, especially when dealing with complex geometry. Recently, neural implicit representations, particularly Neural
Radiance Fields (NeRF) [20], have gained prominence for 3D reconstruction.
NeRF employs a series of calibrated images and cameras to reconstruct the 3D
geometry of objects from various viewpoints. The flexibility and adaptability of
neural implicit representations render them suitable for various tasks beyond
reconstruction, such as generation [34, 35] and deformation [36–38]. However,
density-based representations struggle to capture accurate surface geometry,
limiting their effectiveness for tasks that require precise geometric details. To
address this, NeuS [18] integrates Signed Distance Functions (SDF) with neural
representations. By employing an MLP to fit the SDF and using a distribution to transition between the SDF and density, NeuS enhances the accuracy
of both geometry and rendering. This approach establishes the foundation for
more precise reconstruction, editing, and inverse rendering [39–41].
2.3 Accelerating Neural Radiance Fields
Training Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) models is computationally expensive
and time-consuming. Two primary challenges contribute to this inefficiency
[25]: (1) evaluating a large number of sampling points to numerically integrate
the rendering equation, and (2) computing shading information for each sampling point through a neural network. The original NeRF method addresses
the first issue via importance sampling [20], where the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) is derived from the Probability Density Function (PDF)
of transmittance, and inverse transform sampling aligns the sampling distribution with the spatial density field for each ray emitted from each pixel.
While this reduces variance in the estimation, it substantially increases the
computational burden.
One approach involves adjusting the screen-space sampling rate using a
method similar to that proposed by S-LASSIE[42]. This technique achieves
efficient rendering by employing ray marching on interior cube-map faces and
coupled ray-marching technique, but its practicality for optimizing radiated
fields requires further validation. Another approach is to optimize sampling
along the ray. Barron et al. [23] train a small MLP during the coarse sampling
stage to directly estimate the PDF along the ray, thereby improving the sampling distribution. However, incorporating a new network increases the number
of parameters, thereby exacerbating the training burden. Another solution,
proposed in Instant-NGP [19], uses an occupancy grid to partition space and
accelerate sampling by caching the spatial occupancy status in a binarized
voxel grid. This approach allows the sampling stage to bypass empty spaces

Another category employs explicit spatial encoding representations [19, 25, 44].
These approaches leverage hash tables or spatial voxel grids to partition and

method, in which spatial information is encoded using a trainable hash table

4 Method
Our framework, Hash-NURF, enables fast and accurate reconstruction of
nested transparent objects from multi-view image inputs. Building on the
nested object formulation of NU-NeRF [11], we integrate multi-resolution hash
encoding to accelerate optimization and reduce memory consumption while
simultaneously reconstructing the background environment, outer surface, and
inner surface. Specifically, our method leverages (i) a background NeRF to
represent the scene outside the object, (ii) an outer SDF defining the object
boundary, and (iii) an inner SDF capturing the nested interior surface. To further improve efficiency, we design an unbounded contractive mapping that
projects points outside the unit sphere into the domain of hash grids, ensuring
effective utilization of the encoding space. In addition, we adopt occupancygrid-guided importance sampling to preferentially evaluate informative
samples along rays and introduce a perturbation normal loss to stabilize
optimization. An overview of the pipeline is illustrated in Figure 2.

4.1 Surface Reconstruction for Nested Transparent

We represent the scene with three coupled components:
(1) Background NeRF. The surrounding environment is modeled by
a background radiance field gbkg (p, ω), where p is a 3D location and −ω
is the viewing direction. This captures illumination and environmental color
consistent with the transparent object.
(2) Outer Surface SDF. The zero-level set of the outer SDF defines the
exterior surface of the transparent object. During training, rays intersect this
surface first, and reflection/refraction events are computed.
(3) Inner Surface SDF. After refraction at the outer surface, rays continue propagation to the inner SDF, whose zero-level set defines the internal
surface. This supports reconstruction of nested structures such as liquids or
objects inside containers.
The interaction of rays with these three representations follows the physically motivated “onion-like” iterative strategy of NU-NeRF [11], but our
framework replaces MLP encodings with multi-resolution hash encoding for
both radiance and geometry representations, greatly improving speed and
scalability.

4.3 Unbounded Contractive Mapping for Background
NeRF
To accurately represent background regions in unbounded scenes, it is necessary to transform world coordinates into a bounded domain prior to encoding
with multi-resolution hash encoding. Existing strategies have adopted different approaches for this transformation. For example, NeRF++ [50] represents
unbounded regions using a 4D coordinate system. Specifically, it maps the
space outside the unit sphere onto the direct product of the unit 3-sphere and
the inverse distance to the origin. PermutoSDF [25] then directly encodes these
4D coordinates using a hash-based embedding. However, this method suffers
from two inherent drawbacks. First, the actual encoded data resides on a 3D
manifold embedded in R4 , while the hash table allocates memory over the
entire hypercube (−1, 1)4 . This mismatch leads to a significant underutilization
of hash capacity. Second, the additional dimensionality introduces unnecessary redundancy, thereby reducing both memory efficiency and computational
performance.
In contrast, Mip-NeRF 360 [23] introduces a contractive mapping that
transforms unbounded space into a bounded shell. But since we employ two
distinct representations (SDF and NeRF) for the bounded object and the
unbounded background respectively, separate hash encodings are required.
When this mapping method is applied to the background, its image set
inevitably contains a hole, preventing efficient utilization of the hash-encodable
range. Inspired by this idea, we propose to adopt a compact contractive mapping T that preserves spatial detail inside the unit sphere while smoothly
compressing points outside the sphere into the unit ball, as shwon in Figure

4.4 Occupancy-Grid-Guided Importance Sampling for
Efficient Optimization
To accelerate training and improve sampling efficiency, it is crucial to avoid
redundant network evaluations in empty regions and to concentrate computational resources near object surfaces. To this end, we adopt an occupancygrid-guided importance sampling strategy, which is similar in principle to
NerfAcc [24].
Specifically, the method utilizes a voxelized occupancy grid in which each
voxel stores a running estimate of its occupancy probability. This probability
is estimated from recent density or SDF values and updated via an exponential
moving average (EMA). A binarization step with threshold τ is subsequently
applied to determine whether each voxel is classified as occupied. During rendering, rays query the grid to (i) skip empty regions and (ii) construct a
piecewise-constant probability density function (PDF) along the ray for importance sampling. This approach ensures that fine samples are preferentially
drawn from regions with a high likelihood of containing surfaces.

6 Results and Evaluations
6.1 Experiment Settings
6.1.1 Implementation Details
Our method is implemented using PyTorch [53] and optimized with the Adam
algorithm [54], with a uniform learning rate of 5 × 10−4 . During training, we
utilize batches of 1024 rays per iteration. The outer surface reconstruction
undergoes 200,000 iterations, while the inner surface reconstruction is trained
for 150,000 iterations. All experiments are conducted on a platform equipped
with a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB). Upon completion of training,
explicit meshes are extracted from the learned SDF using the Marching Cubes
algorithm [55].
For the hash tables employed in SDF and background reconstruction, we
utilize 19 resolution levels with a hash table size of 218 , combined with 6 levels
of Fourier feature encoding [20]. The mapping parameter in Equation (5) is
set to β = 0.1. To ensure optimization stability, we adopt a progressive scheme
for gradually activating encoding features. During the initial training phase,
only raw positional encoding is enabled to satisfy the geometric initialization
requirements of SAL [49]. As training progresses, additional Fourier and hash
encoding features are incrementally incorporated to capture finer details.
6.1.2 Datasets and Evaluation Metrics
This paper constructs a synthetic dataset for performance evaluation, comprising various object types whose multi-view images are synthesized using
Blender [56]. The dataset includes the solid object Pig from NeTO [14], and
nested objects: Bunny (outer transparent + inner transparent), Starfish (outer
transparent + inner opaque), and MonkeyHead (outer transparent + inner
translucent).
To quantitatively evaluate the quality of reconstructed surfaces, we employ
Chamfer Distance (CD) [57] and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) [58] as metrics to measure the similarity between reconstruction results and ground-truth
meshes. For both metrics, 216 points are uniformly sampled from the mesh and

6.1.3 Baseline Models
This section evaluates our method and compares it against several state-ofthe-art transparent object reconstruction approaches. Specifically, Gao et al.
[16] and NeMTO [59] are multi-view image-based methods that require only
environment maps for reconstruction. NeTO [14] employs a refraction-tracing
approach to reconstruct solid transparent object surfaces by optimizing correspondences between camera rays and a background screen. To assess inner
surface reconstruction quality for nested transparent objects, we additionally
compare with ReNeuS [17] and NU-NeRF [11]. ReNeuS [17] specializes in
reconstructing opaque objects inside transparent containers with known outer
geometry, while NU-NeRF [11] reconstructs both outer and inner surfaces of
nested transparent objects through ray-tracing-based surface reconstruction.
All methods use a fixed set of 200 uniformly distributed poses generated
via a Fibonacci lattice [60]. For NeTO, we compute the required correspondence between pixels and background-modulated images at these poses using
Mitsuba3 [61]. Other methods are trained using images rendered at these poses
in Blender [56].
6.2 Comparative Experiments
Qualitative and quantitative results are presented in Figures 5, 6 and Tables
1, 2. For reconstructing both solid and nested transparent objects, our method
achieves comparable quality to baseline methods in terms of Chamfer Distance
(CD) [57] and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) [58], while significantly reducing
computation time. Compared to the unoptimized NU-NeRF [11], our optimized approach reduces the full reconstruction pipeline time (outer + inner
surfaces) from 12 hours to 4.7-5 hours—over twice as fast.
6.2.1 Outer Surface Reconstruction
In the outer surface reconstruction stage, for the solid Pig object, our method
captures finer geometric details (e.g., snout region) than previous approaches,
benefiting from the expressive power of hash encoding. This advantage is consistent across other scenes. NeTO, which leverages precise refraction path
modeling, achieves superior EMD metrics compared to NU-NeRF and our
method. For complex nested transparent objects, our approach maintains competitive reconstruction quality with state-of-the-art methods. NeMTO [59]
excels in reconstructing convex geometries like Bunny due to its silhouettebased supervision, but overall, our method performs comparably to NU-NeRF
[11].

Inner Reconstruction
In the inner surface reconstruction stage, the full pipeline still demonstrates
superiority in terms of time efficiency. In terms of reconstruction quality, it
is slightly inferior to the method without occupancy-grid-guided importance
sampling (w/o occ grid). This phenomenon may be attributed to the fact

process. From the ablation experiment without hash (w/o hash), it can be
observed that the superior representational capability of hash encoding brings
significant quality improvement to reconstruction and enables better recovery
of geometric details.
7 Conclusion
In this work, we propose Hash-NURF, an efficient reconstruction framework for
nested transparent object reconstruction. Specifically, we put forward the first
framework that accelerates the reconstruction of nested transparent objects
by integrating multi-resolution hash encoding with neural SDF representations. Our unbounded scene parameterization for hash-based encoding enables
efficient background NeRF optimization in open environments. The reconstruction pipeline in this work incorporating occupancy-grid-guided importance
sampling and SDF regularization not only achieves stable optimization and
high-quality results but also significantly lowers computational costs in comparison to previous methods. Experiments across four scenes demonstrate that
our framework achieves reconstruction quality comparable to state-of-the-art
methods while reducing the time cost of the full reconstruction pipeline from
12 hours to 4.7-5 hours.
Limitation
The proposed method still has several limitations, suggesting directions for
future research. As seen in experimental results, the reconstructed surfaces
tend to be rougher than MLP-based SDF representations due to the inherently lower smoothness of hash encoding. A straightforward solution involves
increasing network capacity or imposing continuity constraints [62], but
this may increase computational demands. Designing representations with
improved smoothness while maintaining computational efficiency remains an

open challenge. Also, reconstruction quality is sensitive to hyperparameters, requiring manual tuning for different scenes. Developing methods with
greater hyperparameter robustness or reduced hyperparameter dependency
holds substantial practical potential.
