Owner
Froxel fog, phase C: fog volumes artists place, such as mist banks and fog walls.
The change
A cell entity can have a fog_volume: box or ellipsoid, with density_per_m up to 4, a soft edge in meters and a tint. Its scale is its half extents. An edge wider than the volume ends at its middle, so its heart always reaches its density. CellAsset goes to format version 2.
Extraction copies them each frame into RenderFrame::fog_volumes, where they're seen.
The renderer sends the nearest 64 in view and in the grid's reach, ranked by distance to their bounding sphere, with a 64-bit mask per slice of those whose sphere reaches it. With clear air, volumes out of view don't keep the fog passes on, but their 3D textures stay for 240 frames, so panning across the view's edge doesn't reallocate them.
fog inject adds their extinction and tint to the froxels they cover, so they're lit, shadowed and reprojected like the rest. Each froxel's middle ray is clipped to the volume and density tested at 3 points through that span, so however thin a volume is the points can't all miss it, and they belong to the volume instead of sliding through it with the camera. They're lit from their first meter, wherever the sun's haze starts, by samples of where they are in the slice, not the air around them, and the world's fog only shares their light within its own span.
The fog pass corrects for the ground: where a pixel's surface is inside a slice with volumes, it takes the share of the slice really in front of that surface, tested along the pixel's own ray.
They fade out over the last tenth of the grid's reach.
The test world gets a mist bank to look at.
Also in this PR:
The stats panel (F1) shows the camera's position, yaw and pitch, which is how the bug below was located. It's its own commit.
The MSVC CI failure seen twice: deengine, de_tests and cooker each copied Slang's module folder to the same place after linking, and collided in a parallel build. One shared target now does it.

Causes:
From that far, a slice is as thick as the mist. One point per slice lands in or out of it as the camera moves.
A pixel read a share of its slice by depth alone, wherever the mist really was. With mist resting on the ground, that error swept across it as slice edges slid over the ground.
What's left comes from the grid itself: its boxes move with the camera through mist that stays still. Removing it means a per-pixel raymarch. The next step planned instead is animated noise and shaped edges, which also make it read as mist, and hide what's left. The decision is in docs/design.md.

Those are means over 6 s. The 65 row is a stress case: about 0.9 ms of the 4.17 ms budget on this GPU, of which the ground correction is about 0.26 ms, and it only falls on pixels whose surface is in a slice with volumes. Those two runs were noisier than usual, with single frames up to about 2 ms in each pass, including fog integrate, which volumes don't touch and which read 0.04 ms in the previous run. So I take its rise as the machine, not the code, but I didn't look further. The buffer holding the frame's constants grows by about 6.6 KB.
Testing
Render: a box in clear air, no grid, soft edge, an edge past the middle, ellipsoid corners, a turned slab, tint, shadow, sun start, the world's fog past the grid not taking a volume's light, a camera with a scale, over frames, gone, camera inside, culling to each side, reaching into the view, reach by view depth, the fade at the reach, passes off when out of view, textures kept while volumes exist, long along the view, the limit keeping the nearest by surface and replacing the farthest, the most that can stack, a volume in the mask's second word, a wall inside a slice before and behind the mist (in both mask words, and with fog past the grid), and mist, thin walls and thin ellipsoids sliding through slices.
World: extraction, moving, removal, and spawning from a cell.
Cooker: reading a cell and all its error messages, including density over 4 and a non-number edge. The fuzz test covers the new array, and the format refuses over-limit density, NaN, a negative edge or tint and an unknown shape.
Mutations against passing controls: 17 earlier, 16 caught. The survivor makes samples keep to the fog's span in a froxel that only a volume fills, where a test image shows no difference. After the review, 16 more on the new code, all caught; three of them survived at first and led to stronger tests. A control failing once caught a limit of mine that was too tight for ellipsoids: even 32 points vary as much, because a thin ellipsoid really does change along one ray.
The later reviews' fixes: 5 more mutations, all caught, again after one test that passed for the wrong reason (the render graph's pool lets go of textures over time) was rewritten. The third review repeated the silhouette finding, and suggested speed-ups of the ground correction and its constants that I haven't taken (about 0.26 ms in the 65-volume stress case). That silhouette finding I couldn't reproduce: the ground correction adds 2 levels to a step of 19 that's there anyway, and easing the share made no difference at any setting, so nothing changed for it.
Tests pass in debug and ship (335 cases); clang-format and clang-tidy clean; smoke runs with 0 validation errors. The user judged the waving by eye in the running game, which is what led to the ground correction.
Limits
A little waving remains at a distance (see above), and the tests can't judge how it looks.
Where world fog and a volume in a slice are comparable, its light samples spread between their spans, so a thin wall's shadow is less exact there. Fine in practice so far, where volumes are far denser than the world's fog.
Past 64 in view, only stats().fog_volumes_dropped tells you: it isn't on the F1 panel yet.
The stats panel's camera line reads the last tick's place, not the interpolated view that's drawn.
de_slang_runtime assumes the presets' multi-config layout.
A volume thinner than a grid column across the view, about 1 m at 100 m, can still fall between columns: a thin layer seen edge-on pops as the camera rises or falls.
Volumes need the grid, so with no volume_distance_m they don't exist, and they end at its reach: from about 115 m they fade out, by 128 m they're gone.
They take the environment's fog colours, so an environment with none gives black mist.
A moving tinted volume's colour lags by about 20 frames, as light in the grid does.
Sharpness is limited by the grid's 8-pixel columns and slices. Soft mist suits it; a crisp wall doesn't.
No animated noise, and no raymarch at screen resolution.
Over 64 in view, the farthest go, and stats().fog_volumes_dropped counts them.
