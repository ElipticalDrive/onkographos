Adds the showcase's first figure of a backward operation, merges the two figure sections into one gallery, and clears two smaller items that were outstanding.
Differentiable rendering showcase
examples/differentiable_rendering/main.py optimises a raw SDF grid into a target shape under multi-view silhouette and depth loss, with diff_marching_cubes carrying the gradient from rendered pixels back into the field. Nothing on the site showed it; the other fourteen figures are all forward operations.
The new fig_diffrender runs that optimisation at the example's own defaults — 128³ grid, 1,000 iterations, 8 random views at 2048×2048 — and photographs it converging on the Happy Buddha. The whole run takes 32 s, so no reduced configuration was needed.

The fourteen existing figures are all forward operations, so nothing on the
site showed that the extractors are differentiable. This adds an Others card
that runs the optimisation from examples/differentiable_rendering/main.py
(--method mc) and photographs it converging.

A 128^3 field is initialised to uniform noise and optimised for 1,000
iterations against 8 random views at 2048x2048, supervised only by silhouette
mask and depth difference against the Happy Buddha. diff_marching_cubes is
what carries the gradient from rendered pixels back into the field; no loss
ever touches the surface directly.

Six panels at the checkpoints where the result actually changes -- iterations
0, 10, 30, 75, the final mesh and the source. Measured: 1,633,759 faces at
loss 1.545 and Chamfer 0.549 falling to 126,852 faces at loss 0.009 and
Chamfer 0.060, against a 98,601-face source. The whole run takes 32s, so the
figure uses the example's own defaults rather than a reduced configuration.

Two details worth knowing:

- The example's camera and mask/depth helper is also named render.py, and
  sys.modules["render"] is already this package's renderer, so a plain import
  would silently return the wrong module. It is loaded by path under its own
  name instead, and raises if the example ever moves.
- docs/_figures/render.py keeps its no-kaolin rule: kaolin drives the
  training loop only, while every panel is still drawn by the site's own
  renderer at a fixed camera, so the trajectory changes shape rather than
  size.
