The mesh path builds a SolidOperation::BoundedHalfSpace as a slab only 2 × the boundary's own extent deep:
kernel/crates/algorithms/construction/construct/src/half_space.rs
Lines 97 to 108
in
975db24
// Size the slab from the boundary's own extent. A profile spanning 10
// units gets a slab proportional to 10, so the construction carries no
// hidden absolute length.
let mut extent: Scalar = 0.0;
for p in boundary.outer.iter().chain(boundary.holes.iter().flatten()) {
extent = extent.max(p.x.abs()).max(p.y.abs());
}
// No zero-extent guard here: a boundary with no area cannot be
// triangulated, so `loft` rejects it downstream with a message naming
// the real problem. A guard here was unreachable, and unreachable
// validation reads as a promise the code cannot keep.
let depth = extent * margin.factor();
It uses ClipMargin::new(2.0):
kernel/crates/execution/compile/src/compiler.rs
Lines 1043 to 1047
in
975db24
let rings = self.boundary_rings(graph, *boundary, chord_error(options), options)?;
// The declared margin is the contract's own knob for how far
// an unbounded half-space extends before it can be meshed.
let margin = axiolid_primitive::ClipMargin::new(2.0)
.expect("2.0 is a valid positive clip margin");
The depth depends only on the boundary. It does not depend on the subject the half-space is combined with. Where the subject reaches further along the normal than that depth on the kept side, the slab ends inside the subject. A difference then removes too little, and an intersection keeps too little, with no error. That holds even with today's normal sweep. A small boundary over a tall subject is enough: a 0.5 m opening boundary clipping a 20 m wall along the normal.
The unbounded HalfSpace does not have this problem. It is sized from its subject (construct::half_space::for_subject), and openbimrs/ifc reports the exact path's exact/clip.rs does the same. The bounded one is built standalone, before its subject is known.
Needed
Size the bounded half-space's slab from the subject it meets: its bounds along the sweep axis, plus margin. Route BoundedHalfSpace operands of a boolean the way HalfSpace clips are routed. Where no subject is known (a bounded half-space as a root, or combined in a way that cannot be sized), refuse by name rather than emit an arbitrary slab. The same applies to the new PlacementZ axis from #321.
Done when
A boundary much smaller than the subject's extent along the sweep cuts the subject's full height. Check it against the analytic volume.
A root BoundedHalfSpace with no subject is refused by name.
A mutation probe covers the sizing.
Reported by openbimrs/ifc (from the ifc#515 work).

Confirmed by openbimrs/ifc on a real third-party model (not shareable). An aligned IfcPolygonalBoundedHalfSpace (+Z plane, AgreementFlag .F., boundary 238.5 × 350 mm) over a 16 m wall removes only a 0.7 m slab (0.35 × ClipMargin 2.0), so the wall stays 4.76 m too tall. The exact compiler gets it right, because it sizes from the subject's envelope. compile_mesh_with_deviation reports a bound of 0.175 m with meets_requested false.
There is a synthetic, parameter-derived repro on openbimrs/ifc main:

test: crates/ifc-geometry/tests/bounded_halfspace_depth.rs
result: mesh 57.226391331354 m³, against 56.887388852960 m³ exact and analytic.
