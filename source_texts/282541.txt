Part of #356. Needs the mesh input (#372) and the BVH (#373).
Goal
Give mesh surfaces their appearance from image textures whose light was computed offline (for example global illumination baked by a path tracer), with defined units, defined filtering and a named spectral method, so the engine's lens, sensor and processing chain runs unchanged on top.
Scope
Material kinds (proposal):
baked-radiance: a texture holds view-independent outgoing radiance (Lambertian assumption) in linear RGB or in declared spectral bands.
albedo-irradiance: an albedo (reflectance) texture on uv0 times an irradiance map on uv1, giving L = ρ·E/π. This decouples high-resolution surface detail from lower-resolution lighting.
albedo under the scene's uniform lighting: test-only (analytic validation and parity with primitives), not intended to depict a lit scene.
Texture input: the caller passes decoded typed arrays with a declared encoding, never image files:
srgb-8bit (IEC 61966-2-1 transfer function, decoded to linear before any filtering), for albedo only;
linear-half or linear-float, required for radiance and irradiance (8-bit is refused for light because it clips and bands).
The engine does no image decoding or network I/O.
Filtering, defined in linear light:
texel-centre convention: glTF 2.0 UV (0,0) is the image's top-left corner;
wrap modes clamp and repeat;
bilinear within a level; mip pyramid built by 2×2 box averaging in linear light (deterministic, part of the digest);
level of detail from the pixel footprint at the hit by a ray cone (Akenine-Möller et al., "Texture Level of Detail Strategies for Real-Time Ray Tracing", Ray Tracing Gems, 2019, ch. 20). The cone follows the pixel footprint only; defocus blur comes from integrating pupil rays, so the cone is not widened by the aperture (that would blur twice);
LOD selection uses only correctly rounded operations, or a portable log2 helper (src/core/portable-math.ts), so tiles stay byte-identical.
Absolute scale (declared, not measured): radiance or irradiance textures carry a declared absolute scale, defined by a reference patch: a surface of declared reflectance (for example 0.18) under a declared illuminance E has L = ρ·E/π. The scale is recorded in assumptions with kind: "declared", so callers can say it is not a measurement.
RGB to spectral: linear RGB is upsampled to spectra by a named, published method, labelled as an approximation in assumptions:
reflectance (bounded 0–1): Jakob and Hanika, "A Low-Dimensional Function Space for Efficient Spectral Upsampling", CGF 38(2) 2019, doi:10.1111/cgf.13626 (proposed default), or Smits 1999 / Mallett and Yuksel 2019 as declared alternatives;
radiance and irradiance (unbounded): the same method on the chromaticity times a declared illuminant, documented.
Spectral-band textures skip upsampling.
Colour space: RGB textures declare primaries and white point (proposed: BT.709 primaries, D65); other spaces are refused rather than guessed.
Non-goals
View-dependent shading (gloss, mirrors, glass, normal or roughness maps), texture compression formats (the caller decodes), procedural textures, image decoding.
Acceptance
Sampling tests: texel centres, wrap modes, bilinear weights, mip construction, LOD selection, all against hand-computed values.
Linear-light test: a 50 % checker in sRGB 8-bit filters to linear 0.5 mean reflectance, not to sRGB 0.5.
Aliasing test: a fine checker at large output pitch converges to its linear mean without moiré beyond a declared tolerance.
Absolute-scale test: a reference patch reproduces L = ρ·E/π to float tolerance; exposure and metering match the equivalent uniform-reflectance primitive.
Upsampling round trip under the declared illuminant within a stated tolerance; recorded as an approximation.
Region tiles byte-identical; seeds unchanged.
Cost of texture lookups measured per site and added to the cost table.
Risks
Baked lighting is correct only for Lambertian surfaces; view-dependent effects cannot be represented and must stay out of scope or be labelled by the caller.
RGB light transport (colour bleeding computed in RGB) differs from spectral transport; the upsampling label must say so.
Lightmap seams and texel density are caller-side authoring concerns, but the filtering rules must be stated so callers can pad charts correctly.
Related: #358, #361, #277.

Two additions to the scope (2026-10-19):
Texture magnification diagnostic. Report, per capture (and per region), the ratio of texel size to the pixel footprint at the hit (texels per pixel footprint), as a minimum and a distribution. When the footprint is smaller than a texel, detail is limited by the texture, not by the lens or sensor; a caller needs that fact to label 100 % views honestly. Example (estimate): at 6 µm pitch and 50 mm, the object-space footprint is p·(s − f)/f = 54 µm at 0.5 m and 114 µm at 1 m, so a 4096-texel texture wrapped around a 0.28 m circumference (69 µm per texel) is texture-limited at 0.5 m.
Memory. Keep textures in their supplied compact form (Uint8 or half/float32) and decode at lookup; never expand them to float64 arrays. Estimate: one 4096² RGB texture is 48 MiB as Uint8 but 384 MiB as float64. Document the per-worker memory, since each worker may hold its own copy. Acceptance adds a measured memory figure for a reference scene.
