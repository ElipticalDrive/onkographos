First of five PRs into the material-textures integration branch. This replaces the old lighting model, which made everything look like generic metal, with physically based shading that every upcoming material (metal, plastic, stone, wood, glass) builds on.
Changes
Linear color space. Light, ambient and material colors (picked in sRGB) are converted to linear in Python (lighting.srgb_to_linear, the exact sRGB curve). The text shader does all its math in linear and encodes its output back to sRGB. The background pass is untouched, so the photo stays bit-exact.
Metallic-roughness shading (GGX distribution, Smith geometry, Schlick Fresnel) replaces Blinn-Phong. MaterialParams.specular / shininess are replaced by metallic (0 = plastic, stone, wood; 1 = metal, which tints its highlight) and roughness (polished ↔ matte).
The photo as environment. Glossy surfaces reflect a blurred copy of the background photo: a sphere-map lookup on the reflection direction, blurred by roughness through the photo's mipmaps. New Lighting controls: Reflect the photo (default on) and Reflections strength. With no photo, or the toggle off, the ambient color is reflected.
Panel: Material gets Metallic and Roughness sliders; Lighting gets the reflections toggle and strength.
Docs: AGENTS.md "Shading" section (linear-light rule, BRDF, reflections, how the render tests measure); README tips updated.
Look change (intended)
The default gold now reflects the scene (sky above, floor below, a horizon line across each letter) instead of reading as flat metallic paint. As agreed, exports no longer match v0.6.0 pixel for pixel.
Verification
uv run pytest -q → 252 passed (+6). New GPU property tests:
metal highlights take the base color, while plastic highlights stay white (saturation of the brightest pixels: <0.1 vs >0.3);
rougher surfaces have dimmer peak highlights;
a mirror-like metal takes on a red vs blue scene's color only when Reflect the photo is on (with it off, interior pixels are unchanged);
reflection strength scales metal brightness;
sRGB conversion matches the standard curve.
The 30 FPS interactive test still passes.
Visual: side-by-side renders of polished gold, red plastic, brushed gold and mirror chrome, plus v0.6.0 vs new default.
Next
Material types and presets (Metal, Plastic, Stone, Wood, Glass) with per-material controls.
