Abstract
Computer graphics rendering is confronted with the constant dilemma of compromising between performance and visual quality. Rasterization prevails in real-time systems with speed but at the cost of realism by approximating lighting. Ray tracing, though achieving photorealistic effects such as reflections and global illumination, is computationally expensive. This work investigates a hybrid rendering scheme where rasterization is used for base visibility and selective ray tracing for high-impact effects. We recommend an optimized pipeline based on acceleration structures and hardware-accelerated ray tracing (e.g., RT cores), in combination with adaptive denoising and temporal filtering methods to provide real-time feasibility. To additionally promote efficiency, we present the Adaptive Ray Budget Allocation (ARBA) algorithm that dynamically allocates ray tracing workloads depending on scene complexity and perceptual importance. Case studies of contemporary games like Marvel’s Spider-Man and Batman: Arkham Knight show enhanced visual quality without compromising frame rate. The framework presented here provides a scalable solution that bridges cinematic realism and interactive performance.
