Abstract
Direct volume rendering (DVR) helps data interpretation by allowing users to focus attention on specific regions in a volume that are of most interest. The effective generation of these regions of interest (ROIs)-based DVR images, however, still remains a major challenge. It involved the intricate process of assigning appropriate optical parameters (opacity and/or color) to the ROIs and other regions in the volume using transfer function (TF) specification. Achieving an optimal TF often requires multiple iterations of a trial-and-error process, starting from scratch in each case, which is time-consuming and labor-intensive. To address this issue, there have been various TF optimization approaches, which could automatically fine-tune an initial TF toward an optimal solution satisfying a pre-defined objective metric. Although effective, the previous objective metrics exhibit two inherent limitations: (i) they were only capable of optimizing ROIs along an individual viewing ray of the volume, and the relationship with neighboring ROIs (as context) from other rays could not be controlled; (ii) the TF parameters to be optimized were restricted to opacity (i.e., the observability of ROIs) and did not include color which is also important to enhance the visual distinction of ROIs. In this work, we propose a new TF optimization approach by introducing an objective metric using visual saliency. Visual saliency is a biologically-inspired measure to aid the identification of ROIs considered important relative to other regions within an image according to the human visual system. Our novel adoption of visual saliency enables the TF optimization to take into account all ROIs in a volume (with its rendered image) implicitly. In addition, both opacity and color parameters can be applied to the TF optimization. Given the user-assigned target visual saliency of ROIs, our approach can produce more controllable and complete ROIs-based DVR images. We outline our approach by applications to a variety of volumetric datasets and highlight its advantages in comparison to the dominant TF optimization approaches that use visibility-based objective metrics.

Introduction
Direct volume rendering (DVR) is one pillar of modern scientific visualization techniques and has been applied to a wide variety of research and industry fields where data are volumetric [1], [2]. The crucial capability of DVR is to enable visual emphasis on particular regions in a volume while at the same time complementing them with additional context, e.g., the position and vicinity of pathologies to other involved anatomies. As such, it can assist a user in effectively analyzing and interpreting relevant information by allowing them to focus attention on those regions of interest (ROIs). Transfer functions (TF) play an indispensable role in generating ROI-based DVR images [3]. TFs enable a user to interactively specify what regions, hence ROIs, are included and how those ROIs are depicted in the resulting DVR images. Users achieve this by assigning appropriate optical parameters (opacity and color) to all voxels of a volume. This manual process in a TF parameter space is non-intuitive, repetitive, and time-consuming [3]. Static TFs, even one that has been carefully pre-specified, cannot be universally applied to all cases, and manual TF optimization must be involved to some extent for new volumes and visualization scenarios. There has been extensive research carried out to address the manual nature of the TF design, and these include multi-dimensional TFs [4], [5], [6], [7] and image-based TFs [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], of which the pros and cons will be discussed in the Related Works section.
Alternative approaches have been investigated to automatically and iteratively fine-tunes the initial TFs toward the optimal solution that satisfies a pre-defined objective metric [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21]. A pioneering, dominant objective metric proposed by Correa et al. [13] was based on visibility, which represents how much each ROI is observable inside a DVR image. The use of this object metric enabled the automated generation of an optimal TF that ensures the user-assigned target visibility of ROIs, by fine-tuning the opacity parameters of other occluding regions. A number of investigators have improved the visibility-based objective metric by introducing different optimization algorithms [14], [15], enhancing visibility computations [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], and voxel-wise modulation [21]. In all of these visibility-based TF optimization approaches, the visibility computation was inherently independent of each viewing ray that was cast from the DVR image to the volume (i.e., volume raycasting). This means that those approaches were only capable of optimizing ROIs along the same ray and could not include neighboring ROIs from the other rays, which need to be optimized as context to be emphasized, otherwise to be suppressed. Additionally, as the name of the objective metric indicates, the TF parameters to be optimized were limited in opacity, and color, which is also important to determine the visual attention of ROIs, was not included.
In this work, we propose a new TF optimization approach utilizing visual saliency as an objective metric. Visual saliency is a biologically-inspired property that refers to regions that the human visual system considers to be important relative to other regions thus it takes into account all regions within an image. In our adaptation, we utilize visual saliency to measure how much each ROI is visually apparent against other regions and to obtain an optimized TF (i.e., DVR) that satisfies the user-assigned target visual saliency of ROIs. Our approach has two novel contributions: it can (i) optimize all ROIs in a volume implicitly due to that our visual saliency-based objective metric is not limited to individual viewing rays of a volume, but can be computed within a DVR image that projects all ROIs along all viewing rays; and (ii) optimize not only opacity but also color parameters in a separate or sequential manner. In addition, our approach enabled the direct bounding box selection of ROIs in the DVR image space a user usually interacts with. This can be more straightforward when compared to the traditional indirect ways, e.g., the one-dimensional (1D) TFs requiring a user to select an intensity range in the TF parameter space. To sum up, we propose an enhanced TF optimization approach that is more controllable and intuitive and produces ROI-based DVR images according to visual attention. We demonstrate the characteristics and utility of our approach via experiments across a variety of scientific volumetric datasets and discuss its advantages over the visibility-based counterparts [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20].

Complex image scenes are a challenge in the collaborative saliency object detection task in the field of saliency detection, such as the inability to accurately locate salient object, surrounding background information affecting object recognition, and the inability to fuse multi-layer collaborative features well. To solve these problems, we propose a long-range dependent and dual-stream guided feature fusion network. Firstly, we enhance saliency feature by the proposed coordinate attention module so that the network can learn a better feature representation. Secondly, we capture the long-range dependency information of image feature by the proposed non-local module, to obtain more comprehensive contextual complex information. At lastly, we propose a dual-stream guided network to fuse multiple layers of synergistic saliency features. The dual-stream guided network includes classification streams and mask streams, and the layers in the decoding network are guided to fuse the feature of each layer to output more accurate synoptic saliency prediction map. The experimental results show that our method is superior to the existing methods on three common datasets: CoSal2015, CoSOD3k, and CoCA.
