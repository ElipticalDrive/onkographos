The last item from the Trainer request: "redesign the skin look (like what was done with the hand model) for the rest of the models to look more human and less blocky/scaly".
Cause
The other models: the generic display mesh (build_model._build_mesh) runs marching cubes on a 0/1 tissue mask at a coarse step, with no smoothing. The voxel staircase is baked into the skin.
The hand: fixed in c60abff with a blurred occupancy field, marching cubes on that soft field, Taubin smoothing and one opaque tone.
Change
services/compute/app/build_skin.py applies the hand's method to the other eight body models. It works from each model's shipped voxel volume, so no source CT is needed:
Patient only: the largest connected piece with holes filled (drops cables and slivers of a neighbouring limb).
Smoothing: a blur of about 1.5 voxels (1.4–2.2 mm), marching cubes at about 2.6 mm (4 mm at 2 mm voxels), then Taubin ×14.
Output: <name>_skin.glb beside the model, plus one skinMesh line in its header (otherwise byte-for-byte unchanged). app.js already shows a skin mesh in place of the plain one when a model ships it, as for the hand.
Tone: the hand photo-skin's mean colour in linear form, which is what glTF COLOR_0 expects. My first build wrote an sRGB tone and rendered pale grey-green under the room's cyan light. The tone is brightened by about a third: a trunk sits mostly outside the collimator lamp's light in the dimmed room, and at the hand's value it read dark brown.
