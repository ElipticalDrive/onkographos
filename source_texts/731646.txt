Summary
Add the rotation action of quaternions on 3-space and the map to 3×3 matrices, generically over a commutative ring or field, with no angles and no floating point. Angles, axis-angle and Euler conventions are the second issue.
Background
For an invertible quaternion q and a pure quaternion v = xi + yj + zk, the map v ↦ q v q⁻¹ preserves the pure quaternions and the norm, so it is a rotation. Since q⁻¹ = q̄ / N(q), the matrix entries are rational functions of the coefficients of q:
```
            1   ⎡ w²+x²−y²−z²   2(xy − wz)     2(xz + wy)  ⎤
R(q) =  ――――――  ⎢ 2(xy + wz)    w²−x²+y²−z²    2(yz − wx)  ⎥
         N(q)   ⎣ 2(xz − wy)    2(yz + wx)     w²−x²−y²+z² ⎦
```
This makes sense over any field, and every nonzero rational quaternion gives an exact rational rotation matrix. That is what lets the tests be exact.
Scope
pure(x, y, z) / vectorPart / isPure on Quaternion<R> (some may exist; reuse).
rotate(field, q, v): the action q v q⁻¹, throwing ArithmeticException when q is not invertible; and the division-free unit version q v q̄ over a ring.
rotationMatrix(field, q): DenseMat<F> by the formula above, and the unnormalized ring version N(q)·R(q).
The statement that q ↦ R(q) is a group homomorphism from the units of the quaternions, with kernel the nonzero scalars (so q and −q, and more generally λq, give the same rotation).
Cross and dot product recovered from the quaternion product of pure quaternions: uv = −⟨u,v⟩ + u×v.
Decide placement: QuaternionAlgebras vs a new QuaternionRotations object. Record the handedness and active/passive convention once, in KDoc and the README.
README section.
Tests (all exact over ℚ, plus a prime field)
R(q) is orthogonal with determinant 1: RᵀR = I, det R = 1.
R(q₁q₂) = R(q₁)R(q₂); R(λq) = R(q) for λ ≠ 0; R(1) = I; R(q̄) = R(q)ᵀ.
rotate(q, v) agrees with R(q)·v; the result is pure; the norm of v is preserved.
The vector part of q is fixed by R(q) (it is the axis).
Concrete cases: q = i, j, k give the three half-turns; q = 1 + i gives the quarter-turn about the x-axis; the 24 Hurwitz units give the rotation group of the tetrahedron (12 distinct matrices).
A non-invertible q throws ArithmeticException.
Out of scope
Angles and transcendental functions (issue 2 of 2); SO(4) via pairs of quaternions; octonionic triality.
