What
Marching cubes gives each vertex the field's gradient at its own position. Until now every vertex of a cell carried the cell's 8-corner gradient. Both kernels change: the unshared one the live path uses and the vertex-sharing one the player uses. There is no switch, and geometry is untouched: positions, winding and triangle counts are bit-identical to main.
The normal. Central differences at the edge's two end voxels, mixed by the vertex's ratio along the edge (the same ratio as the position), then normalized. A vertex on a corner whose distance is the iso-value takes that corner's gradient. Where the gradient vanishes, the normal is the edge's axis toward increasing distance.
Missing neighbours. A neighbour that is unallocated or below the weight threshold is never used. That axis takes the one-sided difference toward the cell, whose voxels the gather already read.
No seams. A corner's gradient depends on its voxel alone, so a normal depends on its edge alone. At block_size ≥ 2 (every in-tree caller uses 8), every cell and both kernels give a vertex the same normal, so shading has no seam between cells or blocks.
Memory and registers. The differences reach one voxel past the cell, so each workgroup also resolves its block's 3×3×3 neighbourhood (27 more probes). Shared memory grows by 108 B in SPIR-V: 44 → 152 B in the default kernel and 8452 → 8560 B in the sharing kernel. What sets the speed is registers: on the RTX 4090 the default kernel goes from 64 to 80 registers and the sharing kernel from 122 to 128 (VK_KHR_pipeline_executable_properties).
Speed. Three choices, each measured:
The eight corner gradients are kept in registers.
Neighbour samples are branch-free. Branching made the extract 5–11% slower on the live room.
The default kernel writes each vertex as soon as it is computed. Holding a triangle's three vertices to the end took it to 116 registers, and its live-room extract to 1.29 ms instead of 1.04 ms on the 4090.
Tried and slower, or no gain (live room, against the kernels above):
computing only the corners a crossing edge touches: slower;
computing each vertex's two corner gradients on demand: +0.11 / +0.23 ms (unshared / sharing) on the 4090 and +0.68 / +0.23 ms on the M5 Max;
one probe round instead of two, skipping the 7 blocks no sample reaches: −0.04 / −0.02 ms on the M5 Max (2% at most);
an early exit from the sharing kernel's owned-edge pass: +0.07 ms on the M5 Max (sharing kernel);
loading the weight and the distance together, or moving the probes to other lanes: no change.
Docs. DESIGN.md (mesh) states the vertex normal and its cost, and the TODO(mesh) is gone. CHANGELOG 0.2.1. No locked decision changes.
Not affected. Mesh-to-SDF conversion (voxelize) signs by face normals computed from the input triangles. It never reads a vertex normal and is unchanged.
Why
Per-cell normals make lighting constant over each cell and jump at every cell face. Curved and tilted surfaces show the blocky, checkered look seen in the viewer and the player.
Tests
marching_cubes_sparse_test now checks:
a welded position carries one normal in each kernel, and the two kernels agree (< 1e-5);
on the analytic sphere, normals are within 0.5° of the true normal, with a mean under 0.1° (measured 0.024° mean / 0.063° worst on M5 Max, 0.027° / 0.066° on RTX 4090; per-cell normals measured 2.7° mean);
with a cap left unobserved (NaN distances, weight 0) and blocks removed, normals stay finite, unit and within 4° (measured 1.27°), in both kernels.
marching_cubes_corner_test now also checks the normal of a vertex on an exact-iso corner: the plane's normal, ±1 along its axis and 0 across it (within 1e-6), in both kernels.
These checks fail against main's kernels. They also fail against a sample that skips the weight test, a wrong neighbourhood, and taking the other end of the edge at an exact-iso corner.
Full suite, Release, VGRID_WARNINGS_AS_ERRORS=ON, VKC_TEST_VALIDATION=1, VKC_REQUIRE_VULKAN_DEVICE=1:
Apple M5 Max (MoltenVK): 70/70 passed.
NVIDIA RTX 4090 (Linux, driver 615.71.10): 70/70 passed.
The suite cannot check that geometry matches main, so a bench compared main and the branch on both devices directly:
Positions. A hash of the sorted triangles (positions and winding) is identical on main and the branch for both workloads and both kernels:

differences at its edge's two end voxels, mixed by the vertex's ratio along
the edge, normalized. An unallocated or unobserved neighbour is never read;
that axis takes the one-sided difference toward the cell. The normal depends

- Each workgroup also resolves its block's 3x3x3 neighbourhood
  (s_gradient_block, 27 probes): the default kernel's shared memory goes
  from 44 to 152 B, the sharing kernel's from 8452 to 8560 B.
- The corner gradients live in eight registers, filled once per emitting
  cell; the sharing kernel's owned-edge pass fills the four it reads.

  0 and is discarded, and the weight is tested after both loads, so a

mcWriteTriangle held all three vertices (27 floats) until the end, beside
the eight corner gradients, because the compiler cannot move a write past
reads of buffers the vertex buffer may alias. On an RTX 4090 the default
kernel took 116 registers; writing each vertex right after mcEdgeVertex
takes it to 80. Table entry k is still vertex 0, 2, 1 (reversed winding).

Interleaved on a decoded live room capture (1.24 M triangles, Release),
the default kernel's extract dispatch goes from 1.29 to 1.04 ms on an RTX
4090 and from 2.17 to 2.10 ms on an M5 Max; the sharing kernel does not
call it. Positions, winding and normals are unchanged on both devices.
DESIGN.md's cost figures follow.
