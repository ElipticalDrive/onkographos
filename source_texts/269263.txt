Why not dsp_fft_r2c
The existing C++ export is a packed real-to-complex 1-D transform capped at N ≤ 4096. An ocean needs a 2-D complex transform of the spectrum grid, which that signature cannot express. Two IFFTs per update, not three: D̃x + i·D̃z rides in one complex grid.
Buoyancy contract
WaveSystem.getWaterHeight() / getWaterHeightBatch() short-circuit to OceanFFTField.heightAt() — a bilinear read of the same heights grid that gets packed into the displacement texture. So Tugboat.tsx, FoamSystem, Ship.tsx bob and the visible surface cannot drift apart.
Rejected: GPU readback (a mapAsync on the render path stalls the frame) and a second analytic evaluation (a different approximation of the same field, guaranteed to diverge under tuning).
Two deliberate approximations, documented in full: choppy horizontal displacement is ignored by height queries (error bounded by choppiness × amplitude, and the vertex shader makes the same approximation for its own normals), and the FFT tier always answers for "now" (every in-tree caller already passes waveSystem.getTime()).
Hull sampling gets cheaper: one bilinear read replaces a 4-layer Gerstner sum per probe.
Determinism
Spectrum seeds through Rng.fork(), which derives an independent stream without advancing the sim RNG.
The field is absent from captureSimSnapshot(), so the headless hash is identical with the tier on or off — asserted directly against runHeadlessTicks. This is what keeps the future GPU path (a non-bit-exact backend) out of the replay fingerprint.
Transform cadence is driven by accumulated SIM_DT, not frame rate.
Drive-by fix worth a look
gerstnerTsl applied elevation to local Y. The water plane is a planeGeometry rotated −90° about X, so local axes map to world as x→+X, y→−Z, z→+Y — elevation belongs on local Z. As written it displaced the sheet sideways and left the surface visually flat. Fixed here because the FFT displacement would otherwise have inherited the same bug and been invisible.
