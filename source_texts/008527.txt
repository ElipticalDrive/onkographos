A Hybrid and Hierarchical Ray Tracing Method for Efficient 3D Gaussian Rendering | alphaXiv
AbstractPaper
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is a powerful representation for real-time neural reconstruction and rendering. While existing 3DGS systems rely on rasterization for efficiency, recent work explores ray tracing to enable secondary lighting, highly distorted cameras, and depth-of-field effects. However, compared with rasterization, ray-traced 3DGS introduces three major challenges: (1) the highly non-uniform spatial distribution of massive numbers of Gaussian primitives creates severe load imbalance; (2) neighboring pixels often generate rays with highly similar traversal paths, leading to substantial redundant traversal overhead; and (3) supporting both Gaussian and triangle primitives yields heterogeneous intersection workloads that are not well matched to existing GPU architectures. We present H2-Ray, a hybrid and hierarchical ray tracing architecture for efficient 3D Gaussian rendering. H2-Ray introduces three techniques. Load-Balanced Spatial BVH (LB-SBVH) partitions the scene into uniform grids and uses density-aware representations to convert an imbalanced global workload into balanced local traversal units. Ray-Cluster Path Reuse (RCPR) lets a proxy ray share traversal state with neighboring rays to amortize redundant traversal. The Unified Intersection Engine (UIE) supports mixed Gaussian and triangle intersections through a mode-multiplexed datapath that shares 40% of its floating-point units across both primitive types. Across 8 scenes, H2-Ray achieves a geometric-mean speedup of 3.77x and a maximum speedup of 9.26x over RaySplats[p8] on the NVIDIA RTX A5000, combining a cycle-accurate model of the modified RT Core with measured CUDA timings.

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents a scene with many soft, anisotropic Gaussian primitives. It renders efficiently by projecting and blending them. Rasterization is fast for ordinary views but less suited to effects that need per-ray visibility, such as reflections, refractions, fisheye cameras, and depth of field. Direct ray tracing runs into uneven Gaussian density, repeated traversal, and mixed primitive types.[p1]
H2-Ray organizes this work at three levels. It divides a lopsided scene into local regions, reuses shared traversal work among nearby rays, and lets Gaussian and triangle intersections share hardware. The authors frame it as extending what 3DGS can render without replacing the rest of the graphics pipeline. On an RTX A5000, it reaches a 3.77× geometric-mean speedup over RaySplats across eight scenes. This result combines cycle-accurately modeled RT-Core behavior with CUDA work measured on the GPU.

The plot combines modeled and measured components. Right: Table V reports cycles per ray for 1-grid and 27-grid cases and isolates modeled traversal.
Local regions and shared routes
A bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) is a tree that lets a ray skip regions unlikely to contain an intersected primitive. The scenes are lopsided. Under a uniform 838^{3}83 grid across the eight scenes, an average 69.8% of cells are empty, 20.9% are sparse (1–99 Gaussian centers), and 9.3% are dense (at least 100).[p4]
H2-Ray's partitioning scheme, LB-SBVH, works in four steps:
It divides the scene into uniform spatial cells.
It assigns each Gaussian to one cell by its center.
It gives dense cells local shallow BVHs and sparse non-empty cells flat lists.
It omits empty cells.
Conservative support-aware bounds let a ray reach a Gaussian's owning cell even if the Gaussian extends beyond its center cell.
The second level, RCPR, reuses routes. In each 3×3 tile, the center ray completes a traversal, and hardware stores its path prefix. Worker rays then start at an intermediate node, reached by "hopping back" a chosen number of BVH levels. More hops preserve more individual traversal and reduce the approximation. Zero hops can be fragile when a tile crosses a foreground/background boundary.
(a) A spatial grid with dense regions assigned local BVHs and sparse regions represented as lists; a conceptual illustration, not measured scene data. (b) In a 3×3 pixel-ray cluster, the center ray is the proxy and traverses the BVH. It shares an interior path with neighboring worker rays when their grid matches; rays that diverge fall back to root traversal.
Testing the shortcut
The authors tested hop distance against a full per-ray C++ renderer, not against RaySplats. At two hops, the mean PSNR difference from that baseline is −0.06 dB across eight selected representative views, and the largest per-scene loss is 0.13 dB. In Garden and Playroom, zero-hop losses reach 5.55 and 10.19 dB, respectively. This contrast explains why the authors use two hops.[p9] Those representative views do not bound performance on every camera.
What the speedup includes
The 3.77× figure is the geometric mean of per-scene speedups over RaySplats, not the ratio of mean runtimes. The arithmetic-mean runtime is 514.59 ms for RaySplats and 131.65 ms for H2-Ray. Scene speedups range from 1.89× on Stump to 9.26× on Garden. Timing excludes training/preprocessing, model loading, and image I/O.[p8]
The result does not isolate the contributions of LB-SBVH and RCPR. Worker-ray intersection and sorting run in CUDA kernels in the evaluated pipeline. As a result, the reported end-to-end runtime does not include the benefit of executing worker rays on the proposed intersection unit.[p8]
Image quality was compared on matched rendered views. There, the arithmetic mean of per-scene average PSNR differences is −0.034 dB versus RaySplats. The views are matched camera names, not a held-out test set, and can overlap training views. These averages do not rule out substantial individual-view differences.[p8]
The authors also examined the two largest drJohnson outliers. On those views, disabling proxy reuse improved PSNR by less than 0.4 dB, and neither that setting nor three hops consistently closed the RaySplats gap. The authors say remaining gaps may reflect implementation differences such as BVH structure, traversal order, or floating-point ordering.[p9]
A route to ray rendering
H2-Ray targets the scheduling and intersection costs of ray-traced Gaussian rendering. It does so with local scene partitioning, shared traversal among nearby rays, and hardware shared across primitive types. Its 3.77× geometric-mean speedup over RaySplats comes from a combined evaluation: modeled RT-Core traversal plus measured CUDA execution. It is not a measured speedup from a fabricated H2-Ray GPU.
