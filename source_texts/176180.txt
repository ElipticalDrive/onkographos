If you are on PC you can get Gracia on Steam here: https://store.steampowered.com/app/2802520/Gracia/
If you want to try on Quest standalone you can get it on sidequest: https://sidequestvr.com/app/29546/gracia
To capture Gaussian splatting you can use apps like Polycam or Luma AI and export the ply file
Before importing in Gracia I would suggest cleaning it up as shown in the video with Supersplat you can find here: https://playcanvas.com/supersplat/editor
If you like this make sure to check also this interview with Michael Rubloff from Radiancefields.com where we dive deeper into the topic of NeRFs and Gaussian splatting:
https://open.substack.com/pub/xraispotlight/p/tools-and-resources-to-learn-and?r=2umm8d&utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web

Less wild than you think -- it's just a duplicated set of splats underneath the plane. You can explore a (not very good) splat I took yesterday of a marina: https://lumalabs.ai/capture/9a162967-a593-430c-aafa-6051faf07d19
If you click and mouse around you can go under the water's surface and see the "reflections" are just fuzzier versions of the stuff up top, inverted.
This only works with flat reflective surfaces -- curved reflections are a much more complex space that splats don't accurately capture.
