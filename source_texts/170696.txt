Interesting ways to represent voxel data?
Hey everyone! I've got a bit of a general topic that I've been thinking about for a few days. I think it could be very applicable to many projects in this sub.
Voxels are a wonderfully tangible way to deal with 3D data. They are very fun to play with, and easy to interact with through code, but above all, extremely intuitive. This makes them the perfect tool for experimentation. What interesting ways have you found to visualize your voxel data? Shaded cube representations are everywhere, but just like raster images, this is a type of data that can be displayed in many ways (mag and min filters come to mind in 2D). Marching cubes are one thing, but what else? Additionally, there are many different things you could store in a voxel beyond just a boolean for whether it exists and a color. Per-voxel normals, for instance, would go a long way towards making a 3D object's voxel-based-nature much less apparent in lit scenes. What about flags for marking a voxel as "sharp" or "smooth" to help with the generation of an optimized, smoothed mesh? Distance fields are a brilliant use of voxel-like data, but rendering those tends to be a bit esoteric, and powerfully tedious to implement. Has anyone tried screen-space filtering for voxel-based objects in order to achieve a smooth silhouette? What about applying linear filtering to the colors of voxels that are sufficiently close to each other? What about rendering very dense voxel meshes? Voxels don't need to be boxes either and could be represented with GL_POINTS (or their equivalent in your API of choice), scaled to cover that voxel's volume, or any number of other techniques that I'm sure I've not even thought of.
Voxels are by far the most approachable form of 3D modelling, and it's a shame that voxel-based works are trapped by their very cute but frequently less-than-functional visual representations. This is a very scattershot post, but I'd really love to know basically anything anyone has tried or mused about. I look forward to your responses and I hope that this topic is welcome in this community!
