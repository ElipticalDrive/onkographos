The Volume Normalization Principle: Resolving Cross-Geometry Inconsistencies in
Parametric Cavity Analysis

Parametric instability analyses across cavity geometries have universally compared systems at
equal fractional boundary displacement ε, treating this as a fair basis for comparison. We show
this convention is thermodynamically inconsistent: parametric coupling strength scales with the
fractional energy modulation rate (1/V )(dV /dt) ∝ d ε̇, where d = 3, 2, 1 for spherical, cylindrical,
and rectangular geometries respectively. We introduce the volume normalization principle: fair
comparison requires equal fractional volume modulation δV /V , yielding εrect = 3εsph for equivalent parametric driving. This unifies the energy-based formalism of gravitational-wave physics
with the displacement-normalized formalism of quantum optomechanics, and quantitatively resolves—without free parameters—a factor-of-three discrepancy in Advanced LIGO parametric instability thresholds observed by Evans et al. [Phys. Rev. Lett. 114, 161102 (2015)] that has persisted
without explanation for a decade. Validation across five experimental platforms spanning nine orders
of magnitude in threshold power confirms the principle’s universality.
INTRODUCTION
Parametric instabilities in cavities with time-varying
boundaries underpin quantum optomechanics [1],
gravitational-wave detection [2], and integrated photonics [3]. Since Moore’s foundational analysis [4], every
field has characterized and compared systems at equal
fractional boundary displacement ε = δR/R or δL/L,
adopted without challenge in all major reviews [1, 5, 6].
A systematic, geometry-correlated anomaly has accumulated in the experimental literature. Advanced
LIGO exhibits parametric instabilities at circulating
powers three times lower than spherical-cavity models
predict [2]. Tabletop Fabry–P’erot cavities exhibit parametric instabilities at power levels orders of magnitude
below spherical-cavity predictions [14]. Microdisk resonators achieve parametric oscillation consistently below
spherical-cavity predictions [15, 16]. These discrepancies,
shown in Fig. 1, form a systematic pattern spanning nine
orders of magnitude that has never been given a unified
explanation.
The resolution is a geometric fact that has been implicit in the physics but never made explicit as a universal comparison principle. Parametric coupling scales
with the fractional energy modulation rate, which for
fixed field amplitude equals the fractional volume rate
δV /V —not ε alone. Because V ∝ Rd , comparing cavities
at equal ε compares systems receiving d times different
amounts of parametric driving per cycle.

UNIFICATION OF TWO RESEARCH
TRADITIONS
The volume normalization principle resolves a methodological schism between two communities that developed
independently.
The gravitational-wave community, starting with Braginsky et al. [8], formulated parametric instability

The quantum optomechanics community standardized
on g0 ∝ ω/L ∝ ε [1, 5], without considering volumetric
equivalence across geometries.
Neither tradition identified δV /V as the universal comparison parameter. Each worked correctly within its own
domain, eliminating any internal motivation to question
the normalization. The error emerged only in crossgeometry comparison, which neither community had occasion to perform until LIGO. The LIGO factor-of-three
discrepancy arose precisely because spherical theory was
applied to rectangular geometry at equal ε—a comparison the formalisms were never designed to make.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
The volume normalization principle identifies δV /V
as the fundamental thermodynamic variable governing
parametric coupling—always present in the physics but
never made explicit as a universal cross-geometry com-
4
parison requirement. Braginsky’s LIGO formalism captured it implicitly for d = 1 through its integral structure; Law’s quantum optomechanical formalism did not,
because single-geometry analyses never required it. Our
contribution is making the principle explicit and universal, simultaneously explaining why each formalism succeeded in its own domain and why their predictions diverged across domains.
Several practical consequences follow immediately.
The factor-of-three threshold advantage of Fabry–Pérot
cavities over spherical resonators is purely geometric and
fully predictable—not the result of ad hoc material or
mode-specific effects. The algebraic coupling structure of
rectangular cavities (Gnn′ ≈ n versus Bessel-suppressed
Fsph ≈ 0.18) compounds with the volume factor to
give further enhancement at higher mode numbers [17].
Cylindrical cavities offer a complementary advantage: azimuthal boundary modulation enables parametric coupling at drive frequencies ∼5× lower than radial resonances [7].
The systematic pattern across five platforms spanning
nine orders of magnitude—from kilometer-scale LIGO
interferometers to nanoscale optomechanical crystals—
demonstrates that volume normalization is not a perturbative correction but the physically correct comparison
framework. The LIGO factor-of-three discrepancy, first
reported in these pages in 2015 [2], is quantitatively resolved.
