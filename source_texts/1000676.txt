Emissive layers take the hull's cubemap reflection
The *_emissive layers (a station's lit panels, a gate's inner ring, a ship's markings) are GoF2/Unlit: opaque and unlit, drawn over parts of the hull with its own atlas. Where they lie, the reflection GoF2/Hull gives the rest of the hull was gone, and those panels looked flat next to the shining hull around them.
New GoF2/EmissiveCube (Resources/GoF2Emissive/EmissiveCube.shader): the Unlit graph's look (_MainTex * vertex colour * _Color * _Glow) plus HullForwardPass's BumpRimCubeShader terms (the cubemap reflection and rim), with the same globals (_GoF2HullReflection, _GoF2HullRimColor, unity_SpecCube0).
New EmissiveReflection.Apply (called from AssembledObject.Awake): each emissive GoF2/Unlit material is swapped for a cached EmissiveCube copy. The normal map and specular come from the object's GoF2/Hull material that uses the same atlas.
Coverage:
117 of the 119 emissive layers reflect.
The Kaamo Club fx and the Void station glow have no specular atlas and stay as they are.
Station interiors use URP Lit, not GoF2/Hull, so they are untouched.
Planet ring sky (stations 120, 126, 130, 132)
Short bright dashes along the rings. sn_skybox_planet_ring_alpha is a thin slab: its top and bottom faces are 4.3 units apart, 76 units from the camera. Both faces were drawn with Cull Off. Their copies of the bands sat a little apart, and the alpha blend changed with the triangles' order in each of the 64 segments. GoF2/SkyLayer gets a _Cull property (default Off), and the ring culls back faces.
Faceted bands. The mesh's 64 segments, interpolated straight across each triangle, turned the quarter-circle texture's bands into chords. With _Polar on (ring only), a band's radius comes from the pixel's distance to the ring's centre (from the mesh bounds), and only the angle from the mesh's UV. The texture's inner and outer edges are transparent, so the 64-sided outline doesn't show.
Star map: the ring on these stations' planets
StarMap::initStarSystem adds map_planet_ring (16853) for texture index 9 / 16 / 21 (already in those prefabs) or stations 120, 126, 130, 132 (Status::orbitHasPlanetRing). The second case was missing.
StarMap.AddPlanetRing copies the ring from galaxymap_planet_009 onto the planet when it doesn't have one.
Affected: Katashán, Bak S'ondorr, O'muarkk, Bervegor.
Freighter lights vanished at distance
cargo_004_vossk_lights_emissive and cargo_001_midorian_lights_emissive were only in LOD 0, so they vanished at 35 000 units.
The original's LOD child meshes for them (17040 / 17041) don't exist in the data.
This HD build never leaves LOD 0 anyway (updateLod's loop is patched out).
They are now in every level, in both the assembled prefabs and assemblies.json (so a rebuild keeps them), like cargo_003_terran_lights_emissive.
(Added to this PR: a second commit)
Asteroid belt (systems 24-26): lit like the original
Lit on the wrong side. The belt's mesh normals point both ways, face by face, and GoF2/SkyLayer took abs(N.L), so the whole belt was lit as if the sun sat in its middle. The belt is a band around the camera, so the face we see is the one toward the camera: it is lit where it turns to the sun (the far side, opposite the sun) and shaded on the sun's side.
Flat asteroids. The original draws the belt with bump shader 28 (material 27160: skybox_asteroid_belt_diffuse + _normal_specular), but the remake used the diffuse only. GoF2/SkyLayer's lit mode now takes _BumpMap and _SpecMap (the _metallic_smoothness alpha) on a cotangent frame from the screen derivatives (the mesh's own normals are unusable), with a Blinn-Phong highlight. Each asteroid is now lit on its sun side.
sky_asteroid_belt.mat references both maps, and SpaceSceneBuilder sets them on rebuild.
Sky layers in the station rooms
StationLevel spawns the orbit's SkyLayers too, so they show through the hangar's and bar's openings like the backdrop already does:
the planet ring sky;
the supernova flares (normal and the "nasty" ones from mission 106);
the storms;
the asteroid belt.
SkyLayers builds the ring and the belt in AddRing / AddBelt.
Fog by distance, not view depth
URP's fog uses the clip-space depth, which is shorter toward the screen's edges for the same distance. A fogged station in the middle of the screen came out clear and close-looking at the edge, though the ship hadn't moved. The original fogs by the eye-to-vertex distance (computeLinearFogFactor(eyeDist)).
GoF2/Hull (HullForwardPass.hlsl) and GoF2/EmissiveCube now apply linear / exp / exp2 fog by the real distance to the camera.
The Shader Graph layers (additive, alpha, unlit, alpha-test) still use URP's fog.
