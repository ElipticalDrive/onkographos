Abstract: Spectral computed tomography (CT) utilizes photon-counting detectors to acquire multi-energy data for
reconstruct images. Due to the limited number of photons in each energy bin, spectral CT imaging suffers from
severe quantum noise, making clinical diagnosis more challenging. To address this limitation, we proposed a spectral
CT reconstruction model combining total variation (TV) and low-rank regularizations. In the proposed model, the
TV regularization was applied to suppress staircase artifacts in spatial domain, while the low-rank regularization
was utilized to reduce noise by exploiting the correlation among the spectral CT images in the spectral dimension.
To improve the efficiency of the spectral CT reconstruction algorithm, the Chambolle-Pock method was employed
to solve our proposed model. Firstly, we constructed the spectral CT reconstruction model combining TV and lowrank regularizations. Secondly, within the Chambolle-Pock optimization framework, the dual variables were
introduced to reformulate the reconstruction model as a convex-concave saddle-point problem, which was then
transformed into a different equivalent form. Finally, we derived the closed-form solution for each sub-problem in
this different equivalent form, thereby obtaining a single-loop iterative reconstruction algorithm. To validate the
algorithm’s efficacy, simulated head and thorax phantom data experiments were performed. Extensive experimental
results demonstrate the proposed algorithm’s outstanding performance in noise suppression, artifact reduction and
detail preservation compared with state-of-the-art methods.
Keywords: spectral CT, Total Variation, Low-Rank, Chambolle-Pock method, single-loop
1. Related Work
Recently, spectral computed tomography (CT) has emerged as a significant research direction in medical
imaging, because of the great potential for material decomposition[1,2] and disease detection[3]. To obtain multiple
sets of projection data with different spectral information in a single exposure, the photon-counting detector is
usually equipped in the spectral CT system. However, spectral projections measured by the photon-counting detector
usually suffer from strong noises[4], due to the limited number of received photons in each energy bin [5]. This
factor could result in the artifacts in spectral CT images. To address this issue, many state-of-art spectral CT
reconstruction algorithms[6-10] are proposed.
Ignoring the correlation between different energy bins, the conventional analytical reconstruction algorithms
(e.g., filtered back-projection algorithm[11] and iterative reconstruction algorithms[12-15] can directly employed to
reconstruct channel-independent images for each energy bin separately. Due to the strong noises in the spectral
projections, these reconstruction algorithms[16] will inevitably lead to obvious artifacts and noise in the
reconstruction results, negatively affecting the diagnostic accuracy in the subsequent procedure. Such reconstruction
strategies ignore the data correlation among different energy bins, leading to sub-optimal reconstruction results. To
overcome this drawback, many algorithms have been developed to make full use of the data correlation in the spectral
domain.
To address the aforementioned issue, model-based iterative reconstruction algorithms are currently widely
adopted in spectral CT imaging. On the one hand, many sparsity regularizations based reconstruction algorithms
have been developed for spectral CT. Tensor dictionary learning is introduced to explore the correlation among the
spectral CT images from different energy bins[4]. Wu et al.[17] integrated the gradient 𝑙0 norm and tensor
dictionary learning to eliminate artifacts effectively. Hu et al.[18] proposed a spectral image similarity-based tensorenhanced reconstruction (SISTER) method that integrates 𝑙0 norm minimization, tensor decomposition, and non-
local similarity for sparse-view CT reconstruction. Cueva et al.[19] proposed a synergistic multi-spectral CT
reconstruction with directional TV to preserve cross-channel structural information, enhancing image quality and
computational efficiency.
On the other hand, numerous works has been proven that the low-rank regularization play an important role in
spectral CT image reconstruction. The nuclear norm is an efficient constraint for low-rank regularization[20].
Semerci et al. [21] presented an iterative reconstruction algorithm for spectral CT that incorporated tensor nuclear
norm regularization. The non-local low-rank cube-based tensor is applied for low-dose spectral CT
reconstruction[22].
Meanwhile, combining both sparsity and low-rank prior information can further improve the reconstruction
performance of spectral CT. Li et al.[23] introduced a low-rank tensor decomposition with spatial-temporal total
variation regularization method to the reconstruction model. Niu et al. [24] presented a nonlocal low-rank and sparse
matrix decomposition for spectral CT reconstruction, which leveraged the similarity of patches. Xia et al.[25] utilized
the similarity in image-spectral tensors to assist spectral image reconstruction and obtained promising results. Chen
et al.[26] proposed a fourth-order nonlocal tensor decomposition model, where low-rank and sparsity constraints are
enforced via tensor-based weighted nuclear norms and TV norms. These iterative reconstruction algorithms
involving both sparsity and low-rank prior information are effective in improving the reconstruction results. However,
due to the nested loop structures, they are usually computationally expensive[18].
In this work, making full use of prior information to suppress noise and eliminate artifacts in the reconstruction
results, we proposed a TV and low-rank based spectral CT reconstruction algorithm (TV-LR-SCT). In the spectral
CT reconstruction model, the TV regularization was utilized to suppress staircase artifacts in each tomographic
image slice and the low-rank regularization was employed to reduce noise by exploiting the correlation among the
spectral CT images. To improve the efficiency of the iterative reconstruction algorithms, the Chambolle-Pock (CP)
method was applied to solve our proposed reconstruction model, obtaining a single-loop iterative reconstruction
algorithm for spectral CT. Avoiding the nested loop structures, our proposed algorithm effectively reduces the
computational complexity. Our contributions were mainly summarized as follows:
1. To sufficiently suppress staircase artifacts in each tomographic image slice and exploit the similarities among
different energy bins in spectral dimension, we proposed a spectral CT reconstruction model combining TV and lowrank regularizations.
2. To improve computational efficiency, we innovatively employed the CP method to solve the spectral CT
reconstruction model, obtaining a single-loop iterative spectral CT reconstruction algorithm.
3. Extensive experimental results demonstrate that our proposed algorithm outperforms several state-of-the-art
methods in both subjective visual assessment and objective quantitative metrics.

3 Experiments and Results
To evaluate the performance of our proposed method, simulated data were used in the experiments. The X-raybased Cancer Imaging Simulation Toolkit (XCIST)[29] is a new implementation of CatSim[30], which is developed
to simulate X-ray imaging for the phantom. In this work, XCIST was used to simulate 2D scans of the Shepp-Logan
head phantom and the clinical CT data from Low-Dose CT Image and Projection Data provided by the Mayo
Clinic[31]. The images of these two kinds of phantoms were converted into water and bone density maps based on
the value of pixels and their CT number, respectively. Then, these density maps were input to XCIST to yield the
corresponding projection data. The voltage and current of the X-ray source were set to 120 keV and 175 mA. To
obtain the spectral projection data, the energy spectrum of X-ray source was divided into 12 energy bins with a 10
keV increment. The size of each energy bin was set to512×512 pixels. There were 720 views evenly distributed over
360°. As we known, the spectral projection data suffer from strong noise, due to the limited number of received
photons in each energy bin. We set the current of the X-ray source to 1750 mA to suppress the noise in each energy
bin and the corresponding reconstruction results were treated as the reference images.
In the experiment, our proposed TV-LR-SCT algorithm was compared with several representative state-of-theart algorithms, including ASD-POCS[12], MDM-PCCT[32], FTVNNR[33], ASSIST[25], and SISTER[18]. In these
algorithms, the ASD-POCS algorithm is a promising algorithm for CT image reconstruction based on TV
regularization. The MDM-PCCT algorithm is a high-performance spectral CT reconstruction algorithm combining
multiple dynamic modulations. The FTVNNR algorithm is a novel reconstruction algorithm of the nested loop
structure based on both the TV regularization and the nuclear norm. The ASSIST and SISTER algorithms are the
spectral CT reconstruction algorithms utilizing the non-local feature similarity of patches in both spatial and spectral
domains. All experiments were performed on a workstation computer platform with an Intel® Core™ i7-9700K
CPU, 32 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. In this work, to quantitatively evaluate our proposed TV-LRSCT algorithm, four metrics were chosen to perform image quality evaluation[34], including peak-signal-to-noise
ratio (PSNR), structural similarity index (SSIM), root-mean-square error (RMSE), and visual information fidelity
(VIF).

Combining Fig. 3 and Table 2, it is found that there are significant differences in the performance of different
spectral CT reconstruction algorithms. Within the four energy bins, the PSNR, SSIM and VIF values of the ASDPOCS algorithm are relatively low, while the RMSE value is relatively high. We can see that the MDM algorithm

within the high-energy bins (e.g., (70, 80] keV). However, it exhibits slightly elevated RMSE values at certain energy
bins and suboptimal VIF performance. It is clear that the SISTER algorithm performs close to optimal in several
values, but there is still a certain gap compared with our proposed algorithm. Consistent with the visual results, the
proposed TV-LR-SCT achieved the highest PSNR, SSIM and VIF, and the lowest RMSE. It can be seen that TVLR-SCT indisputably improved these four metrics.
3.2.2 Clinical CT Data
(1) Visual evaluation
Fig.4 shows the typical thoracic slice reconstructed results by different methods. It can be clearly seen from
Fig.4 that the visual effect of the proposed method is superior to other methods. In the results of ASD-POCS, the
artifacts are not well suppressed in high-energy bins. Moreover, in the low-energy bins, due to the influence of noise
and artifacts, the details may not be clear enough. Although the ASSIST algorithm can effectively improve detail
preservation and suppress artifacts, it fails to maintain structural integrity in complex structures and low-contrast
regions, exhibiting residual artifacts and detail loss. The MDM algorithm effectively reduces noise and artifacts but
suffers from consistent information loss and over-smoothed across all the energy bins. In Fig.4, the FTVNNR
algorithm successfully suppresses noise and preserves structures through LR and TV regularizations, but artifacts
are not well suppressed. It can be seen that the SISTER reconstructed results blurred with indistinct edges. Compared
to the competing algorithms, TV-LR-SCT effectively removes artifacts and completely preserves the structures,
yielding the most similar reconstructed results to the reference images.

4. Discussion
To better suppress staircase artifacts in each tomographic image slice in spatial dimension and exploit the
similarities among different energy bins in spectral dimension, the TV and low-rank regularization terms were
introduced for spectral CT image reconstruction. However, solving this spectral CT reconstruction model always
results in an iterative reconstruction algorithm of the nested loop structure, which probably increases computational
complexity. This study utilized the CP method to solve this TV-LR based spectral CT reconstruction problem, and
finally developed a single-loop iterative TV-LR-SCT algorithm to improve computational efficiency. To evaluate the
reconstruction quality of the proposed method, comparative experiments were conducted on the Shepp-Logan
phantom and thoracic CT images in Mayo Clinic dataset. The results (Figs. 1–6) demonstrate that TV-LR-SCT
outperforms existing methods in noise suppression, artifact reduction, and detail preservation. Moreover, the
quantitative evaluation values PSNR, SSIM, VIF and RMSE (Table 1-2) indicate that the TV-LR-SCT method
achieves significant improvement.
As illustrated in Fig. 7, due to the choice of parameter directly affects the imaging quality, there are two
parameters ( 1 =0.0001 and
2 =0.05) need to be chosen through experimental analysis during the reconstructions.
Through comprehensive analysis of the relationship between parameters and metrics (e.g., PSNR), we identified the
optimal parameter values. Thus, just like with traditional spectral CT reconstruction algorithms, parameter selection
remains a major challenge in spectral CT reconstruction. To further, to optimize parameter selection, we will explore
effectively adaptive methods. In addition, we plan to further validate and assess the performance of our proposed
TV-LR-SCT method on clinical data.
5. Conclusion
In this work, to improve the spectral CT images quality and make full use of prior information of spectral CT
images, we have developed a novel spectral CT image reconstruction model combining total variation and low-rank
regularizations (TV-LR-SCT). The application of the CP optimization framework to our model obtained a singleloop iteration scheme for spectral CT reconstruction, achieving significant improvements in computational efficiency.
Extensive experimental results demonstrated that the proposed algorithm outperforms several state-of-the-art
methods in both qualitative and quantitative aspects.
