Owner
Two deep-dive posts, in English and French, on the 42 graphics projects. Their sources are the 42 subjects, the C repositories (cub3D_42, my_fdf_42) and the code of the live demos.
Posts
/blog/cub3d-raycasting-from-c-to-webgl/: "From C to WebGL: Recreating Wolfenstein 3D’s DDA Raycasting"
Covers grid-stepping (DDA) raycasting, the fisheye correction and the projection plane (with a worked example), the strict .cub parser and its flood fill (including an honest O(n²) note), and each bonus in C and in Game Mode.
Ends with a C/MLX42 vs React Three Fiber comparison table.
/blog/fdf-isometric-projection-and-memory/: "Rendering 500×500 Fractals: Isometric Math and Memory"
Covers the 2:1 isometric projection written as a 2×3 matrix (taken from ft_matrix_mult), Bresenham lines and the edge index buffer, and the bonuses (perspective is the extra, conic projection, which the C bonus did not have).
Shows free() in C next to dispose() in WebGL, with the buffer sizes of a 500×500 map (about 7.7 MB).
Both posts link to the live demos through /?demo=cub3d and /?demo=fdf, which Works opens on load.
The French versions are at /fr/blog/<slug>/.
Accuracy notes. Where the brief's framing differed from the code, the posts follow the code:
The subject never names DDA.
Game Mode's collisions are grid and bounding-box tests, not rays.
The C FdF has no second projection.
Undisposed buffers leak GPU memory, which is not the same thing as losing the context.

C/JS token highlighting, AA in both themes (keywords 7.8/6.0:1, strings 7.0/5.4, numbers 12.2/5.8), with ligatures off.
Markdown figures sized from the image file. Alt text is required: the build fails without it, or when the image is missing.
A "Deep dives" section with cover images leads /blog/.
Tables are now focusable, named regions.
