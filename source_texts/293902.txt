This paper presents a computational framework for determining minimum density lattice structures with guaranteed global face-connectivity in the voxelized domain. A binary search algorithm exploits the monotonicity of voxel activation with respect to strut radius to identify the critical radius at which face-connectivity first emerges, yielding provably optimal density minima for the discrete representation at each resolution. A formally defined completeness predicate verifies that every strut of the intended topology is captured before connectivity is assessed, preventing the search from converging to partial skeletal structures caused by orientation-dependent discretization artifacts; the induced conservatism is shown to vanish as resolution increases. A theoretical result establishing that global face-connectivity implies local face-connectivity at every voxel neighborhood eliminates separate local verification, and connectivity is checked by full-volume BFS with a surface sufficiency theorem enabling boundary-based acceleration. Five voxelization methods are developed to accelerate the repeated distance-field evaluations required by the binary search: a Direct baseline, Vectorized batch processing, Distance Field interpolation with selective boundary refinement, Multi-Resolution adaptive refinement exploiting hierarchical coarse-to-fine evaluation, and GPU-Accelerated parallel computation; all five produce identical results under double-precision arithmetic. Validation across thirty diverse topologies confirms systematic discretization convergence of critical radii and minimum densities, and asymptotic homogenization demonstrates that effective mechanical properties converge with resolution, confirming structural viability at extreme sparsity.
