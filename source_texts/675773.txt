Fast and high-quality scale-aware filtering for 3D images
Shin Yoshizawa1 · Hideo Yokota1
Received: 21 April 2025 / Accepted: 7 November 2025 / Published online: 19 December 2025
© The Author(s) 2025, modified publication 2026
Abstract
Scale-aware image filters are useful in graphics applications, but applying such filters to 3D images has not been well studied
and introduces 3D-specific issues. This paper proposes a fast scale-aware 3D image filter that recursively applies an averagebased joint filter that alternates its input and guidance images at each recursion. The joint filter transforms each voxel domain
via the arc length of the guidance image manifold, and the L 1 Gaussian convolutions are performed efficiently and accurately
on the transformed and decomposed sub-domains. Our filter employs a novel 3D half-box filter to avoid corner-missing
artifacts, incorporates voxel pitches into the formulations, fixes the convolution order to reduce non-uniform convergence,
and introduces a novel convolution kernel to obtain consistent results that avoid distorted edges. The new filter was numerically
examined and compared to conventional filters in terms of accuracy, speed, convergence rate, and visual quality, and was
found to achieve fast computations while providing high-quality results.
Keywords Scale-aware filter · 3D image processing · Alternating guidance · Domain transform · L 1 Gaussian convolution
1 Introduction
Feature-aware smoothing filters have been applied to 3D
images, such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic
resonance imaging (MRI), in many applications including
noise reduction [46], restoration [42], reconstruction [56],
segmentation [55], and biomedical image analysis [24, 25,
31]. Because an image is usually composed of geometric
structures of different sizes, scale-aware filters [6, 11, 18, 20,
26, 27, 45, 52, 54] that remove structures smaller than a specified scale (σ ∈ R>0 ) while preserving salient edge features
have become popular in 2D image processing. Separating
geometric structures at different scales has a long history and
has been well-studied in connection with scale-space theory
[23, 48], and scale-aware filters therefore are used in a wide
range of computer graphics applications, such as denoising,
detail manipulation, edge detection, and synthesis.
Zhang et al. [54] first proposed a scale-aware filter called
rolling guidance (RG, see Fig. 1 for its 2D example). The
RG filter recovers salient features from an initial, linearly
smoothed image by recursively applying an average-based
joint filter [10, 12, 19, 38]. The joint filter typically consists

have been developed. Especially, the alternating guided (AG)
approach [45], which swaps the guidance and integrand at

require quadratic computational complexity with respect to

because simple, fast algorithms (e.g., the moving average
[9]) are available for the box filter. On the other hand, convolution with a box kernel function may produce undesired

the improved variant [33] of Canny’s criteria [4].
Unfortunately, these filters [11, 52] have other drawbacks,
such as edge sharpness [11] and non-uniform convergence
in elongated regions [52] (Fig. 4). We also found that the
vector median filter (VMF) [2] used in the AG filters [45, 52]
introduces well-known corner-missing artifacts [35, 36] (Fig.
5), which occur because the number of corner-neighboring
voxels, whose color is close to the corner color, is smaller than
those of different ones. It is important to avoid such artifacts

In this paper1 , we propose a fast scale-aware 3D image
filter based on extending the computational framework of
AG-DTS-2D [52] to 3D images. Our filter uses the AG
approach [45], and it also recursively applies the DT [12]
and DS [51] methods as a joint filter (DT-DS: DTS). As
compared with the AG-DTS-2D [52], the new filter significantly improves the following four problems in addition to
extending the framework to 3D filtering:
1. It avoids corner-missing artifacts (Fig. 5) of the VMF [2]
used in the AG filter [45] and the AG-DTS-2D [52] by
using a novel 3D extension (Section 2.2) of the half-box
filter [16, 17, 49].
2. It incorporates voxel pitch sizes p = { pd } ∈ R3>0 , d ∈
{1, 2, 3} (ratios of voxel height, width, and depth) with
the DT method [12] (Section 3.1) and initial smoothing
(Section 4.2) to manage 3D images with non-regular cube
voxels (Fig. 6), because p directly affects the scale of
geometric structures in the filtered images.

The 3D convolutions of F and J0 are decomposed into
three successive sets of 1D convolutions {X , Y , Z } known
as separable implementation, where each set corresponds to
each dimension of voxel coordinates. For linear convolutions
with uniform voxel cases (e.g., J0 ), the separated convolutions are commutative; that is, the dimensional order of
successive sets does not change the 3D convolution results

artifacts in elongated regions produced by the AG-DTS-2D

regions [12]. Randomly changing the dimensional order of

4.3 Computational complexity
In theory, both the time and space complexities of our filter are linear with respect to the number of voxels (N ), and
because N of a typical 3D image is simply large, the core
time-consuming module is a 3D convolution process in F.

Limitations: Our half-box filter HBx [·] was modeled only
considering the edges and corners along the coordinate axes,
as illustrated in Fig. 8. Also, the voxel pitches were not considered in HBx [·]. Thus, our filter may not be effective at
salient diagonal features that are not aligned with the coordinate axes, and extending HBx [·] to diagonal features with p is
a promising area of further study. Although our filter reduces
the non-uniform convergence problem, it may not be completely eliminated depending on the input data and {φ, σ }
due to the nature of the separable implementation in the DT
method, and using other joint filters [10, 19, 38] with our filter might help to remove this kind of problem. Also, the edge
recovery effect of our filter improves edge sharpness, which
could produce seemingly zigzag/staircase diagonal features
as shown in Fig. 29, which are caused by limitations in discretizing the shape in voxel space, as is also the case with
other conventional filters.

structures smaller than the specified scale while preserving salient image features. Our filter consists of a recursive
process of nonlinear color averaging with a joint filter that
alternates between the input and guidance images in each
