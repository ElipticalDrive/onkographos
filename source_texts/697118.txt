This change coordinates the volumetric, atmospheric, and screen-space lighting effects to ensure they work together efficiently and accurately.
Key improvements:
Performance: The compute-heavy volumetric injection and integration passes now run during the PreDispatch phase. This allows the GPU to overlap these compute tasks with the hardware-intensive opaque rendering pass (shadows and main scene), improving overall frame latency.
Visual Fidelity: Directional lights now use a relative scattering model. Instead of adding scattering on top of the atmosphere (which causes over-exposure and "double haze"), it now effectively carves shadows out of the existing atmospheric model. This creates much more realistic shadow shafts (God rays) that integrate perfectly with the Rayleigh/Mie haze.
Accuracy: Base atmospheric extinction is now exclusively handled by the Atmosphere pass, while AO and GI from the Unified Screen-Space effect are applied directly to geometry before scattering media are added, ensuring that atmospheric glow correctly obscures darkened corners and distant objects.
Consistency: Depth reconstruction formulas were audited and aligned across the post-processing stack to prevent spatial misalignment artifacts at depth boundaries.
