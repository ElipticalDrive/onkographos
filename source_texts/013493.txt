Runtime global illumination for PbrRenderer: a DDGI-style probe volume fed by a compute
ray tracer over BVHs cooked in the asset pipeline, with the exported SH sky as the miss term.
Built small first, with four data-layout decisions fixed up front so the large-scene version
(cascades, GPU scheduling, thousands of instances) is additive rather than a rewrite.
Constraints that decided the design
Read from the renderer at fe49496:
WebGPU is the only backend (Dawn on desktop, browser WebGPU for wasm). No hardware ray
tracing, no bindless, no geometry shaders or conservative raster. Default limit of 4 bind
groups; groups 0–3 are all taken, so GI resources land in group 3's free slots.
Forward+ over a single HDR target. Ambient is a hemisphere blend or the exported L2 SH sky
(FrameUniforms.ambientSh). No normal buffer; the only prepass writes world position as
Rgba32Float for SSAO. Lights are capped at MaxSceneLights = 64, CPU-binned into froxels.
Compute is in the command stream but not the graph. RenderCommandKind.BeginComputePass/
Dispatch exist; FrameGraph only has AddRasterPass.
Mesh geometry is already a cooked Paradise.BLOB (Paradise.Assets.Mesh), which is where a
per-mesh BVH belongs.
Why probes + a compute BVH tracer
DDGI (Majercik et al.; RTXGI) decouples cost from screen resolution, handles fully dynamic
lights and moving props, gets infinite bounce by shading ray hits with the previous frame's
probes, and needs only compute. The tracer is the one hard part, and WebGPU path tracers have
shown compute BVH traversal is viable on ordinary GPUs.
Rejected:
Option
Why not
Baked lightmaps + probes
Not runtime; procedural and user-built levels (Pingu, CultWithin) have nothing to bake. Possible later fallback, not the core.
SDFGI / voxel cone tracing
Cascaded SDF or voxelization rebuilt as the camera moves; compute voxelization with 3D-texture atomics on WebGPU; memory scales with world volume; leaks through thin walls.
Screen-space GI only
Sees only what is on screen. A later contact-detail layer, not the GI.
Radiance cascades
2D form is trivial; the world-space 3D form is unsettled and ends up probe-based anyway, so the probe infrastructure here carries over if adopted later.
Surfels / Lumen-like
Far more machinery than the games need.
A two-level BVH also scales the right way: one bottom-level per unique mesh, one top-level over
instances, trace cost growing with the log of triangle count. Volume methods grow with world
size, which is exactly what gets worse in a large scene. And a triangle hit reads the
instance's material directly; voxel or SDF hits need a separate lighting cache to know a
surface's color.
The four decisions fixed on day one
Each of these costs almost nothing to do the scalable way now and would force a rewrite if done
the small way:
One merged storage buffer per kind, with per-mesh offsets. All BVH nodes in one buffer,
all triangles in another, all vertices in a third; the instance table carries an offset into
each. One buffer per mesh is impossible without bindless.
Cook the BVH in its final node layout. A wide (8-ary) node with quantized child bounds, in
the style of Ylitie et al.'s compressed wide BVH. Swapping formats later means redoing the
cook, the shader, and every shipped mesh blob.
The probe volume is a value in an array, not the feature's fields. Origin, spacing,
counts and atlas offsets live in a struct the shader indexes. One volume is an array of
length one; cascades are length four plus a selection function.
The ray budget is a parameter, not the probe count. The trace dispatches over a list of
probe indices, initially every probe filled on the CPU. Classification later fills the list
in compute and the dispatch becomes indirect.
Engine fit
Cook a BLAS per .mesh document next to the mesh blob, as a Paradise.BLOB layout in the
merged-buffer node format. Rigid instances go into a CPU-built top-level structure per frame.
Skinned characters receive GI but trace against their rest-pose BLAS under the instance
transform (fine for a bounce that is blurred into probes).
Hit shading: direct light from the existing light array and shadow atlas, factor-only
albedo plus emissive, and the previous frame's probe irradiance for the bounce.
Rays that miss sample the SH sky, so a scene with no probe volume renders exactly as it
does now.
Probe atlases: octahedral irradiance at 8×8 per probe and visibility at 16×16 per probe
(depth and depth²), both Rgba16Float, bound into group 3. pbrCore.slang replaces the ambient
term with a Chebyshev-weighted probe lookup when a volume is present.
One IRenderFeature. The probe GI feature declares its compute passes and publishes its
atlases on the blackboard (Frame graph for PbrRenderer: named events, graph-owned targets, and an IRenderFeature seam #242's seam). It runs unchanged in the Godot editor's play mode
because everything stays inside Dawn.
Phases
Prerequisites. FrameGraph.AddComputePass. Replace the Rgba32Float position prepass with
depth + octahedral normal (SSAO consumes it; later screen-space work needs it). Carry
previous-frame camera matrices for temporal reprojection.
The tracer, proven in isolation. Cook BVHs in the merged-buffer layout, build the
per-frame instance structure, write the WGSL traversal (one thread per ray first), and
validate with a ray-traced ambient-occlusion debug view before any probe code exists.
Request desktop limits/features here: large maxStorageBufferBindingSize, shader-f16,
subgroups, timestamp queries.
Probe volume. Trace, blend irradiance and visibility, sample in the shader. One volume
auto-fit to the static scene, with an authored override from the Godot/Blender exporters.
Scaling. Probe classification and relocation, per-host ray and probe budgets, browser
tuning on an integrated GPU.
Later layers. Screen-space contact GI for detail below probe spacing; reflections as SSR
with a probe fallback (specular is where probe GI is weakest).

Authoring
By design there is almost no manual process: a scene lights itself the moment it loads. What
remains is optional authoring, every item with a working default.
Nothing to do
No bake step. Probes update continuously; a procedural or user-built level gets GI with no
offline pass.
BVH cooking is automatic. The build cooks a BVH beside every .mesh blob the way it already
cooks the mesh; an author never sees it.
Materials need no extra data. Hit shading reads the material's base color factor and
emissive (later the cooked albedo array), all derived from what the material already carries.
The sky is already exported. The SH sky the exporters write today becomes the miss term, so
a scene without a probe volume looks exactly as it does now.

Per-instance participation
Static for anything not skinned
Godot's GeometryInstance3D.gi_mode carried verbatim: Static bounces light and lives in the static top-level structure; Dynamic receives GI but does not bounce; Disabled is ignored by the tracer.

Things to know while authoring
Emissive materials are light sources. Anything emissive bounces into the probes with no
light node needed — handy for signage and lava, a surprise if an emissive factor was only meant
as a glow.
Thin walls and probe spacing. Probe methods leak when spacing is coarser than a wall. The
visibility atlas handles most of it, but paper-thin interior walls want tighter spacing there,
which is the main reason the volume override exists.
Skinned characters do not cast bounce light. They are lit by GI but trace as their rest
pose. A character holding a torch lights the room through its light node, not its body.
The day-to-day workflow is: place geometry and lights, press play. The overrides are for tuning,
not for making it work.
Not carried over
The Rgba32Float world-position prepass (replaced in phase 1 either way).
CPU froxel binning and the 64-light cap eventually give way to the world-space light grid;
that is renderer work a large scene needs regardless of GI, and should be scheduled alongside
phase 1 together with GPU-driven instancing and culling.
