Abstract—Point distance metrics such as Chamfer Distance (CD)
and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) play a key role in evaluating
the quality of reconstructed point clouds and computing training
loss in point cloud reconstruction. However, the metrics show their
own characteristics in evaluation, stemming from differences in
their sensitivity to point cloud error traits, which, in turn, causes
differences in their performance of point cloud evaluation and
reconstruction. In this study, we analyzed in detail the sensitivity
of distance metrics to such error traits as density imbalance and
point dispersion. We showed how the sensitivity difference in
metrics affects their performance in point cloud evaluation and
reconstruction. Recognizing that the sensitivity of a metric is
correlated with the level of bijective one-to-one point
correspondence embedded in the metric, we proposed a new
metric, the nested Chamfer distance (NCD-n), that allows to adjust
its sensitivity to error traits through the nested CD cycle, n, by
which the level of bijective one-to-one point correspondence is
controlled. In addition, we analyzed the effect of shape-induced
point density difference on distance metrics and proposed taking
shape compensation into account in metrics. Based on extensive
experiments, we verified the difference of metrics in their
sensitivities to error traits, the capability of the proposed NCD-n
for adjusting its sensitivity effectively, and the effect of sensitivity
adjustment and shape-compensation on the performance of point
cloud evaluation and reconstruction.

1 INTRODUCTION
The point cloud represents an efficient means of representing
3D data. Recently, several deep point cloud processing
approaches have been proposed with successful applications to
the task of segmentation, recognition, reconstruction,
registration, and pose estimation involved in 3D scenes and
objects. They were largely built upon fundamental point cloud
processing deep architectures such as PointNet [1], FoldingNet
[2] FlowNet [3], DiffusionNet [4], and many of their variants
[5-12]. One of the key elements for implementing deep point
cloud processing networks is the point cloud distance metric for
network training and performance evaluation. For instance, in
implementing a point autoencoder (AE) for point cloud
reconstruction, a point cloud distance metric such as CD [13] is
often used to measure the error between the ground truth and
the reconstructed point cloud to evaluate training loss and

distributed closely to the surfaces, requiring a relatively good
initialization, in particular, for point cloud generation tasks.
1.1 Related Work
The point processing properties of distance metrics such as CD,
EMD, and DCD were reported in the past in association with
various point processing tasks. In [13], it was observed that CD
is less sensitive to mismatched local density. [25] showed
experimentally that a point autoencoder (AE) trained with CD
can induce point collapse, wherein the reconstructed points tend
to aggregate around more densely populated regions. An
intrinsic deficiency associated with CD was claimed in [22]
regarding its insensitivity to different density distributions
between matching point sets while inducing a significant
influence from outliers. [23] showed that CD even undergoes
a non-monotonic behavior with the increase of noise-induced
imbalance levels. By contrast, [25, 26] indicated that EMD is
dominated by global distribution while overlooking the fidelity
of detailed structures, exhibiting a tendency of point dispersion
that may crush the fine details of local shape features. To reform
the deficiencies of CD, particularly regarding the visual quality
in point cloud modeling and generation, [15] proposed DCD,
which is claimed to be stricter with the quality of local point
distributions than CD, averaging many-to-one distance
contributions while being more computationally efficient than
EMD. In addition, DCD offers a more stable metric than CD
by exponentially decaying the distance contributions of
individual point pairs.
Although it is not the main subject of this study, the
computational efficiency associated with point distance metrics
is essential in practice. The original EMD algorithm [20, 27]
suffers from a high computational complexity of O (n3 log n),
with n representing the number of points to match. Efforts
ensued to reduce the computational complexity of EMD
without and with approximation. For instance, [19] cast EMD
as a network flow problem using the L1 distance norm to reach
a quadratic complexity without approximation. For exact EMD,
[12] showed that the minimum complexity we can achieve may
be O (n2). On the other hand, [28] allowed (1+ 𝜖) approximation
of EMD to obtain a sub-quadratic complexity. By setting the
ratio between the approximated and exact EMD bounded, the
wavelet EMD [29] showed that a near-linear complexity is
feasible. Alternatively, [30] proposed a convolutional neural
network (CNN)-based fast approximation of EMD. In the case
of CD, the brute force CD algorithm has a quadratic complexity
of O (n2). Although more efficient than exact EMD, CD's O (n2)
complexity is still not so efficient for dealing with large
datasets. On the other hand, [31] proposed an (1+ε)
approximate algorithm to compute CD with a near-linear
complexity.
1.2 Contribution
In this study, we focused on analyzing the properties of point
cloud processing metrics, in particular, their measurement
sensitivities to the aspect of difference or the error trait between
two matching point sets, with the ultimate objective of
designing a new metric, the sensitivity of which to the error trait
can be adjusted. Specifically, we selected CD, EMD, and DCD
as target metrics for sensitivity analysis regarding the two
distinct error traits: density imbalance and point dispersion
error traits. By conducting a more systematic and in-depth
analysis of the sensitivities of distance metrics to error traits, we
intended to offer a more complete and comprehensive
perspective on sensitivity-related metric properties beyond
what was reported from the previous studies. For instance, we
found that CD is more sensitive to the point dispersion error
trait than the density imbalance error trait, while EMD is quite
the opposite of CD. Knowing the sensitivity property of metrics
to different error traits is helpful for their applications to point
cloud evaluation and reconstruction. A metric with a particular
error sensitivity allows a point cloud to be evaluated with
emphasis on the specific error trait that the metric is sensitive
to. Moreover, we can use a metric of a particular error trait for
reconstruction training such that the point clouds are
reconstructed with emphasis on minimal errors in the specific
error trait that the metric is sensitive to. We considered it
desirable to design a new metric to adjust the sensitivity of
different error traits, allowing optimal sensitivities to be set
flexibly for various point processing tasks. To that end, we
proposed a nested-CD (NCD). NCD can adjust its sensitivity to
error traits efficiently by controlling the level of bijective oneto-one point correspondence in matching, as described in
Section 3. Lastly, we proposed a shape-compensation in
distance metrics to compensate for the shape-induced alteration
in metric values.
This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 analyzes the
existing distance metrics regarding their sensitivities to error
traits. It discusses the necessity of adjusting the sensitivity in
metrics and the role of bijective one-to-one point
correspondence in CD for sensitivity adjustment. Section 3
presents the proposed nested Chamfer distance, NCD-n, where
n represents the number of nested CD operations to increase
bijective one-to-one point correspondence. Section 4 presents
the shape-compensation in metrics for removing shape-induced
metric variations. Section 5 describes the effect of distance
metrics, including NCD and shape-compensated metrics, on
reconstruction training. Finally, the conclusion is provided in
Section 6.

Point Dispersion Error Trait
By the error trait, we mean a particular deformation of the
reconstructed point cloud from its GT counterpart, where the
GT point cloud is assumed to be uniformly distributed. The
density imbalance error trait represents a type of point cloud
deformation in which a cluster of points is aggregated into its
aggregation center. The density imbalance error trait level is
defined by the number of aggregation centers, the size of point
clusters subject to aggregation, and the degree of aggregation
enforced. On the other hand, in the case of point dispersion error
trait, the deformation occurs by adding a Gaussian noise to
individual GT points, such that the standard deviation of the
Gaussian noise applied indicates the level of point dispersion
error trait. Adding Gaussian noise to the uniformly distributed
GT point cloud may disperse point distributions globally while
inducing, partly, local aggregations.

2.4 Sensitivity Control
As described, the sensitivity associated with point distance
metrics plays an essential role in evaluating and reconstructing
point clouds. Therefore, we considered it useful to design a
metric that can set its sensitivity and adjust it to error traits.
DCD was intended to improve the rapidly growing sensitivity
of CD to better suit point cloud modeling and reconstruction
tasks by exponentially decaying distance contribution and
allowing a single contribution from many-to-one matching
correspondences. Although DCD can contain a rapidly growing
sensitivity to distance errors, its overall sensitivity trend follows
CD, as illustrated in Figs. 2,3, and 4. It would be more
significant if we had a metric whose sensitivity trend can be
adjusted for a wider range, covering the two opposite trends of
CD to EMD. Therefore, we considered a new metric that can
combine the properties of CD and EMD with their contributions
adjusted. In other words, the new metric can set its sensitivity
trend between CD and EMD: CD with the higher sensitivity to
point dispersion than density imbalance error traits, and EMD
with the higher sensitivity to density imbalance than point
dispersion error traits. One approach to realize such a combined
effect may be partitioning a point cloud into two groups to
which CD and EMD are applied separately. However, to make
this approach viable, it is necessary to properly address such
non-trivial issues as finding effective partitioning and handling
the inefficiency of using EMD. Note that the combined effect is
determined by the ratio of partitioning and the error traits
associated with the partitioned groups.
Here, we proposed a highly effective approach for realizing the
combined effect, termed here as the nested CD or NCD-n
approach. The proposed NCD-n approach is based on a nested
application of CD n cycles to a matching point cloud pair.
Specifically, at a nested cycle, the approach applies CD to the
currently remaining point clouds to identify those matching
point pairs that are paired by the bijective one-to-one point
correspondence and separates the identified matching pairs
from the rest, such that the next nested cycle is repeated with
the remaining point clouds. The proposed NCD-n implies
combining the properties of CD and EMD, in that the distance
from the point pairs under the bijective one-to-one point
correspondence emulates the property of EMD. In contrast,
from the rest of the point pairs capture the property of CD with
the number of nested cycles, n, adjusting their contributions.
The number of point pairs under the bijective one-to-one
correspondence is expected to increase along with NCD-n
cycles, and so is the contribution from the property of EMD. To
quantify the variation in the number of point pairs under the
bijective one-to-one correspondence, here, we defined the level,
ℓ 1-1, of bijective one-to-one correspondence as the ratio
between the number of point pairs matched under bijective oneto-one correspondence and the total number of points of a point
cloud subject to match.

options that results in the most overall number of bijective oneto-one pairings.
3.2 Sensitivity Adjustment with NCD-n
The proposed NCD-n metric is applied to the 300,000
synthesized testing data generated with 25 levels of density
imbalance, x, and point dispersion, y, error traits to evaluate its
capability of adjusting the sensitivity to error traits. We were
interested in observing the sensitivity trend of NCD-n for the
nested cycle, n, in particular, if it can be adjusted for a wider
range of sensitivities between the sensitivities of CD and EMD,
and if the adjusted sensitivities are in correlation with ℓ1-1. To
this end, we obtained the conditional sensitivities, S(x|y) and
S(y|x), as well as the total sensitivities, S(x) and S(y), associated
with NCD-n by the same way as we obtained them for CD and
EMD in Section 2.3. Fig. 6 (a) and (b) show S(x|y) and S(y|x),
respectively, associated with the 5 levels of y and x while Fig.
7 (a) and (b) show S(x) and S(y), respectively, for NCD-n, n =
1, 3, 5, 10, as well as CD and EMD. On the other hand, Fig. 8
(a) and (b) show the variation of ℓ1-1 for NCD-n, n=1,3,5,10.
Figs. 6 and 7 show the capability of NCD-n for adjusting its
sensitivity to density imbalance and point dispersion error traits
along with nested CD cycles, n. They reveal that the sensitivity
trend of NCD-n to density imbalance and point dispersion error
traits can be adjusted between CD and EMD based on the nested
cycle, n, as the control parameter. On the other hand, Figs. 6,
7, and 8 show, in general, the existence of positive and negative
correlations of ℓ1-1 with the sensitivity to density imbalance and
point dispersion error traits, respectively.
We may theoretically interpret the experimentally verified
capability of NCD-n to adjust its sensitivity to error traits as
follows. As described, the proposed NCD-n, in effect, partitions
the original point clouds into the bijective one-to-one point pair
group and the remaining point group to which CD is applied.
Note that the metric associated with the bijective one-to-one
point pair group carries the same meaning as an approximate
EMD, as the metric is obtained purely from the distances
between bijective one-to-one point pairs. This allows NCD-n to
combine the properties of CD and EMD effectively. However,
looking further into the property of NCD-n, we may observe
that applying CD along nested cycles is considered a greedy
approach to approximating EMD. At each nested cycle, the
approach selects the point pairs that are the minimum in their
mutual distances as if such bijective one-to-one point pairs are
the local optimal solution for an approximated EMD.

SHAPE-COMPENSATED POINT CLOUD METRICS
In point cloud processing, a point cloud is often size-normalized
based on the maximum point-to-point distance of the object
before applying a distance metric to it. A fixed number of points
is then sampled randomly from the size-normalized point cloud
to obtain the metric. However, we observed that the size
normalization alone is insufficient for accurately representing
the error distances between point clouds without biases. When
different shapes of objects are represented by point clouds with
a fixed number of points, their point densities may differ from
each other due to the difference in their surface areas. For
instance, an object with a thinner body tends to have a smaller
surface area than an object with a thicker body. This causes an
object with a thinner body to have a higher point density,
leading to a smaller distance metric. In Fig. 9, we illustrate the
inconsistency of distance metrics for representing the shape
deformation of objects due to their shape-induced difference in
point density.
Fig. 9 Shape-induced inconsistency in metric values: the thin
object (right) with more significant shape deformation has
smaller CD and EMD than the thick object (left), as illustrated
by (a) and (b). The proposed shape-compensated CD and EMD,
CDs and EMDs, corrects such inconsistency with particular
emphasis on shape deformation as illustrated by (c) and (d).
In Fig. 9, the two GT point clouds with notably different shapes
are corrupted by the same levels of density imbalance and point
dispersion error traits. However, the object with a thin body
(right) has a lower CD and EMD than the object with a thick
body (left) due to its higher point density, although its shape
deformation is more severe than that of the left object. To
address this inconsistency, we proposed a shape compensation
in point distance metrics on top of the size normalization.
Specifically, the proposed shape-compensated distance metrics
take account of the relationship between the shape of an object
and its point density in case a fixed number of points is used to
generate point clouds under size normalization. To this end, we
defined the shape compensation index, S(P(𝑂𝑗 )), referred to
here as S-index, associated with the point cloud, P(𝑂𝑗 ), of an
object, 𝑂𝑗 . Formally, S-index, S(P( 𝑂𝑗 )), is defined as the
average point density associated with a size-normalized point
cloud, P(𝑂𝑗 ), as follows:

5.3 Discussions
It is possible to tune the sensitivity trend of NCD-n to error traits
to better fit for purpose by adopting a more general form of
distance formula other than the squared Euclidean distance of
CD, e.g., Euclidean distance of the power r with r to be set
optimally. The effect of shape compensation in metrics can be
adjusted by replacing the S-index with an amplified or
attenuated S-index in the form of the S-index of the power r,
with r to be set optimally.

CONCLUSION
This study showed that point distance metrics exhibit their own
sensitivities to such error traits as density imbalance and point
dispersion. In particular, CD and EMD are shown opposite in
their sensitivity trend: CD and EMD have a higher sensitivity
to point dispersion and density imbalance error traits,
respectively. We found a correlation between the sensitivity of
a distance metric to error traits and the level of bijective one-toone point correspondence embedded in the metric. Since the
error sensitivity of a metric in evaluation inversely affects the
error tendency in reconstruction, as we verified through
extensive experiments, the need to have a metric with
adjustable error sensitivities arises. To this end, we proposed the
nested CD, NCD-n, that can control its sensitivity to error traits
based on the nested CD cycle, n. NCD-n adjusts its error
sensitivity by increasing the bijective one-to-one point
correspondence embedded in it with the nested CD cycle, n. In
addition, we proposed shape-compensation to be applied to
distance metrics based on the shape index, S-index, to
compensate for the difference in point densities due to different
shapes of objects. Through extensive experiments, we verified
that the proposed NCD-n can adjust its error sensitivity
effectively between CD and EMD and that the proposed shape
compensation has an implication of mitigating shape-induced
biases in metrics for the shape index, S-index. Besides, we
verified through experiments the difference of distance metrics
in their sensitivities to error traits, the inverse correlation
between the error sensitivity in evaluation and the error
tendency in reconstruction, and the correlation between the
error sensitivity and the level of bijective one-to-one
correspondence in metrics. We plan to further assess their
potential advantages by applying the proposed NCD-n and
shape compensated distance metrics to wider varieties of point
cloud processing tasks, including segmentation and pose
estimation.
