Lossless and Stateless Compression of
IEC 61850 Sampled Values Flows
Jose Saldana, Senior Member, IEEE, and Miguel A Oliván
L
Abstract— This letter proposes a lossless and stateless
compression method for IEC 61850 Sampled Values flows. A set
of bitmaps is introduced to indicate the size in bytes of the sampled
currents and voltages, and to flag the presence or absence of the
Quality field. The method has been evaluated for different profiles,
and it can provide bandwidth savings between 30 and 52%, with a
very low computational cost as a counterpart. It has been
implemented and tested in two different hardware platforms, with
traffic generated by a Merging Unit. The processing delays have
been found to be acceptable, showing a good tradeoff between
compression rate, complexity and added delay.
Index Terms—Substation Protection, IEC 61850, Sampled
Values, Compression, Synchronized Waveform
I. INTRODUCTION
T
he use of IEC 61850 for the digitalization of substations has
gained significant momentum in recent years, prompting
many companies to hasten their shift towards automated
substations. Often, this digital transformation commences with
the implementation of GOOSE (Generic Object Oriented
Substation Event, defined by IEC 61850-8-1).
GOOSE is an event-based protocol used for the exchange of
information between IEDs (Intelligent Electronic Devices). The
publisher periodically sends messages and, when an event
happens (e.g., a trip, a contactor closed, etc.), a burst of
messages is sent. Its bandwidth consumption is remarkably low,
typically around tens of kilobits per second, with a quite
periodical behavior, and sporadic bursts after certain events.
SV (Sampled Values, defined in IEC 61850-9-2) protocol is
used for waveform-based monitoring, with a periodic exchange
of voltage and current samples between Merging Units and
IEDs. This type of monitoring is increasingly critical for
specific applications [1], as it enables the calculation of
harmonics and other transient events, capabilities not offered by
synchrophasors [2]. SV generates messages with a time interval
which depends on the frequency of the measured signal, and the
samples per period.
In the last years, some companies are starting to deploy
devices based on SV. While this protocol offers a variety of
options, many real-world implementations adhere to the
configurations outlined in the “Implementation Guidance for
Digital Interface to Instrument Transformers Using IEC 618509-2,” also known as IEC 61850-9-2 LE (Light Edition), in
which the dataset is fixed to 4 voltages and 4 currents, with 80
or 256 samples per period (4,000 or 12,800 fps). In recent years,

GOOSE and SV are carried over Ethernet. These protocols
share the same network infrastructure, i.e., the Process Bus, a
part of the Ethernet network of the substation.
In contrast to GOOSE, SV is very bandwidth demanding. As
an example, the F96000S1I4U4 profile sends 96,000 packets
per second, which may result in a bandwidth slightly below 100
Mbps. Considering that many of the deployed Ethernet
networks have speeds of 100 (100BASE-TX or 100BASE-FX)
or 1000 Mbps (1000BASE-LX), this can pose a limit for the
number of SV flows that can share the network: the desire for a
high number of accurate and timely measurements may be
hindered by the constraints of network capacity.
The present letter proposes a lossless method, able to
mitigate this problem by reducing the bandwidth of SV flows.
The next section summarizes the Related Work and the
Contribution; Section III explains our proposal in detail,
Section IV presents the Tests and Results, and the letter ends
with the Conclusions.
II. RELATED WORK AND CONTRIBUTION
Some methods as e.g., Robust Header Compression [4], use
delta compression for increasing fields: instead of sending the
value, only the difference with respect to the previous one is
sent, thus reducing the size. This requires that the sender and
the receiver store a context, i.e., a kind of status of the
compression algorithm. In our case, as SV does not have any
feedback channel, the compression algorithm must be stateless.
Therefore, delta compression is not a possibility: an error in one
message would result on the invalidation of a burst of messages.

compression, aiming to achieve two goals: to decrease

the measurement point to the final application. The method can

(Application Service Data Unit). It has two different stages: a)
it uses differential encoding, i.e., only the differences between
the previous ASDU values are recorded and sent; for example,

In the dataset, the SV protocol transports a set of current and
voltage samples obtained from the analog-to-digital conversion
of the corresponding wave. In both LE and 61869-9 standards,
physical magnitudes are represented as the value, denoted as
instMag.i (both documents enforce the use of 4-byte integers)
and an associated 4-byte quality field, denoted as q. The dataset
is organized as an array of (instMag.i,q) couples.
The magnitude instMag.i is a 32-bit signed integer ranging
between -231 and 231-1. AC instrumentation transformers
sample a sinusoidal signal in centivolts and milliamperes, so 2
or 3 bytes may suffice to express all the values: with 3 bytes, a
value up to 16,777,216 can be expressed, which may
correspond to a peak of 167kV (enough for medium voltage) or
16.7kA. In these cases, positive values (expressed in big-endian
mode) start with a 0x00 byte, and negative ones begin with
0xFF. Lower values of the sinusoid may be expressed with 2
bytes, or even with just one. Fig. 2 shows how different samples
of the same sine wave may require 1, 2, 3 or 4 bytes.

Building on the contributions of [5], another approach called
Slipstream was proposed in [6], which not only improves the
compression rate, but also accelerates the encoding process.
This speed is due to the simplicity of the compression method
and the decreased volume of data to be processed.
The contribution of the present work is the proposal of a
novel compression technique, which works at ASDU level (i.e.,
it compresses each ASDU, not the frame as a whole). The
protocol is lossless and stateless, and it preserves time
synchronization. In addition, it can be easily added to the
current standard, with a minor ASN.1 definition, as we will see.
The proposal has been implemented, and tests of the bandwidth
savings and the incurred delays have been carried out.
The approach is complementary to that of [6], because it
compresses single ASDUs. In fact, both methods could be
applied in cascade, thus increasing the savings.

Regarding the quality, although the standard uses 32 bits,
only 14 are in fact used, and the first two bytes are always 0x00
00. When the validity is good, the two other ones are also null
(this should be the case under normal conditions).
All in all, our proposal is to remove the initial 0x00 / 0xFF
bytes of instMag.i, and the null bytes of q. To allow the lossless
decompression, a 4-bit bitmap is added for each (instMag.i,q)
couple: 3 bits indicate which bytes have been removed from
instMag.i (see Table I), and the fourth bit is set if q is not null.
Let us explain it with an example: a couple with instMag.i
equal to 0x00 00 8a b2, and a q value of 0x00 00 00 01 (invalid),
will be compressed to four bytes (0x8a b2 00 01) and a 1010
bitmap: 1 means that q is present, and 010 means that the two
first bytes of instMag.i are null and have been removed. As a
result, 4 bytes have been saved, at the cost of a 4-bit bitmap.

The compression method has been implemented in user
space in C. A Wireshark dissector has also been built. An
example is presented in Fig. 5, using the LE profile
F4000S1I4U4 with 8 (instMag.i,q) couples. In the example, 7
quality fields are null, and can be removed. Four bytes
including bitmaps were added, 6 magnitudes were compressed
to 1 byte, and two of them were null (compressed to 0 bytes).
As a result, the size of the compressed ASDU has been reduced
in 49 bytes in this case (the original size was 90 bytes).

kind of signal (AC vs DC). For example, in an AC trace with
133kV and 198A RMS, 31% of the frames started with 0x00
(same for 0xFF), and more than 12% started with 0x00 00 (same
for 0xFF FF). In addition, GND signals have smaller values
(940V and 4A), so the compression rate is higher, up to 45%.
Regarding the quality, let p be the probability of having a null
value. 4×N bytes will be saved with p probability, and 2×N
bytes with (1-p), since 2 bytes are always removed.
As a counterpart of the savings, certain bytes are added,
corresponding to: the 0x8A byte; the length; the byte indicating
the length of the bitmaps; and the bitmaps themselves: 4 bits for
each (instMag.i,q) couple. The total overhead is 3 + N/2 bytes,
so the average number of saved bytes will be given by:
instMag.i saving + 2×N×(1+p) – (3 + N/2).

V. CONCLUSION
This letter has proposed a lossless and stateless compression
method for SV flows. Given its operation at the ASDU level, it
remains applicable even for frames with a single ASDU. It
achieves bandwidth savings between 30 and 52%, with a very
low computational cost. It has been implemented and tested
with real traffic from a Merging Unit. The compression ratio
has been calculated, and the processing delays have been found
to be acceptable in two different platforms.
As future work, the proposed method could be tested in
cascade with that of [5] and [6]. Furthermore, to ensure
compatibility with other systems, a device can be introduced in
the network, which may compress the flows passing through. It
could be activated on demand, i.e., whenever the network is
close to congestion.
