Hi, thanks for sharing the ShapeUP paper — impressive work.
I’m observing that image‑conditioned edits often look cleaner and more stable when the underlying 3D reconstruction is a Gaussian Splat (e.g., TripoSplat, HyperGS, GaussianVideo) compared to mesh‑based pipelines such as SF3D.
Could you clarify:
Whether ShapeUP’s editing behavior differs between mesh and splat representations
If splat‑based geometry tends to yield higher‑fidelity or more consistent edits
Whether splat‑native editing is feasible or planned
Thanks for your time.

Hi, Thanks for your interest in ShapeUP!
ShapeUP operates directly in the 3D latent space, and an important assumption in our approach is that the latent space defined by the backbone model’s VAE (Step1X-3D) is semantic rather than explicitly structured or geometric. In other words, the latent representation is a fixed-length sequence of tokens without an explicit geometric or positional structure. We rely on this property at several stages of our method.
In the case of TripoSplat, even though it operates in a 3D latent space, the latent representation itself has an explicit geometric structure. As a result, some of the assumptions we make in ShapeUP would not directly hold.
That said, this doesn’t necessarily mean that ShapeUP couldn’t be adapted to a splat-based representation. Some parts of the method would likely need to be adjusted to account for the structure of the latent space. I actually think this would be a very interesting direction to explore :)
If you’re seriously considering trying this, I’d be happy to discuss it in more detail!

Thanks, this is really helpful. Yes, I’m seriously interested in exploring this.
The distinction you point out is actually close to the question I was trying to get at. I’m interested in whether the geometric structure of a splat latent could be treated as an asset rather than simply an incompatibility with ShapeUP’s assumptions.
In particular, I’ve been working on spatial generative interfaces where explicit geometry acts as a control/constraint layer for generation. One direction I’d like to explore is whether ShapeUP-style semantic latent editing could be adapted so that semantic edits operate alongside explicit spatial constraints, rather than requiring the latent itself to be completely geometry-agnostic.
Now that the source is available, I’d like to dig into exactly which ShapeUP operations depend on the Step1X latent being unstructured and identify what would need to change for something like TripoSplat.
Would definitely be interested in discussing this with you in more detail.

Thanks, this is really helpful! I took a look through the released source, and I think I understand the distinction you’re pointing to.
It looks like the main dependency on the unstructured Step1X latent is in the geometry stage, particularly source-latent conditioning and the correspondence between source information and the latent being edited. The image conditioning and rectified-flow formulation seem less fundamentally dependent on that assumption, and the texture stage seems largely separable.
This makes me wonder if, rather than trying to make a TripoSplat latent behave like an unstructured Step1X latent, the explicit spatial structure could be used as an additional conditioning signal. Semantic attention could determine what should change, while geometric correspondence helps determine where the edit should propagate. That might potentially improve edit locality and identity preservation while retaining ShapeUP’s semantic editability.
I’m seriously interested in trying this. Does that match your intuition about where the main incompatibility lies? I’d be very interested in discussing how you’d approach the adaptation.
