Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently emerged as a powerful 3D representation, providing rich Gaussian attributes that benefit downstream perception tasks (e.g., classification). However, 3DGS-based models often face inevitable domain shifts in real-world deployment, making unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) on 3DGS—termed Gaussian Domain Adaptation (GDA)—essential. Yet, exploring the GDA scenario presents two main challenges: (i) There are no standardized evaluation benchmarks explicitly designed for GDA. (ii) 3DGS introduces heterogeneous attributes beyond coordinates with domain shifts that vary in magnitude and statistics; directly feeding all attributes into a conventional 3D UDA pipeline may therefore amplify attribute-level gaps and cause negative transfer. To address the first challenge, we developed GDA-10, a benchmark consisting of three Gaussian splatted datasets (i.e., ModelSplat-10, ShapeSplat-10, and MVImgSplat-10), each with distinct characteristics for GDA. To address the second challenge, we propose GDA-Net, a dedicated UDA Network for 3D Gaussian Splats, built upon a decouple-then-fuse strategy. At its core is an attribute-guided Mixture-of-Experts strategy (GDA-MoE), which dynamically disentangles and fuses heterogeneous 3DGS attributes to selectively emphasize the reliable, domain-invariant information. To focus on learning domain-invariant features from both the source and target domains, we then implement progressive feature and classifier adaptation based on the refined features of GDA-MoE. Extensive experiments on both single-label and Multi-Label GDA-10 demonstrate the superiority of our approach over state-of-the-art 3D-based UDA methods in terms of both classification accuracy and Macro-F1 score.
Keywords: 3D Gaussian Splatting, Unsupervised Domain Adaptation
