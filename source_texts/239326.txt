In the classic pipeline, a draw reads vertices and indices from buffers and runs a vertex shader per vertex. Mesh shading replaces that front end with two compute-like stages:
a task shader decides, per tile or part of a tile, whether to draw it and at what detail;
a mesh shader builds small batches of triangles (meshlets, for example 8×8 cells: 81 vertices, 128 triangles) directly in on-chip memory, with no index buffer.
To find out
Performance against instanced indirect draws on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel; on some AMD GPUs mesh shaders only match the classic pipeline.
Culling gain: a partly hidden tile can drop most of its meshlets.
Hardware cutoff: NVIDIA Turing (2018) or later, AMD RDNA2 (2020) or later, Intel Arc; the Steam Deck qualifies. Whether to keep a classic fallback.
How morphing, per-tile heights and GPU-driven selection (both in this milestone) fit.
