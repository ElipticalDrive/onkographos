TMUshare
TMUshare digital repository is an open access repository for scholarship and various research and educational outputs produced at Toronto Metropolitan University. TMUshare offers faculty, students, staff, and researchers a free and secure home to share their scholarship openly and in a free to read format. By depositing in TMUshare, researchers can increase the discoverability of their work, and comply with funder and publisher requirements.
TMUshare is also home to TMU’s version of record student digital theses and dissertations.

Understanding COVID-19 Vaccine Hesitancy in Latinx, MENA, and South Asian Communities in Toronto: A Scoping Review
(2023) Zinaic, Rade; Wong, Josephine Pui-Hing
Show more
COVID-19 vaccination is the foundation of Canada’s COVID-19 response. Yet decent initial willingness and uptake revealed serious structural barriers which left many unvaccinated to suffer dire outcomes and overwhelmed the public healthcare system. Our scoping review narrows the broader Canadian context to the Greater Toronto Area (GTA) to explore the effects of pandemic policy on ethnoracial communities. Thus, we inquired into the primary reasons for vaccine
hesitancy in the South Asian, Latinx, and Middle Eastern and North African (MENA) communities. We applied the methods pioneered by Arksey and O’Malley along with Levac, Colquhoun, and O’Brien. A total 3685 articles were retrieved from PubMed, OVID, JSTOR, ERIC. Vetting for research relevance as well as de- duplication left us with 42 relevant sources for data extraction. Our results revealed that there are four intersecting and symbiotic factors for COVID-19 vaccine hesitancy amongst the South Asian, Latinx, and Middle Eastern and North African (MENA) diaspora in Toronto: (a) access; (b) mistrust; (c) racism; and (d) misinformation. We concluded that these intersecting causes of vaccine hesitancy can uphold access barriers while normalizing misinformation in the service of suspicion, thereby doubly stigmatizing racialized peoples by race and vaccination status in a way that further marginalizes them.

Understanding effective evidence-based approaches to health communication for communicable diseases in diverse communities: a scoping review
(2023-12) Zinaic, Rade; Akande, Adedoja; Mishra, Pragya; Li, Alan; Wong, Josephine
Show more
Background: The COVID-19 pandemic has uncovered and amplified numerous health and social inequities and inequalities and laid bare the successes and limitations of health communication in a global health crisis. Specifically, the problem of vaccine hesitancy highlights the need for effective evidence-based approaches to health promotion for use amidst a health emergency that empower individuals to cultivate community-centered networks of ethical health practice.
Methods: Arksey and O’Malley’s (2005) blueprint for scoping reviews and the suggestions of Levac, Colquhoun, and O’Brien (2010) were used to guide this study. A search was conducted using the following health sciences databases: Medline (OVID), CINAHL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Web of Science, and PsycInfo. All references were screened by one team member. References were included that met the following criteria: (a) dealt with health communication and health promotion; (b) were evidence - based, demonstrate effective health communication via concrete results and measurements, deal with diverse communities, and identify barriers to health communication; and (c) focused on communicable diseases. We restricted our search to peer-reviewed research papers, reviews, and grey literature published in English between 2012 and 2022. Three team members independently extracted data on key findings.
Results: Five electronic databases were searched which resulted in 13,501 articles; 8,234 duplicate articles were removed; 5,267 did not meet the inclusion criteria of health communication and health promotion; 213 did not meet the inclusion criteria of evidence-based health communication that deals with diverse communities and communicative barriers; and 83 did not meet the inclusion criteria of communicable diseases. This left a total of 22 articles for this review. This review report addresses the four following themes with their corresponding sub-themes: (1) community-centered approaches; (2) social media; (3) barriers; and (4) trust.
Conclusion: We conclude that health communication that is responsive to the knowledge and concerns alive in disadvantaged communities can create a basis for mutual engagement that empowers people to use social media as one of several approaches towards removing barriers to health literacy and positive health outcomes.

FROM PRACTICE TO POLICY: GRAND MANAN’S CONTESTED FISHERIES, ENVIRONMENTAL KNOWLEDGE, AND GOVERNANCE IN THE BAY OF FUNDY, 1834–1837
(NiCHE, 2026-10) Tingley, Zachary A; Yeomans, Richard
Show more
Over the course of the 1834 winter session of the New Brunswick House of Assembly, the House received two petitions concerning the present state and future of the herring fishery at Grand Manan in the Bay of Fundy. One petition was from inhabitants of the island seeking greater legislative protections for the fishery that they believed was threatened. The other petition came from fishers and merchants of Saint John, who advocated for looser restrictions and more intensive harvesting of herring off the island. For its part, the House took great care in weighing the petitions and expressed interest in making an evidence-based policy decision. A commission was struck in 1835, and findings were published as part of the 1836 Commission to Investigate the State of the Herring Fishery at Grand Manan. The waters around the island were an important part of a mobile and interconnected Peskotomuhkati network, occupied by settlers who lived and worked alongside Indigenous peoples in a shared saltwater space. The depositions from fishers and findings of the Commissioners offer a window into two very different understandings of the herring fishery and its role in supporting the livelihoods of two separate settler fisher communities. At the centre of the debate was the role of the tools used to harvest herring, the resilience of local fish stocks, and ultimately, the use of Grand Manan itself. “From Practice to Policy” explores the competing forms of environmental knowledge that drove the local settler herring fishery at Grand Manan into conflict with fishers from elsewhere in the Bay of Fundy, positioning the island and its inhabitants into questions about access and modes of extraction that have contoured the history of the northwest Atlantic.

Regulating Conflict in Public Sector Labour Relations: The Ontario Experience (1984–1993)
(Industrial Relations Department of Université Laval (Québec, Canada), 2003-03-23) Hebdon, Robert; Mazerolle, Maurice
Show more
Using a comprehensive collective bargaining data set, we examine dispute resolution patterns of all bargaining units in the province of Ontario over a 10-year period. A central finding is that bargaining units covered by legislation requiring compulsory interest arbitration arrive at impasse 8.7 percent to 21.7 percent more often than bargaining units in the right to strike sectors. Even after controlling for legislative jurisdiction, union, bargaining unit size, occupation, agreement length, time trend, and part-time status, strong evidence was found that compulsory arbitration has both chilling and dependence effects on the bargaining process. The problem of failure to reach negotiated settlements is particularly acute in the health care sector, especially among hospitals. Our results also call into question the use of interest arbitration in a central bargaining context. The centralized structure appears to exacerbate the negative effects of interest arbitration.

The Transformation of Toronto’s Little India
(Toronto Metropolitan University, 2026) Menezes, Kaela; Bauder, Harald
Show more
Toronto’s Little India has been an anchor for South Asian culture in the Greater Toronto Area for half a century. However, in recent years, its distinct ethnic identity has been under threat. This study examines the transformation of Little India. Drawing on photographic documentation, municipal planning records, reports, and popular media sources, we trace patterns of business closures and redevelopment over the past decade and a half. The findings show that a range of factors, including gentrification and municipal development have contributed to the displacement of long-standing South Asian businesses even as the local Business Improvement Area continues to support community life through festivals, public art, and local programming. The study highlights tensions between economic development, cultural preservation, and community resilience.
This report is based on Kaela Menezes’ Major Research Paper which she submitted as a degree requirement to the Graduate Program in Immigration and Settlement Studies at Toronto Metropolitan University. We thank Sutama Ghosh for serving as Second Reader.
