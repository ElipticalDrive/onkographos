We introduce DRUID (directional ultrasound implicit field), an end-to-end framework for continuously representing 3-dimensional ultrasound data, useful for compounding sequences of plane-wave or 2D images. DRUID relies on an implicit neural representation, an optimized coarse-to-fine learning process, and several slicing strategies to select subsets of voxels for learning. To the best of our knowledge, DRUID is the first implicit representation adapted to fit plane-wave and radio-frequency data, the later requiring the design of the new loss function LSMAE to handle the signed signals with uneven distribution of negative and positive values and a large dynamic range. A set of experiments conducted on synthetic, in-vitro and in-vivo data, were designed to evaluate the performance for compounding sequences of plane-wave images with different insonification angles, compressing volumetric data or synthesizing unseen 2D views from a volume. Quantitative and qualitative assessments, confirm that implicitly representing US data with radio-frequency instead of B-mode results in a better resolution and improved shape fidelity. The impact of the amount of 2D data used for training is extensively discussed. Finally, our method demonstrates significant storage efficiency with a compression ratio of up to 148:1.
