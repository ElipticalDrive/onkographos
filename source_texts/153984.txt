Flow visualization has been an active research field for several years and various techniques have \nbeen proposed to visualize vector fields, streamlines and textures being the most effective and \npopular ones. While streamlines are suitable to get rough information on the behavior of the flow, \ntextures depict the flow properties at the pixel level. Depending on the situation the suitable \nrepresentation could be streamlines or texture. This paper presents a method to compute a sequence \nof streamline-based images of a vector field with different densities, ranging from sparse to texturelike \nrepresentations. It is based on an effective streamline placement algorithm and a production \nscheme that recalls those used in the multiresolution theory. Indeed a streamline defined at level J of \nthe hierarchy is defined for all levels J’>J. A viewer allows us to interactively select the desired \ndensity while zooming in and out in a vector field. The density of streamlines in the image can also \nbe automatically computed as a function of a derived quantity, such as velocity or vorticity.
