Second step of the renderer milestone: environment lighting and reflections from HDRIs.
What's in it
RenderResources::createEnvironment() bakes an equirectangular image (HDR or LDR) on the GPU with compute shaders:
a background cube map with mips;
a GGX-prefiltered specular cube map, using filtered importance sampling;
a split-sum BRDF table, baked once per RenderResources.
Diffuse irradiance is 9 spherical-harmonics coefficients, computed on the CPU.
SkyLight component (the first one in the scene is used; its Transform rotates the environment) and a skybox pass drawn after opaque geometry.
Forward shading adds image-based lighting with multiple-scattering compensation. Without a SkyLight, the ambient setting goes through the same SH path.
Assets: equirectangular mapping (Poly Haven layout, centre facing -z), ShIrradiance, makeSkyImage(), readPixel().
gltf_viewer: --environment file.hdr, with a procedural sky by default.
ADR 0014, English and Turkish docs, SH projection benchmark.
Verification
MSVC Debug/Release and clang-cl Debug build with zero warnings; all tests pass. GPU tests ran on an RTX 4070 SUPER with zero validation errors.
White furnace test: in a uniform white environment, white rough and smooth metals and dielectrics all match the background within 12/255.
Orientation tests: the background, its rotation and diffuse lighting all point the right way.
CPU tests: SH accuracy (uniform and cosine environments), Radiance HDR decoding, mapping round trips.
A 2K environment bakes in 65 ms in Release; most of that is the CPU SH projection at about 19 ns per texel.
I checked the viewer's screenshot visually: smooth metals reflect the sky and the horizon.
No HDRIs are committed; tests generate their own environments.
