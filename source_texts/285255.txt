Goal
Make the sphere look 3D. Add a point or directional light, shade surfaces by how directly they face it, add a large "ground" sphere or plane, and cast shadows.
Tasks
Return the nearest hit point and surface normal from intersections
Support a list of objects and pick the closest hit
Implement diffuse (Lambertian) shading using the dot product of normal and light direction
Cast a shadow ray from each hit point toward the light; darken the pixel if blocked
Offset shadow-ray origins slightly to avoid "shadow acne"
Add a tests/ folder with pytest tests for Vec3 operations and ray-sphere intersection (hit, miss, tangent, ray starting inside sphere)
Acceptance criteria
Image shows a shaded sphere resting on a ground surface with a visible shadow
No speckled "acne" artifacts on lit surfaces
pytest passes with at least 8 tests, runnable from VS Code's Testing panel
Test coverage of Vec3 and intersection code is at least 80% (pytest --cov)
Tool focus
Writing tests with Claude Code's help, then reviewing them critically: do they actually test edge cases, or just the happy path?
