Abstract
While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has achieved breakthrough performance in real-time novel view synthesis, its inherent volumetric representation often leads to severe geometric thickness redundancy when modeling surfaces and suffers from critical sampling mismatches and aliasing artifacts during cross-scale rendering. To address these issues, we propose a consistency-aware and anti-aliased 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) framework. First, in the geometric manifold construction phase, we introduce a tangent-frame parameterization to explicitly eliminate the normal thickness of primitives. Coupled with multi-source topological regularizations—comprising depth distortion and normal alignment constraints—our method strictly converges Gaussian primitives onto continuous physical manifolds, thoroughly eliminating spatial floaters within the camera frustum. Second, in the anti-aliasing rendering phase, we design a multi-stage adaptive frequency control pipeline. By integrating world-space smoothing, a scale-aware adaptive box filter, and sub-pixel supersampling, this mechanism dynamically intercepts high-frequency signals exceeding the Nyquist limit, effectively suppressing structural erosion and texture dilation artifacts under varying camera distances. Extensive experiments on the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset demonstrate that, compared to prevailing point-based rendering baselines, our method not only achieves state-of-the-art photometric fidelity but also extracts piece-wise smooth normal and depth maps with significantly reduced geometric redundancy, realizing lightweight and physically accurate reconstruction of complex scenes.
Keywords: Industrial Digital Twins, 2D Gaussian Splatting, Anti-Aliasing, Geometric Consistency
