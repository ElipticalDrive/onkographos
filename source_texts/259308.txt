To maximize the informational content of sampled sub-volumes in CT scans, consider the following strategies:
1. Saliency-Guided Sampling
Use a pre-trained or lightweight model to estimate regions of high anatomical or pathological importance.
Edge detection, gradient-based methods, or deep learning-based saliency maps can help prioritize regions containing structures like organs or lesions.
2. Intensity-Based Sampling
CT scans have a lot of empty space (air), which can be filtered out using intensity thresholds.
Focus sampling on areas with Hounsfield Unit (HU) values that correspond to soft tissue, bone, or contrast-enhanced regions.
3. Entropy-Based Sampling
Compute local entropy within 96³ neighborhoods.
Prioritize sub-volumes with high entropy, which often correspond to regions with structural variation (organs, blood vessels, tumors).
4. Anatomical Region-Guided Sampling
Use organ segmentation models to target specific structures (lungs, liver, brain, etc.).
Ensure a diverse set of sub-volumes covering different anatomical regions.
5. Adaptive Grid Sampling
Instead of random sampling, divide the CT scan into overlapping grids and prioritize sub-volumes with the highest structural complexity.
Use a sliding window with an overlap (e.g., 50%) to maximize coverage.
6. Multi-Scale Sampling
Sample at different scales to capture both local and global information.
Example: Extract 96³ sub-volumes but also downsampled 192³ or 48³ sub-volumes for hierarchical learning.
