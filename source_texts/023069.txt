3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can propagate unreliable photometric residuals into geometric parameters in
weakly textured, repetitive-texture, low-light, and occlusion-boundary regions. We investigate VisibilityAware Texture Consensus (VTC), parameter-level loss routing, and local-PCA structural constraints for
texture-degraded reconstruction. Under a strict 80/20 path-isolated protocol on four NeRF Synthetic scenes,
the simplified configuration improves the official-validation macro-average by 0.0661 dB in PSNR,
0.00246 in SSIM, 0.00136 in Alpha IoU, and reduces LPIPS by 0.00036 and Outside alpha by 6.33×10−5
relative to Control. In an additional four-scene, five-seed paired matrix at 300 iterations, PSNR improves
for all five seeds in every scene; the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives p=0.0625.
Controllable texture removal, illumination reduction, and occlusion experiments show monotonic changes
in degradation proxies, supporting the proposed identifiability analysis. Additional DTU and Mip-NeRF
360 experiments verify cross-dataset interfaces, while standard-densification tests delimit transfer behavior.
Overall, the evidence supports improved relative robustness under a low-budget fixed-Gaussian protocol,
while not supporting state-of-the-art claims on real-world data or performance gains from consensus
inheritance.

The core objective of multi-view 3D reconstruction is to recover scene geometry and surface appearance
simultaneously, enabling downstream applications such as virtual reality, digital twins, film and television
production, and robotic perception (see the survey in [1]). Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), based on
implicit neural fields, represent scenes as continuous radiance fields and achieve high-quality novel-view
synthesis [2–4], but dense sampling along camera rays and per-sample network queries incur substantial
training and rendering costs. In contrast, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) parameterizes a scene with explicit
anisotropic 3D Gaussians and combines them with differentiable rasterization for high-quality real-time
rendering [5]. Extensions of 3DGS have continued to advance in anti-aliasing, density control, dynamic
reconstruction, and scene editing [6,7]. Nevertheless, vanilla 3DGS uses a single image-reconstruction
objective to optimize geometry and appearance jointly, allowing unreliable photometric residuals in weakly
textured regions to propagate into geometric variables and thereby limiting its applicability in degraded
scenes.

3DGS updates geometric and appearance parameters using the same rendering error. When a scene
contains weakly textured surfaces, repetitive textures, dark occlusions, or slender structures, cross-view
color correspondences become unstable, and local color, exposure, and occlusion errors may be interpreted
as geometric deviations. The resulting abnormal updates of Gaussian centers, scales, and opacities manifest
as boundary drift, contour expansion, thin-rod fracture, alpha leakage outside the foreground mask, and
floating dark Gaussians (Fig. 1). Accordingly, we define a texture-degraded scene as an observation
condition in which the foreground contains a high proportion of low-texture pixels, large cross-view color
dispersion, or insufficient foreground–background boundary support, such that single-view photometric
supervision cannot stably constrain geometric parameters. This operational definition emphasizes

Existing approaches strengthen geometric reconstruction through depth supervision [8], normal and surface
constraints [9–11], edge regularization, and adaptive density control [12,13]. However, three common
limitations remain. First, visible observations are typically treated as equally reliable, without observationlevel rejection of colors corrupted by occlusion, background mixing, or exposure differences. Second, local
geometric and appearance relationships may be modeled jointly, so color errors can be back-propagated to
geometric parameters through a shared rendering objective. Third, uniform geometric constraints are often
applied to all regions, restricting the optimization freedom of normally textured areas. Texture-degraded
reconstruction therefore requires separate treatment at three levels: filtering unreliable observations,
disentangling geometry from appearance in the parameter pathways, and selectively constraining geometric
updates according to local structure.

We first establish an identifiability analysis of geometric supervision under texture degradation (Sec. 3.2),
and then design the method from three perspectives: observation reliability, parameter routing, and local
structural statistics. Visibility-Aware Texture Consensus (VTC) filters observations using image bounds,
foreground membership, projected visibility, and depth consistency, and constructs cross-view color
consensus and consensus weights from the channel-wise median and median absolute deviation (MAD)
(Sec. 3.3). Parameter routing restricts the parameter groups affected by each loss term, yielding
optimization-level geometry–appearance decoupling (Sec. 3.4). Local principal component analysis (local
PCA) estimates neighborhood structural directions and is used to construct structural confidence and a
geometric anchoring regularizer (Sec. 3.5).

is measured with PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, whereas local diagnostics use Alpha IoU, alpha leakage
outside the foreground mask, and counts of anomalous Gaussians. Experiments on directional
projection and parameter-level gradient routing distinguish changes in optimization behavior from
changes in reconstruction quality. Under the fixed-Gaussian protocol, small but paired improvements

Neural Radiance Fields and 3D Gaussian Scene Representations

Multi-view 3D reconstruction involves camera-pose estimation, geometric recovery, and appearance
modeling. Classical structure-from-motion methods estimate camera parameters and sparse point clouds
through feature matching and multi-view geometric constraints, with COLMAP becoming a standard
preprocessing tool [14]. Building on this pipeline, NeRF represents a scene as a continuous radiance field
conditioned on spatial position and viewing direction and performs high-quality novel-view synthesis
through volume rendering [2]. Mip-NeRF 360 further introduces spatial parameterization and anti-aliasing
mechanisms for unbounded scenes, improving rendering stability under large scale variations [3]. Recent
surveys have systematically reviewed neural radiance fields and inverse-rendering-based reconstruction
[1,4,6]. However, implicit representations typically require dense sampling along camera rays and repeated
network queries, resulting in relatively high training and rendering costs.

3DGS represents a scene with a set of explicit anisotropic 3D Gaussians and uses differentiable
rasterization for high-quality real-time rendering [5], avoiding extensive empty-space sampling and
offering clear advantages in training efficiency and rendering speed. Subsequent work has focused on antialiasing, density control, and primitive management. Mip-Splatting reduces view-scaling aliasing through
3D smoothing and 2D Mip filtering [12]; GaussianPro uses progressive Gaussian propagation to improve
representation in under-reconstructed regions [15]; Localized Points Management refines densification and
pruning from the perspective of local Gaussian management [13]; and Rendering-Aware Bayesian 3DGS
introduces uncertainty estimation and adaptive complexity control from a probabilistic perspective [16].

These methods mainly address representation efficiency, sampling stability, and control of Gaussian count.
Vanilla 3DGS, however, still relies on image-space photometric residuals to optimize geometry and
appearance simultaneously. In weakly textured, repetitive, or dark occluded regions, these residuals lack
stable geometric correspondences and can therefore provide erroneous supervision to Gaussian position,
scale, and opacity.

To address the insufficient geometric constraints of vanilla 3DGS, extensive research has explored depth
priors, surface alignment, and explicit structural relationships. For depth priors, DNGaussian introduces
monocular depth into Gaussian optimization through global-to-local depth normalization under sparse-view
settings [8], while Sparse2DGS prioritizes geometric recovery in sparse views and enhances surface
reconstruction of 2D Gaussians using geometric priors [17]. For surface alignment, SuGaR regularizes 3D
Gaussians to align with scene surfaces and supports mesh extraction [9]; 2D Gaussian Splatting replaces
3D ellipsoidal Gaussians with 2D Gaussian disks having explicit tangent-plane structure, allowing
primitives to better conform to actual surfaces [10]; Geometry Field Splatting represents local surfaces with
Gaussian surfels and uses a geometry field to enhance surface continuity [18]; and SurfaceSplat unifies
explicit surface reconstruction and Gaussian splatting in a collaborative optimization framework [11]. For
explicit structural relationships, GeoGaussian introduces geometry-aware initialization and optimization for
weakly textured planes and geometrically degenerate regions [19]; SAGS builds structure-aware
relationships among Gaussians and constrains primitive motion using local and global structural
information [20]; and MGSR mutually reinforces 2D and 3D representations to improve surface
reconstruction under varying illumination [21]. These studies demonstrate the importance of depth,
normals, surface structure, and Gaussian-neighborhood relationships for geometric recovery. Most
methods, however, constrain all Gaussian parameters through additional geometric supervision or uniform
structural regularization without separating local geometric from appearance relationships. When
appearance variation is inconsistent with true structure, color errors may still propagate to geometric
variables through the shared rendering objective. Section 3.4 complements this line of work by restricting,
at the optimization level, the parameter groups affected by each loss term.

Under sparse-view settings, limited observations can cause Gaussian representations to overfit the training
views, producing geometric drift and appearance artifacts in unobserved regions. SparseGS proposes
Gaussian optimization for panoramic sparse-view synthesis and improves real-time novel-view quality with
few input views [22]. CoR-GS jointly optimizes two Gaussian radiance fields with stochastic differences
and uses point-level and rendering-level disagreements for co-regularization, thereby reducing sparse-view
overfitting [23]. Transient distractors in real scenes, such as pedestrians, vehicles, and reflections, also
break cross-view consistency. DeSplat decomposes static-scene Gaussians from view-dependent distractor
components to prevent transient content from being baked into the scene representation [24]. These
approaches primarily compensate for insufficient observations using model disagreement or additional
priors, or remove anomalous components altogether. Texture-degraded regions, however, are part of the
scene itself and cannot simply be removed; observation reliability must instead be assessed from visibility,
boundary support, and cross-view consistency.

A closely related line of research concerns robust supervision and uncertainty modeling in neural radiance
fields. NeRF-W uses appearance embeddings and a transient head to decompose illumination changes and
temporary occlusions in unconstrained photo collections [28]. RobustNeRF models distractors as
optimization noise and automatically suppresses abnormal observations with robust losses [29]. RawNeRF
improves low-light reconstruction from raw images through noise modeling and exposure stabilization
[30]. Uncertainty-quantification approaches such as Bayes' Rays use posterior uncertainty to characterize
regional reliability [31]. These works address unreliable observations in implicit representations either by
decomposing transient components or by globally down-weighting them through robust objectives. VTC
differs in that it operates on explicit Gaussian representations, explicitly determines visibility and depth
consistency at Gaussian granularity, and down-weights supervision using a fixed robust consensus target

identifiability of geometric supervision under texture degradation has not yet been systematically
examined.

Geometry–Appearance Decoupled Modeling

Geometric structure and surface appearance have distinct physical meanings in Gaussian representations,
yet vanilla 3DGS jointly optimizes Gaussian centers, covariances, opacities, and spherical-harmonic
coefficients through a unified image-reconstruction loss. Although this joint parameterization facilitates
end-to-end training, it may introduce undesirable coupling between geometry and appearance. GStex
equips 2D Gaussians with per-primitive texture representations, separating surface texture from Gaussian
geometric parameters [25]. Geometry Gaussians further pursues explicit appearance–geometry decoupling
through independent representations, reducing the effect of view-dependent color on geometric recovery
[26]. In implicit neural fields, NeRV jointly predicts volumetric density, surface normals, material
parameters, and direction-dependent light visibility to decompose the physical imaging process [27];
related inverse-rendering literature has also surveyed surface reconstruction and material–illumination
estimation [1]. Unlike these approaches, we introduce no new representation parameters and do not
estimate BRDFs or illumination components. Instead, under fixed observation lighting, we delimit the
reliable range of appearance supervision through pre-training visibility filtering and cross-view color
statistics, and we constrain both the parameter groups acted on by each loss and the direction of geometric
updates at the optimization level. In other words, existing decoupling methods primarily separate
representation parameters or texture representations, whereas our method decouples supervision attribution,
parameter pathways, and update directions. Even when appearance and geometry are separated in the
representation, unreliable photometric residuals can still back-propagate into geometric variables through a
joint rendering objective; this optimization-level coupling is the focus of our work.

Our basic hypothesis regarding the failure mechanism is that weakly textured regions and foreground–
background mixed boundaries reduce the identifiability of photometric gradients with respect to geometric
parameters. In a local coordinate system, the Gaussian-center gradient can be decomposed into components
along the principal structural axis or tangent plane and components along the orthogonal (normal) direction.
The former correspond to plausible within-structure adjustments, whereas the latter are more susceptible to
color ambiguity, occlusion ordering, and background mixing. We therefore distinguish two types of
experimental evidence. System-level performance evidence on held-out views (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS,
Alpha IoU, and outside-mask leakage) is used to delimit performance claims, whereas directional
mechanism evidence in the shared-initialization local-PCA coordinate system (allowed-direction and
orthogonal components of cumulative center displacement) is used to delimit mechanism claims. These two
evidence types are independent and jointly define the scope within which the method can be interpreted. A
formal statement of the hypothesis, the identifiability criterion, and a proof sketch are given in Sec. 3.2.

Conclusion
735
736
737
738
739

743
744
745
746
747
This study analyzes how unreliable photometric supervision affects geometric optimization in
texture-degraded scenes and introduces VTC, parameter-level loss routing, and local-PCA structural
constraints. Under the controlled fixed-Gaussian protocol, the simplified configuration produces
consistent paired improvements across four scenes and five random seeds, while the degradation
and mechanism experiments support the intended observation-reliability interpretation. Ablations
further show that VTC should be retained, whereas directional projection and aggressive attenuation
of RGB gradients to geometry do not provide reliable quality gains and are therefore excluded from
the default configuration. The current evidence also identifies a transfer boundary: fixed VTC does
not improve the standard adaptive-densification backend, and the present real-data experiments
validate pipeline compatibility rather than cross-dataset generalization. Overall, the method is best
supported as a robust optimization strategy for texture-degraded reconstruction under controlled
fixed-Gaussian settings. Future work will focus on adaptive consensus for evolving Gaussian
populations and broader real-scene validation.
