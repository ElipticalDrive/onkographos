Existing multi-style image generation methods face critical challenges: insufficient content-style decoupling, high computational costs for high-resolution generation, and structural distortion during style transfer. To address these, we propose the Dual-Conditional Lightweight Style Diffusion Model (DCLSDM), a novel approach enhancing content-style decoupling via a dual-conditional control mechanism. This mechanism independently manages content structure and style expression, enabling better control in style transfer. Experimental results on WikiArt and Summer2Winter Yosemite datasets show DCLSDM outperforms comparative models in SSIM, LPIPS, and FID, with significant improvements in inference time, memory usage, and parameter scale–making it suitable for resource-constrained scenarios. It offers an efficient, controllable solution for multi-style image generation, with potential in content creation and digital art production.

In today’s digital age, the booming new media industry drives escalating demands for diverse, personalized image generation. In advertising and marketing1,2, creating promotional posters with styles from vintage to modern minimalism requires efficient multi-style generation. Film post-production demands unique visual styles for different genres, while social media users seek to showcase individuality through creatively styled images3,4. Traditional manual design models, limited by time-consuming processes and designers’ personal styles, fail to meet the industry’s need for large-scale, varied style production. Recent advances in visual foundation models and zero-shot learning5–9 further underscore the industry’s shift toward efficient, flexible image generation, yet multi-style tasks still lack tailored solutions to balance control and efficiency. Deep learning has revolutionized image generation, with GANs and VAEs achieving notable results10–13. However, existing methods face three critical challenges in multi-style generation: First, insufficient content-style decoupling causes unintended content changes during style transfer14–16. Second, high computational costs for high-resolution generation restrict applications on mobile or resource-constrained devices. Third, structural distortion in style transfer reduces the practical value of generated images. Diffusion models, especially the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM)17–19, offer a new solution. By shifting the diffusion process to a low-dimensional latent space, LDM maintains high generation quality while cutting computational costs20–22. Despite these advantages, existing LDM-based approaches still struggle with precise content-style control and efficient multi-style fusion, highlighting the need for further innovation.
Research objectives and contributions
This study aims to construct an efficient and controllable multi-style image generation model that achieves independent control of content and style while ensuring both generation quality and operational efficiency. To achieve this goal, we propose the Dual-Condition Lightweight Style Diffusion Model (DCLSDM), whose core methodology is as follows:
DCLSDM is based on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) architecture and implements multi-style image generation and control through four collaborative modules. First, the content feature extraction module uses the Perceiver IO lightweight attention network to extract geometric structure and semantic features from input content (images or text descriptions), forming the content condition vector. This module reduces computational complexity while maintaining feature expression ability through cross-modal attention. Second, the style feature processing module supports both image and text style inputs. It extracts style features through convolutional networks or text encoders and uses a weighted Style Interpolation algorithm to achieve weighted fusion of multi-style features, generating the style condition vector. Next, the dual-condition diffusion generation module injects content and style conditions into the LDM’s U-Net network through independent paths. Content conditions constrain structure generation through self-attention layers, while style conditions control texture styles through cross-attention layers, achieving independent control of content and style. Finally, the structural constraint module incorporates ControlNet, working in tandem with content conditions to enhance structural consistency in the generation process, avoiding structural distortion caused by style transfer.
Specific research objectives include: (1) proposing a dual-condition control mechanism to solve the content-style decoupling problem, enabling the model to flexibly apply different styles to the same content or maintain the style while altering the content; (2) designing a lightweight feature extraction scheme to reduce computational costs, making it suitable for more resource-constrained applications; (3) establishing a structural constraint mechanism to enhance the practicality of the generated images, ensuring that the generated images maintain reasonable structure and semantic information while having diverse styles.
The innovations and contributions of this study are mainly reflected in three aspects:
We propose a dual-condition control architecture based on Perceiver IO and cross-attention, which allows for independent extraction and fusion of content structure features and style features. This approach enables flexible application of diverse styles to content while preserving content integrity, achieving effective decoupling of content and style.
We introduce a weighted Style Interpolation algorithm, which facilitates smooth transitions between multiple style feature vectors. By interpolating and weighting the style features, we enhance the model’s ability to generate diverse styles, addressing the challenge of mixing complex styles without losing structural consistency. Additionally, ControlNet is integrated with the dual-condition model to ensure that the structure remains intact during style transfer.
We replace the traditional ViT with Perceiver IO as the content feature extractor. This choice reduces the model’s computational complexity while maintaining the ability to express content features, making the model more efficient and suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
Related workDiffusion-based methods
The development of diffusion models began with the introduction of the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM). Researchers constructed a Markov chain noise addition and removal process, achieving image generation from random noise to real images23–25. DDPM’s core contribution lies in converting image generation into a noise prediction problem, significantly improving the generation quality compared to GANs and VAEs26–29. However, DDPM directly performs the diffusion process in pixel space, resulting in computational complexity that increases exponentially with image resolution. To address this efficiency bottleneck, the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) was proposed. It compresses high-dimensional images into low-dimensional latent spaces using a pre-trained autoencoder, completing the diffusion process in the latent space32. LDM’s three-module architecture (autoencoder, latent diffuser, and conditional encoder) provides a flexible framework for conditional generation tasks. The conditional encoder can convert information from text, images, and other modalities into feature constraints33–35. For example, Stable Diffusion, based on the LDM architecture, incorporates the CLIP text encoder to guide image generation with text, driving the industrial application of diffusion models36–38. However, existing LDM’s conditional injection mechanism is mainly designed for single-style constraints, and the fusion of conditional features and latent features lacks an explicit decoupling mechanism, making it difficult to independently control content and style.
Subsequent research focused on improving the controllability of diffusion models. Specifically, Rombach et al. enhanced the alignment of text-image features in the cross-attention module based on LDM, improving the accuracy of style descriptions39. However, this method overly depends on the precision of text semantics, limiting the flexibility in expressing abstract styles such as “vintage” and “minimalist.” Other researchers proposed StyleALIGN, which injects style feature vectors at multiple time steps during the diffusion process and introduces a style consistency loss function to enhance the generation ability for specific artistic styles40–43. However, this method requires training a separate style encoder for each style, making model fine-tuning costly and unsuitable for scenarios that require rapid style switching. Notably, existing LDM-based multi-conditional models (including the above variants) uniformly adopt shared attention layers for fusing content and style features–this inherent design leads to unavoidable content-style coupling, as style adjustments easily distort core content structures. Additionally, none of these models integrate cross-modal style interpolation that avoids separate encoder training, failing to balance style diversity and adaptation efficiency.
In contrast to such “simple component combination” designs, our DCLSDM builds on LDM’s latent space advantage while addressing its decoupling and multi-style limitations through two fundamental innovations: first, we design dual independent attention paths (one dedicated to Perceiver IO-extracted content features, the other to multi-modal style vectors) with adaptive weight adjustment, which breaks the content-style coupling caused by shared attention layers; second, we integrate a dynamic cross-modal style interpolation module that enables smooth fusion of image/text style inputs without training separate encoders, solving the high fine-tuning cost of single-style encoder methods like StyleALIGN.
Style transfer approaches
Style transfer approaches mainly include neural style transfer and generative style modulation. Neural style transfer originated from the optimization method proposed by Gatys et al., which realizes style transfer by minimizing content loss (based on VGG features) and style loss (based on Gram matrices)44. But this method requires optimizing each image individually, leading to low efficiency. Johnson et al. proposed fast style transfer networks, which embed style transfer parameters into convolutional networks to achieve end-to-end generation, although the diversity of styles is limited45. Generative style modulation methods, represented by StyleGAN, inject style vectors at different layers of the generative network to control style45–47. However, the style vector and content features are coupled at the layer level, making it difficult to adjust the style without distorting the content structure. In the diffusion model framework, text-guided style generation methods use the CLIP text encoder to map style descriptions. However, the semantic ambiguity of abstract styles such as “Impressionism” and “minimalism” leads to significant deviations between the generated results and expectations48.
Against these issues, DCLSDM supports both image and text style inputs to reduce semantic ambiguity, and separates content and style control paths to avoid the coupling-induced structural distortion in StyleGAN-like methods. It is worth noting that existing multi-conditional diffusion models in style transfer either lack dedicated structural constraint mechanisms or simply stack ControlNet with LDM–this naive combination leads to increased computational overhead without optimizing feature interaction. Our DCLSDM differs here by realizing lightweight integration of Perceiver IO (content extraction) and ControlNet (structural constraint) via shared latent space mapping: this design not only retains ControlNet’s structural protection ability but also reduces computational overhead by 30% compared to naive combinations, addressing the efficiency bottleneck of existing multi-conditional style transfer models.

MethodsModel architecture
The Dual-Condition Lightweight Style Diffusion Model (DCLSDM) proposed in this paper aims to address key challenges in multi-style image generation, including content-style decoupling, structural consistency control, and practical efficiency requirements. The model constructs a generative framework that integrates latent diffusion mechanisms with dual-condition control. The overall architecture adopts a modular design, achieving end-to-end generation from input conditions to multi-style images through the collaboration of multiple modules. The DCLSDM architecture consists of five core modules: the latent diffusion base module, content feature extraction module, style feature processing module, dual-condition fusion generation module, and structural constraint module. These modules maintain functional independence while forming an organic whole through feature interaction, collectively supporting the efficient and controllable generation of multi-style images.
The latent diffusion base module, built on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) architecture, serves as the generative engine, generating images from noise in low-dimensional latent space to ensure high-quality generation. The content feature extraction module uses the lightweight Perceiver IO attention network to extract geometric and semantic features from image or text input content, forming a content condition vector for generation constraints. Unlike naive Perceiver IO-LDM integration that only replaces standard encoders without optimizing feature compatibility, this module adds a shared latent space mapping layer between Perceiver IO and the latent diffusion module. This layer aligns content features with LDM’s latent space efficiently, reducing feature conversion overhead by 28% compared to independent processing.
The style feature processing module supports both image and text style inputs. It extracts style features via dedicated encoders and integrates multi-style feature merging to combine diverse style attributes, generating a style condition vector for style control. This merging process uses a style similarity weighting mechanism (calculated by cosine similarity between style feature distributions) to adaptively adjust each style’s weight. It avoids style blurring from linear combination and enables cross-modal style fusion (e.g., text-described Impressionism with Van Gogh image style) which existing LDM-based methods cannot achieve. The dual-condition fusion generation module, as the core control center, differs from existing multi-conditional diffusion models that use shared attention layers (causing content-style coupling). It designs two independent feature interaction paths: the content path injects Perceiver IO-extracted features into U-Net’s middle layers (focused on structural constraint) while the style path injects merged style features into U-Net’s shallow and deep layers (focused on texture control). A dynamic attention weight adjuster is added to each path, which adaptively enhances content path weights based on input content complexity (e.g., multi-object high-complexity scenes vs simple portraits) to prevent structural distortion, solving shared attention’s one-size-fits-all fusion problem. The structural constraint module, based on ControlNet and collaborating with the content feature extraction module, enhances generation structural stability by introducing edge and depth geometric information to avoid style transfer-induced distortion. Unlike direct ControlNet-LDM stacking that doubles computational costs, this module shares the content feature extraction module’s edge and depth geometric information, eliminating redundant extraction and reducing overall model parameters by 15% while maintaining constraint accuracy.
This modular design optimizes each module specifically and enables efficient collaboration via a unified feature interaction interface, ensuring the model maintains high generation quality while supporting independent content-style control, flexible multi-style fusion, and precise structural constraints–meeting new media multi-style image generation requirements.

Dual-condition control mechanism design
The dual-condition control mechanism is the core of DCLSDM for realizing decoupled regulation of content and style. Through independent content feature extraction and style feature injection paths, it achieves refined control over the generation process. This mechanism introduces content conditions and style conditions as independent variables into the diffusion process, enabling generated images to maintain the structural integrity of input content while flexibly adapting to target styles. Through the collaborative work of two sub-modules, it respectively completes accurate extraction of content features and flexible injection and fusion of style features, providing clear constraint signals for the subsequent diffusion generation process.
Content feature extraction module
The core function of the content feature extraction module is to extract geometric structure and semantic information from input content, providing stable content constraints for multi-style generation. This module adopts the Perceiver IO lightweight attention network as the core architecture–this general-purpose framework processes diverse data types via domain-agnostic Transformer-style attention, first mapping inputs to a low-dimensional latent space to reduce computational costs while enabling deep network construction for large-scale inputs like images.

ExperimentDatasets
In this experiment, three publicly available datasets are selected to build the benchmark for the style transfer task, providing comprehensive support for evaluating the model performance across three dimensions: content diversity, artistic style typicality, and scene style generalization. The content dataset is MS-COCO (Microsoft Common Objects in Context), which contains over 330,000 high-resolution natural images, covering 80 object categories and 91 material categories. The images include diverse scenes, such as indoor scenes (e.g., living rooms, kitchens), outdoor scenes (e.g., streets, parks), natural landscapes (e.g., mountains, forests), and human activities (e.g., gatherings, sports). Each image is annotated with comprehensive information such as object detection, semantic segmentation, and image captions, providing rich structural reference samples for the verification of the content feature extraction module, ensuring that the model’s performance can be thoroughly tested under varying content complexity.
The style dataset selected is WikiArt, which contains approximately 85,000 artworks, covering 27 major art movements, including Impressionism, Realism, Abstract Expressionism, Baroque, Rococo, and more. Evaluation metrics selection
This study selects evaluation metrics from three core dimensions: content integrity, generation quality, and decoupling effectiveness, combining quantitative analysis with qualitative visual comparison to comprehensively assess the model’s performance. For content integrity, the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) is used to measure the structural consistency between the generated image and the original content at the pixel level. Additionally, the Intersection over Union (IoU) based on semantic annotations is employed to verify the retention of content semantic regions. To assess generation quality, Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) are introduced. PSNR reflects the degree of image detail preservation from the perspective of pixel-level error, LPIPS captures image differences in accordance with human visual perception using a pre-trained network, and FID measures the closeness between the generated image distribution and the real image distribution. Decoupling effectiveness is quantified by the mutual information reduction rate, which measures the independence between content features and style features, thereby verifying the model’s effectiveness in content-style decoupling control. All metrics are reported as the mean ± variance of 10 independent test runs to ensure statistical reliability.
Experimental results
To comprehensively validate the performance of the proposed model in the content-style decoupled transfer task, this experiment selected nine representative models in the current style transfer field for comparative analysis. These include StyleGAN-NADA58 (a CLIP-guided unsupervised domain adaptation method) based on generator fine-tuning; ControlNet59 (a conditional control framework for text-to-image diffusion models) with a focus on structural constraints; StyleStudio60 (a text-driven style transfer model) with selective control of style elements; DEADiff61(an efficient stylized diffusion model using a decoupled representation); StyleMamba62(an efficient implementation of text-driven image style transfer) based on a state-space model; ArtBank63 (an artistic style transfer method) combining a pre-trained diffusion model with an implicit style cue library; S2WAT64 (a layered visual Transformer with a striped window attention mechanism); InstructG2I65 (a graph context-conditioned diffusion model part of Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems Main Conference Track, designed for generating images from multimodal attributed graphs with controllable generation via graph classifier-free guidance); and PixWizard66 (a versatile image-to-image visual assistant based on free-form language instructions, adopting Diffusion Transformers as the foundation model with a flexible any resolution mechanism and structure-aware, semantic-aware guidance to support image generation, manipulation, and translation). Additionally, MobileDiffusion67 and SnapFusion68, two representative industrial-grade models optimized for mobile devices, were included in the comparative analysis. Using multi-dimensional metrics and qualitative visual comparisons, we systematically evaluated the performance of each model in terms of content preservation, style fidelity, generation quality, and decoupling.

The ablation study results, integrated from Table 6 and Fig. 4, demonstrate that the full Dual-Condition Lightweight Style Diffusion Model (DCLSDM) achieves optimal performance across all evaluation metrics on both the WikiArt and Summer2Winter datasets. This validates the synergistic effectiveness of its core components. All performance differences between the full model and its variants are statistically significant with p < 0.05 or p < 0.01, confirming the genuine contributions of each component rather than random data fluctuations. When replacing the Perceiver IO content feature extractor with ViT, the visual results show that generated images lack fine structural preservation. Artwork outlines in WikiArt and mountain contours in Summer2Winter become blurred. Quantitatively, this translates to a 0.034 SSIM decrease, 0.036 LPIPS increase, 2.13 dB PSNR drop and 4.35 FID rise on WikiArt with p < 0.01. On Summer2Winter, SSIM falls by 0.037, LPIPS increases by 0.031 and FID climbs by 4.13 with p < 0.01. This consistency between visual degradation and quantitative declines confirms Perceiver IO’s superiority in balancing feature expression and computational efficiency–its cross-modal attention mechanism compresses content features into low-dimensional latent space without losing structural details, which aligns with the model’s lightweight design goal. Removing the weighted Style Interpolation module leads to disjointed style fusion in the visual results. Mixed artistic styles in WikiArt appear fragmented, and seasonal tone transitions in Summer2Winter lack coherence. Corresponding metrics show LPIPS increases by 0.025 and FID rises by 2.61 on WikiArt with p < 0.05. On Summer2Winter, LPIPS increases by 0.018 and FID rises by 2.55 with p < 0.05. This verifies the module’s role in enhancing style fidelity and diversity–its dynamic weighting strategy fuses multi-style features smoothly, avoiding the “style averaging” blur seen in other interpolation methods. The most severe degradation occurs in the group without ControlNet. The visual results reveal severe structural distortion. Artwork shapes in WikiArt are distorted, and tree outlines in Summer2Winter are unrecognizable. Quantitatively, this translates to a 0.068 SSIM decrease, 2.95 dB PSNR drop and 6.83 FID rise on WikiArt with p < 0.01. On Summer2Winter, SSIM falls by 0.071, PSNR drops by 2.55 dB and FID increases by 6.57 with p < 0.01. This proves ControlNet’s critical role in maintaining structural consistency–its trainable control module encodes edge and depth information into diffusion features, locking key geometric details during style transfer to avoid distortion. Using a shared attention path causes muddled content-style integration in the visual results. Style textures overwrite content details such as brushstrokes obscuring WikiArt flower shapes. Content also disrupts style as seen in the Summer2Winter mountain edges breaking winter tones. The metrics mirror this with a 0.05 SSIM decrease and 0.04 LPIPS increase on WikiArt with p < 0.01. On Summer2Winter, SSIM falls by 0.046 and FID rises by 4.49 with p < 0.01. This confirms the necessity of dual-condition independent paths–separate content and style interaction channels ensure each factor exerts control without mutual interference, realizing effective content-style decoupling. For the “Perceiver IO with Self-Attention” group, the visual results show reduced cross-modal feature capture. Text-described “snow-capped mountains” in Summer2Winter lose semantic alignment, and WikiArt image content misses fine details. The metrics record SSIM decreasing to 0.801 and 0.812, LPIPS rising to 0.227 and 0.211, FID climbing to 26.14 and 24.98 on WikiArt and Summer2Winter respectively with p < 0.05. In terms of content-style feature mutual information, the full model achieves mutual information values of 1.23 ± 0.11 on the WikiArt dataset and 1.18 ± 0.10 on the Summer2Winter dataset. In contrast, the model without decoupling constraint loss shows significantly higher mutual information values, reaching 1.99 ± 0.15 on WikiArt and 1.83 ± 0.13 on Summer2Winter. The lower mutual information of the full model demonstrates that the decoupling constraint loss effectively reduces the correlation between content and style features, laying a foundation for their independent control at the feature level. For the structural preservation verification under fixed content and multi-style transfer, 3 typical content images were used, with 5 distinct styles applied to each content. The full model achieved an average SSIM of 0.84 ± 0.02 on WikiArt and 0.85 ± 0.02 on Summer2Winter between the generated images and the original content. Even the minimum SSIM values remained above 0.82 ± 0.03 on and 0.83 ± 0.02 on Summer2Winter . This indicates that the model can stably retain the core structure of the content despite significant style differences, verifying the effectiveness of the content control path in ensuring structural integrity. In terms of style fidelity for fixed content and multi-style transfer, the full model achieved an average LPIPS of 0.201 ± 0.018 on WikiArt and 0.192 ± 0.017 on Summer2Winter between the generated images and the target styles. The low LPIPS values confirm that the model does not sacrifice the accuracy of style expression while stably preserving content structure, thereby achieving effective content-style decoupling and meeting the core requirement of “constant content with flexible style switching.”
DiscussionKey findings and limitations
The proposed Dual-Conditional Lightweight Style Diffusion Model demonstrates notable strengths. Its dual-conditional control architecture integrates content feature extraction via Perceiver IO and independent style injection to achieve effective content-style decoupling. This ensures precise preservation of core content structures during style transfer, outperforming models like StyleGAN-NADA, ControlNet, InstructG2I, and PixWizard in maintaining content integrity while infusing diverse styles. Leveraging latent diffusion and lightweight components reduces computational complexity, leading to faster inference and lower memory usage than other models. For example, InstructG2I uses approximately 18% more memory and PixWizard has about 25% longer inference time. This makes DCLSDM especially suitable for resource-constrained scenarios, such as mobile applications. The weighted Style Interpolation and multi-modal input support enable flexible adaptation to various styles, including abstract art, with higher fidelity.
Synthesizing experimental findings from the WikiArt and Summer2Winter Yosemite datasets, these advantages are quantitatively validated. DCLSDM achieves an SSIM of 0.832–0.842 compared to 0.796–0.822 for InstructG2I and PixWizard, and a LPIPS of 0.187–0.195 compared to 0.201–0.232 for InstructG2I and PixWizard, while maintaining fewer parameters (148M vs. 235M and 260M for the two baselines). This confirms that its decoupling design and lightweight framework effectively balance generation quality and efficiency. Ablation studies further reinforce this–removing Perceiver IO reduces SSIM by 0.034, while omitting dynamic style interpolation increases LPIPS by 0.025, proving the synergistic value of each core module. These findings validate that independent conditional paths, rather than shared attention, provide a viable solution to the long-standing content-style coupling issue in latent diffusion models. This offers a new technical paradigm for multi-style generation tasks. Additionally, DCLSDM’s balance of performance and efficiency bridges the gap between academic research and industrial application. It demonstrates that diffusion-based style generation models can be adapted for resource-constrained scenarios, such as mobile new media, which was previously challenging for heavyweight models like ArtBank, as well as InstructG2I and PixWizard (both with higher computational overhead). However, the model does have limitations. While DCLSDM excels in handling a wide range of styles, it can struggle when the input style is significantly different from the ones present in the training data–similar to InstructG2I’s challenges with unseen graph structures and PixWizard’s limitations in rare style categories. In these cases, the model might not fully capture the target style, resulting in suboptimal style transfer. Additionally, the model’s performance is highly sensitive to input quality. For example, ambiguous or poorly defined input descriptions, or low-resolution content images, can lead to degraded results where the content structure is not well preserved, or the style transfer is inconsistent–an issue that also affects the instruction-driven capabilities of PixWizard. These challenges are typical of models that rely on well-defined, high-quality inputs, and while they do not constitute widespread failure, they point to areas for improvement, particularly in expanding the model’s robustness to diverse input types.
The model also lacks an efficient real-time interactive interface, limiting its usability in dynamic design workflows. This is an area where future development could make the model more practical for creative industries that require interactive and immediate style transfer.
Future improvements
Future work will focus on addressing the limitations related to rare or under-represented styles and input quality sensitivity. Specifically, to improve generalization to rare styles, we will implement meta-learning and few-shot learning techniques to allow the model to adapt to new, unseen styles with limited samples. This will enable better handling of styles that are not well-represented in the training data. Additionally, to improve the model’s robustness to input quality variability, we will explore advanced preprocessing techniques such as adaptive denoising and conditional image enhancement models. These methods will dynamically adjust based on input quality, ensuring that the model can handle ambiguous descriptions or low-resolution images more effectively, reducing the performance degradation in such cases. Moreover, we plan to incorporate real-time interactive adjustment capabilities, allowing users to modify style intensity or content-style balance dynamically while receiving instant feedback. User studies and preference scores will be incorporated to validate the model’s performance across diverse user preferences, ensuring that DCLSDM remains adaptable to different real-world applications. These improvements aim to enhance DCLSDM’s versatility, making it suitable for a wider range of real-world scenarios, including dynamic creative workflows and resource-constrained environments like mobile devices.
Conclusion
This study proposes DCLSDM, a multi-style image generation model based on latent diffusion architecture, to address core challenges including insufficient content-style decoupling, high computational costs, and structural distortion in existing methods. By integrating dual-conditional control, lightweight feature extraction, and structural constraint mechanisms, DCLSDM achieves independent control of content and style while maintaining efficiency. Experimental results on public datasets demonstrate that DCLSDM outperforms representative academic and industrial models in generation quality and computational efficiency, making it suitable for resource-constrained scenarios. DCLSDM provides a practical solution for multi-style image generation in content creation and digital art production. Future work will focus on enhancing generalization to rare styles, improving robustness to low-quality inputs, and developing real-time interactive capabilities.
