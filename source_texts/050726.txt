Summary
The QC pack's surface renders, dose-levels sheets, and contour sheets now have a sixth view, from behind, and a volume's renders are sampled at 2 mm, or its own spacing where coarser, however long it is, instead of being coarsened to keep 256 voxels a side. This closes a pre-existing gap in the face preview: a CT, MR, PET, or RT Dose volume more than 510 mm in extent, such as a whole-body CT, was rendered on voxels coarser than 2 mm, about 7 mm for one 1,800 mm long, so a face in it was previewed far more coarsely than in a head scan, and so was a frame's CT guide row on its contour sheet. A volume more than 2,000 mm long, or more than 1,000 mm across, the bounds already set for a contour sheet's frame, is now neither rendered nor projected, so its slices beyond its cine strip are held, failing safe.
This is the third of the changes before the Clean Recognizable Visual Features Option can be enabled. The option stays not enabled. The QC pack's format is unchanged.
ELI5
To check that no face is left in a scan, the review pack builds the patient's skin out of little cubes and shows it from several directions. Until now it showed it from the front, from two angles in between, and from each side, but never from behind, and for a long scan, such as one from head to foot, it used bigger cubes, about 7 mm wide, so the picture stayed small. A face built from cubes that big is blurry, and a face that the scan's labels put at the back could only be seen edge on.
Now every picture also has a view from behind. The preview cubes stay 2 mm across when the scan's own spacing supports that; coarser scans keep their coarser spacing, and long scans give taller pictures. A volume beyond the chosen bounds of 2 m along the patient's head-to-foot axis or 1 m across either other axis has no surface render or projection. Its cine strip is still shown, and slices beyond that strip stay held for review.
Why
#2429's and #2431's descriptions listed this as the next change. #2431 put contour sheets on 2 mm voxels however long the frame, and noted that the CT and dose renders, which qc_surfaces sampled to keep 256 voxels a side, and a frame's CT guide row, which draws on the CT's own grid, were still coarser than 2 mm for a volume more than 510 mm in extent. A reviewer's verdict on a face in a whole-body CT therefore rested on a render about 3.5 times coarser than for a head.
The contour sheets' bound on what they draw also had a stated hole: a mark within two voxels of the edge of its slice is shown only as that edge, which the views drew "from the front and the sides, though not from behind". A view from behind draws an edge that faces the back, and shows a surface that faces the back face on rather than edge on, as a face would be in a volume whose stated orientation puts it there.
What changed
A view from behind (qc_surfaces.py). VIEWS gains 180, from behind, with the patient's left on the viewer's left; its rays look along the voxels as the frontal view's do, so it joins the views that draw rows of further crossings (_ALONG) in surface renders, dose-levels sheets, and contour sheets. Each row is one view wider.
2 mm however long (Surface). A volume is sampled to MINIMUM_VOXEL, 2 mm, or its smallest spacing where that is larger, with no limit on voxels a side (voxel_side's maximum_voxels is now optional, and the defacing finder still passes its own). LONGEST, 2,000 mm, and WIDEST, 1,000 mm, move to qc_surfaces and qc_contours uses them; a volume beyond either is found before any of it is sampled, Surface.large, and has no mask and no image.
Held beyond its cine strip (qc_previews.py). A volume that Surface finds too large has no projection either, and its slices are not decoded for one, so no preview that could show a face draws its slices beyond its cine strip and their pixel data have no SHA-256, as for a series that forms no volume; an RT Dose grid that is too large has no dose levels, so none of its frames is shown.
A few slices at a time. view_depths and view_crossings now render 64 slices at a time, crossings built as 16-bit integers from the start, so that no 64-bit copy of a run's crossings is kept as Hold an ROI whose contour-sheet rows would not fit before drawing them #2433's review noted, and most_crossings counts a few slices at a time; qc_contours uses them in place of its own copies from Draw long ROIs on the QC pack's contour sheets in voxels of 2 mm #2431 and Hold an ROI whose contour-sheet rows would not fit before drawing them #2433. Each slice's rays are stepped alone, so the renders are the same.
Dose levels' hidden rows counted first. hidden_rows takes the room that a level has, as qc_contours._roi_rows does since Hold an ROI whose contour-sheet rows would not fit before drawing them #2433, and gives none, before drawing anything, where a row for each crossing after the first would not fit; Surface.image then holds the grid as too tall, as before.
Masks without a copy, and Otsu in parts. Surface.mask compares the samples directly, NaN comparing below every level as -inf did, instead of copying them; and _otsu counts its histogram a few million values at a time, each value's bin found from it alone, so the counts and the threshold are those of the whole. At 2 mm, a whole-body MR's Otsu threshold peaked at 911 MB from its float64 copy; it now peaks at 355 MB, as a CT's render does.
Docs. The qc_surfaces, qc_contours, and qc_previews docstrings, the PreviewKind.SERIES_SURFACE comment, the D-017 surface-render and contour-sheet bullets, rationale, and tests in the design document, and the register's notes for PS3.15-E.3.2-01, MIDI-BP-15, and MIDI-BP-17 now say six views and from behind, 2 mm however long, the bounds, the hold, and each row about 4,300 pixels wide at most.
Scope and what is deferred
The pre-existing gap, stated: before this change, the face preview of a volume more than 510 mm in extent was coarser than 2 mm, up to about 7.8 mm at 2,000 mm, and nothing previewed any volume from behind. This change fixes both; a volume beyond the new bounds is held instead.
Memory at the bounds. A volume's samples stay float32 for its whole grid, so the largest volume rendered, 1,000 by 1,000 by 2,000 mm, peaks at about 1.4 GB, as the largest contour sheet does. A CT's samples could be kept as booleans, since its level is fixed, which would cut their share, about 1 GB of that, by three quarters; I left it out, since no CT's field of view is 1,000 mm across.
Guides held per series. A frame's guide keeps each series' mask until the contour sheets are drawn, now at 2 mm, about 56 MB for a whole-body CT 500 by 500 by 1,800 mm; packing them is left out.
No change to the QC pack's format, the attestation, or the release report.

Reviewer focus
The hold, and why there. Surface decides from the grid's coordinates alone, before allocating, so an absurd extent costs nothing. Holding the projection too is what makes the hold effective: pictured_frames counts a projection as a preview that could show a face, so without it a volume with no render would still have every slice's pixel data bound by a verdict seen only in a projection. Withholding it leaves the instances in the cine strip pictured, as for any series that forms no volume, and the rest held.
The view from behind and the hidden rows. Its rays are the frontal view's, reversed, so it adds no rows of crossings, only a tile to each, and the layers' hold in _undrawn is unchanged: a voxel with a neighbour outside along its column is already a crossing of a frontal ray.
The bounds. At 2 mm, a view of a volume 1,000 mm across is 711 pixels wide, so a row of six is about 4,300 wide; with a sheet's 32,768 pixels, the crossings found for one region are at most about 32,768 by 711 for each of four views, some 190 MB as 16-bit integers.
Otsu in parts. np.histogram with a fixed range puts each value in its bin from its value alone, so summing parts' counts gives the whole's; the edges are taken from the same range in float64, as before.
