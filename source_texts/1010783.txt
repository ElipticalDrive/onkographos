Currently we use two random number generators: Mersenne Twister for Reference and CPU platforms, and KISS for CUDA and OpenCL. Those are fine generators, but they're no longer state of the art. We should consider moving to something better. I suggest PCG64 DXSM, which is an improved version of the PCG64 algorithm that Numpy uses as its default generator. It has better statistical properties than either of the above two, and is much faster than MT.
Here's a paper describing the theory behind PCG generators: https://www.cs.hmc.edu/tr/hmc-cs-2014-0905.pdf
Here's a reference implementation: https://github.com/imneme/pcg-cpp

I have always used Saru for GPU rng (https://uammd.readthedocs.io/en/latest/RandomNumbers.html), it is described in this article: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2012.12.003
It has a state of just two 32 bit integers and is fast to seed and advance. It also passes every major random test battery.
It is the generator used in HOOMD (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2011.05.021) .

PCG64 isn't very fast on a GPU because it uses a lot of 64-bit integer arithmetic, although it's very fast on CPU and has the best statistical properties. In most cases, random number generation takes negligible time. That's true even on the CPU platform where we use Mersenne Twister, a very slow generator. The main reason for changing would be to get better statistical properties. But even that is more a matter of principle than any expectation of a practical benefit. MT and KISS are not bad methods, even if their statistical properties aren't quite as good as PCG. And when you're only using the generator to add noise to an already chaotic system, even a really bad generator would be unlikely to produce any observable artifacts.
