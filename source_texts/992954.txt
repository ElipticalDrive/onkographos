Hi, thanks for your great work!
I have a question regarding how you convert point clouds into ray distances.
When deploying with the MID360 LiDAR, I implemented a pipeline where:
First, the point cloud is projected into a 2D occupancy grid
Then for each angle, I compute the minimum distance to occupied cells to approximate ray distances
However, I observed an issue:
When the environment contains sparse or cluttered objects, the accumulated point cloud tends to introduce noise, which leads to sudden jumps in the computed ray distances.
I’m wondering:
How do you handle the conversion from point cloud to ray distances in your implementation?
Do you use direct ray casting, probabilistic occupancy, or any filtering/smoothing strategy?
How do you ensure stability of ray distances in the presence of sparse or noisy obstacles?
Any insights or suggestions would be greatly appreciated!
Thanks in advance!

Hi! Here is the general pipeline I used for 3D LiDARs:
Downsample the point cloud first.
Stack multiple frames (e.g., 10+ frames) since a single frame tends to have noise and errors.
Project to 2D using a height threshold. You can simply treat anything below a certain height as ground/flat, and anything above as obstacles.
Calculate 2D rays using a bucketing/binning approach. Within each angle bin, make sure to filter out outliers or take the average to keep the distances stable.
You can check the APPENDIX to see the final result using a Unitree L1 LiDAR.
Honestly, it's been over a year since I worked on this project. The deployment code was left on the robot and unfortunately got lost, so I can only remember these general details right now. Sorry about that, but I hope this gives you a good direction!

About safe_radius:
safe_radius refers to the robot's physical radius, but it doesn't need to be super precise. Since our CBF method is not an exact solution and there are always sensor/perception gaps, a rough estimate is fine.
I usually focus on tuning kappa and damping_factor. Strong suggestion: After training in simulation, adjust these parameters during playback to see how they affect navigation behavior.
About sim-to-real gaps:
Try to measure your ray lengths with 0.1m-level accuracy to ensure reliable obstacle detection.
Also, keep your robot's perception and step frequency stable and high-speed—aim for 50Hz for inference and 200Hz for motor commands.
