This addresses the outstanding points discussed in #269. (Coat darkening, in the implementation, specification and theory, was already addressed in #253.)
Merged into dev_1.1.2 as a single squashed commit (011d9e1). All changes are to index.html.
Thin-walled mode
Subsurface slab structure. The thin-walled subsurface slab is now described explicitly as an infinitesimally thin dense scattering sheet embedded inside a finite-thickness dielectric slab, with f_dielectric on both the upper and lower interfaces.
Diffuse lobes. The diffuse lobes f+, f- are now specified to be Energy-preserving Oren-Nayar lobes (as in the MaterialX graph), flipped into the appropriate hemisphere, with roughness given by base_diffuse_roughness. The existing sentence saying that subsurface_radius/subsurface_radius_scale are ignored in thin-walled mode is retained.
Specular lobe and inter-reflections. A new paragraph states that in both the translucent-base and subsurface cases:
A specular lobe is generated as usual by the embedding dielectric slab, with IOR given by specular_ior (including the specular_weight modulation of Equation [modulated_ior]).
If a coat is present, it modifies the Fresnel factor and apparent roughness of this lobe, as described in the Coat section.
Inter-reflections between the embedding dielectric interfaces physically cause darkening and saturation. In the translucent-base case this is part of the exact thin-sheet solution the spec already describes. In the subsurface case we instead assume this effect is compensated, as in the Glossy-diffuse and Subsurface sections, so that the observed diffuse albedo of the bare slab with vacuum exterior, E_R[f^R_diffuse] + E_T[f^T_diffuse], matches S = subsurface_color. This is closer to what implementations actually do (an albedo-scaled dielectric BRDF plus the diffuse lobes), and avoids a "should" with no formula (see the discussion below).
Subsurface and glossy-diffuse albedos
The definitions of base_color (glossy-diffuse) and subsurface_color (subsurface) in terms of the underlying albedo are now stated to apply to the bare slab with vacuum exterior. This defines the BSDFs and media independently of any coat or other layers above, so the underlying albedos don't change as the coat properties change. The general layered case then follows from that. In the Subsurface section, "directional reflectance" was also corrected to "directional albedo", and a duplicated "the" was removed.

Notes and future work
The thin-wall model is still loosely defined. No single publication works out the necessary BSDFs in full detail, and having two separate "dielectric sheet with embedded stuff" models, with either no scattering or dense scattering, is somewhat ad hoc. A more general thin slab with an arbitrary interior volume (configurable transmittance and albedo) would cover the intermediate regime between thin-walled dielectric and thin-walled subsurface. That would make more sense alongside a unified base volume, which is also future work.
The albedo definitions remain fairly academic, and most implementations likely use an albedo-scaling layering approximation. The text is meant to be physically general while keeping albedo scaling a reasonable approximation.

@najibna, re "Technically, there should also be darkening and saturation due to the inter-reflections between the dielectric interfaces, in both cases":
I wrote that because otherwise someone will ask. We can't just say "don't take the internal bounces into account", because physically they happen. But in practice it's doubtful anyone accounts for them in the subsurface case.
A better approach is to say that the albedo is assumed to be compensated so that the observed diffuse albedo matches subsurface_color, as we already do for base_color in glossy-diffuse and for subsurface_color in the non-thin-walled case. That is a closer match to what implementations actually do (the albedo-scaled dielectric BRDF plus the diffuse lobes, as in the MaterialX graph), and it avoids a "should" with no formula.
For the translucent-base sheet I've kept the physical statement, since the inter-reflection ladder there has an exact solution in the smooth case, which the spec already describes.
Updated in 78ce3c2:
Physically, the inter-reflections between the embedding dielectric interfaces also produce darkening and saturation. In the translucent-base case this is part of the exact thin-sheet solution described above. In the subsurface case, we instead assume (as in the Glossy-diffuse and Subsurface sections) that this effect is compensated, so that the observed diffuse albedo of the bare slab with vacuum exterior, $E_R[f^R_\mathrm{diffuse}] + E_T[f^T_\mathrm{diffuse}]$, matches $S$ = subsurface_color.

Those aren't physical attributes of the dielectric base, though. They're effects of the coat on top. The coat IOR changes the Fresnel factor of the reflection at the coat/base interface (Equation [specular_ior_ratio]), and a rough coat increases the apparent roughness of the base lobes (see the Roughening section). specular_roughness itself isn't modified. Both are physical consequences of the layering, and the Coat section already describes how to account for them. This paragraph only describes the thin-walled base slab itself, so I'd rather not repeat that here.
To make the connection explicit, though, I've added a pointer in 66cba89: "If a coat is present, it also modifies the Fresnel factor and apparent roughness of this specular lobe, as described in the Coat section."
