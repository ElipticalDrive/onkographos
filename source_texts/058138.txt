Next-generation human–machine interfaces (HMIs) are being reshaped by two fast-moving fronts: ultrathin epidermal electronics ("e-tattoos") for continuous on-skin sensing and actuation, and volumetric holography that produces glasses-free, three-dimensional visuals. This paper surveys the state of the art in both domains and examines their convergence toward embodied, closed-loop HMIs. We summarize the materials, mechanics, sensing modalities, power/communication options, and fabrication routes that enable e-tattoos as self-sustaining wearables. We present the core architecture of volumetric holographic displays—covering computer-generated holography, modulation, beam steering/depth control, and illumination. We also propose an integration framework that couples on-skin input/output with spatial 3D feedback, with implications for UI design, safety, and privacy. A comparative table and illustrative figures clarify application opportunities in healthcare, rehabilitation, immersive AR/VR, defense, and cyberphysical systems. We identify cross-cutting challenges— latency and jitter, energy autonomy, occlusion and multiviewer handling, registration, ergonomics, and data governance—and outline research directions including AI-native CGH, tinyML on-tattoo preprocessing, hybrid energy harvesting with thin-film storage, and interoperability metrics. Together, bioelectronic e-tattoos and volumetric holography offer a practical path to natural, responsive, and adaptive HMIs.
