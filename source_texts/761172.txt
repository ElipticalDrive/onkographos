Problem
Currently the renderer fires exactly one ray per pixel, through its center. This produces aliasing artifacts — geometric edges appear as hard staircases ("jaggies") because each pixel makes a binary hit/miss decision with no blending.
What is aliasing
A pixel is a tiny square. The renderer picks one point, the center. If a sphere's edge passes through that pixel but misses the center, the whole pixel becomes background. If it hits the center, the whole pixel becomes the sphere's color. At boundaries between objects this produces a staircase pattern, because the pixel grid and the object's edge never align cleanly. This is aliasing.
What is antialiasing
Instead of making one binary decision per pixel, you make several and average them. If you fire 4 rays through different positions inside the same pixel and 2 hit the sphere while 2 hit the background, the pixel becomes a 50/50 blend — a smooth transition instead of a hard edge. The more samples, the smoother the result.
What is supersampling
Supersampling is the antialiasing technique of casting multiple rays per pixel. The specific variant used here is jittered stratified sampling: the pixel is divided into a √N×√N grid of equal sub-cells, and each ray is fired at a random position inside its assigned cell rather than the cell's center.
