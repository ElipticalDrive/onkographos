Status: future · Plan: plans/1-ortler-worldgen-fixture/ (full design + verifiable steps)
Goals
Using the greater Ortler (Ortles) massif as a real test region:
Adequacy — find out whether crawler's current 14-kind terrain taxonomy is enough
to represent real Alpine terrain somewhat accurately, or whether we need more terrain
types.
Defaults — tune the per-kind default numbers so the classification matches reality.
Sub-goal: verify the result visually with a comparison graphic of the Ortler terrain.
Investigation before any engine change; an engine port is a conditional follow-on.
Design, method, invariants, and the verifiable build steps live in the plan directory.
Extension (2026-07-21) — modelling CLIFF FACES
Not closing this: the first pass answered goals 1–3 (14 kinds suffice at the coarse tier; real-metre transition defaults derived in tuned-defaults.md), but we still have no good way to model cliff faces.
K_FACE exists in the taxonomy; nothing yet produces faces that read as real cliffs at a believable density.
Already settled — do not re-derive:
Slope is useless as a cliff signal at 1.5 km (a hex averages a real face into a ramp) — VERIFIED.
"per-triangle slope → cliff, validated by κ" — FALSIFIED (s6_cliffs.py, +0.000 κ; at ~350 m forest is as steep as rock).
κ cannot referee sub-hex cliffs at all — the loft world is synthetic, so there is no sub-hex ground truth. Gate on invariants, not κ.
The neighbour-coupled talus model SHIPPED under plan Chunked LOD world + loft WebGL viewer (32×32 chunks, base+0.1m detail) #2 S6.2 (src/talus.loft, talustest): bedrock + weathering rubble, repose-driven sliding, stripped bedrock → K_FACE, piled rubble → K_SCREE.
Its cliff-angle threshold is untuned — 50° reads ~80% face on the steepest chunk. That is the visible symptom.
The open question: what makes a face a face? Today it is a bare per-cell angle, which over-produces. Candidates: a coherent vertical step (a shape predicate over connected inter-cell drops) instead of a threshold; whether one K_FACE kind suffices or needs splitting (rock wall / steep rubble / broken crag); and what the ground truth is now that κ is out — an invariant (a face has a top edge, a base, and talus below) or a cold-read recognition test on a rendered PNG.
Method: blueprint phase in Python in this plan's workshop first, then src/talus.loft. Do not tune the 50° constant toward a nicer screenshot.
Split of ownership: plan #2 owns the LOD machinery + src/talus.loft; this plan owns the terrain vocabulary and the adequacy verdict. A kind split or changed face predicate decided here lands as an S6 change there.
Detail: plans/1-ortler-worldgen-fixture/README.md § Open work.
Extension 2 (2026-07-21) — a FLEXIBLE region-fixture system, Severn as region two
PLANNED — not implemented. Design: plans/1-ortler-worldgen-fixture/region-fixtures.md.
One landform family is not enough ground truth: the Ortler is landlocked and has no low-elevation cliffs, so it cannot referee plan #8's F2 (rivers → coastlines) or F3 (hills → cliffs), and "resembles a real range" risks collapsing into "resembles this one seed".
The goal is flexibility, not a second special case. A region becomes a record — {name, anchor_lat, anchor_lon, cols, rows, tile_m, layers} — kept as data, so a third region costs data only and no code. The loft side is already region-parameterized (REGION=, src/regions/<name>.loft, and the Makefile's worked example is literally wales); the Python importer is what is anchored to one region (SUMMIT_LAT/SUMMIT_LON module constants, REGION_DUMPERS = {"ortler": …}) and needs lifting.
Region two — the Severn / Bristol Channel (user direction): the estuary as the basis, upper Cornwall to the south, ordinary sea coast to the west. Three coastal morphologies in one fixture — estuary/ria, cliffed coast, headland-and-bay — plus mature upland (Brecon Beacons / Exmoor / Dartmoor) as the contrast to a young range.
Scale finding: the Ortler is 80×80 @1.5 km ≈ a 120 km square. The full Severn span (Pembrokeshire 5.3 W → Avonmouth 2.6 W, N Cornwall 50.75 N → mid-Wales 52.0 N) is 188 × 139 km = 125 × 93 cells — ~1.6× the cells and non-square. So COLS, ROWS = 80, 80 must become per-region fields. Open: take the full span, or start with the 120 km box (Severn + Gower + Exmoor already covers all three morphologies) and widen later.
New layer + a format decision: coastal regions need natural=coastline + a land/sea mask — as a declared layer, not a fork in the importer. And the Severn has one of the world's largest tidal ranges (~14 m springs) with vast intertidal flats, so the sea datum (LAT / MSL / HAT) is a format-level convention that must be pinned — the Ortler never had to care, and the choice silently biases every coastal comparison.
Refactor gate: the Ortler must keep producing byte-identical output through the generalized path, and I-GEO becomes a per-region round-trip gate.
Window PINNED (2026-07-21): 5.36 W…2.60 W, 50.75 N…51.95 N = 192×134 km = 128 × 89 cells @1.5 km (11,392 cells, 1.8× the Ortler, ~114 DEM calls). Anchor 51.35 N 3.98 W — a geometric centre in the Bristol Channel, not a landmark.
Widened past the Alps-scale 80×80 deliberately: Pembrokeshire (5.36 W) and Avonmouth (2.60 W) are 188 km apart, so a 120 km box had to drop one, and shifting west to catch Pembroke would have lost the inner Severn funnel — the feature that motivated the region. Kept as one region rather than two, because F2 is about the coast being drainage-derived and splitting it would cut the Brecon→estuary→open-sea drainage in half.
ON: St Davids Head, Pembroke, Milford Haven, Tenby, Gower, Swansea, Cardiff, Avonmouth, Exmoor, Ilfracombe, Bude, Brecon Beacons. OFF: Tintagel, Newquay, Dartmoor.
Forces two things in the region record, both wanted: per-region cols/rows (non-square), and an anchor with no semantic meaning.
On the tide (accepted, not a blocker): the Severn's convergent funnel amplifies one of the world's largest tidal ranges, and that range is what builds the flats and marshes — so it is part of why the region is worth using, not just a complication. What the window supplies for F2/F3, in one continuous drainage:
Milford Haven — textbook ria / drowned valley. The single most on-target feature for F2's core claim.
Severn funnel + extreme tidal range → the os_ws taper, convergence amplifying tide.
Intertidal flats + salt marsh (Bridgwater Bay, Gwent Levels) → deposition at high-acc mouths.
Limestone sea cliffs + tidal island (Gower / Worms Head, Stackpole) and very high hogback cliffs (Exmoor, Lynton/Countisbury) and folded-strata cliffs + wave-cut platforms (Bude) → F3 at low elevation, with structural variety.
Headland-and-bay (Pembrokeshire) → the ordinary-coast baseline.
Large dune systems (Kenfig, Merthyr Mawr) → the depositional counterpart.
Brecon Beacons / Exmoor → a mature upland contrasting the young Ortler.
Review path: the user knows this coast first-hand. Since κ is ruled out and the acceptance test is a cold-read recognition on the rendered drawing, a judge who knows the real coastline is the signal a per-cell metric cannot give. Recorded as the intended review path.
Still open: the sea datum (MSL / LAT / HAT) must be pinned as a format convention, with an explicit intertidal mask rather than collapsing the alternating zone to one state — proposed, not decided.
