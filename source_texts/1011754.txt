The webgpu support is still limited and experimental, but intermixed volume rendering is supported and working. I have tested it out and it seems to work well on chrome canary on windows at least. You just set them all up as usual and the WebGPU backend just handles it automatically. They can have different tfuns, etc. Note shading and multicomponent volumes are not supported right now, just haven't gotten to it.
Also I'll probably tweak the intermixing of MIP and composite a bit. Right now it blends MIP/Average/etc on top of composite but I may try to do something more complex in the future. Depth based pixel by pixel mixing of MIP and composite looked horrible (I had it coded that way originally) as the depth of a MIP is a mess. I thought it might look cool, nope, just doesn't make sense. But the MIP on top of composite looks pretty good and works.

That is a slightly different issue, please open a feature request for that. I focused on rendering volumes intermixed correctly (the example given was two volumes). I get where you are coming from now but rendering of lots of volumes is sort of a new issue. A good issue, but a bit different from what I was working on. Much like rendering out of core extremely large volumes is a slightly different feature. For your purposes you need them sorted correctly but probably do not require intermixing at all. Note that sorting volumes correctly has no guaranteed solution (even when they do not overlap) unless you constrain the problem (such as saying they form a spatial hash etc). See https://gitlab.kitware.com/vtk/vtk/-/blob/master/Rendering/Volume/vtkBlockSortHelper.h for the heuristic we use on the C++ side. It is a surprisingly nasty problem that seems like it should be simple but really isn't. But I think I can extend the webgpu side to handle it (maybe with a simplified sort heuristic though)
