Abstract
In this paper, we present a method that allows the conditioning of Neural Fields using Regularization by Denoising
(RED). As opposed to learning a joint convolutional neural
network to condition the output of a neural field, the RED
framework is memory-efficient. It allows us to decouple the
conditioning network and neural field optimization entirely.
We focus our work on applications for 3D sparse-view Xray Computed Tomography (CT) and propose a flexible procedure that does not assume coordinate-friendly partitioning of the forward operator. Indeed, our method applies
to any CT geometry, particularly Cone-Beam CT, which is
the most common setup in industrial inspection. We quantitatively evaluate our approach and show that our method
is either better or on par with the state-of-the-art regarding reconstruction quality while being the most memoryefficient. Code is available at https://github.com/
romainvo/nef-red.
1. Introduction
X-ray computed tomography (CT) is a non-destructive evaluation tool used to inspect and fully visualize the inner
structure of an object. It is widely used in medical imaging,
industrial inspection, and material science for the quality
and high resolution of the 3D image it provides. The reconstruction of CT images requires the measurements of a set
of X-ray radiographies called projections. It involves exposure to ionizing radiation, which may harm patients; hence,
Low-Dose Computed Tomography (LDCT) has become the
de facto approach in bio-medical imaging. For industrial inspection, CT fails to be adopted as a standard technique for
online monitoring due to its excessive cost in terms of acquisition time. Therefore, Sparse-View Computed Tomog* This work was supported by the FOCUS-EJN grant from CEA and

Recently, there has been a growing interest in pre-trained
networks used as learned regularizers. These networks allow the decoupling of the forward model evaluation and the
neural network training and, therefore, need less memory
than Deep Unrolling approaches [23, 41? ? ]. Methods
based on learned regularizers are quite varied in the strategy
they adopt. Several works are using Generative Adversarial
Networks (GAN) [24, 29] or probabilistic models [1, 47] to
learn a regularizer. In this article, we focus on a third class
of learned regularizers that directly use CNN pre-trained for
the underlying task [16, 22] to build a regularization function. These last methods heavily rely on recent Plug-andPlay priors (PNP) [? ] and Regularization by Denoising
(RED) [31] methods, which allow the integration of many
denoising algorithms as prior models into state-of-the-art iterative algorithms.
Besides the previously mentioned work, there has been
considerable interest in NeRF [25] and Neural Field techniques [2, 26] to deal with ill-posed inverse problems in
computer graphics. Successful works have used neural
field techniques to solve sparse-view CT reconstruction
[45], showing their superiority over the classical pixel basis [12, 33, 37, 49]. A few approaches also combine neural
fields with modern regularization techniques to improve the
quality of reconstruction. In IntraTomo [48], Zang et al.
learn an X-ray attenuation field from a sparse set of observations and use the learned field to generate new pixel-basis
projections. These synthetic projections are then used to
promote an augmented data fidelity term in a regularized
forward-backward optimization scheme [7, 9, 15]. With
CoIL, [40] Sun et al. propose a dual approach to IntraTomo
and optimize a coordinate-based Multi-Layer Perceptron to
learn the measurement field and generate new projections to
the same end.
In this paper, we address the challenge of reconstructing high-resolution 3D CT volumes from a sparse set of
views acquired via a cone-beam geometry setup (Fig. 2).
We depart from the traditional works on regularized CT,
which leverage the axial separability of fan-beam geometries (medical setups) to solve independent 2D problems.
Different from biomedical settings, we stress the importance of the cone-beam geometry in industrial setups [8,
14], which is faster than a parallel beam in terms of sampling thanks to the use of a 2D imaging detector instead of
a 1D one.
In summary, the main contributions of this paper are as
follows:
• We present a versatile supervised training framework for
neural field-based CT reconstruction. Unlike previously
mentioned approaches [40, 48] we directly sample the solution to our inverse problem from a regularized neural
field. By directly regularizing the neural field optimization, we avoid the overhead of solving two optimization
problems: the first one to generate synthetic observations
and the second one when switching to a pixel-basis for
the actual reconstruction.
• Inspired by coordinate descent approaches [39, 43] we
introduce a new procedure that fully decouples the
coordinate-descent estimations of the fidelity term and
the regularization term. It allows us to efficiently train
a cone-beam-based neural field and scales well with our
high-resolution problem.
• We evaluate our approach and the baselines for sparseview CT on two datasets: Walnut-CBCT [13] the only
non-simulated Cone-Beam CT collection available; and
our private dataset of natural cork stoppers, which contains 40 FDK reconstructions of size 10243 and projections data of size 720 × 10242 . We show the superiority of our method in terms of reconstruction quality and
memory complexity.
To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to tackle 3D
learned regularized Cone-Beam CT with this level of resolution.
This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 gives
background details on the reconstruction task and the two
classes of methods we propose to combine. In Section 3
we present the methodology of our work, the advantages of
the proposed framework, and the difficulties we overcome
to effectively use it. We present our results in Section 4,
demonstrating the performances of our approach on highresolution data. After a discussion in Section 5, we conclude in Section 6.

2.2. Neural fields
Neural fields are a recent class of deep learning models that
have seen widespread success in 3D shape modeling, novel
view synthesis, or surface reconstruction [25, 45]. Similar
to CT, these applications also require solving an ill-posed
inverse problem and can naturally be adapted to the context
of CT reconstruction.
The main idea behind neural fields is to represent the
solution f as a field, i.e. a quantity which is a function of the
spatial coordinate, and to parametrize this field by a neural
network FΦ with learned weights Φ. In 3D CT we consider
the attenuation field which maps a 3D coordinate x ∈ X to
a scalar value:
FΦ : X → R,
(6)
with X the set of positions x ∈ R3 within the projection
area.
Although NeRFs have achieved outstanding results on
novel view synthesis [25], the vanilla approach only minimizes a rendering loss objective, i.e. a data-fidelity term,
and does not include a priori information on the solution.
This difficulty can be overcome by leveraging local conditioning [45]. Using an encoder-like structure, latent features generated by a CNN encoder can be concatenated
to the input coordinates to enrich the inputs of the neural field [46]. The other way around is to use a decoderlike structure, where the neural field is used to generate latent features thereafter decoded by a CNN to output shape
and appearance [27, 35]. Finally, combining both using an
encoder-decoder-like structure produces state-of-the-art results in 3D generative settings [10, 17]. Nonetheless, these
approaches largely complexify the overall system and necessitate the CNN to be trained jointly with the neural field
in order to learn an implicit representation.

3. Methodology
3.1. Neural field regularization
In this work, we propose to use the RED framework to
efficiently optimize a neural field for reconstructing highresolution CT volumes from a sparse set of views (Fig. 1).
As opposed to radiance fields, in CT, we can leverage
the vast literature on regularization and prior formulation
to propose a more straightforward approach with minimal
overhead in terms of training complexity. The objective is
to rewrite the optimization problem Eq. (2) with our neural
field. We consider Xgrid ⊂ X the finite set of voxel coordinates in the reconstruction grid. Therefore, our problem

4.2. Convergence behaviour
We study the convergence behavior of our approach on the
task of 3D Cone-Beam CT. Because we work with such geometry, which does not admit a coordinate-friendly partitioning, we resort to a disjoint randomized coordinate estimation of the data-fidelity and RED updates. As a consequence, we deviate from the conditions stated in Block Coordinate RED [39]. Moreover, the problem we are trying to
solve is non-linear w.r.t Φ, and thus, we lose the theoretical
convergence properties ensured by RED [30, 31] and BCRED. Nonetheless, our results on the Cork-CBCT dataset
still show empirical convergence to a fixed point and a net
improvement compared to the non-regularized neural field
baseline (Fig. 5).

increased at the expense of the other. This phenomenon has

6. Conclusion
To conclude, we present a versatile framework that allows
the conditioning of Neural Fields using Regularization by
Denoising. We propose a flexible randomized coordinate
estimation procedure for the gradient step and the regularization step, which does not assume coordinate-friendly
partitioning of the forward operator and is, therefore, generalizable for any acquisition geometry. Despite the lack
of theoretical proof due to the nonlinearity of the neural
field, we observe good empirical convergence of the regularized approach. Furthermore, our method presents the advantage of being memory-efficient and proposes the lowest
memory complexity among regularized approaches while
being highly competitive or better on the evaluated benchmarks. We present direct applications for the reconstruction
of sparse-view CT, but naturally, our method directly applies to a wide range of imaging inverse problems such as
MRI reconstruction or more general problems like SuperResolution and Deblurring.
