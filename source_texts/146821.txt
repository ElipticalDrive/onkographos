Abstract
Geometrically complex virtual reality (VR) interiors can produce unstable frame times, while level-of-detail (LOD) transitions may disrupt visual experience. This study aimed to improve rendering stability and reduce transition visibility through an adaptive LOD–progressive-mesh framework that combines mesh simplification, runtime triangle-count control, and geometric morphing. Four rendering conditions were compared at three scene-load levels using 30 repeated 90-s navigation replays per condition–load combination. User experiments assessed transition detection and perceived experience. Under high load, the framework reduced the run-level 95th-percentile GPU frame time from 18.11 to 10.86 ms and the proportion of frames exceeding the 11.11-ms budget from 84.51 to 7.01% relative to full-resolution rendering. Compared with triangle-matched fixed-QEM rendering, these measures decreased by 11.2% and 19.19 percentage points, respectively. Geometric morphing reduced transition detection from 66.6 to 28.4%. Among 100 interior-design students, the adaptive condition received higher ratings for perceived fluency, interaction comfort, and design-support utility after correction for multiple comparisons; differences in visual fidelity and continuance intention did not meet the corrected significance criterion. The findings show that coordinating established rendering techniques can improve frame-time stability and reduce transition visibility in the evaluated VR interior, offering a reproducible approach to rendering and evaluating complex scenes.
