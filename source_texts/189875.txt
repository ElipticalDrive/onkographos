Shrinkwrap
[edit]
For a two dimensional phase retrieval problem, there is a degeneracy of solutions as

This leads to "image twinning" in which the phase retrieval algorithm stagnates producing an image with features of both the object and its conjugate.[3] The shrinkwrap technique periodically updates the estimate of the support by low-pass filtering the current estimate of the object amplitude (by convolution with a Gaussian) and applying a threshold, leading to a reduction in the image ambiguity.[4]
Comparison with digital holography
[edit]
Experimental comparisons of iterative phase retrieval and digital holography methods have been performed.[5] In a 2014 study by Shevkunov et al., two iterative methods, namely the Fresnel-based iterative method (FRIM)[6] and the single-beam multiple-intensity reconstruction (SBMIR),[7][8][9] were evaluated alongside two holographic techniques: complex wave retrieval by digital holography (CWR-DH)[10][11][12] and the Fourier transform method (FT-DH).[13][14] The results demonstrated that FRIM achieved the lowest normalized mean-square error but introduced diffraction artifacts at the reconstructed image boundaries, whereas CWR-DH delivered the highest contrast without such artifacts. FT-DH produced a darker image due to information loss caused by filtering of the zero-order and twin image in the Fourier plane.

Applications
[edit]
Phase retrieval is a key component of coherent diffraction imaging (CDI). In CDI, the intensity of the diffraction pattern scattered from a target is measured. The phase of the diffraction pattern is then obtained using phase retrieval algorithms and an image of the target is constructed. In this way, phase retrieval allows for the conversion of a diffraction pattern into an image without an optical lens.
Using phase retrieval algorithms, it is possible to characterize complex optical systems and their aberrations.[16] For example, phase retrieval was used to diagnose and repair the flawed optics of the Hubble Space Telescope.[17][18]
Other applications of phase retrieval include X-ray crystallography[19] and transmission electron microscopy.
