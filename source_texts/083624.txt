Summary:
While ClickHouse currently provides excellent specialized codecs for numerical data (like Gorilla, Delta, and DoubleDelta), these often face limitations in performance or compression density when handling high-density, multi-dimensional floating-point arrays or complex structured numerical snapshots. I propose adding Blosc2, ZFP, and ZipNN as native compression codecs to improve I/O throughput and storage efficiency for numerical workloads.
Problem Statement:
In analytical scenarios involving massive arrays (e.g., scientific datasets, large-scale feature stores, or dense numerical snapshots), the current codec suite often hits a trade-off ceiling:
Throughput Bottlenecks: For large-scale scans of Float32/64 columns, I/O bandwidth remains a primary bottleneck. Traditional LZ-based codecs (LZ4/ZSTD) are CPU-intensive, and specialized numerical codecs sometimes lack the throughput required for real-time analytical performance.
Structural Limitations: Current Delta-based encodings perform well on monotonic data but lose efficiency on non-sequential or high-entropy numerical blocks where Blosc2 (BitShuffle) or ZFP would excel.
Proposed Solution:
Integrating the following as native CompressionCodec implementations:
Blosc2 (with BitShuffle): Specifically excels at restructuring numerical data by shuffling bits. My benchmarks confirm significant gains in decompression MB/s for float arrays, drastically reducing query latency.
ZFP: A lossy/lossless codec designed specifically for floating-point data, offering superior compression ratios for high-precision scientific arrays.
ZipNN: A high-performance algorithm optimized for structural density in numerical snapshots, providing a balanced performance profile that complements existing codecs.
Performance Benchmarks:
Testing on various datasets (ranging from 100 to 7M elements) shows that these codecs consistently outperform standard defaults in specific high-density scenarios. Notably, Blosc2 shows superior decompression throughput, while ZFP/ZipNN provide higher storage efficiency for structured numerical blocks.
Detailed benchmark results: [Link to your Gist/File]
Why this fits the ClickHouse Roadmap:
Performance: ClickHouse is built for "I/O bound" performance. These codecs enable faster data scanning by reducing the data volume passed through the I/O layer without adding significant CPU overhead.
Specialization: ClickHouse values specialized compression (like Gorilla). Adding Blosc2/ZFP/ZipNN is a natural evolution, extending this "specialized storage" philosophy to broader numerical array types.
Compatibility:
I am familiar with the ClickHouse codec architecture and believe these could be efficiently wrapped, similar to existing compressors. I am happy to assist in defining the performance test cases or providing the wrapper implementation logic.
