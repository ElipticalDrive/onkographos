Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) has been a milestone in 3D reconstruction. However, researchers seldom explore the influence of positional encoding, which is counted in the NeRF. Thus, we explore three novel encoding schemes as devised - sin-priority, grouped sin-cos, and hyperbolic against the original NeRF approach. Evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets reveal significant performance variations: sin-priority enhancing material details but impairing geometric accuracy. Grouped encoding excels in regular structures yet fails catastrophically in complex scenes. Hyperbolic encoding collapses due to non-orthogonality. Phase continuity emerges as the decisive factor for scene reconstruction.
