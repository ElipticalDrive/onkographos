Abstract
FastGS accelerates 3D Gaussian splatting through adaptive density control, but leaves the final primitive count unspecified. We introduce HAB-FastGS (Hard-Budget Fast Gaussian Splatting), a budget layer that enforces an attainable target count while retaining the FastGS reconstruction objective and rasterizer. The layer combines capped pruning during densification with an exact cut before training ends. A population floor prevents native over-pruning, and subsequent optimization adapts the surviving primitives at fixed count. Across thirteen scenes from Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples and Deep Blending, the method reaches every prescribed target. At seed 0, this benchmark includes development scenes. A nominal 18% count reduction yields dataset-mean PSNR decreases of 0.016 to 0.273 dB. Matched-count comparisons on two FastGS development scenes show a larger PSNR effect from delaying the cut to the endpoint than from switching between opacity and joint priority. The timing effect is smaller and changes sign across scenes on vanilla 3DGS. Independent confirmation on four held-out scenes with three seeds meets two of five preregistered criteria (PSNR and SSIM). LPIPS, training wall time and rendering speed do not meet their criteria. Training time decreases by 6.08%, below the required 10% reduction. Rendering speed increases by 5.76%, but its 95% confidence interval [4.15%, 7.63%] crosses the 5% threshold. The LPIPS increase has a 95% upper confidence bound of 0.01168, exceeding the 0.01 margin. These results establish exact count control within FastGS and quantify its quality costs, resource gains and scheduling sensitivity. The source code will be made publicly available upon acceptance.
