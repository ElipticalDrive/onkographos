This change sets the sorting precision of Radix and Aholo to 16 bits, bringing them closer to PlayCanvas WebGL’s configuration for more comparable large-scene benchmarks. Sorting precision can have a noticeable impact on performance in large scenes: finer sorting may make subsequent memory accesses less coherent, potentially reducing L2 cache hit rates and increasing bandwidth costs.
Aholo’s data packing precision and sorting precision are configured independently. Its high-precision path was also designed with raw splat support in mind. Compared with the compressed formats commonly used by other renderers, it allows for higher precision but requires more data reads and sampling, making the overhead more noticeable in large scenes. For most typical large scenes, standard-precision packing with cameraRelativeEnabled is usually sufficient.
This change also uses streaming uploads with retainData: false and enables autoFreeResourceOnGpuPacked. This reduces CPU-side data retention and releases GPU source resources once packing is complete, lowering RAM and VRAM usage for large models.
Aholo previously removed clipXY because scenes containing large Gaussians could exhibit artifacts along the edges of the image. Its current approach is therefore closer to PlayCanvas’s.
Performance differences during camera movement also depend on how frequently sorting and SH precomputation are updated. These largely reflect each renderer’s design trade-offs between update responsiveness and performance, so direct comparisons often have limited value.
Local test results for both the 30M and 60M models are also included.
