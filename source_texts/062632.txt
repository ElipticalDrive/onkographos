Streaming 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes for interactive six-degree-of-freedom (6DoF) viewing raises a scheduling question: given a viewport and a bit budget, which tiles and which Gaussians should be transmitted and processed first? Prior work answers at one granularity only, ranking either tiles by view-dependent properties or Gaussians by per-primitive attributes. We present VCUTS, a two-level, view-conditioned rate-utility model for tiled 3DGS streaming. The model assigns a utility to each tile and a weight to each Gaussian, and a greedy scheduler transmits tiles in utility-per-byte order while packing each tile’s Gaussians by descending weight, enabling finer-grained progressive delivery. We realize the tile utility as an interpretable heuristic and as a learned per-scene model, and compare both against a proxy oracle. We implement this rate-utility model together with a greedy prioritization algorithm and a multi-track scheduling algorithm in a full testbed. We show that: (i) the view-conditioned per-Gaussian weight raises PSNR by up to 9.5 dB over a view-independent baseline, (ii) the learned tile utility increases PSNR by up to 7.5 dB over a view-conditioning-only baseline, at the cost of per-scene training, and (iii) under a streaming trace simulation, the learned tile utility boosts VMAF by up to 10.6 and PSNR by up to 12.8 dB over the tile-based baseline.
