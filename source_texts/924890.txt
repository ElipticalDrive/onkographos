A new Ice workspace shows the backdrop straight on (the reflection window
laid flat across the frame) with up to five blocks of ice hung in front of
it. Each pixel is a ray: it bends into a block, crosses it (mirrored back in
past the critical angle), bends out and lands on the backdrop, whose regions
are then contoured through that landing exactly as the water contours them
through its ripples, so a painted edge seen through ice stays one vector
outline.
ice.js: the optics. A rounded-box SDF (convex, so a hit is a golden-section
minimum plus bisections, and that minimum is the signed silhouette), a
sum-of-waves bump map for melt dimples, Snell refraction, total internal
reflection, Fresnel sheen, glints and a cloudy core.
buildIceView: one clipped layer per block, furthest first. The first
internal-reflection tear is traced from both sides and cut along the Snell
discriminant; later tears get a masked blur of the landings. Dispersion
traces three channels composited with screen. Speckle cleanup drops flecks
below an area in raster pixels.
Per block: position, size, depth, turn/tip/lean, worn edges, melt dimples
and their size, cloudy core. Scene: refractive index, dispersion, backdrop
distance, tint and its colour, shine, speckle cleanup. All in the URL.
Built on the render worker like the 3D pass; SVG/PNG export retrace it at
the export raster. MP4 and paper stack are disabled in this view.
The sky view's backdrop sampling is factored into flatTaps/tapRegions and
shared with the ice view (same arithmetic).
