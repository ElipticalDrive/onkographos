Rays sampled to the wrong side of the face are sampled again against the geometry normal on transmitting surfaces,
or mirrored on opaque ones (84504d7's design). Either way no lobe's density reaches the new direction, so light
sampling covers none of it. Until now that vertex still recorded a light sample at density 0, so emission met after
such a ray took power-heuristic weight 0 and its energy was lost. Escapes from the same rays already counted in full
(escape_pdf = 0). This makes emission do the same: one commit, +25/−8 on main e08ecbe. It merges together with flex-cuda-pt's #24,
the same v2 rule there.
Change
WebGPU path tracer (lightTrace): only a correct-side, non-delta sample records a competing light sample
(nee && !delta && expected == aligned). A wrong-side sample leaves the vertex without one, as delta lobes do, so the
emission it meets counts in full.
ReSTIR walk: the same condition, nee && !delta && !wrong_side.
WebGL2 path tracer: its next event estimation is all-or-nothing (direct_light_emission). A wrong-side ray now
sets it, so the emission it meets counts. (Spotted by flexlight-mis.)
FlexLight has no light leak below opaque faces (unlike flex-cuda-pt's smoother filtering #24): its shadow rays start on the shading
normal's front, and lightBSDF gates light directions by the geometry normal. So the change is the weighting alone.
Regression test
test/furnace.mjs gains lit: the faceted smooth-normal prism in lossless white diffuse (ior 1, no specular), inside
a closed box whose faces emit white inward, with no ambient light. Its exact answer is one everywhere. npm test
appends lit pt and lit restir to main's six scenes. Lit ReSTIR alone catches the walk's change (found by
flexlight-mis).
Test changes beside the new scene, all on main's version of the file (e08ecbe):
Two-sided mean bound for the scenes main bounds by mean > 0.98 (glass and mirror): now |mean − 1| < 0.02.
Their exact answer is one, so a gain is as wrong as a loss. Every existing scene still passes it (table below). Metal
and dielectric keep main's four-standard-error bounds around their derived albedos.
Lit is exempt from brightest < 1.05. In the ambient scenes every sample's weight is exactly one, so no pixel can
exceed one. In the lit box, light sampling and BSDF sampling are combined by MIS weights, so a single pixel's 64-frame
mean scatters around one (up to 1.12 here). That is noise around the right answer, not a gain, and the mean bound
still catches any gain.
Lit ReSTIR is exempt from the black-pixel bound. ReSTIR's reuse lets a single pixel's mean fall below a half (one
of 4096 on Intel) while the image's mean is 1.0006. v1, which loses the energy, still fails on the mean (0.9686), so
the exemption does not admit it.
