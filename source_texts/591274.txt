Review of #195 at dcbc7c8: M69 change 2, static octahedral impostors as a terminal LOD. You asked me to find the root cause of the far-view regression; it is identified and measured below.
Root cause: the impostor fragment shader does a brute-force per-pixel ray march. For each of three frames it takes up to 33 uniform bracket samples plus 8 bisection steps and a final sample, and each sample does two atlas fetches and a five-iteration sway inversion. It runs in full in shadow, depth prepass and forward, because the depth comes from the march (gl_FragDepth, quad at a constant depth), so nothing is rejected early.
Rasterization is not the cost: a variant that discards right after the ray–sphere test costs 0.068 ms against 4.95 ms. Rasterization and setup equal the coarse mesh; essentially everything is reconstruction.
The cost scales with the march: it falls to 1.55 ms with 8 brackets, 1.57 ms with 1 frame, and 0.30 ms with one bracket and no refinement. Removing the sway inversion gives 3.62 ms.
Design divergence: the proposal specified one projected sample per frame. The march replaced it, and its step count was raised to 32 to pass the shadow gate. That compensates for undersampling in a blind uniform search.
Details and the full table are inline on impostor_surface.glsl:12.
Against the proposal (apart from the reconstruction):
the neutral Impostor type sits in core, and bake_impostor is synchronous, isolated in a private capture scene, and cleaned up with defer;
the two-region RGBA8 atlas has gutters;
set_lod_impostor validates and sets a descriptor revision;
a dead atlas falls back to mesh;
every pass has a dedicated variant;
history, revision and frame-triplet rejection are handled;
the tests and acceptance report are present and candid about the regression.
Runs (review worktree, Windows host: i9-14900K, RTX 4090, driver 610.88)
python scripts/build.py --test: 1,523 passed, 0 failed, 31 targets. There was no generated-file drift.
c3c test acceptance --path test/gpu/render: 177 passed.
lod --benchmark, three runs:
far impostor 4.63–4.99 ms against coarse mesh 0.070 and base 0.396–0.402 ms;
near and middle match the report.
Experiments: six variants, each rebuilt with --regen from the committed tree and restored afterwards (worktree clean). The table is inline.
Verdict: HOLD. A terminal LOD that makes the far view about 70× more expensive than the level it replaces defeats the milestone's purpose. The cause is the reconstruction algorithm, not the atlas or the integration.
Replace the march with the proposal's per-frame projection plus a bounded refinement (or a search bounded by bake-time per-cell depth ranges).
Hoist the sway inversion out of the per-sample loop.
Re-run the shader and shadow gates.
Add a benchmark row where the impostor replaces expensive geometry.
I re-check the new head with the gates, the benchmark and CI green.

Root cause of the far-view regression: this per-fragment ray march. Fix before merge.
For each of the three frames, impostor_trace takes up to 33 uniform bracket samples along the whole sphere chord, then 8 bisection steps and a final sample. That is up to 126 sample evaluations per covered fragment, each costing:
two dependent atlas fetches;
one impostor_local, which with sway is six matrix-vector products plus five sway_offset evaluations.
A ray through empty space inside the bounding sphere, common for a tree, always pays all 33 brackets in all three frames.

Quads are cheap; the march is the cost. Rasterizing the 5,000 quads and setting up the ray costs the same as the 12-triangle mesh. Essentially all of the 4.9 ms is reconstruction.
It scales with the work. The cost is linear in bracket count and frame count.
Isolated: near and middle rows were unchanged in every variant, since no impostor is selected there.
The 32 brackets compensate for undersampling. A uniform step of 2r/32 is needed only because the search starts blind at the sphere entry; with 8 steps (r/4) it jumps over thin silhouette features seen from the light, which is the shadow gate's failure.
Please replace the search rather than tune its step count.
Proposal design: the proposal specified one projected sample per frame, with a reconstructed point per frame. Take the frame's stored depth at the projected point as the starting estimate, then do a couple of secant/refinement steps against that frame's depth sheet. The 2-sample variant bounds this at about 0.30 ms.
If a search must remain: bound it with bake-time per-cell data, such as each cell's covered depth range or coverage rectangle. Then the march starts and ends at that frame's surface interval, and rays that miss a frame's covered region exit immediately instead of taking 33 steps.
Re-run the gate on the new reconstruction. Don't relax the gate.

Re-review of #195 at 4bb7bcb: fixes for the reconstruction cost found at dcbc7c8.
The root cause is fixed.
Reconstruction:
The 33-step march with 8-step bisection over the full sphere chord is gone.
Each frame now seeds at the front-plane estimate and takes two finite-difference depth-gradient corrections (a few samples each), with one centre-plane retry only when the coverage/depth check fails.
The worst case is about ten sample pairs per frame instead of about 42, and empty space is no longer walked.
Sway: the inverse is solved once at the ray's reference point. The local ray direction is corrected by the bend derivative there, so no atlas probe calls the solver; the inner loop is a scalar update with hoisted products.
Bake: nearest-surface dilation inside each cell (a breadth-first fill, with scratch allocated once per bake) gives transparent texels usable depth and attributes for the gradient, without changing coverage or crossing cells.
Conservative depth: measured without a gain and omitted, with the numbers documented. That is fine.
New comparison: the 9,600-triangle high_detail case shows the intended use.
Geometry gates (unchanged, per your report and the 178-test run):
cube coverage mismatch 0 across 48 cases;
depth error 0.021795 against 0.078961;
transformed offscreen shadow mismatch 0.111% against 3%.
