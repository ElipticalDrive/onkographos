Jason: "Let's rethink clouds and sky, actually. Can we do the whole thing as a shader? There's some really cool stuff up on shadertoy for that."
How it works (web/js/skyshader.js)
Opaque pass: the opaque world is drawn into a render target, colour plus depth.
Sky pass: a full-screen shader reconstructs each pixel's ray and draws the sky. It ray-marches clouds up to whatever the world put in the way, decoding the log depth buffer. Where a cloud is more than half opaque, it writes the cloud's depth.
Transparent pass: the sea, highways, sparkles and fireflies draw on top, depth-tested against the clouds. A highway behind a cloud is hidden; one in front isn't.
What's in the sky
Cumulus from 1.5 km, ~3 km across: billowy noise eroded by the weather's coverage, rounded on top. The noise is a tileable 32³ inverted-Worley plus value-noise texture, made on the phone from a fixed seed in ~40 ms.
Storms: the strongest tropical cells tower to ~10 km as ~10 km-wide storms, with rain shafts falling in streaks beneath.
Cirrus streaks on a shell 9 km up.
Lighting: Beer-Lambert extinction, a two-step light march, two-lobe Henyey-Greenstein scattering, a powder term, a soft shoulder, and skylight in the dream's lavender and pink.
Geometry: all of it sits on the curved Earth. True altitude is the ray's height plus d²/2R.
Marching: steps grow geometrically, the fine octave fades with distance, and a 4×4 Bayer dither hides banding. Clouds melt into the haze a little before the ground does.
Sky: the same banded palette, plus a banded sun halo, the stylized sun, and hashed stars.
The weather (web/js/weather.js)
Model: a tileable 256² field riding the existing prevailing winds (8 m/s), thresholded by each latitude's cloudiness, with storms where it peaks.
Shared: it depends only on place and unix time, so everyone shares one sky.
Mirrored: the same model lives in JS for tests.
Licensing
This was written from the published techniques, not copied from Shadertoy. The Shadertoy terms page refused an automated read. Secondary sources consistently say shaders with no stated licence default to CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 (not verified first-hand), which doesn't fit this MIT repo.
Replaces
sky.js and clouds.js (puff cumulus and deck). cloudfield.js keeps the winds and cloud belts.
Checked
npm test: 38 pass. The new weather tests cover tropical storms, clear subtropical highs, the same sky for everyone, and riding the wind; the noise is seeded and tileable.
npm run check passes.
