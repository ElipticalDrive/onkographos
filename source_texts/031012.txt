VoxelBeam: A Modular Dual-Plane Laser Projection
System for Real-Time Volumetric Displays
Dane Ulysse
June 25, 2025
Abstract
This paper introduces VoxelBeam, a novel volumetric display system utilizing
dual-plane laser projection to generate real-time three-dimensional images within
a sealed glass enclosure. By precisely intersecting ultra-thin laser beams from platforms positioned above and below the display volume, the system creates visible
light voxels through controlled ionization of air particles. An interactive wearable
finger controller enables intuitive manipulation of holographic objects, advancing
human-computer interaction towards a Tony Stark-style experience. Safety considerations and potential applications are also discussed.
1
Introduction
Volumetric displays offer the unique capability to project three-dimensional images visible
from multiple angles without requiring special glasses or headsets. While recent advances
have produced promising prototypes, many volumetric systems suffer from limited resolution, complex hardware, and safety concerns regarding high-intensity light sources.
The VoxelBeam system presents a scalable and modular approach that leverages dualplane laser projection to create precise light voxels inside a protective glass enclosure.
Coupled with a wearable electromagnetic finger controller, this system enables dynamic,
real-time manipulation of holographic images, bridging the gap between sci-fi interfaces
and practical implementations.
2
Theoretical Background
2.1
Volumetric Display Principles
Volumetric displays generate images by producing discrete light points, or voxels, in threedimensional space. In the VoxelBeam system, these voxels are created by intersecting
two coherent, ultra-thin laser beams within a controlled volume of ionized air. The
ionization excites air particles, causing them to emit visible light at the intersection
points. This technique relies on principles from plasma physics and laser optics to ensure
voxel brightness and stability.
1
2.2
Laser Beam Geometry
Critical to the formation of clear voxels is the control over laser beam thickness, coherence
length, and precise steering. The thinner the beam waist, the smaller and sharper the
voxel produced. Synchronization of beam firing from top and bottom platforms ensures
stable voxel creation at precise spatial coordinates. Steering mirrors, such as galvanometers or MEMS, facilitate rapid beam targeting, enabling real-time image rendering.
3
System Design
3.1
Dual-Plane Laser Projection
The system consists of two platforms positioned at the top and bottom of the display
volume, each equipped with arrays of ultra-thin lasers capable of firing coherent beams.
These beams are carefully synchronized and steered to intersect at programmed coordinates within the airspace, generating voxels through controlled ionization. The modular
design allows scaling the number of lasers and adjusting platform dimensions based on
desired resolution and display size.
3.2
Protective Glass Enclosure
Safety is paramount due to the use of high-intensity lasers capable of ionizing air. The
entire display volume is enclosed within tempered, optical-grade safety glass with antireflective and UV/IR blocking coatings. This enclosure protects users from direct and
scattered laser exposure while maintaining optical clarity to view the holograms. The
sealed design also permits controlled atmospheric conditions, such as the introduction of
inert gases to enhance voxel brightness.
3.3
Wearable Finger Controller
To facilitate intuitive interaction with the holograms, the system incorporates a wearable finger device embedded with electromagnetic emitters and sensors. This device
emits signals tracked by the system to steer laser beams dynamically, allowing users to
“grab,” move, and reshape holographic images in real-time. Gesture recognition algorithms translate finger movements into hologram manipulations, offering a natural and
immersive interface.
4
Implementation Considerations
Key challenges include precise alignment and calibration of laser beams to ensure voxel
accuracy, managing thermal loads generated by continuous laser operation, and maintaining system safety protocols. Advanced beam steering hardware with feedback loops is
essential for stability. Additionally, power supply and cooling systems must be optimized
for prolonged use. Software integration for real-time rendering and gesture recognition
requires high-speed processing and low-latency communication between components.
2
5
Applications and Future Work
The VoxelBeam system has broad applications across entertainment, education, medical
imaging, and augmented reality. It offers new ways to visualize complex data and interact
with digital content in three dimensions. Future developments include multi-color voxel
generation using RGB lasers, incorporation of AI-driven hologram behaviors via assistants
like NEXUS, and miniaturization for portable holographic devices.
6
Conclusion
VoxelBeam represents a significant advancement in volumetric display technology by combining dual-plane laser projection with a protective enclosure and an innovative wearable
controller. This system provides a pathway toward practical, safe, and interactive holographic displays, bringing science fiction interfaces closer to reality.
