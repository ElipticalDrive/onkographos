Adds graphics.ambient_occlusion (off by default, linear pipeline only) and turns it on in the enhanced preset. Each view first draws the level's static faces depth-only into a half-resolution R16F target. Outdoors that is the terrain and building buffers the sun shadows already keep. Indoors it is every visible face, rebuilt when IndoorLocation::geometryVersion changes. A half-resolution pass rebuilds view positions from that depth and the view's projection, asymmetric VR frusta included, takes normals from their derivatives, and tests 12 hemisphere samples turned by a 4x4 tile of angles. A 4x4 bilateral blur evens the tile out. The terrain, building and BSP shaders read the result with a depth-weighted upsample and scale the ambient light and the point lights by it, never the sun's direct light. The occlusion has to exist before shading for that split, so it comes from its own depth pass, not from the world target's depth.
What it does: in Castle Navan the corners where walls meet floor and ceiling and the risers of the stairs darken, and flat walls don't change. In the Grand Temple the arches, the bases of the walls and the ceiling corners darken. The Harmondale town hall's door alcove and the beam above it darken too. Across the 31 scenes with enhanced on, the biggest change is a 10% lower mean brightness, on the close-up town hall views at night.
Cost, GPU ms per frame, against master with the enhanced preset: +0.10 at a 1840x1376 world target and +0.47 at the 3476x3364 eye size in Harmondale, +0.07 and +0.38 in Castle Navan. That is the depth pass (0.03 to 0.04), the occlusion pass (0.28 to 0.31) and the blur (0.03 to 0.04) at eye size, plus the upsample in the world shaders. VR runs it once per eye, so a VR frame pays about 0.75 to 0.95 ms.
With the preset off, the 31-scene set is byte identical to master, and the log's warning and error lines match master both off and with enhanced on. Style, unit tests, game tests and the shader lint, which now covers the AMBIENT_OCCLUSION variants, pass.

The world is lit by an ambient term and point lights that reach every
face the same, so corners, the space under stairs and the floor behind
pillars are as bright as open walls. With graphics.ambient_occlusion and
the linear pipeline, each view first draws the level's static faces
depth-only at half resolution, from the terrain and building buffers the
sun shadows already keep outdoors and from every visible face indoors,
which is rebuilt when IndoorLocation::geometryVersion changes. A
half-resolution pass reconstructs view positions from that depth and
the projection, takes normals from their derivatives and tests twelve
samples in a hemisphere turned by a 4x4 tile of angles. A 4x4 bilateral
blur evens the tile out. The world shaders then read it with a
depth-weighted upsample and scale the ambient light and the point lights
by it, never the sun's direct light, so lit faces keep their look. The
light is summed before the sRGB decode, so they use the occlusion to the
power of 1/2.2. The occlusion has to exist before the shading, which is
why it comes from its own depth pass rather than the world target's
depth. New R16F and RG16F target formats keep the passes cheap. With the
setting off the shaders compile to the stock text.
