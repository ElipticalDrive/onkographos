Why
The ambient occlusion radius and strength and the bloom strength are fixed in their shaders, but a game wants them per scene. Colour grading is limited to contrast and saturation. Artists usually grade with a lookup table made in an image editor.
How other engines do it
Unity (URP/HDRP Volume) and Unreal (Post Process Volume) both expose AO intensity and radius, bloom intensity, threshold and lens dirt, white balance (temperature and tint), lift/gamma/gain or shadows/midtones/highlights, and a 3D LUT (usually 32³), all of which can blend between volumes.
Common tonemappers are ACES, a neutral curve, and AgX (Blender's default), which keeps very bright saturated colours from turning hue.
The LUT workflow: screenshot the game with a neutral LUT pasted in, grade it in Photoshop or GIMP, then crop the LUT back out.
Proposed approach
Move the AO radius and strength and the bloom strength (plus an optional lens dirt texture) into PostSettings.
Add white balance (temperature and tint) and shadows/midtones/highlights in the tonemap pass.
Add a 3D LUT: content.Lut("grades/autumn.png") in the standard strip layout, with a blend amount. Add a tool or command (--screenshot --neutral-lut) to make the starting image.
Add a tonemapper choice: ACES (default), AgX and neutral.
Smoothly blend PostSettings between two values (PostSettings.Lerp), so cutscenes and zones can fade the look.
Done when
Every new setting has before and after screenshots, and zero leaves the image unchanged.
A LUT round-trip test: the neutral LUT gives an identical image.
rendering.md (The look of the picture) and design.md (Post-processing, known simplifications) are updated.
