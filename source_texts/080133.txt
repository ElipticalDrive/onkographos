Our test uses WebGL to create a demanding 3D graphics / volume-shader scene that stress-tests your device's GPU — similar in spirit to other browser graphics and render tests, focused on real-time FPS. Here's how it works:

Cross-Platform Compatibility: Works consistently across different devices, browsers, and operating systems. Whether you're on Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, or Android, the test provides comparable results.
GPU Stress Testing: The volume shader rendering pushes your GPU to its limits, providing an accurate assessment of how well your device handles graphics-intensive tasks like gaming, video editing, or 3D applications.

After running the Volume Shader BM test, you'll receive several key performance metrics. Here's what they mean and how to interpret them:
FPS (Frames Per Second): This is the most important metric. Higher FPS means smoother graphics. 60+ FPS is excellent, 30-60 FPS is good, 15-30 FPS is moderate, and below 15 FPS indicates limited GPU performance.
Frame Time: The time it takes to render each frame. Lower is better. Consistent frame times indicate stable performance, while variable frame times can cause stuttering or lag.
Rendering Stability: Measures how consistent your device's performance is. Stable performance without drops is ideal for smooth graphics rendering.
Visual Quality: The complexity and detail of the 3D visualizations your device can render. Higher quality rendering indicates better GPU capabilities.
Lag Detection: Identifies performance drops and stuttering during graphics processing. Consistent performance without lag indicates a capable device.
Real-Time Metrics: All metrics update in real-time as the test runs, giving you immediate feedback on your device's performance under load.
