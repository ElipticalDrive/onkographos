What problem(s) does this PR solve?
Enhances GPU-based foliage culling to support per-camera view frustum and occlusion buffer (HZB) culling, matching the renderer's culling behavior. Previously, foliage instances were only culled once per frame based on the LOD camera's view. Now they are culled separately for each camera drawn in the scenario, using that camera's frustum and occlusion buffer.
Additionally, when the landscape's LOD is frozen, foliage cells outside the frozen camera's view are hidden (only shadows rendered), consistent with how landscape patches behave.
Additional information
Key Changes
GPU Culling Pipeline Refactoring:
Split run() into run_lod() (once per frame, classifies LOD levels and emits shadow list) and per-camera emission via a renderer callback
Added set_outputs() to decouple output setup from culling execution
Added set_view_settings() to configure the LOD camera view for frozen LOD scenarios
Introduced _emit() helper to handle instance emission for a specific list (main/shadow) and camera view
View and Occlusion Support:
New LandscapeFoliageViewParams struct (560 bytes, std140) containing frustum planes, camera matrices, projection, and HZB mip layout
Added _upload_occlusion() to transfer the viewport's occlusion buffer (HZB) to GPU storage
Added _draw_view() callback invoked by the renderer for each camera, handling per-camera culling
Occlusion test in shader (is_occluded()) matches RendererSceneOcclusionCull::HZBuffer logic
Shader Updates (landscape_foliage_cull.glsl):
MODE_CLASSIFY: Simplified to only classify LOD levels (frustum culling removed, now per-camera)
MODE_EMIT: Added frustum and occlusion culling for main list; shadow list emits all instances within cull distance
New uniforms: view frustum planes, camera matrices, projection, HZB buffer with mip metadata
Shared counters for frustum/occlusion culled instances per list
CPU-Side View Culling:
Added _update_view() to track the LOD camera's frustum in landscape space
Added _is_in_view() for AABB frustum testing
Added _update_view_culling() to hide cells outside the frozen LOD camera's view
Cell out_of_view flag controls visibility: out-of-view cells only render shadows
Statistics and Lifecycle:
Stats struct now includes frustum_culled and occlusion_culled counts
Changed shared_users counter to a LocalVector<LandscapeFoliageGPU*> for proper multi-instance tracking
Added _camera_drawn() static callback registered with the renderer
Async readback of counters via _stats_received() callback
Memory and Resource Management:
Per-instance HZB buffer slots (2 buffers for double-buffering)
View parameters uniform buffer per foliage instance
Proper cleanup in _free_entry() and _free_gpu()
Testing
Existing tests updated and new test case added:
test_landscape_foliage.h: Updated frozen LOD test to verify cells_out_of_view stat
New test "Culling with the frozen view of the camera" validates that cells outside the frozen camera's frustum are hidden while their shadows remain visible
Documentation
Updated README.md (Russian) and LandscapeFoliage3D.xml to document:
Per-camera culling behavior
Frozen LOD view culling (cells hidden like landscape patches)
GPU culling now includes occlusion buffer testing
Update order prioritizes cells in view, then nearest cells

GPU indirect rendering (LandscapeFoliage3D.gpu_indirect):
- the levels of detail and the shadow lists are still chosen once per frame
  for the camera of the LOD of the landscape, but the main lists are now
  culled for every camera drawn, just before it is drawn: the renderer calls
  back (RenderingMethod::camera_callback_add()) once the occlusion buffer of
  the viewport is up to date for the camera,
- the bounding sphere of every instance is tested against the view frustum of
  the camera and against the occlusion buffer of its viewport (HZB, the same
  test as HZBuffer::_is_occluded(), the buffer is uploaded once per camera):
  the terrain, buildings and the foliage itself hide the instances behind
  them (gpu_occlusion_culling), every viewport sees its own instances,
- while the LOD of the landscape is frozen, the last view of its camera is
  kept, with its occlusion buffer,
- the main lists have stable bounds (every cell, only growing), and the
  renderer no longer records the changes of shadow-only instances: their
  bounds changing when the camera moved made the HZB distrust the depth
  buffer around the camera,
- the capacity of the outputs follows the most instances of every camera and
  only shrinks after 2 s mostly unused (no dropped instances when turning
  around), the MultiMeshes are handed to the GPU before they're freed,
- statistics: instances culled by the view frustum and by occlusion.

Chunked rendering:
- while the LOD of the landscape is frozen, the cells out of the view of its
  camera are hidden like its patches (only their shadows are drawn),
- the cells in the view of the camera are updated first.

Any update of an instance (a MultiMesh reallocated with another instance
count, its bounds set again, a batch freed) made the renderer distrust the
depth buffer where it was for up to 8 frames. The foliage cells sorted per
instance are updated every meter of camera movement, and the cells switching
levels of detail free a batch, so a band of cells at every LOD and cull
distance kept disabling HZB occlusion culling for everything behind them:
draw calls went back to the level without occlusion culling as soon as the
camera moved.

- RenderingServer.INSTANCE_FLAG_HZB_STABLE_CONTENT: for instances that change
  what they draw while staying in place, only moving, resizing or hiding them
  invalidates the depth buffers for HZB occlusion culling,
- the foliage sets it on the batches of the cells and on the indirect
  MultiMeshes of the GPU culling,
- the batch of a level of a cell is emptied instead of freed while the cell is
  drawn (a MultiMesh without instances draws nothing).

Landscape: cull the foliage hidden by the terrain with its horizon

The occlusion culling of the renderer (HZB) relies on a depth buffer of a few
frames ago reprojected into the camera: when the camera moves, the edges of
the objects and the silhouettes of the hills stop occluding, and most of the
culling is lost. What the terrain hides is now culled without depth buffer:

- LandscapeHorizon: the horizon of the terrain seen from a point, the
  steepest elevation of the terrain closer than every distance (64 rings) in
  every azimuth (1024), marched through the LOD tree of the landscape on a few
  threads. Conservative: the lowest height of the nodes covering each sample,
  minus margins for the geometric error of the LOD and the micro detail, the
  nodes with a hole or outside of the terrain hide nothing,
- Landscape3D::get_horizon(): built again when the camera moves or the
  terrain changes, up to the cull distance of the foliage, none below the
  terrain or with an orthogonal camera; LandscapeData::get_hole_cells() keeps
  a mask of the patches with holes,
- LandscapeFoliage3D.terrain_occlusion_culling (on by default): the cells
  hidden from the camera of the landscape only draw their shadows (tested in
  frame_pre_draw with the camera of the frame), the GPU culling tests every
  instance against the horizon in the views of that camera, statistics,
- the renderer no longer stops trusting the depth buffer in front of an
  instance that changed or disappeared (only where the depth isn't nearer
  than it): foliage hidden behind a hill doesn't disable the HZB of the hill.
