Abstract
Model Order Reduction (MOR) techniques have extensive applications across scientific and engineering disciplines, such as neutron field reconstruction of nuclear reactor cores, thermoelastic field reconstruction, fluid, and solid mechanics. In the process of building a Reduced Order Model (ROM), the selection of the basis functions in the offline stage is crucial and directly depends on the parameter space sampling strategy. This problem has always been a challenge in MOR. Research into adaptive sampling algorithms has become a hot topic in recent years. To better understand the application of these algorithms to MOR, this paper focuses on three prevalent adaptive sampling algorithms: pseudo-gradient sampling, adaptive sparse grid sampling, adaptive training set extension. These have been successfully applied in various applications, including nuclear reactor cores, molten salt reactor system, power system for convection problems. We systematically assess and compare their performance, finding that adaptive sampling algorithms excel in sampling divergent and oscillating areas and are generally better than the standard sampling strategy. Specifically, the pseudo-gradient sampling algorithm is effective for small-scale scenarios, while the other two algorithms are designed for large-scale sampling. Their practicality is confirmed through successful applications in nuclear reactor cores.
Keywords: Adaptive sampling algorithm, Reduced order model, Pseudo-gradient sampling, Adaptive sparse grid sampling, Adaptive training set extension
1. Introduction
In science and engineering, many complex systems are described by the interaction of multiple physical field phenomena. Complex mathematical models are needed to capture the interactions between these physical fields. With the advancement of industry and the development of technology, the solution accuracy and computing power of the numerical simulation software have been improved, which is widely disseminated and applied in many fields, such as fluid mechanics, thermal engineering, nuclear engineering and electromagnetism. Nevertheless, due to the huge volume of actual engineering software, typical engineering problems have a size of O(106∼109) degrees of freedom, which results in significant computational costs. This computational burden poses challenges to engineering applications that require repeated evaluation or realtime response [40]. MOR aims to accelerate simulation of the large-scale Full Ordel Reduction (FOM) by constructing a small-scale system, called the ROM. ROM is an effective technique to reduce the dimensionality of large-scale complex systems. It replaces the high fidelity model with a low-dimensional efficient model preserving only the prominent dynamics of the system. The ROM can then be used to provide fast solutions with a controlled level of accuracy. From a computational and memory perspective, engineers can achieve rapid reconstruction of physical fields using far fewer computational resources than solving FOM directly.
Among all ROM methods, Reduced Basis (RB) is a well-established and widely used class of ROM techniques, which are based on an offline-online paradigm. In the offline stage, a set of RB functions is learned from an ensemble of high-fidelity solutions, called snapshots. In the online stage, instead of solving the FOM, the linear combination of RB functions can be used to solve any parameter point. According to the calculation method of expansion coefficient, ROM can be divided into two categories: projector-based intrusive ROM and map-based non-intrusive ROM. As for non-intrusive methods, the Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) algorithm, Empirical Interpolation Method (EIM) [9] and its generalized version Generalized Empirical Interpolation Method (GEIM) [34] are widely used. Recently, Nguyen and Peraire innovatively proposed a first-order EIM algorithm on this basis [37]. As for intrusive methods, Reduced Basis Method (RBM) [25], [41] and Pod-Glerkin algorithm [8], [45] are common algorithms for building intrusive ROM. In this work, we focus on the offline stage of the ROM.
The offline phase of the ROM method requires the selection of representative parameter points from the parameter space to construct the set, called the training set. The construction of a training set is extremely challenging. On the one hand, if the training set is too small, the ROM will produce overfitting phenomenon, and the accurate reconstruction of the physical field cannot be achieved. On the other hand, if the training set is too fine, the offline time will increase, especially for the high-dimensional parameter space, there will be a dimension explosion, which limits the application of ROM in engineering.
In general, uniform sampling, random sampling, Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations (CVT) sampling [30], Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) [36], etc. are some common general sampling strategies, for both intrusive and non-intrusive. However, the lack of adaptivity of these methods is a limitation in practical applications. Several authors have attempted to address this issue by proposing innovative adaptive algorithms. Adaptive sampling algorithms can dynamically adjust the sampling strategy based on the previous sampling results and the characteristics of the problem, so as to more intelligently explore the key regions of the parameter space. At present, the research direction of adaptive sampling algorithm mainly divided into: intrusive ROM's sampling algorithm and non-intrusive ROM's sampling algorithm.
In the field of intrusive ROM, the proposed sampling strategies mainly fall into two categories, one is for POD-Galerkin algorithm, and the other is for RBM. For the former, Tezzele and Ballarin et al. [46] used the gradient information of the target output relative to the parameters to identify the active subspace in the parameter space [14], [15], and then combined it with the POD-Galerkin algorithm to improve its computational efficiency. Chen and Ghattas [13] proposed a new sampling strategy based on Hessian matrix. By random Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) algorithm [7], [42], the main eigenvectors of the Hessian matrix were identified and stretched into a low-dimensional subspace. Meanwhile, the high-dimensional parameter space was projected onto it to achieve dimensionality reduction. With the introduction of new snapshots into the optimization process, Kunisch and Volkwein shifted the focus of the optimization problem to selecting the optimal snapshot for the model and realizing the optimization of the snapshot itself [32]. Valentin built a ROM based on POD-Galerkin algorithm and developed a trust region algorithm for large-scale bounded Partial Differential Equation (PDE) constraint optimization, which can control the inaccuracy of the ROM and adaptively improve the prediction accuracy in the optimization process [48]. For the latter, S. Sen first proposed an adaptive sampling strategy for the RBM algorithm [43]. He defined a multi-stage sampling strategy and ran the RBM algorithm several times on a small training set of random sampling to generate a basis function. In [38], a two-stage sampling strategy is proposed. At the same time, the machine learning method of k-means clustering [33] was used in the sampling process to identify points with high probability of large errors in the parameter space and added them to the training set to realize adaptive sampling for the high-dimensional parameter space. Hesthaven et al. [27] introduced Analysis of variance (ANOVA) [20], [19] into RBM algorithm and proposed a three-step RB-ANOVA-RB method for constructing the ROM. T. Hellappa et al. proposed a strategy of subsampling fine training set in the offline stage, but they also pointed out that this method has the problem of low stability, which can be oversampled to improve the robustness of the method [11]. Hesthaven et al. proposed a dynamic management strategy for training set, which can realize the addition and removal of sampling points in training set [26]. Chellappa and Feng et al. adopted additional fine training set to achieve dynamic update of rough training set [12]. Jiang and Chen et al. proposed an idea to reduce the computational cost, that was, only a subset of the fine training set was kept active during the iteration of the greedy algorithm [29]. The recent literature [28] has synthesized the concepts of the literature [26], [43] and improved the subsampling strategy in [29]. On this basis, they proposed two new hybrid methods, and proved that these methods can significantly improve the computing speed without affecting the quality of the ROM. Eftang and Patera et al. first proposed the idea of controlling the number of online stage basis functions to save online reconstruction costs, and introduced the method of “hp” type RBM. The main idea of this method is to partition the parameter space based on hierarchical tree and generate an independent ROM for each partition [17], [16]. Different from the parameter space partitioning method used here, Haasdonka et al. adopted structured grid to partition parameter space. They proposed a partitioning method based on parameter space adaptive grid to reduce the cost of online computation [24]. Literature [35] also adopted the partitioning idea. Unlike [24], [17], [16], Mayday et al. did not make a clear division of parameter space, but defined the local approximation space through the distance function constructed by experience [35]. Bui-Thanh et al. improved the RBM algorithm and introduced a method to select the optimal snapshot by solving the optimization problem constrained by PDE and maximizing the difference between the output of a large-scale system and a reduced order system [10]. Urban and Karsten et al. used gradient method to solve nonlinear optimization problems, thereby building a ROM [47]. Mayday et al. developed a local adaptive greedy algorithm based on Hessian matrix, which realizes anisotropic sampling of parameter space through adaptive updating of training set [35]. They not only used the Hessian matrix to define the distance measure between sample points, but innovatively proposed a strategy for adding samples to the training set.
In the field of non-intrusive ROM, many researchers have proposed research methods for sampling. For example, Alsayyari et al. first proposed the idea of combining sparse grid with POD algorithm. Their core idea is to select the sparse grid layer number according to the singular value attenuation rate of POD base, which successfully realize effective sampling [2]. In the process of building the ROM, literature [3] proposed an adaptive POD algorithm, which identifies important dimensions and ranges of parameter space by introducing adaptive sparse grids. Building on this foundation, subsequent studies [4] and [5] extended this algorithm, applying it to time-dependent and multiple outputs models, thereby enhancing the general applicability of the adaptive POD algorithm. Guenot and Lepot et al. introduced two kinds of non-intrusive adaptive sampling strategies for ROM based on POD algorithm. In the study, they innovatively defined criteria to measure the enrichment potential of new sampling points for models [23]. Eftang and Stamm proposed two kinds of “hp” techniques for EIM algorithm to realize partition sampling of parameter space [18].

It can be seen that the research and application of adaptive sampling algorithms have garnered interest from academia and also penetrated into the field of MOR. However, there is no systematic comparative study of these adaptive algorithms. Realworld sampling problems often have very complex internal and external factors, and various adaptive sampling algorithms have different effects on reconstruction performance.

Our primary contribution lies in the meticulous study of how hyperparameters affect the performance of adaptive sampling algorithms, ensuring the optimal performance of ROMs and offering a preliminary analysis of the sampling points' characteristics. We then focus on a comparative analysis of three distinct adaptive sampling algorithms, evaluating their sampling efficiency and computational complexity within a unified framework. By applying these algorithms to the complex nuclear reactor cores environment, we demonstrate their effectiveness. Our numerical experiments substantiate the viability and unique advantages of adaptive sampling algorithms, enriching the theoretical knowledge in the field and contributing to the future evolution and enhancement of adaptive sampling algorithms. The outcome of this research can be a helpful guidance for the practical implementation of sampling strategies to any ROM related industry software.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we introduce three adaptive sampling algorithms. Then, the relevant settings for numerical experiments are presented in Section 3. In Section 4, we systematically address the adaptive sampling algorithm, beginning by analyzing the influence of hyperparameters in Section 4.1 and providing an overview of sampling point characteristics in Section 4.2. We then concentrate on comparing the reconstruction effect and computational complexity in Section 4.3. We also demonstrate the practical application of these adaptive sampling algorithms within the context of nuclear reactor cores in Section 4.4. Finally, the paper draws the corresponding conclusions and summarizes the current challenges and the direction of future research in Section 5.
2. Three adaptive sampling algorithms
2.1. Adaptive sparse grid sampling
Smolyak sparse grid plays an important role in high-dimensional multivariate integration and interpolation problems, which was first proposed by Smolyak [44] in 1963. Because it is not limited by dimension, it can be very effective for large-scale problems, such us orthography, data mining, finance, and other fields. [21], [31], [39]. The construction of sparse grid is based on a hierarchical strategy that successively builds the new grid on the basis of previous grid selection. Such construction is suitable for adaptive sampling idea since it can be used to build an algorithm that dynamically samples based on past sampling results.
The hierarchical grid is obtained by a specific combination of tensor products, which is characterized by the preservation of one-dimensional regularity. Specifically, this is done by selecting a set of nested nodes xi in each dimension of the parameter space and then tensioning these points in some way, where the one-dimensional nested nodes xi are generated in a hierarchical manner by levels. Many choices are possible for selecting the one-dimensional nested nodes, such as equidistant nodes and Chebyshev nodes. Chebyshev nodes are denser at the boundary and sparser in the central region, which increases the risk of ignoring the central region, whereas equidistant nodes provide a more uniform coverage, allowing for a greater extent of separation of the parameter space. Therefore, we choose equidistant nodes to generate nested nodes in this paper. Its construction satisfies the following formula (2.1):

Adaptive sparse grid algorithm combines Smolyak sparse grid with POD algorithm, which was first proposed by Alsayyari et al. [3]. Its basic idea involves constructing a surrogate model for POD coefficients using Smolyak interpolation, followed by the development of a ROM that reconstructs the associated nodes in a non-intrusive manner. The Smolyak interpolation builds the surrogate model by considering an operator Ui(c)(x) that approximates the coefficient function c(x) in the POD algorithm by an expansion as follows:
c(x)≈Ui(c)(x)=∑xjic(xji)axjii(x).
(2.3)
During the execution of this algorithm, the forward point principle is used to generate the nodes for testing, and a critical criterion is established to identify significant points whose reconstruction errors surpass a predefined threshold γint. Subsequently, the grid is iteratively refined by incorporating solely the selected important points. In this way, the dynamic adjustment of Smolyak sparse grid can be realized through this adaptive sampling mechanism. The flowchart of the algorithm is shown in Fig. 3. 2.2. Adaptive training set extension
The adaptive training set extension algorithm studied in this paper was proposed by Haasdonk et al. [24]. The algorithm is based on the refinement of a mesh covering the parameter space, the final vertices of which are served as sampling points within the parameter space. The mesh refinement strategy is based on the element index η(ω), which is defined as follows:
η(ω)=γ(ω)s(ω)+ε(ω)ε(ω)=maxμ∈V(ω)∪c(ω)e(μ),
(2.4)
the element index η(ω) is composed of two parts: the count of preceding refinement steps that failed to refine the cell ω, represented by s(ω), and a term for the reconstruction error, denoted by ε(ω). The value of s(ω) is essentially the number of times the cell ω has been overlooked for refinement, and when multiplied by the positive weighting parameter γ(ω), it contributes to the overall element index. The reconstruction error ε(ω) is determined by identifying the maximum error e(μ) across all parameter points μ, which includes the vertex set V(ω) and the center c(ω) of the mesh cell.
Element index η(ω) takes into account the influence of both the reconstruction error ε(ω) and the refinement step count s(ω). Consequently, cells that do not meet the refinement criteria are penalized from one refinement step to the next, ensuring that all mesh cells are asymptotically refined.
The main process of adaptive training set extension can be summarized as follows: (1) the element index η(ω) of each mesh cell ω is calculated and the mesh cell whose η(ω) is greater than the specified refinement threshold τ is marked; (2) the marked mesh cells are structurally refined. Repeat the steps 1-2 above until the error of all mesh cell is reduced to the specified tolerance level. For more details on this algorithm, we refer the reader to [24]. 2.3. Pseudo-gradient sampling
The adaptive sampling algorithm based on pseudo-gradient was first proposed by Gong [22]. This method can be well adapted to the dimensionality and characteristic of the parameter space of the target problem, especially when the number of sampling points is limited and the parameter space is high dimensional. Therefore, the salient advantage of this method is that the most important information in the parameter space can be obtained first, enabling an effective reconstruction of the target physical field. The core of the algorithm is to realize the adaptive expansion of the training set through the defined pseudo-gradient index. In the p-dimensional parameter space D: [μmin(1),μmax(1)]×…×[μmin(p),μmax(p)], each μi represents a parameter point. The function associated with μi is denoted by u(⋅,μi). The pseudo-gradient index is then defined as follows:
dμ(ui,uj)=d[u](ui,uj)d[μ](μi,μj),
(2.5)
where d[u](ui,uj) is the distance between function ui and uj, defined as:
d[u](ui,uj)=‖ui‖ui‖2−uj‖uj‖2‖2.
(2.6)
Here, ‖⋅‖2 denotes the L2 norm, and the term d[μ](μi,μj) represents the normalized distance between the parameters μi and μj. This distance is defined as follows:
d[μ](μi,μj)=∑n=1p(μi(n)−μj(n)μmax(n)−μmin(n))2.
(2.7)
Based on the defined pseudo-gradient index, we calculate the indices between each pair of sampling points into a pseudo-gradient matrix Dμ. We then identify the pair of parameter points associated with the maximum element in Dμ and select their midpoint as the point to be sampled. If the distance of this midpoint from all existing sampling points exceeds a predefined tolerance rtol, the midpoint is added to the sampling set and the pseudo-gradient matrix is updated accordingly. If not, the process advances to the next iteration. This iterative procedure continues until the maximum pseudo-gradient index falls below the ξtol. In conclusion, the deliberate design of experimental objects allows for a thorough assessment of algorithmic sampling performance. This comprehensive evaluation not only systematically gauges the robustness and applicability of adaptive sampling algorithms but also aids in the selection of the most appropriate algorithms for a variety of practical engineering scenarios.
3.2. Evaluation criteria
After a set of reconstruction objectives is proposed, we must propose the model reduction method and the evaluation criteria for it. The main MOR techniques we use in this paper are: EIM and POD. The two methods are used to reconstruct the field u in a sufficiently dense test set, and the reconstruction errors are compared to judge the sampling effect of the adaptive sampling strategy. The main evaluation criterion we use in this paper is: maximum relative error based on L2 norm.
Adopting the maximum relative error as a benchmark for evaluation is instrumental in assessing the overall performance of the obtained ROM across the entire test set, so as to better evaluate the applicability of the sampling strategy. The smaller the maximum relative error of the model, the better the applicability of the adaptive sampling algorithm. Furthermore, the sensitivity of the maximum relative error to outliers aligns well with the stringent precision demands typically encountered in practical engineering applications.

4. Results
In this section, we initiate our research with an examination of how hyperparameter values affect adaptive sampling outcomes. We systematically investigate the performance of three adaptive sampling strategies, focusing on their sampling characteristics, reconstruction effects, and computational costs to evaluate their efficiency. To assess their practical applicability, we apply these algorithms to data from nuclear reactor cores.
4.1. Hyperparameter research of adaptive sampling algorithm
4.1.1. Parameter research in pseudo-gradient sampling
In the pseudo-gradient sampling algorithm, there are two key hyperparameters: rtol and ξtol. The hyperparameter rtol measures the minimum sampling density in the parameter space. Sampling is executed by the algorithm only when the distance between prospective parameters and existing samples meets the stipulated tolerance, thereby ensuring a sufficient disparity between the parameters to be sampled and those already sampled. Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 illustrate the outcomes of sampling 25 points for Test case 2, along with their corresponding reconstruction errors, at rtol values of 0.01, 0.02, and 0.05, respectively. It is evident that an overly small rtol results in an overly dense selection of sampling points, which in turn makes the distinction between the final training sets minimally apparent, failing to optimally represent the entire parameter space. Conversely, an overly large rtol leads to sparse sampling and may omit critical points. On the other hand, ξtol is the lower limit of the pseudo-gradient value, which reflects the degree of change of the function relative to the parameter. An excessively large ξtol may cause the algorithm to terminate prematurely before fully exploring the parameter space, potentially resulting in not reaching the desired sample size.

4.1.2. Parameter research in adaptive training set extension
In the adaptive training set extension algorithm, the refinement threshold τ determines whether each grid element can be labeled and refined, which will indirectly affect the sampling of the final parameter space. Based on our experience in many numerical experiments, it has been observed that the threshold should be neither excessively high nor unduly low. Instead, it should be selected appropriately based on the actual situation to ensure the optimal reconstruction efficacy of the ROM.
Taking Test case 1 as an example, we sampled the first 100 points at refinement thresholds of 0.01, 0.001, and 0.00001, generated the corresponding ROMs, and compared the reconstruction errors. Fig. 8 shows the reconstruction errors for different values of τ. We can see that when τ=0.001, the reconstruction accuracy is higher than that with τ set to 0.01 and 0.00001, the reasons for this may be:
•
An excessively low setting for τ forces the algorithm to refine the parameter space with an unnecessary level of precision. Consequently, with a fixed training set size, an excessive number of samples are allocated to non-critical regions. This allocation may cause the sampling process to conclude prematurely, before critical regions are adequately refined, thus not fully leveraging the algorithm's potential.
•
Conversely, a large τ leads to a coarse refinement of the parameter space by the algorithm, which fails to thoroughly explore the parameter space. This insufficient exploration can overlook critical regions, compromising the performance of the ROM.

In Test case 2 and Test case 3, the parameter μ2 is embedded within a cosine function, which imparts oscillatory characteristic to the system. 5. Conclusion
An effective sampling strategy is pivotal for enhancing the construction efficiency of ROM, saving the calculation cost, and ensuring the accurate reconstruction of the physical field. Currently, sampling technology has gained widespread adoption in the field of MOR. With the rapid development of adaptive sampling technology, the research of sampling strategies has gradually shifted from traditional standard methods to adaptive sampling algorithms. However, there is no systematic comparative study on these adaptive algorithms. In this work, we endeavor to fill this gap by initiating a preliminary comparative analysis. To achieve this, we apply three adaptive sampling algorithms: adaptive training set extension, adaptive sparse grid sampling, and pseudo-gradient sampling.
The aforementioned adaptive sampling algorithms have been tested on a variety of scenarios, including three analytic functions and nuclear reactor cores, and the results demonstrate that these algorithms can sample effectively in parameter spaces featuring divergent and oscillating regions. Furthermore, they achieve better accuracy in constructing the ROM compared to standard sampling strategies. Specifically, the performance of these adaptive algorithms varies under different conditions, as detailed below:
•
The pseudo-gradient sampling is the best especially for the relatively small training set case, which can accurately locate the most critical parameter points in the parameter space at the beginning, thereby enabling effective reconstruction.
•
With the increase of training set size, the adaptive training set extension algorithm and the adaptive sparse grid algorithm usually become the best choice. Especially, the former is more suitable for large-scale training sets.
Our study is definitive, highlighting the merits of adaptive sampling algorithms and providing significant advantages for practical engineering. By enabling more accurate ROM through dynamic sampling, these algorithms optimize complex system design and analysis, notably in costly simulation fields such as nuclear reactor cores simulations, thermoelastic field reconstruction, fluid dynamics, and solid mechanics. They ensure accuracy while reducing computational cost and are instrumental for rapid, informed decision-making in dynamic scenarios. The research on adaptive sampling algorithms is progressively enhancing our theoretical understanding and paving the way for their integration into industrial applications.
Through the experiments, we observed that adaptive sampling algorithms can sample key parameter points according to the actual situation, so as to obtain more accurate reconstruction results. Despite the achievements of our research, certain limitations warrant attention.
•
There are few sampling studies on non-intrusive ROM
The majority of established sampling algorithms are predicated on intrusive order reduction techniques. With the development of industry, numerical simulation software in physical fields such as fluid mechanics, thermal engineering, nuclear engineering and electromagnetism has reached a high level of maturity. However, these actual engineering software are usually very large in volume and cannot access the underlying governing equations. Therefore, it is unrealistic to reconstruct physical field by using projection based intrusive model reduction techniques. This limitation underscores the necessity for the development of more efficient sampling algorithms tailored to non-intrusive ROM.
•
High-dimensional parameter space sampling challenge
The adaptive sampling algorithm adopted in this paper has only been verified in numerical examples with fewer than 10 dimensions. However, the sampling results and reconstruction effects of these methods in numerical examples with more than 10 dimensions have not been fully evaluated.
•
Our parameter research is mainly limited to the qualitative exploration of adaptive sampling algorithms
Currently, our research on the parameters of adaptive sampling algorithms provides a general direction for selection but lacks clarity on the specific parameter range and scientific optimization methods.
The existing problems provide guidance for the main research direction of sampling algorithm in the future. As future work: First, we propose to focus on the study of sampling algorithms for non-intrusive ROM, thus, on the premise of ensuring the accuracy of model reconstruction, the sampling times are reduced as much as possible, in order to improve the reconstruction efficiency of ROM. Second, due to the complexity of practical applications, it is necessary to further explore the feasibility and effect of these sampling algorithms in high-dimensional situations. Third, we aim to refine the hyperparameter settings of these algorithms to achieve more explicit configurations tailored to various problems.
