Builds on #22 (the rendering foundation, now on main). This diff holds only the SDF trial's commits.
What this is
A hidden SDF trial developer layer. Each island is a real mesh built from a 3D signed distance field. Dual contouring turns the field into triangles. Everything runs in WebGPU compute.
Field: each island is a set of level slabs. Each slab is a flat top at the landform's height, cut by a sloped wall. The wall stands on the exact distance to the coast or to a re-measured escarpment. Lips are a sharp max and feet a smooth min. The geometry has no noise.
GPU passes: per island, in order:
columns: coast distance, owner, land and seabed;
node field;
dual-contouring cells: false-position crossings, normals from the gradient, a closed-form QEF vertex with half-cell slack;
quads, appended with atomics;
a per-vertex lighting pass.
The seabed is a height field over the whole chart, also computed on the GPU. The water is a translucent sheet: it reads the scene depth and applies Beer–Lambert absorption.
Drawing: one indirect draw per island, with nothing read back to the CPU. There is no index attribute: the vertex shader pulls indices[vertexIndex] from storage.
Level of detail: spacing is 1 unit at overview and 0.5 close up. An island rebuilds when its level changes. All 16 islands on the chart are meshed.
WebGL 2: the layer is gated on the WebGPU backend. Under WebGL 2 it draws nothing, and the geology preview remains the fallback. There is no CPU meshing fallback.
CPU reference: the CPU field and mesher (sdf-island.ts, sdf-mesh.ts) stay as the reference for parity tests. The slab formula is one KernelMath kernel (sdf-kernel.ts) shared by the CPU, emulated-f32 and TSL backends.
The default render does not change, and the app bar is untouched.

Parity
Columns: GPU against CPU over 5 islands, worst difference 2e-4.
Mesh vertices against the CPU field: median 3e-4 to 8e-4, p95 ≈0.011, max 0.12 world units.
Normals: dot product ≥ 0.99999 at p5.
Vitest: the f32 kernel against the CPU field (worst < 2e-3), and the QEF solve on a corner, a lip edge and a flat patch.
Known gaps and follow-ups
First show: about a 1 s hitch. Planning takes ≈0.6 s, most of it building the chart world on the CPU; pipeline setup and the first compile take the rest.
Memory: buffers take 240 MB on the GPU. three's StorageBufferAttribute keeps the same 240 MB as a CPU copy, which should be dropped after upload.
Rebuild stalls: every island whose level changes rebuilds in the same frame, about 27 ms on the largest cluster. Spread the rebuilds over several frames.
Vertex capacity: the fullest island reaches 82% at 0.5 spacing. Overflow is guarded but leaves the mesh truncated.
Visual:
a pale jagged fringe at the beach lip;

the seabed doesn't reach the overview's far edges;
motus read taller and blockier than in the preview;
no sea stacks, waves or open-sea darkening yet;
islands don't shadow one another.
Console noise: "Vertex attribute position not found" warnings from vertex pulling.
Checks

Full vitest passes. export.test.ts needs dist/web, so it passes after a build.

Every island of the chart is built from the plan in compute passes with
nothing read back: the column passes (exact distance to the island's own
coast, landform, seabed and inlets, escarpment and lagoon footprints by
marching squares measured exactly within reach, normalisations), the
field at lattice nodes, dual contouring's cell pass (false-position
crossings, field normals, the shared closed-form QEF vertex) and quad
pass, appending into one shared vertex and index buffer by atomics. Each
island draws its range by an indirect draw, pulling its vertices from
storage in the vertex stage. Columns match the CPU reference to 2e-4;
vertices lie within 0.012 world units of the CPU field at p95.

On CI the dev server tests hung until their timeout: the test stopped
reading the server's stdout once it saw the URL, and on Linux writes to
a pipe are synchronous, so a full pipe can block the server. Its signal
handler also closed both servers without exiting, leaving turbo waiting.
The test now drains stdout, bounds each fetch, reports the server's
output on failure, and kills the server if it outlives its signal.
