What and why
Part of ROADMAP.md's "Faster world creation and entry on small CPUs" (noise sampling). Four changes to how noise and density volumes are computed; every value is bit-identical:
Perlin (Noise: a perlin volume tabulates its corner terms when y cells change often): a column's corner XZ dot products and Y gradients depend on the y cell only through each corner's gradient index. When a volume's y samples change cell at least four times, each column tabulates the sixteen gradients' terms once (the same operations as before) and every cell change reads them. Measured alone: a high-frequency volume 22.8 → 19.7 µs, mid 6.9 → 6.05, low 3.9 → 3.6.
Interpolation (Density: interpolation fills a cell's four-column rows side by side): a cell row four columns wide advances its four columns' addition chains together, so they overlap instead of waiting on each other. Each column keeps the same operations in the same order; corner samples are read through precomputed column offsets. Partial rows, and rows whose columns fail validation, keep the one-column loop and its error order. Measured alone: 219 → 170 µs for a 16×384×16 volume.
Range choice (Density: a bulk range choice selects with a branch-free loop): the in/out-of-range merge was a conditional store, a branch per value on unpredictable noise selectors; it is now a select, which vectorizes. Same values.
Packed storage (Storage: packed reads and writes inline into other crates): PackedStorage::read and write get #[inline], so horizon-world's section fills keep the slot arithmetic in their own loops instead of an out-of-line call per block.
Measured with chunkgen_bench at one thread: the noise stage is 7% faster over the 12 test areas below (single runs). Small on its own, but it is the largest generation stage once the surface pass is cheaper.
We found it while porting Horizon to the Nintendo Switch (JohnUzoka/horizon, homebrew). On its 1 GHz Cortex-A57 cores these commits gained more than on x86: profiles of the same chunks generated by /pregen put the noise fill's CPU time 15% lower (interpolation 22%, Perlin 13%).
Same output: the terrain and decorated snapshots (chunkgen_bench --size 16 --threads 1 --snapshot) are byte-identical to main's on 12 areas: seeds 12345, 1 and −4172144997902289642 at 0,0, jungle, stony and frozen peaks, grove, two badlands, the Nether, the End and its outer islands. World generation is on the "local proof first" list, so that is the proof.

The in-range/out-of-range merge stored the out-of-range value only where
the selector was outside the range, a conditional store LLVM leaves as a
branch per value (unpredictable for noise selectors). It now writes
every value as a select between the in-range and out-of-range samples,
which vectorizes; the selected values are the same.
