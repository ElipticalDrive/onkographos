Sorry for the late reply!
After reading your description, I think your question is actually not something related to "rendering". "Rendering" means, given a camera and a scene, how to compute the final image that is taken by this camera. This process plays with "colors", for example, how to compute shadows, speculations, etc. I have a couple of resource that helped me a lot: https://learnopengl.com/ https://raytracing.github.io/books/RayTracingInOneWeekend.html
Ok so back to your original question. Your question, concerning render_path_spiral, poses_avg, and other from load_llff.py, is related to camera pose, which is determined before rendering, and it is independent of "rendering": how the colors are calculated.
Initially, the input cameras have some poses (calculated by COLMAP), you can think of them as relative positions in the space. Since these are just relative positions, absolute camera positions can have arbitrary scale, e.g. (10000, 10000, 10000) in 3d. In order to train NeRF, NeRF requires that all positions lie inside [-1, 1]^3 (because of positional encoding), so what poses_avg does is to scale the poses appropriately (e.g. divide the above example by 10000) so that the numbers lie inside [-1, 1].
Next, after NeRF is trained, now we want to generate novel views to see if it is correctly trained. That is, we want to put the camera in new positions, i.e. new poses, to see how the images look like in these poses. render_path_spiral does this. it generates camera poses that follow https://github.com/kwea123/nerf_pl/blob/master/datasets/llff.py#L88 this spiral path.
To sum up, I think you need to clearly separate two independent processes, one is camera and another is rendering. To elaborate more, camera setting actually includes "intrinsics" and "extrinsics". Intrinsics means for example the resolution of the image, the focal length of the camera, etc. resource. In NeRF we assume the camera is always the same (same resolution, same focal length), so we didn't touch this part, but this part is actually modifiable if you wish. Extrinsics means camera poses, their [x y z roll pitch yaw] in 6 DoF. In the above example I only mentioned the position [x y z] for simplicity. Finally, rendering means after determining the camera and the scene, how are each pixel calculated. In NeRF, they are calculated using "volume rendering". In traditional graphics when rendering some meshes, you can think of Phong shading, etc.
