Summary
Build a working physically based path tracer for AztecCraft, inspired by the rendering quality and workflow of Autodesk Arnold. Prioritize accurate light transport and film-quality results. Start with a minimal runnable implementation and demo scene, then add capabilities incrementally.
Scope
Progressive path tracing with global illumination and configurable multiple light bounces.
Energy-conserving materials, including rough reflections and refraction/transmission.
Area lights, HDRI/environment lighting, and soft shadows.
Depth of field.
Configurable AOVs (arbitrary output variables):
Beauty
Diffuse direct and indirect
Specular direct and indirect
Transmission and emission
Normals and depth
Object and material IDs
Multilayer OpenEXR export for selected AOVs.
UV-space texture baking for lighting, ambient occlusion, and material properties, including padding/dilation across UV seams.
Suggested incremental plan
Runnable foundation: camera rays, scene intersection, a minimal energy-aware material, direct lighting, progressive accumulation, and a demo scene with a reproducible render.
Light transport: multi-bounce global illumination, area lights, HDRI lighting, soft shadows, rough reflection, and refraction/transmission.
Camera and outputs: depth of field, configurable AOV collection, and multilayer EXR export.
UV baking: lighting, ambient occlusion, and material-property baking with configurable padding/dilation across seams.
Quality and usability: sampling/denoising controls as appropriate, render controls, documentation, and image-quality/performance evaluation.
Acceptance criteria
A fresh checkout can run the renderer and render the included demo scene through a documented command or UI workflow.
Progressive samples visibly converge, with configurable bounce depth and sampling settings.
Materials and supported light types produce stable, energy-conscious results, including indirect illumination, soft shadows, rough reflections, and transmission.
Users can select requested AOVs, inspect their outputs, and export them together in a multilayer EXR.
UV baking supports the listed bake types and configurable seam padding/dilation.
Documented demo renders demonstrate the intended quality and feature coverage.
Notes
Implement this in stages so the minimal end-to-end renderer is usable early. Keep scene, material, lighting, and output interfaces extensible as features are added.
