The feature, motivation and pitch
grid_sample accepts 4-D and 5-D input, and mode="bicubic" accepts 4-D only:
```
import torch
torch.nn.functional.grid_sample(torch.zeros(1, 1, 4, 4, 4), torch.zeros(1, 2, 2, 2, 3), mode="bicubic")
# RuntimeError: grid_sampler(): bicubic interpolation only supports 4D input   (torch 2.12.1)
```
The volumetric counterpart is a tricubic sampler: the same separable cubic kernel over the third axis. This is
the volumetric half of #25039, whose bicubic box is ticked for images.
Medical volumes are resampled through a stored transform or a displacement field, which is what grid_sample
is for, and cubic is the interpolation those pipelines use for intensity data. It is what ITK's
BSplineInterpolateImageFunction and SimpleITK's sitkBSpline give, so a torch implementation either drops to
trilinear, which is a different result, or gathers the 64 neighbours by hand and loses the fused kernel.
For an order of magnitude, on torch 2.12.1 with a 128^3 volume sampled on a 64^3 grid, best of five: the fused
trilinear kernel takes 7.09 ms on CPU and 0.03 ms on one RTX PRO 5000, against 693.26 ms and 1.04 ms for a
straightforward hand written gather of the same eight corners. That compares a plain gather rather than the
best achievable one, and the cubic case needs 64 corners rather than 8.
Alternatives
Gathering the neighbours by hand, which is what we do, or resampling on the CPU through ITK, which leaves the
device.
