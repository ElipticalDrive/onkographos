Summary
Replaces the PPLL (shared node pool + global atomic counter + per-pixel head pointers) with a fixed per-pixel slot array held in an RGBA32UI 2D-array image, handed out by a per-pixel atomic count in an R32UI 2D-array image whose other layers accumulate an order-independent tail for fragments past the last slot. The resolve reads at most K slots at fixed addresses, sorts them and composites them over the tail.
Capture shaders (alphaF, pbralphaF, materialF, fullbrightF): one imageAtomicAdd + one imageStore per fragment; overflow goes to five fixed-point tail accumulators. No fragment falls through to in-place unsorted blending any more.
Resolve shader: no pointer chase, no traversal cap; sorted slots with the tail composited at the depth of its nearest fragment (alpha groups arrive far to near, so the tail is usually the nearest block; rigged alpha arrives first whatever its depth, so it can also be the farther one).
pipeline.cpp: node pool, atomic counter and SSBO budget logic removed; K = RenderAlphaOITNodesPerPixel (1..32), reduced to fit RenderAlphaOITMemoryMB. RenderAlphaOITStats now logs a real per-pixel layer histogram every 64th frame (GL 4.5 readback).
RenderAlphaOITMaxPixelLayers and its spinner removed; the other two settings relabelled. The panel keeps the PPLL name; keys and the RenderAlphaPPLLAvailable flag are unchanged.
Driver finding: NVIDIA 621.01 applies glClearTexImage after later shader atomics while the texture is still bound to an image unit. Capture unbinds image units 0/1 before the clear; the resolve leaves none bound.
scripts/perf/test_ppll_capture.py rewritten for the new layout (48 cases pass on NVIDIA). New scripts/perf/bench_alpha_oit.py times both implementations' real shaders offline, plus a no-A-buffer blending floor (used to rule out a single-layer skip: the per-pixel saving would be ~0.12 ms per full screen of single-layer pixels, less than any pre-pass that could identify them).

The capture pass no longer appends to a shared node pool through a global atomic
counter and per-pixel head pointers. Each pixel owns a fixed number of slots
(RenderAlphaOITNodesPerPixel, default 8) in an RGBA32UI 2D-array image; a
per-pixel atomic count in layer 0 of an R32UI 2D-array image hands them out, and
layers 1..5 of that image accumulate an order-independent tail (fixed-point
atomics: coverage-weighted colour, coverage, log transmittance) for fragments
past the last slot. The resolve reads at most K slots at fixed addresses,
depth-sorts them and composites them over the tail. Nothing falls through to
in-place unsorted blending any more, overflow is confined to the pixels that
overflow, memory is a fixed function of resolution and K (reduced to fit
RenderAlphaOITMemoryMB), and the pool, counter, SSBO budget check, traversal cap
and RenderAlphaOITMaxPixelLayers setting are gone.

Measured offline with scripts/perf/bench_alpha_oit.py (real shaders of both
implementations, 2560x1440, RTX 5070 Ti, NVIDIA 621.01, median of 20 frames,
reset + capture + resolve): 0.30 vs 0.42 ms at 1 layer, 1.14 vs 2.51 ms at 8
layers, 1.92 vs 3.62 ms at 16 layers full screen; 0.78 vs 3.33 ms for 24 layers
over 25% of the screen in 64x64 blocks, where the pointer chase dominated the old
resolve. The per-frame clear grows from 0.03 to 0.15 ms (84 MiB meta image).

NVIDIA 621.01 applies glClearTexImage after later shader atomics while the
texture is still bound to an image unit; capture unbinds both image units before
the clear and the resolve leaves none bound. RenderAlphaOITStats now reads back
the per-pixel counts every 64th frame (GL 4.5) and logs coverage, peak depth and
tail overflow, which is the histogram that tunes K. The preferences panel keeps
the PPLL name. scripts/perf/test_ppll_capture.py covers the new append for all
four shader families: detached depth, LEQUAL equality, occlusion, slot fill,
tail overflow and reset (48 cases pass).

The per-frame glClearTexImage of all six meta layers (84 MiB at 2560x1440,
0.15 ms) becomes a glClearTexSubImage of layer 0 (0.03 ms). The tail layers are
written only by overflowing pixels and read only by the resolve in the same
frame, which now stores zeros back after the composite, so they are clean at
the next frame start by construction. A full clear remains on allocation and
whenever a capture ran without its resolve (mAlphaOITTailDirty).

The zeroing stores sit at the end of the resolve shader: placed right after the
tail reads they made the resolve three times slower on overflowing pixels
(0.71 -> 2.16 ms at 16 full-screen layers, NVIDIA 621.01); at the end they cost
nothing measurable. They go through a separate write-only binding (image unit
2) so the loads keep their read-only image. Benchmark totals vs the PPLL now
0.34x (1 layer), 0.39x (8), 0.49x (16), 0.78x (32) full screen and 0.20x (24),
0.32x (48) on a quarter of the screen. bench_alpha_oit.py gains a "direct"
(no A-buffer) floor and scene/implementation selection through the environment.

Review of #283. Alpha groups are submitted far to near, so the first K arrivals
in the slots are the farthest layers and the overflow in the tail is usually
the nearest block; compositing the tail behind the sorted slots hid near
layers behind far ones. Each overflow fragment now also records its depth in
meta layer 6 (imageAtomicMax of the inverted float bits over the zero-cleared
layer = the minimum depth), and the resolve composites the tail between the
slots at or behind that depth and the nearer ones. Rigged alpha is captured
first whatever its depth, so the tail can also be the farther block; the depth
split covers both. Cost: one more atomic per overflow fragment (16 full-screen
layers 1.77 -> 2.49 ms, PPLL 4.08; 24 layers on a quarter screen 0.66 -> 0.79,
PPLL 3.43); non-overflowing scenes unchanged.

The "memory budget allows N of M slots" message moved below the unchanged-
allocation return: it is logged once per allocation instead of every frame.
The array allocations' GL error is read before LLRenderTarget::allocate(),
which clears the error state, so a failed slot array now falls back to legacy
alpha instead of capturing into nothing.
