FreeNeRF: Improving Few-shot Neural Rendering with Free Frequency Regularization
CVPR 2023 project
In one word: propose a simple yet intriguing baseline approach for few-shot neural rendering
Motivation:
(1) Gradually increasing the input frequency in positinoal encoding helps
related work: nerifes, Barf, Hf-neus
Inspirations:
(1) Analyze: The most common failure mode of few-shot neural rendering is overfitting. The overfitting issue in few-shot neural rendering is presumably exacerbated by high-frequency inputs.
The over-fast convergence on high-frequency impedes NeRF from exploring low-frequency information and significantly biases NeRF towards undesired high-frequency artifacts.
(2) Strategy: at the beginning, use low-frequency encoding to avoid overfitting; then Gradually increases to avoid smoothness.
(2) simple occlusion regularization: regulate the points in near-camera areas.
Limitations
Our FreeNeRF has two limitations. First, a longer frequency curriculum can make the scene smoother but may decrease LPIPS scores despite achieving competitive PSNR scores. Second, occlusion regularization can cause over-regularization and incomplete representations of near-camera objects in the DTU dataset. Per-scene tuning regularization range can alleviate this issue but we opt not to use it in this paper. Further discussion on these limitations can be found in the Appendix. Addressing these limitations can significantly improve FreeNeRF and we leave them as future work. Still, we consider FreeNeRF to be a simple yet intriguing baseline approach for few-shot neural rendering that differs from the current trend of constructing more intricate pipelines.
Thinking:
The simple occlusion regularization has already been used in DVGO codes for dealing with 360 $^{\circ}$. On the setting of few-shot DVGO, it doesn't make a big impact.
