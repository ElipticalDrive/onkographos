Generators can technically receive blocks of any size for you to fill with voxel data. Depending on how the terrain is configured, sizes may come up different.
In the case of VoxelLodTerrain, by default, chunks are generated on the fly. Instead of generating voxels for every chunk and keeping them in memory, they are generated directly at meshing time and not actually kept in memory until edited, which saves... well, memory.
That means however, that generators must provide an area that the mesher needs, which is usually a bit more than the area covered by one chunk (basically the chunk, and some neighbor voxels). It can even happen that a thin slice of 32x32x1 gets requested (or similar).
That's how this terrain works.
On the other hand, VoxelTerrain (non-LOD) works in a more "classic" way of generating all voxel chunks and keeping them in memory, so in that sense it will request chunks of same size. But then it uses more memory.

Generally I would heavily recommend your generator to be deterministic, as the engine assumes that's the case.
