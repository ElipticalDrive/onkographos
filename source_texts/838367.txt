Owner
Single-file WebGL game (downhill.html), third-person view from behind the
rider, built for phones. No dependencies, no assets — everything is
generated procedurally.
Rendering
Hand-written WebGL2 renderer with WebGL1 + ANGLE_instanced_arrays fallback
Terrain is a projected grid whose height is evaluated analytically in the
vertex shader (same function mirrored in JS for physics), so nothing has
to be uploaded per frame
Procedural sky shader: gradient, sun/moon with glow, stars, aurora and a
360° parallax mountain panorama derived from the view ray
Smooth-shaded low-poly models (ellipsoids and swept tubes) for rider,
trees, rocks and gates; instanced draws with per-instance tint
Board track, drop shadow, snow spray and falling snow as soft billboards
Day cycle (morning/day/evening/night) interpolated over distance
Gameplay
Drag to steer, tap to jump, tap in mid-air for a 360° spin that always
completes — steering and tricks never conflict on a touch screen
Gates, crystals, kickers, rocks and trees; combo multiplier and a flow
boost that fills from tricks and pickups
Difficulty scales with distance: obstacle density rises and the clear
corridor narrows
Procedural audio (wind, carving, chimes, impacts) via WebAudio, plus
haptics; best score persisted in localStorage
Balance verified by simulating the physics headless: runs last 60-180 s and
end on obstacles rather than on failed landings.

e03f81f
```
Physics: the rider was pinned to the surface, so cresting a rise at speed
just glued him to it. The ground branch now integrates vertical motion and
only re-attaches when the body actually reaches the surface, so a fast crest
launches him - 31 natural take-offs per two minutes of riding, where there
were none. Jump height is down from ~2.7 m to ~1.1 m; spin rate rises to keep

Tapping is now context-aware: with the constant small hops from the new
physics, a tap in mid-air used to start a spin that could never finish and
always ended in a crash. A tap during a low hop is a jump, and a spin only
starts when the remaining airtime covers it; landing slightly short pulls the
rotation through instead of punishing it.

Collision now accounts for object height, so trees can no longer be flown
over. To keep the run demanding, obstacles form rows across the valley with a
single gap, and crystals mark the way through.

Rider: arms are bent with hands held forward instead of the T-pose.
Crashes: limbs flail and settle, the body slides out and comes to rest, and
the board tears loose and tumbles away on its own physics.

The lean was still backwards. Rather than guessing signs again, the torso
matrix is now checked numerically: the z component of the body axis was -0.51

Rebuild the mountain: varied terrain, zones, huts and post-processing
…
e3c7cf0
```
Terrain is no longer one gorge with two walls. Slope, valley width, flank
steepness and openness all vary along the run as smooth functions of z, so the
mountain runs endlessly without repeating: gradient swings between 0.11 and
0.40, the valley between 12 and 68 m wide, from a tight gully to an open face.
The average fall line is the analytic integral of the varying gradient, so it
stays continuous.

The shimmering on the flanks was detail aliasing: the projected grid gets
coarse with distance while the height function kept its high-frequency terms.
Fine detail now fades out between 60 and 280 m, and the per-cell colour hash
that flickered with it is gone. View distance is up from 285 to 440 m so the
slope no longer ends in mid-air behind a crest, and the distant ridges in the
sky shader are taller and more defined.

Zones give the run character: forest density and grooming vary independently,
so the route passes through dense woods, open boulder fields and pisted
sections with marker poles and huts. Huts are solid.

Look: the snow is a procedural material now - wind drift, ripples, grain and
groomed lanes, lit with half-lambert, sky term, specular and rim, exposed so
the surface keeps its shading instead of blowing out to white. Rendering goes
through an offscreen buffer with a bright pass, separable blur and a composite
that adds bloom, grading and vignette, replacing the CSS overlays.
