What are Voxel Editors?
Voxel editors are computer software used for creating and editing three-dimensional models. They use small cubes, called voxels, to build objects or landscapes in a grid-like fashion. These editors often have a simple interface, making them accessible to users of all skill levels. They can be used for various purposes such as game development, architectural design, and 3D printing. Voxel editors allow for precise control over the positioning and arrangement of voxels to create detailed and intricate designs.

Voxel Editors Guide
A voxel editor is a type of software that allows users to create and manipulate three-dimensional digital models using voxels. Voxels, short for "volume elements," are 3D pixels that make up the building blocks of these models.
One major difference between voxel editors and traditional 3D modeling software is the use of voxels instead of polygons. Traditional software creates models using interconnected triangles, while voxel editors use individual cubes or blocks to form the model. This allows for more precise control over the shape and detail of the model.
Voxel editors were first developed in the late 1980s, but have gained popularity in recent years due to advancements in technology and their ease of use. They are commonly used in video game development, virtual reality applications, and even medical imaging.
One advantage of using a voxel editor is its simplicity compared to traditional 3D modeling software. The interface is often more intuitive, making it easier for beginners to learn and create complex models. Because voxels are based on a grid system, it's easy to manipulate individual blocks or groups of blocks to achieve desired shapes and designs.
Another benefit is that voxel editors typically require less processing power than traditional modeling software. This makes it possible for users with lower-end computers or laptops to create detailed 3D models without experiencing lag or other performance issues.
In addition, voxel editors offer unique features such as dynamic lighting and shadows, which can add depth and realism to models. Some tools also include built-in physics engines, allowing for realistic simulations and interactions within the created world.
There are several popular voxel editing tools available on the market today, each offering different features and capabilities. One well-known example is MagicaVoxel, which offers a free version with basic features as well as a paid version with more advanced tools.
While there are many advantages to using voxel editors, there are also some limitations compared to traditional 3D modeling software. The use of cubes to create the model can result in a blocky or pixelated appearance, making it more difficult to achieve smooth curves or intricate details. Additionally, voxel editors may not be suitable for highly realistic or detailed models.
Voxel editors are powerful tools for creating 3D models using voxels instead of polygons. They offer a more user-friendly interface and require less processing power than traditional software, making them accessible to a wider range of users. While they have some limitations, their unique features and capabilities make them valuable tools for various industries and applications.
Voxel Editors Features
Voxel editing is a process of creating and manipulating 3D objects through the use of small cube-like units called voxels. Voxel editors are tools that allow users to easily create, edit, and modify voxel-based models.
Here are some key features provided by voxel editors:
Creating/Importing Models: Voxel editors typically have tools that allow users to create 3D models from scratch using simple shapes such as cubes, spheres, cones, etc. They also support importing existing models in various file formats such as OBJ, FBX, STL, etc.
Sculpting Tools: These tools enable users to manipulate the shape and size of voxels by "sculpting" them with brushes or other shapes provided by the editor. This allows for more organic and detailed modeling compared to traditional 3D modeling software.
Painting/Texturing: To add color and texture to their creations, voxel editors come equipped with painting tools that allow users to paint on individual voxels or groups of voxels. This feature is crucial when creating realistic-looking models.
Layer Management: Similar to traditional image-editing software, voxel editors also provide layer management features where users can organize their models into different layers for easier manipulation and editing.
Animation: Some advanced voxel editors offer animation tools where users can animate their models using keyframes and timelines similar to traditional animation software.
Lighting/Shading: To make the model look more realistic or stylized, voxel editors provide lighting and shading options that allow users to control factors such as light intensity, shadows, reflections, etc.
Voxeland (Voxeland): Voxeland is a proprietary technology used in some voxel editors that enables smooth terrain generation for games or simulations. It allows for fast rendering of large-scale terrains without compromising quality.
Multiplayer Collaboration: Some voxel editors come with built-in multiplayer collaboration features that allow users to work on the same project simultaneously. This is particularly useful for teams working on game development or architectural projects.
Exporting/Rendering: Once the model is completed, voxel editors provide options to export or render it in various file formats such as PNG, JPG, GIF, etc. Some advanced editors also support exporting models for 3D printing.
Plug-ins/Extensions: To enhance functionality and add new features, voxel editors often allow for the integration of plug-ins or extensions developed by third parties. These can range from color palettes to advanced sculpting tools.
Voxel editors offer a wide range of features and tools that make them a powerful tool for creating 3D models. Their user-friendly interface and specialized functions make them an ideal choice for artists and designers looking to create unique and detailed objects in their projects.
Different Types of Voxel Editors
Voxel editors are 3D modeling tools that allow users to manipulate individual cubes or "voxels" to create complex objects. They have gained popularity in recent years due to their ease of use, versatility, and ability to create detailed and intricate designs. There are several types of voxel editors available, each with its own unique features and capabilities. Some of the most common types include:
Traditional Voxel Editors: These are the most basic types of voxel editors that allow users to manipulate individual voxels on a grid-based system. The user can add or remove voxels, change their color, rotate and scale them, and combine them to create more complex shapes.
Sculpting Voxel Editors: These editors offer more advanced features than traditional ones by allowing users to shape individual voxels using sculpting tools such as push, pull, smooth, flatten, etc. This allows for more organic and natural-looking models.
Procedural Voxel Editors: These types of editors use algorithms and mathematical equations to generate voxel structures automatically based on user-defined parameters or rulesets. This greatly speeds up the modeling process and allows for the creation of complex structures with ease.
Rendered Voxel Editors: Unlike traditional voxel editors that display simple flat colors on a grid system, rendered voxel editors offer a more realistic preview by applying lighting effects and textures to the voxels in real-time.
Multiplayer Voxel Editors: These editors allow multiple users to work together in real-time on the same project remotely through an internet connection. This makes collaboration easier and faster when working on large-scale projects.
Augmented Reality Voxel Editors: With the rise of AR technology, some voxel editing software has incorporated this feature into their platforms allowing users to interact with virtual objects in real-world settings using their devices' cameras.
Mobile Voxel Editors: As mobile devices become more powerful, there has been a rise in the number of voxel editors designed specifically for use on smartphones and tablets. These apps offer a simplified interface and streamlined features, making it easier for beginners to create 3D models on-the-go.
Game Engine Voxel Editors: Some game engines have built-in voxel editing tools that allow developers to create game assets and environments directly within the engine. This integration saves time and effort by eliminating the need to export models from external software.
Voxel editors come in many forms, each with its own unique set of features and capabilities. Whether you are a beginner or an experienced 3D artist, there is a suitable voxel editor available for your needs. As technology continues to advance, we can expect to see more innovative features being incorporated into these editors, making them even more powerful and versatile tools for creating 3D models.
Advantages of Voxel Editors
Some advantages of voxel editors include:
Flexibility in creating 3D models: Voxel editors allow for more flexibility and freedom when creating 3D models compared to traditional polygon-based modeling software. This is because voxels are essentially 3D pixels, allowing for more precise manipulation and placement of each individual voxel.
Easy to learn and use: Voxel editors usually have a simple user interface, making them relatively easy to learn and use. This makes them ideal for beginners or artists who may not have a strong background in 3D modeling.
Real-time editing: Voxel editors often have real-time preview capabilities, allowing users to see their changes immediately as they make them. This can speed up the design process and provide better visualization of the final model.
Efficient memory usage: Unlike traditional polygon-based modeling software which requires complex data structures and high memory usage, voxel editors only need to store color information for each voxel. This results in efficient memory usage, allowing for larger and more detailed models without causing performance issues.
Ideal for organic shapes: One major advantage of voxel editors is their ability to easily create organic shapes such as characters or creatures due to the natural flow of voxels. These types of shapes can be challenging to create with traditional software that relies on geometric primitives.
Texturing capabilities: Many advanced voxel editors offer texturing capabilities where users can apply textures directly onto the voxels in real-time. This allows for more intricate detailing on models without having to rely on external tools.
Accessibility across different platforms: Voxel editing software is often available across various platforms such as desktop computers, mobile devices, and even web browsers, making it accessible to users regardless of their preferred device or operating system.
Possibility for collaborative work: Since most voxel editing software allows for easy file sharing through cloud storage or project collaboration tools, it enables multiple users to work on the same project simultaneously. This feature is especially useful for teams working on large-scale projects.
Integration with other software: Voxel editors can often be integrated with other software, such as animation or game development tools, allowing for smoother workflow and more efficient production pipelines.
Low cost: Many voxel editing software options are available at a lower cost compared to traditional modeling software. This makes them a more affordable option for artists and designers, especially those just starting out.
Voxel editors provide several advantages that make them a valuable tool for creating 3D models, especially for beginners or those looking for an alternative to traditional modeling methods. With their flexibility, ease of use, real-time editing capabilities, and accessibility across platforms, they have become increasingly popular in the 3D design industry.

How Much Do Voxel Editors Cost?
The cost of voxel editors can vary depending on the specific software and its features, but typically they can range from $20 to $300. Some basic voxel editors, such as MagicaVoxel, are available for free while others like Qubicle or 3D-Coat may have a one-time fee of $60 to $150. Advanced and professional-level voxel editors like VoxelShop or VoxelMax can cost upwards of $300.
One reason for the price differences is the complexity and power of the software. Basic tools may have limited functions and tools, while more expensive options offer advanced features like animation capabilities, advanced lighting tools, and texture mapping.
Additionally, some voxel editors may require a monthly subscription instead of a one-time fee. This is often seen with web-based or cloud-based tools that allow users to access their projects from any device. These subscriptions can range from $5 to $20 per month.
It's also important to consider any additional costs that may come with using a voxel editor. For example, some tools may offer add-ons or extensions for an extra cost that can enhance your creations even further.
In addition to the initial cost of purchasing a voxel editor, it's important to factor in any ongoing expenses such as updates and upgrades. Many software companies release new versions of their tools regularly with bug fixes and added features. These updates usually come at an additional cost.
For those looking for budget-friendly options, there are also open source voxel editors available that are completely free to use. While they may not have all the advanced features offered by paid tools, they can still be powerful tools for creating voxels.
The price of a voxel editor depends on your needs and budget. If you're just starting out with voxels or don't require advanced features, there are plenty of affordable options available. However, if you need advanced tools for professional projects or more flexibility in your workflow, investing in a pricier option may be worth it in the long run.
