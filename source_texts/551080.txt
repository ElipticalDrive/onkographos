A chunk is no longer a bitmap. It is now the list of its voxels that a ray from the eye may reach. Each voxel is painted as one hexagon of one colour (a splat) on the slice's centre plane.
Why a hexagon
The hexagon is the voxel's outline seen straight along the face's axis: √2 wide, 2 high, with the rhombic dodecahedron's corners.
It is the same from every side.
Seen along the axis, the splats of a surface cover it the way its voxels do.
The shape comes from the lattice, not from a pixel grid (design principle 1).
A splat's colour is the mean of its voxel's open faces' shades. So a voxel is shaded by the way its surface faces, and it looks the same along all 12 faces. The look is impressionist rather than exact faces.
How it is drawn
Instanced, at screen resolution. The compositor draws the splats instanced, at the screen's resolution. Chunk bitmaps, their magnification and the choice of texture sampling are gone.
Painting order. Within a chunk, painting order is primitive order: a later splat covers an earlier one at the same depth (LEQUAL).
Gap filling. Grown splats are drawn at depth i + ½, so they fill only where the chunk paints nothing and still cover layer i + 1.
Clipping. A chunk draws only on its own cell. Each pixel's ray decides this, so neighbouring chunks neither overlap nor leave seams.
What stays
Bakers still fill a mask of what a chunk draws, for coverage. render_scale becomes mask_scale, and only the mask's blocks leave the worker.
The baker still bakes one chunk per eye. Face corners are no longer handed to it.
Cached chunks are counted in bytes (MAX_HIDDEN_CHUNK_BYTES; the stat is now "Cached Chunks", in MB).
Tests
bake.test.ts checks, against rays cast from the eye, that every voxel a ray meets first is painted. It also checks that each splat sits at its site's projection, far to near.
The end-to-end leak-bound test (step 8) now composites splats, and it passes with the same bound.
picture.test-helper.ts gains rasterise, a stand-in for the compositor in tests.
Not measured yet
GPU cost. Every splat in view is drawn every frame, and the forest has millions in view. Headless Chromium's software WebGL takes over a second per frame; real GPUs have not been measured. This is in docs/todo.md.
Coverage mask. The mask is coarser than the screen, so a gap narrower than a mask pixel may count as covered. Also in docs/todo.md.
Next
Project splats from the eye in the vertex shader instead of in the baker. A chunk's splats would then no longer depend on the eye, only its mask would, so moving would rebake masks, not splats. Recorded in docs/todo.md.
