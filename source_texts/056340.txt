3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as the state-of-the-art (SOTA) technique for high-fidelity, real-time novel view synthesis. However, sorting of all contributing primitives fundamentally serializes the rendering process, breaking dataflow parallelism and posing a significant implementation challenge for resource-constrained hardware accelerators. While prior sort-free algorithms based on commutative weighted sums eliminate this dependency, they introduce visual artifacts and have redundant computation. This paper introduces a geometry-guided coarse-to-fine rendering algorithm that fundamentally resolves the trade-off between sorting and computation efficiency. Our method employs a two-stage pipeline: First, a lightweight 2D Gaussian (2DGS) model is rendered via the nearest Gaussian's color. Subsequently, this depth map is utilized as geometric guidance for the second stage: a sort-free, weighted rendering of the complete 3DGS model. We further propose CODA (A Co-Optimized Depth-guided Architecture), a unified hardware accelerator co-optimized to execute this 2D-3D hybrid pipeline in a deeply pipelined dataflow. By reusing computational resources for both the 2D and the 3D Gaussian rendering pass, CODA maximizes hardware utilization. Experimental results demonstrate that CODA exhibits an 8.4× to 11.6× speedup over the NVIDIA RTX 3060M and a 2.7× to 3.9× speedup over the NVIDIA RTX 4090. When compared with previous 3DGS accelerators, CODA exhibits a 6.1× and 1.3× speedup over the GSCore and Local-GS, respectively.
