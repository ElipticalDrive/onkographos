Generalize [[probes]] (today: points and straight lines, cell averages, CSV) to any dimension with the same flexibility as the source regions of #317.
Geometry by dimension
0D: points, point lists, point clouds from a file.
1D: lines, polylines, circles and arcs, parametric curves x(s), y(s), z(s).
2D: oriented planes (rectangle, disk, annulus), sphere, cylinder and cone surfaces, parametric surfaces, boundary zones, and exact cut planes through the mesh (cell-plane intersections rather than resampling).
3D: any Source terms: GPU-native expressions, species, solution dependence, multiple sources #317 CSG region, including annular cylinders, oriented boxes, booleans and implicit f(x,y,z) < 0.
Transforms (translate, rotate, scale) apply at every dimension.
Sampling
Cell average, or high-order point values from the TENO reconstruction polynomials (order-consistent) with linear least squares as the fallback.
Optional sampling resolution (n per direction) for curves, surfaces and volumes, or the native cells and faces they cut.
Interval in steps or in simulation time.
Output
Raw samples: CSV for small probes, HDF5 or VTK (vtp for surfaces) for large ones.
Reductions over a probe: integral, area- or volume-weighted mean, mass-weighted (Favre) mean, min, max, RMS, and fluxes through surfaces (mass flow, momentum, enthalpy and species flux, with the normal convention documented).
Hook into [statistics] (More in-situ statistics: spectra, two-point correlations, phase averages #284) for time-averaged probes.
Performance
Locate on setup; evaluate on the device on owning ranks, then gather.
A probe that doesn't fire costs nothing; cost should stay small relative to a step, even for a cut plane through a large 3D mesh.
Done
Input docs and examples.
Tests:
high-order point probes converge at the scheme's order;
the mass flux through a closed surface matches the volume integral of the time derivative;
surface and volume integrals are exact for polynomial fields;
serial, MPI and GPU give identical results.
README and website sync.

Scope addition: first-class boundary data. Any boundary zone (a surface in 3D, an edge/curve in 2D) is a one-line probe (boundary = "<zone>", several zones or all walls allowed), and outputs face values at the boundary, not just adjacent cell averages:
primitive variables and composition at the face (from the boundary condition's face state / reconstruction);
wall quantities: wall shear stress vector and magnitude, skin friction C_f and pressure coefficient C_p (given reference state), heat flux, y+, u_tau, species diffusive fluxes; wall-model quantities when Wall-modeled LES (physics-based; no ML models) #256 is active;
mass, momentum, energy and species fluxes through inlets and outlets.
Output as vtp (3D) or ordered polylines with arc length (2D, CSV or HDF5), plus zone reductions (integrals, means, forces and moments consistent with [[forces]]), and time averages via [statistics].
