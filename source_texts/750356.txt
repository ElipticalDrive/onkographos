Is your feature request related to a problem? Please describe.
Volumetric media do not shadow themselves or each other. Renderer/CloudShadowMap.{h,cpp} projects a cloud shadow onto the scene (a 2D shadow texture from above), and VolumetricFogPass / FroxelFogScatter.comp scatter light through the froxel grid, but neither computes transmittance through the medium along the light direction. So a cumulus cloud is uniformly lit rather than bright on the sun side and dark underneath, and a thick fog volume shows no self-occlusion — the single most recognisable cue that a volume has depth.
Describe the solution you'd like
A volumetric shadow map: raymarch the medium from the light's point of view, accumulating optical depth into a low-resolution volume (or a 2D layered/epipolar representation), then sample that transmittance during the scattering march. Applies to both the cloudscape and fog volumes, since both already march a density field.
Describe alternatives you've considered
Analytic/hacked density-based darkening in the cloud shader — cheap but does not respond correctly to light direction and breaks as soon as two volumes overlap.
Additional context
Reference: D:\repos\Timberdoodle\src\rendering\volumetric\raymarch_volumetric_shadow_map.hlsl, alongside raymarch_clouds.hlsl, compose_clouds.hlsl, volumetric.hpp, src/shader_lib/volumetric.hlsl, shared types in src/shader_shared/volumetric.inl. Apache-2.0.
Related: #633 (closed — atmosphere/weather/volumetric clouds), #435 (froxel fog). Note docs/agent-rules/volumetric-cloud-debugging.md for the known failure modes when working in this area.
Acceptance criteria
A cloud layer shows directional self-shadowing that tracks the sun through a full day cycle.
A dense fog volume occludes light along its own depth.
Multi-angle screenshots at several times of day; no golden-test flicker from the added temporal term.
