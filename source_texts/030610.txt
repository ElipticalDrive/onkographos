Abstract—3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offers breakthrough
rendering efﬁciency, yet its massive storage overhead hinders practical deployment. To enable standardized transmission, MPEG is actively investigating the adaptation of legacy
video and point cloud coding standards for a fast-track 3DGS
compression solution. Focusing on the Geometry-based Point
Cloud Compression (G-PCC) pathway, the Region Adaptive
Hierarchical Transform (RAHT) serves as the primary tool
for compressing the high-dimensional attributes that constitute
the bulk of 3DGS data. However, the legacy RAHT design
relies on uniform quantization and limited ﬁxed-point precision,
which constrains its effectiveness for high-dynamic-range 3DGS
attributes. In this paper, we propose a standard-compatible,
rate-distortion optimized (RDO) RAHT framework. We ﬁrst
introduce a conﬁgurable precision extension to reduce numerical
errors, and then derive a rate–distortion model that allocates
bits according to the hierarchical contribution of RAHT layers.
Experimental results show that our method achieves an average
BD-rate gain of 37.87% over existing G-PCC RAHT. Moreover,
the proposed method is able to serve as an effective training-free
transform layer to enhance other 3DGS compression pipelines.
Index Terms—3D Gaussian splatting, G-PCC, RAHT, Ratedistortion optimization
I. I NTRODUCTION
Recent advancements in neural rendering have positioned
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a breakthrough technology
in the ﬁeld of novel view synthesis, offering high-quality
and real-time rendering [1]. However, its explicit representation incurs massive storage costs, restricting applications in
bandwidth-constrained scenarios like streaming and mobile
AR [2]. To address this, prior research has largely focused
on model compaction, employing techniques including structured representations [3], pruning [4], and quantization [5].
While effective, these approaches often rely solely on entropy
coding for bitstream generation, overlooking the efﬁciency of
mature coding modules, e.g., transform, that deﬁne modern
compression standards.

To bridge this gap, the ISO/IEC MPEG standardization
committee is actively investigating the adaptation of legacy
video and point cloud codecs to 3DGS [6] [7]. By incorporating complete coding pipelines, these standards offer a robust
path for “fast-track” standardization. A primary candidate
is the Geometry-based Point Cloud Compression (G-PCC)
standard. Within G-PCC, while geometry is encoded via octrees, the Region Adaptive Hierarchical Transform (RAHT) [8]
handles the attribute data. Given that 3DGS attributes (e.g.,
Spherical Harmonics) typically comprise 56 dimensions compared to only 3 for position [1], the compression efﬁciency of
RAHT dominates the overall performance.
Although recent studies demonstrate that RAHT can outperform state-of-the-art end-to-end learned attribute codecs [9],
the legacy RAHT framework is not optimized for the highdimensional, high-dynamic-range nature of 3DGS. While
MPEG targets G-PCC Amendment 1 (AMD 1) to support
3DGS, optimizations must remain backwards compatible. For
this reason, we focus on RAHT quantization, since it is a
non-normative stage and can be adjusted through the existing
layer-wise QP offset mechanism without affecting bitstream
compatibility. The current bottleneck manifests in two forms:
(1) the standard 15-bit ﬁxed-point arithmetic in RAHT transform acts as a coarse, implicit quantization ﬂoor that truncates
high-precision 3DGS attributes; and (2) the globally uniform
Quantization Parameter (QP) ignores the hierarchical sensitivity of the RAHT structure. Because these mechanisms are nondifferentiable, end-to-end neural optimization is not directly
applicable, motivating the need for an analytical and standardcompatible quantization strategy.
Therefore, this paper proposes a rate-distortion optimized
quantization framework tailored for G-PCC RAHT. We introduce a backward-compatible precision extension to mitigate
ﬁxed-point errors and derive an analytical model that optimizes
layer-wise bit allocation. Crucially, unlike neural compression
approaches that rely on expensive training or ﬁne-tuning, our
method is entirely training-free. This framework is designed to
be versatile: it can serve as a standalone codec compliant with

II. R ELATED WORK
In this section, we provide a brief overview of transformbased coding frameworks and model-centric quantization
strategies that are relevant to the proposed method.
A. The Application of RAHT for 3DGS Compression
Given the high dimensionality of 3DGS attributes, transform coding is essential for removing inter-component and
spatial redundancies. Consequently, several recent works have
adopted the Region Adaptive Hierarchical Transform (RAHT)
from the G-PCC standard, leveraging its effectiveness in sparse
3D data representation [10]. For instance, MesonGS [11] utilizes RAHT to transform attributes into coefﬁcients prior to entropy coding, signiﬁcantly enhancing compression efﬁciency.
Similarly, SizeGS [12] applies RAHT across all attribute
sets. Building on this foundation, Gallina et al. [9] establish
a specialized bitrate model for RAHT-based 3DGS coding,
achieving performance comparable to fully learned schemes.
Furthermore, Wang et al. [13] propose a hybrid approach by
ﬁrst converting Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefﬁcients into the
YUV domain before compressing them via RAHT.
However, these methods typically treat RAHT as a ﬁxed
black-box module rather than optimizing its internal mechanics. Furthermore, the current RAHT applies a globally uniform
QP across all transform layers. This rigid approach neglects
the hierarchical nature of the octree, where coefﬁcients at
different depths contribute disproportionately to the ﬁnal rendering quality. Consequently, the potential for rate-distortion
optimization within RAHT remains largely unexplored.
B. Quantization Study for 3DGS Compaction
Apart from transform coding, quantization of Gaussian
attributes has been widely studied for reducing model size.
While Elmgs [14] implements scalar quantization using
learned step sizes, codebook-based vector quantization (VQ)
approaches have gained signiﬁcant traction. For instance,
Niedermayr et al. [15] employ sensitivity-weighted k-means,
while Lee et al. [16] utilize multi-level residual codebooks
for high-ﬁdelity compression of scale and rotation. Wang et
al. [17] further reﬁne VQ by integrating entropy constraints
to optimize the rate-distortion trade-off. Additionally, FlexGaussian [18] and LightGaussian [4] demonstrate the efﬁcacy
of sensitivity-based bit allocation, selectively reducing the
precision of spherical harmonic coefﬁcients with minimal
impact on visual quality.
While effective, these techniques are typically applied as
independent pre-processing steps, prior to the actual encoding
stage. Consequently, they treat the downstream codec as a
ﬁxed module and cannot adapt different parameters of the
codec in a content-adaptive way, limiting their ability to
achieve a more optimal rate–distortion trade-off.

D. Rendering-aware QP Offset Modelling
Although Fig. 5 shows that the optimal QP offsets exhibit a
linear trend relative to the RAHT layer index, this relationship
cannot be directly generalized, as the total number of RAHT
layers varies signiﬁcantly between sequences. Therefore, we
propose a rendering-aware model to approximate the optimal
QP distribution, using the results from Eq. 11 as the ground
truth. This model is explicitly designed to account for the
error accumulation that occurs during the splatting projection,
linking the hierarchical structure of the 3D data to its ﬁnal
visual impact.
1) Intra-Sequence Modelling: The results in Fig. 5 consistently show that layers closer to the root node (with a
smaller layer index) require smaller QP values. We attribute
this to the error accumulation mechanism in 3DGS rendering.
Since the ﬁnal pixel colour is obtained by accumulating overlapping Gaussian primitives, the correlation of errors among
these primitives dictates the magnitude of the ﬁnal distortion.
Quantization errors in AC coefﬁcients at layers closer to the
root node propagate to a vast number of child primitives
with consistent signs (correlated noise). When these primitives
project to the same pixel, their errors accumulate constructively, leading to severe visual distortion. In contrast, errors in
leaf layers tend to be random in direction (uncorrelated noise),
allowing them to cancel each other out.
Accordingly, we model the shape of the layer-wise QP
offsets using N̄i , the average number of Gaussian primitives
contained by each node at layer i, which serves as a proxy for
this hierarchical impact. This relationship is approximated by
the following model:

A. Experimental Setup
Experiments were conducted on a diverse set of benchmarks, including 8 forward-facing and 12 object-centric
sequences from the MPEG Common Test Conditions
(CTC) [19], as well as the widely used Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks
and Temples, and Deep Blending datasets. For the proposed
conﬁgurable ﬁxed-point precision for RAHT, we evaluated
the ﬁxed-point precision of the RAHT weights from 15 to
20 bits to mitigate numerical errors. For the proposed layerwise QP offset model, we enabled the QP offset feature in
G-PCC RAHT and applied the proposed model to adaptively
determine the QP offset value for each RAHT transform layer.

V. C ONCLUSION
This paper proposes an efﬁcient encoding method for
3D Gaussian attributes based on point cloud codecs, which
achieves signiﬁcant model size reduction by adaptively selecting optimal QPs for different layers. By separating the analysis
of precision errors from quantization noise, we address the
speciﬁc bottlenecks of legacy RAHT for high-ﬁdelity 3DGS
data. The proposed method is highly versatile and trainingfree, as it can function as a standalone standard-compliant
codec or be combined with other pruning-based strategies.
Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method
effectively reduces storage overhead while maintaining high
visual ﬁdelity, providing strong support for the standardization
and practical deployment of 3DGS compression.
