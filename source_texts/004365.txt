Abstract
Recent advances in graphics processing unit (GPU) have enabled direct volume rendering at interactive rates. However, although perspective volume rendering for opaque isosurface is rapidly performed using conventional GPU-based method, perspective volume rendering for non-opaque volume such as translucency rendering is still slow. In this paper, we propose an efficient GPU-based acceleration technique of fast perspective volume ray casting for translucency rendering in computed tomography (CT) colonography. The empty space searching step is separated from the shading and compositing steps, and they are divided into separate processing passes in the GPU. Using this multi-pass acceleration, empty space leaping is performed exactly at the voxel level rather than at the block level, so that the efficiency of empty space leaping is maximized for colon data set, which has many curved or narrow regions. In addition, the numbers of shading and compositing steps are fixed, and additional empty space leapings between colon walls are performed to increase computational efficiency further near the haustral folds. Experiments were performed to illustrate the efficiency of the proposed scheme compared with the conventional GPU-based method, which has been known to be the fastest algorithm. The experimental results showed that the rendering speed of our method was 7.72 fps for translucency rendering of 1024×1024 colonoscopy image, which was about 3.54 times faster than that of the conventional method. Since our method performed the fully optimized empty space leaping for any kind of colon inner shapes, the frame-rate variations of our method were about two times smaller than that of the conventional method to guarantee smooth navigation. The proposed method could be successfully applied to help diagnose colon cancer using translucency rendering in virtual colonoscopy.

Introduction
Virtual colonoscopy is a computerized polyp detection method that does not require the insertion of an optical probe or contrast material into the colon. With the recent introduction of multi-detector computed tomography capable of generating 16 images in 0.5 s, computed tomography (CT) processing has become remarkably fast and the polyp detection sensitivity is enhanced [1]. While optical endoscopy can only examine along the movement direction of an optical probe, a virtual camera can move in any direction and can thoroughly examine the colon, improving the efficiency of diagnosis [2]. To support interactive virtual navigation inside the colon, it is crucial to visualize the colon interior rapidly in large colon data sets. However, perspective volume ray casting has been notorious for requiring huge computational power.
During the last decade, many acceleration methods for perspective volume ray casting have been proposed. Conventional approaches can be categorized into software-based and hardware-based methods. In software-based methods, Levoy [3] proposed early ray termination based on accumulated opacity. He also proposed an empty space leaping algorithm to skip over transparent regions of the volume by encoding information about the data's coherence in the volume. Novins et al. [4] performed ray casting in slice orders. Data processing was efficient because each slice of volume data was loaded into memory only once. However, calculating re-sampling weights required much processing time. Lacroute et al. [5] proposed perspective shear-warp rendering. A volume was transformed to a sheared object space for a perspective projection by translating and scaling each slice. The projection in sheared object space was simple and efficient. However, this method was also expensive in computing re-sampling weights. Brady et al. [6] took advantage of the coherence inherent in adjacent frames during navigation. The volume was divided into many regions according to distance from the viewpoint. They used previous rendering results for regions far from the view point, possibly yielding inaccurate rendering results. Furthermore, many parameters were determined to trade off between speed and image quality. Neubauer et al. [7] proposed the cell-based first-hit ray casting method. It was accelerated by the reduction of average ray path lengths to only a few steps per pixel. Although there were many acceleration techniques for software-based approaches, expensive computations for perspective volume ray casting could not be carried out in real-time on central processing unit (CPU).
Hardware-based methods can be divided into high-performance hardware-based and GPU-based methods. In high-performance hardware-based methods, Li et al. [8] used a slab structure to accelerate volume ray casting. The volume was divided into sets of axis-aligned slabs. This method rendered sub-volumes enclosed by the slabs, and blended the slab images. By utilizing a Mitsubishi VolumePro board, high-quality images of large volumetric data were generated in real-time. Although high-performance hardware-based approaches showed better performance than software-based approaches, they required expensive hardware for parallel processing. In GPU-based methods, Roettger et al. [9] combined volumetric clipping with the pre-integration technique to achieve high-quality volume visualizations. They increased the rendering quality by implementing efficient over-sampling with the pixel shader of GPU and the rendering speed by performing early ray termination. Krüger et al. [10] integrated early ray termination, and empty space leaping into texture-based volume ray casting on a GPU. Empty space leaping was performed at the block rather than at the voxel level. Scharsach et al. [11] used a novel ray-casting pipeline for GPUs that was optimized for the requirements of virtual endoscopy and also allowed easy incorporation of auxiliary geometry. However, empty space leaping was performed at the block level. Although conventional GPU-based rendering methods did not require any expensive hardware, its block-based technique can be further accelerated to improve the speed for the rendering of the non-opaque volume such as translucency rendering.
Translucency rendering represents a combination of attenuation-dependent transparency with color mapping. The color assignments are set to optimize the distinction between true and false polyps. Translucency rendering has been proven to be the efficient way to eliminate false lesions and decrease interpretation time greatly in three dimensional (3D) CT colonography [12]. The volume rendering of the characteristic color pattern of true soft-tissue lesions versus those that can be completely ignored is performed for 3D polyp evaluation. Then, translucency rendering improves polyp specificity and overall diagnostic confidence, especially when barium tagging of residual stool is used.
In this paper, we propose GPU-based acceleration techniques for fast perspective volume ray casting. Our method is optimized for colon data sets and shows the best performance for the rendering of non-opaque volume such as translucency rendering. Conventional methods [10], [11] performed empty space leaping at the block level. Although empty blocks can be skipped by conventional methods [10], [11], ray propagation on empty regions still occurs, because there are many empty regions in a non-transparent block of colon data sets that have many curved or narrow regions. On the other hand, our empty space leaping is calculated at the voxel level. Therefore, our method can skip a lot of empty voxels in non-transparent blocks to improve computational efficiency. In addition, we fix the number of ray propagation steps for shading and compositing and perform additional empty space searching steps between colon walls to increase computational efficiency further.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly explain the ray casting method on a GPU for perspective volume rendering and discuss the problems of conventional acceleration techniques. Section 3 provides a detailed description of our method. The experimental results are presented with a brief discussion in Section 4, and the paper concludes in Section 5.

Our method was optimized for colon data sets and showed the best performance for the rendering of non-opaque volume such as translucency rendering.
