What this adds
A third Frontier workspace for authoring planets — continents, islands, gulfs and coasts — as a signed-distance field, with a real simulated ocean. This first milestone is sculpting only, as requested; erosion, sediment beds, fractures and cryo weathering are staged in the outliner as planned layers.
No heightmap, no streaming
The planet is a function, F(x,y,z) = y − elevation(x,z), evaluated from a document of operations. A 3 200 km world saves as < 32 KB of JSON (asserted in tests). Tiles are derived on demand and evicted under a byte budget, so nothing is streamed from disk — which is what makes it work on an HDD rather than merely tolerating one.
Landform stamps: island, continent, plateau, mesa, ridge, delta, canyon, basin, strait, crater.
Sculpt brushes: raise, lower, smooth, flatten, terrace, sharpen, noise — stored as strokes, not baked.
Spectral terrain maths with a per-sample octave budget: amplitude_i = A₀·gain^i with amplitude given at the base wavelength, so slope stays constant across bands instead of stacking static at the small scales.
Stamps are windowed to reach exactly zero at their bounds, so the bounds cull never slices a visible rectangle.
The ocean is simulated, not painted
A Mei-style shallow-water solve on the SDF bed (init/flux/integrate fragment passes into half-float targets, 320²), with the domain following the camera and re-seeding from the analytic bed. Shading is Beer-Lambert absorption with per-channel extinction, suspended sediment over the shelf, Fresnel reflection of the analytic sky, two-lobe sun specular, advected whitewater and a surf band derived from bed depth. The surface normal comes from the simulated water height, not the bed — a bed-derived normal tilts the ocean to follow the sea floor, which is the classic fake-ocean tell at the shoreline.
Bugs this work found and fixed
Ocean surface normals were taken from the bed (whole ocean visually bent towards the sea floor).
The bed texture's surf channel was never written, so breaker was always 0 — the shader read a constant. It is now derived from bed depth with a shoreward-travelling pulse.
_refreshSnowLine() was called but did not exist (studio would throw on first load).
layerMetaText() was referenced by the outliner but never defined.
SKY_GLSL redeclared uSkyZenith/uSkyHorizon alongside its includers — a hard GLSL compile error.
The workspace switcher only knew two modes, so Landscape Studio was unreachable; #landscape routing and panel/overlay scoping added.
_storage was assigned after the document was loaded, so saved sessions never restored.
Brush picking used a coarse field LOD relative to the march step, landing strokes kilometres from the cursor.
Verification
114 tests pass (npm test), including: every shader stage parsed as real GLSL ES 3.0 with three.js's preamble (plus duplicate-uniform and cross-stage type agreement checks); the studio UI driven through a DOM stub (boot, frames, LOD budget, stamping, sculpting, inspector, persistence); and the document round-tripping byte-identically.
npx vite build succeeds; dev server serves all modules clean.
Offline QA renderer: node tools/render-landscape.mjs --stamps --topdown --view-span 240000.
Architecture and the port sequence: Docs/PlanetLandmassPlan.md.
Deliberately not in this PR
Erosion (next, ported feature by feature from the Terrain Studio), sediment beds, SDF fracture/crack generation, cryo weathering, and mesh baking beyond the existing OBJ export. Fractures are the open question — thin carved wedges evaluated as a subtracted distance field look like the viable route, since a surface-only approach cannot produce the undercut edges real cracks have.

Third workspace in the Frontier shell. Landmasses are authored as a
signed-distance field (kilobyte JSON document of stamps and brush strokes,
no heightmap, no streamed geometry) and derived on demand under a memory
budget, so the planet is usable on an HDD.

- core: sparse planet document, spectral noise field with per-term LOD
  budgets, memory-budgeted LRU tile scheduler, region meshing
- app: shared terrain/ocean/sky GLSL, GPU Mei shallow-water solver with
  advected whitewater and a bed-derived surf band, clipmap viewport,
  tool rail, outliner/inspector studio controller
- UI: workspace switcher reaches the studio (#landscape), panels and
  overlays scoped to lm-mode, no duplicate ocean material
- tests: 114 passing, incl. real GLSL 300 es parsing of every stage,
  DOM wiring of the studio UI and a headless workspace harness
- docs: Docs/PlanetLandmassPlan.md, README workspace section
