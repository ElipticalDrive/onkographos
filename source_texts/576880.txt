Requested by Tobias · project thread
Before: every mesh was drawn at full detail wherever it was, so a million-triangle boulder cost a million triangles even as a speck on the horizon, and scenes had to stay low-poly to stay fast.
After: dense opaque meshes (50 000 triangles or more) are drawn as GPU-culled clusters that pick their own level of detail from how big they are on screen. A 1.3 million triangle boulder looks the same up close as the full mesh and costs a few thousand triangles far away; a field of such boulders draws a fraction of its source triangles. This is step 1 and 2 of the virtual geometry plan (Tobias's "Nanite, but better" ask, engine-design/virtual-geometry.md).
How: each dense mesh gets a cluster level-of-detail hierarchy built in the background (until it is ready, and while a changed mesh rebuilds, it is drawn instanced as before). Every frame a compute pass culls the clustered instances against the camera, the shadow cascades and the sky occlusion map, picks each cluster's level by its projected error (cluster_error_px on screen, cluster_shadow_error_texels in shadow maps), drops clusters outside the view or facing away, and writes indirect draws. Clusters go through the depth prepass, the scene pass and the shadow maps with the same vertex math and StandardMaterial shading as mesh.wgsl, so AO, reflections and TAA work on them unchanged. Only storage buffers, 32-bit atomics and indirect draws, so it runs on every backend including lavapipe. Mesh data lives in shared GPU pools, so a damaged wall rebuilds only its own clusters. A NoVirtualGeometry marker component keeps a dense mesh on the instanced path (debris, fragments); DebugView::Clusters and ClusterLevels show the clusters, and render.stats reports clusters drawn.
No public struct gains a field (other crates build MeshRenderer and FeatureStats field by field): the opt-out is a marker and the cluster count comes from a defaulted RenderFeature::clusters_drawn. Next: occlusion culling against a depth pyramid.
Tests compare the 1.3M-triangle rock against the instanced path up close, check the triangle count far away, fly through level changes looking for cracks, check frustum culling, mirrored instances, the opt-out and rebuilding one changed mesh. Screenshots: engine-screenshots/rendering/virtual-geometry/. Checked locally: cargo fmt, clippy -D warnings and every crate's tests on the whole stack merged with main.

Virtual geometry: build a cluster level-of-detail DAG from dense meshes
…
00a8dd3
```
Dense meshes are split into clusters of up to 128 triangles. Neighboring
clusters are grouped eight at a time, each group is simplified to half its
triangles with its outer border locked (quadric error, vertices never
move), and the result is split into new clusters, level after level, down
to a single coarse cluster. Every cluster records its bounding sphere,
normal cone, its own error and the error of the group simplified from it.
Errors and spheres only grow toward the root, so any view picks exactly
one crack-free cut through the hierarchy.

Tests check the cluster limits, that every level covers the surface, that
cuts mixing levels stay watertight at every threshold, that open borders
and seams are kept, that errors grow monotonically, and that each level
roughly halves the triangles. Also adds Mesh::icosphere for dense,
evenly tessellated test meshes.
```

```
Opaque meshes with at least 50 000 triangles (RenderSettings::
virtual_geometry_min_triangles) are now drawn as clusters once their
cluster hierarchy has been built in the background; until then, and
while a changed mesh is rebuilt, they are drawn instanced as before.

Every frame a compute pass culls the clustered instances against the
camera, the shadow cascades and the sky occlusion map, picks each
cluster's level of detail by its projected error (cluster_error_px on
screen, cluster_shadow_error_texels in shadow maps), drops clusters
outside the view or facing away (normal cones), and writes visible
lists with indirect draws. The clusters are drawn into the depth
prepass, the scene pass and the shadow maps with the same vertex math
and StandardMaterial shading as mesh.wgsl, so ambient occlusion,
reflections and TAA work on them unchanged. Only storage buffers,
32-bit atomics and indirect draws are used, so it runs on every backend
including lavapipe.

Mesh data lives in shared GPU pools that hand out ranges per mesh, so a
changed mesh (a damaged wall) rebuilds only its own clusters; small
meshes have a build slot of their own, and meshes that change every
frame are never built. MeshRenderer::without_virtual_geometry() keeps a
dense mesh on the instanced path (debris, fragments). DebugView::Clusters
and ClusterLevels show the clusters, and render.stats reports the
clusters and triangles drawn.

Tests compare a 1.3 million triangle rock against the instanced path up
close, check the triangle count far away, fly the camera through level
changes looking for background pixels inside the rock, check frustum
culling, mirrored instances, the opt-out and rebuilding one changed
mesh. An example renders a gallery of shots.
```
