Abstract
Neural radiance fields (NeRF) offer photorealistic novel-view synthesis, but their use
for public art remains sensitive to reflective materials, uncontrolled illumination,
viewpoint gaps, and browser-side latency. This study presents a reliability-aware NeRF
workflow for sculpture reconstruction and interactive visualization. The scientific
contribution is not a new radiance-field representation; it is a visibility-weighted
cross-view supervision strategy that uses background, occlusion, and reflection masks
to reduce unreliable correspondences, together with a unified evaluation protocol
linking reconstruction fidelity, view consistency, and interaction performance. Five
indoor and outdoor artworks were captured from 65 to 120 viewpoints. Under
a common image split and camera-pose protocol, the proposed configuration
achieved a normalized geometric error of 1.20 ± 0.20%, PSNR of 28.46 ± 0.70 dB,
SSIM of 0.921 ± 0.010, and multi-view Recall of 96.4%. Exploratory paired tests across
the five samples indicated lower geometric error and higher PSNR and SSIM than
the two classical pipeline baselines (all p < 0.001), although the small purposive
sample limits generalization. The browser prototype reached 27 fps with an initial
loading latency of 1.3 s, and the 24-participant evaluation yielded an overall rating of
4.42 ± 0.34. The results indicate that the reliability-aware cross-view strategy improves
reconstruction consistency for the evaluated artworks while maintaining browser-side
interaction efficiency. Because Instant-NGP, Mip-NeRF 360, and 3D Gaussian Splatting
were not executed on the same dataset, the findings are limited to comparisons with
the standard NeRF and the two classical reconstruction pipelines.

With the rapid advancement of neural rendering technology, Neural Radiance Fields

reproduction capabilities and viewpoint continuity have garnered significant attention
in computer vision and graphics. By combining implicit volumetric representation with
multi-view image supervision, NeRF achieves photorealistic reproduction of complex
scenes without requiring explicit mesh modeling. This technology enables the faithful

As part of the urban cultural ecosystem, public art’s visibility, interactivity, and public
nature dictate that its digital reconstruction is not merely an engineering task of “replicating” physical appearance, but rather a process of media conversion and meaning
reinterpretation. A key unresolved challenge is how to maintain structural accuracy and
material-level texture fidelity while enabling smooth free-viewpoint interaction in real
exhibition and public-space environments. For sculptures and public art, reconstruction quality is affected not only by geometric complexity, but also by reflective surfaces,
changing illumination, partial occlusion, and uneven viewpoint distribution. Therefore,
introducing NeRF into this scenario requires a complete workflow that links data acquisition, image preprocessing, volumetric modeling, multi-view consistency optimization,
and browser-based interactive display. This study addresses this requirement by constructing a high-fidelity reconstruction and visualization framework for sculptures and
public art, with emphasis on both objective reconstruction metrics and user-oriented
display performance.

Although NeRF has demonstrated promising results in indoor scene reconstruction

multi-view Recall, are used to evaluate geometric fidelity, visual quality, and crossview stability. In addition, user testing is conducted to assess response fluency, detail
recognition, operational intuitiveness, and immersion. By combining objective metrics
with subjective evaluation, the experiment provides a multidimensional assessment of
whether NeRF reconstruction results can be effectively transformed into practical interactive display resources.
Given the heterogeneity of artwork forms, materials, and spatial arrangements, this
study selected several representative sculptures and public installations as experimental samples. These encompassed diverse materials such as bronze, stone, and stainless
steel, and spanned multi-scale types ranging from indoor exhibits to large-scale urban
plaza installations. For data acquisition, multi-angle image sequences were employed
alongside frame-by-frame scene annotation and occlusion handling to ensure input data
completeness and accuracy. During the modeling phase, the standard NeRF configuration and three progressively enhanced configurations were evaluated through ablation
experiments. The analysis focused on the effects of reliability masking, cross-view consistency regularization, and adaptive browser rendering on reconstruction quality, view
consistency, and interactive performance.

backbone, a novel normalization operation, or a new WebGL renderer. Its scientific
focus is a reliability-aware cross-view objective in which mask-derived visibility information reduces the contribution of occluded, reflective, and unstable background
regions. The study also links geometric, image-based, cross-view, runtime, and useroriented indicators within one controlled protocol. The browser component is treated
as a deployment layer used to test whether reconstruction quality can be retained under
interactive constraints.

2 Related work
NeRF has emerged as an influential representation paradigm for three-dimensional
reconstruction by modeling scene geometry and appearance through continuous volumetric neural functions. Compared with traditional mesh-based or point cloud–based
approaches, NeRF enables view-dependent rendering directly from multi-view images
without explicit surface reconstruction, providing strong advantages in handling complex geometries, fine texture details, and illumination variations. In recent years, NeRFbased methods have been increasingly explored across a wide range of reconstruction
scenarios, including infrastructure inspection, cultural heritage digitization, natural
object modeling, and large-scale outdoor scenes.
Early application-oriented studies demonstrated the feasibility of NeRF in structured
and semi-structured environments. Zeng et al. [1] applied NeRF to the reconstruction of
power transmission lines using progressive motion sequence images, showing improved
geometric continuity and robustness under elongated structures. In the context of cultural heritage, Mazzacca et al. [2] investigated NeRF for heritage 3D reconstruction and
confirmed its effectiveness in preserving fine surface textures and complex appearance
details. Zainuddin et al. [3] further evaluated NeRF for rock art documentation, reporting superior visual fidelity compared with traditional image-based reconstruction methods, while noting challenges related to reconstruction efficiency.
NeRF has also been examined in outdoor and natural environments characterized by
irregular structures and uncontrolled acquisition conditions. Arshad et al. [4] assessed
NeRF for plant geometry reconstruction in field scenarios and demonstrated its robustness to occlusion and morphological complexity, although sensitivity to lighting variations remained evident. Hermann et al. [5] extended NeRF to large-scale aerial imagery,
enabling landscape- and city-level reconstruction, but highlighted the substantial computational cost associated with large-scale volumetric modeling. These studies collectively indicate that while NeRF is well suited for complex outdoor scenes, scalability and
efficiency remain important challenges.
Recent research has focused on improving the semantic expressiveness and adaptability of NeRF models. Biswas et al. [6] proposed TFS-NeRF, a template-free architecture for semantic 3D reconstruction of dynamic scenes, enhancing structural flexibility
without relying on predefined models. Liang et al. [7] introduced a perceptual quality
assessment framework for NeRF and neural view synthesis methods, demonstrating
that conventional metrics such as PSNR and SSIM alone are insufficient to characterize

Recent neural-rendering research has explored reconstruction quality, rendering efficiency, and practical deployment from multiple perspectives. 3D Gaussian Splatting provides an explicit representation that supports high-rate rendering [20], while Nerfstudio

The remaining gap is therefore narrower than the introduction of NeRF to cultural
objects. Public artworks combine heterogeneous materials, changing illumination, partial occlusion, and uneven acquisition geometry, yet many application studies do not

reconstruction measures with browser interaction measurements. The present work
addresses that integration gap. It does not establish superiority over Instant-NGP, MipNeRF 360, or 3D Gaussian Splatting because those methods were not executed on the
five-sample dataset; this absence is reported as a limitation rather than concealed by the
classical baselines.
Accordingly, the study evaluates a reliability-aware NeRF configuration against two
classical reconstruction families under identical images, camera poses, and test views.
The evaluation combines normalized geometric error, PSNR, SSIM, MSE, edge sharpness, multi-view Recall, loading latency, frame rate, and structured user ratings. The
design is intended to determine whether the proposed reliability weighting improves
stability within the selected cases and whether the resulting representation remains
usable in a browser environment.
3 Materials and methods
This section describes the methodological framework adopted for high-fidelity reconstruction and interactive visualization of sculptures and public art using NeRF. The proposed approach is designed as an integrated and reproducible workflow, covering data
acquisition, preprocessing and annotation, volumetric reconstruction, and interactive
display integration. Through systematic control of input data quality, modular modeling design, and interaction-oriented output optimization, the framework establishes
a complete experimental pipeline from real-world image capture to multi-terminal
visualization.
Specifically, the workflow begins with multi-view image acquisition of sculpture and
public art samples under real-world conditions. The collected images are then subjected to standardized preprocessing and scene annotation to enhance data consistency
and training stability. Based on the processed inputs, a NeRF-based volumetric modeling strategy is employed to reconstruct continuous three-dimensional representations,

3.3 Data preprocessing and scene annotation
In NeRF-based three-dimensional reconstruction, the quality and consistency of input
data play a critical role in accurately fitting volumetric density distributions and viewdependent color fields. For sculpture and public art datasets, variations in exposure,
illumination, and partial occlusions during image acquisition can introduce significant
feature fluctuations, which may lead to unstable training and degraded reconstruction
quality. To address these issues, a dual-stage data preprocessing strategy is adopted prior
to NeRF modeling. The overall preprocessing and scene annotation workflow is illustrated in Fig. 4, highlighting the normalization, masking, and annotation steps applied
before NeRF training.

differences across cameras and lighting conditions before pose-aligned supervision.

against illumination perturbations. In addition, the color regression branch is frozen
during the early training phase, allowing the density branch to prioritize learning stable
spatial structures. This staged optimization strategy improves early convergence behavior and enhances the overall robustness of the reconstruction results.

3.6 Design of visual presentation with integrated interactive display
The browser component was constructed to test deployment feasibility rather than to
claim a new rendering engine. Reconstructed outputs were reorganized into multiresolution assets, cached through a manifest, and rendered with WebGL and Three.js.
Device load, recent frame time, current interaction velocity, and local texture complexity controlled the display level. Coarse assets were shown during rapid motion, while
higher-resolution content was requested after the viewpoint stabilized (Fig. 6).
The reconstructed NeRF models were deployed in a browser-based interactive visualization system using WebGL and Three.js. Users could rotate, zoom, inspect local
details, and trigger hotspot information through real-time interaction. The rendering

utilization, browser memory, model texture complexity, and current interaction frequency. During rotation or zooming, the system first loads lower-resolution visual tiles
to maintain smooth feedback, and then progressively refines local details when the viewpoint becomes stable. This progressive rendering strategy reduces initial waiting time
and improves interaction continuity during free-viewpoint exploration.
The front end supported rotation, zooming, local focus, and hotspot inspection. Interaction events were encoded as a compact state containing motion direction, velocity,
and dwell time. The controller used this state together with device load to select a rendering level, while the back end progressively refined the view after interaction slowed.

also inspected to identify boundary fragmentation, concave-convex loss, and occlusionrelated collapse.
The proposed configuration produced lower normalized error in each of the five

5 Discussion
The five-case results indicate that propagating scene-reliability masks into cross-view
supervision can improve within-dataset reconstruction stability. The gain should be
attributed to selective cross-view weighting rather than to normalization or WebGL
Shen Discover Artificial Intelligence
(2026) 6:1490
deployment. The ablation pattern is consistent with this interpretation: mask guidance
improves image measures, cross-view weighting increases view consistency, and adaptive rendering affects interaction latency rather than the learned field itself.
The evidence has four important limits. The artwork sample is small and purposively
selected; the geometric reference is quality-controlled photogrammetry rather than a
metrological scan; modern neural baselines were not executed on the same data; and
complete software-version, hardware, peak-memory, and baseline runtime records were
not retained. The reported paired tests therefore describe consistency among the five
cases and do not establish broad state-of-the-art performance.
The user study provides preliminary usability evidence, but its volunteer recruitment
and undocumented order counterbalancing may introduce selection and sequence
effects. Comparator-level dispersion was unavailable, which prevented inferential testing between visualization systems. A confirmatory study should preregister the protocol,
randomize system order, retain participant-level data, and report reliability measures for
the questionnaire.
Outdoor reflectance, weak texture, moving occluders, and uneven camera coverage
remain difficult. Reliability masks reduce contradictory supervision but cannot recover
surfaces that were never observed. Larger evaluations should include specular metal,
translucent materials, severe backlighting, and repeated acquisitions across weather and
time. Absolute geometry should be validated against laser scanning or another metrological reference.
Recent methods such as Instant-NGP and 3D Gaussian Splatting provide strong efficiency baselines [20, 27], while Mip-NeRF 360 addresses unbounded scenes [22]. Future
work should execute these methods under identical acquisition, pose, and test-view conditions and report quality, training time, peak memory, model size, and browser rendering cost. Such comparison is necessary before recommending the proposed workflow
beyond pilot exhibition and educational settings.
6 Conclusion
This study evaluated a reliability-aware NeRF workflow for the reconstruction and
browser-based visualization of five sculpture and public-art cases. The methodological contribution lies in using scene-reliability masks to weight pose-aligned cross-view
supervision, thereby reducing the influence of occlusion, reflection, saturation, and
unstable background content. Image normalization and browser deployment are treated
as implementation components rather than independent algorithmic innovations.
Under the reported protocol, the proposed configuration achieved a normalized geometric error of 1.20 ± 0.20%, a PSNR of 28.46 ± 0.70 dB, an SSIM of 0.921 ± 0.010, and a
multi-view Recall of 96.4%. The browser prototype reached 27 fps with an initial loading
latency of 1.3 s, while the 24-participant interaction evaluation produced an overall rating of 4.42 ± 0.34. These results indicate feasibility for the selected artworks and deployment conditions.
The evidence does not establish superiority over recent neural-rendering methods
because Instant-NGP, Mip-NeRF 360, and 3D Gaussian Splatting were not executed on
the same dataset. Broader validation should include more artwork materials, metrological reference geometry, common hardware measurements, direct modern-baseline comparisons, and counterbalanced user evaluation. The present findings should therefore be
