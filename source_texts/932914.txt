Vanishing Point
Vanishing Point
Vanishing Point: is a point in the image where a set of
any number of parallel lines in 3D appear to
disappear.
The exact location of the vanishing point in the
image depends on the orientation of the parallel
lines in 3D.
Vanishing Point
Parallel scene lines converge at a single image point
Finding Vanishing Point
Finding Vanishing Point
Finding Vanishing Point
1. Define the direction of the parallel lines using the
vector 〈𝑙x, 𝑙y, 𝑙z〉.
The origin of the coordinate frame lies at the pinhole. Therefore,
the vector 〈𝑙x, 𝑙y, 𝑙z〉 corresponds to a point P that lies on the line
that is parallel to the tracks and passes through the pinhole.
2. Project the point P onto the image using
perspective projection equations.
3. The resulting image point (xvp, yvp) is the
vanishing point for the tracks.

What is the Ideal Pinhole Size?
When the pinhole is too large,
it lets through a bundle of rays of light from each
point in the scene, which results in a disk in the
image over which light is distributed.
What is the Ideal Pinhole Size?
When the pinhole is too tiny,
It causes the image to get blurry again.
This is due to an effect in wave optics called diffraction.
What is the Ideal Pinhole Size?
The pinhole must be tiny,
but if it’s too tiny it will cause diffraction.

Chromatic Aberration
A lens is made of a certain material, such as glass or
plastic, which has a certain refractive index (bending
power).
The refractive index is a function of the wavelength
of light.
Since the refractive index depends on the
wavelength, the focal length of a lens also depends
on the wavelength.
Chromatic Aberration
Chromatic aberration is a color distortion that occurs when a lens
fails to focus all colors of light onto the same point, creating an
undesirable outline of color at the edges of subjects.
