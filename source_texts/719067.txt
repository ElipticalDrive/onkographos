Bidirectional path tracing (BDPT) connects light paths starting from both the camera and the light sources, then connects their vertices. This dramatically improves convergence for paths that standard unidirectional path tracing struggles to find — particularly through refractive objects (glass, water, etc.) and caustics (focused light through glass).
Currently the trace() function is purely unidirectional (camera → scene → lights). Light paths that transmit through glass and then hit a light source are rare, making glass objects appear dim or noisy.
Implementation sketch:
Trace paths from the camera (existing trace()): record vertices (position, normal, material, PDF)
Trace paths from each light source: record separate vertices (position, direction, throughput, PDF)
Connect each camera vertex to each light vertex with a shadow ray
Weight each connection using multiple importance sampling (MIS) to reduce variance
Light sub-paths terminate after N bounces or Russian roulette
Key benefit: paths that go camera → glass (transmit) → glass (transmit) → light are hard to find unidirectionally but natural when starting from the light side. This is what makes glass 'pop' with bright caustics and realistic highlights.
Related: area lights (issue #78) would further enhance the refractive lighting quality by providing smoother caustics.
