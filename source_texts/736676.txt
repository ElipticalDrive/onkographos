Problem
When a ray goes exactly through an edge or a vertex of the 3D mesh, several facets give the same intersection point, up to ~1e-22 m. This happens often, because sensor grid nodes and gmsh mesh vertices both tend to fall on "round" coordinates.
The previous code kept only one facet per intersection point. It then deduced the tetrahedron crossed between two consecutive points from the pair of facets (identify_tet). The kept facet was not necessarily a face of the next tetrahedron. identify_tet then returned −1, and a segment of one to a few nanometers was counted outside the material, losing its magnetization.
Example on the 40 nm test sphere, ray at y = +1 nm:
```
facet=(4,55,512)  then  facet=(4,434,1133)   → only one common vertex
seg 1: dz=1.64 nm  tet=-1                     → 1.6 nm lost
```
Fix
Clustering: intersection points closer than 1e-9 × the ray length form a single point, and all their facets are kept.
Tetrahedron located from the segment midpoint: among the tetrahedrons sharing the facets of the two end clusters, the one containing the midpoint of the segment is chosen (barycentric coordinates, tolerance 1e-6).
identify_tet and quad_to_tetId are removed. This also removes a second defect: identify_tet kept the 4 smallest indices of two facets, and for non-adjacent facets this could match an unrelated tetrahedron. A cavity could then be counted as material.

give (nearly) the same intersection point. Only one facet per point was
kept, and the tetrahedron crossed between two consecutive points was
deduced from the pair of facets by identify_tet(). The kept facet was not

Now:
- intersection points closer than 1e-9 times the ray length are grouped
  into a cluster, and all their facets are kept
- the tetrahedron of a segment is the one, among the tetrahedrons sharing
  the facets of its two end clusters, that contains the middle of the
  segment (barycentric coordinates)

identify_tet() and quad_to_tetId are removed. This also removes a false
positive of identify_tet(), which kept the 4 smallest indices of two
non-adjacent facets and could match an unrelated tetrahedron.

With a uniform magnetization, Mx_integral = path_length * Js / mu0 now
holds on every node (max relative error 3e-11, was up to 10 %). Rays that
do not go through an edge or a vertex give bitwise identical results.
