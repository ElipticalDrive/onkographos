This is indeed important to know. Personally, I’m quite addicted to the total non-destructiveness of MagicaCSG’s workflow. It makes modeling and even coloring completely adjustable, at any point in time. The drawback of that SDF approach is that it currently doesn’t allow for a sculpt-like workflow with high-level details, as @Musashidan also wrote.
In terms of speed, I haven’t been able to slow down MagicaCSG yet, not even with scenes that exist of hundreds of blended SDF objects. I’ve got a GeForce RTX 3090 Ti.
The same non-destructive SDF workflow is present in the sculpt editor of the upcoming Unbound game editor. MagicaCSG is more powerful than Unbound in the sculpting area, but Unbound has a few tricks up its sleeve, such as animation and non-destructive arrays.
I’ve been in the 3D CG biz for decades, and I’m confident that SDF will be a 3D creation force to be reckoned with. I predict that the major CAD systems will also shift towards SDF, because SDF allows you to model with complete freedom, without the geometric restrictions of NURBS, let alone polygons. Fillets, chamfers, blends, real-time Booleans, etcetera are processed effortlessly in the SDF realm.
Have a look at the initial post for a range of links, including all SDF-based tools I know of so far.
