Question
Which voxel data structures suit micro-voxels (on the order of 2–25 cm) at Planet scale, given frequent Edits, path tracing, LOD, and network replication?
Survey sparse voxel octrees, sparse voxel DAGs (including editable/hashed DAGs), brickmaps / two-level grids, 64-trees, run-length or palette-compressed chunks, and the approaches used by John Lin, Teardown, and Douglas Dwyer's voxel engine, plus other relevant prior art. For each, compare:
Memory per km² of surface at different voxel sizes
Cost of an Edit
Ray traversal performance
Natural LOD and mip support
How easy it is to stream or replicate
Produce rough memory numbers so a Voxel size can be chosen.

Resolved. Use sparse 8³ bricks: a 64-byte occupancy mask plus palette-compressed Material IDs, under a sparse top level (a hash map or a shallow 64-tree) per region. Build LOD as mip levels of bricks in camera-centred rings out to orbit. Regenerate unedited terrain from the seed, and store and replicate only Edits.
With LOD tied to screen pixels, resident GPU voxel data is about 0.2–0.7 GB whatever the Voxel size (estimate). Voxel size mainly drives full-resolution costs: Edit/physics regions, server memory and generation.
Full resolution per km² of surface (estimate, brick format): 2 cm ≈ 16–41 GB, 5 cm ≈ 2.6–6.5 GB, 10 cm ≈ 0.66–1.6 GB, 25 cm ≈ 0.1–0.26 GB. The whole Planet can never be resident.
SVDAGs compress best, but Edits are hard and Materials inflate them about 6×. Keep them as a later option for far LOD or disk. On memory alone, 10 cm is comfortable, 5 cm is feasible, and 2 cm only works if full-resolution regions stay small.
