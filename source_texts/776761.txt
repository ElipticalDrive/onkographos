Member
Adds mola::TSDF, a truncated signed distance field local map stored as a voxel hash.
Every existing map class in mola_metric_maps stores points and answers a query by selecting among them: a representative per cell, a k-NN set, a candidate list. A field map stores no points at all. The residual of a query point is the trilinearly interpolated field value at it, and the surface normal is the analytical gradient of that interpolant, so there is no correspondence search and no argmin anywhere in the query path.
It exposes mp2p_icp::NearestPlaneCapable, so Matcher_Point2Plane and Solver_GaussNewton use it unchanged -- no new matcher, no new solver, no new residual type. Adding it to a pipeline is a localmap_generator change.
What is in here
insertPoint(pt, sensorPt) fuses one measurement into a narrow cylinder around its own ray, truncated at truncation_distance either side of the surface, by a weighted running average capped at max_weight.
queryField(p) returns the interpolated value and its analytical gradient, and is valid only when all eight surrounding voxels exist and carry at least min_weight_for_query of evidence.
nn_search_pt2pl() converts that into a point-to-plane pairing: the metric distance is d/|grad d| (the stored value is a distance along the measurement ray, which is a projective distance, not a metric one), the foot of the perpendicular is the pairing point, and the normalized gradient is the plane normal.
Extent pruning and a hard voxel-count ceiling. Eviction is by distance, not by age, so the map stays a pure function of its contents and the sensor pose and does not depend on insertion order.
Two things worth reviewing closely
The tube has two knobs, not one, and that is deliberate. ray_tube_voxels is the coverage cutoff: it must reach at least half the spacing between adjacent beams at working range, or the field is undefined between them. tube_sigma_voxels is the weight profile. They have to be separate because a sample stored as its distance along its own ray over-estimates the distance to the surface for an off-axis voxel, so averaging over a wide tube pushes the zero level set away from the sensor. Measured on a synthetic plane, that displacement is -36.9 mm with a flat profile and -1.8 mm with a narrow one at the same cutoff. A unit test guards both the shipped default and the knob's liveness, so a future edit cannot quietly widen the profile back.
This class does not use mola::index3d_hash. That functor truncates the hash to 20 bits, capping it at 2^20 distinct values. That has never mattered, because a point-based voxel map holds one cell per occupied voxel and no map in this library had come near a million. A field map is the first that does: it fills a band around every surface, so at a 0.3 m voxel it passes a million voxels within seconds of driving, and past that point every new key collides. Measured end to end on one KITTI sequence: 112 GB and no trajectory with the shared hash against 0.83 GB and a complete one with a full-entropy hash. The shared functor is deliberately untouched here -- widening it reorders the iteration of every existing voxel map, and with it the summation order of anything accumulating over one, so it needs its own change with its own before/after evidence. That is a separate PR.
Testing
Nine unit tests (test-mola_metric_maps_tsdf), all passing: the field of a known plane; the surface displacement above; the analytical gradient against finite differences on a tilted plane (18 checks); the pt2pl pairing; the sign convention end to end; noise averaging (5.3 mm RMS out of 50 mm of input noise); serialization round-trip; pruning; and the voxel budget as a ceiling.
Closed-loop on KITTI 00-10 through mola_lidar_odometry, against a control that runs the same point-to-plane residual over the shipped point map (Matcher_Points_KnnPlane), so the map representation is the only difference: tilt drift 0.582x (7/7 sequences better), APE 0.846x (6/7), vertical drift rate 0.621x (6/7), translation drift 0.882x (6/6). Run-to-run determinism is byte-exact (n=4).
Known limitation, and why this is not wired into any shipped profile
At a fine voxel the map does not bootstrap: a single scan does not fill eight corners of a cell anywhere useful, so the first ICP finds no field, the local map resets, and the run never starts. On KITTI at 0.25 m this affects 4 of 11 sequences. A field map is empty in a way a point map is not -- one scan of points is immediately queryable, one scan of field is not. The follow-up is a warm-up policy that registers against an IncrementalPointCloud until the field is dense enough to answer, and switches then. Until that lands, this class is opt-in and no shipped pipeline references it.

```
A voxel-hashed TSDF, so that a scan registers against a surface fused from
every measurement that ever saw it, rather than against stored points. Each
point contributes a signed distance along its own ray to the voxels in a tube
around it; a query interpolates the field trilinearly and takes the
interpolant's gradient as the surface normal.

The class implements mp2p_icp::NearestPlaneCapable and answers with the plane
through the query point's own projection onto the zero level set, so
Matcher_Point2Plane and Solver_GaussNewton consume it unchanged: no new
matcher, no new solver, no new residual.

Two options control the ray tube, and they are separate on purpose. The
cutoff radius is a coverage knob: it has to reach half the beam spacing at
working range, or the field is undefined between beams. The Gaussian profile
width is a bias knob: a sample is stored as its distance along its own ray,
which over-estimates the distance to the surface for a voxel the ray passes
to one side of, so a wide flat tube pushes the zero level set away from the
sensor. Measured on a synthetic plane, that displacement is 37 mm at a
1.5-voxel flat tube and 1.8 mm at the same cutoff with a narrow profile.

Unit tests cover the field and its gradient against a known plane, the
pt2pl pairing, the variance reduction from fusing noisy measurements
(7.6x at 50 mm of measurement noise), the surface displacement above, the
serialization round trip and the moving-window prune.

TSDF: pin the sign convention end to end
…
705f2ef
```
The solver's point-to-plane term is a vector residual whose direction reverses
across the plane, so the map has to carry the field's sign into the optimizer,
not just its magnitude. The unsigned distance the matcher returns is only the
acceptance gate and never reaches the solver.

Checked at both sides of a ground plane: the emitted plane's own signed
evaluation reproduces the query height with its sign, and the normal points
towards free space on both sides. Unlike a plane fitted by PCA, whose normal
sign is arbitrary, the gradient of a signed field is consistently oriented; a
flipped normal would leave the squared cost unchanged while sending the step the
wrong way, so nothing but a test catches it.

…thing

The extent alone is not a memory bound. At a fixed extent the voxel count still
scales as the inverse cube of the voxel size and with how much surface the scene
puts inside it, and a run that diverges sweeps an arbitrary volume. Measured on
kitti-05 at a 0.3 m voxel, the point-map extent default peaked at 110 GB, and a
0.25 m voxel at an 80 m extent reached 52 GB and was still climbing when it was
stopped by hand.

max_voxels is a ceiling on the count: once exceeded, the effective radius is
shrunk until the map fits. The eviction is by distance rather than by age, so it
stays a pure function of the map contents and the sensor pose and does not
depend on insertion order, which the determinism floor needs. Default 0 (off),
so nothing changes for an existing configuration.

TSDF: use a full-entropy voxel hash
…
dff2d97
```
mola::index3d_hash truncates the spatial hash to 20 bits, capping it at 2^20
distinct values. That is ample for a point-based voxel map, which holds one cell
per occupied voxel at a decimetre-to-metre cell size, and no map class in this
library had come near it. A field map is the first that does: it fills a band
around every surface at a fine voxel, so it passes a million voxels within
seconds of driving.

Past that point every new key collides with an existing hash and the container
degrades. Measured on a synthetic drive: a flat 51 bytes per voxel and a flat
insertion rate up to 1.0M voxels, then neither the memory nor the time completes
another doubling. End to end on kitti-05 at a 0.25 m voxel that is 112 GB and no
trajectory, against 0.83 GB and a complete one when the count is held under the
hash's capacity. With the full 64-bit mix, 50 bytes per voxel and a flat
insertion rate hold to 4M voxels and 0.25 GB.

The shared functor is deliberately left alone. Changing it would alter the
iteration order of every existing voxel map, and with it the summation order of
anything that accumulates over one, which is a corpus-wide A/B and not a
drive-by fix.

TSDF: warm up on points, then switch to the field
…
22c797b
```
A field map is empty in a way a point map is not. One scan of points is
immediately queryable; one scan of field is not, because a trilinear query
needs all eight surrounding voxels and adjacent beams are more than a voxel
apart at working range. So at a fine voxel the first registration found no
surface anywhere, the caller discarded the map as a failed start, and the
run never got going: at a 0.25 m voxel, 4 of 11 KITTI sequences emitted
between 3 and 5 poses.

Queries are now answered by a plane fitted to the points accumulated so far,
while the field fills in behind them, and the map switches over as soon as
the field can answer. Readiness is measured, not assumed: after each scan
the field is queried at a decimated copy of that scan's own points, and the
warm-up ends when the valid fraction reaches the configured threshold. The
measurement happens between scans, so the answer a query gets never depends
on how far through a scan it arrived.

The warm-up is bounded. If the field still cannot answer after
bootstrap_max_scans, the switch happens anyway and says so: a map that
quietly stayed a point cloud under this class's name would misreport what
was being measured.

The two paths agree. On a plane they place the surface within the field's
own tube displacement of each other, which a test pins, so the switch does
not move the residual under the optimizer.

TSDF: set the warm-up defaults to the ones that actually start
…
0755ee6
```
The first parameterization was a guess and it was a bad one: it rescued the
four KITTI sequences a 0.25 m voxel could not start, and broke three others
that had been running end to end. Measured on the sequences that fail, the
two knobs turn out not to be separable. Lengthening the warm-up alone leaves
the run at 393 and 141 poses of 801 and 271; loosening the plane-fit gate
alone is worse than doing nothing at all, at 3 and 2; doing both completes
every sequence at its full frame count, 11 of 11.

The reason is that the first registrations need *many* pairings more than
they need good ones. A poorly fitted plane is diluted by the others in the
same solve, while a rejected one removes its information outright, so a tight
planarity gate starves exactly the scans that have the least to spare. Once
the warm-up runs long enough for the accumulated cloud to be dense, the gate
has nothing useful left to reject.

TSDF: the warm-up plane fit must not throw on a degenerate neighborhood
…
9f35cab
```
`mrpt::math::getRegressionPlane()` throws when its points are collinear. A
k-NN neighborhood over the first scans can easily be a single scan line, so
on a handheld corpus this fired almost immediately, and the exception
escaped the matcher: the caller logs it as fatal and discards every
subsequent observation. Nine of thirteen sequences produced a single pose,
and no combination of the warm-up knobs made any difference, because the
failure was never about the knobs.

The fit now uses the same principal-component primitive every other map
class in this library uses, which reports degeneracy instead of throwing.
A neighborhood whose second eigenvalue is negligible against the largest has
no second spread direction and therefore no tangent plane, and is skipped.

The planarity gate moves with it: the smallest eigenvalue is the variance
across the fitted plane, so `bootstrap_max_plane_deviation` is now the RMS
distance of the neighborhood from that plane rather than the largest single
one. That is the more robust statistic and it comes out of the decomposition
for free.

A test pins the case: a map built from a single straight line of points must
answer with no pairing, not with an exception.

TSDF: the warm-up degeneracy guard must test degeneracy, not planarity
…
d19a054
```
Requiring the second eigenvalue to be a thousandth of the largest is a
planarity test, and it rejected most of what a car's first scans offer: ten
nearest neighbors in a decimated cloud frequently lie along one scan ring,
elongated but perfectly usable. With those gone the solver saw too few
pairings to move, reported no motion, and the caller therefore never created
a keyframe -- so the map was updated twice in a 271-scan sequence and the
trajectory froze after two poses.

The guard is now numerical only, which is all it was ever for: it exists so
that a neighborhood with no second spread direction does not produce an
arbitrary normal. How planar a usable neighborhood must be is what
`bootstrap_max_plane_deviation` decides, and that is a knob rather than a
constant because the answer differs by sensor.

TSDF: restore the plane fit that starts, and harden the warm-up inputs
…
444e3b4
```
The regression fit is back, with its one real defect guarded rather than
worked around: it throws on collinear points, which a k-NN neighborhood over
the first scans genuinely can be, since LiDAR samples are far denser along a
scan ring than across rings. That is a "no plane here" answer and is now
returned as one. Replacing the fit outright had changed the normals it
produces, which cost several sequences that had been starting.

Two hardening fixes from review, both real:

An observation carrying no points said nothing about the field, yet still
counted as a warm-up scan. A run of empty observations could therefore
exhaust the warm-up and discard the fallback cloud before any real scan
arrived.

The scan limits were read as signed and cast to unsigned, so a negative value
wrapped to a huge bound and left the warm-up retaining every scan it saw. All
three counts are now clamped before conversion, the neighbor count to the
three points a plane needs.
