Abstract
Machine learning combined with geometric reasoning is a promising approach for generating new perspectives of a scene using limited image captures, known as neural rendering techniques. Neural radiance fields (NeRF) represent complex 3D scenes by learning the radiance implicit function and have shown impressive performance in various applications. However, the influence of NeRF's parameters on its overall performance remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we conduct an extensive grid search on NeRF's multi-layer perceptron (MLP) parameters to examine their impact on image quality. We analyze the results by varying parameters related to the MLP architecture, such as hidden layer count and neurons per layer. Our findings reveal that the choice of MLP architecture significantly affects image quality. Our proposed approach outperforms NeRF's default settings on challenging scenes, including synthetic and real-world datasets. These results shed light on the impact of different parameters on NeRF's performance, assisting practitioners in selecting suitable settings for their specific applications.
