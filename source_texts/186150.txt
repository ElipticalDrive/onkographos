I am looking for a formula for the volume Vi(r,L)Vi(r,L) of the intersection between a sphere of radius rr and a cube of edge LL with the same center.
For r<L2r<L2, ViVi is simply equal to the volume of the sphere:
Vi(r,L)=43πr3 r<L2Vi(r,L)=43πr3 r<L2
For r>3√2Lr>32L, it is equal to the volume of the cube:
Vi(r,L)=L3 r>3–√2LVi(r,L)=L3 r>32L
For L2<r<2√2LL2<r<22L, it is equal to the volume of the sphere minus six times the volume of a spherical cap. The result is
Vi(r,L)=43πr3−π4(2r−L)2(4r+L) L2<r<2–√2LVi(r,L)=43πr3−π4(2r−L)2(4r+L) L2<r<22L
However, for 2√2L<r<3√2L22L<r<32L I have not been able to think of an easy way to calculate Vi(r,L)Vi(r,L).

As John Hughes notes, you can fix the side of the cube or radius of the sphere and work in terms of the ratio. The strategy here is to fix the cube's edge length to be 22, calculate the area of plane slices parallel to one face of the cube, then apply Cavalieri's theorem.
Theorem: The intersection of the square [−1,1]2[−1,1]2 with the disk of radius ρρ centered at the origin has area
A(ρ)=⎧⎩⎨⎪⎪πρ2πρ2−4ρ2arccos(1/ρ)+4ρ2−1−−−−−√40≤ρ≤1,1<ρ<2–√,2–√≤ρ.A(ρ)={πρ20≤ρ≤1,πρ2−4ρ2arccos⁡(1/ρ)+4ρ2−11<ρ<2,42≤ρ.

Since you speak of an "easy way" to compute the volume in this third case, I can only say this: I don't think that there is an easy way. My thoughts and (modest) initial efforts to solve this follow, but they don't finish with an actual formula, so read on only for inspiration, not a solution.
First, fix the sphere radius rr at 11. You'll get an answer U(L)U(L) in terms of LL, but for any (L,r)(L,r) pair, the answer will just be
Vol(L,R)=r3U(L/r)Vol(L,R)=r3U(L/r)
so that simpler answer will implicitly provide the more complex one
The "interesting case" is when the corner of the cube "pokes out" of the sphere, but any two adjacent "poking out" bits don't poke out far enough to reveal the entire edge between them on the outside of the sphere.
To address this case, I want to rotate the whole problem, so that one vertex of the cube lies at P=c(0,1,0)P=c(0,1,0), where c=3–√L/2c=3L/2, and one edge of the cube lies in the yzyz plane with z>0z>0, i.e., θ=0θ=0 in polar coordinates (where y=1y=1 is the north pole, where ϕ=0ϕ=0, and at (0,0,1)(0,0,1) we have θ=0,ϕ=π/2θ=0,ϕ=π/2, just to establish the coordinates I'll be using.
Now looking down on the north pole, we see the three edges of the protruding corner of the cube; in polar coordinates, they lie along the planes θ=0,θ=2π/3,θ=4π/3θ=0,θ=2π/3,θ=4π/3. I'm going to compute the volume VV of one of the three "wedges" of this protruding piece.
The volume of the whole protrusion will be 3V3V, and the volume of all protrusions will 24V24V, so the total cube-sphere intersection will be the cube volume L3L3, with these protrusion subtracted, i.e.,
U(L)=L3−24V.U(L)=L3−24V.
So what is VV? It's an integral, in polar coordinates, of the radius-function h(θ,ϕ)h(θ,ϕ) for the cube minus the radius function for the sphere, which is just the constant 11. The limits of the integral are
0≤θ≤2π/30≤θ≤2π/3
and
0≤ϕ≤???0≤ϕ≤???
To figure out that missing bound, I'm going to write the equation, in polar coordinates, for the cube-face that lies between θ=0θ=0 and θ=2π/3θ=2π/3, i.e., the face on which xx is always positive, and which contains c(0,1,0)c(0,1,0).
The unit normal to this plane is
n=⎡⎣⎢⎢⎢⎢12√13√16√⎤⎦⎥⎥⎥⎥n=[121316]
The plane equation is then
X⋅n=L,X⋅n=L,
When you convert this to polar coordinates, you get a rather messy expression for the radius of the vector XX, which is what I've called h(θ,phi)h(θ,phi). Setting this messy expression to r=1r=1 determines the curve of intersection between the cube-face and the sphere, which you can solve for ϕϕ in terms of θθ, getting some expression
ϕ=q(θ)ϕ=q(θ)
And now
V=∫2π3θ=0∫q(θ)ϕ=0s dθ.V=∫θ=02π3∫ϕ=0q(θ)s dθ.
V=∫2π3θ=0∫q(θ)ϕ=0(h(θ,ϕ)−1)J(θ,ϕ) dϕ dθ.V=∫θ=02π3∫ϕ=0q(θ)(h(θ,ϕ)−1)J(θ,ϕ) dϕ dθ.
where JJ is the appropriate Jacobian (sinϕ?cosϕ?sin⁡ϕ?cos⁡ϕ?) for this set of polar coordinates.
