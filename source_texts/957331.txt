Variable voxels were close to invisible: a thin unshaded rim in the 2D editor, and nothing at all in the two real-rendering 3D styles. This makes them a visible, lighter, see-through region of the same hue in both GUIs, and since the work exposed how uneven the legacy piece colours are in brightness, replaces the palette with one built at matched lightness, with a dark-background set for the Qt GUI. The commits, by topic:
Variable voxels drawn as a see-through layer (165fafaa, a47aa027, 1511eb02)
2D editor: a variable cell was an inset square leaving a thin rim of background, too subtle to notice. It is now the whole cell in a lighter tint of the same hue, on a milder chequerboard so the tint stands out. The five per-grid drawVariableTile() implementations go away.
3D view, edge-line and STL styles: the edge-line style only showed the voxel style's black marker, and the STL style (whose chamfer mesher takes the filled voxels) drew nothing for variable voxels. Both styles now mesh the variable voxels as a solid of their own and draw it as one translucent layer with the nearest-surface rule the solver view uses for fading pieces: variable voxels hide each other (nothing piles up through several of them), the fixed voxels and the background show through the region, and fixed voxels in front hide it. The tint keeps more of the hue in 3D than in the editor, since half of it is lost to the alpha. The voxel style and gridline mode are unchanged.
The edge-line style draws a thin, faint line between voxels across a flat face, with the full line kept for the shape's edges.
The Qt GUI (4be44872, abbf9c2f, 561be062, and the two above)
The same look in its flat and STL styles: the variable region as one see-through body in the renderer's nearest-surface mode, the STL variable solid translucent, thin lines between voxels and full lines at the shape's edges (the flat style used to outline every voxel face). The piece colours follow the theme: Theme::updateDark() switches to a dark-background set, the shapes list and status table re-emit their colour roles and the viewport rebuilds its mesh.
Piece colours from Okhsl, at one apparent brightness (7ea714ac, a1f1a677, 09e746c3)
The legacy table was the sRGB primaries and their 0.6 mixes, so blue sat at about a quarter of yellow's perceptual lightness and every shade derived from a shape's colour came out differently per hue. The base colours are now built in Okhsl at full saturation from eight anchor hues at matched lightness (red 25°/0.50, brown 55°/0.55, mustard 100°/0.60, green 155°/0.50, cyan 235°/0.60, blue 265°/0.50, purple 305°/0.50, magenta 340°/0.55). The first eight shapes take them in the table's old order (blue, green, red, cyan, mustard for yellow, magenta, then purple and brown); the next eight are the hues halfway between neighbouring anchors, the next sixteen the quarter points, and so on, interpolating hue and lightness with saturation kept at 1. Copies of a shape nudge hue and lightness instead of jittering RGB channels. src/uicore/okhsl.cpp is Björn Ottosson's reference conversion (MIT); test_ui_palette pins the anchors' hex codes, the luminance band, and that no colour repeats in the first 64 shapes. A dark-background set of the same hues (lighter, for the Qt GUI's dark theme) sits behind setPieceColorsForDarkBackground().
Verified: just build, just test-all, just test-regression (20/20), cppcheck on the touched files, just build-release (-Werror) and just test-release, and the Qt GUI's test suites against Qt 6.9; all render styles of both GUIs and the 2D editor checked by eye on CubeInCage.

…in lines between voxels

Variable voxels are "less there" than fixed ones, so they are now a
lighter tint of the piece's hue (variableTint, halfway and a bit to
white) rather than a darker shade, and in the 3D views see-through at
50 % (kVariableAlpha). They are drawn as one translucent layer with the
nearest-surface rule the FLTK solver view composites fading pieces
with: variable voxels hide each other, so nothing piles up through
several of them; the fixed voxels and the background show through the
region; and fixed voxels in front of it hide it. In the FLTK view the
variable mesh moves from the opaque pass into a layer of its own
(variableLayer); in the Qt view the flat and STL styles use the
renderer's nearest-surface mode, the variable region is one body
without inner faces, and the STL style's variable solid is translucent.

The flat styles draw a thin, faint line between two voxels across a
flat face again, with the full line kept for the shape's edges: the
Qt shader carries a kThinOutline edge offset, the FLTK edge-line style
a second, 1-pixel pass over the coplanar edges.

The 2D editor draws variable cells in the same tint, and its
chequerboard is milder (editorDarkShade / editorLightShade, 0.95 in
place of 0.9) so the tint stands out from it.
