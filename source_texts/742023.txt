rayMeshIntersectAll( mp, Line3d, ... ) decides every ray-triangle intersection exactly, in integer coordinates of the segment's ends and the triangle's vertices, with simulation of simplicity. But the boxes it used to clip the ray and to cull triangles were only one float ulp larger than the triangles (computeFaceBox expands leaf boxes by one ulp). Near zero coordinate, one float ulp is smaller than one step of the integer grid, which has about 2^31 steps along the largest dimension of the mesh's box. This broke the count in two ways:
The segment is the ray clipped by the root box. If a face lies in the plane of the box, the clipped end landed exactly on that face in integer coordinates. Simulation of simplicity then put the end inside the mesh, and the crossing of that face was lost. For the cube [-1,0]^3, every ray along X got 1 intersection instead of 2.
A box could be culled although its triangle touched the segment in integer coordinates. A ray passing 1e-12 from a vertex at zero coordinate lost the crossing near that vertex.
Both cases give an odd number of crossings of a closed mesh. They break everything that decides inside/outside by this count:
makeInsideMeshVolume and the distance volumes with SignDetectionMode::OddCrossings: every voxel inside the cube [-1,0]^3 came out outside;
signedDistanceToMesh with OddCrossings;
findDisorientedFaces.
Case 1 needs a face in the plane of the bounding box near zero coordinate, as in the cube [-1,0]^3, but not in [-0.5,0.5]^3 or [0,1]^3. Case 2 can happen near any vertex with a coordinate close to zero, so meshes centered at zero are affected too.
Now the double version works as follows:
The segment's ends are rounded to integers directly from double. Before, they were rounded to float first, which could move them by many integer steps.
All boxes, both for clipping the ray and for culling, are expanded by two integer steps. Rounding to the nearest integer moves the ends and the vertices by at most half a step each, so in integers they can come closer than in double by at most one step. The second step covers the errors of computations in double, which stay far below a step while the ray starts near the mesh.
The intersection point is computed from the same integer coordinates by the new findTriangleSegmentIntersectionPreciseInt. findTriangleSegmentIntersectionPrecise now calls it and returns the same results as before.
Every hit accepted by the precise test is reported. The segment is already limited by [rayStart, rayEnd], and the former check of the distance, computed from the hit point rounded to float, could drop a hit near a range end, e.g. on the face where a ray starts. So the distanceAlongLine of a hit at a finite range end can be slightly outside the range.
The float version is unchanged.
Tests:
MRMesh.MeshIntersectAllPrecise checks both cases. On master it finds 1 intersection instead of 2 in each case. It also checks that rays starting exactly on a face give the same parity in both directions.
MRMesh.findTriangleSegmentIntersectionPreciseInt checks the new function.
Local checks, not committed:
120,000 lines through vertices and edge midpoints of closed meshes, or 1e-17 to 1e-8 of the mesh size away from them, over two cubes and a marching-cubes mesh placed centered, ending at zero and far from the origin. All lines now cross the meshes an even number of times. On master, 4,208 lines got an odd count, 6 of them on the centered mesh.
Cubes in 9 placements: makeInsideMeshVolume and signedDistanceToMesh with OddCrossings are now correct for all of them. On master, 5 placements were wrong, all with a face at a max coordinate of 0 or 1e-4.
makeInsideMeshVolume still reproduces the voxels of marching-cubes meshes exactly, also in negative coordinates and far from the origin.

This rayStart/rayEnd check is now redundant in the precise branch, and it can only do harm. The integer segment is already cut to [rayStart, rayEnd] (through s and e), so doTriangleSegmentIntersect has already decided whether a hit lies in the range. The check repeats that decision with t computed from the float-rounded hit point, so near a segment end it can drop a hit that the exact test accepted, and the number of crossings becomes odd.
In practice this matters only with a finite range and a hit within float rounding of a range end, e.g. signedDistanceToMesh with OddCrossings for a query point within float rounding of the surface (rayStart = 0). makeInsideMeshVolume passes an infinite range, and findDisorientedFaces skips its own face anyway.
Suggestion: drop the check in the precise branch and always report the hit, so the count follows the exact test alone.
