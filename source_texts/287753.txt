Neural Implicit Surfaces with Frequency-Adaptive Positional Encoding
Yan Xing, Yongxin Wu, Cheng Yang, Xiaonan Luo*, Fang Li and Jiale Hong

Abstract: As the Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have achieved significant success in view synthesis,
some works attempt to apply volume rendering to 3D reconstruction task. However, these methods use
positional encoding with uniform frequency for the whole scenario during training, ignoring different
regions of the scene and different training stage. Therefore, we aim to design a frequencyadaptive positional encoding. The adaptivity here refers to two points: first, it is adaptive to scene
regions, meaning different regions of each scene have different frequency of positional encoding.
Second, it is adaptive to the training process, meaning frequency changes during training to adapt to
the convergence of the network. To achieve this adaptivity, we use a neural network to learn the
frequency field of scenes, that is, using the network to predict the required frequency for each point
during the training process. The introduction of the frequency field allows each scene to obtain an
adaptive positional encoding, enabling the neural network to learn the geometric information of scenes
in a more flexible and efficient manner. Experimental results show that our approach produces more
accurate surfaces compared to baseline methods, especially in scenes with complex geometry.

4.1
Datasets
Following previous work, we select 15 scenes
from the DTU dataset to evaluate our method. Each
scene consists of 49 or 64 images with a resolution of
1600x1200 pixels. The scenes in the DTU dataset
contain real objects with varying shapes and materials,
enabling us to test our method’s performance on realworld scenes. Since most scenes in the DTU dataset
have relatively smooth surfaces, we further select six
scenes from the NeRF synthetic dataset. Each scene in
the NeRF synthetic dataset consists of 100 images with
a resolution of 800x800 pixels. The scenes in the NeRF
synthetic dataset exhibit more intricate geometries,
allowing us to evaluate our method's capability in
reconstructing high-frequency details. For both
datasets, we utilize the provided camera poses.

Table 1 displays a quantitative comparison of our
method with several baseline methods on the DTU
dataset (15 scenes). We report the Chamfer distance as
a metric for evaluating reconstruction surface accuracy,
where lower values indicate better performance. The
results demonstrate that our method outperforms the
baseline methods (NeuS, VolSDF and HF-NeuS),
achieving superior numerical results on most scenes
and on average. It is worth noting that Geo-NeuS
currently represents the state-of-the-art method in
surface reconstruction quality. The performance gap
between our method and Geo-NeuS primarily arises
from their utilization of multiple additional constraints
(e.g., photometric consistency and point cloud
supervision). Since our work focuses on the innovation
of adaptive frequency encoding, we did not incorporate
these constraints, leading to slightly inferior
performance in complex geometric regions compared
to Geo-NeuS. However, our approach demonstrates
clear advantages over HF-NeuS, which is the most
directly comparable method aligned with our research
direction (both leveraging frequency optimization
approaches based on NeuS). This validates that our
adaptive frequency prediction strategy better balances
detail reconstruction and stability compared to fixedfrequency methods. Future work may explore
integrating our frequency adaptation framework with
the strengths of Geo-NeuS.

Ablation Study
We investigate the impact of initializing the
learnable parameter s in Eq. (8) to different values on
the reconstruction results. As illustrated in Fig. 6, it is
evident that initializing s to a lower value enables our
method to capture the intricate surface details of
objects, whereas using a higher initial value results in
overly smooth surfaces. This is because excessively
high initial value of s impedes the gradient
backpropagation in the frequency network, causing the
adaptive positional encoding to remain at lower
frequency levels throughout the training process.
Ultimately, we initialize s to 1 to ensure that our
method can effectively capture the complex geometry
of objects.
4.5
Limitation
Despite the overall improvements, our method has
an inherent limitation in specific scenarios. Notably, on
smooth object surfaces (e.g., Scene 63 in the DTU
dataset), the reconstruction quality deteriorates
compared to the baseline method. This issue arises
because the learning framework misinterprets
illumination-induced color variations on these surfaces
as geometric changes. In such cases, our frequency
Tsinghua Science and Technology
network inadvertently exacerbates the problem by
assigning higher-frequency positional encoding to
these regions. Consequently, the network learns
spurious geometric fluctuations caused by lighting
effects instead of the true smooth surface. A key focus
for future work is to address the challenge of
disentangling geometry from appearance (color)
changes induced by illumination during adaptive
frequency encoding.

In this paper, we propose a frequency-adaptive
positional encoding for multi-view neural surface
reconstruction. Our method employs a neural network
to predict the frequency of positional encoding for each
point, enabling it to adapt to both the scene and the
training process. This adaptability allows our
reconstruction method to accurately represent both the
high-frequency and low-frequency geometric features
of the scene. Compared to methods that use positional
encoding with a fixed frequency, our model produces
more precise reconstructions in scenes with complex
geometry. One limitation of our approach is that the
introduction of the frequency network increases the
training time and complexity. Furthermore, the
optimization of the frequency network lacks additional
constraints, which may lead to suboptimal frequency
assignment for positional encoding in certain regions of
the scene. A promising direction for future work is to
integrate our adaptive positional encoding with fast
reconstruction methods, aiming to enhance efficiency
for real-world application.
