Abstract
Volumetric datasets have already been used in multiple domains. Recent improvements in acquisition devices have boosted the size of available datasets. We present an out-of-core algorithm for iso-surface extraction from huge volumetric data. Our algorithm uses a divide and conquer approach that divides the volume and processes every meta-cell sequentially. We combine our approach with a dual surface extraction algorithm in order to build adaptive meshes. Our solution produces patches of adaptive meshes that can finally be combined to generate a manifold and closed surface. As our approach processes only a part of the volume in-core, with a minimum of redundancy, it can handle very big volumes by modifying the meta-cells size to fit to the in-core memory available. Moreover, our algorithm can be parallelized in order to boost processing times and increase its interactivity. We present examples of the application of our solution to huge segmented volumes.

Introduction
Iso-surface extraction is one of the most active research fields in computer graphics. It consists in the generation of a polygonal approximation for the surface of an implicit or indicator function F(x) that represents an object V inside a domain Ω. An indicator function F(x) is defined as follows:F(x)=1ifx∈V0otherwiseThe Discrete Indicator Function (DIF) is a widely used data representation in many domains. For example, in medical imaging, a DIF can be extracted from the segmentation of magnetic resonance (MRI) or tomography images (TI). A segmented volume can be seen as a DIF where all the voxels whose centre is inside V are part of the volumetric representation of V as introduced by Stelldinger et al. [1]. The discrete surface ∂V∗ contains the voxels that are intersected by ∂V creating a volume representation of its surface that has to be well formed in the sense of Latecki [2] in order to produce a manifold surface.
However, volumetric representation is not always suitable for visualization or numerical simulation. For these applications, it is more desirable to obtain a polygonal approximation that can be more compact and easy to process. In addition, recent improvements in capture techniques allow scientists to obtain datasets of several million voxels. This size makes it impossible to load them entirely in the memory. This has boosted the development of all kinds of multi-resolution data structures such as octrees, and kd-trees, in order to limit the data kept in memory. However, when datasets are bigger than available memory in the computer, these approaches have to be completed by out-of-core and streaming strategies to only load the necessary part of the dataset into the memory. These algorithms benefit from the spatial coherence of geometric datasets and can be combined with preprocessing steps to organize and optimize data access.
In this article, we propose an out-of-core surface extraction method for binary volumes. Binary volumes can be seen as a DIF where voxels inside the surface can be clearly identified. This can also be the case of grayscale volumes if the iso-value is fixed in advance. Our solution is able to handle very large datasets and it can extract geometrical information contained in the volume in order to generate a more precise and geometrically optimal reconstruction. In addition, it guarantees the generation of an adaptive and closed manifold surface at any level of resolution.
In this article, we have been focused on a one-shot extraction of an adaptive surface for a fixed iso-value q and a specific curvature threshold δ. Our method is not intended to support queries for various (q,δ) parameters mainly because the subdivision process of the octree data structure is guided by the topology and geometry of the volume produced by a specific iso-value. A change of iso-value can potentially compromise all the coherence of the cell subdivision in the octree. This can require a partial or total re-computation of the subdivision data structure.

Our entire solution is based on a divide and conquer strategy combined with a dual iso-surface extraction algorithm. The input of our algorithm is a binary volume considered as a regular grid of voxels. Voxels can be inside or outside the volume of interest V depending on theirs values. An accurate coarse-to-fine out-of-core outlier removal method is proposed for large-scale indoor point clouds by mining the geometric shape constraints. In coarse processing stage, a low-resolution point cloud (LPC) is obtained using random downsampling. LPC has the same density distribution as the raw point clouds (RPC), which is important information for outlier removal. The correspondences from the LPC to the RPC are also recorded. The outliers in the LPC are removed via a global threshold. The outliers in the RPC are roughly removed guided by the cleaned LPC. In refinement processing stage, the cleaned LPC is segmented into planar and non-planar segments; and the LPC segmentation is transferred to the RPC. Finally, the outliers in each RPC segment are removed elaborately via a local threshold by exploring the shape information. The experiments show that the proposed method improves the quality of outlier removal results.
