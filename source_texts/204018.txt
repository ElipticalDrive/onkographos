Implements Plan 1 (docs/performance/01-barnes-hut-repulsion.md), on top of Plans 2 (#99) and 3 (#98).
Why
Repulsion was 85–98% of every step at N ≥ 512 and cleanly quadratic: 48 ms at N=1024 against a 50 ms tick budget, 810 ms at N=4096. Plans 2 and 3 only moved the constant factor; this is the algorithmic change.
What changed
New pure src/Octree.ts, added to PURE_MODULES. Structure-of-arrays over pooled Float64Array/Int32Array, rebuilt each step from the frozen pre-step positions, allocating nothing steady-state. Cells hold centre, half-extent, charge total and centre of mass, or a leaf bucket sliced out of a shared body permutation. Near-coincident points stop at barnesHutMaxDepth and stay a bucket, so build always terminates.
Traversal applies the exact pairwise law inside a leaf and mass × magnitude at the cell's centre of mass for an accepted cell. The magnitude law now lives once in src/Kernel.ts, shared by the exact kernel, the per-node reference and the octree.
Self-exclusion is explicit: the walk tracks whether a cell contains the body being evaluated and never accepts such a cell, so no opening angle can make a node repel itself.
Below barnesHutMinNodes (64) the exact pairwise kernel still runs, unchanged, so demo-scale behaviour is bit-for-bit.
K gains barnesHutTheta 0.5, barnesHutMinNodes 64, barnesHutMaxDepth 28. The README complexity section no longer claims there is no spatial subdivision.
The benchmark gains per-order mean/max force error against the exact reference, plus an opening-angle table. test/support/physics.ts gains the shared seeded sparse-graph fixture.
Findings worth reviewing
The plan's θ bound is wrong and unnecessary. "d ≤ √3·half, so s/d ≥ 2/√3" assumes the centre of mass is at the cell centre; it can sit at the opposite corner, so a θ-only guarantee needs θ < 1/√3 ≈ 0.577. Rather than rely on a tighter θ, the traversal excludes the containing cell explicitly, which is exact for every angle. barnesHutTheta is still clamped to the plan's 2/√3 ceiling as a sanity bound. The plan doc records this.
test/forces.test.ts needed no edit. The plan expected its exactness and pair-count tests to change; because N=8 stays on the exact side of the crossover, they keep their original meaning. The crossover itself is now pinned by a dedicated test.
The error metric is normalised by the graph's mean exact force magnitude, not per-node. A body near a force balance has an exact magnitude near zero and dominates a per-node ratio while its absolute error is tiny (one such body pushed a max above 15% at N=256).
