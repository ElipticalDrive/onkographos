In the high-stakes world of cinematic visual effects (VFX), fire remains one of the most difficult elements to simulate convincingly. While traditional fluid dynamics solvers in software like Houdini or FumeFX offer total control, they require significant compute time and technical expertise. Generative AI has emerged as a powerful alternative, yet many professional artists encounter a common frustration: "cardboard fire." This phenomenon occurs when the AI generates flames that lack depth, appearing as flat, two-dimensional cards rather than a three-dimensional, volumetric energy source.
For professional filmmakers and creative agencies using Hailuo AI as a virtual studio, the goal is not just a "cool video" but a high-fidelity asset that integrates seamlessly into a professional pipeline. To achieve this, we must move beyond generic prompting and understand how to constrain the AI’s probability envelope using physics-based terminology and strategic workflow integration.
01The Physics of Volumetric Realism
To generate fire that feels "heavy" and integrated, we must first understand what makes fire look real to the human eye. In traditional computer graphics, we refer to this as volumetric density. Fire is not just a light source; it is a collection of glowing particles (soot) and gases undergoing rapid chemical change.
One of the most frequent mistakes we see in the VFX community is prompting for "fire" without specifying its physical behavior. This often results in a lack of turbulence—the chaotic, swirling motion caused by the interaction of hot gases and the surrounding air. When we model our prompts, we lean on the principles of convective updraft. In our internal testing and observation of professional outputs, we have found that fire generated without environmental context defaults to a "floating" look.
By anchoring the fire to a specific fuel source—such as a "wooden log fire in a stone hearth"—we provide the AI with the necessary constraints to calculate how the flames should lick around the fuel and how the smoke should rise.

02Prompt Engineering for Fluid Dynamics
To achieve cinematic fire in Hailuo AI, your vocabulary must shift from aesthetic adjectives to physical descriptors. We have found that layering terms for turbulence is the single most effective way to eliminate the "flat" look. Instead of "burning," we use "billowing," "roiling," or "swirling."
The Importance of Heat Haze and Embers
Real combustion creates a refractive index change in the air. This "heat haze" is a critical visual cue for the audience. Without it, the fire looks like it was "pasted" onto the scene. Similarly, embers provide a sense of scale and wind direction.
According to research into Real-time Procedural Volumetric Fire, the visual complexity of fire is largely determined by the interaction between the flame front and the surrounding atmosphere. In a generative context, we simulate this interaction by including atmospheric cues in our prompts.
Key Parameters for High-Fidelity Fire:
Fuel Source: Define what is burning (e.g., gasoline, dry wood, magnesium).
Turbulence: Use "roiling" for heavy fuel or "billowing" for large-scale fires.
Atmospheric Interaction: Include "heat haze distortion" and "convective updraft."
Scale: Specify if it is a "candle flame" or a "forest wildfire" to set the motion frequency.
We often observe that when users omit the scale, the AI may apply the motion physics of a small flame to a large-scale explosion, creating a "miniature" effect that ruins the cinematic immersion. This observation is based on common patterns from our technical support and community feedback (not a controlled lab study).
03 — Workflow IntegrationThe "Black Pass" Strategy
Hailuo AI is a powerful generator, but for professional VFX, it is rarely the final stop. To maintain ultimate control over the composite, we recommend a "pass-based" approach. Rather than trying to generate a perfect composite of fire and your live-action plate in one go, we generate the fire as a separate element.
Generating for Compositing
By prompting for fire against a "solid black background" or "neutral nocturnal environment," you create an asset that is much easier to isolate. In post-production software like Nuke or DaVinci Resolve, you can use a "Screen" or "Add" blending mode to composite this fire onto your footage.
This workflow allows you to:
Adjust Color: Fine-tune the temperature of the flames to match the plate’s lighting.
Control Opacity: Dial back the fire’s intensity without re-generating.
Mask and Track: Better integrate the fire behind foreground objects.
Based on our experience with professional VFX pipelines, generating 3-5 variations of the same prompt is standard. This allows the artist to select the "take" with the most organic movement and the least amount of AI-generated artifacts.

04Consistency and the "Reroll" Reality
In professional video generation, "one-shotting" a perfect result is a myth. The McKinsey report on the Economic Potential of Generative AI highlights that the true value of GenAI lies in its ability to accelerate the iterative process. For a VFX artist, this means using Hailuo AI to rapidly prototype different "looks" for a fire effect before committing to a final render.
We suggest using an Image-to-Video (I2V) workflow for maximum consistency. Start by creating a high-quality static image of your fire (using a tool like Midjourney or DALL-E) and then use that as the reference frame in Hailuo AI. This ensures the color, fuel shape, and lighting are locked in before the motion is calculated.

05Governance, Ethics, and Standards
As generative AI becomes a staple in the creative economy, adhering to emerging standards is vital for agencies and studios. The EU Artificial Intelligence Act is set to introduce requirements for transparency in AI-generated content. For VFX professionals, this means being prepared to disclose the use of synthetic elements in commercial productions.
Furthermore, aligning with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework helps studios manage the risks associated with bias and "hallucinations" in generated media. By treating AI as a "Virtual Studio" component rather than an autonomous creator, you maintain the human-in-the-loop oversight necessary for professional-grade reliability.
According to the Stanford HAI AI Index, the adoption of generative tools in the creative industry is accelerating, but the "trust gap" remains a hurdle. We bridge this gap by prioritizing physical logic and workflow transparency over "magic" shortcuts.
06Mastering the Virtual Studio
Achieving realistic combustion in Hailuo AI requires a shift in perspective. You are not just a "prompter"; you are a digital cinematographer and a physics supervisor. By using concrete parameters—like focal length, volumetric density, and convective updraft—you constrain the AI to produce results that respect the laws of nature.
Remember that the most successful VFX are those the audience never notices. By generating fire as isolated passes and integrating them into professional software, you leverage the speed of AI with the precision of traditional VFX. This hybrid approach is the hallmark of the modern "Virtual Studio."
Final Checklist for Volumetric Fire
Fuel Source: Is it dry wood, gas, or chemical?
Turbulence: Did you use "roiling" or "billowing"?
Atmosphere: Is there heat haze and rising embers?
Background: Is the background neutral enough for a clean composite?
Iteration: Have you generated at least 3 variations to find the best motion?
