Goal
Render Gaussians with the image formation model 3DGS actually uses. The Blender adapter currently renders them as lit, opaque, hard-edged solids, so every image splat render produces has the wrong colours and the wrong geometry.
Background
adapters/render/_blender_script.py builds a ShaderNodeBsdfPrincipled with Roughness=0.5, wires sh_dc-derived colour into Base Color and opacity into Alpha, and main() adds a SUN light at energy=2.5. Each Gaussian becomes an IcoSphere of radius 1.0 scaled by its linear sigma, at subdivisions=1 (a 42-vertex icosphere).
3D Gaussian splatting is emissive volumetric alpha compositing: no lights, no BRDF, no specular, and each kernel has an exp(-0.5 * r^2) falloff integrated out to roughly 3 sigma.
Measured consequences (see docs/pipeline.md §9, with rendered images in docs/images/): input frames of bright pastel Voronoi texture on white render as dark iridescent glass shards. Every colour in the output is a lighting response rather than the Gaussian's own colour. A 1-sigma hard ellipsoid at subdivisions=1 reads as discrete faceted polygons rather than continuous surface, and the kernels are roughly 3x too small.
_blender_script.py is 165 statements at 0% test coverage, which is why this shipped.
Blender GPU selection is already correct and verified live (scene.cycles.device = "GPU" with the Metal device enabled) - this issue is only about shading and kernel geometry.
Scope
Replace ShaderNodeBsdfPrincipled with ShaderNodeEmission (colour = Col, strength 1.0) plus alpha from pt_opacity, and remove the SUN light entirely.
Scale kernels to cover the Gaussian's real support (roughly 2.5-3x sigma) rather than 1 sigma.
Address the hard edge: either raise subdivisions and accept an opacity gradient approximation, or instance a camera-facing billboard carrying a Gaussian-falloff alpha texture (cheaper and closer to a real rasterizer's output).
Make the world background configurable (--background <hex>|transparent) instead of inheriting --factory-startup's grey.
Remove the dead "CPU" term in _enable_gpu_compute - device.type in (backend, "CPU") and device.type != "CPU" simplifies to device.type == backend; the behaviour is right but the expression is misleading.
Surface Blender's stdout/stderr under a verbose flag; it is currently captured and discarded on success, so there is no way to confirm from splat which device rendered or how long it took.
Add a timeout to subprocess.run in adapters/render/blender.py.
Add a golden-image test: render a fixed set of ~100 synthetic Gaussians at low resolution and compare against a committed PNG within tolerance. This is the smallest thing that would have caught the current state.
Notes
Prerequisite for making #81 (apple/Sharp) actually presentable - a correct reconstruction still renders wrong today. Pairs with the camera-controls issue.
