3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) has recently emerged as a break-through in photorealistic 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis in real time. However, it struggles to faithfully capture mirror-like reflections and refractive effects because of the limited expressiveness of low-order spherical harmonics, which fail to model high-frequency reflective properties. Existing extensions of 3D-GS address this issue through planar mirror approximations, but they rely on accurate mirror mask annotations and do not generalize well to scenes with multiple reflective surfaces. We propose ReAnisoGS, a novel framework that enhances 3D-GS’s ability to model specular and refractive phenomena without requiring mirror segmentation masks. Our approach uses K-planes to construct deformed anchors and encode features, with feature decoding implemented through multilayer perceptrons. This design overcomes the expressive bottleneck of low-order SH and substantially improves the anisotropic modeling capacity. Extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements in the reconstruction quality for reflective scenes.
