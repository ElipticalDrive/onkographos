Ray-tracing is the most commonly used algorithm for rendering photorealistic graphics. How to accelerate its process has attracted lots of attention since a long time ago. This thesis proposes several techniques for efficient implementation of the tree-traversal and ray-triangle intersection test, which represent the two most critical operations in ray-tracing. For the traversal of bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) tree, this thesis proposes a dual-node intersection test scheme based on reusing boundary data inherited from the parent node such that the tree storage, on-chip stack access frequency, and the number of traversal operations can be reduced by 41%, 80%, and 50% respectively. Coupled with the proposed ray vector scaling approach, the number of multipliers required in the ray-node intersection unit design can be further reduced from six to four. The second main contribution of this thesis is to propose a more efficient implementation of ray-triangle intersection test, which only requires seven multiplication operations, while the minimum number required from the past literature was ten. By further dividing our ray-triangle intersection algorithm into two separate phases, one more multiplication can be saved coupled with about 23% less triangle-data access amount. The proposed techniques can contribute to the future development of more efficient ray-tracing system
