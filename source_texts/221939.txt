Closes #362. Part of #372 (Phase 1).
Problem
The colorize depth buffer splatted each projected map point over an isotropic disc. A circle over-culls specifically at grazing incidence: a surface seen nearly edge-on projects to a sliver whose samples pile up on screen along the tilt direction, yet every one of them stamped a full circle of its own depth and occluded its own neighbours. That is exactly the geometry a vehicle-mounted camera sees most — the road ahead, the facades running alongside.
The normals were already computed by the geometry pre-pass (ColorizeGeometry::normals) and already consumed by the incidence weight. The rasterizers simply never received them.
What changed
Splat the projected surfel — the disc of radius spacing/2 lying in the plane the point's normal defines — instead of a screen-space circle. A disc projects to an ellipse, which covers exactly the direction the samples are sparse in and stops where they are dense.
New colorize_splat.hpp holds the EWA form specialized to a pinhole:
```
C = radius_world^2 * J (I - n n^T) J^T
```
with J the 2x3 projection Jacobian. Factoring J = K/z collapses this to a handful of flops and needs no tangent basis for the surfel plane. The footprint is stored as the symmetric 2x2 C (the ellipse {q : qᵀC⁻¹q ≤ 1}), so the degenerate edge-on case stays representable and division-free; the inner loop swaps a circle test for an inverse-free quadratic form of the same cost.
Backward compatible where it matters. A {0,0,0} normal (the pre-pass's "no normal" sentinel) and an empty/mismatched normals span both fall back to the previous f_avg * spacing / 2z disc bit-for-bit. Dynamic scan splats stay circular — raw returns carry no orientation.
The cap is preserved. splat_radius_max_px now bounds the major semi-axis; the ellipse is scaled down uniformly so the cap bounds the footprint without undoing its foreshortening. The square root that needs is kept off the common path (squared comparison first) since it runs per projected point per image.
Both backends. The CUDA kernel mirrors the math in FP32, the same way its distortion kernels mirror image::distort_normalized.
BREAKING: both factories grow a normals span after spacings.
Measured effect
8-camera 4K validation bag (YuY5LyKH…mcap, 2.2 M map points, ~4700 images), RTX 3080 Ti, --backend cuda, 3 runs each:
circular (before)
elliptical (after)
uncolored map points
3219
346 (−89%)
colorize stage
65.5 s
71.6 s (+9%)
Per-camera coverage rose +0.4% to +6.5%, consistently on every one of the 8 cameras.
On cost: #362 predicted "no measurable extra cost". That is not what I measured — there is a consistent ~9% on the colorize stage. Part is real overhead (the normals are a third 12 B/point stream the kernel now reads per image, plus the rotation and C); part is simply more work, since more points now survive to be weighted and accumulated. Flagging it rather than repeating the prediction.
Tests
colorize_splat_test.cpp (new, 12 cases) — fronto-parallel reduces to the focal-scaled disc; tilt compresses by |cos θ|; edge-on collapses to a bounded segment; off-axis perspective stretch; normal-sign invariance; zero-normal fallback; major-axis clamp preserves aspect; degenerate inputs.
colorize_rasterizer_test.cpp (new, 8 cases — the CPU rasterizer had no direct test) — the headline pair is CircularFootprintOverCullsGrazingGeometry (only the nearest road sample survives) vs EllipticalFootprintKeepsGrazingGeometryVisible (all three do).
colorize_rasterizer_gpu_test.cpp — new EllipticalFootprintMatchesCpu parity case on a laterally-staggered grazing surface (exercises the off-diagonal term), asserting CPU == GPU and that the oriented set beats the isotropic one.
map_colorizer_test.cpp — SplatClosesHolesBetweenSparseOccluders rebuilt on a real 9×9 plane. Its old 5-point cloud had no valid surface: PCA over 4 coplanar points plus the far occludee put the smallest-variance axis sideways, so the front points got an edge-on normal. That is a genuine limitation worth knowing — where the neighbourhood cannot support a plane, the footprint follows a meaningless normal — but it made the test assert against garbage geometry rather than against splatting.
