Fix
Give every box-ray candidate its own interval so a miss or rejected hit cannot suppress a later intersection or corrupt an accepted hit. Null object filters accept all objects; hit filters receive complete RayHit3d candidates and retain the existing true means reject convention.
Retain only the accepted cutoff/index during the scan, then update RayHit and SetObject without replacing caller Tag/ObjectStack or allocating an empty stack. Read-only box access and inlining keep the unfiltered scan fast.
Index slicing, clipped-volume overlap and boundary/tie semantics remain unchanged: starts inside boxes report tmin, not an exit surface. Closest-point queries, other primitive sets and kd-tree traversal are untouched; successful flat-set kd-tree queries still clear their stack. XML and geometry guidance describe these contracts.
Performance
Warmed comparisons against the original source use a non-null accept-all object filter on both versions and no hit filter. Across 13 direct/kd-tree workloads with matching hit flags, indices and ray payloads (1, 64 or 4,096 boxes), elapsed medians improve 5.6–38.9%, repeating at 5.2–41.9% with longer batches. Identical-binary controls range −4.6% to +1.6%. Successful queries drop from 32 B to 0 B; misses remain 0 B. These are synthetic scan results, not a universal speedup guarantee.
Restored results are separate: the miss-before-hit reproduction changes from an incorrect miss at 15.3 ns to the correct hit at 18.1 ns (+18.1%). Reversing the boxes changes a corrupt t=2 result at 28.4 ns to correct t=4 at 18.1 ns. Neither is counted among the same-hit speedups.
