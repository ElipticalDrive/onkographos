Abstract
Recent advancements in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have established a
strong benchmark for dynamic head avatar reconstruction. However, achieving
improved physical consistency and local photorealism remains challenging, as
inherent tracking jitter often blurs high-frequency details, and the complex oral
topology frequently leads to non-physical geometric distortions. Furthermore,
standard shading networks struggle to accurately disentangle material properties for regions with distinct optical characteristics, such as eyes and teeth. To
address these limitations, we propose a 3D Gaussian head avatar framework that
explicitly targets pose jitter, intra-oral geometric instability, and specular degradation. We counteract tracking instability by integrating a Perceptually-Guided
Adaptive Pose Refinement module. This module incorporates LPIPS-driven gradients into the pose optimization loop, enhancing texture sharpness and reducing
noise-induced blur. It does so by refining the pose parameters based on perceptual quality, to achieve more accurate and stable head geometry. Addressing
anatomical plausibility, we redesign the deformation field with an AnatomicallyAdaptive Semi-Rigid Constraint, coupling rigid mandibular transformations with
soft skinning to mitigate geometric collapse in the oral cavity. Complementing
these geometric improvements, a Semantically-Aware Specular Injection mechanism is introduced during the rendering stage, leveraging semantic priors to
recalibrate material attributes for enhanced specularity in eyes and teeth. Experiments on INSTA and HDTF show improved perceptual quality and more stable
intra-oral geometry compared with prior 3DGS head avatar baselines.

1 Introduction
The creation of photorealistic, animatable digital head avatars from monocular video
sequences is a longstanding pursuit in computer vision and computer graphics, with
extensive applications in telepresence, virtual reality, and movie production. Traditional approaches often relied on explicit textured meshes or parametric models like
3DMM [1], which offer robust control but frequently lack fine-scale geometric details.
The advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [2] revolutionized the field by introducing implicit representations that achieve photorealistic rendering. Early deformable
NeRF methods, such as Nerfies [3] and HyperNeRF [4], demonstrated the capability
to handle non-rigid deformations, paving the way for head-specific methods [5–9].
However, the high computational cost of implicit sampling limits the real-time
applicability of NeRF-based methods. Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [10]
has emerged as a compelling alternative, combining the explicit nature of point clouds
with differentiable rasterization to achieve real-time rendering speeds and high-quality
visuals. Following this paradigm, recent works including GaussianAvatars [11], MonoGaussianAvatar [12], and Flash-avatar [13] have successfully integrated 3D Gaussians
with parametric face models (e.g., FLAME [14]). By attaching Gaussians to the
canonical space of the parametric model, these methods leverage the underlying geometry to drive facial deformations while learning residual details to compensate for
the model’s limited expressiveness. Additionally, approaches like Gaussian Blendshapes [15] have explored semantic control. State-of-the-art frameworks, represented
by HRAvatar [16] and GaussianHead [17], have established a high benchmark in
this domain by incorporating hybrid representations and physically-based shading to
handle complex deformations and lighting.
Despite these significant advancements, achieving micro-scale fidelity and strict
physical consistency from monocular videos remains a persistent challenge. While
existing methods appear plausible at a macroscopic level, specific artifacts become
apparent when scrutinized under high-definition conditions. A primary obstacle is the
inevitable tracking jitter inherent to monocular settings. As discussed in BARF [18],
inaccuracies in camera poses can severely blur high-frequency details. In the context
of avatars, slight misalignments in the canonical space force the network to smooth
out features like skin pores and hair strands to minimize reconstruction loss. Beyond
tracking instability, the geometric modeling of rigid anatomical structures presents
another difficulty. Widely used Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) [11, 19] treats the oral
cavity as a continuous deformable surface. As noted in IMAvatar [20], standard
implicit surfaces often struggle to represent the topological changes inside the mouth,
and similarly, Gaussian-based LBS often causes rigid structures like teeth to undergo
unrealistic stretching or geometric collapse during large jaw movements. Furthermore,
2
current PBR pipelines often struggle with material disentanglement, resulting in visually flat rendering for complex regions like eyes and teeth, which fail to exhibit the
vivid specularity and wetness inherent to biological tissues [21].
To address these issues, we propose a refined 3D Gaussian head avatar framework
that prioritizes physical correctness and perceptual sharpness. To counteract tracking noise, we introduce a Perceptually-Guided Adaptive Pose Refinement strategy.
Inspired by the joint optimization of pose and geometry in NeRF– [22], we achieve
sub-pixel alignment by embedding LPIPS-driven gradients [23], effectively recovering sharp high-frequency details. To ensure anatomical validity, drawing inspiration
from the geometric constraints in GeoAvatar [24], we propose an Anatomy-Guided
Rigid Motion Constraint. By strictly locking the motion of teeth Gaussians to the
jaw bone transformation, we effectively mitigate non-physical deformations within the
oral cavity. Finally, to further enhance visual realism, we incorporate a SemanticallyAware Specular Injection mechanism during the rendering phase, explicitly boosting
the specular attributes of eyes and teeth to restore their natural vitality. Our main
contributions are summarized as follows:
• We develop a joint optimization framework that combines Perceptually-Guided
Adaptive Pose Refinement with perceptual loss to mitigate tracking jitter, effectively
preserving high-frequency textural details.
• We implement an Anatomically-Adaptive Semi-Rigid Constraint that enforces rigid
motion for the oral cavity, ensuring geometric stability and alleviating stretching
artifacts during large expressions.
• We propose a Semantically-Aware Specular Injection strategy that enhances the
specular rendering of eyes and teeth, improving the overall material plausibility of
the avatar.
2 Related Work
2.1 Efficient Neural Head Avatars
The evolution of digital head reconstruction has transitioned from traditional explicit
meshes to implicit neural representations, and recently, to hybrid primitives. Early
approaches largely relied on 3D Morphable Models (3DMMs) [1, 14], which offer robust
semantic control but are limited by the fixed topology and low resolution of the underlying mesh. The advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [2]revolutionized the field
by modeling the head as a continuous volumetric field, enabling photorealistic rendering of complex details like hair [20, 25]. However, the implicit nature of NeRFs imposes
a severe computational bottleneck: rendering a single image requires volumetric raymarching, which necessitates querying a coordinate-based Multi-Layer Perceptron
(MLP) hundreds of times per pixel. This heavy computational load restricts most
NeRF-based methods from achieving real-time performance.
To overcome these limitations, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [10] has emerged as
a paradigm shift, combining the efficiency of rasterization with the flexibility of pointbased rendering. Unlike NeRFs, 3DGS represents the scene with explicit anisotropic
3
Gaussians, allowing for real-time inference without costly neural network evaluations during rendering. Recent state-of-the-art methods, such as GaussianAvatars [11],
MonoGaussianAvatar [12], Flash-avatar [13]and HRAvatar [16], integrate this representation with FLAME [14] to enable animatable avatars. Despite their efficiency,
these methods face a new challenge in monocular settings: sensitivity to tracking noise.
While implicit fields naturally smooth out high-frequency jitter, the explicit nature of
3D Gaussians tends to overfit to noisy camera poses [12, 18], resulting in visual artifacts that require specific refinement strategies. In this paper, we address this issue
by integrating a Perceptually-Guided Adaptive Pose Refinement strategy directly into
the optimization loop, aiming to recover high-frequency details from noisy monocular
tracking.
2.2 Geometry-Aware Facial Animation
To achieve controllable animation, prevalent 3DGS-based frameworks typically drive
the scene using Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) derived from the underlying parametric model. Since the coarse FLAME mesh lacks fine geometric details, methods like
HeadGaS [26] and GaussianAvatars [11] introduce learnable position offsets or neural
deformation fields to capture non-rigid dynamics such as facial wrinkles and subtle
expressions. However, a critical limitation in these hybrid representations is the uniform treatment of heterogeneous facial tissues. Most existing pipelines operate under
the assumption that the entire head is a continuous deformable surface governed by
smooth skinning weights. This assumption fails for anatomically rigid structures, particularly within the oral cavity. Because the skinning weights are typically smoothed
to ensure seamless transitions on the skin, teeth Gaussians driven by these weights
are often erroneously interpolated during large mandibular movements. This leads to
non-physical stretching or ”rubber-like” distortions, where the teeth appear to deform
elastically rather than moving as a rigid body. Although recent attempts like Hugs [27]
employ geometry-guided deformations to better align Gaussians with the mesh surface, they generally lack explicit constraints to enforce strict rigidity for the upper
and lower dental structures, resulting in geometric inconsistencies and texture sliding
artifacts during extreme expressions. To mitigate these geometric inconsistencies, we
propose an anatomy-guided constraint that enforces strict rigid motion for the oral
cavity, ensuring geometric stability even during extreme expressions.
2.3 Relightable Appearance Modeling
Decomposing appearance into intrinsic physical attributes (albedo, roughness, normal)
from monocular video is essential for photorealistic relighting but remains a challenging inverse problem. For static scenes, implicit neural representations [28, 29] have
successfully integrated BRDF models to enable relightable reconstruction. However,
extending these methods to dynamic head avatars faces greater difficulties due to the
lack of multi-view constraints. While recent point-based approaches like Relightable
3D Gaussian [19] and GaussianShader [30] have introduced physically based rendering (PBR) into the 3DGS pipeline, they often struggle with the ambiguity between
lighting and material properties in monocular settings. This limitation is particularly
4
evident in complex facial regions such as eyes and teeth, where specular highlights
are frequently baked into the albedo or overly smoothed, resulting in a flat appearance. In this work, rather than attempting a fully physically accurate decomposition,
we adopt a semantic-guided inference strategy to improve perceptual realism. By
utilizing semantic masks to explicitly modulate the reflectance parameters of highly
specular regions during the rendering phase, we empirically enhance the visibility of
specular highlights, mitigating the dullness often observed in purely learning-based
decompositions.
3 Method
As mentioned, previous methods for head reconstruction are plagued by blurring
artifacts caused by tracking jitter, geometric collapse of intra-oral structures due to
non-physical deformations, and the struggle to render realistic specular highlights
under novel lighting. To tackle these challenges, we first propose a Perceptually-Guided
Adaptive Pose Refinement strategy to compensate for tracking noise via learnable
residuals (Sec. 3.1). Subsequently, we introduce an Anatomically-Adaptive Semi-Rigid
Constraint to strictly couple oral geometry with skeletal transformations (Sec. 3.2).
Finally, a Semantic-Aware Specular Injection strategy is employed during inference
to enforce physical plausibility for high-specular regions, enabling realistic relighting
(Sec. 3.3). The overall pipeline is illustrated in Fig. 1.

introduce a Learnable Pose Correction Module structurally integrated into the 3DGS
pipeline Figure 2. Instead of treating camera extrinsics as immutable constants, we

geometry.

3.3 Semantic-Aware Specular Injection
The Ambiguity of Monocular Decomposition. While our framework successfully
reconstructs geometry, disentangling intrinsic material properties—specifically albedo,
roughness, and specular reflectance—from monocular video remains a fundamentally ill-posed inverse problem. Without multi-view photometric cues, self-supervised
networks inevitably suffer from parameter ambiguity [21], often ”baking” specular
highlights directly into the albedo texture. This phenomenon leads to severe artifacts
in highly specular biological regions, such as the eyes and teeth, rendering them dull,
foggy, or plastic-like under novel illumination conditions.
Physically-Grounded Specular Enhancement. To restore the dynamic vitality of biological tissues, we propose a Semantic-Aware Specular Enhancement strategy
that incorporates domain-specific physical priors into the inference pipeline. To
bypass the uncertainty of network predictions for high-frequency specular areas, we
explicitly model the dielectric properties of human biological tissues. We utilize semantic parsing masks [33] to identify specific semantic regions Msem (i.e., eyes and
teeth). For these regions, we override the network-predicted parameters by injecting
physically-calibrated priors:

4 Experiments
4.1 Experimental Setup
Implementation Details. We implement our framework in PyTorch [34] and optimize the model using the Adam optimizer [35] on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU. For
data preprocessing and initialization, we strictly adhere to the protocols established in
HRAvatar [16]. Specifically, all video sequences are cropped and resized to a resolution of 512 × 512. To isolate the avatar, we apply matting to extract the foreground and
set the background to black. Regarding initialization, we utilize pre-tracked FLAME
parameters and leverage pre-trained weights from SMIRK [36] for the encoder. The
model is trained for 15 epochs per subject.
Datasets and Preprocessing. We conducted a comprehensive evaluation on two
publicly available benchmarks: the INSTA dataset [37] and the HDTF dataset [38].
Specifically, we utilized 10 subjects from the INSTA dataset, which provides preprocessed sequences. Following standard protocols, we held out the last 350 frames
of each video for self-reenactment testing. To further assess robustness, we included
8 subjects collected from the internet-based HDTF dataset, with the final 500 frames
reserved for testing. Consistent with baselines, all sequences underwent identical cropping and background segmentation. Additionally, to facilitate our Semantic-Aware
specular injection, we employed a pre-trained face parsing network (BiSeNet [33]) to
generate pixel-wise semantic masks for all frames, specifically extracting the eye and
oral cavity (including teeth) regions to guide the material attribute optimization.
Baselines and Metrics. We conduct a comprehensive comparison with several
state-of-the-art methods, including INSTA [37], Point-Avatar [39], Flash-Avatar [40],
3D Gaussian Blendshapes (GBS) [41], and our direct baseline HRAvatar [16]. Following the official evaluation protocol of HRAvatar, all baseline methods are compared
using the quantitative results re-implemented and reported in HRAvatar under identical data splits and rendering resolution. We evaluate SRP-Avatar using the same
evaluation pipeline to ensure a fair and consistent comparison. Standard image quality metrics, including PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, are used for quantitative evaluation.
In addition, we report MAE∗ (scaled by 102 ) to measure pixel-level reconstruction
accuracy.

Visual Comparisons The visual comparison of our method with the HRAvatar
baseline on self-reenactment is shown in Figure 4. While HRAvatar generates generally plausible results, it often struggles with high-frequency tracking jitter, resulting in
blurring artifacts around detailed areas such as hair strands and skin textures. This is
largely attributed to the tracking lag inherent in monocular pose estimation, which the
baseline fails to fully compensate for. In contrast, our method, aided by Pose Refinement, effectively aligns these temporal inconsistencies, restoring sharper textures and
cleaner details. Regarding geometric accuracy, HRAvatar tends to exhibit instability
in the oral cavity during challenging poses or extreme expressions (e.g., wide mouth
openings), occasionally leading to floating teeth artifacts. Our method handles these
scenarios more effectively; the Semi-Rigid Constraint ensures the teeth remain rigidly
anchored to the jaw, preserving precise geometric structure even under large deformations. We also qualitatively compare the visual differences in relighting. As shown
in Figure 5 , due to the entangled modeling of texture and lighting, HRAvatar often
reconstructs eyes with a dull, diffuse appearance under novel lighting conditions. In
contrast, by incorporating Physics-based Material Injection during the relighting process, our method successfully synthesizes realistic eye specularity and vivid reflections,
leading to more consistent and lifelike lighting effects.

cavity are frequently assigned erroneous non-zero weights from the surrounding lips or

geometric fidelity. Qualitatively, this formulation fails to preserve the volumetric rigidity of the dentition. During extreme mouth expressions (e.g., wide opening), the teeth
undergo non-physical elastic deformation, appearing to stretch like rubber or suffering from texture sliding artifacts. Our semi-rigid constraint effectively eliminates these
distortions by enforcing a kinematic coupling between the dental geometry and the
jaw bone.

our method achieves competitive visual quality, particularly in maintaining structural
integrity and texture sharpness.
Limitations. While our approach leverages the FLAME topology to ensure robust
facial kinetics, it remains constrained by this geometric prior when modeling highly
dynamic non-facial elements; consequently, features like long loose hair or complex
accessories that do not follow the underlying head motion may exhibit reconstruction artifacts. Similarly, although our deformation field handles most articulations,
extreme expressions deviating significantly from the training distribution can still pose
challenges, potentially leading to minor geometric inconsistencies. Regarding appearance modeling, due to the additive nature of our inference-time material injection,
the superposition of physical highlights onto an albedo with residual baked-in lighting may occasionally lead to pixel saturation or compounding brightness artifacts in

highly specular regions. Furthermore, the disentanglement of complex global illumination effects, such as cast shadows from hair or self-occlusions, remains simplified,
suggesting avenues for future physics-based simulations.
