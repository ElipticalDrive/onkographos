Abstract
Today, due to the ever increasing amount of data generated by analytical instruments, good compression methods are desired to keep computation time acceptable. The lower the volume and noise content of data, the easier it becomes to investigate and interpret the modeling results. Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) is an effective data compression and noise suppression tool. Compression can be performed at different levels, in each, the size of signal part of the data reduces to half the size. This work includes an approach for determining an acceptable level of compression of data where the aim is to achieve minimal loss of information and no significant change in the structure of data, which could mean, e.g. no loss in predictive ability or the effective rank of the data-set. The method is based on estimation of the Singular Values (SVs) from a data matrix and the Singular Values at each level of compression followed by the application of Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) of the correlation between original SVs and compression SVs as a simple statistical test for the determination of the optimum level of compression. We illustrate the method using FT-Raman data from aqueous solutions of three sugars (glucose, trehalose and sucrose) and NMR data from mixtures of three alcohols. A sudden change in prediction error sum of square plots from Partial Least Squares (PLS) modeling confirms the results from MAD statistics.

Introduction
Since 1989 numerous papers have been published which cover the application of Wavelet Transforms (WTs) in various fields of chemistry, including analytical chemistry, chemical physics, and quantum chemistry [1]. Main applications of this signal processing technique are in data compression [2], data smoothing and de-noising [3], [4], baseline and background correction [5], resolution of multi-component overlapping signals [6], [7], regression and classification [8], [9], and analytical images processing [10], [11]. The method can be applied to a great diversity of data types [7], [8], [10], [12], [13], [14]. Discrete Wavelet Transformation (DWT) has been applied in combination with other pre-processing methods such as orthogonal signal correction (OSC) [15] and for calibration transfer [16]. General tutorials on the wavelet transforms and wavelet domain analysis, oriented towards the chemometrics area, can be found in literature [17], [18], [19]. Generally, wavelets try to approximate a signal as well as possible using a structured family based on the so-called mother wavelet which is localized in both position along the signal axis and in scale (window width). The analyzing mother wavelet used in this paper is the popular Daubechies-6 wavelet function, which forms an orthogonal set of basis functions.
Compression of data using DWT, as used in this study, results in a decrease in length of the signal vector and performs a noise reduction at the same time. In this way it is somewhat compatible to the application of Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on a data matrix, although the resulting data is not projected on an abstract space and a physically meaningful shape of the signal is kept. The benefits of performing a modeling operation, such as regression analysis or classification, on the relevant signal part of the wavelet coefficients are reduced computation time and possibly a better distinction between the systematic and sporadic directions of the data-set, enhancing the model complexity selection process in factor models such as PCA and PLS. But, similar to any other noise reduction method, there is often a delicate balance between removing noise and destroying valuable information. Compressing the data higher than a proper level results in a change in the data structure, which is typically undesired. Hence, we use the concept “acceptable level of compression” to indicate a safe and non-destructive data reduction.
In this work DWT with different levels of compression is applied on FT-Raman spectra from sugar solutions and NMR spectra from alcohol mixtures. The main goal of this study is to present an easy way for determination of the correct or safe level of compression using wavelets. Changes in the trend of Singular Value (SV) plots at different levels of compression are considered where Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) is employed as a simple non-parametric statistical evaluation criterion [20], [21]. Desired results were obtained from the application of the MAD statistic to determine the proper level of compression.

Conclusion
Discrete Wavelet Transformation is a very powerful method for data compression. It is easy to understand and implement and can reduce the computational time required significantly. Our work includes the application of DWT in combination with a Median Absolute Deviation statistic to determine an acceptable level of compression as an effective data reduction technique in multivariate tasks, such as calibration. MAD statistics offer a simple, quick and easy way to determine the acceptable levels

An approach is proposed and illustrated for the joint selection of essential samples and essential variables of a data matrix in the frame of spectral unmixing. These essential features carry the signals required to linearly recover all the information available in the rows and columns of a data set. Working with hyperspectral images, this approach translates into the selection of essential spectral pixels (ESPs) and essential spatial variables (ESVs). This results in a highly-reduced data set, the benefits of which can be minimized computational effort, meticulous data mining, easier model building as well as better problem understanding or interpretation. Working with both simulated and real data, we show that (i) reduction rates of over 99% can be typically obtained, (ii) multivariate curve resolution – alternating least squares (MCR-ALS) can be easily applied on the reduced data sets and (iii) the full distribution maps and spectral profiles can be readily obtained from the reduced profiles and the reduced data sets (without using the full data matrix).
