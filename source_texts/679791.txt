The hexahedral output's pillow copies and sliding skin nodes were projected
to their closest skin point. Near an edge or corner of the skin a node is
about as near two or three faces, so a face's copies went to a side face
and flattened the pillow cells: a unit box at min_size 0.05, graded, with
features off had 383 corners of zero scaled Jacobian.
Copies and sliding nodes are projected along the boundary's own normal
(closest point where that misses). Pillow nodes are inset no deeper than
0.45 of the local thickness.
Chain nodes are snapped by arc length. A node where the traced path turns
inside a quadrilateral is released and tethered within 0.08 cells, since
pinning it flattens that quadrilateral.
The untangler (robust mode) adds a trilinear-reflection candidate, 26
directions with a line search below 0.2, a capped-sum tie-break that
leaves two-node plateaus, and one restart.
A repair releases the feature nodes of cells still below 0.05.
When the result is below 0.2, the 18.0.0 fit runs too and the better one
is kept. The internal hook MESHIOPLUSPLUS_OCTREE_MESH_HEX_FIT=body_fitted
forces it, byte-identical to 18.0.0.
Minimum corner scaled Jacobian, uniform / graded:
capped cylinder: 0.003 / 0.006 -> 0.53 / 0.28
Stanford bunny: -0.16 / -0.62 -> -0.006 / 0.28 (five ear-tip cells stay
just below zero in the uniform grid; documented as open)
box with features off: no flat corner
Only the hexahedral output changes: no option, no ABI change. Tests for the
cylinder, the box with features on and off, the two-node plateau, the
boundary normals and the 18.0.0 hook digests. Roadmap 7.9.3 records
multi-material hexahedra.

Roadmap 7.9.3: output="hex" with conformity="cleaving" gives one material
per nested shell, as the tetrahedral output does. It writes the
octree_mesh:material cell data and the material_<k> regions.
- Each skin triangle belongs to the shell of the material just inside it,
  with its own closest-point query.
- For each level, the set of higher materials keeps one layer off the
  previous interface and the boundary. It is made manifold by adding allowed
  hexahedra (hex_make_manifold gains an optional mask), else by removing its
  own (hex_shrink_manifold).
- Each interface is pillowed on both sides (hex_pillow_interface), then
  fitted to its own shell. The two sides go no more than half way to their
  nearest neighbour.
- hausdorff_to_skin also measures the interfaces.
- A one-shell skin gives the same bytes as stuffing, and no option or ABI
  changes.

Roadmap 7.9.4, narrowed to 7.9.3: the robust fit no longer snaps feature
chains shorter than a cell. They are counted in warnings and in
num_feature_chains_missed. Hexahedra with a non-positive corner, uniform /
graded:
- Stanford bunny: 5 / 0 -> 2 / 0
- Happy Buddha: 684 / 486 -> 283 / 102
- XYZ RGB dragon: 1516 / 1237 -> 254 / 66
A new warning counts those lying where the skin is thinner than two cells.

Fixed: graded hexahedra dropped the dual centres within a tenth of a cell of
any skin, which opened a cavity at a nested shell. Two nested spheres reached
-0.99 in 18.0.0 and reach 0.47 now. Only the boundary's centres are dropped,
decided by winding-number probes; a centre on a skin on the lattice's planes
takes the middle of its two sides.

Single-shell outputs and every bench_ops digest are unchanged; the
determinism check agrees across SEQ and OpenMP at 1, 4 and 8 threads.
