The terrain lab had no cloud shadows, and on a large world its clouds covered only the middle. cloud_sky was one fixed 60x60x28 slab (1.28 km across in terrain_lab). Its ground shadow was a 96² bake of shade x band albedo that replaced the ground node's texture, so terrain_lab set shadow: 0. Ordinary shadows on textures are fine: buildings shade the ground through the shadow map, which dims the lighting. Only that bake replaced textures. The clouds can't use the shadow map either: they're a volume (not a caster), and the map covers 150-900 m around the camera.
What changes
Shade in the receivers' shaders, not their textures. The deck builds a small periodic mask: the cloud optical depth along the sun from each point of the deck's base plane. Every receiver reads it at its fragment's base-plane point and scales only the direct light (diffuse + specular), so textures and colours are untouched. Receivers are the ground: node and everything under it (every terrain LOD rung), plus the nodes in receivers: (terrain_lab: buildings, roads, props, foliage). New meshes, such as LOD rungs made late or a regenerated world, are picked up once a second. The shadow follows drift exactly (a translation) and morph exactly (the maps crossfade linearly, so the optical depths do too).
A deck over the whole world (cvc::world::cloud_layer, GL-free, world_cloud_test):
A few L-system cumulus maps are scattered over a periodic grid of cells: which cells hold a cloud (coverage), each cloud's offset in its cell, and a per-cell sequence of maps it morphs through.
The period is wider than the drawn area, so each cloud has exactly one copy drawn and every texture wraps.
Each map is scaled to its own peak. The grammar grows some variants nearly empty: variant 1 peaked at 0.054 of the shared normalization, so the old slab spent half of its morph cycle almost empty.
The shadow and far slices integrate the volume's transfer-function opacity. Integrating raw density cast square shadows from haze the transfer function hides.
Coverage ladder (LSYSTEM roadmap 8.4's cloud ladder, a first part):
A pool of near VolumeNodes (6 in terrain_lab, at most 8) on the clouds nearest the camera.
One mesh of 10 translucent shells for the rest. They sample a coarse opacity texture and turn it into optical depth along the view ray, so they read like the volumes.
A cloud crossfades between the two. The shells cut out the near volumes' squares by uniform, so a fade never re-composites.
The deck follows the ground's extent live (the World half slider).
GeometryNode gains:
shared shader inputs: one set (floats, vec3/vec4, mat4, textures) that many meshes read at draw time;
fragment replacements applied after VTK's own, so a receiver can rewrite the lighting sum VTK generates.
That sum is the one place both mappers keep the direct terms apart: the WebGL2 low-memory mapper scopes diffuse/specular to its lighting block and writes fragOutput0.
terrain_lab.ari:
The deck: 500 m cumulus, deck at 300-520 m, coverage 0.6, 6 near volumes, cast along the key light (az -60, el 28).
The light rig: fill / environment / ambient 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.32 -> 0.15 / 0.2 / 0.2. With more light from the sky than from the sun, building and cloud shadows washed out. These are the values from the earlier native A/B; I applied them here as part of judging the cloud shadows.
lsystem_forest.ari: horizon: 0, so the island's deck stays over the island and the camera frames it as before. The island now keeps its own band colours instead of the old 96² bake.
Checked (Windows, Release, cvcpkg prefix)
world_cloud_test 8/8, lsys_fields_test 6/6, cvcgl_ariadne_realize (new checks: the deck's near volumes and far shells exist; the sky's first tick wires its shade into the ground's shader, which held none before).
arigl snapshot of terrain_lab at 1.2 km and 11.2 km (half 600 / 5600), several cameras, shade on vs shadow: 0, and a top-down debug comparing cloud and shadow positions. Shadows sit where the sun puts them and drift with the clouds.
The WebGL2 mapper natively (CVCGL_LOWMEM_MAPPER=force): it applies the post-VTK replacement and compiles the deck's shaders. The GLSL ES compile itself is unchecked (no local emsdk).
Cost
Native, 1280x720 offscreen:
The sky's tick averages 0.4 ms/frame on both the 1.2 km and the 11.2 km world.
The shade (at most 512²) and far (at most 256² x 10) composites are spread over 8 frames, once a second. Their uploads (~0.4 ms and ~1 ms) go to double-buffered textures.
Walking the receivers for new meshes takes ~1.5 ms, once a second.
The old slab cost about the same: a 60x60x28 crossfade and a 96² bake every third frame. Browser: expect 2-3x the CPU on single-threaded wasm (~1 ms/frame, ~4 ms once a second). On the GPU: up to 6 small volume ray casts plus 10 shell layers' fill. Not measured in a browser yet.
Known and left for later (docs/roadmap/GENERATED-WORLD-ROADMAP.md, G6)
Shells streak at grazing angles near the horizon.
Near volumes are flat white beside the shells' height shading.
Clouds stop short of the horizon past the world's edge (the deck is in the scene's bounds, which the camera fits and clips to).
The receiver walk is periodic.
One top-down view showed a straight-edged cloud, likely where two near volumes' boxes overlap.
With the forced WebGL2 mapper natively, terrain_bump itself fails to compile: it picks the normal's name by __EMSCRIPTEN__, not by mapper. That predates this PR.

The five issues #1013 left:
- The far shells leave the camera's bounds (GeometryNode::setUseBounds: VTK's UseBounds and the
  scene's box) and reach far_reach (4 km) past the ground; a vertex past the far plane is drawn on
  it (clip depth clamped to 0.999 w), so the deck reaches the horizon without moving the near plane.
- Each shell marches the view ray across its slab of deck (6 samples) instead of reading one
  layer, so the slices join into one cloud seen edge-on, from below or above.
- The volumes stay unshaded (VTK's volume lighting reads as storm cloud) and share the shells'
  colour ramp: grey-blue thin, white dense.
- The grammar clamps its turtle at the grid's y edges and piled puffs against the face, which
  the window cut off straight; each map now fades out over its outer 40% radius.
- terrain_bump and bark name the normal by the node's mapper (GeometryNode::usesLowMemoryMapper),
  not __EMSCRIPTEN__, so CVCGL_LOWMEM_MAPPER=force draws the whole terrain lab natively.
