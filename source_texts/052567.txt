Related issue: #34991
Description
#34991 showed that a sphere resting on the floor exhibits light bleeding issues with VXGI. Near the contact point, the cones start inside the sphere's hollow voxel shell and that produce the wrong lighting.
I've used the last days to search for a solution and found one based on Schwarz and Seidel, Fast Parallel Surface and Solid Voxelization on GPUs, ACM Transactions on Graphics 2010.
The basic idea is to treat a voxel as solid if its centre lies inside a closed, outward-facing object. For that we have to determine whether a geometry is closed or not and mark the respective voxels inside. That only adds overhead during voxelization. So there is no per-frame overhead during the cone tracing and not when lighting dynamically changes.
The processing is not for free though: Depending on the scene, voxelization is about 5-30% more expensive. Given that VXGI in general is intended for scenes with static occluders, that seems appropriate to me.
The technique effectively fixes light bleeding so the sphere in #34991 has now no bright ring anymore.
PR:
Dev:
Side effect: Closed objects are now opaque at every level of detail. Less light leaks through them but areas right next to large closed objects can get slightly darker. You can see that in webgpu_vxgi: The floor next to the tall box is a bit darker. Since that looks more like occlusion, it's an acceptable side effect (in any event better than the high-frequency light leak).
