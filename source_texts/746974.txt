This study evaluates the VAEAC (Variational Autoencoder with Arbitrary Conditioning) algorithm for procedural terrain heightmap synthesis, focusing on its ability to generate missing image features and regions from sparse input fragments. Tested using the CelebA benchmark and a custom dataset of 5,000 grayscale heightmaps (HMs) at a resolution of 128x128 pixels, VAEAC demonstrated high creativity and non-determinism, successfully producing diverse and plausible outputs from minimal input data. However, prolonged training periods failed to resolve a notable limitation: the generated heightmaps exhibited noticeable blurring and lacked fine-grained sharpness. Despite this drawback, VAEAC's non-deterministic nature and robust conditional generation capabilities make it a promising foundational candidate for automated level design and procedural terrain generation pipelines, warranting further optimization.
