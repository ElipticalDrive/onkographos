What
VoxelTerrain.lod_pixel_error_back (off by default: 0 = lod_pixel_error everywhere) and lod_view_margin_deg (default 20). Outside a view cone (the frustum's diagonal half-FOV + margin), the LOD gets coarser, up to the back error behind the camera. Flags: --lod_error_back=F --lod_view_margin=DEG in bench.gd and the main scene.
Front-first load order in both meshers: chunks in the view cone first, then by LOD, in Morton order inside a queue.
bench.gd --scenario=turn: one full 360° yaw after the load, then settle and steady. Discoverer stats gain out_of_view.
How (where it differs from the plan)
One table and an effective distance, not two blended tables. The split test compares ChunkMath::lod_distance = distance − r + (back/front − 1) × the gap between the node's bounding sphere and the cone. The gap to the cone is 1-Lipschitz, so the effective distance changes by at most back/front per unit, which keeps face neighbours within one LOD. The plan's smoothstep lerp over a 20° margin changes ~13× faster at its steepest point, which can produce jumps of 2. In the cone the test is today's, bit for bit, and with the feature off the scan is identical (the existing reference-walk test still passes).
Unit-tested at 720p, 3 view directions: error 1 | 4 keeps every face jump ≤ 1, and the in-cone chunks equal the error-1 scan's. I also tried 2 | 8 (holds) and 2 | 16 (gives 31–54 faces with jumps of 2). setup_lod warns past 4×.
Load order keeps Morton batches. The plan's global distance / extent sort would spread each 128-chunk batch over a ring around the camera and widen page AABBs. Instead the queues are split into 2 × LODs: in the cone first, then LOD, Morton order inside.
A scan carries its ScanView (camera chunk, forward, cone, back_scale), and the discovery audit and --discovery_compare re-scan with the same view. A turn past 8° rescans only when the split is on. With it off, the stored forward is still refreshed, so the next scan orders the queues by the current direction.
face_lod_jumps (the discovery audit's face check) moves from voxel_terrain.cpp to lod_structures.hpp, so the unit test uses the same check.

What this means for the 20 fps case
In this scene, steady-state frame time doesn't move. Pages are already frustum-culled in the main pass, and the bench light's shadow pass loses only 5 draw calls. The gains are load time, VRAM and meshing work. If the user's scene is slow because of the trees, this PR won't fix it: measure error 1 vs 4 and with the trees hidden first.
New bottleneck: page fragmentation after turning. A turn re-meshes the cone's edges. That churn leaves pages with dead slots and wide AABBs, as a fly already does on dev (fly out and back: draw calls 286 → 470). After one full turn, render GPU time is +26% vs not splitting, and it doesn't recover while the camera stays still. The fix would be a locality-aware compaction (re-pack pages by space, not only those under half full); that is out of scope for this PR.
Loading p99 frame time rises from 37 to 120 ms: the split load takes 89 frames instead of 142, so its p99 lands on the startup spike. The max is unchanged (150 ms).
Discoverer: a full turn is 36–42 rescans of ~35 ms each (95% cull-cache hits), on the worker thread. The destroy check costs 85 ms in total over the turn, ~0.25 ms per frame, on the main thread.
