The third slice of density volumes (#71): meshing a volume's surface in the library.
volume_mesh(chunk, volume, voxel_size) -> Option<VolumeMesh> is a naive surface net.
Each cube of eight voxel centres that the surface passes through gets one vertex, at the average of its edges' zero crossings.
Each crossed edge that starts at one of the chunk's voxels gets a quad.
The mesher reads one column beyond each side, from the eight neighbouring volumes, so chunks meet without a seam.
Each quad faces from solid to empty, and is split along whichever diagonal keeps both triangles facing that way, because surface-net quads need not be planar.
Normals follow the density gradient. They agree with the triangles wherever features are wider than a voxel. Around features about one voxel across they can point every which way; the module docs say so.
The volume's lowest and highest levels bound the surface. A volume that is solid at the bottom and empty at the top is closed.
One mesh for drawing and collision: positions are in a Y-up engine's axes, relative to the chunk's corner like the ground's. Drawing it and colliding with it in Godot and Bevy is the next slice.
tests/volume_mesh.rs checks:
a flat surface where the volume is solid below a height, two triangles per column, facing up;
four 8-column chunks mesh exactly the triangles of one 16-column chunk;
where features are wider than a voxel, every triangle faces the way its normals point;
no surface until the neighbours' volumes have arrived.
A unit test pins the diagonal choice on a folded quad. Root workspace: fmt, clippy and 433 passing tests.
