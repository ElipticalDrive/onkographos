A bstract
decomThis thesis presents a general framework for rendering multiresolution decom­
posed volume data in which the properties of the multiresolution structure of 3D
data are explored systematically. Instead of solving the rendering integral over the
usoriginal volume data set, we first project it into a multiresolution representation us­
ing a wavelet transform or Laplacian pyramid. The final integration process is then
performed on the resulting coefficients directly. A large number of coefficients can be
ignored while still achieving accurate results. This leads to a dramatic reduction in
renthe amount of memory used during the rendering process. Furthermore, direct ren­
dering of multiresolution volume data using block-wise reconstruction is implemented
along a cache data structure that retains reconstructed voxels along a ray, which can
be used for adjacent rays. This reduces voxel reconstruction redundancies. Adaptive
and progressive rendering algorithms are also implemented in this thesis that increase
the efficiency of the rendering process in time as well. The proposed technique gives
average workstations the ability to efficiently render ultra-size (> 512 MB) volume
data, as well as enabling current P C's
’s to visualize average-size (16-64 MB) volume
data.

voluVolume visualization is a method of extracting meaningful information from volu­
metric data sets through the use of interactive graphics and imaging. It addresses the
mechrepresentation, manipulation, and rendering of volumetric data sets, providing mech­
anisms for peering into structures and understanding their complexity and dynamics.
The data set is represented as a 3D regular grid of volume elements (voxels) and is
large 3D array of voxels. There are
axe two approaches to
stored in a volume buffer, a laxge

visualization: surface rendering and volume rendering.
Surface rendering computes a representation of the structure from the image data
as a set of certain basic elements, usually voxels, and their faces [48]. In a crucial step
called segm entation, a decision is made as to which aspects of the image constitute
the interior or the boundary of the structure and which its exterior.
In contrast to surface rendering, volume rendering is the direct rendering of data
sampled in three dimensions. Volume rendering does not compute an explicit model
of the structure, but defines a model implicitly by considering the entire volume data
to be a semi-transparent material and by carefully assigning opacities to voxels, an
operation usually called classification. By modelling each voxel as both a source and
struc­
an attenuator of light, volume rendering allows the visualization of the interior structure and its relations to exterior forms (Figure 1.1).

Although some researchers [47] have been using the Gabor transform in computer

structure of the house to the details on one of the window curtains. With a given
window size, it is difficult to analyze both the fine and the large structure. In order

D CT Volume R en dering
Yeo and Liu [57] proposed a volume rendering algorithm based on the DCT transtrans­
macroformation of 3D scalar data. The 3D data set is first divided into overlapping macro­
blocks of size 32 x 32 x 32, of which only one at a time is kept in memory and
processed. Each macro-block is comprised of an 8 x 8 x 8 block. Each 8 x 8 xx 88 block
in a macro-block is then compressed using 3D DCT.
To avoid the full reconstruction of the original volume data during the rendering
process, voxel values are reconstructed on demand. If the global D CT transformation
is used, the cost of reconstructing individual voxels will be very high. Thus, the
volume data is divided into overlapping blocks. Each block is compressed by using
the DCT-based technique (similar to JPEG). During the ray tracing process, blocks
of voxel values are reconstructed on demand. The reason for the use of overlapping
blocks is to avoid the artifacts which otherwise would be generated along the block
boundaries in the resultant image.

Wavelet B ased V o lume Rendering
Westermann [51] and Gross [17] introduced a point-wise reconstruction method
from wavelet coefficients during the rendering process. In their technique, non-zero
voxels are directly reconstructed individually on demand during the rendering process.
In a preprocessing step, these coefficients are computed, and depending on the
prescribed error, all coefficients below a certain threshold are eliminated. After the
preprocessing of the volume data, the traversal procedure through the volume along
the view ray begins. The procedure starts with the smallest integration step size,
which is chosen to be half the size of a voxel.
At each sample point along the ray, the wavelet coefficients have to be traversed
from the finest to the coarsest level, examining those coefficients that influence the
actual sample. For each of them, the product, with the corresponding basis function,
is calculated and added to the final value.
On each level of the wavelet transform a new volume with half the resolution of
the previous one is obtained. Reordering these volumes as linear arrays, all entries are
stored with a leading mask defining which coefficients have been removed followed by
coeffithe coefficient itself. An additional run-length coding of entries with only zero coeffi­
cients has also been applied. To speed up the traversing of the data structure, further
offset information has been added, indicating the number of bytes from a certain
stream to another. As shown in Chapter 5, there is a fairly large redundancy implicimplic­
itly associated with this point-wise reconstruction process. It results in a relatively

Muraki
Muraki [36, 37] first introduced the idea of using wavelets for the approximation
of volume data. In his implementation, the rendering was performed with different

Although some researchers [47] have been using the Gabor transform in computer
vision, this decomposition has several drawbacks when applied to image analysis.
The main drawback is that the spatial and frequency resolution of a windowed Fourier
transform is constant. This localization limit is generally not acceptable. If the signal
has important features of very different sizes, we cannot define an optimal resolution
for analyzing the signal. This is typically the case with images. For example, in the
image of a house, the pattern which we want to analyze might range from the overall
structure of the house to the details on one of the windows curtains. With a given
window size, it is difficult to analyze both the fine and the large structure. In order
to avoid the inconvenience of a transform having a fixed resolution in the spatial and
frequency domains, Monet
Morlet [18] defined a decomposition based on dilation, which is
called the wavelet transform.

4.4
A d aptive R en dering
During the ray tracing, when a ray enters the volume, there usually are a sese­
requence of voxels with zero values. To quickly skip these zero voxels and avoid re­
constructing them, various acceleration techniques have been developed. The runrunlength coding technique used in the shear-warp algorithm [22] is the most efficient
approach to skip long sequences of zero values. While this technique is very suitable
tracingfor projection-based object-precision algorithms, it is ill-conditioned for ray tracingbased algorithms. Another drawback of the run-length coding method [22] is that
three redundant run-length codings, one along the x dimension, one along the y and
one along the z respectively, exist in order to handle arbitrary projection orders when
the view angle is allowed to change arbitrarily in 3D.
Another acceleration technique is to use an auxiliary hierarchical structure, called
an octree [24]. A ray is tested against the octree. Empty sub-volumes can be avoided.
strucA drawback of using it is the extra memory cost for storing the auxiliary octree struc­
ture. On the other hand, wavelet transformation is also a hierarchical decomposition
transforof the volume data set. It is possible to explore the properties of wavelet transfor­
mation to develop an acceleration technique that is equivalent to the octree-based
bentechnique, but that works within the framework of wavelet transformation. The ben­
efits will be not only the accelerated calculation of colours and opacities, but also the
70
Reproduced with permission of the copyright owner. Further reproduction prohibited without permission.
elimination of the extra m emory cost for the auxiliary octree structure. Motivated
by these factors, we developed an adaptive voxel reconstruction method as described
below.
The strategy is to ray trace the lower resolution data (that is, the LLL part) first.
If a voxel with a zero value is encountered while marching along the ray, it is simply
skipped. Only when a non-zero voxel is encountered the block reconstruction function
will be invoked to obtain a block of voxels at the higher resolution. At the same time,
time.
the ray tracing is switched to the higher resolution level. Because the lower resolution
volume data is ray traced first, the empty space can be skipped more quickly than in
high resolution. Blocks containing totally zero value voxels are not reconstructed.
The above adaptive strategy can be extended to more than one level. That is, in
reconstructing the LLL part, a few intermediate LLL's
LLL’s in the previous levels can also
be retained. By starting ray tracing at the lowest level, and adaptively proceeding
to the next higher level, the new technique is conceptually equivalent to the original
octree technique.
The number of levels to be used depends on the data set. A cell at different lower
levels corresponds to a sub-volume of different sizes in the final volume data set. It
will be counter-productive to go to too low a level, such that the volume data does
not have blocks of associated size containing completely zero voxels. From one to
three levels are
axe suitable choices, based on our experiment.
The voxels reconstructed along one projection ray are
axe very likely to be used by
adjacent rays. This observation motivated us to reduce the number of reconstructions
for adjacent rays by using a cache memory to temporarily retain the reconstructed

blocks [14].

As mentioned before, the reconstruction overhead is the bottleneck in the direct
rendering of multiresolution decomposed volume data. To overcome this problem, a
cache technique is proposed and im plemented in this thesis. This technique eliminates
80

permission.
almost all the voxel reconstruction redundancy (introduced in previous section) by
maintaining all the relevant reconstructed voxels in a cache data structure and using
them in the tracing of adjacent voxels. The way this process works is explained in
this section.
After a block of voxels is reconstructed, it is temporarily buffered in a cache
memory, instead of being discarded right away. When the tracing of one projection
ray is completed, a sequence of blocks reconstructed along a ray will be temporarily
maintained in the cache memory.
With this cache structure, a requested voxel is first searched for in the cache
memory. For efficient testing, an auxiliary list structure, called the buffered-block, is
built. Each node in the list contains the boundaries of a reconstructed block currently
existing in the cache. The list of nodes is maintained in sorted order along the ray
-volume list
according to the depth of block. Given a voxel access, the buffered-sub
buffered-sub-volume
is looked at first. If the requested voxel is within the blocks, the voxel value can
be retrieved from the cache immediately. Otherwise, the reconstruction procedure is
invoked. The newly reconstructed block will be buffered. Figure 5.5 illustrates this
concept. The shaded boxes correspond to the blocks that have been reconstructed
and saved in the cache memory.
When the cache memory is full, a simple replacement policy is used. That is,
the oldest block will be substituted by a new block in the cache memory, and the
buffered-block array will be updated as well.

Adaptive or incremental reconstruction is a technique that may take full advantage
of the hierarchical structure of multiresolution data in the rendering process. Since
reconstruction in lower resolution of the hierarchy of volume data is much faster than
in higher resolution levels, we may apply the rendering algorithm to the low resolution
data as if we want to do the process up to certain level, say three. In this case the
non-useful or empty voxels will be swept out much faster than if we begin it in higher
resolution.
If, at any moment, a ray reaches a non-empty voxel, the ray will be
shifted to the next higher resolution level above the current level and ray tracing will
be continued from there.

7.1
S u mmary
The issues related to volume rendering based on multiresolution decomposed data
matheis investigated systematically in this thesis. First, we introduce an abstract mathe­
matical definition of the volume rendering equation. Then, the multiresolution dede­
composition representation is put in the context of the abstract volume rendering
equation for analysis. This analysis provides a guideline to the design of visualization
algorithms based directly on multiresolution decomposed data.
imFurthermore, direct rendering of multiresolution decomposed volume data is im­
plemented in this thesis.
In this technique rendering is applied on wavelet- and
Laplacian pyramid-based volume data, a multiresolution decomposed volume data.
Each voxel is reconstructed on-the-fly during the rendering process. Implementation
of these techniques leads to a dramatical reduction in the amount of m emory used
during the rendering process, as well as reduction in rendering time. In the following,
summarized:
the main parts or methods implemented in the above algorithm are summarized:
• B lock-wise reconstruction
In the proposed algorithm, the block-wise reconstruction is used to eliminate
voxel reconstruction redundancy existing in point-wise reconstruction. Since
neighboring voxels in different levels of a hierarchical data structure contribute
to reconstruction of each voxel, the block-wise technique takes advantage of this
property, whereas in point-wise technique, reconstructed voxels are
axe discarded
and do not participate in reconstructing other voxels.

permission.
• Cache data structure
A cache data structure is used to retain a limited number of reconstructed blocks
to be used in the tracing of neighboring voxels. As shown in previous chapters,
this technique eliminates almost all the voxel reconstruction redundancy that
is the main bottleneck in the direct rendering of multiresolution data. The size
of the cache data structure increases only by a factor of 2, while the size of
the data set increases by a factor of 3. This enables us to visualize ultra-size
volume data on common workstations without having GBs of memory. Blockwise reconstruction, along with the use of the proposed cache data structure,
increases rendering efficiency by 50% as compare to the previous techniques.
• Adaptive rendering
The hierarchical data structure of multiresolution decomposed data facilitates
low resolution rendering, i.e., the user can apply the rendering algorithm to lower
resolution levels of the hierarchy, rather than to higher resolution levels. The
im mediate result of this will be dramatic decrease in rendering time. However,
the quality of the final image will be decreased as well, accordingly. In the
adaptive rendering technique, though, we take advantage of time efficiency of
rendering in low resolution for bypassing non-useful voxels first, and then switch
to high resolution levels to prevent poor image quality. Adaptive rendering
reduces the rendering time by a factor of magnitude of 2, as shown in Chapter
6.

• Progressive rendering
This method also take full advantage of the hierarchical structure of multiresomultireso­
lution data in which one can render a few low resolution levels of data during
exthe transmission of data through the network and then progressively add ex­
tra information to the previous image. This may be a suitable technique for
rendering through a network.
• Improved progressive rendering
To prevent the distortion effects resulting from rendering low resolution data,
an improved progressive technique is proposed and implemented in this thesis.
In this technique, the rendering algorithm is applied to lower resolution levels
for calculating density value, but applied to higher resolution for calculating the
shading value of each voxel along a ray. The result is a higher quality image, of
course, with a slight sacrifice in time efficiency.
