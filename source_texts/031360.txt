### Abstract

In recent years, 3D Gaussian splatting has achieved significant progress as an efficient rasterization rendering method in real-time 3D reconstruction and rendering. However, existing methods still face limitations in terms of geometric accuracy, material realism, and adaptability to complex scenes, particularly in balancing the representation of planar and thin structures, achieving global geometric consistency, and optimizing specular reflection performance. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a high-precision 3D Gaussian splatting rendering method that integrates hybrid Gaussian representations with geometry-guided techniques. The method adaptively selects 2D or 3D Gaussian splatting within a unified rendering pipeline to enhance the representation efficiency of planar and volumetric structures. It combines TensoSDF geometry guidance to achieve efficient implicit surface constraints and global geometric consistency through tensor decomposition. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms the original 3DGS in terms of rendering quality and geometric accuracy across multiple benchmark datasets, while maintaining high training and rendering efficiency. It is applicable to diverse scenarios, including architectural reconstruction, vegetation rendering, human modeling, and real-time interaction.

## 1 INTRODUCTION

The rapid development of virtual reality, augmented reality, digital twins, film special effects, and gaming has significantly increased the demand for 3D visual content production and rendering. Efficient rendering that maintains geometric accuracy and photorealism is now a central research objective in computer graphics and visual computing. Traditional mesh- and voxel-based 3D representations, although well-studied, often exhibit high storage redundancy, low rendering efficiency, and limited geometric expressiveness in complex scenes. Recent methods such as Neural Implicit Representations (NIR) and Point-based Rendering (PBR) have addressed some of these limitations. Notably, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a leading approach for novel view synthesis and high-quality 3D reconstruction, offering advantages in spatially continuous representation and rasterization efficiency. [1] [2] [3] [4]

3DGS represents scene geometry and appearance using parameterized 3D Gaussian distributions, which are projected onto screen space via a rasterization pipeline for efficient rendering. Compared to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), 3DGS provides superior rendering efficiency and real-time performance. However, current 3DGS methods exhibit notable limitations. When modeling planar or thinlayer structures, Gaussian spheres or ellipsoids require numerous redundant instances to approximate geometry, resulting in inefficient storage and computation. Additionally, 3DGS lacks explicit global geometric constraints, leading to geometric inconsistencies or local distortions in complex scenes. [5] [6] [7]

To overcome these limitations, this study introduces a 3DGS rendering framework that integrates hybrid Gaussian representations

- TensoSDF-based guidance using a low-rank implicit SDF to adjust Gaussian position, scale, and orientation during projection, preventing drift and enforcing global geometric consistency via parallel, end-to-end optimization;

Our experiments on several datasets indicate that the proposed approach often outperforms the original 3DGS and representative reconstruction–rendering baselines. We observe improved geometric consistency in complex scenes and more faithful reproduction of challenging materials, while maintaining competitive efficiency.

### 2 METHOD OVERVIEW

As shown in Figure 1, we introduce a hybrid Gaussian representation and a geometry-guided high-precision 3D Gaussian splatter rendering method designed to unify the optimization objectives of geometric accuracy, material realism, and rendering efficiency. Architecturally, the method employs a hybrid Gaussian representation system that adaptively selects between 2D and 3D Gaussians based on local geometric features. This approach reduces parameter redundancy in planar and thin regions while preserving comprehensive 3D Gaussian representation in volumetric complex structures, achieving a balance between geometric fidelity and computational efficiency. The adaptive mechanism utilizes tangent plane parameterization and local curvature analysis to ensure optimal representation across diverse geometric surfaces.

### 4 EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS ANALYSIS

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with CUDA 11.3, using a PyTorch 1.12-based training framework. We selected publicly available multi-view 3D reconstruction datasets, including indoor scenes, complex geometric objects, and real-world scenes with high-frequency textured materials. Comparison methods include original 3D Gaussian splatter, Mip-NeRF360, Instant-NGP, Plenoxels, and an ablation control without the specular module (w/o SM). All methods were trained and tested under identical data preprocessing, image resolution, and iteration counts.

### 4.2 Results Analysis

Table 1 quantitatively evaluates the performance of this method against multiple traditional approaches on the Mip-NeRF360, Deep-Blending, and Tanks&Temples datasets using several metrics. The

results demonstrate that this method achieves significant improvements over 3DGS across these metrics. Comparison with data from the control group w/o SM ablation indicates that the specular module exerts a more pronounced effect on all parameters.

Table 2 illustrates PSNR comparisons across various scenes within the NeRF-synthetic dataset, and our method attains an average PSNR improvement of 6.1% over 3DGS. Comparison with data from the control group without SM ablation indicates that the specular module affects data differently across various scenarios, though it generally enhances rendering quality significantly.

Figure 3 shows the reconstruction comparison results for nine scenes in the NeRF synthetic dataset. Our method outperforms the original 3DGS method in terms of global geometric consistency and reconstruction performance for highly reflective materials.

Additionally, on the Mip-NeRF dataset, the average memory consumption of 3DGS was 734MB, while our method required 845MB—an increase of approximately 15.1%. This figure corresponds to increases of 16.1% and 18.2% on the other two datasets in the table. This indicates that the incorporation of multiple Gaussian primitives slightly elevates the algorithm's memory requirements.

### 5 CONCLUSION

The proposed 3DGS enhancement framework effectively overcomes the limitations of the original 3DGS method in key technical dimensions by integrating hybrid Gaussian representations with tensor SDF geometry-guided rendering. Specifically, the geometry-guided hybrid Gaussian representation significantly reduces redundancy in modeling planar and thin-layer structures through adaptive selection of 2D/3D Gaussian instances. TensoSDF's global geometric constraints substantially enhance geometric consistency and modeling accuracy in complex scenes. The synergistic interaction between these two components collectively improves geometric modeling precision, material realism, and scene adaptability.

Cross-dataset experiments further validate the method's effectiveness: Compared to the original 3DGS and other representative reconstruction-rendering benchmarks, the proposed framework achieves significant improvements in geometric consistency metrics and visual fidelity for complex materials. Simultaneously, through Gaussian redundancy optimization and parallel processing, its rendering efficiency matches that of the original 3DGS, successfully balancing "accuracy-realism-efficiency." This provides a viable technical solution for high-quality 3D visual content demands in fields such as virtual reality and digital twins.

Despite significant progress, further optimization remains possible. On one hand, the current hybrid Gaussian representation and tensor SDF-guided mechanism may introduce additional storage overhead. On the other hand, hybrid Gaussians exhibit suboptimal performance in reconstructing certain special scenarios, such as highly transparent environments. Addressing these two aspects will be our key focus in subsequent work.
