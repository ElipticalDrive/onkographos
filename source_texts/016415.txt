Abstract
An optical image recognition system based on the mechanism of crystal volume holography and wavelet packet compression method is proposed and constructed. Volume holographic associative storage in a photo-refractive crystal has some special properties, which can provide a suitable mechanism to develop an optical correlation system for image recognition. The method of wavelet packet is introduced in this optical system to reduce the number of images stored in the crystal. Through best wavelet packet basis selection, a set of best eigenimages, which will be stored in the crystal as the reference images for recognition, are extracted from a large number of training images. Correlation output between these eigenimages and input ones can be applied for classification and recognition according to the different light intensities of angularly separated beams. Experimental results showed this scheme is practical and can significantly compress the data stored in the crystal while the accurate rate of recognition is still high.

Introduction
Optical image-processing systems based on volume holographic storage in a photo-refractive crystal are being increasingly used to construct optical correlators due to their suitable properties such as multi-channel operation, high parallel processing, and real-time response [1], [2], [3]. One important application of these optical correlators is image recognition [4], [5]. When a set of reference images are stored in a photo-refractive crystal using the interference technique, they can be employed as a database for image recognition. Once any image for identification correlates with these stored reference images in the crystal, angularly separated beams with different light intensities are obtained simultaneously, which represent the optical correlation output between the input and the reference images, and can be applied for classification and recognition. It has been reported that 10 000 images now can be recorded in a single crystal [6]. However, because of the erasing effect of the crystals, the diffraction efficiency of hologram becomes lower and lower while the number of stored images increases [7]. As a result, the correlation beams will be obscure and not distinct enough for evaluation.
One of the settlements to this problem is using less information stored in a single crystal to recognize more images, so that the optical output could not be so obscure. In our scheme, we attempt to use wavelet packet to compress the information stored in the crystal. Wavelet packet method is a novel technique for signal feature extraction and has become popular in many applications because of its flexibility and precise multi-scale analysis [8]. Through wavelet packet bases, signals such as sounds and images can be decomposed into a set of sub-signals in different frequency domains. And for a special kind of signals such as human faces or military targets, most features of them are distributed in some very narrow finite frequency domains. According to Coifman and Wickerhauser [9], [10], through wavelet packet decomposition and entropy-based best basis selection, a set of best eigensignals whose frequency domains are these ones can be selected to present most features of this kind of signals. And since most of these signals can be exclusively constructed by different linear combinations of the best eigensignals, the coefficients of these best eigensignals can form a characteristic vector for every signal. From the properties of photo-refractive crystals, if a set of best eigenimages are stored in such a crystal and correlated with an input image, the characteristic vector for the image can be constructed. By identifying such a characteristic vector, the image then can be recognized. Experimental results show that through this method, 15 eigenimages can be extracted from 800 original ones for recognition and the accuracy of recognition is still as high as 97.5%.

Best wavelet packet basis selection

Compare with the normal wavelet analysis, it has special abilities to achieve higher discrimination by analyzing the higher frequency domains of a signal [11]. What is more, the frequency domains divided by the wavelet packet can be easily selected and classified according to the characteristics of the analyzing signal [12]. So the wavelet packet is more suitable than wavelet in signal analysis and has much wider applications.

Optical processing of images has received much attention recently. Experimental breakthroughs have been achieved mainly by studying correlation applications for identification and tracking, two-dimensional and three-dimensional holography, compression and encryption of images, etc. While images are originally optical, numerical processing is often realized to fully exploit their information content. Within this context, our aim is to review the recent progress made in the field of optical processing of information. We consider techniques allowing us to increase image quality to render them more useful for correlation and reconstruction applications. The chapter is divided in two parts. In the first part, techniques for increasing image quality are described in detail. Interestingly, those dealing with color encoding are addressed. In addition, methods for denoising images are dealt with. The second part considers the polarization encoding methods. Throughout this review chapter, many examples illustrating the performances of these techniques are given. Future prospects for optimizing current techniques of optical processing of images are suggested.

Seo Y.-H., …, Kim D.-W.
Signal Processing: Image Communication • Volume 22 • 2007

In this paper, we proposed an efficient coding method for digital hologram video using a three-dimensional (3D) scanning method and two-dimensional (2D) video compression technique. It consists of separation of the captured 3D image into R, G, and B color space components, localization by segmenting the fringe pattern in to M×N [pixel2], frequency-transform by 2D discrete cosine transform (2D DCT), 3D-scanning the segments to form a video sequence, classification of coefficients, and hybrid video coding with H.264/AVC, differential pulse code modulation (DPCM), and lossless coding method. The experimental results with this method showed that the proposed method has compression ratios of 8–16 times higher than the previous researches. Thus, we expect it to contribute to reduce the amount of digital hologram data for communication or storage.
