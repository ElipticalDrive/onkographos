Abstract
Texture slicing based volume rendering is widely used for data visualization, but dynamic high-fidelity rendering for large-scale meteorological data is still a challenge. The traditional octree-based multiresolution method increases the slicing and rendering batches, and it also has the visual defect of brick borders. This paper introduces an efficient dynamic volume rendering method for large-scale meteorological data in a virtual globe. First, the volumetric data is resampled and transformed adaptively according to the camera view. Second, the proxy geometry is calculated by slicing the whole spherical shaped volume along the ray from the camera to Earth center based on GPU. Finally, the rendering efficiency is optimized for interactive dynamic visualization. The proposed method is tested by visualizing Typhoon Rananim in an open source software, called World Wind. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method is more efficient and the visual effect is smoother than the octree-based multiresolution method.
Introduction
Physical science phenomena are inherently complex and multidimensional, which often require appropriate visualization to help understand physical processes and underlying driving factors (Rautenhaus et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2011). Volume rendering plays a crucial role in multidimensional visualization to represent data with sufficient 3D details. The volume rendering methods include ray-casting, splatting, shear-warp, and texture slicing (Kaufman and Mueller, 2005). Among them, texture slicing is a relatively high-fidelity and efficient choice for large-amount volume visualization (Beyer et al., 2015).
Meteorological phenomena and processes encompass a wide range of spatiotemporal scales, from small-scale turbulence to global climate. It is increasingly demanding to make interactive visualization techniques and systems scale with growing data volumes (Rautenhaus et al., 2017). For large-scale meteorological data, Virtual Globe (VG) is an ideal visualization environment by integrating global geospatial data (Goodchild et al., 2012). However, dynamically visualizing large-scale meteorological data by texture slicing presents a variety of challenges: (1) As the meteorological data span a wide range, it has to be processed and rendered in a spherical coordinate system based on virtual globe. (2) Spherical shaped volume data makes the calculation of proxy geometry difficult and time-consuming. (3) Interactive dynamic rendering exacerbates performance issue in rendering time complexity.
Most conventional large-scale volume rendering algorithms adopt octree-based multiresolution strategy (Feng and Han, 2018; Li et al., 2011; Liu et al., 2015a). Considering the characteristics of wide range and large amount, hierarchical subdivision based renderings benefit both the fit of spherical shaped Earth surface and efficient multiresolution visualization. However, there are some limitations to these methods. First, the subdivision increases the slicing and rendering batches which will slow down the rendering efficiency. Second, the hierarchical structure leads to different resolutions for different bricks, which results in border effects between the adjacent bricks.
In this paper, we address these challenges to realize an efficient dynamic volume rendering method for large-scale meteorological data in a virtual globe. Since the volume data covers a large area, the viewing line can be approximately calculated by the ray from the camera to Earth center. Compared with the octree-based multiresolution method, the key point of our method is to slice the spherical shaped volume along the viewing ray as a whole. The method involves three parts: (1) the volume data is adaptively resampled and transformed in core before rendering; (2) the spherical shaped volume is sliced along the ray from the camera to Earth center by equal steps based on GPU; (3) the interactive dynamic rendering is optimized to reduce the computational amount. By changing the slicing strategy and improving the algorithm efficiency, the method is expected to visualize large-scale dynamic meteorological data efficiently.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the state art of the large-scale data volume rendering. Section 3 introduces the experiment data. Section 4 describes in detail the efficient volume rendering method including data processing and rendering algorithm. The analysis and discussion of the proposed method are presented in Section 5. Finally, Section 6 demonstrates the conclusions and future work.

There are limitations to traditional visualization solutions regarding real-time 3D visualization of time-varying and large-volume 3D gridded oceanographic data in a web environment. We adopted the open-source visualization technologies to implement a browser-based 3D visualization framework. The developed 3D visualization interfaces provide users 3DGIS experiences on a virtual globe and simultaneously provide efficient 3D volume rendering and enriched interactive volume analysis. Our experiments suggest that the well-designed Cesium and Plotly.js API allow researchers to easily establish 3D visualization applications while avoiding the requirements of intensive programming and computations. The case study conducted shows that the proposed methods is a feasible alternative web-based 3D visualization solution, which provides a faster rendering speed, high visual effects and on-the-fly 3D visualization of oceanographic data. Due to its open-source architecture and the simplicity of the adopted technologies, the visualization framework can be easily customized to visualize other scientific data with few modifications.
