Abstract
We present a novel learning model with attention and prior guidance for view synthesis. In contrast to previous works that
focus on optimizing for specific scenes with densely captured views, our model explores a generic deep neural framework
to reconstruct radiance fields from a limited number of input views. To address challenges arising from under-constrained
conditions, our approach employs cost volumes for geometry-aware scene reasoning, and integrates relevant knowledge
from the ray-cast space and the surrounding-view space using an attention model. Additionally, a denoising diffusion model
learns a prior over scene color, facilitating regularization of the training process and enabling high-quality radiance field
reconstruction. Experimental results on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach can generalize across
scenes and produce realistic view synthesis results using only three input images, surpassing the performance of previous
state-of-the-art methods. Moreover, our reconstructed radiance field can be effectively optimized by fine-tuning the target
scene to achieve higher quality results with reduced optimization time. The code will be released at https://github.com/dsdefv/
nerf.

Synthesis of novel views is a fundamental and challenging
task within the fields of computer vision and graphics [1].
The objective is to produce realistic images of a given scene
from new viewpoints. This process necessitates the capacity
to comprehend the three-dimensional layout and geometric
interconnections of the scene, and to precisely predict and
render an image from a previously unseen perspective [2, 3].
Early researches focus on modelling 3D shapes by discrete geometric 3D representations, such as mesh surface
[4], point cloud [5] and voxel grid [6]. Although explicit 3D
geometry-based representations are intuitive, they encounter
challenges related to the need for explicit proxy geometry or
dense input views. Under-constraint conditions is difficult to
achieve estimation with high precision, resulting in rendering
artifacts. A recent and promising approach, known as neural
scene representations [7, 8], has demonstrated the potential to
generate new views by representing scenes using neural network weights. This research area has made notable progress,
particularly through the application of neural radiance field
(NeRF) [9]. NeRF utilizes multi-layer perceptron (MLP) and
positional encodings to learn a continuous 5D radiance field

for a specific scene, enabling differentiable rendering from
arbitrary viewpoints.
Neural scene representations, such as NeRF, have shown
significant promise in accurately predicting and rendering novel views by leveraging image relationships within
large datasets [10]. However, these models require the optimization of a scene-specific 3D representation for each
individual scene, which is computationally expensive and
fails to capture shared information across different scenes.
Furthermore, NeRF often produces unrealistic geometries
and low-quality surface appearances when relying on a limited number of input views, resulting in noticeable artifacts.
To address these challenges, various hand-crafted regularizers and learned priors have been proposed. These include
hand-engineered priors to constrain scene geometry [11],
learned priors designed to ensure plausible renderings from
arbitrary viewpoints [12], and approaches leveraging singleimage depth and normal estimation to impose high-level
constraints on scene geometry [13]. Additionally, approaches
like ColNeRF [14] integrate collaborative compensation and
constraints into NeRF, significantly improving its generalization capabilities. Despite these advancements, no existing
methods explicitly learn a joint probability distribution of
scene geometry and color. Moreover, inaccuracies persist due
to inconsistencies in the mapping between 2D pixels in the
source images and the corresponding queried 3D locations.
As a result, these methodologies often encounter modeling
inaccuracies, struggling to maintain geometric and visual
coherence across diverse viewpoints. This underscores the
critical importance of adequately accounting for inter-view
correlations and collaborative interactions.
To address these issues, we present a practical approach
to neural scene reconstruction and rendering that focuses on
efficient radiation field estimation. The proposed network
incorporates two core designs tailored for learning a general
continuous 3D scene representation from a limited number
of observed views. The first key element involves encoding scene information and utilizing key knowledge to aid
density value and color estimation. Our designs build on the
transformer-based framework and utilize the attention mechanism to integrate deep relationships between the rendering
view and observed views. By leveraging the attention mechanism, we consider adjacent 3D points on the target ray and
multiple views simultaneously, enhancing the local consistency of a 3D scene representation in both the ray-cast space
and the surrounding view space. Consequently, we can learn a
more comprehensive neural radiance field from limited-view
images and effectively generalize to novel scenes without
per-scene fine-tuning.
The second essential aspect under consideration is the
generation of high-quality geometry and appearance under
a limited number of input views. In contrast to prevailing
methods in radiance field reconstruction [16, 17], which pri-

– We present an innovative and feasible approach for
reconstructing and rendering neural scenes by combining information from multiple viewpoints and leveraging
prior knowledge. The use of cross-attention and relevant
knowledge exploitation mechanisms enables the matching of point densities and colors across different views,
thereby enhancing the precision of prediction.
– We integrate a denoising diffusion model as a regularization term to reduce noise and enhance the smoothness

the challenges associated with low-quality geometry and

– We conduct experiments on various widely used benchmark datasets and show that the proposed method is
comparable to existing methods both qualitatively and
quantitatively, especially for realistic view synthesis

into image-based rendering and neural scene representation.

Our method
Neural scene representation focuses on utilizing neural network models to learn and depict the structural and visual
information of 3D scenes. Recent developments in this area
have made significant progress in representing the shape
and appearance of scenes using neural networks. Previous
approaches [10, 30, 31] to neural scene representation primarily utilize MLPs as implicit shape representations, with
the weights of the MLPs establishing a mapping between
continuous spatial coordinates and either signed distance or
occupancy values. Advancements in rendering techniques
[24, 32] have led to a shift in research towards learning the
spatial structure and visual characteristics of scenes through
the analysis of multiple viewpoints [33–35]. For example,
the NeRF model [9] trains a deep neural network to encode
the density and color information of a scene into a volume, yielding impressive results for novel view synthesis.
However, NeRF focuses on optimizing the representation of
each scene individually, without considering potential shared
information among scenes, which can be time-consuming
and inefficient. Other concurrent works [17, 36] attempt to
address this issue and generalize NeRF, but the poolingbased fusion models employed in these methods have limited
capability to effectively analyze the intricate interconnections among multiple perspectives, hindering comprehensive
comprehension of 3D scenes. While our approach utilizes
knowledge from multiple viewpoints and priors to regularize
the rendering process, reducing artifacts and inconsistencies.

Overview
The proposed rendering network with attention and prior
guidance design, depicted in Fig. 2, consists of three main
modules: a cost volume construction module, a radiance
field reconstruction module and a volume rendering module.
Given N input views Ii (i = 1, . . . , N ) of a real scene and
their corresponding camera parameters φi , the network initially projects the 2D image features from the input images
captured from different viewpoints onto a planar scanning
volume in the reference view. Subsequently, it constructs a
cost volume at the reference view by warping the features
from nearby input views onto sweeping planes in the reference view’s frustrum, similar to [37], where each voxel
contains information about feature variation at a specific
depth.
In the module for reconstructing radiance fields, we
employ a 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to generate a volumetric representation of a neural scene, containing
neural features at each voxel that capture details about the
local scene’s geometry and visual characteristics. We then
employ cross-attention and relevant knowledge exploitation
mechanisms to decode the volume density and radiance at
arbitrary continuous locations. Unlike traditional methods
that predict density and color independently, we utilize an
attention mechanism to aggregate all sample information
along the ray path and views, and allocate specific weights

However, when the input views are limited, the rendering
procedure may give rise to numerous artifacts, commonly
referred to as “ghosting”, which are erroneous projections not
corresponding to the scene objects. To tackle this challenge,
we introduce a denoising diffusion model as a learning prior
for color and geometry. The denoising diffusion model continuously assesses the level of noise introduced to the image
during the diffusion process and gradually learns to restore
the noise-free image through denoising, thereby enabling
the prediction of potential scene structure and details. This
results in more realistic and accurate generated outcomes.
The overall time complexity of the process is determined by
the number of points in the scene and the sampling count per
point. Generally, the time complexity of our model can be
approximated as O(N M), where N denotes the total number of points in the scene, and M represents the sampling
count per point.

Density and color prediction
Neural encoding volume. For rendering tasks, relying solely
on geometric information is insufficient. To achieve highquality rendering outcomes, a thorough comprehension of
the scene’s visual properties, including lighting, textures, and
materials, is essential. Therefore, it is imperative to deduce
appearance-related information from the cost volume and
integrate it into the reconstruction process. Specifically, we
employ a 3D CNN B with a UNet [38] structure to extract significant scene geometry and appearance characteristics from
the constructed cost volume P, and represent them as a new
neural feature volume S. This process can be formulated as
S = B(P).
(4)
The scene encoding volume S can be seen as a highdimensional feature space, where individual channels represent distinct attributes of the scene. Through the interpolation
and transformation of these features, it becomes possible to
infer characteristics such as volume density and brightness
dependent on viewpoint. This process empowers us to leverage these features in subsequent tasks, thereby enhancing the
precision of volume rendering results.

Experiments
In this section, we will commence by outlining the experimental parameters in “Experimental settings”. Following
this, we will assess the efficacy of our method through qualitative and quantitative analyses in “Evaluations and results”.
Experimental settings
Datasets
To evaluate the effectiveness, a series of comprehensive
experiments are carried out using both synthetic and real
datasets. These datasets include the NeRF synthetic dataset
[43], DTU dataset [15] and LLFF dataset [44]. The NeRF
synthetic dataset comprises eight scenes and eight distinct
objects, each with 100 input views and 200 test views. The
DTU dataset consists of images of objects placed on a table
against a black background, captured by a 6-axis industrial
robot equipped with a camera and a structured light scanner.
The LLFF dataset contains eight real-world scenes captured
by a handheld camera, with camera poses, intrinsic parameters, and 3D scene bounds estimated using COLMAP [29].
The resolution for the synthetic, DTU and LLFF datasets are
800 × 800, 800 × 600 and 1008 × 756, respectively.
Implementation details
In our experiment, we train our network using the DTU
dataset with the aim of acquiring a generalizable framework.
The DTU dataset is divided into 88 training scenes and 16
testing scenes, following a similar approach to PixelNeRF
[33]. We also evaluate the performance of our model (merely
trained on the DTU dataset) on the NeRF synthetic dataset
and LLFF dataset, which possess distinct scene and view
distributions compared to our training set. For each testing
scene, we select 20 nearby views, from which 3 center views
are chosen as input, and 4 images are designated as testing
views. In cases where fine-tuning is necessary, the remaining
13 images are utilized as additional input for per-scene finetuning. Our feature extraction employs 32 channels, which

Evaluations and results
Evaluations on our network. We first conduct studies on our
network architecture to validate the importance of different
modules in our whole model, including cross-attention-based
density prediction, difference-based color prediction and diffusion regularizer. To ensure a fair comparison, all models
are retrained, and additional layers are incorporated into the
various variants to ensure a similar number of parameters
as our final model. For “No AMIF”, we remove the design
of our cross-attention-based density prediction and replace
it with MLPs. In this way, the density prediction for samples on each ray is independent and local. As shown in
Table 1 and Fig. 5, when removing this module, the network cannot accurately predict the object density and local
artifacts appear. For “No DRKE”, we remove the differencebased relevant knowledge exploitation for color prediction
and employ MLPs to estimate color. Removing view differences reduces our network’s ability to combine relevant
information from surrounding views to perform predictions
and harmful factors affect the accuracy of color predictions.
For “No DDM”, we directly train our model with L p loss,
resulting in blurry reconstruction results and disappearance
of detailed information.

Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a learning model with attention and
prior guidance design, which presents a novel approach for
optimizing NeRF in data-limited scenarios. The model operates under the assumption that scenes remain static across
different views and that the multi-view data is precise and
reliable. Our key insight is that NeRF’s performance significantly deteriorates in sparse input scenes due to the difficult
optimization of scene geometry. To overcome this limitation, we leverage a cross-attention mechanism to integrate
information from surrounding views and neighbouring points
along query rays, facilitating more effective learning of scene
geometry. Additionally, we analyze and fuse view differences to highlight critical information for improving color
prediction. Recognizing the impact of limited input views on
geometry and appearance quality, we further incorporate a
denoising diffusion model to learn scene priors, enhancing
regularization during training. Consequently, our approach
learns consistent 3D representations, enabling the synthesis
of high-quality novel views. Comparative evaluations across
diverse test sets and scenes show that our method outperforms
existing approaches, especially under conditions with a limited number of input views. However, for highly challenging
scenes featuring high glossiness, mirror-like reflections, or
motion blur, directly recovering pronounced view-dependent
effects through network inference remains difficult. Addressing these complexities may require extended fine-tuning and
additional view pre-processing to fully enhance these effects.
Consequently, further improvements in this area are necessary and will be actively pursued. In future research, we aim
to develop more efficient methods to improve the learning
process under extreme imaging conditions. We will focus on
several strategies, including integrating multi-scale feature
volume representations, enhancing the utilization of sampled
points, and applying frequency regularization constraints.
