CF-Splat: Closed-Form Triangle Splatting with Mesh Priors
arXiv:2610.22108v1 [cs.RO] 15 Oct 2026
Yixin Fang1 , Liang Zhao1,†
Abstract—Splatting-based radiance-field representations have
recently gained popularity in robotic perception, enabling highquality novel-view synthesis (NVS) through explicit scene primitives. However, existing splatting methods typically rely on
iterative nonlinear optimization that jointly optimizes millions of
coupled primitive parameters, resulting in substantial computation time, peak GPU memory consumption, and susceptibility to
local minima. In this paper, we propose CF-Splat, a novel closedform triangle-splatting framework for efficient scene appearance
estimation over a known mesh either pre-existing or provided by
upstream mapping or reconstruction pipelines. Our key insight
is that, by properly formulating the problem over the given mesh
geometry and calibrated cameras, the rendered pixel colors can
be expressed linearly with respect to the per-vertex spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients, allowing multi-view scene appearance
estimation to be formulated as a global linear least-squares (LLS)
problem without the need of iterative nonlinear optimization.
To guarantee a well-posed solution under uneven multi-view
observation coverage, we introduce adaptive SH regularization
that constrains insufficiently supported high-order SH coefficients
and yields a unique global minimizer. To ensure the scalability to
large-scale scenes, we develop a triangle-structured matrix-free
preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver that avoids explicitly
constructing the large sparse normal system while producing the
same LLS solution. Experiments on diverse real-world datasets
demonstrate that CF-Splat achieves competitive or superior NVS
quality compared with state-of-the-art splatting methods. While,
CF-Splat requires approximately 27.5×–378.0× less computation
time and 1.8× lower peak GPU memory on average than 3DGS
across scenes of different scales on a consumer-grade laptop.
Interactive live demos are available on an anonymous website1 .
We will release the source code upon acceptance of this paper.
Index Terms—Scene Appearance Estimation, Novel View Synthesis, Triangle Splatting.

These limitations of 3DGS primarily lie in its training
pipeline, which jointly optimizes millions of coupled Gaussian
parameters, including geometry parameters such as positions,
opacities, and covariances, as well as appearance parameters
represented by spherical harmonics (SH), through iterative
gradient-based optimization on the Adam optimizer [6]. During this nonlinear optimization shown in Fig. 1, additional
Gaussians are progressively introduced, moved, reshaped, and
recolored to better fit the appearance with photometric-only
residual at each per-view iteration. This inevitably increases
optimization time and GPU memory overhead, while potentially trapping the optimization in view-specific appearance
overfitting and degrading the underlying geometric structure.
Existing methods have attempted to alleviate these limitations from different perspectives. Multi-view regularizationbased methods [10] introduce cross-view constraints to
improve global consistency. Geometry-aware representations [11], [12] employ more compact primitives and surfacealigned constraints to better conform to the underlying geomet-
2
ric structure. Triangle-integrated approaches [13], [14], [15]
incorporate mesh constraints or directly adopt triangle-based
representations to achieve a better balance between geometry and appearance fitting. More recently, efficiency-oriented
methods [16], [17], [18] regulate Gaussian densification and
pruning to suppress redundant primitives, thereby reducing
computational and memory costs. In parallel, numerical optimization methods [19], [20] explore LM-based and matrixfree solvers to efficiently compute parameter updates within
iterative nonlinear optimization. Despite these advances, they
do not fundamentally alter the iterative nonlinear optimization
framework of 3DGS, leaving its inherent bottlenecks unresolved. Therefore, a clear gap remains toward a closed-form
splatting formulation for more efficient appearance estimation.
To this end, we propose CF-Splat, a novel triangle-splatting
framework that leverages a mesh prior to enable closedform scene appearance estimation. In many real-world robotic
systems, this scene geometry prior is readily available such as
pre-operative CT scans in surgical robotics or CAD models for
object manipulation, or can be obtained from upstream mapping or reconstruction pipelines, such as SLAM systems [21],
[22], [23], [24] and SDF-based reconstruction methods [25],
[26], [8]. Rather than re-optimizing geometry together with appearance, by properly formulating view-dependent appearance
over the given mesh and calibrated cameras, we can establish
a linear relationship between rendered pixel colors and pervertex spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients. Based on this,
scene appearance estimation can be formulated as a global
linear least-squares (LLS) problem and a closed-form solution
can be obtained without any iteration. To further ensure a
unique global minimizer, we introduce adaptive SH regularization for uneven observation coverage to make the LLS problem
well posed. The proposed LLS formulation also results in a
block-sparse structure corresponding to mesh topology that
leads us to develop an efficient triangle-structured matrix-free
preconditioned conjugate-gradient (PCG) solver, extending its
scalability across different scene sizes. Extensive experiments
on diverse real-world datasets demonstrate the accuracy and
efficiency of CF-Splat.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
1) We propose CF-Splat, a novel closed-form trianglesplatting framework for efficient scene appearance estimation over a known mesh.
2) We derive a global LLS formulation for scene appearance estimation by exploiting the linear dependence
of rendered pixel colors on per-vertex SH coefficients,
thereby eliminating iterative nonlinear optimization and
substantially reducing computation cost.
3) We introduce adaptive SH regularization to address
uneven multi-view observation coverage, resulting in a
well-posed LLS problem with a unique global minimizer
and improved cross-view stability of the solution.
4) We develop a triangle-structured matrix-free PCG solver
that avoids explicitly constructing the global normal system, reducing GPU memory consumption and enabling
the proposed LLS formulation to large-scale scenes.
5) We extensively evaluate CF-Splat on diverse real-world
datasets against state-of-the-art splatting methods. CF-
Splat achieves competitive or even superior NVS quality
while maintaining high computational efficiency on a
consumer-grade laptop. The source code will be released
upon the acceptance of this paper.

II. R ELATED W ORK
In this section, we discuss some related works on neural
radiance fields and explicit splatting representations, accelerated and memory-efficient splatting methods, optimization
strategies for differentiable rendering, and classical meshbased view-dependent appearance modeling.
A. Neural Radiance Fields and Explicit Splatting
NeRF [4] represents scene density and view-dependent radiance using continuous neural functions optimized from posed
images. Although capable of high-quality NVS, volumetric
rendering and per-scene neural optimization remain computationally expensive. 3DGS [5] improves efficiency through
visibility-aware rasterization of explicit anisotropic Gaussian
primitives, whose geometry, opacity, and SH appearance coefficients are jointly optimized with adaptive densification.
Subsequent methods improve geometric fidelity using more
surface-aware primitives and constraints. 2DGS [11] replaces
volumetric Gaussians with oriented planar primitives and
introduces geometric regularization, while PGSR [12] exploits
locally planar Gaussian structures with multi-view geometric
constraints. MVGS [10] further incorporates multi-view regulated learning to improve cross-view consistency.
Explicit surface structures have also been incorporated into
splatting representations. GaMeS [13] parameterizes Gaussian
primitives with respect to triangles, allowing them to follow an
underlying mesh while retaining differentiable Gaussian rendering. Triangle-Splat [14] directly adopts triangles as rendering primitives, whereas Mesh-Splat [15] introduces connected
and opaque mesh structures compatible with conventional
graphics pipelines.
Despite their surface-aligned representations, these methods
still estimate scene parameters through iterative nonlinear
optimization. Moreover, incorporating additional constraints
further increases optimization burdens. In contrast, CF-Splat
builds upon a known mesh and formulates scene appearance
estimation as an efficient closed-form problem.
B. Accelerated and Memory-Efficient Gaussian Splatting
The large number of Gaussian primitives generated during 3DGS optimization has motivated extensive research on
reducing training, rendering, and storage costs. One direction focuses on compacting the final representation. Compact3DGS [27] removes redundant Gaussians and compresses
Gaussian attributes using compact appearance representations
3
and vector quantization, while LightGaussian [28] prunes
insignificant primitives and further compresses SH and geometric attributes through distillation and quantization.
Another direction directly targets computational and memory costs during training. Taming-3DGS [16] controls Gaussian growth through resource-aware densification under prescribed primitive and memory budgets. Speedy-Splat [17]
combines sparse pixel processing with aggressive pruning to
accelerate training and rendering, while FastGS [18] regulates
densification and pruning using multi-view consistency to
suppress redundant Gaussian growth and shorten training time.
Although these approaches significantly improve efficiency,
their gains mainly arise from reducing the number of Gaussians within the same gradient-based pipeline from 3DGS. In
contrast, CF-Splat improves efficiency by replacing per-view
gradient-based optimization with a global LLS formulation
that does not require any iteration, substantially reducing
computational cost.

C. Optimization Strategies and Solvers for Splatting
A. Problem Statement
Most splatting methods formulate scene reconstruction as
nonlinear optimization. In particular, 3DGS [5] and many of its
derivatives employ first-order optimizers such as Adam [6] to
update Gaussians through differentiable rendering. Although
effective and highly parallelizable, their convergence and computational cost depend on initialization, learning rates, iteration
schedules, densification, and other optimization settings.
Recent studies have explored more advanced numerical
strategies for 3DGS. 3DGS2 [29] replaces first-order updates
with local Newton steps using small per-parameter systems.
3DGS-LM [19] introduces a Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimizer with GPU-parallelized PCG, while LM-RS [20] further exploits sparse residual dependencies through matrix-free
Jacobian-vector products and conjugate-gradient solves. More
recently, 3DGS2 -TR [30] adopts a matrix-free trust-region
strategy, and Opt3DGS [31] combines stochastic exploration
with curvature-aware quasi-Newton guidance.
Despite their different numerical strategies, these methods retain a nonlinear formulation, where Newton, LM, or
conjugate-gradient procedures compute local updates within
an outer iterative optimization process. However in CF-Splat,
a scalable solver is developed based on PCG for sufficiently
solving large-scale global LLS problem. The proposed matrixfree solver avoids explicitly constructing the global normal
system, reducing GPU memory overhead while converging to
the same LLS solution within a prescribed numerical tolerance.

Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete closed-form scene
appearance estimation pipeline. For each calibrated view,
triangle rasterization establishes the pixel-to-triangle correspondence, from which the local SH observation features, confidence weights, and per-view matrices Ak , Bk , and Wk are
constructed. These per-view components are then assembled
into the global matrices A, B, and W. Based on the available
view support at each vertex, the adaptive SH regularizer Λ is
constructed, yielding the global normal system defined by H
and G. In practice, the inverse of H is not explicitly formed;
instead, the resulting system is solved using the trianglestructured matrix-free PCG solver proposed in Section IV to
obtain the final appearance estimate X∗ .
Triangles with large Sj are selected for subdivision by concentrating additional vertices.
For each selected triangle, the barycentric weights introduced in Section III-B are used to map its covered pixels back
to the triangle surface. A new vertex is placed at the mean of
these surface samples, and the parent triangle is replaced by
three child triangles sharing the new vertex.
The refined mesh is re-rasterized after each densification
round. After a number of rounds, densification is terminated
and the resulting mesh is used to construct the linear pixel
model in (14) and the global LLS system in (28). Importantly,
densification is completed before appearance estimation and
thus does not introduce extra iterations when solving the global
LLS problem.

V. E XPERIMENTS
In this section, we evaluate CF-Splat on 33 scenes from
four real-world datasets spanning different scene scales, and
compare it against 12 state-of-the-art baselines. All experiments are conducted on a consumer-grade laptop equipped
with an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU (8 GB) and an AMD Ryzen
9 7940HS CPU (48 GB).
A. Baselines
We select 12 baselines from complementary lines of splatting research discussed in Section II to comprehensively
evaluate CF-Splat in terms of representation for NVS, computational efficiency, and optimization strategy.
a) Primitive representations: We compare against
Gaussian-based methods, including 3DGS [5], 2DGS [11],
PGSR [12], and MVGS [10]; triangle-based methods, including Triangle-Splat [14] and Mesh-Splat [15]; and the hybrid
Gaussian–triangle representation GaMeS [13].
b) Accelerated and memory-efficient Methods: We include FastGS [18], Speedy-Splat [17] and Taming-3DGS [16]
to evaluate computing efficiency and memory consumption.
c) Optimization strategies and solvers: We further compare with 3DGS-LM [19] and LM-RS [20], which represent
alternative optimization and numerical solution strategies for
splatting-based representations.
In addition, we implement CF-Splat (Adam), a gradientbased variant from CF-Splat. It optimizes the same objective
in (28) over X using Adam [6], instead of solving its normal
system using the matrix-free PCG solver in Section IV. It
also serves as a representative gradient-based mesh-rendering
baseline following the optimization paradigm commonly used
with differentiable rasterizers [36], [37], [38].
B. Datasets
The datasets used for evaluation are summarized in Table I.
C3VD [41] is a colonoscopy dataset acquired using a
high-definition clinical colonoscope and high-fidelity colon
phantoms. We select 10 sequences covering three anatomical
regions: cecum (c t1 b, c t2 b, and c t3 a), sigmoid colon
(s t1 a, s t2 a, and s t3 a), and transverse colon (t t1 b,
t t2 c, t t4 a, and t t4 b).
OmniObject3D [42] is a real-world 3D object dataset acquired using structured-light scanning, covering a wide range
of object categories. We select 10 objects: apple, backpack,
ball, bed, banana, battery, book, bottle, bowl, and box.
BundleFusion [25] provides RGB-D sequences of real
indoor environments captured using an iPad-based RGB-D
scanning system. We evaluate all 8 released scenes: apt0, apt1,
apt2, copyroom, office0, office1, office2, and office3.
Oxford Spires [7] is a large-scale multimodal dataset captured across Oxford using a custom-built multi-sensor platform
equipped with RGB cameras and LiDAR. We select 5 representative sites: college, quarter, library, church, and palace.
These four datasets span surgical, object-centric, indoor, and
outdoor environments across scene scales ranging from tiny to
large, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of the performance
and scalability of the compared methods.
C. Experimental Setup
To ensure a fair comparison, all evaluated methods use the
same posed RGB observations and geometry source obtained
from identical sources.
For OmniObject3D, every 10-th frame is held out as a test
view. For C3VD, BundleFusion, and Oxford Spires, every 20th frame is selected as a keyframe, and every 10-th keyframe is
held out as a test view. The remaining frames or keyframes are
used as input observations. For Oxford Spires, regions without
valid geometric support, such as sky areas, are excluded from
both optimization and quantitative evaluation for all compared
methods. For qualitative visualization only, the corresponding
9
invalid regions in the rendered images are replaced with
ground-truth pixels to avoid distracting empty regions.
For C3VD and OmniObject3D, we use the provided meshes
and camera poses. For BundleFusion, we reconstruct the
indoor geometry by applying TSDF fusion [43] with a 3cm
voxel resolution to the RGB-D observations using camera
poses estimated with COLMAP [44], [45]. For Oxford Spires,
we reconstruct the large-scale outdoor meshes from LiDAR
measurements using VDBFusion [8] with a 0.5m voxel resolution, with the LiDAR poses estimated by Vilens-SLAM [46]
and refined by HBA [9]. The resulting meshes are used directly
by GaMeS, CF-Splat (Adam), and CF-Splat, while vertices
extracted from the same meshes are used to initialize the
remaining baselines.
For CF-Splat and CF-Splat (Adam), unless otherwise specified, we use a maximum SH degree of L = 3 for C3VD
and OmniObject3D, and L = 2 for BundleFusion and Oxford
Spires. Before constructing the LLS system, we perform 10
gradient-guided densification rounds. We set the supportedand unsupported-band regularization strengths to γs = 10−3
and γu = 103 , respectively, with the confidence lower bound
ϵw = 10−3 . The matrix-free PCG solver uses a relative
residual tolerance of δ = 10−6 and a maximum of Tmax = 300
iterations. All other baselines are evaluated using their publicly
released implementations and default configurations, with 30K
optimization iterations.
D. Metrics
We evaluate NVS quality using peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity index measure (SSIM), and
learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS). PSNR
measures pixel-level reconstruction fidelity, SSIM evaluates
structural similarity, and LPIPS assesses perceptual similarity
using deep visual features. Computational efficiency is measured in terms of peak GPU memory consumption (GB) and
computation time (min). To qualitatively assess the resulting
scene appearances, we directly visualize the reconstructed
models. For the Gaussian-based methods, the resulting Gaussian models are visualized using SuperSplat2 . For the trianglebased methods, we extract the corresponding meshes using
their officially provided extraction code and visualize them
using MeshLab3 . The mesh representations produced by CFSplat and CF-Splat (Adam) are likewise visualized using
MeshLab for consistent comparison.
E. Novel-View Synthesis Results
Table II, III, IV, and V report the quantitative NVS results, with the corresponding qualitative comparisons shown
in Fig. 2, 3, 4, and 5. Overall, CF-Splat achieves the best or
near-best average NVS quality across the four datasets.
1) C3VD Dataset: C3VD contains narrow endoscopic
scenes with strong specular highlights and substantial crossview appearance variation, making view-dependent appearance particularly challenging. As shown in Table II, CFSplat achieves the strongest overall PSNR and SSIM while

3 https://www.meshlab.net/
remaining competitive in LPIPS. The qualitative results of CFSplat in Fig. 2 also show stable local structures under the
dynamic view-dependent illumination.
Compared Gaussian- and triangle-based baselines provide
greater flexibility for fitting dynamic lighting through iterative adjustments of primitive positions, shapes, and opacity.
However, such nonlinear optimization may converge to a local
minimizer that better fits the training views but produces
inconsistent appearance in novel views. In particular, irregular
primitive distributions and opacity can introduce noticeable
image-space blurring, as observed on the c t1 b and t t2 c
scenes in Fig. 2.
CF-Splat instead estimates the per-vertex SH coefficients
jointly from all calibrated observations through the global LLS
formulation in (28). The adaptive SH regularization further
constrains directional components that are insufficiently supported by the available views. As proved in Section III-E, the
LLS formulation admits a unique global minimizer, avoiding
the local-minimizer ambiguity of iterative nonlinear fitting.
This results that CF-Splat remains effective under strong crossview photometric variation while preserving consistent novelview appearance.
2) OmniObject3D Dataset: OmniObject3D provides accurate object geometry together with sharp boundaries and
high-frequency surface textures. It therefore offers a favorable
setting for isolating the quality of the appearance representation with limited geometric uncertainty. CF-Splat achieves the
strongest overall PSNR and LPIPS while remaining highly
competitive in SSIM, as reported in Table III.
The qualitative comparisons in Fig. 3 show that trianglebased representations generally preserve fine texture structures
more clearly than spatially extended Gaussian primitives. For
CF-Splat, the gradient-guided densification in Section III-F
further allocates additional vertices to regions with stronger
image-space variation. This increases local appearance resolution without introducing additional geometric variables into
the subsequent appearance estimation. The strong performance
on OmniObject3D therefore demonstrates the benefit of combining an explicit surface representation with adaptive spatial
appearance capacity. At the same time, this fully exploits the
advantage of the closed-form formulation by enabling CFSplat to recover the unique global optimum of the appearance
estimation problem.
3) BundleFusion: BundleFusion presents a different challenge because its input geometry is obtained from RGB-D
reconstruction and therefore contains residual surface and registration errors. Despite these imperfections, CF-Splat achieves
the best average performance across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS,
indicating that it does not require near-perfect input geometry.
Compared Gaussian- and triangle-based methods tend to
rely on densification and flexible primitive reshaping or moving for image-space fitting. However, in regions with many
object boundaries, as commonly observed in BundleFusion,
freely distributed primitives can be more sensitive to residual
geometry and registration errors. During optimization, these
methods may compensate for such inconsistencies by redistributing primitives and reducing their opacity, which increases

4) Oxford Spires: Oxford Spires evaluates the methods
at a substantially larger scale, with large outdoor geometry,
high-resolution images, and extensive multi-view observations.
Under this setting, CF-Splat maintains a clear advantage in
average PSNR and LPIPS while remaining competitive in
SSIM. The qualitative comparison in Fig. 5 further shows that
this advantage extends to challenging large-scale structures.
For iterative splatting baselines, increasing the scene scale
introduces a larger number of primitives and correspondingly
enlarges the nonlinear optimization problem. Redundant primitives can be difficult to sufficiently optimize or prune, resulting
in visible floaters and unstable structures. This behavior is
particularly noticeable in the palace and quarter scenes in
Fig. 5. Although FastGS removes a large number of redundant
Gaussians during optimization, the remaining primitives still
need to be jointly optimized through the nonlinear splatting
process, making accurate appearance fitting increasingly difficult at large scale.
In CF-Splat, increasing the scene scale mainly increases the
dimension of the global SH appearance state X in (9), while
the global LLS formulation in (28) remains linear. Therefore,
the larger problem does not introduce additional nonlinear
optimization variables or local minima. The proposed matrixfree PCG solver further solves the resulting large normal
system without explicitly constructing the normal system and
achieves stable numerical convergence. This allows CF-Splat
to retain stable appearance estimation as the scene scale
increases. Detailed analyses of efficiency and scalability will
be discussed in Section V-G and Section V-I, respectively.
F. Scene Appearance Estimation Results
Beyond image-space NVS metrics, we quantitatively evaluate the geometric accuracy and appearance fidelity of the reconstructed surface representations. We restrict this evaluation
to triangle-based baselines, since Gaussian-based representations do not explicitly define a surface, and extracting reliable
surfaces can be affected by floating Gaussians. We conduct
this evaluation on OmniObject3D, which provides high-quality
textured meshes as reference geometry and appearance.
For geometric evaluation, we measure the bidirectional
mean surface distance between the ground-truth and reconstructed meshes. Specifically, we compute CD = 12 (F + B),
where F denotes the mean reference-to-prediction surface distance and B denotes the mean prediction-to-reference surface
distance. For appearance evaluation, we reuse the predictionto-reference surface correspondences established when computing B. For each sampled location on the predicted surface,
we compare its color with that at the corresponding location
on the ground-truth surface. Both colors are converted to the
CIELAB color space, and their appearance discrepancy is
quantified by the mean CIEDE2000 color difference [47].

GPU memory consumption as the scene scale increases. More
detailed scalability analyses are presented in Section V-I.

M. Discussion
Similar to triangle-based splatting methods [14], [15], CFSplat solves a slightly different problem comparing to existing
Gaussian splatting methods in its optimization formulation.
Although all methods share the same high-level objective
of estimating scene appearance for NVS, CF-Splat exploits
the linear structure of the appearance model and formulates
the problem as a global LLS system over the input mesh,
rather than optimizing an α-blending-based nonlinear objective. Therefore, the global optimality guaranteed by CF-Splat
is specific to our global LLS formulation and should not
be interpreted as global optimality over all possible splatting
objectives. This also explains why our goal is not to dominate
every baseline in image-space NVS quality, but to achieve
competitive or superior rendering quality under a substantially
more structured optimization formulation.
Another important distinction is that CF-Splat preserves the
geometry of the input mesh and focuses on appearance estimation over this surface representation. Apart from appearancedriven densification, the underlying geometry is not jointly
optimized during scene fitting. This deliberately simplifies the
splatting problem, but also avoids degrading geometry solely
for marginal improvements in image-space rendering quality.
Such a property is particularly valuable in robotic pipelines,
where the reconstructed geometry may also serve downstream
tasks and therefore should remain consistent with the output
of upstream mapping or reconstruction modules.
Finally, the use of matrix-free PCG does not conflict with
the closed-form nature of CF-Splat. The underlying appearance estimation problem remains a well-posed global LLS
problem with a unique global minimizer, while the proposed
PCG solver serves only as an efficient numerical procedure
for solving the resulting LLS system at scale. In this sense,
the closed-form formulation defines the optimization structure,
whereas the matrix-free solver provides a practically stable
implementation of that structure.
VI. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we presented CF-Splat, a novel closed-form
triangle-splatting framework for efficient scene appearance
estimation over a known triangular mesh. Given the mesh
geometry and calibrated cameras, CF-Splat reveals that rasterized pixel colors are linear with respect to the per-vertex
SH coefficients, allowing multi-view appearance estimation
to be formulated as a global LLS problem with a unique
global minimizer. Gradient-guided triangle densification adaptively allocates spatial appearance capacity without altering the
mapped surface, while observation-adaptive SH regularization
improves robustness under uneven directional support. To scale
the resulting global system, the proposed triangle-structured
matrix-free PCG solver exploits triangle-local observation
support and avoids explicitly constructing the global normal
system while converging to the same LLS solution within
the prescribed numerical tolerance. Experiments across four
real-world datasets demonstrate that CF-Splat achieves competitive or superior NVS quality while substantially reducing
computation time and peak GPU memory consumption on
a consumer-grade laptop. These results show that CF-Splat
provides a new closed-form perspective on splatting-based
scene representation, demonstrating that high-quality appearance estimation can be achieved through a globally structured
linear formulation rather than iterative nonlinear scene fitting.
The current formulation assumes relatively accurate mesh
geometry and camera calibration, so the errors in the upstream map are not intentionally corrected. Future work will
therefore investigate uncertainty-aware appearance estimation
under imperfect geometry and poses, as well as incremental
formulations that update the global appearance state as new
observations become available.
