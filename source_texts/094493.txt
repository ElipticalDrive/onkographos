Abstract
Point clouds have gained widespread application in various f i e l d s ,
but their high resolution often results in large data volumes, posing
challenges for storage, transmission, and processing. Traditional 2D
image or video compression methods are unsuitable due to the spatial
irregularity
and
sparseness
of
point
clouds.
Inspired
by
the
effectiveness of autoencoders in visual analysis tasks and image
compression, this paper proposes a novel stacked autoencoder-based
geometry compression method for point clouds. By transforming point
clouds into Morton codes using a linear octree structure and further
encoding them into integer sequences, the proposed method leverages
stacked autoencoders to reduce the dimensions of these sequences,
achieving both high reconstruction quality and high compression
ratios. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms
many other geometry compression methods, especially for small-size
point clouds. By increasing the coding depth of the linear octree, our
approach can even achieve lossless compression results, showcasing its
potential as an ef f e c t i v e geometry compression technique for point
clouds.
Keywords: point cloud compression, deep learning, linear octree,
stacked autoencoder

1 Introduction
The 3D point clouds are becoming more and more important in Industry Measurement,
Bio-medicine, Smart City, VR/AR and so on. [1] But as the performance of data
acquisition gradually improves, the amount of points in obtained 3D point clouds is
also increasing, which brings heavy pressure on the storage, transmission, rending and
other processing steps. [2] And some works have also shown compressed point clouds
can be beneficial to detection [3] and classification [4]. Therefore, innovative
compression methods for point clouds need to be proposed.
In the early research of point cloud compression, due to the lack of computation
capability and sampling eﬃciency, the methods of constructing meshes or
down-sampling were often used to achieve compression and transmission, which led
to low compression ratio as well as huge loss of point cloud information. Later, a lot
of data structures and division techniques have been used to improve the results.
Normally, after the point cloud been divided and represented by these structures,
many traditional signal process methods then can be used in encoding and
compression. And when the deep-learning becomes popular, several neural networks
have been proposed to extract the hidden information of point cloud to achieve the
point cloud compression.
In this paper, a Stacked Autoencoder-based Compression of Point Cloud
Geometry is proposed. It transforms the point cloud compression into sequence
compression. The proposed method firstly encoded the point cloud to get integer
sequences, and then used autoencoders to compress these sequences. The results
demonstrate that proposed method has better reconstruction quality and higher
compression ratios. At the same time, the sequence compression is much simpler than
point cloud compression, so the proposed model structure can be simpler than many
other models, which leads to easier training and testing.
2 Related Works
Several approaches for point cloud geometry compression have been proposed. One
structure, octree [5] was commonly used to divide and represent 3D models. R.
Schnabel et al. [6] firstly used octree in point cloud compression. This work encodes
the point cloud in terms of the occupied octree nodes, and then uses prediction
encoding techniques to compress it. Y. Huang et al. [7] further developed this into a
progressive encoding method. They used a Level of Details strategy to encode all the
attributes to achieve a lossless compression. J. Smith et al. [8] used a series of planes
to ﬁt the points in octree to achieve great compression ratio and accuracy. Huang et al.
[9] improved the check of terminating conditions of octree encoding to accelerate the
algorithm. Koh et al. [10] proposed truncated octree. They pruned the top hierarchy
2
and represented the deep octree by a set of shallow sub-octrees to reduce the encoding
complexity. Besides octree structure, many other methods and structures have been
adopted. Draco [11] proposed by Google respectively encodes the point’s connection
information and the vertex’s attribute information of point cloud. G-PCC [12]
proposed by MPEG not only uses octree but also uses triangle soup in point cloud
compression. In triangle soup, only the coordinates of vertexes will be recorded, and
triangle meshes will be used to reconstruct the point cloud in the decoder. E. Pavez et
al. [13] used a mixed representation between polygonal meshes and point clouds,
called polygon clouds, which are much easier for compression.
Recently, 3D computer vision has made great progress. Many compression
methods combined with deep-learning have been proposed. Compared with traditional
methods, deep-learning methods based on coarse-to-fine geometry can better preserve
the features. [14] Nguyen et al. [15] used a deep generative model to calculate the
possibility of whether a voxel in octree is occupied. J. Wang et al. [16] used 3D CNN
with variable autoencoder to compress the partitioned point cloud. They also proposed
an update version [17] later, which introduced a multi-scale structure, and had great
effects on sparse point clouds. G. Fang et al. [18] proposed a deep entropy model not
only concerning initial prediction results, but also the Inter-Channel Correlation, to
achieve better reconstruction quality. There are also few works focus on the idea of
autoencoder. Guarda et al. [19] used several 3D CNN blocks to form an autoencoder
structure to encode and compress the point cloud. W. Yan et al. [20] used Multilayer
Perception to extract the features first, and then applied the autoencoder to compress
these features to achieve point cloud compression. Achlioptas et al. [21] proposed a
deep autoencoder with great reconstruction quality and generation ability. T. Huang et
al. [22] proposed a modified hierarchical autoencoder. This method extracts the
information through different layers and applies a sparse coding layer in the end. To
the best of our knowledge, most compression techniques with autoencoder actually
consist of many 2D/3D computer vision models, and try to compress the point cloud
in very high dimension.
3 Methodology
In this section, the proposed geometry compression is introduced. This proposed
compression method transforms point cloud compression into sequence compression.
So an octree-based module O will firstly encode the raw point cloud to get its 1D
representation: morton code. Then the sequence encoding module S will firstly divide
the morton code bits by bits, and then further encode morton code into integer
sequences. Lastly, a SAE (stacked autoencoder) structure is used to decrease
dimensions of those encoded sequences to finish compression. SAE actually consists
of several same autoencoders to compress the sequences at different encoding levels.
It also contains a smaller autoencoder to compress the results from previous

compressed result. R means the decoding process to the corresponding encoding

3.1 Morton encoding
To encode a point cloud, the spatial correlation between points needs to be extracted.
This help to reorder the points in a more regular pattern. But point clouds are often
irregular, so building indexes for point clouds is an effective method. And octree
structure is frequently used to divide and represent 3D models. For point clouds, it
will recursively divide them into 2 N*2 N *2 N blocks, to find the relationship between
each point. I. Gargantini et al. [23] proposed a linear octree strategy only storing the
occupied leaf nodes and the path from root to leaf, so this method can transform
octree into a equivalent linear list. And the leaf nodes in linear octree can be
represented by morton code. During the encoding process, we treated every individual
point as a voxel, whose length is a unit.
Since morton code is only suitable for positive integers, so the coordinates need
to shine upon positive integer domain. Firstly, the significant digits n to be preserved
will be determined. Then the coordinates of points will all be increased by 10 n times,
and the decimal part will be omitted. This step is very similar with quantization. And
the quantified i-th point can be regarded as (x, y, z). Next, the minimum of each
coordinate will be found: [x min , y min , z min ], and the coordinates of each point will be
transformed as: x T = x - xmin , y T = y - y min , zT = z - zmin . In this way, the original
coordinates are all transformed into positive integer form.

3.2 Sequence encoding
Because a linear octree has been established to represent the point cloud, so only the
first point and the relative positions of neighboring points need to be recorded to
encode the whole point cloud. In this way, the redundancy of information can be
greatly reduced. And if the point cloud is highly ordered, the information to be stored
can be relatively low. In this step, the morton code will be sorted from little to large,
and then the difference between neighboring codes will be calculated. Actually, this
process can’t reduce the length of codes, but it can greatly reduce the information
entropy, which can lead to higher compression ratio in later steps.
After difference encoding, the codes will be further encoded to get the integer
sequences. Firstly, the difference codes will be transformed into binary bitstream.
Then the bitstream will be divided into many shorter sequences. These binary
sequences will contain the same amount of binary bits. Lastly, each binary sequence
will be transformed into a decimal integer to form the integer sequence. And these
sequences will be normalized before put into SAE. What needs to mention is that, to
get great accuracy with autoencoders, the bits contained in binary bitstream can’t be
too much, which means the decimal integers actually are relatively small.
3.3 Stacked autoencoder structure
Generally, autoencoder is an unsupervised neural network model. It uses the input X
as supervisor, to guide the neural network to learn a mapping relationship and then
reconstruct an output XR. The final goal of autoencoder is to make output XR equal to
5
the input X. This basic principle makes autoencoder an ideal model for compression.
And it can be formulated as:
X R  H (X )
(4)
However, autoencoder is highly data independent. This means the trained
autoencoder can only achieve great performance on the test datasets which are very
similar to the train datasets. And 2D images or 3D point clouds are usually very
different from each other, which brings difficulties to application. But for proposed
method, the input of autoencoder will all be normalized integer sequences. This
makes it much easier for network to extract similarities and correlations between the
data, and can achieve higher reconstruction accuracy in general.
In practice, to get great reconstruction quality, the dimension of input can’t be
reduced too much at one time. So in order to reach high compression ratio, while
maintaining high reconstruction quality, a stacked autoencoder structure is used for
compression. It consists of three same autoencoders, and can fully compress the point
cloud in different levels. All these autoencoders are composed of two linear layers.
The first linear layer will decrease the sequences from input dimension to hidden
layer dimension, while the second linear layer will further decrease the sequences
from hidden layer dimension to output dimension. Usually, the input dimension is
equal to the number of pixels in the input image. But in this method, the number of
sequences is much more, so we decided to only compress a part of the sequences at
one time. Inspired by the dimension of traditional autoencoders, we set the input
dimension close to the number of pixels in a small image (600 to 800), the output
dimension close to the traditional compression result (around 100), while the hidden
layer dimension is in the between. And because the latter autoencoder will take the
former autoencoder’s output as input, so this structure needs to be trained level by
level. Therefore, the pre-trained autoencoders can ﬁt the data to a certain degree, and
then make the whole network stay at a initial state, which may be much easier to reach
convergence.
After multi-level compression, another smaller and simpler autoencoder is used
to do the final compression. This autoencoder only contains one linear layer, and will
decrease the sequences directly from input dimension to output dimension. The whole
structure is shown in Figure 2, and the compression process can be formulated as:
X R  f 0 ( f 3 ( f 2 ( f1 ( X ))))
(5)
where X and XR are the input and output respectively; and each f represents one
autoencoder. Thus, the optimal target function is:
MinLoss  dist ( X , X R )

where dist calculates the distance between X and XR. In this paper, we use MSE
loss (Mean Square Error) to measure this distance in training.

4 Experiment results
4.1 Datasets
Training ModelNet40 datasets were used for training. It contains plenty of different
shapes, which is helpful for network to learn. ModelNet40 actually is the collection of
different CAD models, and users can get point clouds by sampling these models. Due
to the generative steps, the gotten point clouds are all axis aligned and centered into
the unit sphere, which makes it more suitable for deep-learning methods. And the
point clouds used in training all contain 4096 points. Some models are illustrated in
Figure 3.
Testing We chose two different test sets, shown in Figure 4 and details in table 1.
Class A were adopted by MPEG PCC and JPEG Pleno groups, while class B were
extracted by 3D cameras. These testing datasets present different structures and
properties. Specifically, class A are full bodies, exhibiting smooth surface and
complete shape; Class B are shot from real objects, exhibiting small size but
complicated structures.

4.3 Compression results
We mainly compare our method with other point cloud geometry compression frameworks, including: 1) octree-based codec in Point Cloud Library (PCL); 2) Testing
Model Category 1 and 3, proposed by MPEG, also known as G-PCC. There are two
different encoding strategies provided in G-PCC, one using octree structure and the
other using trisoup structure. We compared our method with these two strategies
respectively. They are noted as G-PCC (octree) and G-PCC (trisoup).
During comparison, these algorithms were set as below:

For PCL, we used Voxel Downsampling to process the voxels. This means center
points will be used to represent all the points in the voxel when reconstructing.
And we changed the minimize size of octree node to achieve different
compression ration.
9

For G-PCC (octree), we changed the Position Quantization Scale from 0.3 to 0.7
to achieve different compression ration. And the other parameters were set as
default.

For G-PCC (trisoup), we changed the Trisoup Node Size from 1 to 3 to achieve
different compression ration. And the other parameters were set as default.
Result comparison is shown in tables 2 and 3. We calculated the average PSNR
between 0.3 bpp and 0.5 bpp for different datasets. The proposed method can reach
much higher PSNR while maintaining almost the same compression ratio. Traditional
octree or trisoup encoding method cannot preserve too many details inside the voxels,
but since our method directly processes the coordinates, so more details can be
reconstructed in decoding. Thus, when processing the point clouds exhibiting
complicated structures, such as class B datasets, our method can reach much better
results. Illustrative PSNR curves are also presented in Figures 5 and 6. The PSNR
curve of proposed method stays at a much higher level than others, which further
prove its effectiveness.

5 Conclusion
In this paper, a general stacked autoencoder-based geometry compression method for
point clouds is proposed. Compared with other geometry compression methods, the
proposed method can reach higher reconstruction quality, also with higher
compression ratio. And when processing small-size point clouds with complicated
structure, the method can perform very well in reconstructing details. These
comparison results can prove that proposed method is an effective geometry encoding
codec for point clouds.
Also, the proposed method transforms point cloud compression into sequence
compression, making the model structure much simpler. So it will be very helpful to
future research of point cloud compression. The algorithm proposed may also be
extended to work on attribute compression or even dynamic point cloud compression,
to have a more general application.
