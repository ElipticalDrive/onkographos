Abstract:
Radiance field methods can achieve high-fidelity view synthesis from plenty of captured images, where voxel radiance fields are known for their efficient training and rendering. When input views are sparse, however, voxel radiance field reconstruction is an under-constrained problem, leading to unsatisfactory rendered images at novel viewpoints. In this paper, we propose a novel method for few-shot voxel radiance field reconstruction without the introduction of any learned prior. To be specific, we utilize a generator to generate the geometry grid to restrain floater artifact in free space. To smooth the geometry and appearance of objects, we further impose total variation regularization on geometry and appearance grids. Experimental results on NeRF-Synthetic dataset exhibit that our method is effective and superior to other few-shot voxel radiance fields methods.

I. Introduction
Novel view synthesis can render high-quality images at novel viewpoints from a set of calibrated inputs, which has been applied to some domains including virtual reality, autonomous driving and robotics. At present, radiance fields methods represented by neural radiance fields [1] (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting [2] (3DGS) have been the cutting-edge view synthesis technology and caused widespread research interest. NeRF parameterizes a static scene as a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) and capitalizes on classical volume rendering to synthesis images. Due to frequent queries on MLP, training and rendering NeRF is very inefficient. Therefore, subsequent grid-based scene representations are proposed to accelerate training and rendering, including DVGO [3], Plenoxels [4], InstantNPG [5], TensoRF [6] and K-Planes [7]. In sparse-view setting, however, these radiance field models are prone to overfitting to training images, resulting in significant performance degradation at novel viewpoints.
