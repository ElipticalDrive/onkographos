TensoRF (Tensorial radiance fields) combines advantages of fast optimization, high-fidelity rendering and compact representation. When inputs are sparse, however, the performance of TensoRF will significantly degrade. Deep image prior has been used to alleviate overfitting in sparse-view setting, i.e. ZeroRF, but there still exist some floater artifacts in the case of very few input images. In this work, we introduce two schemes to improve ZeroRF for few-shot$\mathbf{3 6 0}^{\circ}$reconstruction: 1) total variation regularization to smooth geometry and reduce floaters; 2) perceptual loss to enhance textural details and improve visual quality. Experiments on OpenIllumination dataset validate the effectiveness of our method. Demos are shown at https://pjungh.github.io.
