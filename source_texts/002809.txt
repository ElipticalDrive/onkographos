Abstract
Volume rendering and reconstruction centers around solving two
related integral equations: a volume rendering integral (a generalized Radon transform) and a filtered back projection integral (the
inverse Radon transform). Both of theseequationsare of the same
mathematicalform and can be dimensionally decomposedand approximated using Riemannsumsover a seriesof resampledimages.
When viewed as a form of texture mapping and frame buffer accumulation, enormoushardwareenabledperformanceacceleration is
possible.

Webegin by developingthe mathematicalbasisof volume rendering

directly proportional to the volumetric density of the object at that
spatial position, a reconstructedimage of f(z, y) portrays a two
dimensional non-negativedensity distribution.

FFT and filtering complexity
A typical modem generation CT scannerhas on the order of 512
detectorsand is capableof taking 1024projections per second.For
a fan beamreconstruction each data set must undergo Fast Fourier
Transformation (m), a multiply, and inverse FFT to accomplish
the “bow tie”, w, convolution operation. To prevent aliasing the
data set must be extendedto 1024 [2,9]. The FFT will then consist
of 10 setsof 512 butterfly calculations each of which is a complex
multiply and two complex adds or four simple multiplies and six
simple additions. Thus the FFT will require 10*10*512 floating
point operations or 50 KFLOPs. This is followed by a complex
multiplication of 6 KFLOPs and an inversetransform of 50 KFLOPs
for a total of 106 KFLOPs for a single projection. Multiplying this
result by the 1024projections yields 106 MFLOPs. Generally, it is
desirableto perform reconstruction in the time required to acquire
the scandata, typically one second.

The computational complexity implied by the discrete form of the
Radontransform andthe filtered back projection is sufficiently large
that most CT scannersinclude specialized hardware to perform
thesetasks. However, two trends are rapidly changing CT medical
imaging. One is that increasing pressureto reduce costs is driving
a transition from special purposehardwareto general purpose“off
the shelf’ hardware. The second is that technological advances
are increasing the quantity and quality of the data which can be
collected for use in diagnosis.
These trends call for a computer system architecture which addressesdata visualization and reconstruction in a general purpose
way. This is not, however, the same as saying that the problem
must be solved by a Von Neumantype processorof sufficient spec
mark rating. Although that may somedaybe possible it will never
representan optimal solution for this type of problem.

Performance results
The hardware acceleratedalgorithms for volume rendering and reconstruction result in formidable performanceincreases.All timing
measurementswere performed on a four Raster Manager (RM)
RealityEngine Onyx with a 15OMhzR4400 CPU. Since both the
reconstruction and volume rendering algorithms are a linear function of the fill rate, removing RMs affects performance in linear
manner. All imagesare screencapturesof the actual testcodesused
to implement the algorithms describedhere.
All three texture mappedbased reconstmction algorithms have
been implemented and tested. This algorithm has shown roughly
100 times speedimprovement over a CPU basedimplementation.
Images 3 and 4 illustrates the quality of the reconstruction of a
5 12x804 sinogram into a 5 12x512 reconstruction space. This normally takes7+ minutesto reconstructusing an optimized CPU based
algorithm. As indicated in the caption the hardwareimplementation
performs the sametask in 2.1 seconds. Plate 1 shows the results
of applying the cone beam reconstruction algorithm on a 3 dimensional version of the Shepp-Loganphantom[9]. The reconstruction

These times do not include the time it takes to filter p. Traditionally the time to filter p was overwhelmedby the reconstruction
time and thus was not viewed as a bottleneck. The filtering time
becomespreponderantwhen performing hardwareacceleratedback
projection. It is, however, well known how to implement high
performanceparallel and hardware acceleratedFFT filtering.[4]
We haveimplementedboth the perspectiveand parallel plane volume rendering algorithm. This algorithm can render a 5 12x512x64
8bit volume into 5 12x512 window in 0.1seconds.This time includes

density to RGB~Ycolor look up transfer functions. Since the planar sampling described in section 6 is arbitrary and oblique with
respect to the overall volume, it is trivial to provide for arbitrary
half-plane slicing as seenin Plate 3. When coupled with arbitrary
