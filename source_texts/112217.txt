This thesis presents a differentiable rendering–based approach to solving the inverse texturing problem, where the goal is to recover physically consistent texture maps of a 3D object from reference photographs and a known mesh. Unlike conventional photogrammetry pipelines, which often produce non-riggable meshes with baked lighting artifacts, this work focuses on reconstructing editable material maps—diffuse, roughness, specular albedo, and bump maps—for a fixed, animation-ready mesh.

The methodology integrates physically based rendering with gradient-based optimization, implemented using NVIDIA’s nvdiffrast and Kaolin frameworks. The rendering process is modeled analytically through the Cook–Torrance BRDF and GGX microfacet shader, enabling end-to-end differentiation of camera, lighting, and material parameters. Optimization proceeds in three sequential stages: (1) camera calibration via silhouette matching, (2) lighting estimation using multi-illumination datasets, and (3) texture reconstruction through inverse rendering.

Experiments conducted on datasets provided by Remedy Entertainment demonstrate that the proposed pipeline can accurately reproduce surface appearance and lighting-independent textures. Despite limitations due to GPU memory and simplified point-light illumination, the results validate the feasibility of a fully analytic, gradient-driven framework for realistic material reconstruction. The work provides a foundation for future research toward high-fidelity, scalable, and physically grounded inverse texturing systems
