First PR of the new-renderer series: moves the fast3d vertex pipeline to the GPU and implements proper mipmapping.
GPU pipeline: matrix transformation, lighting, texgen, normals and the color combiner move from the CPU interpreter into the vertex/fragment shaders (GL, Metal, D3D11). The interpreter now streams raw vertex data and per-draw uniforms instead of pre-transformed triangles.
Mipmapping / LOD: RT64-style mipmapping, two-tile LOD support, and multiple texture-selection/tmem fixes; sprite shading fixes.
Shader templates gain shared include files (fast3d_vs.glsli, filter.glsli, noise.glsli, palette.glsli).
DrawAndRunGraphicsCommands gains a dlReplacements parameter for display-list replacement during interpolation.
Stacked PR — review and merge bottom-up; until the layers below merge, each PR's diff includes them:

Tested: compiles and links on macOS (Metal + GL) against current main. Runtime-verified extensively on the SDL2-era branch in Ghostship (SM64); re-verification on this SDL3 rebase is in progress.

The SHADE vertex attribute is packed (and declared in the input layout)
whenever shade is used OR lighting is enabled — normals travel in the
shade slot. The HLSL template only counted its floats under o_shade, so
numFloats (the IASetVertexBuffers stride) came up 3-4 floats short for
lit-but-unshaded materials, making their vertices read shifted garbage
(exploded triangles). GL/Metal/Vulkan already counted o_shade || o_lighting.

Two walls in one large outdoor scene collide but never draw, on both the OpenGL and Vulkan
backends. Bisected to the vertex transform moving into the shader: restoring the CPU
transform in GfxSpVertex - writing clip coordinates and pointing at the identity palette
entry, the way the rect path does - is the only thing that makes them appear.
Ruled out by experiment: backface culling (forced off), depth compare (LESS vs LEQUAL),
alpha and discard (forced opaque fragments, no discard), texture packs, LTO and
optimisation level, matrix-history capacity (64 -> 256), and palette slot selection
(capped the mapping at one entry so every draw reads slot 0 - the walls stayed missing).
Ruled out by instrumentation: the palette contents are right. Comparing per vertex, at
draw time, the clip position the CPU would have computed at vertex-load time against what
mTransform.mtx_palette[slot] produces gives 0 mismatches in 21M vertices.
So the triangles are submitted (per-frame counts are normal), they carry the correct
matrix CPU-side, the slot they read does not matter, and they still never rasterise. The
remaining difference between the two paths is the vertex data itself: object coordinates
plus a palette matrix, versus finished clip coordinates. I have not been able to narrow it
further without a GPU capture.
Two unrelated things noticed in the same code:
mtx_slots[3] in GfxSpTri1 is left uninitialised when the two-attempt palette loop
cannot satisfy a triangle, and is then used to index the uniform array. Unreachable at
palette size 8, since a triangle would need vertices from 9 matrices.
GFX_MTX_PALETTE_SIZE sizes TransformUniforms while the shaders hard-code
mtx_palette[32]; lowering the constant alone renders black.
