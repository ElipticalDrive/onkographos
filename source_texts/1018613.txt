Requested by João · project thread
Before: there was no ambient occlusion and the ambient light was the same everywhere. Distant forests looked lit from inside, because past the shadow map range nothing shaded them. Exposure only followed the time of day, and opaque surfaces were drawn by material, with no regard to depth.
After: valleys, cliff bases and the foot of walls get soft occlusion. Tree crowns shade and green-tint what is under them, even beyond the real-time shadows, and the light bouncing off the ground takes its colour. The exposure adapts to what you look at, opening up in a dark wood. Opaque surfaces are drawn near to far, so the GPU can skip shading what is hidden.
This is the cheap stand-in for ray-traced lighting. The expensive part (visibility and bounce) is baked once at load, and shading only reads it back.
How:
world/worldLight.ts builds a 512² half-float light map at load: horizon-traced sky visibility over the terrain plus every static solid mesh's triangles, ground height, canopy cover and canopy top height (from foliage crown ellipsoids measured from the tree models). It also builds a 128² ground bounce-tint map from the splat. The toon plugin (GLSL + WGSL, textureSampleLevel) and the grass vertex shader read them with two taps. The sky light takes the full occlusion and direct light half of it. Canopy also dims the sun outside the shadow-map disc (worldLight.shadow). The bake takes about 0.4 s during loading in headless Chromium.
world/postfx.ts adds eye adaptation on the GPU only. After the prefilter, an EffectRenderer writes 32×18 log luminance and then a ping-pong 1×1 adapted value. The composite reads it, so there's no read-back and no material uniforms change per frame. The target is a day/night key calibrated on open views, so a meadow at noon stays at the tuned exposure. Measured factors: dense forest ×1.13–1.3, wide valley ×0.95. It is clamped to ×0.7–1.5 and switched off in the creator.
world/drawOrder.ts sorts opaque and alpha-tested meshes near to far in coarse distance bands (material batching is kept inside each band). Thin-instanced tree sets use their LOD ring distance.
I also tried choosing terrain detail by measured error. Under a sub-pixel error budget it gave no saving over the current distance rule, so I left terrain unchanged.
Checks: typecheck, client build and the 18 tests pass. WebGL2 headless screenshots of the heroes, monsters, meadow, valley, forest and night: night is unchanged, and the others keep their overall brightness (within ±6%, valley 5% darker) with more depth in forests and crowns. WebGPU was checked by reading the code only.
