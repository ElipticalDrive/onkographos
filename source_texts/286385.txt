In any hierarchical radiosity method, the most expensive part is the evaluation of the visibility. Many methods \nuse sampling and ray casting to determine this term. Space partitioning considerably speeds up the computation \nprocess. Partitioning with shafts, leads to a quite precise subdivision of 3D space, as far as interactions between \npair of objects are concerned. \nThe use of bounding boxes allows to speed-up many computations, such as collision or intersection detection. \nThose intersections can profitably be used to determine visibility between objects. Axis aligned bounding boxes \nallow very fast evaluation of intersection, but are not that precise, whereas oriented bounding boxes, much closer \nto the 3D object achieve more accurate visibility evaluation. \nWe present here a method that allow to quickly and accurately determine the relative position of an object and a \nshaft (inside, outside, occluding), and how to implement it in a hierarchical radiosity algorithm, in order to limit \nthe hierarchy construction where not necessary.
