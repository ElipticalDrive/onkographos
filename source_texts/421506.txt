ABSTRACT
1
Recent advances in volumetric video technologies are opening the
door to new interactive and immersive experiences. This paper
presents novel technological enablers to provide realistic and volumetric reconstructions of real-world elements. On the one hand,
novel Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) based methods are proposed
to increase the quality of 3D scenes and objects reconstructions,
with improved smoothness of the surfaces. On the other hand, a
real-time volumetric video pipeline is proposed to overcome limitations in terms of visual quality and resources usage (bandwidth and
processing) of state-of-the-art solutions adopted in the holographic
communications domain. Preliminary results not only prove the
benefits of such enablers when compared to state-of-the-art solutions, but the potential of their joint integration to provide new
compelling and engaging interactive virtual experiences.
Immersive video technologies are largely evolving in the last few
years. On the one hand, off-the-shelf low-cost video and depth
sensors (e.g., Azure Kinect) can be used to capture and reconstruct
human bodies (i.e., holograms) in real-time, which in turn can be
used to represents the users in holographic communication services
(e.g., [6, 7]). On the other hand, emerging volumetric video techniques allow for providing accurate and realistic reconstructions
of environments and objects, even in near real-time, from associated sparse two-dimensional images captured using affordable
video sensors (e.g., smartphones). One promising example is Neural
Radiance Field (NeRF) [12], which exploits Deep Learning for 3D
scene reconstruction and novel view synthesis. Although the scientific community is devoting major efforts on providing innovative
technological enablers for both (offline or real-time) humans and
(offline or even near real-time) scene reconstruction, key challenges
still need to be addressed, and their joint treatment and integration in Metaverse-like scenarios have not yet been explored. This
paper reports on an ongoing research line which targets not only
at advancing the state-of-the-art in these two related topics, but
also at being pioneering in treating them in an integrated manner.
By combining realistic and volumetric reconstructions of humans,
objects and scenes, a new era of interactive and shared 3D services
and experiences can be enabled, which can be further enriched if
complemented with real-time multiuser communication technologies for tele-ported users and with the integration of dynamic and
interactive elements in the virtual environment. Such envisioned
technological enablers are expected to provide remarkable benefits when compared to synthetic representations of humans (i.e.,
avatars) and environments (i.e., 3D models) in most state-of-the-art
Social Virtual Reality (VR) or Metaverse platforms, without requiring the availability of complex and expensive capture and rendering
setups.

2.2), including associated communication platforms for the latter.

2.1
Volumetric reconstruction of Scenes /
Environments
This section reviews diverse NeRF-based methods for 3D reconstruction from a set of calibrated multi-view images, ranging from
volume-based to surface-based methods.
Volume-based 3D reconstruction methods. These methods typically acquire an implicit representation of the environment as a
density field and yield promising visual outcomes. NeRF fits into
this classification, typically representing the environment with the
plenoptic function. Following the acquisition of a scene representation through re-rendering objectives, new perspectives can be generated by projecting rays into the scene from a new target camera
position. However, original NeRF methods are inefficient in terms of
processing times for optimization and rendering. Consequently, subsequent studies have aimed to optimize both training and rendering
processes by adjusting the plenoptic function representation using
voxel grids (e.g., [23]) or point-based representations (e.g., [22]),
among other enhancements. Additional optimization categories for
NeRF-based approaches involve integrating depth information [4]
and reconstructing large-scale scenes [19]. Despite their growing
popularity, volume-based reconstruction techniques lack surfacebased regularization, leading to artifacts like floating elements when
extracting a 3D triangle mesh, resulting in inconsistent geometry.
Surface-based reconstruction methods. In volume-based rendering, each pixel’s rendering necessitates blending colors and density
values from multiple sampled locations (NeRF). Conversely, surface
rendering exclusively samples points near the surface’s vicinity.
This includes sampling a single point, which coincides exactly with
the surface intersection, which is the case of optimizing a triangle mesh. Furthermore, explicit surface representations offer the
advantage of efficient rendering through hardware-accelerated rasterization. Implicit surface-based reconstruction methods (e.g., [16])
typically optimize a signed distance function (SDF), convertible to
density values. Samples nearer to the zero-level set of the function
exhibit higher density values. Despite yielding satisfactory surface
reconstructions, these methods face limitations imposed by the resolution of the Marching Cubes [10] algorithm grid during conversion
into an explicit triangle mesh. Higher resolutions produce highquality meshes but escalate vertex counts exponentially. Conversely,
lower resolutions sacrifice spatial detail, as evidenced in the Neuralangelo method [9]. Recent approaches, such as NeRF2Mesh [18],
tackle this limitation by employing a two-stage optimization process. However, this process converts the background into an explicit
mesh, leading to associated artifacts. Our research aims to explore
a better formulation for two-stage approaches to better reconstruct
object surfaces, exploiting an estimation of the normal vector to
the surface.
2.2
Real-time Human Capture and
Reconstruction for Holographic Comms
Immersive technologies are getting increasingly adopted to connect remote users for social interaction and/or collaboration. In
this context, the Social VR concept has recently emerged to refer
to this new promising medium, in which multiple remote users
can be integrated, in real-time, into a shared virtual environment.
A key element in Social VR is the user’s representation format.
Huerta et al.
While most existing Social VR platforms rely on the use of synthetic (either cartoon- or human-like) avatars representations, a few
initiatives have started to explore technological enablers to integrate realistic and volumetric representations captured in real-time
(e.g., [1, 6, 8, 15]), using a plethora of immersive video formats [20].
The work in [15] presented a pioneering holoportation system by
Microsoft, focused on Augmented Reality (AR) scenarios and on one
single holoported user, using a mesh-based representation format
and requiring a complex and expensive capturing setup (24 sensors
clustered in 8 pods). The work in [1] presented a system that used
four Kinect sensors, along associated calibration and reconstruction
methods, to deliver a meshed representation of the human body
plus volume-based motion tracking data. Apart from (time varying) meshes, other recent works have aimed at providing human
representations based in Point Clouds, which becomes a promising alternative for representing natural content. The work in [17]
provides an overview of technological advances and related standardization efforts for Point Cloud compression. The work in [11]
presents a pioneering Point Cloud codec for immersive communications, which was later integrated into an end-to-end pipeline
in [8]. The work in [7] integrated the capture and reconstruction
sub-system from [1] into an end-to-end Social VR platform for the
recreation of a virtual TV show, in which real-time mesh-based
reconstructions of users were holoported to a virtual TV set recreated combining synthetic and video-based formats. Then, the work
in [6] integrated the pipeline from [8] into an evolved version of
the Social VR platform in [7] to enable the holoportation of Point
Cloud reconstructions of up to four users into a virtual meeting
room to hold interactive conferencing and collaborative sessions. In
this domain, as reviewed in [6, 13, 14], recent studies have provided
initial evidence on benefits provided by: (i) Social VR when compared to traditional 2D video conferencing solutions; and (ii) the
availability of realistic users’ representations when compared to
avatar-based representations in Social VR, in terms of embodiment,
quality of interaction, and co-presence levels. While advances in
this domain are remarkable, further research is needed to optimize
the performance in terms of visual quality of the reconstructions,
processing and bandwidth requirements, and adaptability, which
will unlock a wide adoptability of the associated technologies.

Human Capture and Tele-Portation. Our novel proposed method for
human body reconstruction and compression outperforms state-ofthe-art Point Cloud-based reconstruction and encoding methods [6,
8, 11] (Table 1), by: (i) approximately quadrupling the resolution
(i.e., voxels per frame); (ii) doubling the frame per second (fps)
granularity; and (iii) reducing the latency levels up to approximately
50%. A minor drawback is that the bandwidth requirements are
slightly increased, although less than in a linear manner with regard
to the resolution increase, and the encoding settings can be finetuned to fit the available resources.
Environment & Object Capture and Reconstruction. On the one
hand, we adopted the MipNeRF360 dataset [2] to compare our
proposed NeRF method against reference state-of-the-art methods
(MobileNeRF [3], NeRF2Mesh [18] and Neuralangelo [9]) in terms
of 3D scene modeling and reconstruction for different elements: a
bonsai, a room, and a garden. From Table 2, it can be observed that
our proposed method consistently achieved the best results in terms
of view synthesis quality, with a decreased mesh size and execution time. On the other hand, we captured two different sequences
with varying sizes and from different viewpoints (Figure 2): (i) a
4-min recording of a plant (medium-size object, 30x30x100cm), for
which 308 frames were selected to model the object; and (ii) a 4-min
recording of a dining room (larger scene, 10x4x4m) including multiple objects (e.g., tables, chairs. . . ) and with different illumination
sources, for which 309 frames were selected to model the scene. For
these custom recordings, our proposed method also outperformed
state-of-the-art alternatives in terms of the metrics used in Table 2,
generating a much more lightweight 3D mesh with smooth and
consistent surfaces. For the dining room sequence, Neuralangelo [9]
produced better view synthesis results, but a much worse and heavier mesh output. Specifically, Neuralangelo generates many floaters
and inconsistent geometry of this sequence when extracted when
Marching Cubes, which is even hard to visualize. In summary, our
method achieved better view synthesis results while generating
meshes that are 600 times lighter.
Volumetric Reconstruction and Encoding of
Holograms for Multi-user Communications
Our current line of action in this domain is to overcome key limitations of existing volumetric reconstruction and Point Cloud compression solutions in terms of visual quality and real-time performance [6, 11], by applying strategic position and color mapping
and conversion methods, which allow exploiting the advantages of
mature 2D video codecs (e.g., H.264) in terms of resolution support
and optimized GPU processing.

The real-time encoded volumetric video frames can be ingested
from single-sensor capture and reconstruction setups (which can
e.g. provide the frontal view of a human body) or from multi-sensor
capture setups (which can provide full volumetric reconstructions of
the human body), by using affordable sensors like Azure Kinect, or
even more advanced ones like light field cameras. Then, the resulting encoded 3D holograms are then distributed to the target clients
in multi-user Social VR sessions by adopting classical architectures
based on the adoption of Selective Forwarding Units (SFU) [6] or
more advanced ones based on the adoption of Multipoint Control
Units (MCU) [5].

CONCLUSIONS & FUTURE WORK
This paper has presented novel technological enablers to provide realistic and volumetric reconstructions of real environments, objects,
and persons, and to seamlessly combine them in virtual scenarios
and experiences. In conjunction, these enablers open to door to new
realistic Social VR and Metaverse experiences, as well as to innovative virtual productions. Future work will be targeted at: (i) evolving
and refining these technological enablers in terms of perceived visual quality, latency, and resources usage (bandwidth, processing
requirements), e.g., exploring new approaches such as 3D Gaussian
Splatting (3DGS) [21]; (ii) seamlessly integrating interaction features, when it comes to semantic understanding and segmentation
of scenes, inclusion of dynamic elements, and human-to-human
interaction; and (iii) exploring potential applicability scenarios in a
variety of sectors (e.g., education, tourism, broadcast).
