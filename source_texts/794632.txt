Replaces PPLL with depth-based stochastic transparency (Enderton, Sintorn, Shirley and Luebke, I3D 2010, §3.4), ported from the owner's Aurora Viewer feat/alpha-oit implementation, following the owner's decision of 2026-11-05. After this PR the viewer has two alpha methods: sorted alpha (0) and stochastic transparency (1, the default). Details: doc/oit/stochastic-transparency.md.

The method in this renderer
The deferred scene is single-sampled (FXAA/SMAA after it), so, as Aurora does without MSAA, the passes over blended faces run on their own multisampled targets, at up to 8 samples per pass (NVIDIA 621.01 honours only the low 4 bits of a texture-fetched gl_SampleMask at 16): RGBA16F accumulation, RG16F transparency + glow, D24 stochastic depth; S = 16 by default, run as two passes of 8.
Opaque depth spread to every sample; total alpha (prod(1 − a), ZERO / ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA) through each program's alpha-only variant.
Per iteration: stochastic depth (variant writes gl_SampleMask, R = floor(aS + ξ) bits from the 2048 × (S+1) mask table, seeded by pixel, face identity and gl_PrimitiveID), then accumulation with the ordinary programs (SRC_ALPHA, ONE; early-Z kept) and the faces' glow into the green channel.
Composite: scene dimmed by the transparency (glow in alpha dimmed alike), colour × total/accumulated alpha, then the corrected glow added to the scene alpha.
As PPLL: the rigged depth replay, then residual draws (particles, custom blend), selection highlight, DoF depth.
A face lying on an opaque surface (decal) would fail half its samples (pixel-centre opaque depth vs per-sample face depth), so the passes use glPolygonOffset(-1, -1) (accumulation −2 units, glow −3); the offscreen test shows the failure without it.

Measurements
Against sorted alpha (OIT off), with shading — the owner's comparison
Sorted alpha (method 0, "OIT off") shades every blended fragment once. The stochastic
accumulation shades only the fragments at or in front of the stochastic depth (the early depth
test stays on), and the alpha-only passes shade for alpha alone, so the figures depend on what a
fragment costs. The bench cannot run the viewer's alpha programs (their lighting, probes and
atmospherics need the whole viewer); OIT_BENCH_SHADE=heavy gives direct (sorted alpha), the
PPLL capture and the accumulation a proxy of that cost: diffuse, normal and specular fetches, a
4-tap shadow, eight scattered probe lookups, seven point lights and fog. It does not cover draw
calls: each blended draw is issued once by sorted alpha, five times by stochastic transparency
at S = 16 (total alpha, two depth and two accumulation runs) and three times at S = 8, CPU time
the bench does not see.

Stochastic transparency is slower than PPLL's fixed slots on every bench scene (3.6–3.9× summed at S = 16, 2.0–2.2× at S = 8; Zink 4.8×), least where PPLL overflows (foliage). Quality: noisier where PPLL is exact (≤ 8 layers: RMSE 4.0 vs 0.7 8-bit steps at S = 16), better where PPLL overflows (32 layers 3.9 vs 12.2; foliage 1.0 vs 11.2). The owner's Aurora evidence compared against per-pixel linked lists with overflow handling, not this viewer's fixed slots. In-viewer timer queries were not taken: no grid login here. Memory: 128 B/px (253 MiB at 1080p, 450 MiB at 1440p) vs PPLL's 92 B/px.
Needs the owner's in-world check
Glass and glass behind glass, hair (grain at S = 16 vs 8), clothing layers, particles over transparent faces, water reflections and underwater views, glow on blended faces (now dimmed by the layers in front, like sorted alpha), selection highlight, depth of field, decals on sloped surfaces, and the frame time against the previous release.

Depth-based stochastic transparency (Enderton, Sintorn, Shirley and Luebke,
I3D 2010, section 3.4), ported from the owner's Aurora Viewer feat/alpha-oit
implementation and brought in by its author under the dual licence. The
blended faces PPLL captures (simple, fullbright, legacy materials, PBR blend,
static and rigged, main post-water view) are drawn in four passes over
multisampled targets at up to 8 samples: total alpha, stochastic depth with a
stratified coverage mask (gl_SampleMask, the 2048 x (S + 1) mask table, seeded
by pixel, face identity and gl_PrimitiveID), accumulation with the glowing
faces' glow, then the alpha-corrected composite and the glow into the scene
alpha. 16 samples run as two passes of 8. The alpha programs get alpha-only
variants (ALPHA_STOCHASTIC); the ordinary programs are unchanged. Particles,
custom blend functions, HUDs, mirrors, probes and pre-water alpha keep the
stock path, as with PPLL. Selected with RenderAlphaSortMethod 2 for now.
