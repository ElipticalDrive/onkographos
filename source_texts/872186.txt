Iterative reconstruction algorithms for computed tomography represent alternative solutions when analytic algorithms are not suited: case of missing and/or noisy data, which lead to a large number of artifacts; case of non-standard trajectory (neither circular nor helical trajectory), for which the hypothesis of analytical algorithm are not fulfilled.  However, such iterative algorithms still suffer from artifacts that degrade the quality of the reconstructed volume when additional constraints are encountered, such as lateral truncations, which can appear when dealing with large objects exceeding the field-of-view of the detector. Another constraint is closely linked to the rapidly growing field of in-line tomography, where both acquisition and computing time are to be reduced, meaning a drastic reduction of the number of projections, and a reduction of the number of iterations of the reconstruction algorithm. In order to address these challenges, we propose to use the CAD information, when available, as a mask inside of which the reconstruction is performed. This method is of particular interest for the detection and measurement of internal flaws in sparse-view CT and is evaluated on both simulated and experimental data. For this purpose, we compare the results obtained with a classical SART algorithm with the results from a SART + mask strategy.
