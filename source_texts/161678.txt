performance in novel view synthesis. However, exploiting complex
network structures to achieve generalizable NeRF usually results
in inefficient rendering. Existing methods for accelerating rendering directly employ simpler inference networks or fewer sampling
points, leading to unsatisfactory synthesis quality. To address the
challenge of balancing rendering speed and quality in generalizable NeRF, we propose a novel framework, NeRF-SDP, which
achieves both efficiency and high fidelity by introducing scene
depth perception. We incorporate more scene information into the

INTRODUCTION
Novel view synthesis refers to the process of synthesizing images
under any novel viewpoint from a limited number of images captured around the scene. It is an important task in the field of computer vision and computer graphics due to its crucial role in emerging immersive media such as Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality,
and has attracted increasing interest from researchers and industry
practitioners. In recent years, methods based on Neural Radiance
Field (NeRF) [22] have achieved remarkable performances by utilizing multi-layer perceptrons (MLP) to implicitly fit the 3D scene.
This kind of implicit representation can obtain photo-realistic results with a simple and differentiable pipeline. Although NeRF’s
performance is impressive, it is limited to supporting a single scene
and lacks generalization ability, which brings significant challenges
to its practical applications in production and daily life.
Based on this issue, existing generalizable NeRF algorithms extract features from reference images of the scene and utilize MLPs
to decode them, enabling the models to perform novel view synthesis on unseen scenes [5, 9, 15, 20, 32, 35, 42]. Due to the different
structures, textures and scales among various scenes, many methods utilize relatively complex designs to achieve better synthesis
results but lead to low efficiency during the inference stage [15, 35].
In contrast, some methods have accelerated the rendering process
of generalizable NeRF by using simpler networks and trilinear interpolation [5], or by directly reducing the number of sampling
points [19]. But these methods can result in severe artifacts, leading
to poor image quality. Neither of these approaches has achieved a
good balance between efficiency and image synthesis quality.
We propose a new generalizable NeRF, namely NeRF-SDP, by
introducing geometry feature extraction and depth-encoded ray transformer. NeRF-SDP can significantly accelerate the rendering process by reducing sampling points while maintaining comparable
rendering quality to other state-of-the-art methods. In the next
paragraphs, we will elaborate on our proposed pipeline to address
the shortcomings of existing methods.
First, existing algorithms rely solely on appearance information
to infer query points, leading to ambiguous explanations of the
scene [37]. To this end, we introduce a geometry feature extraction
module for accurate reconstruction. Concretely, we extract additional relative depth maps of reference images via a pre-trained
monocular depth estimation model and insert them into the feature
extraction network to obtain geometry features. As far as we know,
NeRF-SDP is the first to use the estimated depth during the feature
extraction stage in generalizable methods.
Next, due to different structures and scales among various scenes,
generalizable NeRFs usually infer query points without the positional information of them, while only relying on features from
reference images. In order to make full use of the coarse structure of
the scene calculated by multi-view stereo matching, we propose the
depth-encoded ray transformer, namely DERT, for structure-aware
reconstruction. To be specific, we encode relative distances between
query points and the estimated depth into the ray transformer to
help NeRF-SDP to infer the density of the points. Benefiting from
Qiuwen Wang, Shuai Guo, Haoning Wu, Rong Xie, Li Song, and Wenjun Zhang
this, NeRF-SDP has a deeper understanding of the structure of
scenes with stronger inference capabilities, and becomes robust
to the varying number of sampling points, thus can adaptively adjust the rendering speed and quality by taking different number
of sampling points on the rays. To our best knowledge, we are
the first to combine depth with ray transformer and eliminate the
sensitivity to varying number of sampling points introduced by the
ray transformer in generalizable NeRFs.
Finally, by utilizing volume rendering, the target view can be
synthesized based on the inferred density and color of each query
point. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:
(i) We propose a novel approach that utilizes monocular depth
estimation for geometry feature extraction, effectively boosting the
qualitative and quantitative performances of generalizable NeRF.
(ii) We propose a novel depth-encoded ray transformer, which
not only improves the scene synthesis quality but also alleviates
the sensitivity of transformer to the number of sampling points.
(iii) We construct an efficient generalizable neural radiance field,
namely NeRF-SDP, which can significantly improve rendering
efficiency while maintaining comparable rendering quality to other
state-of-the-art methods.
2 RELATED WORKS
2.1 Generalizable NeRF
In recent years, the introduction of NeRF [22] has provided a new
paradigm for the implicit representation of 3D scenes. To eliminate the limitation of NeRF in reconstructing single scene, many
researchers have focused on improving the generalization capability of the model [5, 9, 15, 18–20, 32, 35, 39, 40, 42]. Generalizable
NeRFs are mainly achieved by combining the ideas of other kinds
of view synthesis methods with NeRF’s scene representation. One
widely used approach is to adopt the ideas of image-based rendering [2–4, 10, 14, 31], using features extracted from reference
images instead of fixed 5D coordinates to make NeRF generalizable [9, 32, 35, 42]. In addition, researchers have combined the idea
of continuous random sampling in NeRF with other scene representation methods [18]. Due to the differences in content among
different scenes, using a simple MLP to process texture features of
reference images is difficult to generalize to complex scenes. Therefore, more prior knowledge of the scene [5, 15, 20, 27, 39] and more
complex network structures [15, 20, 35] have been taken into consideration to boost analysis and reasoning capabilities, leading to
lower rendering efficiency. Some methods use simple networks [5]
or extremely sparse sampling points [19] to accelerate rendering
speed, but leading to unsatisfactory quality for complex scenes.
2.2
NeRF with depth priors
Depth priors have been introduced to aid the learning process of
neural radiance fields, allowing NeRF to adapt to sparse view inputs [6, 7, 11, 34], or improve the training and inference speed [23,
37], or extend for reconstruction of dynamic scenes [1, 38]. In addition, many generalizable NeRFs have also integrated depth priors
for novel view synthesis [15, 19, 24, 30, 39], where the extracted
depth is utilized to guide the sampling process [19, 24, 30, 39], or
termed as a criterion for judging the visibility of reference viewpoints [15, 24]. However, these methods ignore the fact that depth

maps contain valuable information about scene geometry and fail
to fully leverage it. Additionally, the rough surfaces of a scene can
provide useful clues for inferring the density of sampling points.
Considering these factors, we propose geometry feature extraction and the depth-enhanced ray transformer. This not only improves
the quality of scene synthesis but also solves the problem of the
model’s hypersensitivity to sampling points. Concurrent work [8]
also uses the idea of extracting geometry features based on monocular depth estimation for panoramic image reconstruction, indirectly
demonstrating the effectiveness of this method.

4 EXPERIMENTS
4.1 Implementation Details
We inherit a pre-trained DPT [26] model for monocular depth estimation of the reference views. The other parts of NeRF-SDP are
jointly trained with random initialization. Specifically, we utilize
a UNet [28] with residual architecture [13] to extract 2D features,
and use MVSNet [41] to process the cost volume. Notably, to eliminate checkerboard artifacts caused by the deconvolution layers,
we replace them with combinations of upsampling and convolution layers. Three datasets: LLFF [21], IBRNet [35], and Google
Scanned [12] are used to jointly train the model on a single Nvidia
Geforce RTX 3090 for 250,000 steps, with a batch size of 500. We
use Adam optimizer [17] and set the learning rate of Res-UNet
and other parts as 1 × 10 −3 and 5 × 10 −4 severally. During the
first 170,000 steps, our proposed auxiliary losses and rendering
loss are jointly optimized, where 𝜆𝑠𝑚𝑜𝑜𝑡ℎ = 0.5, 𝜆𝑠𝑡𝑑 = 0.1 and
𝑠𝑚𝑎𝑥 = 16 (𝑡 𝑓 𝑎𝑟 − 𝑡𝑛𝑒𝑎𝑟 ). And for the later 80,000 steps, only the
rendering loss is considered for optimization. We set 𝐿 = 16 in the
cost volume and set 𝑢 = 64 in DERT. The dimensions of 2D and 3D
features are set to 24 and 8 respectively.

Ablation Study
To demonstrate the importance of the proposed modules in NeRFSDP, we conduct ablation experiments on two aspects: geometry
feature extraction and depth-encoded ray transformer.
Geometry Feature Extraction. To explore the effectiveness of
geometry feature extraction, we remove this module from NeRFSDP and insert it separately into IBRNet. The results presented
in Table 2 have demonstrated that the proposed geometry feature
extraction can significantly improve the synthesis quality of both
NeRF-SDP and IBRNet. In fact, it can be easily integrated into any
generalizable NeRF to enhance the scene understanding ability.
Depth Information Conditioning. We incorporate the depth
information into the volume density inference process in two ways:
3D feature volume and depth-encoded ray transformer. We compare
the model without depth information (A), the model without depth
encoding (B), and the model without 3D feature volume (C) with
full NeRF-SDP. Notably, model B uses the vanilla ray transformer
based on absolute order encoding as in [35]. From the quantitative
comparisons presented in Table 3, we can observe that both types
can boost the model’s reasoning ability.
Furthermore, experiments on the model’s robustness to varying
numbers of points have demonstrated that relative depth encoding
can effectively improve the ability to perceive scenes. Specifically,
we conduct two experiments: train with 64 points but infer with
32 points; and train with 32 points but infer with 64 points. The
qualitative results shown in Figure 4 indicate that there are more
black holes or bubble-shaped artifacts when no depth information
serves as conditional input. The use of 3D features can help alleviate
this phenomenon, but cannot eliminate it absolutely. With the aid of
our proposed DERT, NeRF-SDP can synthesize novel views without
artifacts, indicating that it can improve the model’s judgment of
the relationship between query points and scene surfaces, thereby
improving the robustness to varying numbers of sampling points.
5
CONCLUSION
We propose NeRF-SDP, an efficient and high-fidelity generalizable
neural radiance field that enhances the model’s inference capabilities by incorporating depth perception of the scene. By extracting
geometry features from monocular estimated depth maps and combining depth information from multi-view stereo matching with ray
transformers, we provide more scene information for the model, so
as to deepen its understanding of the scene’s structure with sparse
sampling points. Experiment results demonstrate that NeRF-SDP
achieves synthesis quality comparable to state-of-the-art methods
and significantly outperforms them in rendering efficiency.
