Extracting the boundary surface of a volume mesh with get_boundary_mesh() produces triangles that inherit their vertex order from their parent tetrahedra rather than having a consistent orientation across faces. On main, computing an intrinsic point gradient on that surface (or on a non-orientable surface such as a Möbius strip) gives wrong answers even in the middle of a flat face:
```
import torch
from physicsnemo.mesh.primitives.volumes import cube_volume

surf = cube_volume.load(subdivisions=4).to(torch.float64).get_boundary_mesh()
g = torch.tensor([1.0, 2.0, -3.0], dtype=torch.float64)

grad = surf.gradient(surf.points @ g)  # default: method="lsq", gradient_type="intrinsic"
# At the 54 vertices strictly inside the 6 flat cube faces:
# main:    max |grad - exact_projection| = 3.606
# this PR: max |grad - exact_projection| = 4.4e-16
```
Why this happens and how it is fixed
For codimension-1 meshes, both intrinsic LSQ (_lsq_intrinsic.py) and the point branch of project_to_tangent_space (gradient.py, used by DEC) build each vertex's tangent plane from mesh.point_normals. Because point_normals is an area-angle-weighted sum of incident cell_normals, two coplanar triangles with opposite winding cancel out or tilt the normal into the face plane, projecting out part of the tangential gradient.
The tangent plane at a vertex does not depend on normal sign. At the same time, consistently wound meshes (including sharp cone tips and thin trailing edges) should produce the exact same gradients as main. This PR therefore modifies only vertices where incident cells have opposite windings:
_inconsistently_wound_points (physicsnemo/mesh/repair/orientation.py) flags vertices that lie on a facet shared by two cells that induce the same orientation on that facet (or shared by $\ge 3$ cells). It sorts packed (facet, parity) keys without host-device synchronization and caches the boolean mask under ("topology", "inconsistently_wound_points").
_unoriented_point_normals (physicsnemo/mesh/calculus/gradient.py) computes the tangent-plane normals. At flagged vertices, any incident cell normal pointing more than $120^\circ$ ($\cos\theta &lt; -0.5$) away from the vertex's largest-area incident cell normal is negated before accumulation. Taking the largest-area incident cell as reference prevents zero-area or roundoff slivers from skewing the reference direction.
At unflagged vertices, flip is False everywhere, so the result is bit-for-bit identical to mesh.point_normals. mesh.point_normals and mesh.compute_point_normals() themselves are unchanged.
Scope, limits, and cost
Consistently wound meshes (tested on spheres, tori, the cone apex, and a $10^\circ$ wedge) produce the exact same intrinsic gradients as main.
On inconsistently wound meshes, interior face vertices and smooth surfaces up to $60^\circ$ dihedral angles are orientation-independent, and a Möbius strip's intrinsic gradient stays tangent at every vertex. At sharp $90^\circ$ creases (such as cube edges) where facets are also inconsistently wound, opposing cell normals meet at $\sim 90^\circ$ rather than $&gt; 120^\circ$, so local normals cannot distinguish a crease from a flip. Call fix_orientation when consistent crease normals are needed on orientable surfaces.
On an RTX 4090 Laptop GPU, the first intrinsic LSQ gradient call on a fresh 40,962-point icosphere takes 9.6 ms vs 5.4 ms on main (best of 30 runs, and 11.0 ms vs 11.8 ms at 163,842 points), and the topology flag mask is computed once per connectivity. The public API is unchanged.

Intrinsic point gradients, LSQ and DEC, took their tangent planes from
Mesh.point_normals, whose sum of cell normals cancels where neighboring
cells are wound oppositely, as in get_boundary_mesh output and on the
Mobius strip. They now use point normals that, at points on a facet
that two cells orient alike, flip each incident cell normal more than
120 degrees from that of the incident cell of largest area before
summing. Elsewhere they are point_normals, so consistently wound meshes
give bit-identical results. Mesh.point_normals is unchanged.

The points are flagged by sorting the facets with the orientations
that their cells induce, without a host-device sync, and cached with
the topology.
