Make metal a GGX microfacet conductor
Isotropic GGX with the height-correlated Smith term and Schlick's Fresnel on f0 = albedo. Sampling is Dupuy & Benyoub 2023, "Sampling Visible GGX Normals with Spherical Caps", rather than Heitz 2018: same distribution, no stretched frame to build, no special case for a wo the shading normal disagrees with. The sample weight collapses to F * G2 / G1(wo) -- the standard VNDF cancellation -- which is what removes the uncompensated break. A wi below the horizon still ends the path, but that is the single-scatter shadowing term rather than a loss, and it is exactly what the furnace test measures. The pdf G1(wo) * D(h) / (4 cos_o) feeds prev_bsdf_pdf and the MIS denominator, so a rough metal takes a shadow ray like any other spread lobe.
alpha = fuzz * fuzz, with a Dirac path below alpha < 1e-3 so Metal::new(.., 0.) stays an exact mirror. trace_guide now ends its specular chain on a rough metal too: a rough reflection is not a sharp image, and following it would make neighbouring pixels record unrelated guides.
Put back what single-scatter GGX loses
Turquin 2019's 1 + f0 * (1 / E - 1), applied to f alone in both bsdf_sample and bsdf_eval and never to the sampling density, so both estimators at a vertex scale identically and the MIS algebra is untouched. E is a sixteen-term fit in fuzz and cos_o, fitted in log space against E computed from the BRDF's own definition by quadrature, rather than Kulla-Conty's table and the question of how naga handles a large const array initialiser.

`ray_trace.wgsl`'s conductor was the Ray Tracing in One Weekend metal: a
sphere offset around the mirror direction, `throughput *= albedo`, and a
`break` on any scatter that landed below the horizon. Four things were
wrong with it. No Fresnel, so a metal reflected its own colour at every
angle instead of going white at a grazing one. No pdf, so it could not
participate in MIS, so `prev_specular` was set and a rough metal got no
next-event estimation however rough it was -- a fuzzy metal lit by a
small light had to find that light by chance through its own lobe. The
`break` lost energy outright, and the loss grew with fuzz. And `fuzz`
was a sphere radius calibrated against nothing, so it could not be
matched against another renderer.

The conductor arms of `bsdf_sample` and `bsdf_eval` are now isotropic
GGX with the height-correlated Smith term and Schlick's Fresnel on
`f0 = albedo`. Sampling is Dupuy & Benyoub 2023, "Sampling Visible GGX
Normals with Spherical Caps", rather than Heitz 2018: same distribution,
no stretched frame to build, and no special case for a `wo` the shading
normal disagrees with. The sample weight collapses to `F * G2 / G1(wo)`
-- the standard VNDF cancellation -- which is what removes the
uncompensated `break`. A `wi` below the horizon still ends the path, but
that is the single-scatter shadowing term rather than a loss: it is
exactly what the furnace test below measures. The sampling pdf is
`G1(wo) * D(h) / (4 cos_o)`, and it is what feeds `prev_bsdf_pdf` and
the MIS denominator, so a rough metal now takes a shadow ray like any
other spread lobe.

`alpha = fuzz * fuzz`, the squared-roughness convention every other
renderer's slider means, with a Dirac path below `alpha < 1e-3` so
`Metal::new(.., 0.)` stays an exact mirror. Small fuzz values therefore
read noticeably sharper than they did, and a scene sitting at
`fuzz ~ 0.3` changes appearance; `alpha = fuzz` would have been closer
to the old spread, and the square won because the old parameter matched
nothing. `albedo` becomes f0 rather than a flat multiplier, documented
on `Metal::new`. `trace_guide` now ends its specular chain on a rough
metal as well: a rough reflection is not a sharp image, and following it
would make neighbouring pixels record unrelated guides.

Tests. `create_rough_metal_scene` is five metal spheres at fuzz 0 /
0.15 / 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.8 over the textured floor under one small quad
light -- the configuration the old metal rendered as pure noise.
`test_rough_metal_converges_with_nee` renders it at 64 spp against its
own 1024 spp reference and measures RMSE over the spheres, relative to
the reference's own mean rather than absolute: the old metal's image was
six times darker, and an absolute error would have scored it better for
being dark. The old model ran 0.886 of its mean, GGX with next-event
estimation runs 0.346, and the bound is pinned at 0.45. The golden
harness provably cannot see this -- it downscales to 100x50 first, which
averages away exactly the defect being fixed.

`test_ggx_metal_is_energy_conserving_in_a_furnace` puts a white metal
under a uniform unit environment with no lights at all, where an
energy-conserving BRDF must read exactly 1, and averages only the
pixels inside the sphere's projected disc -- the whole-image mean is
dominated by background pixels that read 1.0 whatever the BRDF does. It
reads 1.0000 / 0.9942 / 0.8976 / 0.6318 / 0.3503 at fuzz 0 / 0.25 / 0.5
/ 0.75 / 1, and a CPU quadrature of the BRDF's definition agrees with
every one to 0.0015. That deficit is the multiple scattering this model
does not carry, and compensating for it is the next commit. An albedo-1
Lambertian sphere in the same furnace reads 1.0000, which is what says
a departure is the BRDF rather than the mask, the stride or a tone
mapper. A sphere is convex, so every path is one bounce and the
background: Russian roulette (depth 3) never runs and the firefly clamp
(10.0) is never approached, so the shipping estimator is measured
without either being disabled.

`Renderer::new` no longer rejects a scene whose only light is its
background, which is what a furnace is; a scene with no light of either
kind still is. `test_bsdf_refactor_is_image_preserving` and its
reference buffers go out as #42 said they would, metal's draws having
changed on purpose: GGX draws one pair where the fuzz sphere drew a
pair and a scalar. `random_in_unit_sphere` and `random_unit_vector` had
no other caller and are deleted.

The ISA dump prices the bigger shader at nothing that matters: VGPRs
128 and 8 subgroups/SIMD either way, no VGPR spills, SGPR spills 11 ->
14, code size 35024 -> 36896 bytes, 6793 -> 7105 instructions.
`cargo bench -- render` on the diffuse test scene, which contains no
metal at all, runs 192.29 ms against the old shader's 192.67 ms on the
same machine in the same session (p = 0.78) -- the drift from the 188.7
ms recorded under #42 is the machine, not this change. The new arm,
`rough_metal_800x600_64spp`, records 37.55 ms.

The furnace test the previous commit added reads 1.0000 / 0.9942 /
0.8976 / 0.6318 / 0.3503 at fuzz 0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1: a fully
rough white metal keeps a third of the light it is given. The rest is
every ray a microfacet reflected onto another microfacet, which a
single-scatter model drops on the floor.

Turquin 2019, "Practical multiple scattering compensation for microfacet
models", puts it back with one multiplicative factor,
`1 + f0 * (1 / E - 1)`, where `E` is the directional albedo of the
single-scatter lobe with F = 1. The f0 weighting is what makes a
coloured metal saturate rather than merely brighten -- light that
bounces twice between microfacets is tinted twice. It multiplies `f`
alone in both `bsdf_sample` and `bsdf_eval`, never the sampling density,
so both estimators at a vertex are scaled identically and the MIS
algebra is untouched.

`E` is an analytic fit rather than Kulla-Conty's table, which would
raise the question of how naga handles a large `const` array
initialiser. Sixteen terms in `fuzz` and `cos_o`, fitted in log space --
relative error is what matters when the factor divides by E -- against
`E` computed from the BRDF's own definition by quadrature. In `fuzz`
rather than `alpha` because the same sixteen terms are worth 1.4%
against 1.8% for `cos_o >= 0.4` and 3.3% against 6.8% over the whole
range: in `alpha` nearly all of the variation is crowded into the first
quarter. The residual 3.3% is the last few degrees of the silhouette,
where `E` is nearly 1 everywhere except a narrow dip no low-order
polynomial follows.

The furnace now reads 1.0000 / 0.9970 / 0.9983 / 0.9980 / 0.9975, and
the test gains a second assert -- above 0.98 whatever the pinned table
says -- so that the criterion is energy conservation rather than
agreement with a recorded number. `albedo = 1` is the hardest case for
the fit and not the easiest: with f0 = 1 the factor is exactly `1 / E`,
so the reading is `E_true / E_fit` with the fit's error undiluted. The
disc average does better than the pointwise fit because it cancels
errors of both signs.

`test_rough_metal_converges_with_nee` improves from 0.346 to 0.256 of
the reference's mean, the compensation having raised the mean the error
is measured against, and the bound tightens from 0.45 to 0.35. The
rough metal golden is regenerated: the three roughest spheres are
visibly brighter and more saturated.

Costs nothing measurable. `render/rough_metal_800x600_64spp` moves
37.55 ms -> 37.25 ms, inside the noise threshold; the ISA grows by 420
bytes and 64 instructions, with VGPRs, spills and occupancy unchanged.

Closes #43.
