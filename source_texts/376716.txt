Fits labelled Drosophila neurons from the FISBe benchmark — long, thin, widely branching — threaded through the counterstained brain they live in, as one volume-rendered Gaussian splat cloud.
Two things make it worth having, and they pull in opposite directions:
Thin filaments are the hard case for a Gaussian basis. Every other microscopy gsplat demo here fits blobby content. These neurites approach the resolution limit, span the whole brain, and occupy ~0.1% of voxels.
The neuropil is the showcase for volumetric compositing. A dense semi-transparent medium that occludes front-to-back, with sparse filaments passing behind it and genuinely being dimmed — not glowing through as they would under additive.
1,141,538 splats (786,991 neurons + 354,547 neuropil), 22:1 over the source voxels
(counting the per-splat RGBA this scene also ships, not just centres/Cholesky/amplitude).
The neuropil isn't in FISBe
The sample's channel_spec is sssr — three signal channels plus a reference — and FISBe distributes only the signal. The reference channel (the counterstain that makes this read as neurons inside a brain) comes from the FlyLight H5J on S3, decoded from its HEVC-in-HDF5 streams. That needs ffmpeg; without it the demo degrades to a neurons-only scene rather than failing.
Bonus: the H5J's root attrs carry voxel_size = [0.44, 0.44, 0.44], independently confirming the value taken from the FISBe paper.
Five findings, all measured, all in the docstring
1. gsplat cal cannot calibrate K here. The blind-spot protocol needs image noise; FISBe's raw is effectively noise-free (Workstation stitching scrubs it). Sweeps return signal_limited / still_climbing, sigma_hat ~1e-10 — the reported "K*" is just the top of the supplied grid.
2. The floor is the most consequential knob, and a percentile floor is brutal. --floor p99 subtracts 0.0337 here: it zeroes 99% of voxels and removes 97.5% of the image's energy before a single splat is placed, most of the faint neurites with it. auto — the CLI's own default, which this demo should never have overridden — beats a zero floor on the foreground with 30% fewer splats. What hid it: scoring against the floor-suppressed target, which has the same signal deleted from it (the p99 fit reads a respectable 28.10 dB against that reference). Seeds are not the splat count either — the optimiser works a fixed pool and then culls by cumulative amplitude mass, and shrinking seeds to hit a target count throws away the search that makes the survivors good: 128,000 seeds at retention 1.0 scored worse (fg 25.97) than 18,623 splats drawn from a 1.2M pool (fg 27.60). Keep seeds high; tune retention.
Background suppression therefore happens at render time instead: each neuron splat's alpha ramps with its own amplitude, so haze goes optically thin while the neurites stay opaque. The ramp bottoms out at a small non-zero alpha — alpha 0 is folded into a splat's contribution before the shader's discard, so it would delete the splat as thoroughly as the floor did.
3. Global PSNR is the wrong number. Only ~1.5% of raw energy is inside the annotated neurons — 41% once the floor is suppressed. At K=30,000 a fit reads 42 dB globally while dropping half the neurite brightness, and the neurites visibly break into disconnected beads. A ~17 dB global-to-foreground gap.
4. One node, and alpha is optical depth. Two nodes covering the same volume have no correct draw order, so they're merged and depth-sorted together, distinguished by per-splat RGBA. Alpha accumulates: across ~356K neuropil splats spanning ~350 µm, NEUROPIL_ALPHA = 0.12 renders the brain effectively opaque (the chosen look); ~0.01 gives a translucent haze with every neurite visible. Cost: the Layers panel can no longer fade the neuropil independently.
The initial camera is measured, not defaulted. FISBe ships the unaligned FlyLight stack, so the specimen sits at whatever angle it was mounted (~52 deg here). Rotating the splats would desynchronise them from FISBe's annotations and mean rotating every covariance, so the demo measures the specimen's principal axis and rolls the camera instead, framed close. Per-sample, measured at authoring time.
5. Don't fit the three MCFO channels separately. MCFO is a stochastic label — a neuron's colour is a ratio of channels at the same voxels. Independent fits don't co-locate and each axon renders candy-striped. The demo fits the per-voxel channel max once and colours each splat at its own centre, balancing channel gains first (unbalanced, the brightest channel wins 96% of splats and the whole brain reads red).
Fetching
One FISBe sample is range-extracted from the 7.1 GB Zenodo archive, so a first run transfers ~415 MB rather than the whole thing. Zenodo's HEAD returns 200 with no Accept-Ranges, so the demo probes with a one-byte ranged GET and reads the size from Content-Range. Plus ~58 MB for the H5J.
Licensing
Manifest row is bucket=local-compute but redistribute=true. Unlike the other local-compute rows this data is CC BY 4.0 — it's local-compute only because the cc-by Zenodo record doesn't exist yet. Promotion later is a one-line manifest change.

single volumetric node, which is the shape this dataset actually wants.
