Need some help with a blocky Smoke render
Help
Hi. I need some help/ideas. I’m new to Houdini and I already know that I know nothing (just like John Snow), especially about smoke, so I’ll be really happy if someone could solve my problem.
So, I have a scene from a different artist, that I needed to make some little changes on. It’s a Pyro simulation of a smoke, kinda big(it's one sim, then copied, moved to the side and "vdb merged" with the original sim. The problem is when the camera is close to the sim, the Render is showing it like it’s low res, a little blocky and I don’t know how to make it look smoother. I have interactive light from geometry too, from sparks (don’t know if it’s relevant or not, I don’t see difference whether the light is turn on or off, but decide to tell you about it).
The emitter are 3 grids with scattered points on them. There’s point wrangle for pscale=0.1 after them.
The voxel size is 0.02, both in “volume rasterize…” node and the “Pyro solver”. (the whole scene is scaled by 0.01).
The Density of the Pyro solver is 15, and then in the “Pyro Bake Volume” node: 2.
I’m rendering in Mantra.
Any ideas for solving my problem are welcomed.
Thanks in advance.

I'm not sure on your overall scene scale, but 0.02 voxels sounds pretty low, you may have to go a lot lower to get those done details. By the looks of things, you could go down to 0.005 at least, if not lower.
If you're using the pyro sop solver, you can also try checking on 'extrapolate velocity into new tiles' on the bound tab. Sometimes fast velocities can cause blockiness on sparse solves.
If you're worried about large file sizes with the lower voxel settings, you can also make sure you're only exporting the volumes you'll be making use of, and also culling or resampling the vel volume in the export tab

The voxel size isn't saying much, because it's a number that is relative to your overall size of the scene/complexity of the sim etc. 0.02 cpuld be too high, could be too low. The number itself doesn't say much. (Likely it's too low for your case, but the number itself doesn't say much.)
More helpful is when you middle click on the result (of the sim in the middle) - how many voxels do you get? If it's below 1M, then it's definitely too low. Generally I agree that 0.02 can simply be too low. for your case.
Why not check the voxel scale of the other sim you're merging with? Just use the same voxel scale to make them look the same. (although thinking about it - this is actually what might happen here - the merge might lower the sim resolution, because it will convert to the same resolution as the first input.Worth checking if the resolution before and after the merge has changed!)
As far as I can tell this is not a problem of edge voxels, since they are simply not at the edge of the sim.
