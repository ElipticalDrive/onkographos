VOXEL OCTREES FOR REPRESENTATION OF THE GEOMETRY

Department of Computers and Informatics, Faculty of Electrical Engineering and Informatics,
Technical University of Košice, Letná 9, 042 00 Košice, Slovak Republic, Tel. +421 55 602 3023

ABSTRACT
The paper deals with the problematics of representation of the geometry of three-dimensional scenes in computer graphics using
domain-specific hierarchical data structures based on octant trees or directed acyclic graphs as the means of space saving binary
representation of this information. Several hierarchical data structures were introduced in this field in past decades. In this paper we
are moving this problematic to the field of the representation of three-dimensional scenes which are rather dynamic in time, so their
geometry is changing on the regular basis. We are investigating the influence of this time-variability on the size of the related
hierarchical data structure on binary level. We are comparing the approach in which each time step is considered as the stand-alone
static three-dimensional scene and therefore is represented by its own hierarchical data structure with the approach when this timevariable scene is considered as the four dimensional space and spatio-temporal sparse voxel octree hierarchical data structure is used
for the representation of the geometry of this scene. We tested both approaches on three different scenes which were obtained by
voxelization into two different resolutions of polygonal surface models represented originally as the mesh of triangles in Wavefront
Object file format. Results of the tests show that spatio-temporal sparse voxel octree is better solution, when we obtained from 1.73 to
2.08 more compact binary representation, in comparison to the solution when time frames were encoded separately as the set of sparse
voxel octrees.
Keywords: lossless data compression, voxelized spatio-temporal three-dimensional scenes, geometry of the scene, hierarchical data
structures, sparse voxel octrees
1. INTRODUCTION
The use of domain-specific hierarchical data structures
(HDSs) such as quadtrees for 2D data or octrees for 3D data
has been explored for decades. We can mention works from
the 80s of the last century - the data structure 2Dimensional
Template-Based Encoding (2DTE) [1] or works [2, 3, 4].
These HDSs represent homogeneous sub-volumes in an
economical way. If they are homogeneously filled with
passive voxels, they are considered passive, if they are
homogeneously filled with active voxels, they are
considered active. If they are not homogeneously filled,
they are intended for decomposition.
Modern hierarchical data structures are referred to as
GPU friendly. High Resolution Sparse Voxel Directed
Acyclic Graphs (HR SVDAGs) were introduced in 2013
[5] (from which GPU friendly HDS Sparse Voxel Octrees
- SVOs were later retroactively defined). Other hierarchical
data structures were later derived (more in the Related
works section). Those HDSs are intended for the
representation of 3D voxelized scenes that contain a very
small proportion of active voxels. So they are sparse and
that transformed into the names of these data structures. In
the case of these modern HDSs, passive octants are
homogeneously filled with passive voxels. Active octants
are those octants that contain at least one active voxel, and
these octants are subject of recursive decomposition.
Much of the research in this area has been devoted to
the design of HDSs for the representation of the geometry
of static three-dimensional scenes. Proposed HDSs have
been very successful in compacting this information at the
binary level.

Ad-hoc modification of the geometry of static 3D
scenes, however, means computationally demanding
decompression of the data structure, making of the change
and recompression [6]. A special problem is the
representation of the geometry of 3D scenes, which are
time-variable, that is, which geometry changes
systematically in individual time frames.
The paper deals with this issue and its contribution is in
the examination of the assumption that the use of spatiotemporal Sparse Voxel Octrees (SVOs) allows more
compact storage of the geometry of a time-variable scene
compared to its storage in the form of SVO sets, when SVO
is built for each time frame separately.
The structure of the paper is as follows:
Section 2 of the paper is devoted to related works in the
field of representing the geometry of 3D scenes through
domain-specific hierarchical data structures based on
Sparse Voxel Octrees (SVOs) and Sparse Voxel Directed
Acyclic Graphs (SVDAGs) and data structures derived
from them.
Section 3 of the paper deals with the description of the
representation of the time-varying scene geometry by
means of classical SVOs and through spatio-temporal
Sparse Voxel Octrees.
Section 4 of the paper summarizes the results of tests
carried out on three scenes voxelized into two resolutions,
the geometry of which was stored in the form of SVO sets
and in the form of spatio-temporal Sparse Voxel Octrees.
Section 5 of the paper presents the conclusions that were
made based on the tests, the results of which were
summarized in the previous section of the paper.

A section of the paper deals with domain-specific
hierarchical data structures used to represent the geometry
of voxelized three-dimensional scenes, devoting only
resources that are particularly very closely related to the
topic of this paper.
The basic data structure intended for representing the
geometry of a voxelized 3D scene is Sparse Voxel Octrees
(SVOs). For its construction, a two-step out-of-core
algorithm was created in 2013 [7, 8]. In the first step of this
algorithm, voxelization takes place, the input of which is a
mesh of triangles. In the second step, the output of the first
step, which is the list of active voxels, is used and the SVO
is constructed. In 2015, an algorithm was presented [9]
enabling voxelization into SVO without the need for an
intermediate product. SVO HDS contains pointers to child
nodes. If pointers are absent, the data structure is called
Pointerless Sparse Voxel Octrees (PSVOs).
In 2022, HDS called Clustered Sparse Voxel Octrees
(CSVO) was introduced [10]. It brings a more compact
binary representation of the HDS at the cost of a more
complex construction of nodes, of which there are more
compared to other HDSs. CSVO consists not only of
internal nodes (INODEs) and leaf nodes (LNODEs) as
other HDSs do, but also of so-called preleaf nodes
(PLNODEs). Nodes support multiple lengths of binary
representation of pointers to child nodes.
While SVO represents a tree-based HDS, in 2013 a
directed acyclic graph-based HDS, called High Resolution
Sparse Voxel Directed Acyclic Graphs (HR SVDAGs) was
introduced [5]. While a tree does not allow a child node to
have multiple parent nodes, a directed acyclic graph does.
The main difference compared to SVO is the possibility to
search for matching subtrees within one data structure and
to represent them economically in such a way that these
trees are excluded from the structure, leaving only one of
them and referring to it multiple times. This is called
Common Subtree Merge (CSM).
In 2016, HDS called Symmetry-aware Sparse Voxel
Directed Acyclic Graphs (SSVDAGs) was reported in
[11,12]. In this data structure reflective symmetry is used,
i.e. mirroring, which can be independently realized in each
of the axes of space, i.e. in the 𝑥, 𝑦 and 𝑧 axes. Multiple
lengths of pointers to child nodes are used at the same time,
when short pointers are 16b and long pointers are 32b. The
most frequently addressed nodes are located in the address
space covered by short pointers. Therefore, nodes in each
layer of this HDS are sorted according to their addressing
frequency. This is called Frequency Based Compaction
(FBC). Grids of voxels with size 43 are used as leaf nodes.
In 2020, an HDS called Pointerless Sparse Voxel
Directed Acyclic Graphs (PSVDAGs) was introduced [13].
It enables CSM in the absence of pointers to child nodes,
which is possible thanks to the fact that labels have been
introduced to mark subtrees that will be referenced multiple
times and callers have been introduced to call these subtrees
from their multiple occurrence locations. HDS has a wide
range of pointer lengths to child nodes and uses FBC. Quick
transformation algorithm from PSVDAG to SVDAG was
introduced in [14].
In this paper we are comparing two approaches to the
representation of the geometry of a time-varying threedimensional voxelized scene.
The first approach is to view each frame of a timevarying scene as a separate static three-dimensional scene.
In that case, its geometry can be represented through data
structures that are currently established. These include
Sparse Voxel Octrees (SVOs), Sparse Voxel Directed
Acyclic Graphs (SVDAGs), Symmetry-aware Sparse
Voxel Directed Acyclic Graphs (SSVDAGs) or Clustered
Sparse Voxel Octrees (CSVOs). SVO was used in this
paper. In the case of a time-variable scene with 𝑛 time
frames, an ordered set of SVOs with cardinality 𝑛 will be
created. The scene must have dimensions 𝑁 3 and 𝑁 = 2𝑚 ;
𝑁, 𝑚 ∈ ℕ. The number of time frames 𝑛 can be arbitrary in
this case.
In the case of a time-varying two-dimensional image,
each time frame can be considered as the new static twodimensional image, similar to the previous approach
(quadrant trees could be used to store their geometry in that
case). However, it is possible to integrate these twodimensional time frames into the form of three-dimensional
space and represent its geometry with the help of classic
SVO, as shown in Fig. 1a. Similarly, one can look at the
time frames of a 3D scene and integrate them into a form
of 4D space, as shown in Fig. 1b, and represent the
geometry of this space through a single hierarchical data
structure that is analogous to SVO, and which is named as
spatio-temporal SVO in this paper.
A limiting factor of this approach may be that the
generated scene must meet the conditions when it has
dimensions 𝑁 4 and 𝑁 = 2𝑚 ; 𝑁, 𝑚 ∈ ℕ. The number of
time frames must therefore be the same as the geometric
dimension 𝑁.
The consequences of this limiting assumption can be
mitigated so that if the number of time frames is greater
than 𝑁, they are divided into sets of cardinality 𝑁 (the last
of them will thus have cardinality ≤ 𝑁). If the number of
time frames is less than 𝑁, the missing time frames will be
filled in, and their voxels will be set as homogeneously
passive. Each completed frame will contribute 𝑁 3 voxels
to the total number of voxels of all time frames. Each set of
frames will be then compressed to spatio-temporal SVO.

Another option to mitigate this consequence is to adjust
the geometric dimensions of the scene, when 𝑁 = 2𝑚 is
adjusted to 𝑁1 = 2𝑚1 , where 𝑚1 > 𝑚. The number of
coded time frames can thus become ≤ 𝑁1 . If the value of 𝑚
is increased by 1, the number of voxels of each time frame
is increased by 7 × 𝑁 3 .
Both, increasing the number of time frames and
increasing the geometric resolution of the scene, contribute
a large number of new voxels to the grid of scene voxels.
However, although large subspaces of the scene are
created, they are homogeneously filled with passive
(empty) voxels. The binary representation of such
homogeneous passive subspaces of scenes using
hierarchical data structures such as SVO is extremely
economical, so the addition of these passive voxels will not
significantly affect the overall size of the binary
representation of the data structures.

4. RESULTS
In the tests we used three models, Angel Lucy, Skull
and Porsche, originally stored as polygonal surface models
composed of a mesh of triangles. Angel Lucy was
composed from 488,880 triangles; Skull from 80,016
triangles; and Porsche from 22,011 triangles. Subsequently,
they were each stored in their own scene and these scenes
were voxelized into two resolutions 1283 and 2563 voxels.
The number of active voxels of individual static threedimensional scenes is shown in Table I. Visualizations of
these scenes can be seen in Fig. 2.
In all tests, the number of time frames generated was
identical to the geometric resolution of the scene. In the
case of scenes with a dimension of 1283, it was 128 time
frames, in the case of a resolution of 2563, it was 256 time
frames.
The temporal variability of the scenes was achieved by
moving the models in the linear trajectory in one or more
of the 𝑥, 𝑦 and 𝑧 axes of the scene, both in terms of
trajectory and speed. In each time frame, the object in the
scene moved by one voxel in the given axis, so it can be
said that position of the model in the scene was the function
of the number of time frame.
In tests, movements of objects in scenes were realized
in the manner that in the test no. 1 movement was in the 𝑥
axis, in the test no. 2 movement was in the 𝑦 axis, in the
test no. 3 movement was in the 𝑧 axis, in the test no. 4
movement was in the 𝑥 and 𝑦 axis, in the test no. 5
movement was in the 𝑥 and 𝑧 axis and in the test no. 6
movement was in the 𝑥, 𝑦 and 𝑧 axis.
The compaction of the binary representation of the
information regarded the geometry, that was achieved in
tests, ranged from 1.73 to 2.08 times when spatio-temporal
SVO was used instead of the set of SVOs, as it can be seen
in Table II.

5. CONCLUSIONS
The paper dealt with the issue of coding the information
regarding the geometry of voxelized three-dimensional
scenes using domain-specific hierarchical data structures
based on octant trees and directed acyclic graphs including
SVOs, CSVOs, SVDAGs, SSVDAGs and others. The
paper moves from static scenes and concentrates on the
coding of the time-variable scenes geometry. In
consideration were two different approaches. First
approach treated each time frame as a static 3D scene and
used classical SVO HDS to store the geometry. Thus, an
ordered set of SVOs was created. The second approach
considered the time-variable scene as a 4D scene and used
spatio-temporal Sparse Voxel Octree HDS to store its
geometry in one HDS.

Performed tests showed that the use of spatio-temporal
Sparse Voxel Octree enables a more compact

scenes compared to the set of SVOs. The compaction of the
binary representation of this information, that was achieved
in tests, using spatio-temporal SVO instead of the set of
SVOs ranged from 1.73 to 2.08 times.
The following research in this topic will be focused on
the development of a more sophisticated hierarchical data
structures that are able to use time coherency between
frames, while the influence of time variability factors on the
achieved compression ratios also will be considered.
