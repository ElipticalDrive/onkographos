# Spherical Perlin-Style Noise Generation

This project implements **procedural noise directly on the surface of a sphere**, designed to reduce **anisotropy**, **polar artifacts**, and **seams** that commonly arise when adapting planar noise methods (e.g. classic Perlin noise) to spherical domains.

The implementation focuses on **geometric correctness**, **controlled randomness**, and **practical performance**, making it suitable for scientific visualization, rendering, and procedural planet generation.

---

## Motivation

Traditional Perlin-style noise is defined on Euclidean grids. When mapped onto a sphere, these methods often introduce:

- Directional bias (anisotropy)
- Discontinuities or seams
- Distortion near the poles
- Unnecessary computational overhead

The goal of this project was to design a **Perlin-inspired noise function that natively operates on the sphere**, preserving isotropy and continuity **without requiring unmanageable computation**.

---

## High-Level Approach

The algorithm works in two phases:

1. **Construct well-distributed point sets on the sphere**
2. **Evaluate noise using local geometric relationships**
