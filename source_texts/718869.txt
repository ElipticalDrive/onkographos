Why
The roadmap says "Rendition's efficiency with many thousands of objects still has to be measured and improved." Colony and city builders draw tens of thousands of buildings, props and characters. We need numbers first, then the fixes the numbers point at.
How other engines do it
Unity's GPU Resident Drawer (BatchRendererGroup) keeps instance data on the GPU across frames, and only uploads what changed.
Unreal keeps scene primitives in GPU buffers and does culling and LOD selection on the GPU, then issues indirect draws.
Crowds use GPU skinning (compute) and sometimes vertex animation textures, so thousands of animated characters stay cheap.
Proposed approach
Benchmarks first (in tests or tools, runnable with --profile):
City: 50k static props across 200 models.
Colony: 2,000 skinned characters walking, plus 10k props.
Forest: 200k instances of 10 tree and grass models.
Record CPU frame time, GPU time per pass, draw calls and allocations, and keep a results table in profiling.md.
Persistent instance data: static (Cached) draws upload once and stay on the GPU. Only changed transforms are uploaded each frame.
GPU culling and LOD: a compute pass does frustum culling and LOD selection, and fills indirect draw commands with vkCmdDrawIndexedIndirectCount, for the camera and each shadow cascade. This is the base for Occlusion culling #27.
Compute skinning or animation instancing for crowds, if the colony benchmark shows skinning is the bottleneck.
Done when
The benchmark scenes exist, run in CI or on demand, and their before and after numbers are in the PR and in profiling.md.
No managed allocations per frame (--check).
roadmap.md (large numbers of objects) and design.md are updated.
