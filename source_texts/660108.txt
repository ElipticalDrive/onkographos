Issue body actions
A texture today is either absent or fully resident: every mip level loads before it can be drawn, and it is evicted whole. Textures should stream by mip level instead.
Goals
Smallest mips first: a texture's small mip levels (512² and below are about 0.4% of a 4096² texture) load before the rest, so a scene appears blurred almost at once and sharpens as the larger levels arrive.
Only the mips that are needed: a texture loads only the levels its size on screen requires.
Top mips go first under memory pressure: the budget can reclaim a texture's largest levels and keep the rest drawing, instead of evicting it whole.
Constraints
A descriptor that a frame in flight may read cannot be rewritten, so a texture cannot switch from a partial view to a full one in place. One way that fits: one view over the full mip chain, with each bindless slot carrying its first resident mip, which shaders apply as a minimum level of detail (shaderResourceMinLod). Images being filled stay in GENERAL layout, where copying and sampling are both valid.
KTX2 stores each mip level at a known offset, so the small levels can be read separately from the rest of the file.
To decide
How it relates to the Streamer (Streamer: budgeted residency of paged data #286), which can also serve large textures, and whether textures become one of its clients.
How screen-space mip needs are measured (GPU feedback or CPU estimate).
How resident mips count in the shared memory budget and its eviction order (Automatic eviction priority for cached resources and streamed pages #283).
Done when
A scene's textures appear with their small mips first and sharpen without stalls.
Under an artificially small budget, textures lose their top mips before being evicted whole.
No validation messages.
Depends on #285, designed alongside #286.
Pack data
None: engine work, independent of any pack.

The cdlod branch adds the mechanism, used by terrain cube maps only:
StreamedMips kinds (CubeMap, the client's HeightMap) keep resident only the levels their MipHandles demand (MipDemand). A reload reads the missing levels from the KTX2 file; the shared levels are copied on the GPU in the frame's command buffer (Resources::record_mip_copies), in optimal layouts. That's the Unreal shape, rather than GENERAL layout or a minLod per slot.
Levels no longer wanted stay for the demand's retention (terrain.texture-retention), or until the budget needs them.
Still open here:
2D textures and texture arrays as StreamedMips kinds.
Demand from screen size (GPU feedback or a CPU estimate), instead of the distance-based demand terrain states.
Under budget pressure, dropping demanded levels with the smallest on-screen error first. This needs admission logic so loads and evictions don't thrash.
