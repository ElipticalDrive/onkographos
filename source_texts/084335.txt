Follow-up to the survey of computer-graphics methods for the radiation code (2026-09-26). Conditional: worth building only once a graded VoxelAbsorption medium is the cost in a real study; today the Sozzi work uses UniformAbsorption.
The cost it attacks
With a graded medium, every (receiver, facet) pair walks the absorbance grid along its own segment (VoxelAbsorption.optical_depth, nx + ny + nz + 1 Simpson pieces a pair). #528 made that walk carry its total and go in passes, so its memory is bounded, but its cost is still n_receivers x n_facets x (nx + ny + nz) per call. On record (#528): the graded per-call field is ~19-21 s against ~0.4 s uniform, on a 4,096-facet lamp, 3,954 receivers and a 12 x 12 x 16 grid; at mesh scale that ratio multiplies the whole gather.
What rendering does
Shadow maps (Williams 1978) and deep shadow maps (Lokovic & Veach, SIGGRAPH 2000): rather than trace every receiver's path to the light, compute the transmittance from the light's side once, into a structure indexed by position, and look it up per receiver. Deep shadow maps store the transmittance as a function of depth along each light ray, for participating media.
Light-space transmittance volumes in volume rendering are the same idea on a 3D grid: march once from the light through the medium, filling a grid of transmittance values, and read each shaded point by interpolation.
How it would look here
For each emitter cluster (a group of lamp facets, from #565, or the lamp as a whole for a distant receiver), march outward through the absorbance grid once and store the optical depth from that cluster to every grid sample point. A receiver then reads tau from its cluster's volume by trilinear interpolation, which is O(1) instead of a walk. The volume is a JAX function of the absorbance field, so dG/d(absorbance) stays exact through it.
The hard parts
It is an approximation twice over: one representative point per cluster for the path's origin, and interpolation of tau between samples. tau is smooth in the receiver position for a fixed origin, but not near the origin itself, so the near field keeps its exact walk.
Storage: one grid of optical depths per cluster. Fine for tens of clusters on a coarse absorbance grid; not for one per facet.
It depends on clustering: without emitter clusters, one volume per facet costs more than the walk it replaces.
Acceptance
G against the exact walk on a graded medium, near the lamp and elsewhere, at a stated cluster count and grid.
Wall clock and memory against the exact walk in one process, and dG/d(absorbance) against a finite difference.
