Abstract Reconstructing 3D Computed Tomography (CT) images from a few X-ray projections is a highly ill-posed inverse problem due to the loss of volumetric information. We propose PhyDiCT a training-free framework that integrates a differentiable Physics-based forward model, grounded in the Beer–Lambert law, with a text-conditioned Diffusion as a strong prior to reconstruct 3D lung CT images. We refer to our approach as training-free since the prior model is used without fine-tuning, and our goal is to steer the denoising procedure to generate samples consistent with X-ray observations. We guide the diffusion generation using Split Gibbs sampling to jointly optimize for projection fidelity (reward) and consistency with prior knowledge. Also, we introduce a test-time refinement step that enhances image realism and anatomical coherence. We extensively evaluate our method on publicly available 3D CT datasets using both perceptual and semantic metrics, demonstrating that it surpasses existing plug-and-play diffusion and fully trained reconstruction approaches. Our findings highlight that combining a strong generative prior with the underlying physics of image formation substantially improves reconstruction quality, e.g., 7.5% improvement on SSIM compared to full training methods. Code will be released at https://github.com/batmanlab/PhyDiCT.git. We are encouraged by the positive comments acknowledging our contributions in CT reconstruction with a text-guided prior and a physics-based reward function (R1, R2, R3), as well as the solid experimental demonstrations and result discussions (R2, R3). We address the key concerns below: 1) R1 & R2–Out-of-domain (noised) observation & LPIPS incorporation Thank R1 for pointing LPIPS is not coercive, you are correct. We found that observation methods such as DeepDRR produce realistic noise patterns and beam hardening effect, which are not differentiable. Therefore, the discrepancy introduces domain shifts with respect to our rendering method in Eq.4.Thus, LPIPS term is used as an additional regularization of guidance for perceptual consistency to address out-of-domain observation shifts, where the diffusion trajectory is guided partially by the perceptual gradient. We will add the ablation study without LPIPS term, and revise LPIPS incorporation to make the distinction clearer. 2) R1–Novelty of SGS and EC & R2–Novelty Claims & R3–Explanation of EC We do not claim SGS, nor SDEdit, as our contribution. Instead, we claim the following contributions:1) Incorporating a physical 3D-to-2D projection into the reward function with both MSE and LPIPS regularization (Tab. 1, two rendering settings); 2) Training-free lung 3D CT reconstruction from sparse X-ray (from reward function) and a text-guided prior; 3) A post-hoc EC step to remove artifacts (Fig. 2 last 2 columns, Tab. 1 “w/o EC”). We acknowledge the high-level resemblance between our EC step and DAPS (Zhang et al.), in that both involve adding noise. However, the motivation and mechanism differ substantially. 2.1) Motivation: DAPS aims to solve complicated nonlinear inverse problems through posterior exploration and Langevin-style sampling. However, our EC goal is artifact suppression and manifold alignment after posterior guidance. 2.2) Mechanism: DAPS introduces Decoupled Noise Annealing at each timestep together with posterior guidance. However, our EC is a post-hoc artifact-correction stage, where we add noise through the DDPM forward process and apply denoising steps only (Eqs. 1–2), without reward guidance, after the inverse problem has been solved. The goal is to produce visually cleaner reconstructions (Fig. 2 last 2 columns) and aligning with the data manifold closely. We also theoretically proved the manifold alignment (EC drift is within an upper-bound) but did not include it due to page limit. 3) R1–Studying the effect of text We show quantitative results in Tab. 1 under “ours w/o text.” Removing text slightly degrades CLIP-T2I performance (as expected without text guidance), while focusing more on image quality (CLIP-I2I improves). The remaining metrics stay largely on par with main results. 4) R1–Notation & minor clarification We will provide more details of CBCT, and we will modify the notation. We cited DAPS, and we will cite the other two. We refer to our model as plug-and-play because it requires no training on the target dataset. The text guidance is intended as a strong prior. 5) R2–Two optimization steps per iteration The gradient steps can be viewed as proximal steps for sampler. SGS formulated this procedure as posterior sampling. Therefore, the optimization step is posterior sampling, and two steps follow prior methods such as DAPS and PnP-DM (Tab. 1). 6) R3–Trade off CLIP-T2I & Application scenario of full-training methods The lower CLIP-T2I score in Tab. 1 can be explained by the in-domain training of X2CT and Xray2CTPA, where all other methods adopt a prior model (MedSyn) trained on a different dataset, and it is used as a plug-in module without fine-tuning. If sufficient in-domain paired data are available, full-training is preferred. But for good performance across out-of-distribution observations / datasets, posterior sampling methods are preferred. Please describe the contribution of the paper
This paper proposes PhyDiCT, a plug-and-play framework for reconstructing 3D lung CT volumes from sparse 2D X-ray projections. The method combines three key components: (1) a physics-based differentiable forward model grounded in the Beer–Lambert law to simulate X-ray projections from candidate CT volumes, (2) a pre-trained text-conditioned 3D diffusion model as a strong anatomical prior, and (3) an iterative guidance strategy based on Split Gibbs sampling to balance projection fidelity with prior realism during inference. In addition, the paper introduces a test-time refinement step (EC) to further improve image realism and anatomical consistency. Experiments on public CT datasets with synthetically generated X-rays show improvements over both fully trained reconstruction baselines and prior plug-and-play diffusion methods.

Author Feedback
We thank all reviewers (R1, R2, R3) for their informative feedback and valuable suggestions. We are encouraged by the positive comments acknowledging our contributions in CT reconstruction with a text-guided prior and a physics-based reward function (R1, R2, R3), as well as the solid experimental demonstrations and result discussions (R2, R3). We address the key concerns below:
1) R1 & R2–Out-of-domain (noised) observation & LPIPS incorporation
Thank R1 for pointing LPIPS is not coercive, you are correct.
We found that observation methods such as DeepDRR produce realistic noise patterns and beam hardening effect, which are not differentiable. Therefore, the discrepancy introduces domain shifts with respect to our rendering method in Eq.4.Thus, LPIPS term is used as an additional regularization of guidance for perceptual consistency to address out-of-domain observation shifts, where the diffusion trajectory is guided partially by the perceptual gradient. We will add the ablation study without LPIPS term, and revise LPIPS incorporation to make the distinction clearer.
2) R1–Novelty of SGS and EC & R2–Novelty Claims & R3–Explanation of EC
We do not claim SGS, nor SDEdit, as our contribution. Instead, we claim the following contributions:1) Incorporating a physical 3D-to-2D projection into the reward function with both MSE and LPIPS regularization (Tab. 1, two rendering settings); 2) Training-free lung 3D CT reconstruction from sparse X-ray (from reward function) and a text-guided prior; 3) A post-hoc EC step to remove artifacts (Fig. 2 last 2 columns, Tab. 1 “w/o EC”).
We acknowledge the high-level resemblance between our EC step and DAPS (Zhang et al.), in that both involve adding noise. However, the motivation and mechanism differ substantially.
2.1) Motivation: DAPS aims to solve complicated nonlinear inverse problems through posterior exploration and Langevin-style sampling. However, our EC goal is artifact suppression and manifold alignment after posterior guidance.
2.2) Mechanism: DAPS introduces Decoupled Noise Annealing at each timestep together with posterior guidance. However, our EC is a post-hoc artifact-correction stage, where we add noise through the DDPM forward process and apply denoising steps only (Eqs. 1–2), without reward guidance, after the inverse problem has been solved. The goal is to produce visually cleaner reconstructions (Fig. 2 last 2 columns) and aligning with the data manifold closely. We also theoretically proved the manifold alignment (EC drift is within an upper-bound) but did not include it due to page limit.
3) R1–Studying the effect of text
We show quantitative results in Tab. 1 under “ours w/o text.” Removing text slightly degrades CLIP-T2I performance (as expected without text guidance), while focusing more on image quality (CLIP-I2I improves). The remaining metrics stay largely on par with main results.
4) R1–Notation & minor clarification
We will provide more details of CBCT, and we will modify the notation. We cited DAPS, and we will cite the other two. We refer to our model as plug-and-play because it requires no training on the target dataset. The text guidance is intended as a strong prior.
5) R2–Two optimization steps per iteration
The gradient steps can be viewed as proximal steps for sampler. SGS formulated this procedure as posterior sampling. Therefore, the optimization step is posterior sampling, and two steps follow prior methods such as DAPS and PnP-DM (Tab. 1).
6) R3–Trade off CLIP-T2I & Application scenario of full-training methods
The lower CLIP-T2I score in Tab. 1 can be explained by the in-domain training of X2CT and Xray2CTPA, where all other methods adopt a prior model (MedSyn) trained on a different dataset, and it is used as a plug-in module without fine-tuning. If sufficient in-domain paired data are available, full-training is preferred. But for good performance across out-of-distribution observations / datasets, posterior sampling methods are preferred.
