Summary
The README no longer refers to a previous project. It removes:
the comparison table;
the line about ported shaders;
the old 3D size cap.
The introduction presents FERRUM as a new viewer that combines peer-reviewed volume-visualization techniques in one modern single-pass GPU pipeline.
The Rendering section has a table of techniques, each tied to its publication: Levoy 1988, Max 1995, Krüger & Westermann 2003, Hadwiger et al. 2005, Engel et al. 2006, Wallis et al. 1989, Stewart 2003 and Hernell et al. 2010. A new References section gives the full citations. I checked each listed technique against volume.wgsl, and all of them are implemented: front-to-back compositing, opacity correction, early ray termination, Phong shading, bisection refinement, empty-space skipping and ambient occlusion.
The Performance section now names the test machine: Intel Xeon 2.8 GHz with 4 cores, 16 GB RAM, llvmpipe from Mesa 25.2 (a software Vulkan rasteriser, no hardware GPU), and a release build with Rust 1.98. It keeps only FERRUM's own numbers, adding the NIfTI and 19-slice load times.
dev_plan.md and docs/architecture.md drop the old feature-mapping notes.
ADRs stay as written, because they are append-only history.

Documentation only.
