Most existing real-time volumetric cloud rendering algorithms adopt ray marching due to its simple and flexible implementation.However， since the shading results of marching sample points exhibit strong inter-frame correlation that traditional methods fail to exploit， rendering performance remains suboptimal.To address this issue， this paper proposes a rendering algorithm based on the temporal view frustum.Specifically， ray marching sampling points are alternately assigned to different working modes.In this setup， heavy sample points update 3D history texture data， while light sample points reuse historical data， with their roles switching in the subsequent frame.By distributing the sampling workload across multiple frames temporally and across different sampling points spatially， the proposed method significantly reduces the number of secondary ray marching sampling points required for calculating shadow transmittance， thereby achieving efficient rendering.Experimental results show that， given the same number of samples， the proposed method maintains minor overall rendering quality loss while achieving significant improvements in rendering efficiency.In the ground-level test scene， compared to the uniform step-length method， the proposed approach exhibits an average PSNR loss of 3.05% and 19.48% under low and high sampling count conditions， respectively.However， under all conditions， the average SSIM loss remains below 0.6%， while rendering performance improves by an average of 33.91%.
