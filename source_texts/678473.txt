Expands the Eiffel example to voxelize the tower and compare to trimesh — a deliberately different case from the watertight bunny.
The Eiffel is an open, high-genus lattice (a truss: 0.1 % boundary edges, Euler ≈ −12 000 — thousands of through-holes), so solid voxelization is ill-defined:
Solid occupancy (our exact ray-parity) fills only the genuinely enclosed region — sparse on a lattice, but it still recovers the tower's core (spine, spire, legs). Honest counterpart to the bunny, where a watertight input makes solid occupancy well-defined (IoU 0.85 there).
Surface voxelization is the well-defined notion for an open surface. Our signed-distance field gives it (|sdf| < 0.7*spacing, defined for any mesh) and matches trimesh's surface voxels at IoU 0.70 / Dice 0.83 (ours 2650 vs trimesh 2526 cells).
```
our occupancy (solid core): 1111 cells;  SDF surface shell: 2650 cells
surface vs trimesh: IoU=0.704 Dice=0.827
```
Light decimation (cm.simplify(grid=120), ~35k of 140k tris) only to keep the brute-force SDF tractable — occupancy runs on the full mesh. trimesh is optional (skipped with a note if absent). Example + docs + image only; no core change.
