DUALF-C: Disentangled Light Field Compression
with Entropy-Aware Bitstream Generation
Soheib Takhtardeshir∗† , Roger Olsson∗ , Christine Guillemot† and Mårten Sjöström∗
∗ Department of Computer and Electrical Engineering, Mid Sweden University, Sundsvall, Sweden
† INRIA, Rennes, France
Email: {firstname.lastname}@miun.se, {firstname.lastname}@inria.fr
Abstract—Recent advancements in learned light field
(LF) image compression highlight the advantages of
modeling spatial and angular redundancies using deep
generative models. Among these, Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have shown strong potential in learning
compact latent representations of LF data. However,
many existing approaches rely on simple uniform quantization and basic entropy coding, which limits compression efficiency in practical applications. This paper
introduces DUALF-C, a lightweight and retrainingfree compression pipeline that augments pretrained
VAE-based LF compression models using structured
post-encoding transformations. The proposed framework integrates bitplane slicing, latent channel reordering, non-uniform quantization, and patch-based
vector quantization to improve bitrate efficiency while
preserving reconstruction quality. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that DUALF-C significantly reduces bit-per-pixel (BPP) without degrading image
quality, making it a practical solution for bandwidthconstrained immersive imaging systems.
Index Terms—Light field compression, variational
autoencoder, quantization, feature reordering, vector
quantization
I. Introduction
Light field (LF) imaging captures spatial and angular
scene information, enabling applications such as immersive rendering, depth estimation, and view synthesis [1],
[2]. However, its high dimensionality imposes substantial
storage and transmission demands [3].
Traditional LF compression often adapts video coding
standards like HEVC, or frameworks such as JPEG Pleno,
using pseudo-sequences, disparity compensation, or transform coding [4]–[6]. While effective, such methods lack
scalability and adaptability to real-world LF statistics.
Learned compression offers data-driven representations
via deep neural networks [7], [8]. Variational Autoencoders
(VAEs) are especially suited for LF compression, as they
disentangle spatial and angular features into compact
latent codes [9], [10].
Despite progress, most VAE-based LF compressors still
use uniform quantization and basic entropy coding, limiting practical efficiency [11], [12]. This opens opportunities

to integrate classical coding with modern latent frameworks.
We build on a VAE-based framework that disentangles
spatial and angular LF features into two latent maps [9]. A
later extension, referred to as DUALF-D, introduced dual
hyperpriors to model the entropy of each latent more effectively [13]. In this work, we propose a fully post-encoding
pipeline that applies entropy-optimized transformations
without modifying or retraining the model. DUALF-D
serves as the fixed pretrained baseline throughout our
experiments.
Our contributions in DUALF-C extends DUALF-D to
include four key post-processing modules: bitplane slicing
for bit-level entropy shaping, channel reordering for progressive transmission, analytic non-uniform quantization
aligned with latent priors, and patch-based vector quantization for exploiting local redundancy. These components
can be composed into hybrid pipelines tailored to different
compression goals.
Experiments show that DUALF-C reduces BPP
while maintaining high quality, confirming that postencoding enhancements can extend learned compression
in bandwidth-limited scenarios.
II. Background & Related Work
Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have become a cornerstone of learned image compression, offering principled
latent variable models for mapping images into compact
representations [7], [11]. Their effectiveness is further
enhanced by hyperpriors that enable adaptive entropy
modeling, resulting in state-of-the-art compression performance for natural images. These approaches serve as the
foundational architecture for many learned compression
systems.
To extend these methods to light field (LF) data, recent
works have adopted various strategies to manage angular
redundancy while preserving spatial structure [14], [15].
Some treat LF images as pseudo-video sequences, applying
temporal models to angular views, while others leverage
multi-stream architectures to disentangle spatial and angular components. These strategies have improved the
expressiveness of LF latent representations.
Despite architectural innovations, many learned compression methods continue to rely on basic quantization
and coding steps, typically uniform quantization followed
by arithmetic or range coding [7], [12]. While sufficient
for initial prototypes, these simple techniques leave substantial room for improvement, particularly for practical
deployment in bandwidth-constrained environments.
Classical compression literature offers a rich set of tools
that remain underexplored in deep models. These include
non-uniform quantization techniques designed to match
statistical priors [16], entropy-optimized vector quantization methods [17], and bitplane-based coding schemes for
progressive transmission [18]. Integrating these tools with
learned models offers a pathway to better rate–distortion
performance without requiring architectural retraining.
This work draws inspiration from both domains, proposing a hybrid compression pipeline that leverages classical
tools on the latent representations produced by a pretrained VAE-based LF compression model. Our approach
highlights the complementary strengths of conventional
and learned compression in building scalable and efficient
systems.
III. Methodology
This work enhances a pretrained variational autoencoder (VAE)-based compression model for light field (LF)
images by introducing a modular, post-encoding pipeline.
Unlike end-to-end learned schemes, our approach applies
compression optimizations directly on the latent representations without altering the encoder or retraining the network. These post-processing steps are designed to exploit
statistical structures in the latents while maintaining full
compatibility with the original decoder.
We introduce four compression modules, each targeting
a specific source of redundancy: (1) bit-level entropy,
(2) inter-channel importance, (3) prior-matching quantization, and (4) patch-level similarity. Each module operates
independently and is fully invertible, allowing flexible
composition without loss of reconstruction fidelity.
A. Bitplane Slicing for Entropy Shaping
To reduce bit cost without added distortion, we apply
bitplane slicing after quantization. Compatible with both
uniform and non-uniform schemes, this technique decomposes each latent value into binary bitplanes, allowing
entropy coding to exploit bit-level redundancy.
Since quantized latents follow a zero-centered Gaussianlike distribution, most values cluster near zero, resulting
in high redundancy in the most significant bits (MSBs).
We convert each value to an 8-bit fixed-point binary form
and split the tensor into n bitplanes per channel.
Each bitplane is then compressed independently using a
static entropy coder. While zlib or range coding suffice for
prototyping, more efficient options like arithmetic coding
or ANS are preferred in practice.
This binary decomposition concentrates entropy in
fewer bitplanes, improving coding efficiency and enabling
progressive decoding.
B. Latent Channel Reordering for Prioritization
To support progressive transmission, we reorder latent
channels based on their reconstruction importance. Some
channels carry critical structural cues, while others encode
less relevant details. After quantization, uniform or nonuniform, we assess channel importance using metrics like
entropy, variance, or mean absolute magnitude. Channels
are sorted by significance, and the reordered latents are encoded accordingly. The permutation is sent as lightweight
metadata to restore the original layout at the decoder.
Since reordering often introduces structured sparsity,
we apply run-length encoding (RLE) to further reduce
redundancy before entropy coding. This prioritization improves rate–distortion performance and supports efficient
streaming under bandwidth constraints.
C. Analytic Non-Uniform Quantization Aligned with Latent Prior
To improve quantization efficiency beyond uniform binning, we replace uniform quantization with analytic nonuniform quantizers that align better with the prior distribution of latent values. VAE-based models often assume a
zero-mean Gaussian or Laplacian prior over latents, which
implies that most values cluster tightly around zero.
Instead of learning quantizers from data or histograms,
we apply fixed analytic functions such as µ-law or logarithmic quantizers that concentrate bins near the origin. During encoding, each latent value is transformed
using the quantizer’s forward mapping, discretized, and
then entropy coded. At decoding, a deterministic inverse
mapping is applied to restore the dequantized value. This
quantizer design reduces entropy near the origin without
introducing training overhead, thereby lowering bitrates
while maintaining reconstruction quality.
D. Patch-Based Vector Quantization for Local Redundancy
To exploit local spatial and cross-channel redundancy
within latent tensors, we apply vector quantization (VQ)
at the patch level. Latent feature maps often contain
recurring patterns that can be effectively clustered and
represented using a compact codebook.
We divide the concatenated latent representation,
composed of spatial and angular branches, into nonoverlapping 3D patches (e.g., 2×2×C), where C is the
number of channels. These patches are flattened and
matched against a pre-computed codebook of representative vectors trained using K-means clustering. During
encoding, each patch is replaced by the index of its nearest
codeword. At decoding, patches are reconstructed via a
simple codebook lookup. This operation greatly reduces
transmission cost in high-dimensional latent spaces with
internal redundancy.
Compression Strategies in DUALF-C
We define four compression strategies that operate on
the latent representations produced by the encoder, referred to here as post-encoding transformations.1 These
strategies can be evaluated independently or combined
into hybrid pipelines to support different compression
objectives and deployment scenarios.
Strategy 1: Bitplane Slicing with Static Coding.
This pipeline first applies uniform quantization to the latent representations. The quantized values are then sliced
into 8-bit binary planes, which are individually compressed
using static entropy coders. The objective is to enhance
entropy shaping while preserving reconstruction fidelity.
Bitplane slicing exposes bit-level redundancy and enables
efficient compression with minimal added complexity or
quantization loss.
Strategy 2: Latent Channel Reordering. After
quantization, either uniform or non-uniform, the latent
channels are ranked based on importance measures such as
entropy or variance. The reordered channels are transmitted along with permutation metadata to ensure correct reconstruction. This method aims to improve rate allocation
and support progressive transmission. By prioritizing highimpact channels, it enhances compression performance
under bandwidth constraints and enables graceful degradation when only partial data is available.
Strategy 3: Prior-Aware Non-Uniform Quantization. This approach replaces uniform quantization with
a fixed non-uniform quantizer, such as µ-law [19], to
align quantization bins with the latent distribution. The
quantized values are then compressed using static entropy
coding and later reconstructed via a deterministic inverse
mapping. Tailoring the quantizer to match a Gaussianlike prior reduces bitrate while maintaining reconstruction
quality and sample consistency.
Strategy 4: Patch-Based Vector Quantization.
The latent space is divided into 3D patches, and each patch
is assigned to a representative codeword from a static
vector quantization codebook. Instead of transmitting fullprecision values, only the patch indices are stored and
transmitted. This pipeline captures spatial redundancy
using discrete patch-level representations. Patch-based
clustering allows for significant compression by leveraging repetitive local patterns observed across spatial and
angular views.
Hybrid Pipelines
While each Strategy can be applied independently, certain combinations are compatible and offer complementary
benefits:
• S1 + S2: Uniform quantization with bitplane slicing
and channel prioritization for progressive transmission
and entropy shaping.

IV. Results and Analyses
We evaluate our compression strategies using a pretrained dual-branch VAE on light field images from the
EPFL dataset. All post-encoding transformations are applied without retraining or modifying the encoder–decoder
structure. We report bit-per-pixel (BPP), PSNR, and inference time to assess module-level impact on compression
performance.
A. Experimental Protocol
We use the EPFL light field dataset [20], which provides 15×15 sub-aperture views at 625×434 resolution. To
avoid vignetting and distortion in corner views, we crop
each sample to the central 13×13 views. Each sample is
processed by the pretrained VAE encoder [13] to produce
a 128-channel latent tensor of spatial size 8×12. Postencoding modules are applied to this tensor, followed by
zlib-based entropy coding. We report BPP, PSNR, and
runtime using consistent hardware [13]. This controlled
setup isolates the effect of each transformation and ensures
fair, reproducible comparisons.
B. Strategy -Level Evaluation
Bitplane Slicing (S1) enhances entropy shaping by
decomposing quantized latents into binary planes. It
achieves a 10–15% BPP reduction over uniform quantization, with negligible quality loss when all bitplanes
are preserved. Discarding lower planes (e.g., retaining 6–7
MSBs) leads to a minor PSNR drop (0.2–0.4 dB). Runtime
increases slightly due to per-plane entropy coding, but
remains stable across scenes.
Latent Channel Reordering (S2) prioritizes informative latent channels by ranking them based on entropy
or variance. On its own, this yields a modest BPP gain
(2–5%), but significantly improves progressive decoding by
allocating bits to critical structure early in the stream.
For further compression, channels below a significance
threshold can be omitted, and reordered tensors often
exhibit structured sparsity that we exploit using runlength encoding (RLE). These enhancements introduce
minimal runtime overhead while improving rate–distortion
performance.
Prior-Aware Non-Uniform Quantization (S3)
aligns quantization bins with the latent distribution using fixed analytic functions such as µ-law. Compared to
uniform quantization, this reduces BPP by 10–15% and
may increase PSNR by up to 0.5 dB, particularly in lowenergy regions. The forward and inverse mappings are
table-driven and fast, making this module highly efficient
and training-free.
Patch-Based Vector Quantization (S4) captures
spatial and cross-channel redundancy by clustering local
3D latent patches into a fixed codebook.This module
delivers the largest single-method BPP savings, typically
15–25%, while preserving visual quality using a compact
256-codeword vocabulary. Larger codebooks can further
enhance fidelity at high bitrates but come with additional
runtime and memory costs. Although computationally
more demanding due to nearest-neighbor search, it is
particularly effective on repetitive scene structures and
justifies the added latency in exchange for higher compression efficiency.

C. Hybrid Pipeline Performance
Hybrid configurations of the proposed modules deliver
further gains. Their effectiveness depends on both the
content structure and the compression goal:
• S4+S2 (Patch VQ + Reordering) achieves the
best overall performance, yielding up to 31% BPP
reduction with minimal PSNR loss and strong streaming performance.
• S3+S2 (Non-Uniform + Reordering) performs
best for low-frequency or distribution-aligned scenes,
Our results show that structured post-encoding modules
significantly improve compression efficiency when applied
to a pretrained VAE-based LF model (DUALF-D). Without retraining, we reduce bitrate by up to 31% on EPFL
data, with minimal visual quality loss and modest runtime
increase. These lightweight methods suit practical scenarios where decoder simplicity and retraining avoidance
are key. Hybrid pipelines offer tunable trade-offs between
efficiency and complexity, supporting both streaming and
offline modes.
V. Conclusion
We introduced DUALF-C, a modular framework that
enhances pretrained VAE-based LF compression via postencoding transformations. By applying bitplane slicing,
channel reordering, non-uniform quantization, and patchbased vector quantization, we improve compression performance without altering the model or retraining. Each
module targets a distinct latent redundancy and supports
flexible deployment scenarios. Experiments confirm that
DUALF-C achieves lower bitrates and competitive visual
quality at practical inference times. This post-training
approach bridges learned and classical compression techniques and may extend to temporal LF data and perceptual tasks in immersive systems.
