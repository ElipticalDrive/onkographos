Hi!
In this implementation, a perspective projection matrix is used, but X-ray imaging (e.g., CBCT) typically follows a ​cone-beam projection model, which differs from standard perspective projection.
​1.Is the perspective projection matrix truly capable of accurately modeling CBCT's cone-beam geometry, or is this an approximation/coincidence?
2.If it’s an approximation, what are the limitations or assumptions being made?
3.I’m struggling to reconcile this with accurate CBCT projection modeling—could you clarify the rationale behind the choice of projection matrix?
Standard perspective projection matrices (e.g., OpenGL-style) assume a symmetric frustum and parallel rays converging at a single "eye" point, while CBCT uses a ​point source with divergent rays hitting a detector.
The standard perspective matrix maps 3D points to NDC via a homogeneous divide, but CBCT requires explicit ray parameterization.
How does the perspective matrix account for ​divergent rays and ​detector tilt (if applicable)?

Hi,
We follow TIGRE in modeling CBCT using a perspective camera model, which works well for both synthetic and real data. You can refer to this illustration (credit to SAX-NeRF) for a visual example: the camera center corresponds to the X-ray source, and the image plane represents the detector—although unlike RGB cameras, the detector is positioned behind the object.
Unfortunately, our current work does not account for ray divergence or detector tilt, which also poses challenges for vanilla 3DGS (or rasterization). We hope to address these limitations in future work. One possible way is to use per-ray volume rendering, like our previous work NAF, rather than per-image rasterization.
