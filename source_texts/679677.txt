3D Gaussian Splatting has emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time novel view synthesis, yet persistent floating artifacts fundamentally compromise its reconstruction fidelity. Through comprehensive analysis tracking primitive evolution across optimization, we reveal that these artifacts originate from initialization bias where fixed \(k\)-nearest neighbor sampling produces oversized primitives in sparse regions, creating "floater traps" that trigger cascading contamination. Building on this insight, we propose FFGS, a preemptive framework that eliminates artifacts at their source through intelligent initialization filtering. Rather than uniformly instantiating primitives from all Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points, FFGS evaluates each candidate's local reliability to dynamically calibrate initialization parameters. By filtering out unreliable SfM points that would spawn ill-conditioned primitives, our method establishes a geometrically consistent foundation from the outset, enabling subsequent optimization to focus on detail refinement rather than artifact mitigation. This preemptive filtering mechanism transforms 3DGS from artifact-prone to robust reconstruction. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FFGS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving superior reconstruction quality while exhibiting remarkable resilience to degraded SfM inputs that severely compromise existing approaches.
