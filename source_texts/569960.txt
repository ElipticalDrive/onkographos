feat: draw segmentations as isosurface meshes in the 3D view
…
836214a
```
Segmentations in the 3D view are now smoothed surfaces instead of a cloud of
boundary voxels; "Segs · points" in the 3D controls brings the points back.

The surfaces are computed in the browser from the store the 2D label
layers already read: the segmentation level is read through idetik's chunk
loader (the same contract ChannelViewSource uses), the finest level with at
most 32M voxels, or a single level coarsened by block maximum so structures
one voxel thin survive. A worker then meshes each value (label, instance,
or panoptic (label, instance) pair) with vtk.js: marching cubes on the
value's padded mask, windowed-sinc smoothing (as 3D Slicer smooths segment
surfaces) and point normals, oriented outward by signed volume. vtk.js is
loaded only in the worker (149 kB chunk); the main bundle grows by ~5 kB.
Above 1M triangles the volume is coarsened 2× and meshed again.

Meshes are coloured like the 2D layers (hidden and solo instance IDs,
panoptic objects / instances / both) and merged by colour, so draw calls
stay bounded by the number of colours. Colour changes regroup the meshes
without computing them again. They are drawn with idetik's MeshRenderable
through MeshObjectsLayer, from our own arrays in the layout idetik's GLB
loader produces (its Geometry class is not exported).

Vertices follow the copick convention (x/y/z, voxel i at i·spacing): the
full-resolution isosurface of 10521's boundary-clean valid-sample
segmentation matches the published mesh of the same name within a voxel.

fix: light 3D surfaces from above and below, so their undersides are …
…
40b83d3
```
…not dark

idetik's mesh shader has one light, fixed along (0, -1, 1), and leaves faces
turned away from it at ambient brightness, so surfaces seen from below were
flat and dark. Surfaces in the 3D view now share one lighting: a key light
from above and a fill light from below (no direction below 0.45; facing up
0.86, facing down 0.75). The segmentation points use it directly, and for
meshes it is folded into the normals passed to idetik (ambient 0, diffuse 1):
each normal becomes the unit vector whose dot product with idetik's light is
the brightness wanted, keeping its component across the light so shading
still follows the surface. Positions are unchanged; copick meshes get the same
lighting.
