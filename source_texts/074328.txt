Reduces world size by fixing compression of unused buffer space, sharing repeated metadata for empty cubes, and adding Zstd as the default compression. GZIP, LZ4 and uncompressed storage remain available.
Also adds automatic region compaction during server/world startup to reclaim unused space, with a loading progress bar in singleplayer and progress logs on servers. It does not run during gameplay or shutdown.
Existing compression settings are preserved, and existing worlds keep their storage format. Compact empty-cube storage is the default for new worlds.
The total save size was reduced by around 30-50%.

I did some testing with a ~1GB GTNH world (128 chunks pregen'd with DH)
While your compact approach works, I have an idea that might be simpler and more effective: We could skip cubes completely if we can easily regen them.
Most sectionless cubes carry almost no information: no blocks, entities, tile entities or ticks, and their only real state is a few flags plus the heightmap snapshot.
In this world that is 61% of all cubes. Instead of writing them, we store one state byte per cube Y in the column (region2d) and rebuild the cube directly on load, without running the generator. The byte holds:
skipped yes/no
population (0 or 255)
initLightDone, isSurfaceTracked
whether the saved heightmap is entirely below or entirely above the cube (cubes whose heightmap crosses them are written normally)
Conceptionally it's very similar to your idea, but it seems a lot simpler than the template approach.
Not sure how far you want to go, but there's also other things we can improve - if we make a new format we might as well make it as effective as we can rather than have multiple format changes in succession:
Clip:
Every cube stores LastHeightMap as 256 absolute Y values, but only their position relative to the cube matters. Clamping them to [cubeBaseY, cubeBaseY + 17] gives identical results, stays compatible both ways, and saves ~4.6% here.
Grouping:
The idea is to group cubes in 2x4x2 or 1x16x1 sections - currently our cubes are very small and we miss out on a lot of compression potential. Of course, this might have overhead for loading/saving.
Each record occupies whole 512 B sectors, and neighbouring cubes share most of their content, which per-record compression can't see. One Zstd stream per group of 16 cubes recovers nearly all of that. Saving a cube becomes read group, replace cube, recompress, write though.
Palette:
Per section, a palette of full block states:
(id = Blocks | Add<<8 | BlocksB2Hi<<12, meta = Data | Data1High<<4 | Data2<<8, so it covers the ChunkAPI arrays)
plus one byte per voxel, stored columnar inside the group.
GTNH sections average 14 states and none had more than 256. Zstd doesn't seem to do this on its own since the data is spread out too much.
Could also group columns, saves 38.9 -> 13.8 MB here
If you want I could PR some changes unto your PR?
