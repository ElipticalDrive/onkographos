A Beautiful Ray/Triangle Intersection Method
2014-05-23
Tavian Barnes
Comments
3D ray/triangle intersections are obviously an important part of much of computer graphics.
The Möller–Trumbore algorithm, for example, computes these intersections very quickly.
But there is another method that I believe is more elegant, and in some cases allows you to compute the intersection for “free.”
Say your triangle has corners at AAA, BBB, and CCC.
If your triangle is not degenerate, those three points define a plane.
Writing the vector from AAA to BBB as AB→=B→−A→\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{B} - \overrightarrow{A}AB=B−A, and similarly for AC→\overrightarrow{AC}AC, the normal vector perpendicular to that plane is given by
N→=AB→×AC→\overrightarrow{N} = \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
N=AB×AC
Since AB→\overrightarrow{AB}AB, AC→\overrightarrow{AC}AC, and N→\overrightarrow{N}N are all linearly independent, they form an alternative basis for all of 3-space.
That is, any point p→\overrightarrow{p}p​ can be represented by a unique triple ⟨u,v,w⟩\langle u, v, w \rangle⟨u,v,w⟩ such that
p⃗=u AB→+v AC→+w N→.\vec{p} = u\,\overrightarrow{AB} + v\,\overrightarrow{AC} + w\,\overrightarrow{N}.
p​=uAB+vAC+wN.
We can make this space even nicer by translating AAA to the origin, giving
p⃗=u AB→+v AC→+w N→+A→.\vec{p} = u\,\overrightarrow{AB} + v\,\overrightarrow{AC} + w\,\overrightarrow{N} + \overrightarrow{A}.
p​=uAB+vAC+wN+A.
In this space, the three corners of the triangle are at ⟨0,0,0⟩\langle 0,0,0 \rangle⟨0,0,0⟩, ⟨1,0,0⟩\langle 1,0,0 \rangle⟨1,0,0⟩, and ⟨0,1,0⟩\langle 0,1,0 \rangle⟨0,1,0⟩.
It is easy to see that a point is within the triangle if and only if u,v≥0u,v \ge 0u,v≥0, u+v≤1u + v \le 1u+v≤1, and w=0w = 0w=0.
To transform a point (x,y,z)(x,y,z)(x,y,z) into this nicer space, we can use an affine change of basis matrix

\overrightarrow{AB} & \overrightarrow{AC} & \overrightarrow{N} & \overrightarrow{A} \\

\end{bmatrix}^{-1}.

Solving for the intersection point is easy:

And as above, this can be quickly checked with u,v≥0u, v \ge 0u,v≥0, u+v≤1u + v \le 1u+v≤1 (and t≥0t \ge 0t≥0 to only count intersections “in front” of the ray).
The beauty of this method is that often, objects are already associated with a transformation matrix, so if you left-multiply that matrix by PPP, the change of basis is performed for free at the same time as other transformations, and only the short block above is needed to actually perform the intersection test.
At a cost of 1 division, 2 multiplications, 2 or 3 additions, and some comparisons, that’s about as fast as possible without special-casing triangles versus other objects.
Of course, if you’re working with lots of triangles (a mesh, for example), you can save memory and time by not associating a transformation matrix with each triangle.
In that case, other algorithms such as the one linked above may be faster as they can avoid the two matrix multiplications.
An implementation of this method can be seen here in my ray tracer Dimension.
