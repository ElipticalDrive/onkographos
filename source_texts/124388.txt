Voxelization of point cloud data serves as a foundational process in 3-D perception. Current methodologies predominantly employ uniform voxel partitioning, which proves inefficient for autonomous driving scenarios characterized by sparse point distributions. This conventional approach presents a critical tradeoff dilemma: reducing spatial resolution to maintain real-time processing severely compromises small object detection accuracy, while increasing resolution for performance enhancement incurs prohibitive computational costs. To resolve this efficiency–performance paradox, we propose two innovative voxelization paradigms: 1) density-aware nonuniform voxelization based on spatial point distribution patterns, and 2) context-aware dynamic voxelization adaptively optimizes voxel allocation based on object presence or road topology. These methods enable improved performance on small objects without significantly increasing latency. Using these methods, we achieved over 2.5 APH/L2 improvement for Pedestrian/Cyclist categories on the Waymo Open Dataset, and up to 10 average precision improvement for Pedestrian/Traffic Cone categories on nuScenes.
