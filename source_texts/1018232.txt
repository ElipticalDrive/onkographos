Summary
Render as far as the eye can see: a far-vista terrain layer that shows the whole world from any vantage point, while every existing per-frame cost stays where it was.
The one fog distance the renderer used to have is split in two:
Detail envelope: terrain chunks, props, the town, foliage, water and zone streaming keep culling against Renderer.detailFogFar, which eases exactly like scene fog used to (residency clamp included) and never exceeds the classic MAX_OUTDOOR_FOG_FAR (850). Their workload is unchanged by construction.
Vista fog: scene.fog itself now opens to 3200u on high/ultra and 2200u on medium (camera far 3600/2600, near plane 0.2 for far-field depth precision). The band between the two is filled by a new whole-world coarse mesh.
The far mesh (src/render/far_terrain_core.ts, a pure Node-tested core, plus the thin far_terrain.ts painter) is 12 static 960u tiles sharing one Lambert vertex-color material and one index buffer, built once per session across idle slots (about 130ms of terrainHeight sampling, nearest tiles first, one tap per vertex with grid-derived normals). Colors come from terrain_palette.ts, extracted verbatim from terrain.ts, so near and far ground can never drift apart; the recipe carries the biome palette blends, forest masses, shorelines, slope rock, snowlines (never on the Drakelands' volcanic cones), marsh mud and the world-rim haze.
Tricks that keep it cheap and seamless:
Fragments inside the detail envelope are discarded in the shader against the live detail radius, so the coarse mesh can never poke through real terrain on steep ridges (fixed drops cannot absorb 60u-wall chord error) and pays nothing where the detail world owns the pixels.
Tiles whose nearest edge sits past the live fog wall hide outright; the murk realms (marsh, Wraithwood...) shed almost the whole layer, and their authored fog signature is preserved by a smoothstep vista curve: presets authored at the classic envelope open fully, authored-murk presets barely move.
The ocean apron grows only on vista tiers so its rim always sits behind the fog from any camera position.
Zone streaming is untouched: after a teleport the far mesh stands in for unprepared zones (coarse ground instead of a 45u fog wall) and detail sweeps in as zones build.
Low tier and constrained-memory devices keep the classic renderer byte for byte (farVistaPlan disabled arm; camera far 950, fog wall, no far mesh).
Measured on the probe (scripts/_probe_far_perf.mjs, vale spawn + Thornpeak ridge, high tier): the layer adds about 12 draws worst case and 40-140k triangles against the 4.5M high-tier budget; spawn-scene deltas sit inside run-to-run variance (about +-75 calls / +-200k tris from NPC wander and grass build state).
Because src/render/renderer.ts is part of the Eastbrook polish evidence provenance, the after-captures were regenerated per the capture contract (metadata + performance runs for both profiles) and the three test pins updated.

Known tradeoffs (documented, deliberate)
Foliage impostor cones now stand in partially clear air on vista tiers (the fog wall that used to hide the swap is far away); tree draw ranges are unchanged, so distant hillsides beyond 850 read from the far mesh's baked forest masses instead of individual trees.
Content pop at the detail envelope is visible in clear air (props/town/trees appear at ~850u), the standard open-world streaming look; fog haze softens it.
The far mesh does not live-update during editor sculpting (full rebuilds and map loads do rebuild it).

What holds up. The detail/vista split is the right shape and the cost claim checks out: subsystemCullFar() is capped at MAX_OUTDOOR_FOG_FAR, and since no outdoor preset exceeds 850 and farScale <= 1, the cap is a no-op, so terrain/props/foliage/water/zone-streaming really do cull exactly as before. terrainView.update culls on the chunk's NEAREST point, so the 60u discard margin cannot open a ring hole at the handoff. terrain_palette.ts is a verbatim extraction (I diffed every BIOME_PALETTE entry, ROCK_SLOPE_START and the TERRAIN_TONES hexes against the pre-PR terrain.ts, zero drift). The core is genuinely pure/deterministic, the builder's padded-grid indexing is in bounds, the sky dome is depthWrite:false + renderOrder -10 so the 3200u horizon draws over it correctly, and the phong-water arm is low/constrained only, where the vista is off, so the apron change cannot leave a rim. Entity/nameplate ranges are untouched, so no graphics-fairness exposure.
Two things I would fix before merge.
1. (blocking) docs/screenshots/far-vista/after-thornpeak-ridge.png shows the far mesh drawing over the near world, including the player.
Compare the pair at the same camera pose (both minimaps read 168, 894, same yaw, same nameplate anchor):
before: a detailed, textured rock wall on the right, and the avatar visible at the crest.
after: that wall is a flat, untextured near-black slab with long straight silhouette edges, and the avatar is gone: only its DOM nameplate remains, so something opaque is between the camera and a character about 5u away.
That reads as the coarse mesh, not a lighting difference (an in-shadow cliff would still carry the splat/normal detail the before shot has, and the silhouette would not straighten out).
The mechanism I believe explains it is the discard floor in src/render/far_terrain.ts:180:
```
farCut.value.set(camX, camZ, Math.max(0, detailFar - FAR_DISCARD_MARGIN));
```
fogFarForPreparedZones floors at MIN_OUTDOOR_FOG_FAR = 45 (src/render/zone_streaming.ts:16), and detailFogFar snaps inward immediately. So any time an unprepared zone is within ~53u, the cutoff evaluates to max(0, 45 - 60) = 0 and NOTHING is discarded: the whole world mesh draws through the near field, where a 1.8u drop cannot absorb the chord error of a 10-16u cell (exactly the case the discard exists for). The Thornpeak shot's chat log ends on "Entering Thornpeak Heights", i.e. the shot was taken in precisely that just-crossed-a-boundary window, and the near detail in it ends very close to the camera.
The tension in the fix is real (raising the cutoff floor to, say, 120 while the detail cull is still at 45 would open a 45-120 ring with nothing in it), so it probably wants both ends moved together: keep the near terrain/prop cull at max(detailFar, floor) while the vista is on, or hide far tiles whose footprint contains the camera whenever detailFar < FAR_DISCARD_MARGIN. A far_terrain_core unit test pinning "the cutoff never collapses to 0 while the layer is visible" would lock it in, and the ridge capture should be redone once the destination zone is resident so the evidence shows the feature rather than the artifact.
2. (should fix) scripts/assets/eastbrook_grand_armoury/capture_ingame.mjs turns an existing cross-source check into a tautology.
```
perfReportSummary: {
  ...normalizeReport(raw.report),
  textures: evidence.resourcesMedian.textures,
},
```
tests/eastbrook_polish_artifact_integrity.test.ts:818 asserts perfReportSummary.textures === resourcesMedian.textures. Until this diff those were two independent sources (the renderer's own end-of-block report vs the sampled median), so the assertion actually verified attribution; now the capture assigns one from the other and the check can never fail again for any future capture. If the transient HDRI-prewarm read is the real problem, I would rather keep the raw report value under its own key and assert a bounded delta (or settle the block before reading), so the cross-check survives. Worth a line in the PR body too: it is a semantic change to the release-evidence pipeline, and the body only mentions "the three test pins updated".
Smaller notes, non-blocking:
far_terrain.ts:107 builds new THREE.MeshLambertMaterial directly rather than going through surfaceMat (src/render/CLAUDE.md's material factory). One material, so no dedupe cost, but it is a deviation worth a comment if deliberate.
The indexCache BufferAttribute is shared across every tile geometry, so the first geometry.dispose() in dispose() drops the shared GL index buffer for the rest. Harmless as written (dispose is all-or-nothing and the editor rebuild path re-uploads), but it is a sharp edge for anyone who later disposes one tile.
Process: this targets feature/procedural-dungeons rather than the latest release branch. Fine if the stack is intentional, just flagging it against the default workflow rule.
Nice work on the layer itself, in particular the vista fog curve preserving the authored murk signatures and the tile-level fog-wall cull that makes the marsh pay almost nothing. Happy to re-review as soon as the ridge case is sorted.
