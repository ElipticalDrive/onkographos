Photorealistic avatars are vital for immersive Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR), enabling realistic digital embodiment. Yet, most existing avatar generation methods produce static, single-outfit models that lack flexibility. In real-world VR/AR applications, users often need avatars that can adapt clothing or style across diverse social, professional, and training scenarios. Reconstructing new avatars for each appearance is costly and time-consuming, limiting personalization and scalability. Meanwhile, recent diffusion and language models have revolutionized 2D image editing through natural-language control, but extending these advances to 3D avatars remains difficult due to multi-view inconsistency and missing rigging information. As VR/AR applications expand into education, healthcare, and remote collaboration, the inability to customize photorealistic avatars in real time is becoming a critical bottleneck. To overcome these challenges, we introduce GSAC-D, a diffusion-driven framework that bridges 2D text-guided human image editing with 3D photorealistic, animatable avatar reconstruction. GSAC-D customizes Gaussian Splatting avatars from a single text prompt while ensuring cross-view coherence and preserving rigging integrity. It enables fully animatable, language-driven customization within 20 minutes—without requiring additional data capture or manual intervention. Our results demonstrate that diffusion-based editing provides a practical and scalable solution for creating flexible, expressive, and context-aware photorealistic avatars, paving the way for next-generation VR/AR experiences that are both personalized and adaptive.
