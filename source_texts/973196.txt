Question or suggestion
I've built a lens blur on my fork: click what should stay sharp, choose an f-number, and the rest is blurred by its distance, as a wider aperture would. Lightroom, Photoshop and Topaz Photo AI have this. Before I tidy it into PRs, I'd like to know whether you'd want it in Open Photo AI at all, and if so in what form.
How it works:
Depth, once per photograph: MoGe-2 ViT-B (MIT), which gives metric depth and the camera's field of view from one photograph, so the blur can follow a real lens (thin-lens circle of confusion, from EXIF focal length and sensor size, or MoGe-2's estimate without EXIF).
Edges: depth alone gives soft, wrong outlines, so a trimap is drawn around the focus plane, and the edge is matted at full resolution with ViTMatte-S (Apache-2.0); BiRefNet-matting (MIT) finds holes the depth calls solid (gaps in branches, between chair slats).
Render in linear light with disc kernels, far to near, so highlights become bokeh rather than a smear and the sharp subject doesn't bleed into the background. With an Ultra HDR gain map, the gain map is blurred with the same matte.
In the GUI it's an enhancement with an f-number slider and click-to-focus; the CLI takes --lens-blur --lb-f 1.4 --lb-focus 0.5,0.3.
Cost, on an M4 Pro Mac mini (24 GB):
Downloads: about 1.2 GB of models (depth 2 x 310 MB, portrait and landscape graphs; BiRefNet 467 MB; ViTMatte-S 114 MB).
Time: roughly 15–30 s for a first lens blur on a photograph (most of it the two matting models); moving the focus or the aperture afterwards reuses the cached depth and mattes.
Memory: peak about 11–12 GB for one photograph.
To avoid tuning it on a handful of my own photographs, I scored it against open datasets with ground truth (AIM-500 mattes, Spring depth) with a small benchmark I can share.
Questions for you:
Is it in scope, given its size and that it changes the photograph rather than restoring it?
If yes, would you host the three models in the model repository, as with the others? On my fork they're installed locally.
If not, I'll keep it on my fork; some general fixes I found along the way (cancelling a running model, Sharpen tile artefacts) I'd offer separately anyway.
Note
AI disclosure: this was prepared with Claude Code (Opus 5.5) at my behest; I tested its application in practice.
