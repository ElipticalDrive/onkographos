Summary
This PR starts embedding/, the geometry that places a tessellated solid on
the background grid, with the two operations moment fitting needs for the
divergence theorem. Both act on single facets, segments of two-dimensional
and triangles of three-dimensional parameter spaces oriented by their outward
normals.
It adds:
embedding/slicer.hpp, whose slice_triangle and slice_segment turn a
facet crossing an axis-aligned plane or line into its oriented section one
dimension down, with vertices on the plane counting as below it, so that
closed boundaries always slice into closed curves without tolerances;
embedding/clipper.hpp, whose clip_triangle (Sutherland-Hodgman) and
clip_segment (Liang-Barsky) keep the part of a facet inside a closed box
with its orientation, triangles becoming convex polygons of at most nine
vertices held off the heap.
Testing
The test suite covers:
slices of the unit tetrahedron on every axis, which enclose the exact
cross-sections, with signed crossings summing to the chord lengths;
vertices on the slicing plane, which slice as if just above it;
the volume of the tetrahedron and the unit cube inside boxes, computed
from clipped facets and the section at the upper face as the divergence
theorem does, against exact values, including facets on the lower and
upper faces of a box;
the area of a triangle inside a rectangle, computed the same way.

Slice facets by axis-aligned lines and planes

```
Add embedding/slicer.hpp with slice_triangle and slice_segment. A
triangle crossing a plane x_axis = coordinate becomes the segment where
it crosses it, oriented so that the slice of the domain lies on its
left, and a segment crossing a line becomes a point with +1 where the
line leaves the domain and -1 where it enters. A vertex on the plane
counts as below it, so a closed boundary always slices into closed
curves. Moment fitting will slice the facets down to the upper faces of
a cell for the divergence theorem, and down to lines for inside tests.

```
Add embedding/clipper.hpp with clip_segment and clip_triangle. A segment
keeps the range of its parameter that stays in the box on every axis
(Liang-Barsky), and a triangle is cut by the six planes of the box in
turn (Sutherland-Hodgman) into a convex polygon of at most nine
vertices, held in a fixed-capacity matrix off the heap. Both keep the
order of the facet, and with it its outward normal, and treat the box
as closed, so facets on a cell face stay for the divergence theorem.

Co-authored-by: Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>
