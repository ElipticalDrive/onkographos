3D Gaussian Splatting is currently emerging as a strong backend for dense SLAM systems. Their fast, distinguishable rasterization makes 3DGS-SLAM to run real-time for both the rendering and updating. A drawback in the majority of these methods is the underutilization of inertial information. We propose GVI-SLAM solving this problem by leveraging 3DGS for visual-inertial SLAM. It comes with a tracking module, end-to-end, and 3DGS mapping backend. To provide more-stable data fusion, we further built a transformer-based Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) by efficiently merging the advantages of visual with inertial features through the contextual power of a transformer. This architecture leads to enhanced tracking precision, robustness, and efficiency. We test on the TUM-RGBD datasets and our method can achieve the SOTA tracking performance at 1.78 cm against visual-only 3DGS methods including MonoGS (3.88 cm). Our stable tracking also translates to better map quality with a PSNR of 24.55. This work emphasizes the role that VIO plays in the development of scalable and high accuracy 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems, enabling more reliable dense mapping on the robotic platform.
