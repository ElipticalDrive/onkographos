Abstract:
Medical image registration is a crucial process in medical image analysis. However, traditional 2D and 3D medical image registration methods often struggle to accommodate the complex variations and conditions present in medical images. To address these challenges, this study introduces a novel 2D/3D image registration framework, termed 2D/3D-MGR, which leverages digitally reconstructed radiographs (DRR) to establish consistency between X-ray and computed tomography (CT) images. This research introduces the DCT-Net model for style transfer between DRR and X-ray images, and the CR-Net model for precise registration of intraoperative X-ray images with preoperative CT images. To validate the effectiveness of the proposed models, orthopedic X-ray and CT images were collected from Peking Union Medical College Hospital to create the XC dataset. Empirical results demonstrate that the DCT-Net model achieves a transformation quality level endorsed by medical experts, and the realistic style transfer is significant for subsequent image registration and surgical navigation. Additionally, the CR-Net model outperforms other state-of-the-art models in image registration accuracy, as evidenced by outstanding performance metrics on the XC spine dataset: a Pointwise Location Consistency (PLC) of 99.997% and a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 0.1707. This novel image registration method significantly enhances the accuracy and efficiency of medical image processing, providing a valuable reference for future research in medical image registration.

SECTION I.
Introduction
Medical images play a crucial role in enabling doctors to understand the anatomical structures and physiological information of patients, which is vital for effective medical treatment. Medical image registration [1], a fundamental task in medical image analysis, has been a prominent research focus. The core of this task is to determine the optimal spatial transformation between images, ensuring that corresponding points in two or more medical images align spatially and anatomically according to a specified similarity metric. However, well-aligned image pairs are scarce in fields such as orthopedic surgery. Research on the 2D/3D registration of X-ray images (2D) with CT images (3D) is relatively limited, particularly within the context of orthopedic surgery. This niche area requires the real-time fusion of intraoperative X-ray images with preoperative CT images, representing a critical challenge that warrants further investigation.
In actual clinical surgeries, the use of intraoperative CT is not always feasible or readily available. This is primarily due to its high cost, significant radiation dose, and stringent requirements for operating room space. While intraoperative X-ray images are easy to obtain and simple to operate, they offer relatively low accuracy in medical information. In contrast, intraoperative CT images provide high-precision medical information but are limited by the aforementioned factors. Therefore, registering preoperative CT images with intraoperative X-ray images becomes particularly important. Preoperative CT scans provide detailed 3D anatomical information, while intraoperative X-ray images offer real-time 2D views. By registering these two modalities, accurate positional information can be obtained [2], allowing doctors to more precisely apply preoperative plans to actual procedures. This enhances surgical accuracy and success rates. Additionally, it enables doctors to dynamically adjust preoperative plans based on intraoperative X-ray information, thereby reducing reliance on expensive intraoperative CT scans and decreasing radiation exposure for both patients and medical staff. In summary, 2D/3D registration research holds significant importance and broad application prospects in orthopedic clinical surgery. For instance, in screw placement assessment, 2D/3D registration can substantially improve accuracy, enabling more efficient, safe, and precise surgical operations, thereby enhancing overall medical quality [3].
Among the algorithms based on the alignment aspects of 2D/3D medical images, which are mainly divided into two main categories [4]: based on traditional methods [5], [6], [7], [8], [9] and based on deep learning methods [10], [11], [12], [13], [14].
Traditional algorithms for 2D/3D medical image alignment predominantly rely on hand-crafted feature descriptors for image feature extraction, such as Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) [15] and Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) [16]. These methods necessitate manual selection and extraction of features, rendering them less adaptable to the complexity and variability inherent in medical images, thereby constraining their effectiveness.
The evolution of deep learning has markedly influenced the realm of medical imaging, with its methodologies being progressively employed to address a multitude of imaging tasks. This surge in application is attributed to the potent capability of deep neural networks to autonomously extract intricate features [12]. Particularly, the deployment of deep neural networks for 2D/3D medical image alignment tasks is witnessing a growing trajectory of adoption. Miao et al. [10] employed Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for feature extraction from Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs (DRRs) and utilized neural networks to estimate six transformation offsets for image alignment. Hamid et al. [13] introduced a pairwise domain adaptation module within a CNN framework, significantly enhancing the model’s versatility and adaptability. However, a limitation of CNNs lies in their primary focus on local features, which may compromise their ability to capture global semantic information, potentially affecting the model’s accuracy. Miao et al. [14] leveraged a fully convolutional neural network to enhance model accuracy by focusing on the alignment of local regions. However, this approach relies on Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs (DRRs) for alignment, which substantially differ from real intraoperative X-ray images. This discrepancy poses challenges for the direct application of such methods in surgical procedures, highlighting a critical gap in the translation of these techniques to clinical practice.
Recent advancements in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [17] have propelled the field of image generation, with their application in medical imaging tasks witnessing a significant upsurge [18]. Notably, the introduction of CycleGAN [19] has refined the training of adversarial networks by incorporating cyclic consistency loss, thereby enhancing the image-to-image mapping process. This development offers innovative perspectives for addressing mapping challenges in adversarial network training.
In this work, we present a deep learning-based framework for 2D/3D image registration, named 2D/3D-MGR. This framework includes the DCT-Net, which maps X-ray images to Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs (DRRs), and the CR-Net, designed specifically for image registration. Together, these components create a robust medical image registration framework, optimized for direct application in X-ray and CT image alignment during surgical procedures. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
The 2D/3D-MGR framework is proposed to achieve automatic registration of preoperative CT images and intraoperative X-ray images.
This research propose DCT-Net, a network designed to achieve image style conversion from intraoperative X-ray images to DRR images using a generative model. Additionally, we propose CR-Net, a registration network that predicts the positional parameters of X-ray images based on DRR images to accomplish precise registration.
This study conducted experiments on the XC dataset provided by the hospital to validate the effectiveness of the 2D/3D-MGR framework.
This paper is organized as follows: Section II offers a concise overview of current techniques in image generation and feature extraction. Section III delves into the architecture of the deep learning-driven 2D/3D image alignment framework, termed 2D/3D-MGR. Section IV presents the experimental outcomes and their corresponding analyses. The paper concludes with a summary in Section V, where future directions of this research are also discussed.
SECTION II.
Relate Work
A. CycleGAN
In recent years, significant attention has been directed toward the domain of image generation within the research community [17]. Isola et al. [20] introduced the Pix2Pix framework, designed for image-to-image translation tasks, enabling applications such as transforming monochromatic sketches into vibrant color images and rendering conceptual clothing designs into photorealistic representations. However, a notable limitation of Pix2Pix is its dependency on paired datasets for training, rendering it inapplicable in scenarios devoid of directly corresponding image pairs. Addressing this constraint, Zhu et al. [19] proposed the CycleGAN model, which leverages cycle consistency loss and adversarial training strategies. This innovation allows CycleGAN to facilitate domain adaptation between two image categories without the necessity for matched data.
Wang et al. [21] employed CycleGAN to synthesize artificial CT images, facilitating attenuation correction in PET-MR imaging. Concurrently, Vitale et al. [22] aimed at augmenting the authenticity of simulated ultrasound (US) images. Through the application of CycleGAN, they achieved a notable enhancement in realism by transforming CT images into US counterparts, thereby significantly elevating the fidelity of the simulated imagery. Contrary to ultrasound imagery, which typically comprises single grayscale images, X-ray acquisition presents a distinct challenge due to the variability in recording parameters across different devices. This variability results in significant differences between X-rays of the same subject, complicating the application of CycleGAN for the conversion between CT images and X-rays. Gadermayr et al. [23] investigated the inherent many-to-one mapping challenge in image conversion tasks addressed by CycleGAN. They adapted the model for many-to-one tasks by refining its loss function. This research aims to design a style transformation model, DCT-Net, to establish a mapping relationship between 2D DRR images from CT scans and X-ray images, proposing new ideas for 2D/3D image registration tasks.
B. Image Registration Network
Registration of 2D/3D medical images utilizing grayscale information is a prevalent technique in medical image processing. This method aligns two-dimensional and three-dimensional medical images to a unified reference coordinate system. The process typically involves generating Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs (DRRs), evaluating similarity metrics between DRRs and X-ray images, and optimizing the search space. Variations in algorithms for computing similarity metrics and optimizing the search space can significantly influence the outcomes of the alignment. In the context of 3D transformations, parameterization is achievable using vectors comprising six components, which are further divided into three in-plane and three out-of-plane transformation parameters [24]. The in-plane transformation parameters comprise two translation parameters $T_{x}$
, $T_{y}$
, and one rotation parameter $R_{z}$
, effectively mimicking a 2D rigid-body transformation. Meanwhile, the out-of-plane parameters include a single translation parameter $T_{z}$
, which denotes image scaling, and two rotation parameter $R_{x}$
, $R_{y}$
, that modify the image’s shape [25].
C. Feature Extraction Network
The ongoing progress in deep learning technology has significantly advanced image feature extraction techniques. Notably, the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) stands out as a prevalent method for image feature extraction within the deep learning domain. ResNet [26], with its introduction of residual blocks for enhanced image feature extraction, has emerged as a popular network architecture in medical imaging tasks. Zhang et al. [27] developed a segmentation network utilizing ResNet as the foundational backbone, effectively integrating deep and shallow features for comprehensive image information extraction, yielding impressive segmentation outcomes. Lee et al. [28] utilized ResNet for the segmentation of brain tumors in MR images. These methods leverage the robust local feature extraction capabilities of convolutional networks, yet fall short in contexts that demand the extraction of global features. The Vision Transformer (ViT) [28] integrates the attention mechanism into image analysis, surpassing convolutional neural networks in capturing global image features, particularly in high-resolution contexts. Building upon ResNet and ViT, Liu et al. [30] introduced ConvNeXt, which incorporates ViT’ s principles into convolutional architectures to adeptly extract both local and global image features, yielding promising results. However, the initial model design, primarily intended for natural images, underperforms when directly applied to medical imaging. Consequently, customizing deep learning models for medical imaging applications has emerged as a crucial area of research [11].
SECTION III.
Methodology
A. The 2D/3D-MGR Framework
The 2D/3D-MGR framework introduced in this research facilitates the registration of intraoperative X-ray images and preoperative CT images.The framework’s workflow, illustrated in Figure 1, involves two key processes. Firstly, DCT-Net, a DRR generation framework, achieves the style transformation from X-ray to DRR images, maintaining structural consistency while altering the image style. Secondly, through the DRR registration framework CR-Net, features of the DRR image are extracted, and spatial transformation parameters across six dimensions are predicted to accomplish registration. Consequently, this framework enables the 2D/3D registration of intraoperative X-ray images with preoperative CT scans, leveraging preoperative data (DRR images derived from preoperative CT scans).

Within the DCT-Net framework, the generator is tasked with training a model to convert the style of X-ray images to DRR images, a process vital for the preoperative phase. This task encompasses both learning the characteristics of the original image and managing the visual outcome of the produced image to achieve style conversion. Conversely, the discriminator assesses whether an image is produced by the generator, serving a supervisory role in evaluating the generator’s performance.
1) Generator
In the domain of medical image analysis, images comprise not only salient contour features but also encapsulate critical detail information imperative for ensuring diagnostic accuracy. Traditional encoder-decoder architectures, predominantly reliant on the extraction of features from deep network strata, often overlook nuanced detail features. This oversight can result in a significant loss of detail information in the synthesized medical images, consequently impairing the diagnostic outcome.
The generator in the DCT-Net network framework, as shown in Figure 3, addresses this by leveraging the concept of multi-scale fusion and drawing on the U-net [31] structure. By connecting information during the down sampling and up sampling processes, the generated images not only preserve overall contours but also emphasize detailed information. Given the distinction between foreground and background in medical images, this paper aims to develop a network that focuses more on specific content structures while performing style transformation. To achieve this, a Convolutional Block Attention Module [32] (CBAM) is introduced into the network. Additionally, during the design process, the idea of grouped convolution is implemented, and three sets of parameters are trained through the attention mechanism to achieve better results.

2) Discriminator
The discriminator utilizes the network architecture depicted in Figure 4 for feature extraction to evaluate image authenticity. The image is segmented into smaller patches, allowing for both a holistic and granular assessment of authenticity. The ultimate verdict is derived from a collective analysis of each patch’s evaluation, employing a voting mechanism. This strategy augments the model’s responsiveness to local nuances, thereby enhancing the precision of authenticity determinations. In the development of the discriminator architecture, we employ a convolutional layer with a stride of two for downsampling, which serves the dual purpose of progressively extracting deep features from the image and reducing the spatial dimensions of the feature map. The use of convolutional layers is extended to facilitate weight sharing within localized feature regions, thereby enabling the delineation and scoring of discrete image segments, or patches. Score aggregation across these patches is efficiently accomplished through the application of a global pooling technique. Regarding normalization strategies, the discriminator employs Instance Normalization instead of the conventional Batch Normalization, due to InstanceNorm’s focused attention on the pixel-level characteristics of individual image instances. This specificity renders InstanceNorm particularly effective in domains such as image style transfer, where it outperforms BatchNorm in terms of performance. In terms of activation functions, the discriminator utilizes Leaky ReLU in place of the standard ReLU. Leaky ReLU permits a small amount of negative signal to pass through, effectively mitigating premature saturation of the activation values and thereby reducing information loss. This feature is particularly crucial in the task of discriminating between authentic and synthetic images.

C. CR-Net
CR-Net improves upon the ConvNeXt architecture to predict spatial transformation parameters for X-ray images. Traditional convolutional neural networks add an activation function after each convolution operation, while Transformer architectures reduce the use of activation functions, utilizing them primarily in MLPs, CR-Net adopts this approach by incorporating a GELU activation function only between two convolution operations. The model architecture integrates the residual block concept from the ResNet framework, as shown in Figure 5. Each block initially applies a large $7\times 7$
convolutional kernel, followed by layer normalization and two $1\times 1$
convolutional kernels serving as linear layers to output positional parameters. Finally, a gamma parameter is used to determine the dropout rate, preventing overfitting.

SECTION IV.
Experiment
We present two research questions (RQs) and design experiments to address these inquiries:
RQ1: Can DCT-Net effectively perform the transformation between DRR and X-Ray images?
RQ2: Is CR-Net capable of accurately completing registration tasks?
A. Dateset
To ensure robustness, we collected two unpublished datasets from the Orthopedic Team at Peking Union Medical College Hospital, named the XC dataset, which includes both generation and registration data.
DCT-Net utilizes the generation portion of the XC dataset, consisting of 440 tibia DRR images and 110 X-ray images taken by different devices. The core task of DCT-Net is to learn the mapping relationship between DRR and X-ray images, facilitating the conversion between these two image styles.CR-Net employs the registration portion of the XC dataset, containing 10,000 spinal DRR images.
All data were provided by orthopedic physicians from Peking Union Medical College Hospital. To validate and enhance the generalizability of the 2D/3D-MGR framework, we trained and tested on two different subsets of the XC dataset during the style conversion phase. Although using the same dataset might improve performance, it would not adequately test the framework’s generalization capability. This research aims to convert any DRR image to an X-ray to assist other practitioners in overcoming the challenges of collecting medical datasets, thereby advancing medical imaging and providing more reliable support for clinical diagnosis and treatment.
B. Experimental Setting
All experiments were conducted using Pytorch 1.9.1 and trained on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. The default hyperparameters for both CR-Net and DCT-Net included the AdamW optimizer and a multi-scale sampler with scales of (160,160), (192,192), (256,256), (288,288), and (320,320). The learning rate was adjusted using a cosine annealing schedule.
During the training of DCT-Net, cycle-consistency loss and self-loss are both optimized using the L1 loss, while adversarial loss is optimized using the mean squared error (MSE) loss function. In CR-Net, the loss function consistently employs MSE loss. The performance of the model is evaluated using four Image Quality Assessment (IQA) metrics: Pointwise Location Consistency (PLC), Similarity Rate of Overlapping Coefficient (SROC), Key point Registration Overlapping Coefficient (KROC), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE).
In comparative experiments with CR-Net, we benchmarked its performance against several models including MobileVit [33], MobileNet V2 [34], Swin-Transformer [35], ResNet [26], Vit [27] and Vanillanet [36], to assess the efficacy of CR-Net.

The Vanillanet, ResNet, and Vision-Transformer models perform well in PLC, SROC, and KROC metrics but have RMSE values well above 0.5. Therefore, there are extremely high accuracy requirements for models in this field. This is why most registration tasks in real medical environments still rely on traditional algorithms or even the expertise of physicians. CR-Net demonstrates high accuracy and low error rates in image registration, enabling more precise identification and matching of bone structures in orthopedic images. This improves the precision of surgical navigation and further ensures patient safety.
SECTION V.
Conclusion
This research presents a novel medical image registration framework: 2D/3D-MGR. This framework comprises two innovative models: DCT-Net and CR-Net, which address the style transformation between X-ray images and DRR images, and the registration of preoperative CT images with intraoperative X-ray images, respectively. Compared to various style transformation and registration models, DCT-Net and CR-Net demonstrate significant performance improvements. DCT-Net excels in style transformation, producing more realistic images compared to traditional models like CycleGAN and DualGAN, making the style transformation between X-ray and DRR images more natural. This realistic transformation is crucial for subsequent image registration and surgical navigation. CR-Net, on the other hand, excels in the image registration task, outperforming common CNN and Transformer models in terms of accuracy and stability when registering preoperative CT images with intraoperative X-ray images. This higher precision in identifying and matching critical anatomical structures during surgery enhances surgical navigation accuracy and reduces surgical risks.
The proposed 2D/3D-MGR framework is not limited to spinal surgeries but can also be extended to other orthopedic procedures, such as joint replacement and fracture fixation. In joint replacement surgeries, accurate image registration ensures precise prosthesis positioning, enhancing surgical outcomes and reducing postoperative complications. In fracture fixation surgeries, precise image registration aids in better fracture reduction, ensuring stable fixation. By improving overall surgical quality, reducing complications, and shortening recovery times, the 2D/3D-MGR framework and its models hold promise for widespread clinical application. The results of this study indicate that this novel image registration method significantly enhances the accuracy and efficiency of medical image processing, ultimately improving patient outcomes. Although the dataset used in this study was limited to one hospital, future work will focus on collecting more data from different hospitals and patients. This will help improve the robustness and generalizability of the model, ensuring its reliability and effectiveness in various clinical settings. By expanding the dataset, we aim to develop more versatile models, further advancing medical image registration technology and supporting a wider range of surgeries.
