Neural Radiance Fields
Last Updated : 26 Jun, 2025
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) is a technique in deep learning that creates realistic 3D views of a scene using just a few 2D pictures taken from different angles. Instead of creating a 3D model manually, NeRF learns the scene by looking at these images and then generates new realistic views. This method has changed how we can recreate and view 3D scenes making them more detailed and realistic.
NeRF Architecture
Lets look at its architecture to have a better understanding of its working:
NEURAL RADIANCE FIELDS
1. Core Model: NeRF uses a fully connected neural network specifically a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) with no convolutional layers.
2. Inputs:: The network takes a 5D vector as input:
3D spatial coordinates ( x , y , z )
2D viewing direction (θ,ϕ)
3. Positional Encoding: Positional Encoding capture fine details and high-frequency scene variations, the input coordinates are first transformed using positional encoding techniques like Fourier features.
4. Network Structure: Typically Multi Layer Perceptron (MLP) has 8 hidden layers with ReLU activations. A skip connection is added at the 5th layer to concatenate the original input, improving gradient flow and detail preservation. The network outputs are:
Volume density (σ): Opacity at the queried point.
Feature vector: Used for color prediction.
The feature vector and encoded viewing direction are processed by additional layers to predict the final RGB color at that point.
How NeRF Works
Data Collection: Multiple 2D images of a scene are captured from different known camera positions and orientations. Camera poses are estimated using techniques like Structure from Motion (SfM).
Ray Sampling: For each pixel in a target view, a camera ray is cast into the scene. Along each ray multiple 3D points are sampled.
Network Query: Each sampled point (with its viewing direction) is passed through the MLP to obtain its color and density.
Volume Rendering: Using classical volume rendering techniques the colors and densities along each ray are composited to produce the final pixel color for the rendered image. This allows synthesizing images from arbitrary viewpoints.
Training:The network is trained to minimize the difference between rendered images and the actual captured images, optimizing the MLP weights so the radiance field accurately represents the scene.
Optimizations and Scene Representation
Continuous Representation: Unlike traditional 3D meshes or voxels, NeRF represents the scene as a continuous volumetric function allowing for smooth interpolation and high-resolution detail.
Positional Encoding and Hierarchical Sampling: These techniques enable NeRF to capture both coarse and fine details efficiently and reduce training/rendering time.
Efficient 3D Construction: NeRF’s neural representation is compact and can model complex geometry, lighting and view-dependent effects like specular highlights that are difficult with conventional methods.
Novel View Synthesis: Once trained it can generate photorealistic images of the scene from any viewpoint, enabling applications like virtual tours, 3D asset creation and AR/VR content.
Advantages and Impact
Optimized Scene Representation: NeRF’s neural approach allows for detailed, memory-efficient encoding of scenes and outperforms traditional mesh or voxel-based methods in realism and flexibility.
View-dependent Effects: The model can represent effects that change with the viewing angle such as reflections and transparency by conditioning on the viewing direction.
Flexible Rerendering: The continuous nature of the neural field allows for smooth, high-quality rerendering from arbitrary perspectives making it a useful tool for 3D scene visualization and manipulation.
Use Cases of Neural Radiance Filler
Content Creation: It is used in generation of highly realistic 3D models and environments from simple 2D images hence transforming industries like movies, video games and product design by democratizing high-quality 3D content creation.
Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR): They are used to build immersive, explorable virtual worlds and enhance AR experiences with life like 3D objects and scenes, improving realism and user engagement.
Medical Imaging: It reconstruct detailed 3D medical images from limited 2D scans like X-rays or CT allowing safer and more accurate diagnostics.
Robotics and Autonomous Systems: It provides robots and autonomous vehicles with detailed, realistic 3D scene understanding, aiding navigation, manipulation and perception especially in challenging environments with transparent or reflective objects.
Urban Planning and Digital Archiving: They are used to model cities, buildings and historical artifacts in 3D for planning, simulation, preservation and virtual tourism making complex environments accessible for analysis and exploration.
