What and why
Two medium findings from the renderer review, one commit each.
Light-tree importance (PT#4). When the shading point lies inside a node's bounding sphere, _pt_lt_importance used θ_u = π/2. That leaves a residual angle, so a parent of spots whose cones do contain the point could score zero. The descent then gave a spot that lights the point probability 0. Spots are delta lights with no MIS backstop, so their light was lost: this is bias, not noise. The fix uses θ_u = π (cos_u = -1), PBRT-v4's EntireSphere. On the review's two-spot rig the pmf went from (0, 0, 1) to nonzero for both spots. On a random rig of 24 downward spots, 15 lost light/point pairs went to none.
Clearcoat and sheen in the path tracer (PT#7, LM#2). The path tracer's lit BSDF ignored slots 17-23, so a coated or sheened MeshPhysicalMaterial path-traced exactly like the bare base, with no warning. The shipped PLASTIC and CERAMIC presets carry a coat. The two layers are now composed as three.js composes them:
f = (1 - cc·F_c(n·v)) · [s(v,l)·f_base + f_sheen] + cc·f_coat
The coat is a colourless GGX dielectric (F0 0.04) with its own roughness and exact Smith, sampled by VNDF.
The sheen is Charlie × Neubelt, sampled cosine-weighted. The base is scaled by three.js's sheen albedo factor.
Both lobes are in _pt_lit_f_pdf and in the continuation's lobe pick, so MIS sees the same mixture pdf at both ends.
A material with neither layer has every scale exactly 1 and both weights exactly 0, so it renders bit for bit as before.
Known gaps, documented in renderer_limitations.rst:
The coat does not dim emission.
three.js's own low-roughness sheen gains energy at grazing angles; it is kept to match the deterministic renderer.
Rendered output
Fast baselines: unchanged. tests/fast has no clearcoat or sheen, and it renders at spp=1, so the light-tree change does not reach it. pytest -q --fast passes against the existing baselines.
Heavy baselines: not run here. The release-hosted tests/path_traced and tests/full_renders baselines are machine-specific.
Any path-traced scene that uses a coated or sheened physical material, including PLASTIC and CERAMIC, will change, deliberately.
Multi-emitter scenes can change in light-tree sampling, and spot-lit ones in brightness wherever the old importance zeroed a spot.
Verification
All runs were on CPU.
tests/unit_tests/test_pt_clearcoat_sheen.py is new and has 24 tests, all quadrature-based. They check that:
each lobe's sampler agrees with its evaluator;
the mixture pdf carries every lobe at its selection probability;
a coat never adds energy to a white base;
coat plus sheen conserves energy at sheen roughness 1.
New tests in test_path_tracer.py:
test_light_tree_never_zeroes_a_spot_whose_cone_holds_the_point: fails before the fix.
test_clearcoat_and_sheen_change_a_path_traced_render: fails before the fix, when the renders were identical (max 138 both). After the fix the coat max is 215 and sheen brightens the sphere ×1.26.
test_clearcoat_furnace_does_not_gain_energy: furnace level 63.75. The coated sphere reads 64.1–64.2, against 64.25 bare.
test_path_tracer.py in full: 86 passed. test_pt_clearcoat_sheen.py, test_dielectric_fresnel.py and test_lit_bsdf_sidedness.py: 56 passed.
pytest -q --fast: 736 passed.

The GGX albedo PR changes _pt_lit_lobes to take the energy table. After both merge, the probes in test_pt_clearcoat_sheen.py need that argument and must bind pt_energy_tables(). That patch is written and verified on a trial merge.

The path tracer's lit BSDF ignored clearcoat (slots 17-18) and sheen
(slots 19-23), so a clearcoated or sheened physical material path-traced
exactly like the bare base. They are now layered the way three.js
composes them:

  f = (1 - cc F_c(n.v)) * [s(v,l) f_base + f_sheen] + cc f_coat

- clearcoat: a dielectric GGX lobe (f0 0.04, its own roughness, exact
  height-correlated Smith, no multiple-scattering compensation, as in
  three.js), sampled by VNDF; the base is attenuated by 1 - cc F_c(n.v).
- sheen: Charlie D with Neubelt visibility, sampled cosine-weighted; the
  base is scaled by 1 - max(sheenColor) max(E(n.v), E(n.l)) with E the
  IBLSheenBRDF fit, as three.js does.

Both lobes are in _pt_lit_f_pdf (NEE f and mixture pdf) and in the
continuation's one-sample lobe selection, weighted cc F_c(n.v) and
max(sheenColor) E(n.v), so MIS uses the same mixture pdf on both ends.
Materials without either layer are untouched: every scale is exactly 1
and both weights exactly 0. _pt_lit_lobes now also returns the layer
vector and _pt_lit_f_pdf / _pt_vertex_f_pdf take it as a last argument;
the GGX energy compensation (_pt_ggx_energy, e1/e1s/comp) is unchanged.

Emission is not coat-attenuated, and three.js's sheen model (Neubelt
lobe vs the IBLSheenBRDF albedo fit) gains energy at grazing angles with
a low sheen roughness; both are documented in renderer_limitations.rst.

Tests: test_pt_clearcoat_sheen.py checks each sampler against its
evaluator by quadrature, that the mixture pdf carries every lobe at its
selection probability, and that clearcoat (and clearcoat + sheen at
sheen roughness 1) never adds energy to a white base. In
test_path_tracer.py a clearcoat=1 render gains a highlight (max 138 ->
215; failed before the fix with identical renders) and sheen brightens
the sphere (x1.26), and a white-furnace render of a clearcoated sphere
stays at or below the furnace.
