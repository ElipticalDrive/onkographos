Security Findings and Attack Paths
inferred — A workflow submitter can supply many valid light dictionaries through an existing value wrapper and connect the result to RenderLight. Resilience and Maintainability Implications
observed — Interruption is checked at ray-chunk boundaries and between soft-shadow samples, but not during per-light output allocation or every light contribution. Hardening Proposals
proposed — Enforce a light-count and aggregate output-memory budget at the renderer boundary before allocating buffers, accounting for final stacking and CPU versus device placement. ## Summary
Interactive three.js preview mirroring the backend RenderLight studio
scene (clay sphere + ground) lit by the lights being edited. The light
list lives in a single editor_state widget value (CreateBoundingBoxes
pattern), so any number of directional, point, and spot lights can be
edited: a rig per entry with click-to-select markers, orbit
yaw/pitch/distance handles bound to the selected light, transform gizmos
for emitter position and target, real-time shadows, and two-way widget
sync via the LIGHT_INFO_PREVIEW input type.

Chrome: gizmo controls on top with icon-only collapse below a width
threshold, output-view reset and camera-lock buttons bottom-right, a
light chip row (select/add/remove) and a per-light property panel (type,
color, intensity, range, cone angles) below the viewport. Drag handles
share the camera-info tolerance picker, hover highlight, and grab
cursors.
