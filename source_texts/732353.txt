Matrix-to-quaternion extraction fixes the sign of the largest quaternion component. When nearby rotation axes cross from positive-X dominance to negative-Y dominance, the extracted unit quaternions have opposite signs. Interpolating them directly follows the long arc even though the physical rotations are close.
Align the second quaternion to the first hemisphere before spherical interpolation in both NumPy and Torch. This preserves the rotation endpoints and follows the short geodesic in SO(3).
The regression constructs fixed 120-degree and 170-degree rotations with axes at 44 and 46 degrees in the XY plane. Its reference uses independently constructed same-hemisphere unit quaternions, analytic spherical interpolation weights, and an explicit quaternion-to-matrix formula. It checks five interpolation times and orthogonality. Existing Z-axis interpolation and SE(3) translation controls are retained.
Validation with genuine full-package imports on Windows, Python 3.12.10, NumPy 2.5.3, and PyTorch 2.14.1+cpu (CPU): upstream runs three unittest methods, with the two original control methods passing and the added branch case producing four assertion-failure subtest records. The identical frozen regression file passes all three methods after the change, with no skips or errors. These four records are parameterized subtests, not four collected methods. The repository has no formal test suite; its root test.py is an unrelated PLY benchmark. CUDA, rasterization, and downstream model runs were not performed.
