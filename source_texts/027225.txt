🔴🔴 2026-10-08 — RDR2 already built this exact system, and wrote the verdict
Researched against primary sources. Red Dead Redemption 2 shipped a unified volumetric system whose stated goal is this issue's goal, word for word: "Unified volumetric rendering system / Single lighting and data model / Cover large and small scale effects". Their conclusion, slide 49:
"Frustum Volumes: Undersample data at large distances. Limited resolution. Exponential slice distribution. Unstable, even with temporal integration. Need a system to 'take over' at some distance."
Their froxel grid covers <160 m. Beyond it, a raymarch — same data model, same shading model, same materials, two samplers. So the shape is not "clouds apart from volumetrics": it is one system, two samplers.

🔴 The shipped success in stellar_delivery was clouds in froxels at ~1 km with 64 slices, where the far slice is tens of metres. At 20 km with 24 slices it is 5844 m — 180× worse. It is not the same experiment under a different range; it is a different experiment wearing the same name.
Lateral footprint compounds it: with 4096 cells at 16:9 (~85×48) and 60° hfov, one froxel spans 2.7 m at 200 m, 13.9 m at 1 km, 270 m at 20 km.
To hold a log grid's far slice under 100 m out to 20 km takes 1655 slices. Under 8 m — Nubis3's voxel size — takes 20731.
⚠️ And raising detail_spread makes the far end worse, not better: it concentrates precision near the camera. At 20 km with 24 slices, spread 2 gives a 1632 m last slice; spread 4 gives 3131 m.
So the failure at planetary range is topological, not qualitative: a 270 × 470 × 5844 m froxel is not a participating medium with structure, it is a slab. The cloud stops having an inside and an outside.
Nobody does clouds in the froxel grid
Checked explicitly. RDR2: froxels <160 m, "tracing starts where voxels end", cirrus at 8 km is a flat 2D texture. Horizon Zero Dawn / Nubis: spherical raymarch, and above 4000 m scrolling 2D textures. Nubis3 (Decima): raymarch over a world voxel grid with compressed SDF — not frustum-aligned. Frostbite 2016: clouds in their own half-res pass; the 32×32×16 froxel volume is aerial perspective only. UE5: a dedicated VolumetricRenderTarget, separate from Volumetric Fog's grid. Godot documents that volumetric fog "has a finite range and will eventually stop being rendered in the distance".
No production title found rendering clouds in an extended-range froxel grid.
🟢 What this unblocks: motion vectors were never the blocker
This issue and #977 both list geometry motion vectors (#481/#732) as the real schedule dependency. No production volumetric system uses them.
Frostbite (clouds): "we re-project the previous result according to previous and current camera properties, i.e. projection and transform." Camera matrices, nothing else.
RDR2 (material volume): "wind velocity as motion vector", modulated by an R8 reprojection-magnitude volume so it does not filter between interior and exterior. The medium's own velocity — which this engine generates analytically.
And the cloud's own motion vector is four lines, from the transmittance-weighted depth RDR2 already computes for its ESM and its upscale kernel:
```
depth = lerp(depth, ray.t, pow(transmittance, 4));
```
That same Depth_cloud gives three things at once: where to apply aerial perspective once (Frostbite 2016, eq. 21 — not per march step), the full-res upscale kernel width, and a world position to reproject against last frame's view-projection.
⚠️ Do NOT temporally filter the scattered light volume
RDR2 tried and removed it: "Light ghosting with moving lights. Trailing with strong forward scattering on moving camera." Dithered lookups + TAA there instead. Temporal goes on the material volume and the shadow volume only. UE admits the same artefact: "fast-changing lights, like flashlights and muzzle flashes, leave lighting trails."
The measured path from 39.83 ms
Guerrilla's cascade, in the order they measured it: LOD + adaptive step (22 → 8.1 ms), light only where there is density (→ 3.34), cull below the horizon (→ 1.81), conservative depth culling (→ 1.2 ms). 18× cumulative, on PS4.
Plus half-res with 1 ray in 4 reconstructed over 4 frames. RDR2's ray placement took three attempts, and slides 53–55 save us two: uniform-by-frame-index starves near depth; checkerboard alone is not enough; checkerboard plus comparing the current 2×2 tile's min/max depth against its 8 neighbours, placing the ray where depth is under-represented.
RDR2's whole volumetric system — shadow + material + scattering volumes, far raymarch, reconstruction, cloud ESM — is 2.61 ms on PS4.
⚠️ Every figure above is PS4 (176 GB/s dedicated) or Xbox One. A 10 W handheld APU shares ~90–120 GB/s with the CPU, and Frostbite's froxel cost scales ~3.55× for 4× froxels — bandwidth-bound, so expect a penalty nearer 2× than 1×. No published froxel measurement on RDNA at 10 W exists.
Revised order
More slices before more range. 24 → 64 costs 1.24 MB more per volume (0.75 → 1.99 MB) and takes the far slice from 28.5 m to 11.2 m at 200 m. Memory is not the constraint here — the whole grid is under a megabyte against Frostbite's 7 MB.
Depth_cloud weighted by transmittance — unblocks aerial perspective, the upscale, and the reprojection.
Reprojection from camera matrices + medium velocity. Not feat(render): the engine's own temporal upscaler — WGSL, ported from FSR2's shaders #481/feat(render): temporal upscaling on both vendors — motion vectors, FSR, DLSS, XeSS #732.
Half-res raymarch beyond where the froxels end, with RDR2's ray placement.
Sources: RDR2, SIGGRAPH 2019 · Frostbite 2015 · Frostbite 2016 · HZD 2015 · Nubis 2017 · Nubis Cubed 2023 · Hillaire 2020
2026-08-26 — the owner already shipped clouds AS fog, and it was cheap
Stated by the owner, and it settles a question this issue was still
treating as open: in stellar_delivery he used Godot's volumetric fog
as the cloud medium, not a separate cloud technique, and it performed
well.
So there is no second long-range grid to design. volumetric_fog_length
reaches 1024 m and volumetric_fog_detail_spread biases the Z
distribution, which is what makes one froxel volume cover both the fog
near the camera and the layer above it. That was raised here as an
obstacle — clouds live at kilometre scale, fog grids default to 64 m —
and it is not one: the range is a parameter and it was already used that
way in a shipped project.
🔴 What it means for #977: the grid serves FOUR consumers, not three —
clustering (#780), aerial perspective (#976), fog, and clouds. Sizing it
is now the whole design, and it is decided once.
⚠️ Unchanged: the measured win here is quarter resolution plus temporal
reprojection, and that still waits on motion vectors (#481/#732). The
froxel volume replaces the per-pixel march; the reprojection is what
takes it from affordable to cheap.
🔴🔴 2026-08-13: the clouds are 39 ms of the handheld's frame. Measured.
They were turned off to find out, on the device:

97 % of the sky pass is this feature. Everything else in that pass —
gradient, frag_depth, overdraw — is 1.2 ms together. Against a 13.9 ms
budget, the clouds as they exist today cost nearly three whole frames.
So this issue stops being "nicer clouds, per planet" and becomes the
condition on clouds existing at all: they cannot come back in their
current form. cloud_coverage: 0 is the only setting the handheld can
afford right now, which is not a feature — it is the feature switched
off.
⚠️ And that is a scene value, not a build one. Any project that sets
coverage above zero pays it. Nothing warns.
What has to be true before they return
Already written below: the froxel technique from Godot rather than
Bevy's per-pixel raymarch, and its grid is #780's cluster grid. Adding
from the survey of current practice:
Quarter resolution + temporal reprojection. Guerrilla updates 1
pixel in 16 per frame (Nubis). 🔴 Needs motion vectors, which do not
exist — feat(render): the engine's own temporal upscaler — WGSL, ported from FSR2's shaders #481/feat(render): temporal upscaling on both vendors — motion vectors, FSR, DLSS, XeSS #732. That dependency is the real schedule here.
Fewer steps with jitter and analytic integration — comparable
results at 1/16 the steps. Cheapest thing to try against the
current shader, and it does not need new infrastructure.
Empty-space skipping by weather column, and clipping the active Y
range with an early-out for rays that miss the cloud profile.
Motion vectors derived from the march itself, weighting the
absorption position — clouds reproject without waiting for the
geometry's motion vectors.
🔴 The current shader is already at the end of its tuning: 48→32 steps,
4→3 light steps, 800→500 length, early-out 1 %→5 %, each paying quality.
There is nothing left to shave. It is a technique change or nothing.
🔴 2026-08-12 — the technique to copy is Godot's, not Bevy's
Raised by the user, who ships Godot's volumetric fog in stellar_delivery
and found it cheap. Investigated rather than assumed, and he is right —
this is one of the few places where Bevy is the expensive one and the
port-literal policy does not apply.

🔴 A per-pixel raymarch is the technique that just cost us 47 ms
(#771). Adopting Bevy's fog would be adopting it again, next to the one
we are removing.
🔴🔴 The froxel buffer IS the cluster grid — this belongs to #780
Godot's froxels and Bevy's clusters are the same structure: frustum-
aligned cells, small near the camera, growing with distance. #780 builds
exactly that for lights, and Bevy's own grid already carries
CLUSTERABLE_OBJECT_TYPE_* for decals, irradiance volumes and reflection
probes — things attached to a region rather than lights.
So #780 is the input of three separate things, not one: VSM page
marking (#477), the single shadow-page pool (#477's redesign), and this.
Building volumetric fog before the cluster grid means writing the grid
twice.
⏭️ Ordering: #771/#248 (the sky) → #780 (clustering) → this. And when
it gets written, the reference is Godot's froxel pass, with Bevy's
FogVolume component shape only if it happens to fit.
Goal
Cloud layers and fog that wrap a planet, not a box.
Sibling of #248 (AtmosphereVolume). Same framing — a shell between two radii around a planet centre — and a different integrand: #248 integrates scattering through air, this integrates density through cloud.
Why a separate issue, and why not Bevy's fog volumes
Bevy's FogVolume (0.15) is an axis-aligned bounding box carrying a 3D density texture, with scrolling for movement. That composes beautifully with chunks and does not compose with a planet at all: a cloud layer is a spherical shell, and an AABB large enough to hold one is mostly empty space that every ray still has to march through.
The technique for a shell is raymarching between an inner and outer radius, with density from noise rather than from a finite texture. Guerrilla's Nubis work for Horizon Zero Dawn is the reference — cloud types by altitude, coverage and erosion from separate noise fields, cheap lighting through a few in-scattering samples rather than a second march.
Scope
CloudLayer component beside AtmosphereVolume on the same planet entity: inner radius, outer radius, coverage, density, wind, and a material asset for the noise parameters.
Ray-sphere intersection shared with feat(render): AtmosphereVolume component per-planet (sky PR B) #248. Both need "where does this ray enter and leave a shell around this centre"; writing it twice is how the two drift apart.
Marched in the same pass as the atmosphere, or ordered against it — clouds sit inside the atmosphere, so scattering applies to the light that reaches them and to the light they send on. Deciding that ordering is part of this issue.
Distance policy. Same rule as the rest of the engine at scale: from orbit, a cloud layer is a texture on a sphere; from the ground it is a volume you fly through. The transition has to be continuous, and where it happens has to be measured, not guessed.
Explicitly for universes
N planets, each with its own layer. The camera can be inside zero, one, or none of them, and the shader early-outs on a missed shell exactly as feat(render): AtmosphereVolume component per-planet (sky PR B) #248 does.
Nothing here may assume a single planet or a horizontal layer. A flat cloud plane is the thing this issue exists to not build.
Out of scope
Ground fog and local fog volumes — an AABB is the right shape for those, and Bevy's FogVolume is the design to copy when they land.
Weather simulation. Coverage is an authored parameter here, not a simulation output.
Depends on
#248 for the shell maths and the atmosphere it composes with. Do not start before it: clouds lit by a sun that ignores the atmosphere look wrong in a way no cloud tuning fixes.
Relationship to #771
#771 is the same march's cost: the sky that exists today evaluates
procedural FBM per sample — up to 8 192 hashes per pixel — and does it
before the depth pass, so hidden pixels pay in full. That has to come
down whatever shape the volume is.
#771 goes first. Reshaping a march this expensive means porting the
expense into the shell. This issue then inherits its sampling technique
(3D noise textures, empty-space skipping, fixed step size) and gives it
the ray-shell intersection shared with #248.
⚠️ The budget both are spent against is #769: 13.9 ms for the whole
frame, at 10 W on the OneXFly.

The froxel grid is already half of this, and the roadmap already said so
"Build the grid once. #477 (VSM page marking), #731 (the fog froxel
buffer) and the single shadow-page pool all want this same structure.
Three grids is the failure mode." — ROADMAP.md
What was not written down is why it is worth more than sharing an
allocation. The expensive question a volumetric pass asks per froxel
is how much light reaches this cell, and the cluster grid already
answers it: every cell knows which lights touch it (#780). The work
being shared is the light culling, not the buffer.
Godot arrived at the same structure — a frustum-aligned froxel buffer,
64×64×64 by default — and renders every FogVolume into that one buffer
rather than each independently, which "allows it to detach light
rendering from FogVolume rendering and support large numbers of lights
independent from the number of FogVolumes".
🔴 But the grid does not reach the clouds
The froxel buffer ends at ClusterSettings::far — 200 m by default, and
Godot's fog defaults to around 100 m. The clouds in this issue are a
spherical shell around a planet, kilometres out. They are outside
the grid by two orders of magnitude.
So the two are complementary rather than one technique:
structure
fog, god rays, local volumetrics
froxel buffer, sharing the cluster's light lists
planetary clouds
raymarch against the shell, its own pass
Assuming one covers the other is the same mistake as setting far: 40
for a scene the camera views from further than that (#820) — the grid
silently stops describing what is being asked of it.
⚠️ And the budget has not moved
These clouds cost 39 ms and are switched off, which is the only
reason the frame meets budget at all. Sharing the light lists makes a
volumetric pass cheaper; it does not make it free — it is still a
raymarch per froxel plus an integration. The frame is currently at twice
budget on direct lighting alone (#821), so this waits behind #824 /
#825 / #826.
References worth having before building it
Wronski, Volumetric Fog: Unified Compute Shader-Based Solution to
Atmospheric Scattering, SIGGRAPH 2014 — the origin of froxel-based
volumetrics, compute-shader based, reusing the light culling. The
paper this issue's near-field half should be built from.
https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/index.html
Valient, Reflections and Volumetrics of Killzone Shadow Fall,
SIGGRAPH 2014 — volumetrics with temporal reprojection, same course.
Schneider, The Real-Time Volumetric Cloudscapes of Horizon Zero
Dawn, SIGGRAPH 2015 — the canonical volumetric cloud paper, and it
models the clouds as a spherical shell over the planet, which is
exactly this issue's shape. Not in the 2014 course; it is the one to
read for the cloud half.
