Goal
Give VICE Runtime a stable temporal/post-processing backbone on top of the HDR scene path from #163, so TAA, reconstruction, bloom, auto exposure, SSR/RT denoising, and later image effects can plug into explicit frame inputs instead of being hard-wired into scene rendering.
Part of #159 Stage A.
Scope
introduce an explicit Runtime post-process pass plan and frame-input contract
keep tone mapping as the enabled terminal presentation pass
reserve ordered slots for temporal resolve and bloom without pretending those effects exist yet
replace the single HDR scene target with two drawable-sized RGBA16F targets used as persistent ping-pong frame history
expose current scene color, previous scene color, motion vectors, depth, frame index, history validity, and current/previous camera transforms through the Runtime post-process frame contract
generate a real drawable-sized RGBA16F motion-vector target every frame
derive per-pixel screen/UV-space velocity from current and previous camera view-projection matrices plus current and previous per-entity model transforms
emit zero motion on the first valid frame, newly seen entities, or after history invalidation
invalidate temporal history on resize/minimize and camera identity discontinuity
commit history only after a successful submitted/presented frame
preserve existing PBR, lighting, shadow cascades, HDR tone mapping, exposure, and project material ownership
add regression tests for post-process pass ordering and history lifecycle/continuity contracts
require exact-head macOS ARM64 and Windows x64 CI green, including Windows DXIL smoke
Out of scope
TAA filtering itself
temporal upscaling/reconstruction
bloom filtering
jittered projection
disocclusion/depth rejection
reactive masks
automatic exposure
HDR10/EDR output
Exit criteria
A Runtime frame produces HDR scene color plus real motion vectors, retains the prior HDR frame through a deterministic ping-pong history contract, executes presentation through an explicit ordered post-process plan, and resets history safely across resize/camera discontinuities on Metal and D3D12.
Part of #159.
