In order to better understand the effects of propagation loss caused by fog, Sandia National
Laboratories (SNL) has developed a fog chamber to characterize and qualify test
equipment, typically thermal imagers, visible cameras, and Class 3B/4 laser systems, in an
environmentally controlled chamber. Because natural occurring fog is difficult to measure,
due to inconsistency in density, visibility, and formation, the SNL Fog Chamber can provide
a unique national capability for validation testing. Currently, the chamber can produce
either a coastal/salt water based fog or inland/multi-constituent ground based fog to
introduce variations in propagation media. Located in a tunnel system, the chamber has
relatively stable environmental conditions such as temperature, humidity, air pressure, and
airflow, making it ideal for laboratory testing. The goal of this research is to improve upon
current techniques and instrumentation to overcome visibility loss by providing a realistic,
yet controllable environment that could be later implemented into automotive or aircraft
industries, for example.

Particle size distribution was measured in 1 second
intervals using a Malvern Spraytec and Inhalation Cell
accessory. The Malvern used an indirect measurement of
light scatter off a particle into a detector to calculate particle
size, which is inversely proportional to its scattering angle.
Mie theory, which predicts absorption and light scatter
intensity of a spherical particle, was the fundamental model
which the Malvern software used to determine particle size.
Additionally, the inhalation cell accessory was used as an
enclosed measurement zone with a controlled sample flow
rate. This was important for particle size accuracy and postmeasurement concentration calculations, which utilize the
Beer-Lambert Law, absorption of light through the particle
cloud, and particle diameter to determine volume
concentration.

Optical Tests
Reduced visibility due to the presence of fog can impact the imaging performance of a system significantly,
impacting areas from aviation to physical security. Areas of atmospheric science have utilized visibility as a
metric to capture the detrimental effects of fog on intensity propagation through the atmosphere. We report on
the calculation of imager visibility and spatial frequency response of three different commercial security
cameras as a function of fog density and particle number at various distances. Commonly used in aviation,
Meteorological Optical Range (MOR) was used in this study to determine visibility degradation. MOR is
defined as the distance at which illuminator intensity drops to 5% the nominal intensity.
