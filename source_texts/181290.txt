Triangular/hex prism voxels
Question
First of all, I’m from a a wildly different industry than most people here I imagine. I’m in additive manufacturing.
The crossovers from GPU compete to my world are becoming abundant and much needed.
I have an idea for a non cubic voxel that solves some challenges with additive, particularly powder bed laser additive… but.. it’s pretty unconventional. I was just looking for some insight from people who are closer to this tech than me to give me their thoughts on feasibility and challenges of developing this concept.
The grid would be a w,x,y,z where w,x and y would be orthogonal to Z and perpendicular to the edges the of tessellation of regular triangles on a planar slice along Z. The Z axis would remain as typical with cubic or rectangular voxels.
The advantages for my application is that laser scan vectors running east-west (0/180) are problematic, so this can be avoided by only using 30/210 degree edge headings, 90/270 and 120/330.
The octree parent child decomposition would still be 1 parent and 8 children when dividing a triangular prism into 8 triangular prisms and this could continue indefinitely. 4 equilateral 2D triangles form a 4X larger equilateral triangle just like in cubic octrees. And it’s also a upper and lower row, just like cubic octrees.
A hexagonal and triangular tessellation can coexist. So, the interesting twist is that you could also have hexagonal prism dodeca-tree parents of 12 triangular prism children.
This data structure… might just have more “sparse” capabilities due to the higher efficiency of a dual-cell grid with a hex prism parent being 50% more efficient at housing children than a triangular prism parent.
It would however complicate some things a bit. By having 4 axis instead of 3 in standard Cartesian it would add some calculations.
One example very relevant to my word is voxelization. Taking a polygon faceted body or a boundary rep solid and converting it to a voxel representation is widely available tech. The techniques of converting triangle facets to bounding rectangular prisms and checking against x,y,z projections for voxel binning is routine.
All the well established code would have to be re-factored for dealing with bounded oblique hexagonal prisms and their projections to triangular or Hexagonal prisms with 4 projections instead of the conventional 3.
Please give me some thoughts on any challenges I might have in developing this, or any comments to the novelty. Maybe it could have other applications beyond what my needs are.
