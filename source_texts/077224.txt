Issue body actions
A volume viewport steps an oblique slice by the L2 spacing. The step must use the L1 spacing, because a slice step is an overlap test. The L2 value is shorter than the L1 value for an oblique normal, so two consecutive oblique slices show the same voxels.
Summary
planarSliceBasis.ts gets the distance between two slices from getSpacingInNormalDirection. That function returns the L2 length of the projected spacing vector:
```
S_n = sqrt( Σᵢ (n · aᵢ · sᵢ)² )
```
The correct measure for a slice step is the L1 length, which getVoxelThicknessAlongNormal returns:
```
T_v = Σᵢ |n · aᵢ| · sᵢ
```
The two values are equal when the normal is parallel to a voxel axis. The two values differ for an oblique normal, and T_v is always the larger one. The viewport therefore steps a distance that is shorter than the thickness of one voxel, and two consecutive slices show some of the same voxels.
Affected code
packages/core/src/RenderingEngine/GenericViewport/Planar/planarSliceBasis.ts, line 384.
```
const spacingInNormalDirection = Math.max(
  getSpacingInNormalDirection(imageVolume, viewPlaneNormal),
  ...
);
```
The same value gives the number of slices at line 405, and the depth of a slice index at line 656.
Why the L1 value is correct
A voxel is a box. The L1 value is the width of that box along the normal. Two planes that are less than T_v apart both pass through some of the same voxels, and a plane shows the voxels that the plane passes through. A step of T_v is therefore the smallest step that shows a new set of voxels.
The set of voxels that a plane passes through is a standard digital plane. Two such sets share no voxel when the two planes are T_v apart, and together the two sets leave no voxel out. Consecutive slices at a step of T_v tile the volume. This is the 3D form of the digital line: a line of single pixels that repeats without a gap and without an overlap.
The docstring of getEffectiveSpacingAlongDirection already names the three measures, and already says that the L1 value "is what a voxel/slab overlap test needs".
How to reproduce
Open a volume viewport on an isotropic 1 mm volume.
Rotate the camera 30 degrees about an in-plane axis.
Paint one brush stroke with the labelmap brush.
Step to the next slice, and then to the previous slice.
The brush stroke is visible on the next slice and on the previous slice. The brush stroke must be visible on one slice only.
The defect is visible with a fill, because a fill writes a definite set of voxels. The same defect applies to every voxel that a viewport shows.

The CT case uses a spacing of 0.7 / 0.7 / 3 mm. The other cases use an isotropic spacing of 1 mm.
Three results matter:
A step of T_v gives an overlap of zero in every case, and a coverage of 100% in every case. No hole and no overlap occur together.
A step of S_n gives an overlap of 22% to 40% of the voxels for an oblique normal.
The axis aligned case gives the same result for both steps, which is why the defect is not visible in an acquisition-orientation view.
The 45 degree case also gives an overlap of zero at a step of S_n. The ratio T_v / S_n is sqrt(2) there, and the voxel centres fall on planes that are S_n apart. That result is a property of the 45 degree angle, and the result does not hold at another angle.
Effect of a correction
A step of T_v is larger than a step of S_n, so an oblique view gets fewer slices. The reduction is 27% at 30 degrees and 41% at 35/25 degrees. An acquisition-orientation view gets the same number of slices as before, because T_v and S_n are equal there.
Related
PR fix: improved brush shape for rotated images #2743 adds Rule F, which makes a brush fill write the voxels that the plane passes through. That PR does not change the slice step.
packages/docs/docs/concepts/cornerstone-tools/annotation/voxel-statistics.md gives Rule M, Rule F and Rule D. The section "Voxel thickness along the normal" compares the L1 measure, the L2 measure and the harmonic measure.
Issue [Feature Request] Define voxel-relative annotation statistics and implement a shared oblique-capable voxel iterator #2889 gives Rule M and Rule D.

Result of a test in a browser
A test in the labelmapSegmentationTools example confirms the report, and the test also shows a second defect. PR #2743 adds an experimental flag, rendering.sliceStepMeasure, and a toggle in that example. The flag takes the value 'l2' by default, which keeps the historic behaviour.
The L1 step gives one plane per slice
With sliceStepMeasure: 'l1', one brush fill on an oblique plane fills one slice completely, and no other slice shows a part of that fill. With 'l2', each neighbouring slice shows a part of the fill.
Two diagnostics report the numbers. Both are off by default:
cs3d.core.utilities.getTargetVolumeAndSpacingInNormalDir reports the measure, the L1 value, the L2 value, and the value that the viewport uses. It reports an oblique viewport only.
cs3d.tools.tools.segmentation.brushVoxelSlab reports the depth spread of the voxels that one fill writes. A fill of one digital plane gives a depthSpanInVoxels below 1.
A second defect: the focal point must sit on a slice position
The L1 step alone is not sufficient. A viewport must also put the focal point on a slice position, and the code that sets an oblique camera does not do that.
The example showed this defect clearly. The angle slider set the focal point to the centre of the volume. That centre does not sit on the grid of slice positions of the new normal, and the offset was as much as half of one voxel. A fill then wrote one plane between two slice positions. The slice that the user painted on looked solid, and the slice before and the slice after each showed a part of the same plane as a set of horizontal lines. The lines appeared on both neighbours, because the plane was off the grid in both directions.
A call to scroll(0) removes the lines, because snapFocalPointToSlice rounds the focal point onto the nearest slice position. The example does this now.
The offset rule that this issue asks for
A call to scroll(0) is not an acceptable answer for an application. An application gives a focal point, and the application must not need to reset that focal point to a value that Cornerstone chooses. Two problems follow from a reset:
The reset moves the plane that the application asked for. The application asked for a plane through a specific point, such as a point that a report gives, or a point that another viewport shares.
The reset is easy to omit. Each place that sets a camera must remember to call it, and nothing reports the omission.
The correct behaviour keeps the focal point that the application gives, and measures every slice position from that focal point. The offset of slice m is then m * T_v from the given focal point, and not min + m * T_v from a corner of the volume. Each slice index then shows exactly one planar view, whatever focal point the application gives.
Two requirements follow.
User requirements
U1. An application sets a focal point, and the viewport keeps that exact focal point. The viewport does not move the focal point onto a grid of its own.
U2. Each slice index shows exactly one plane of voxels. No voxel of the plane of slice m appears on slice m + 1 or on slice m - 1.
U3. U1 and U2 hold together, and they hold for an oblique normal as well as for an acquisition orientation.
Implementation requirements
I1. The slice step uses the L1 measure, getVoxelThicknessAlongNormal, and not the L2 measure, getSpacingInNormalDirection.
I2. The grid of slice positions has its origin at the focal point that the application gives, and not at a corner of the volume. snapFocalPointToSlice measures from min, which is the origin of the problem.
I3. The count of slices and the index of a slice both come from that same origin. A negative index is possible, so the code must map the index range to the extent of the volume rather than assume an index of 0 at the first corner.
How to reproduce
Run the labelmapSegmentationTools example.
Turn on "Slice step: voxel width (L1)".
Press "Reset Cameras", and set "Axial Oblique Angle" to 45 degrees.
Paint one circle in the centre of the axial viewport.
Step one slice forward, and then two slices backward.
The fill fills one slice completely, and the slice before and the slice after show nothing. Turn the toggle off and repeat to see the historic behaviour.
To see the second defect, remove the scroll(0) call from setAxialObliqueAngle in the example. Each neighbouring slice then shows a set of horizontal lines.

The L2 length is never greater than the L1 length, so the step is never greater than the thickness of one plane. The current behaviour therefore shows a voxel two times, and the current behaviour never hides a voxel. This is the safe direction of the two.
A viewport misses a plane in one case only: the step becomes the slab thickness, through the useSlabThickness argument of getVolumeViewportScrollInfo, and that slab thickness is greater than T_v. A step of 6 mm missed 52% to 83% of the voxels in the same model. That model gives each slice one plane, and a viewport with a real slab renders the whole slab, so read this case as the general rule: a step greater than T_v hides planes.

At 45 degrees the depths of the voxel centres are multiples of T_v / 2, so the centres fall exactly on the boundaries of the slab. isWithinSlab uses a strict test, |d| < h - epsilon, so those voxels belong to neither of the two adjacent slabs. Every second plane of the lattice is then unreachable.
An angle that gives no such coincidence has no such loss. The loss appears at 45 degrees and at the other angles that make the depths of the centres commensurate with T_v.
The cause is the interval, and not the step. A standard digital plane is half-open, 0 <= g · x + c < ||g||₁. The code tests an open interval that is symmetric about the plane and has a width of T_v, and an open interval drops both of its endpoints. A test that assigns each voxel by floor((d - min) / T_v) gives, for every case in the table above and for 45 degrees as well: 68921 voxels shown one time, 0 shown two times, 0 never shown.
This adds one implementation requirement.
I4. The depth test along the normal is half-open, and the depth test is not symmetric and strict. Without I4 the L1 step is worse than the current behaviour at 45 degrees.
3. The snap has no basis focal point
snapFocalPointToSlice takes its grid from the current focal point, and not from a fixed origin. The function computes f = (current - min) / (max - min) * steps, and steps = round((max - min) / spacing). The rounding makes steps * spacing different from max - min, so the mapping carries a scale factor k = steps * spacing / (max - min) that is not 1.

Three properties fail for an oblique normal, and all three hold for an acquisition orientation:
The snap is not idempotent. A second call to the snap moves the focal point again. The sequence converges, and the sequence does not stop at the first value.
A round trip does not return. One step forward and one step backward give a focal point that differs from the start. The error is bounded, because the map contracts, and the error does not accumulate without limit. I did not measure a long sequence of round trips.
There is no single snap position. Two focal points inside one slice snap to two different positions. The position that a user reaches depends on the position that the user came from.
The errors are small, about 0.008 mm against a voxel of 1.37 mm. The structure is the defect, and not the size of the error. An application cannot predict the position that a viewport gives, an application cannot return to a position, and an annotation that an application creates at one position does not lie on the grid that a later navigation produces. A voxel that sits within 0.008 mm of a boundary of the slab moves to the other plane, which is about 0.6% of the voxels of one plane.
The same code also holds two different conventions. snapFocalPointToSlice builds a grid that is relative to the current focal point. planarSliceBasis maps an index to a depth with min + index * spacing, which is a grid that is absolute and anchored at a corner of the volume. The two must agree.
This adds one user requirement and two implementation requirements.
U4. A viewport returns to the same focal point after the user navigates away and back. An application that gives a focal point reaches that same focal point again, and an annotation created there lies on a slice position.
I5. The grid of slice positions has one basis focal point, and the code measures every position as basis + m * T_v. The basis is the focal point that the application gives, and the basis is not a corner of the volume and not the current focal point.
I6. snapFocalPointToSlice and planarSliceBasis use that one basis and that one rule. A snap is then idempotent, a round trip returns exactly, and every focal point inside one slice snaps to one position.
The requirements together
User requirements
U1. An application sets a focal point, and the viewport keeps that exact focal point.
U2. Each slice index shows exactly one plane of voxels.
U3. U1 and U2 hold together, for an oblique normal as well as for an acquisition orientation.
U4. A viewport returns to the same focal point after the user navigates away and back.
Implementation requirements
I1. The slice step uses the L1 measure, getVoxelThicknessAlongNormal.
I2. The grid of slice positions takes its origin from the given focal point.
I3. The count of slices and the index of a slice come from that same origin, and a negative index is possible.
I4. The depth test along the normal is half-open.
I5. The grid has one basis focal point, and every position is basis + m * T_v.
I6. snapFocalPointToSlice and planarSliceBasis use that one basis and that one rule.

I4 is implemented, and a correction to the tables above
I4 is done, in PR #2743
Commit 7a5b0fba3 gives Rule F a half-open depth interval. The low boundary is the closed end, so each voxel belongs to exactly one of two consecutive fills.
The change is in three parts:
indexSpaceSlab.ts gains integersWithProductInHalfOpenInterval, next to the open form that it already had.
buildIndexSpaceSlab and iterateVoxelsInShape take a depthInterval option. The option takes the value 'open' by default.
The brush fill passes 'half-open'. Rule F is defined with the half-open interval, and the value is not a choice that a caller makes.
The epsilon moves the interval instead of narrowing it, so the interval keeps a width of exactly 2 * halfWidth. A narrower interval leaves a gap between two consecutive fills.

Rule M and Rule D keep the open interval, and the change moves no measurement. A test asserts that a call without the option gives the same voxels as a call with depthInterval: 'open'. Rule M needs the open interval, because the widened slab of Rule M must report both layers for a plane midway between two layers.
The half-open interval has a second effect on a thick fill. A half-open interval of width F holds exactly F / T_v layers, whatever the position of the plane between two layers. The open interval gave one layer more or one layer less by that position.
A correction: the tables above measure a fill, and not a display
The tables in my earlier comment carry the column names "shown once", "shown twice or more" and "never shown". Those names are wrong, and this paragraph corrects them. The measurement uses collectVoxelsInShape, which selects the voxels that a fill writes. The measurement does not read the renderer.
Read "never shown" as "no fill can write this voxel". The value of 49.97% at 45 degrees means that half of the voxels of the volume were unreachable by a brush stroke at any slice, and the value does not describe what a viewport paints.
