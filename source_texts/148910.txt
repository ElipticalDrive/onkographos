Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) methods have shown promising results in 3D rendering of objects and scenes from multi-view close-range images, yet there is a lack of validation research in the field of remote sensing. This paper aims to evaluate the performance of NeRF methods in 3D reconstruction tasks using remote sensing imagery especially on geometric reconstruction accuracy. We introduce and compared various NeRF methods using both nadir and oblique aerial remote sensing imagery. Results, including rendered RGB images and depth maps, were evaluated using multiple criteria, including PSNR and RMSE. The findings indicate that the current NeRF methods have limited applicability in large scenes, struggling to achieve the same level of RGB image rendering quality as in close-range scenes, and perform much worse in restoring the geometric structure of outdoor large scenes compared to conventional methods. Among them, methods based on Signed Distance Fields (SDF) showed better geometric effects than other NeRF methods, while the Gaussian Splatting method exhibited the best overall performance in terms of color rendering quality and geometric structure understanding.
