High-quality real-time novel view synthesis in 360° scenes remains challenging under sparse viewpoints and widebaseline camera trajectories. While Three-Dimensional Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a real-time alternative to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for novel view synthesis, recent graphguided extensions, such as GraphGS, rely on fixed parameters and global poses, limiting their effectiveness in such 360° scenes. In this work, we propose a robust camera graph construction framework specifically tailored for 360° novel view synthesis. Our approach introduces three key enhancements: a corrected edge-weight formulation that avoids instability under orthogonal views, binary encoding of relative position and orientation to mitigate global drift, and a dynamic quadrant filtering mechanism based on common 3D feature points obtained from COLMAP. These improvements produce a geometrically consistent and statistically adaptive camera graph that guides the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization process. Experiments on the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset demonstrate that our method outperforms the baseline 3DGS and achieves results comparable to state-of-the-art models, while retaining the real-time advantages of 3DGS.
