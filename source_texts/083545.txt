Now. Every 3D frame re-evaluates the fractal DE along every ray, so an orbit / dolly of the camera over an unchanged fractal pays the full formula cost each frame. Expensive User Bulb maps are seconds per frame.
Idea. When only the camera moves, bake the DE once into a sparse brick volume:
a coarse grid of bricks around the surface (DE < brick size), each a small 3D texture of distances;
the empty-space distance stored at the coarse level;
raymarch the cache during camera interaction (trilinear distance, hardware texture filtering on the GPU).
Rebuild only on a parameter change. The full-detail DE render runs once the camera settles. That's the interactive-preview path only, so the final frame is unchanged.
Watch.
Memory: cap the brick count; detail is bounded by the brick resolution (a preview, not final).
Bake time must be less than a few frames' worth to pay off. Bake progressively, coarse first.
Verify. The cached-distance error is ≤ half a voxel diagonal near the surface (sampled against the true DE). The interactive frame-time gain is measured on User Bulb heavy maps and KIFS.
Win. Interactive camera moves on expensive formulas go from seconds to near real time. Large for weak hardware; large for strong too on heavy maps.
Related: #389 (3D roadmap), #1110 / #1111 (User Bulb compile speed: complementary).
From the 2026-10-15 GPU / rendering performance survey (ideas borrowed from games, offline renderers and GPU computing), filed as Phase 3b of #1298. Gains are estimates until measured.
