# Real-organ computational spaces from medical images

This toolkit converts a medical image of an organ into a **3D numerical domain** on a
regular Cartesian grid: a binary tissue mask plus a signed distance field (SDF). The
domain can be used directly by finite-difference (FDM) or finite-volume (FVM) solvers
and by agent-based models. It is the method that produced the 16 primary-organ and
20 secondary-site computational spaces of the SOMATA tumour model
(`../Primary`, `../Secondary`).

Everything needed to reproduce our results is in this folder. One worked example
(liver, CT) is bundled with its data and reproduces our deployed liver domain
**bit for bit**.

---

## 1. Where the computational spaces come from

All shapes come from **real data**. No shape is drawn by hand or generated by formula.
The 19 built organs come from three kinds of source:

| Source | Count | Organs | Datasets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CT** (computed tomography) | 17 | Liver, Kidney, Lung, Pancreas, Prostate, Bladder, Stomach, Gallbladder, Colon, Cervix, Head & neck (larynx), Spleen, Heart, Thyroid, Adrenal, Bone (L3 vertebra), Brain | 3D-IRCADb-01; TotalSegmentator v2.0.1; AMOS22; HaN-Seg |
| **MRI** (magnetic resonance imaging) | 1 | Ovary | UT-EndoMRI |
| **Anatomical mesh** (triangle surface, not a patient scan) | 1 | Testicle | BodyParts3D 4.0 |

In every case the input to our method is an **organ segmentation**: for each voxel,
"is this voxel the organ?". The datasets above ship expert or model segmentations
together with the images. A user who has only a raw image must first segment it
(§4.1); this toolkit includes and validates that step for CT.

The remaining sites of the model have no real shape: Neuroendocrine reuses the
pancreas; Melanoma/Skin, Breast, the thin membranes (meninges, pericardium, pleura,
peritoneum), soft tissue, lymph node and "other" keep a cubic domain. Reasons are
recorded per organ in `../Primary/<Organ>/source.txt` and `../Secondary/<Site>/source.txt`.

## 2. The chain: image → domain

```
 raw image          organ mask            signed distance field        FDM / FVM grid
 (CT in HU,   ──►   (true/false per  ──►  phi on a regular grid   ──►  active nodes = phi < 0,
  DICOM/NIfTI)       native voxel)        phi < 0 inside              boundary distance = |phi|
      │                   │                     │
  step 1:            step 2:               step 3:
  segmentation       read + orient         resample, distance transform,
  (TotalSegmentator  (io_ct.jl)            anti-alias, re-distance
   or expert)                              (common/sdf_core.jl)
```

| Folder | Content |
|---|---|
| `common/` | Modality-independent core: `sdf_core.jl` (mask → SDF), `validate.jl` (acceptance), `render.jl` (3-view figure) |
| `CT/` | `io_ct.jl` (DICOM series and NIfTI readers), `segment_ct.ps1` / `segment_ct.sh` (raw CT → mask), `dice.jl` (automatic vs expert mask), `example_liver/` (bundled data) |
| `Other/MRI/` | `run_mri_mask.jl` (MRI mask → SDF; paired-organ selection) |
| `Other/Mesh/` | `obj_voxelize.jl` (closed OBJ mesh → mask), `example_testicle/` (bundled mesh) |

## 3. Mathematics of step 3 (`common/sdf_core.jl`)

Input: binary mask $M$ on a native grid with voxel sizes $(\Delta_x,\Delta_y,\Delta_z)$ mm.

1. **Optional cavity fill.** Non-tissue voxels that cannot be reached from the volume
   border (6-connectivity) are set to tissue. Used for hollow organs (stomach, bladder,
   colon, heart), which the library treats as solid.
2. **Crop and scale.** Bounding box of $M$; uniform scale $s = L_\text{target}/L_\text{max}$
   (simulation units per mm), $L_\text{max}$ = longest bbox side. The bbox centre becomes
   the origin. Relative sizes between organs are not preserved (each organ is scaled to
   the same long axis).
3. **Resample.** Nearest-neighbour onto a grid of spacing $h$ (`dx_sdf`), padded by a
   margin. Node $(i,j,k)$ sits at `origin + ((i,j,k) - 1) * h`.
4. **Exact signed distance.** Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher separable squared Euclidean
   distance transform, run twice (sources = tissue, sources = background):
   $\varphi = -d(\text{node},\ \text{background})$ inside, $+d(\text{node},\ \text{tissue})$ outside.
5. **Anti-alias.** Nearest-neighbour resampling leaves a staircase whose step is the
   native voxel in simulation units, $\Delta s$. A separable Gaussian with
   $\sigma = \tfrac12 \Delta_\text{max} s$ removes it. The surface moves by less than the
   source resolution, so no shape detail is invented.
6. **Re-distance.** Gaussian smoothing averages gradient vectors of different directions
   and pushes $|\nabla\varphi|$ below 1. The interior $\{\varphi<0\}$ of the smoothed field
   is therefore distance-transformed again (step 4). The smoothed surface is kept and
   $|\nabla\varphi| = 1$ is restored.
7. **Derived quantities.** Centroid of tissue nodes; `seed_center` = deep node
   ($\varphi \le \tfrac12\min\varphi$) nearest the centroid (organs are non-convex, so the
   centroid itself may lie outside); `x_lo`, `x_hi` = tissue extent in $x$.

Output `<name>_geometry.jld2`: `sdf` (Float32), `mask` (Bool), `spacing`, `origin`,
`nx, ny, nz`, `centroid`, `seed_center`, `x_lo`, `x_hi`, `scale_units_per_mm`, `source`, `note`.
