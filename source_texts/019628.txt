ABSTRACT
Tomographic reconstruction techniques, using radio frequency electromagnetic wave data, have been applied to the monitoring of fluid distributions at three different enhanced recovery pilot sites.
The attenuation of waves propagating between observation wells was measured at multiple frequencies in the range from 1 to 30 MHz. On two sites more than one pair of wells were available, permitting varied views of the reservoir.
Different versions of published tomographic reconstruction algorithms were applied to both real and synthetic data. The algorithms used include those with both single ray path and lumped system convergence principals.
Results show that tomographic reconstruction requirements vary with reservoir and "system" operations, i.e., well spacing, geometry, frequency, rock/fluid parameters, dynamic range, density of data, and computational techniques. Reconstructions of the same reservoir zone at several frequencies appears to be especially diagnostic.
For those unfamiliar with the terminology, "tomograph" is a "picture of a slice;" in this case a vertical slice bounded by the two boreholes. The reconstruction technology is analogous to that currently used in diagnostic medicine to create "pictures" of the interior of the human body using x-rays or ultrasound.
In "geo-tomography" the probing energy is electromagnetic or elastic (seismic) waves.
