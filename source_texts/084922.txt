Marching Cubes is great and all, but it is an old algorithm with a couple drawbacks. Mainly it's slow, and only designed to work with uniform-density voxel data. Given that we're using non-uniform chunk sizes for performant LODs, this can produce very harsh artifacts and seams between LODs.
Other algorithms, such as Dual Contouring, offer more understandable geometry, and work better with non-unfirom and complex shapes.
https://www.cs.rice.edu/~jwarren/papers/dualcontour.pdf
https://www.boristhebrave.com/2018/04/15/dual-contouring-tutorial/
One focus of Dual Contouring is the ability to generate both sharp and soft edges based on additional vertex data, which while nice, is not strictly necessary for base terrain modeling, which is usually quite smooth on a large scale. Perhaps rocky cliffs could be an argument to revisit this, but such functionality is not needed as of yet. An alternative, while keeping the same benefits, is Surface Nets, a less complex extension of Dual Contouring that focuses just on Isosurface Extraction. This is commonly used for terrain rendering, and there is even a lot of material on Godot-specific implementations.
https://cerbion.net/blog/understanding-surface-nets/
https://medium.com/@ryandremer/implementing-surface-nets-in-godot-f48ecd5f29ff
