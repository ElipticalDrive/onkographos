Addresses #726: sample the fluence rate on an octree refined only where interpolating it fails, then interpolate it to any points (for example a flow mesh's cell centres, for a dose).
What's in it
aquaflux.octree, a generic package that imports nothing of aquaflux but its neutral leaves.
Octree: the one octree, shared with the mesher's hex core (see below).
sample_adaptively(sample, region, ...) → SampledField: the refinement loop.
Each pass samples only its new points and measures each one's miss against the coarser tree. A cell is split where a miss exceeds the tolerance.
The interpolant is hierarchical: each node carries a surplus on a hat function. It is continuous where coarse cells meet fine ones and passes through every sampled node, so the tree needs no 2:1 balance.
Cut points are placed where cell edges cross the region's boundary, a standoff inside it by signed distance. The standoff defaults to a thousandth of the finest cell, so it scales with the case.
Nodes outside the region get ghost values. These are fitted to the cut points and nearby samples, bounded by them, and refitted as cells refine.
A cell the boundary cuts is also sampled at its centre (a probe). Its corners outside the region are fitted values, so nothing else tests its interior.
Linear / Logarithm transforms (ln G gives a relative tolerance, with a floor below which misses are ignored).
SampledField.hexahedra() for viewing the tree.
Elsewhere:
aquaflux.io.write_hexahedra: axis-aligned hexahedra with point and cell data, as .vtu.
aquaflux.radiation.fluence_rate_at(scene, points): the scene solved with those points as its only receivers.
validation/sozzi_radiation/adaptive_grid.py: the study below, built entirely from the reactor's STEP drawing with no mesh.
One Octree for the mesher and the sampler
Main's aquaflux/meshing/octree.py (#779/#788) and this branch's tree held the same data:
leaves stored as a level plus a lower corner in integer units of the finest cube;
children appended after the kept leaves, in the same order.
Only the operations differed, so they are now one class in aquaflux.octree. The sampler cannot import meshing, but meshing may import a generic package.
What it keeps: main's API unchanged (lattice, refined, refined_by, balanced, leaf_at, the level/corner/unit/depth fields).
What it gains for the sampler:
a depth fixed at construction;
locate for points;
cube_corners, point_level and subset;
keys / coordinates, which also replace the hex core's private _decode.
What stays in meshing: NearSurface and cube_samples, now in meshing/near_surface.py.
Renames:
RefinementCriterion is exported from aquaflux.octree instead of aquaflux.meshing; Octree likewise. There are no aliases left behind.
sample_adaptively's max_level → depth, the tree's word.
Hierarchy.at_lattice → at_units.
The sampler's output is bit-identical before and after on the test vessel: every sample, miss and interpolated value matches. The hex core's and the octree balancing tests pass unchanged apart from imports.

```
A new generic package, aquaflux.octree, samples a field over a region on an
octree refined where the coarser tree failed to predict its own new samples:
split a cell, sample only its new points, compare each with the previous
interpolant (its miss), split again where a miss exceeds the tolerance.

- Octree: root cells over a box on one integer lattice; split, locate,
  node levels; only the root cells the region can reach are kept.
- Hierarchy: a hierarchical (hat-function) interpolant with one surplus per
  node -- continuous where coarse cells meet finer ones, through every
  sampled node, and needing no 2:1 balance.
- Cut points where cell edges cross the region's boundary, placed where the
  region's signed distance is minus a standoff, so no sample lies nearer the
  boundary than that.
- Ghost values for nodes outside the region (or within the standoff), fitted
  to the cut points and nearby samples, re-fitted as finer cells appear and
  bounded by the range of their data.
- sample_adaptively / SampledField (callable, hexahedra()); Linear and
  Logarithm transforms; compiled region queries.

Around it: radiation.fluence_rate_at(scene, points), the scene as a sampler;
io.write_hexahedra and point data in vtu_parts, to write an octree's leaves.

validation/sozzi_radiation/adaptive_grid.py compares adaptive trees with
uniform 8/4/2 mm grids on the Sozzi reactor read from its STEP drawing, against
exact gathers at random points banded by distance from the sleeve. Tolerance
0.05 matches or beats uniform 2 mm within 5 mm of the sleeve at 29 % of its
samples; matching 4 mm everywhere costs 2.3x 4 mm's samples, so the issue's
"fraction of its points" is not met: the bulk needs ~4 mm to reach 4 mm's
error. The README records the table and its configuration.

A cell with a corner that is not sampled has ghosts there, fitted to
agree with its cut points, so its own samples cannot show how it
interpolates between them. On the Sozzi reactor a strip of 4 mm cells
along the lamp was never split and overshot by 10 % from 0.5 to 2.5 mm
off the sleeve. Such a cell's centre is now sampled as a probe, missed
against the interpolant the pass built, and entered as a node.

The Sozzi harness takes a standoff per arm (_s<mm>) and keeps each
arm's estimates and field for diagnosis.

Default the octree standoff to a thousandth of the finest cell (#726)

```
A fixed length does not travel between cases of different sizes. The
region is the drawn shape, and a convex lamp's facets lie inside it, so
any point of the drawn water is clear of them; the standoff only has to
keep samples off the boundary itself, where a lattice node can lie in a
facet's plane. Unset, it is now a thousandth of the finest cell's edge.

The Sozzi harness uses the default and takes _l<levels> for a deeper tree.
