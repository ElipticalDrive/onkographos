Before: there was no way to measure how well Arabic book text compresses, or what it costs to read a single page from compressed storage.
After: bench/compression downloads a reproducible sample of Shamela books and compares gzip, brotli, zstd and zstd with a trained dictionary, both as whole files and as per-page, 4 KB, 16 KB and 64 KB blocks. It reports compression ratio, decode speed, and the time and download size needed to read one random page.
How: fetch_openiti.py samples 26 test books by size, plus 30 different books used only to train the dictionary. They come from OpenITI's republished Shamela texts, since src/shamela stores only the catalog. prepare.py strips OpenITI markup and restores page breaks. bench.py runs every codec and block size, and report.py writes a Markdown summary. These are standalone Python scripts with no changes to existing code. Downloaded data and results go to the gitignored bench/compression/data/ folder.
