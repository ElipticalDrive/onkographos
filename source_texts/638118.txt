The 3D nodes now have world-space bounds and the cameras a frustum, the foundation of the culling of BAL-16. Nothing reads them to cull yet, so the rendering does not change.
Bounding volumes (math). balder::math::bounds re-exports, with def, one submodule per type:
Aabb, a box that is empty by default and merges with another box. transformed(m) encloses the transformed box from its center and the absolute values of the linear part of m (Arvo), without walking its eight corners.
Sphere, empty when its radius is negative.
Plane, normalized from its equation, with a signed distance to a point.
Frustum, whose six planes are extracted from a view-projection matrix (Gribb–Hartmann, clip volume -w ≤ x, y, z ≤ w). classify places a sphere or a box OUTSIDE, INTERSECTING or INSIDE it, and intersects says whether a part of it may be seen. Telling inside from intersecting lets a BVH skip the children of a node fully inside.
Local box (scene). MeshArray.aabb and VisualInstance3D.aabb return an Aabb instead of a (vec3, vec3).
World bounds (scene). Node3D.bounds() returns the box enclosing the node and its children in world space.
It is computed when read after a change only: a move, a child entering or leaving the tree. A move marks its ancestors up to the first one already marked.
VisualInstance3D.boundingSphere() returns a Sphere computed along with the box, instead of being computed at each call. The level-of-detail selection reads it at each tick.
Frustum (camera). Camera.frustum is extracted where the camera computes its matrices, for the perspective and the orthographic cameras.
Tests: 839 pass, 34 of them new (bounding_volumes: the boxes, the planes and the classification against a perspective and an orthographic projection; node_bounds: the bounds of the nodes following their moves, their children and the tree; mesh_array: the box of a mesh).
