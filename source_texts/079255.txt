What's broken
render_with_gradients is vastly slower than the forward render. On an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, render_with_gradients at a 256 px detector, 1.0 mm step and a $128^3$ volume measures 2.365 ms against 0.263 ms for the raw forward render, and a full optimisation step through SlangDiffDRRFunction measures 2.954 ms. Netting out the re-rendered forward, the readbacks and the launch overhead, roughly 1.8 ms is bwd_diff execution alone, about seven times the marching kernel it differentiates. The reason behind this is that the reverse-mode transform of the [MaxIters(2048)] march loop must store or recompute per-iteration state, and the package pays that price on every pixel of every backward pass.
It does not, actually, need to. The adjoint of a Beer-Lambert line integral is, as we all remember, a single forward-like march: accumulate the optical depth and its six-component pose derivative in one pass, multiply at the end. A hand-written adjoint kernel should cost roughly one forward, bounding the whole optimisation step near 1.0 to 1.1 ms at this configuration, an end-to-end speedup of about 2.7x.
Where we lose time
(all references are to commit 945948d, which is what head is at the time of writing)
The backward kernel (diffdrr_slang.slang lines 341 to 386) calls bwd_diff(computePixelIntensity) per pixel (lines 373 to 381). The march inside computePixelIntensity iterates up to MAX_STEPS = 2048 with trilinear fetches per step; reverse-mode through that loop synthesises per-iteration state handling that the forward never pays.

Maths time
Per pixel, with samples $x_i = s(\theta) + t_i(\theta),d(\theta)$ and weights $\Delta t$,
$$I = i_0 \ e^{-A} \qquad A = \sum_i \mu(x_i)\ \Delta t, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta} = - \frac{\partial L}{\partial I}\ I \frac{\partial A}{\partial \theta}.$$
The pose derivative of the optical depth is one march:
$$\frac{\partial A}{\partial \theta} =\ \sum_i \nabla\mu(x_i)\cdot\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial \theta}\ \Delta t +\ \sum_i \mu(x_i) \ \frac{\partial \Delta t}{\partial \theta} $$
where $\partial x_i/\partial\theta = \partial s/\partial\theta + t_i,\partial d/\partial\theta + d,\partial t_i/\partial\theta$. Every per-ray Jacobian ($\partial s$, $\partial d$, $\partial t_\mathrm{near}$, $\partial t_\mathrm{far}$ with respect to the six pose parameters) is closed form and computed once per pixel: three Euler derivative matrices applied to constant vectors + the slab-face formulas for the intersection derivatives.
Since the eight corner values already fetched determine it exactly, the trilinear gradient $\nabla\mu$ is free at each sample. So the adjoint is a single march accumulating $A$ and the six-vector $\partial A/\partial\theta$ together, followed by one multiplication. The cost of that is the forward's fetches + a modicum of extra arithmetic, say 1-1.5x the forward march, giving us a 5.5x save. As a bonus the forward intensity never needs re-rendering.
Reproduction
The big shared ablator here has it.
```
pip install numpy slangpy matplotlib torch  # CUDA build
python throughput_ablation.py --repo /path/to/i4h-sensor-simulation/fluoro-simulator --out report_perf
```
With the adjoint in place, the optimisation step is bounded by one forward march, one adjoint march, the gradient reduction and the fixed overheads That empirically gives around 1.0-1.1 ms vs the measured 2.954 ms, near 2.7x, without even addressing residency or batching. Free perfomance is good.
