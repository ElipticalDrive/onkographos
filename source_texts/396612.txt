Summary
Adds viewer/pet_volume_render.html — a standalone, offline-capable Three.js volume renderer for the S18750/S29811 PET series. No Colab, no server, no internet connection needed after download (Three.js r128 is inlined, ~600 KB, and the full volume is embedded as a base64 3D texture, ~27.7 MB).
Why not another MIP
A Maximum Intensity Projection always shows the single brightest voxel along each ray — a low-count, "cold" region sitting behind a hot one is completely invisible, no matter the rotation angle. This viewer instead does true volumetric ray marching with front-to-back alpha compositing (Beer-Lambert absorption), so faint voxels stay partially visible rather than being occluded. Rotating the volume reveals a low-activity void inside the source that never showed up in the MIP.
Implementation
DICOM slices loaded with pydicom, rescaled to Bq/mL, normalized to the 99.9th percentile (9385 Bq/mL) and packed into a uint8 3D texture (182×338×338)
THREE.DataTexture3D + RawShaderMaterial (GLSL3/WebGL2) ray-marches through the volume per pixel
Absorption is normalized to the actual ray-segment length, so the ray steps (quality) slider changes smoothness, not overall opacity — a common bug in naive volume shaders
Box geometry is scaled to the true 2 mm-isotropic physical proportions (676×676×364 mm) — the same aspect-ratio fix applied to the MIP notebook in Fix MIP aspect ratio distortion #2, avoiding that distortion here too
Controls
Window min/max — intensity range mapped into the colormap
Gamma — boosts or suppresses faint voxel visibility
Opacity gain — overall absorption strength
Ray steps — render quality (50–500)
Colormap — Hot / Grayscale / Viridis-ish
Auto-rotate toggle, plus mouse drag-to-rotate and scroll-to-zoom
Verification
Rendered and interacted with in headless Chromium (Playwright) before committing: confirmed WebGL2 context creation, zero page/console errors, correct volume geometry, working drag-rotation (screenshot diff across silhouettes), working auto-rotate (pixel-diffed consecutive frames), and colormap switching. Screenshots reviewed for the default view, a rotated view revealing internal structure, a boosted-contrast view, and the viridis colormap.
