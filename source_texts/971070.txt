Stacked on #99.
Drawables in the 3D viewer are lit as GTA V lights them on a clear afternoon, with a shadow from the sun. Before, they had one fixed light and a flat two-colour ambient.
What it does
The game's light. It comes from GTA V's timecycle for clear weather (EXTRASUNNY, global region; the file is the same in both editions), interpolated between keyframes at the viewer's 14:30. The fields used are in Shaders/timecycle.json:
the directional light's colour × multiplier;
the natural ambient's down light (from the sky) and up light (from the ground), with the down wrap;
the directional ambient;
the vehicle and prop ambient-occlusion multipliers;
the exposure range.
The game's lighting passes, decoded from the Enhanced shader DB and run in the same pass as the material:
PS_directional_just_dir: diffuse × N·L plus the normalised Blinn-Phong highlight, × the light colour, with no 1/π.
PS_directional_ambient: the natural hemisphere plus the directional ambient, × albedo, minus what the surface reflects. It's scaled by the natural ambient the deferred shaders store, 1 − m·(1 − vertex R) with the timecycle's occlusion multiplier m, and the pass squares that.
The game's exposure and tone map:
PS_CalculateExposure: visualsettings.dat's curve (-106.2·L^0.007 + 104.7 stops, plus the timecycle's exposure), clamped to the timecycle's range. The game measures the whole frame; the viewer has no world around the model, so it measures open mid-grey ground under the same light. At 14:30 that's about −3.2 stops.
PS_Composite: the filmic (Hable) curve with visualsettings.dat's bright and dark settings (the timecycle doesn't override them in clear weather), blended by exposure and divided by the curve at the white point. Colour correction, shift and desaturation are neutral in this weather, and the sRGB target does the gamma.
Where the light comes from: RedFury's DayCycle solar path (sun by day, moon by night). The timecycle gives the colour and strength.
Shadows: one shadow map fitted to the model's bounding sphere, deep enough for long shadows. It's sampled with RedFury's cascade_pcf: nine comparison taps, with the normal offset scaled by the texel footprint. It falls on the model, and on the backdrop's ground through a multiply pass that finds the ground under each pixel as the backdrop does.
HDR: drawables are drawn into an RGBA16F target with coverage, then tone-mapped over the backdrop with a display-space dither. The backdrop, collisions and navmeshes are drawn as before, in the theme's colours.
The first version of this PR used RedFury's physical sun and sky (Hillaire LUTs, harmonics and atmosphere). That's made for PBR shading, so GTA's materials, with albedo and occlusion squared, came out too dark. It's removed.
Inferred, not read from the game
Which way the hemisphere runs: the down light is taken to be the one on up-facing surfaces, since it's the bluish sky colour.
The direction of the directional ambient: the sun's, turned below the horizon by light_directional_amb_bounce_enabled. The pass reads it from a constant whose source isn't in the shaders.
The bright-to-dark blend: by the exposure, between visualsettings.dat's −0.5 and 3 stops.

Other weathers' timecycles, and the artificial ambient (interiors, and nearly nothing outdoors by day).
Blended surfaces (glass) don't cast, and masked ones cast as solid, as there's no alpha test in the shadow pass.
Tests
The afternoon sun is up and west of noon; at night the moon is the light and the timecycle dims it; hours wrap.
The timecycle returns its keyframes and interpolates each field between them, through midnight.
The natural ambient is the down light facing up and the up light facing down, with the wrap in between.
The exposure follows visualsettings.dat's curve: about −3 stops by day, brighter at night, inside the range.
The filmic curve maps black to 0 and the white point to 1, with a shoulder.

Models came out too dark: GTA's material arithmetic (albedo and ambient
occlusion squared, no 1/pi) was being fed RedFury's physical sun and sky,
which are made for PBR shading. The light is now the game's own.

- The light at the viewer's time of day comes from the timecycle (clear
  weather, its global region, the same in both editions), interpolated
  between keyframes: the directional light's colour and multiplier, the
  natural ambient's down and up light with the down wrap, the directional
  ambient, and the vehicle and prop occlusion multipliers.

  the directional ambient, scaled by the natural ambient the deferred
  shaders store (one less the occlusion multiplier's share of what the
  vertex leaves out), squared.

  measured on open ground under that light, in the timecycle's range; the
  tone map is PS_Composite's filmic curve with visualsettings.dat's bright
  and dark settings.
- RedFury's sky tables, harmonics and atmosphere are gone; the day cycle
  only says where the sun or moon is. Its shadows stay.
