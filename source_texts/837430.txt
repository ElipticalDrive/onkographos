so i woke up this morning and had this idea of building a 3d renderer from scratch by making projectional mappings of a 3d equation onto a flat surface. i jotted down some math first. given a point (x, y, z), projecting it onto a flat plane at a given distance z is simple if you just strip out the z coordinate. doing orthogonal projection first made sense since i always start with the simplest sub problem, but it was way too easy and left us without perspective. so it would be a map (x,y,f(x,y)) -> (x,y)
so right then i moved on to the harder problem of adding perspective. i analyzed that when z is equal to zero, projected size should be infinite, and when z is at infinity, size should be zero. the simplest scaling was dividing x and y by z. so (x,y,z)-> (x/z,y/z) that felt almost too simple, so i explored other ideas like dividing x and y by e^(-z). with the exponential version, at infinity the size goes to zero, but at eye level when z = 0, it stays at regular size instead of blowing up to infinity. (x,y,z)->(xe^z, ye^z)
later in the afternoon i sat down to test this in desmos starting with a parametric ring. to avoid a crude static equation, i set up three angles, converted them to radians, and built x, y, z rotation matrices applied one after another to a parametric vector. initially i divided the returning vector by e^z, and projected the points as x/z and y/z.
before keeping perspective on, i verified it without perspective first, and the rotation projected perfectly. then i re-added perspective. it took a bit to figure out how to map pairwise elements from a list of matrices in desmos, which was fun since desmos only recently added matrix support.
testing both, the exponential model looked similar to the rational one at far distances, but it messed up parameters near the center of the plane and heavily distorted the geometry at low camera distances. division by z looked right, which makes sense geometrically since it comes from the similarity of triangles maintaining side ratios, is i found from a little sketching in my book. subtracting the z point vector from camera distance gave the actual projection, and i added a z scaling factor for depth perception along with a toggle to switch between perspective and orthographic modes. the only artifact was when z values went negative behind the camera, stretching back to infinity and flipping out front.
i tried rendering a proper 3d surface like z = sin(x) + sin(y), but desmos hit size limits computing cross products of two free parameters. to bypass that constraint, i made two sine waves, rotated one by 90 degrees to form a paired perpendicular wave grid, and passed that through rotation and perspective. it worked smoothly, leaving me with a working 3d renderer of a parametric equation in 2d desmos by evening.
i was later playing with it, trying to make other 3d shapes, i made an helix, a sphere, experimenting with trig parameters, and i tried making p and d orbitals but using the parametric equation i found for p and d ( i tried the dz^2), they were generating only one lobe, even though i used the abolute value. so i tried by making two equations with opposite sines and it works, but then there wasnt that torus i expected. i also realized i couldn't have made the helix so easily in the desmos's own 3d renderer which plots entire surfaces.
i realized this way of plotting points allowed it to have different usecases perhaps?
