Abstract
We introduce k-planes, a white-box model for radiance fields in arbitrary dimensions. Our model uses d choose 2 planes to represent a d-dimensional scene, providing a seamless way to go from static (d=3) to dynamic (d=4) scenes. This planar factorization makes adding dimension-specific priors easy, e.g. temporal smoothness and multi-resolution spatial structure, and induces a natural decomposition of static and dynamic components of a scene. We use a linear feature decoder with a learned color basis that yields similar performance as a nonlinear black-box MLP decoder. Across a range of synthetic and real, static and dynamic, fixed and varying appearance scenes, k-planes yields competitive and often state-of-the-art reconstruction fidelity with low memory usage, achieving 1000x compression over a full 4D grid, and fast optimization with a pure PyTorch implementation. Introduction
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have transformed 3D scene representation and view synthesis, but existing approaches face significant challenges when scaling to higher dimensions or handling complex real-world scenarios. The original NeRF model, while producing impressive results, suffers from slow training times, large memory requirements, and limited interpretability due to its reliance on multilayer perceptrons (MLPs).
K-Planes addresses these limitations by proposing an explicit radiance field representation that decomposes d-dimensional spaces into k=(d choose 2) two-dimensional planes. This approach enables efficient modeling of static scenes (3D), dynamic scenes (4D with time), and scenes with varying appearance conditions within a unified framework.
Figure: K-Planes method overview showing the planar factorization approach for 4D dynamic scenes. The method decomposes space-time into six planes: three space-only planes (xy, xz, yz) and three space-time planes (xt, yt, zt), which are combined through multiscale bilinear interpolation and Hadamard product to produce scene features.
Core Methodology
The fundamental innovation of K-Planes lies in its planar factorization strategy. For a d-dimensional space, the method represents the scene using k=(d choose 2) two-dimensional feature planes. Each plane captures interactions between a specific pair of dimensions:
3D Static Scenes: Uses 3 planes (tri-planes: Pxy, Pxz, Pyz)
4D Dynamic Scenes: Uses 6 planes (hex-planes: three space-only planes Pxy, Pxz, Pyz and three space-time planes Pxt, Pyt, Pzt)
For any point q in d-dimensional space, features are extracted by:
Projection: The point is projected onto each of the k planes
Multiscale Interpolation: Bilinear interpolation retrieves features from multiple resolution levels
Hadamard Product: Features from all planes are combined through elementwise multiplication
The final feature vector is computed as:

where S represents different scales and C represents the set of planes.
A crucial design choice is the use of the Hadamard product (elementwise multiplication) rather than addition for combining plane features. This multiplicative approach enables the model to produce spatially localized signals and significantly enhances expressivity, allowing even linear decoders to achieve competitive performance.
Technical Architecture
K-Planes offers two decoder variants:
Explicit Model (White-box): Uses a learned color basis approach where a small MLP maps view directions to RGB basis vectors. The final color is obtained through dot products between the combined feature vector and these basis vectors:
c(q,d)=f(q)⋅bRGB(d)c(q,d) = f(q) \cdot b_{RGB}(d)c(q,d)=f(q)⋅bRGB​(d)
Hybrid Model: Employs small MLPs to decode features into density and color, similar to traditional NeRF but with significantly reduced computational requirements due to the explicit plane representation.
The method incorporates several dimension-specific regularization techniques:
Total Variation in Space: L2 regularization encouraging spatial smoothness
Temporal Smoothness: 1D Laplacian regularization on time dimensions promoting smooth motion
Sparse Transients: ℓ1 regularization encouraging space-time planes to deviate from identity only when necessary
Experimental Results and Performance
K-Planes demonstrates competitive performance across multiple challenging scenarios:
Static Scenes: On NeRF synthetic datasets, both explicit and hybrid versions achieve PSNR values comparable to state-of-the-art methods like TensoRF and Instant-NGP while significantly outperforming earlier approaches.
Dynamic Scenes: The method shows substantial improvements on D-NeRF synthetic data, with the hybrid version achieving superior PSNR and SSIM compared to the original D-NeRF. On real-world DyNeRF datasets, K-Planes produces high-quality video reconstructions competitive with recent specialized methods.
Variable Appearance: K-Planes successfully handles challenging phototourism datasets with varying lighting conditions, representing the first fully explicit method to tackle such unconstrained scenes effectively.
Efficiency Metrics: The approach achieves remarkable compression ratios (up to 1000x for 4D volumes) while maintaining fast training times (hours vs. days/weeks for some baselines) on single GPUs without custom CUDA kernels.
Interpretability and Analysis
A key advantage of K-Planes is its interpretable structure. The natural decomposition into space-only and space-time planes allows direct visualization of static versus dynamic scene components. The space-time planes clearly highlight regions of motion in dynamic videos, while the multiplicative combination enables localized feature representation.
The method's "white-box" nature facilitates:
Easy debugging and understanding of model behavior
Integration of domain-specific priors and regularization
Potential for advanced scene editing and manipulation
Disentanglement of appearance from geometry through global appearance codes
Significance and Impact
K-Planes represents a significant step toward making neural rendering more accessible and interpretable. By achieving state-of-the-art results with dramatically reduced computational requirements and providing a unified framework for diverse scene types, it democratizes advanced 3D/4D reconstruction capabilities.
The method's explicit, interpretable structure opens new possibilities for scene understanding, content creation, and real-time applications. Its generalizability to arbitrary dimensions provides a robust foundation for future research in neural scene representation, while its efficiency makes it practical for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
The work successfully challenges the assumption that implicit MLP-based models are inherently more expressive, demonstrating that explicit, mathematically interpretable approaches can achieve comparable or superior performance while offering greater transparency and control.
Tensorf: Tensorial radiance fields
This work is a direct and highly relevant precursor to K-planes for static scenes, introducing the idea of using tensor decomposition (specifically planes and vectors) to create a compact and efficient radiance field. K-planes builds upon this by proposing a more generalized and principled planar factorization that extends naturally to higher dimensions for representing dynamic scenes.
Anpei Chen, Zexiang Xu, Andreas Geiger, Jingyi Yu, and Hao Su. Tensorf: Tensorial radiance fields. In ECCV, 2022.
Nerf: Representing scenes as neural radiance fields for view synthesis
This is the foundational paper that introduced Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), establishing the problem of novel view synthesis using an implicit neural representation. K-planes positions itself as an alternative to the original NeRF, aiming to overcome its slow training time and black-box nature with a fast, explicit, and interpretable planar model.
Ben Mildenhall, Pratul P Srinivasan, Matthew Tancik, Jonathan T Barron, Ravi Ramamoorthi, and Ren Ng. Nerf: Representing scenes as neural radiance fields for view synthesis. In ECCV, pages 405–421. Springer, 2020.
Plenoxels: Radiance fields without neural networks
This paper is a key inspiration for K-planes, as it demonstrated the viability of high-quality, fully explicit radiance fields that train rapidly without relying on MLPs. K-planes adopts this 'explicit' philosophy but replaces the memory-intensive voxel grid of Plenoxels with a more compact and scalable planar decomposition, making it tractable for higher-dimensional data like videos.
Sara Fridovich-Keil, Alex Yu, Matthew Tancik, Qinhong Chen, Benjamin Recht, and Angjoo Kanazawa. Plenoxels: Radiance fields without neural networks. In CVPR, pages 5491–5500. IEEE, 2022.
Neural 3d video synthesis from multi-view video
This paper, which introduced the DyNeRF dataset, serves as a primary benchmark for the dynamic scene (4D) capabilities of K-planes. The K-planes paper directly compares its performance, and especially its significantly faster training time, against DyNeRF to showcase its efficiency and effectiveness in representing space-time volumes.
Tianye Li, Mira Slavcheva, Michael Zollhoefer, Simon Green, Christoph Lassner, Changil Kim, Tanner Schmidt, Steven Lovegrove, Michael Goesele, Richard Newcombe, and Zhaoyang Lv. Neural 3d video synthesis from multi-view video. In CVPR, pages 5511–5521, 2022.
NeRF in the Wild: Neural Radiance Fields for Unconstrained Photo Collections
This paper is essential for contextualizing the variable appearance aspect of K-planes. NeRF-W introduced methods to handle scenes with varying lighting and transient objects, and K-planes demonstrates its own versatility by adopting a similar per-image appearance code to successfully reconstruct these challenging unconstrained photo collections.
Ricardo Martin-Brualla, Noha Radwan, Mehdi S. M. Sajjadi, Jonathan T. Barron, Alexey Dosovitskiy, and Daniel Duckworth. NeRF in the Wild: Neural Radiance Fields for Unconstrained Photo Collections. In CVPR, 2021.
