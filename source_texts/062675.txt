## Abstract

Language 3D Gaussian Splatting enables open-vocabulary scene understanding by associating Gaussian primitives with language features. These semantic attributes increase model storage, while most existing 3DGS compression methods are designed mainly to preserve RGB rendering quality. One straightforward approach is to apply sparsification and quantization sequentially. However, pruning based only on rendering quality can remove Gaussians that have small RGB contributions but are important for semantic prediction. Once removed, their semantic information cannot be recovered by later quantization. We propose CLUNOGS*, a compact framework for Language 3DGS that combines semantics-aware Gaussian sparsification and vector quantization in a unified constrained ob-*

jective. Semantic supervision is used during sparsification, and the objective jointly considers rendering quality, semantic accuracy, and compactness. We optimize the unified objective with alternating primal, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates. We evaluate CLUNOGS on open-vocabulary localization and segmentation using the LERF and ScanNet-10 benchmarks. Under aggressive compression, our unified, semantics-aware formulation substantially alleviates the degradation of semantic representations caused by sequential compression and semanticsagnostic Gaussian sparsification. Ablations further show that jointly coordinating sparsification and quantization better preserves semantic accuracy than applying them independently.

## 1. Introduction

Open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding enables objects in a 3D scene to be localized or segmented using arbitrary natural-language queries, without restricting predictions to a predefined class set [28]. This capability supports applications such as AR/VR [19], autonomous driving [8], and robotic manipulation [14, 31]. Earlier neural-field methods encoded semantics in implicit representations, but repeated volumetric sampling and network evaluation made training and rendering costly [17, 36]. Language 3DGS instead attaches vision-language features to explicit Gaussian primitives and reuses the tile-based splatting pipeline of 3DGS to render semantic feature maps efficiently, avoiding repeated volumetric sampling and per-sample network evaluation [16, 30, 41].

Directly attaching high-dimensional vision-language features to individual Gaussians incurs substantial storage and rendering overhead [30, 41]. LangSplat mitigates this problem using a scene-specific low-dimensional autoencoder, while LangSplatV2 further replaces the heavyweight decoder with sparse per-Gaussian coefficients over shared dictionaries, enabling efficient high-dimensional feature splatting [22, 30]. These advances reduce the cost of semantic feature representation and inference, but do not target compression of the underlying RGB Gaussian scene. Consequently, a complete Language 3DGS model still contains the original 3DGS attributes together with additional semantic parameters. Its compression must therefore preserve both photometric fidelity and semantic responses.

Standard 3DGS compression often combines sparsification or pruning [1, 12, 13, 38, 40] with quantization [21, 24, 25]. Applying them sequentially decouples semantic learning from Gaussian selection, so RGB-driven sparsification can discard semantically important Gaussians that subsequent stages cannot recover. Figure 1 illustrates this failure using LangSplatV2 [22], GaussianSpa, and CompGS: semantic representations degrade whether semantic learning occurs before or after RGB-driven Gaussian sparsification. Our unified formulation instead coordinates semantic learning, sparsification, and quantization throughout optimization.

To address this gap, we propose CLUNOGS (Compact Language via Unified Optimization for 3D Gaussian Splatting). The framework combines RGB reconstruction and semantic supervision with a cardinality constraint on Gaussian opacities and vector quantization (VQ) of selected attributes. Updating the scene parameters, sparse support, and quantized representations within the same constrained problem allows them to adapt to one another instead of inheriting irreversible decisions from a preceding stage. We solve the resulting non-smooth problem with an alternating primal–dual procedure.

Our contributions are:

- • We introduce a unified constrained formulation that couples an RGB–semantic task objective with Gaussian sparsification and attribute vector quantization for compact Language 3DGS.
- • We develop a compact Language 3DGS optimization framework and solve the unified problem using an alternating primal–dual procedure that coordinates differentiable scene optimization, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates.
- • We evaluate CLUNOGS on LERF and ScanNet-10 using recent language-field baselines, sequential compression and ordering controls, qualitative comparisons, and controlled ablations. Across the two benchmarks, CLUNOGS reduces serialized storage by 80.9–82.8% relative to the corresponding uncompressed models, with losses of at most 1.6 mIoU points, 1.1 accuracy points, and 0.07 dB PSNR.

## 2. Related Work

Language 3DGS. Language-aware 3D representations lift 2D foundation-model features into queryable 3D fields, with LERF providing an early NeRF-based formulation [17]. Language 3DGS methods mainly follow two paradigms. Render-supervised methods learn per-Gaussian features or codes by matching rendered feature maps to 2D supervision, as in LangSplat, Feature 3DGS, GAGS, and LangSplatV2 [22, 29, 30, 41]. Direct-lifting methods instead register or aggregate multi-view features onto reconstructed Gaussians without iterative semantic fitting, as in Dr. Splat and Occam’s LGS [7, 18]. LightSplat and Extrin-Splat further replace dense per-Gaussian embeddings with compact semantic indices or an external object-group index [2, 11]. We build on LangSplatV2 because its semantic coefficients and RGB attributes share the same Gaussian support, allowing sparsification and attribute quantization to account for both rendering and semantic quality.

Compact Language 3DGS. Recent work has explored compact representations for the semantic features in Language 3DGS. DF-3DGS separates RGB and semantic Gaussians and sparsifies the semantic field, while CF3 compresses feature Gaussians through pruning, merging, and autoencoding [10, 20]. CSGaussian progressively entropy-codes both color and semantic attributes [34]. SCOUP represents semantic features with sparse codes, whereas CoSAG predicts and entropy-codes compact semantic bindings for individual Gaussians [4, 15]. These methods reduce semantic storage through specialized feature representations and coding schemes.

General 3DGS Compression. RGB-only 3DGS compression reduces active primitives, attribute storage, or bit-

stream size. GaussianSpa and PUP 3D-GS sparsify optimized scenes using constrained or uncertainty-based criteria [13, 40]. Compact 3DGS, C3DGS, and CompGS use clustering or quantization to reduce attribute storage [21, 25, 26]. RDO-Gaussian jointly optimizes masks and entropy-constrained attributes, while HAC++ and LocoGS model context or local coherence for compact coding [6, 32, 35]. FCGS applies a feed-forward codec to an optimized Gaussian scene [5]. Joint sparsification and quantization are therefore established in RGB 3DGS. The concurrent GETA-3DGS preprint also studies joint structured pruning and heterogeneous quantization [39]. CLUNOGS instead optimizes opacity with both RGB and semantic supervision before cardinality projection, and coordinates Gaussian sparsification with attribute vector quantization in the same constrained formulation.

### 3. Background

3D Gaussian Splatting uses a collection of Gaussians to model 3D scene geometry and appearance. Specifically, each Gaussian G(x) is defined by a mean vector µ ∈ R 3 and a covariance matrix Σ:

$$G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \right), \quad (1)$$

where x is a location in the 3D scene. The learnable parameters of a Gaussian are {µ, c, o, R,S}, which correspond to its position, color, opacity, rotation, and scale.

To optimize the Gaussian parameters, they are projected onto 2D image planes, and tile-based rasterization [42] is used for efficiency. The pixel color Cpixel is rendered by:



|                                                                                                                                   | $i=1$ |  |  |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------|--|--|
| $C_{\text{pixel}} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j), \quad \alpha_i = o_i G'_i, \quad (2)$ |       |  |  |

where ci is the color of the i-th Gaussian, oi is its opacity, N is the set of ordered Gaussians contributing to the rasterization at the target rendering pixel, and G′ i represents the projection of the Gaussian onto the 2D plane.

LangSplatV2 [22] attaches sparse semantic coefficients to each Gaussian. For semantic level h ∈ {1, 2, 3}, let w (h) i ∈ R 1×L denote the coefficient vector of Gaussian i, and let B(h) ∈ R L×D denote the scene-level semantic dictionary shared by all Gaussians at that level. Only the top-K entries of w (h) i are nonzero, and these entries are normalized to sum to one. The high-dimensional feature is reconstructed after coefficient rendering and is not stored as a separate per-Gaussian vector.

The sparse coefficients can be rendered before feature reconstruction. For pixel p, let αi,p = oiG′ i (p) and ei,p = αi,p Q j− αj,p). The rendered semantic feature is


$$\mathbf{F}_p^{(h)} = \left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}_p} e_{i,p} \mathbf{w}_i^{(h)} \right) \mathbf{B}^{(h)}. \quad (3)$$

This rendering shares the geometry and opacity-dependent compositing weights of RGB rendering. The rendered features are compared with text queries to produce per-pixel relevance maps for localization or segmentation under the corresponding benchmark protocol [17].

### 4. Methodology: CLUNOGS

To compact a Language 3DGS model while preserving semantic and RGB quality, we propose a unified compression framework, CLUNOGS. Our method places semanticsaware Gaussian sparsification and vector quantization in a single constrained objective. We solve it with a primal–dual procedure that alternates among differentiable scene optimization, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates.

#### 4.1. Problem Formulation

Language-Embedded Training Objective. In standard 3DGS training [16], the Gaussian parameters are learned using a combination of pixelwise ℓ1 loss and differentiable SSIM loss between rendered RGB images and groundtruth multi-view images. The rendering loss is LRGB = (1 − λ)L1 + λLD-SSIM, where λ ∈ [0, 1] balances the two terms.

For semantic supervision, we follow LangSplatV2. As introduced in the previous section, each Gaussian is associated with sparse semantic coefficients over scene-level dictionaries, and the high-dimensional semantic features are reconstructed after coefficient rendering. We match them to the SAM-derived CLIP targets F¯ (h) p at all three semantic levels using pixelwise cosine distance:

$$F_{\text{lang}} = \frac{1}{3|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{h=1}^3 \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \left( 1 - \frac{\langle \mathbf{F}_p^{(h)}, \bar{\mathbf{F}}_p^{(h)} \rangle}{\|\mathbf{F}_p^{(h)}\|_2 \|\bar{\mathbf{F}}_p^{(h)}\|_2} \right), \quad (4)$$

where P is the set of image pixels. The RGB and language objectives are combined as

$$L = L_{\text{RGB}} + \gamma L_{\text{lang}}, \quad (5)$$

where γ denotes the weighting factor that balances the rendering and language losses. Since RGB and semantic rendering share the same opacity-dependent compositing weights, semantic supervision can also affect the Gaussian opacities and thereby influence which Gaussians are retained during sparsification.



Figure 2. Overview of the CLUNOGS framework, illustrating the four updates performed in each iteration of the optimization loop. The diagram suppresses the language-loss weight and attribute-group indices.

Unified Objective with Sparsity and Vector Quantization. From Eqs. 2 and 3, Gaussian opacity directly affects both RGB and semantic compositing. We therefore impose sparsity on opacity to reduce the number of active Gaussian primitives and use vector quantization to reduce the storage of the remaining continuous per-Gaussian attributes.

Given a 3DGS model with N initial Gaussians, we represent their opacities as o = [o1, o2, . . . , oN ] ∈ R N , where oi is the opacity of the i-th Gaussian. We use Θ = {θ1, θ2, . . . , θN } to denote the continuous per-Gaussian attributes subject to vector quantization. In our implementation, these include color coefficients, higher-order spherical harmonic coefficients, scale, rotation, and semantic coefficient values. The 3D positions and scene-level semantic dictionaries remain in full precision, while the discrete top-K semantic atom indices are stored separately.

Let Q = {(Qj , qj )}M j=1 denote the quantization state, where Qj is a cluster and qj is its centroid. The centroids form the quantization codebook. For concise notation, we suppress attribute-group indices below; in implementation, vector quantization is applied separately to different attribute groups using independent codebooks.

With the clusters and codebook defined, the unified optimization objective is given by


$$\min_{o, \Theta, \mathcal{Q}} L(o, \Theta) + \sum_{j=1}^M \sum_{\theta_i \in Q_j} \|\theta_i - q_j\|_2^2, \quad (6)$$
s.t.  $\text{card}(o) \leq \kappa.$ 

The sparsity constraint card(o) ≤ κ limits the number of Gaussians with nonzero opacity to at most κ. Here, card(o) is the number of nonzero elements in o. Importantly, the opacity parameters are optimized under both RGB and semantic supervision before the sparsity constraint is enforced. The retained Gaussians therefore depend on their contribution to the joint RGB–semantic objective rather than on RGB reconstruction alone. The same decision is applied to all point-aligned attributes of each Gaussian.

The quantization penalty PM j=1 P θi∈Qj ∥θi − qj∥ 2 2 penalizes deviations of each parameter vector θi from its assigned cluster centroid qj . This encourages the continuous attributes to approach a small set of representative values. After training, each vector can be approximated by its cluster centroid, requiring only its codeword index and the corresponding codebook to be stored.

#### 4.2. Optimization

Figure 2 provides an overview of our four-step primal– dual optimization. The unified objective in Eq. 6 contains two non-differentiable components: the sparsity constraint card(o) ≤ κ and the discrete assignments in vector quantization. We introduce auxiliary variables to separate differentiable scene optimization from sparsification and quantization.

We first introduce an auxiliary variable y to separate the sparsity constraint from o, and use Θˆ = {θˆ1, . . . , θˆN } as the quantized copy of Θ. We define g(·) as the indicator function of the sparsity constraint: g(y) = 0 when card(y) ≤ κ and g(y) = +∞ otherwise. Hence, Eq. 6 can be reformulated as


$$\min_{o, \Theta, y, \hat{\Theta}, \boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}} L(o, \Theta) + g(y) + \sum_{j=1}^M \sum_{\hat{\theta}_i \in Q_j} \|\hat{\theta}_i - q_j\|_2^2, \quad (7)$$

$$\text{s.t. } o = y, \Theta = \hat{\Theta}.$$

This reformulation allows the differentiable variables o and Θ to be optimized using gradient-based methods, while the sparsity constraint and discrete vector-quantization assignments are handled through the corresponding auxiliary variables. The resulting alternating procedure is summarized in Alg. 1, and its four updates are derived below.

#### Algorithm 1 Unified Optimization for CLUNOGS

Input: Language 3DGS variables Θ, opacity o, Gaussian budget κ, number of clusters M, penalty coefficients ρ1, ρ2, learning rates η1, η2, maximum iterations T, and convergence thresholds ϵ1, ϵ2.

Output: Retained Gaussians, quantized parameters Θˆ , and quantization state Q.

1: Initialize Q by k-means on Θ; 2: y ←o, Θˆ ←ΠQ(Θ), u ←0, V ←0; 3: ro, rΘ ←∞, t ←0; 4: while (ro > ϵ1 or rΘ > ϵ2) and t < T do 5: Update o, Θ with Eq. 12; ▷ Primal Update 6: Update y with Eq. 13; ▷ Sparsification 7: Update Q, Θˆ with Eqs. 14–15; ▷ Vector Quant. 8: Update u,V with Eqs. 16–17; ▷ Dual Update 9: ro ←∥o t+1 − y t+1∥2, rΘ ←∥Θt+1 − Θˆ t+1∥2; 10: t ←t + 1; 11: end while

We solve the equality-constrained problem in Eq. 7 by constructing an augmented Lagrangian with scaled Lagrange multipliers u,V and penalty coefficients ρ1, ρ2:

$$L_\rho(o, \Theta, y, \hat{\Theta}, u, V, \mathcal{Q}) = L(o, \Theta) + g(y) + \sum_{j=1}^M \sum_{\hat{\theta}_i \in Q_j} \|\hat{\theta}_i - q_j\|_2^2 \quad (8)$$

$$+ \frac{\rho_1}{2} \|o - y + u\|_2^2 + \frac{\rho_2}{2} \|\Theta - \hat{\Theta} + V\|_2^2.$$

The quadratic penalties enforce the equality constraints.

1 Primal Update. At iteration t, only the model parameters o and Θ are updated; all other variables remain fixed. This gives the differentiable subproblem:


$$(o^{t+1}, \Theta^{t+1}) = \arg \min_{o, \Theta} L(o, \Theta) + \frac{\rho_1}{2} \|o - y^t + u^t\|_2^2 + \frac{\rho_2}{2} \|\Theta - \hat{\Theta}^t + V^t\|_2^2. \quad (9)$$

Here, L(o, Θ) is the differentiable RGB–semantic training objective, while the two quadratic terms couple the continuous parameters to their sparse and quantized counterparts. Consequently, Eq. 9 can be optimized using standard gradient-based optimizers. The gradients for o and Θ at iteration t are

$$\left(\frac{\partial L_\rho}{\partial o}\right)^t = \frac{\partial L(\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{\Theta}^t)}{\partial o} + \rho_1(\mathbf{o}^t - \mathbf{y}^t + \mathbf{u}^t), \quad (10)$$

$$\left(\frac{\partial L_\rho}{\partial \Theta}\right)^t = \frac{\partial L(\boldsymbol{o}^t, \boldsymbol{\Theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\Theta}} + \rho_2(\boldsymbol{\Theta}^t - \hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}^t + \mathbf{V}^t). \quad (11)$$

The parameters are then updated as


$$\boldsymbol{o}^{t+1} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{o}^t - \eta_1 \left( \frac{\partial L_{\rho}}{\partial \boldsymbol{o}} \right)^t, \quad \boldsymbol{\Theta}^{t+1} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\Theta}^t - \eta_2 \left( \frac{\partial L_{\rho}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\Theta}} \right), \quad (12)$$

where η1 > 0 and η2 > 0 are the learning rates for o and Θ, respectively.

Since L(o, Θ) contains both LRGB and Llang, the language loss contributes directly to the opacity update. Thus, before the following sparsification step selects the retained Gaussians, their opacities have already been adapted using both RGB and semantic supervision. The 3D positions and scene-level semantic dictionaries are optimized by the task objective but receive no quantization penalty.

2 Sparsification Update. In this step, the auxiliary variable y is updated by solving miny g(y) + ρ1 2 ∥o t+1 − y + u t∥ 2 2 . The closed-form solution [3, 27] is


$$y^{t+1} \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{S}}(o^{t+1} + u^t), \quad (13)$$

where ΠS denotes projection onto the sparsity-constrained set. When S = {y | card(y) ≤ κ}, the projection retains the κ entries with the largest magnitudes and sets all others to zero.

Because of the quadratic penalty term ρ1 ∥o t+1 − y + u t∥ 2 2 , the projection is applied to o t+1+u t rather than opacity alone. The scaled dual variable u accumulates the discrepancy between o and y across iterations. The nonzero entries of y t+1 identify the retained Gaussians, and the same selection is applied to their point-aligned RGB and semantic attributes.

3 Vector Quantization Update. In the third step, we update the quantization state Q and the quantized Gaussian parameters Θˆ . In implementation, this operation is applied independently to different attribute groups using separate codebooks; we suppress the group index for clarity.

The clusters and centroids in Q are updated using standard k-means. Each centroid is first computed as the mean of its assigned parameter vectors, after which each vector is reassigned to its nearest centroid. Formally, for j = 1, . . . , M:


$$\begin{cases} \mathbf{q}_j^{t+1} = \frac{1}{|Q_j^t|} \sum \boldsymbol{\theta}_{i^{t+1} \in Q_j^t}^t \boldsymbol{\theta}_i^{t+1}, \\ \mathbf{Q}_j^{t+1} = \left\{ \boldsymbol{\theta}_i^{t+1} \mid \arg \min_{k=1, \dots, M} \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_i^{t+1} - \mathbf{q}_k^{t+1}\|^2 = j \right\}. \end{cases} \quad (14)$$

We then project the dual-corrected Gaussian parameter vectors onto the updated centroids:


$$\hat{\Theta}^{t+1} \leftarrow \Pi_{Q^{t+1}}(\Theta^{t+1} + V^t), \quad (15)$$

where ΠQ replaces each parameter vector with its nearest cluster centroid.

Due to the quadratic penalty term  $\frac{\rho_2}{2} \|\Theta^{t+1} - \hat{\Theta} + \mathbf{V}^t\|_2^2$ , the projection is applied to  $\Theta^{t+1} + \mathbf{V}^t$ . The scaled dual variable  $\mathbf{V}$  tracks the discrepancy between  $\Theta$  and  $\hat{\Theta}$  across iterations and carries this residual into subsequent quantization updates.

4 Dual Update. In the final step of iteration  $t$ , the scaled dual variables  $\mathbf{u}$  and  $\mathbf{V}$  are updated as

$$\mathbf{u}^{t+1} \leftarrow \mathbf{u}^t + \mathbf{o}^{t+1} - \mathbf{y}^{t+1}, \quad (16)$$

$$\mathbf{V}^{t+1} \leftarrow \mathbf{V}^t + \Theta^{t+1} - \hat{\Theta}^{t+1}. \quad (17)$$

The update of  $\mathbf{u}$  accumulates the residual between continuous opacity and its sparse counterpart, while  $\mathbf{V}$  performs the analogous correction for the continuous and quantized attributes. These corrections enter the sparsification and quantization operations of the next iteration.

The four updates alternate, allowing the trainable scene parameters, retained Gaussian set, and quantized attributes to adapt under the same RGB-semantic objective. We repeat this procedure until the residual thresholds are met or the fixed iteration budget is exhausted. The nonzero entries of the final  $\mathbf{y}$  determine the retained Gaussians, while  $\hat{\Theta}$  and the corresponding codebooks define their quantized attributes. A convergence analysis of the resulting primal-dual scheme is provided in Sec. B of the supplementary material.

## 5. Experiments

### 5.1. Settings

Benchmarks, metrics, and baselines. We evaluate on four LERF scenes [17] and ScanNet-10 [9], reporting semantic accuracy, RGB PSNR, storage, and peak memory. Language baselines cover direct-lifting methods (Occam’s LGS and Dr. Splat [7, 18]) and render-supervised semantic fields (LangSplatV2, CCL-LGS, GAGS, and CF3 [20, 22, 29, 33]). Compression baselines pair three Gaussian sparsifiers (opacity Top- $K$ , PUP 3D-GS, and GaussianSpa) with CompGS or C3DGS for attribute quantization [13, 25, 26, 40].

On LERF, all query relevance maps use the same post-processing and a fixed 0.4 threshold. mIoU averages query-mask IoU, while localization Acc. checks whether the peak response lies inside a ground-truth box. On ScanNet-10, we follow OpenGaussian’s label protocol and the ten-scene split used by LUDVIG [23, 37]. Each method uses its native decoder and scale-selection rule to render ten class-score maps. We bilinearly sample these scores at projected ScanNet points when the projected and sensor depths differ by at most 5 cm, average them over valid views, and take the pointwise argmax. Evaluation includes points observed in at least one view after applying the same 0.1 activated-opacity threshold to each RGB 3DGS. Per-scene mIoU and

mAcc average class IoU and recall over the ground-truth classes present in that scene, followed by an unweighted average over the ten scenes.

Implementation details. Each scene starts from a 3DGS model [16] trained for 30k iterations. We store all three LangSplatV2 semantic levels in one Gaussian model and optimize it for 30k iterations; the released implementation instead trains three separate level-specific models for 10k iterations each [22]. The first 20k iterations follow the original language-field training, while the final 10k activate our unified sparsification-quantization updates. All rate-matched compressed variants use the same 20% Gaussian-retention budget. Detailed schedules are provided in the supplement. Storage includes all files required for inference. Dr. Splat therefore includes its PQ index, whereas LangSplatV2 includes the semantic coefficients and dictionaries for all three levels together with one shared RGB 3DGS. Peak memory is measured from a fresh process through one native-resolution text-query rendering.

### 5.2. Overall Performance

Table 1 evaluates whether aggressive compression can preserve both semantic prediction accuracy and RGB rendering quality. Across LERF and ScanNet-10, we compare deployable systems from two Language 3DGS paradigms: direct-lifting methods and render-supervised semantic fields. Semantic metrics and PSNR measure quality, while storage and peak memory measure deployment cost.

Compared with CF3, the smallest competitor on both benchmarks, CLUNOGS achieves over 5× storage reduction while also attaining the lowest peak memory. It nevertheless ranks second in every semantic and RGB quality metric; its maximum gaps to LangSplatV2 are only 1.6 mIoU points, 1.1 accuracy points, and 0.07 dB PSNR. The same trend on both datasets shows that this advantage is not benchmark-specific.

LangSplatV2 remains the quality leader because its full model retains detailed semantic coefficients and dictionaries at all three scales. While direct-lifting methods are designed for efficient querying, both Occam’s LGS and Dr. Splat trail this render-supervised model in semantic quality. Occam’s LGS is also storage-intensive on both datasets and incurs the highest peak memory on LERF.

### B. Convergence Analysis and Proof

### B.1. Preliminaries

Constrained Objective. Our optimization seeks to minimize an objective with sparsity and vector quantization. By introducing auxiliary variables y and Θˆ to decouple the non-differentiable terms, we reformulate the problem into an equality-constrained form:


$$\min_{o, \Theta, y, \hat{\Theta}, \mathcal{Q}} L(o, \Theta) + g(y) + \sum_{j=1}^M \sum_{\hat{\theta}_i \in Q_j} \|\hat{\theta}_i - q_j\|_2^2, \quad (\text{S1})$$

$$\text{s.t. } o = y, \Theta = \hat{\Theta}.$$

Here, Q contains both the cluster assignments and their centroids and is optimized jointly with the other variables; it is not held fixed in the analysis below.

Augmented Lagrangian. To solve Eq. S1, we incorporate the constraints through the augmented Lagrangian with scaled multipliers u,V and penalty coefficients ρ1, ρ2:


$$L_\rho(o, \Theta, y, \hat{\Theta}, u, V, \mathcal{Q}) = L(o, \Theta) + g(y) + \sum_{j=1}^M \sum_{\hat{\theta}_i \in Q_j} \|\hat{\theta}_i - q_j\|_2^2 + \frac{\rho_1}{2} \|o - y + u\|_2^2 + \frac{\rho_2}{2} \|\Theta - \hat{\Theta} + V\|_2^2. \quad (S2)$$

Primal Updates. At iteration t, o t and Θt are updated by gradient descent on the augmented Lagrangian:


$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{o}^{t+1} &\leftarrow \mathbf{o}^t - \eta_1 \left( \frac{\partial L_\rho}{\partial \mathbf{o}} \right)^t, \\ \mathbf{\Theta}^{t+1} &\leftarrow \mathbf{\Theta}^t - \eta_2 \left( \frac{\partial L_\rho}{\partial \mathbf{\Theta}} \right)^t, \end{aligned} \tag{S3}$$

where η1, η2 are learning rates.

Sparsification Update. The auxiliary variable y admits a closed-form update through the sparsity operator ΠS:


$$y^{t+1} \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{S}}(o^{t+1} + u^t). \quad (\text{S4})$$

Concretely, ΠS keeps the κ entries of (o t+1 + u t ) with the largest magnitudes and zeros the rest.

Quantization Update. For the quantization block, Q is explicitly updated by the two Lloyd steps used by standard k-means. For j = 1, . . . , M,


$$q_j^{t+1} = \frac{1}{|Q_j^t|} \sum_{\theta_i^{t+1} \in Q_j^t} \theta_i^{t+1}, \quad (\text{S5})$$

$$Q_j^{t+1} = \left\{ \theta_i^{t+1} : j \in \arg \min_k \|\theta_i^{t+1} - q_k^{t+1}\|_2^2 \right\}.$$

Ties are resolved deterministically. The quantized parameters are then obtained by projecting the dual-corrected model parameters onto the updated centroids:


$$\hat{\Theta}^{t+1} \leftarrow \Pi_{Q^{t+1}}(\Theta^{t+1} + \mathbf{V}^t), \quad (\text{S6})$$

where ΠQ maps each vector to its assigned centroid.

#### B.2. Analysis and Proof

This section analyzes the proposed optimization scheme. We show that its Lyapunov sequence converges and that every limit point satisfies the continuous first-order conditions together with the projection and codebook fixed-point conditions of Eq. S1. The proof retains the standard sufficientdecrease structure for non-convex, non-smooth primal–dual optimization.

Theoretical Assumptions. We use the following conditions:

- 1. Properties of the Loss Function. The loss L(o, Θ) is continuously differentiable and L-smooth in (o, Θ), i.e., for some L > 0,

$$\begin{aligned} & \|\nabla L(\mathbf{o}_1, \mathbf{\Theta}_1) - \nabla L(\mathbf{o}_2, \mathbf{\Theta}_2)\| \\ & \leq L \|(\mathbf{o}_1, \mathbf{\Theta}_1) - (\mathbf{o}_2, \mathbf{\Theta}_2)\|. \end{aligned}$$

- 2. Lower Boundedness. The objective function, including the non-smooth terms, is bounded below by 0.
- 3. Penalty and Step Size. The penalty parameters ρ1, ρ2 > 0 and the learning rates η1, η2 > 0 satisfy

$$\eta_1 < \frac{2}{L + \rho_1}, \quad \eta_2 < \frac{2}{L + \rho_2}.$$

- 4. Limit Point Existence. The sequence generated by our optimization scheme, {(ot, Θt, yt, Θˆ t,ut,Vt, Qt)}t∈N, has at least one limit point.
- 5. Quantization-Block Descent. The k-means block has no empty clusters, uses deterministic tie breaking, and performs Lloyd updates until a coordinate-wise fixed point. Each centroid and assignment step therefore does not increase its within-cluster squared error. The resulting combined update of (Q, Θˆ ) does not increase the corresponding augmented block objective.
- 6. Dual-residual Control. For the inexact primal updates, the dual-step contribution Et defined below satisfies

$$\mathcal{E}_t \leq b_0 \|\Delta \boldsymbol{o}_t\|_2^2 + b_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}} \|\Delta \boldsymbol{\Theta}_t\|_2^2 - \sum_{i=1}^2 d_i \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1}\|_2^2,$$

for di > 0, 0 ≤ bo < co, and 0 ≤ bΘ < cΘ. This is the relative-error condition required to keep the inexact primal and dual steps within the descent supplied by Lemma 1.

Augmented Lagrangian and Lyapunov Function. For the augmented Lagrangian in Eq. S2, we use the following residual-augmented Lyapunov function:

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi_t = & L_\rho(\mathbf{o}_t, \Theta_t, \mathbf{y}_t, \hat{\Theta}_t, \mathbf{u}_t, \mathbf{V}_t, \mathbf{Q}_t) \\ & + \frac{\gamma_1}{2} \|\mathbf{o}_t - \mathbf{y}_t\|_2^2 + \frac{\gamma_2}{2} \|\Theta_t - \hat{\Theta}_t\|_2^2, \end{aligned} \quad (S7)$$

where 0 < γi < ρi . This definition makes explicit the residual terms used in the descent accounting below. For brevity, we introduce the notation:

$$\begin{aligned}\Delta o_t &= o_{t+1} - o_t, \\ \Delta \Theta_t &= \Theta_{t+1} - \Theta_t, \\ r_o^{t+1} &= o_{t+1} - y_{t+1}, \\ r_\Theta^{t+1} &= \Theta_{t+1} - \hat{\Theta}_{t+1}.\end{aligned}\tag{S8}$$

The dual-step contribution in Assumption 6 is


$$\mathcal{E}_t = \sum_{i=1}^2 \frac{\rho_i}{2} (2\langle \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{u}_i^t, \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} \rangle + \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1}\|_2^2) \quad (\text{S9})$$

$$+ \sum_{i=1}^2 \frac{\gamma_i}{2} (\|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1}\|_2^2 - \|\mathbf{r}_i^t\|_2^2).$$

Lemma 1 (Model-parameter update yields sufficient decrease). Let ot+1 and Θt+1 be updated as in Eq. S3 under Assumptions 1 and 3. Then:


$$\begin{aligned} & L_\rho(o_{t+1}, \Theta_{t+1}, y_t, \hat{\Theta}_t, u_t, V_t, \mathcal{Q}_t) \\ & \leq L_\rho(o_t, \Theta_t, y_t, \hat{\Theta}_t, u_t, V_t, \mathcal{Q}_t) \\ & \quad - c_o \|\Delta o_t\|_2^2 - c_\Theta \|\Delta \Theta_t\|_2^2, \end{aligned} \tag{S10}$$

with explicit positive constants


$$c_o = \frac{1}{\eta_1} - \frac{L}{2} - \frac{\rho_1}{2} > 0, \quad (S11)$$

$$c_\Theta = \frac{1}{\eta_2} - \frac{L}{2} - \frac{\rho_2}{2} > 0.$$

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta L_\rho &\leq \langle \nabla_{\mathbf{o}} L_\rho, \Delta \mathbf{o}_t \rangle + \frac{L + \rho_1}{2} \|\Delta \mathbf{o}_t\|_2^2 \\ &\quad + \langle \nabla_{\mathbf{\Theta}} L_\rho, \Delta \mathbf{\Theta}_t \rangle + \frac{L + \rho_2}{2} \|\Delta \mathbf{\Theta}_t\|_2^2. \end{aligned} \quad (S12)$$

where both gradients are evaluated at iteration t. Substituting the two gradient steps from Eq. S3 yields Eq. S10 with the constants in Eq. S11.

Lemma 3 (Dual ascent and Lyapunov accounting). Let Φt+1/2 denote the Lyapunov value after the primal, auxiliary, and codebook updates but before the dual update. Writing u 1 = u and u 2 = V , we have

$$\begin{aligned} u_{t+1}^1 &= u_t^1 + (o_{t+1} - y_{t+1}) = u_t^1 + r_1^{t+1}, \\ u_{t+1}^2 &= u_t^2 + (\Theta_{t+1} - \hat{\Theta}_{t+1}) = u_t^2 + r_2^{t+1}, \end{aligned} \quad (\text{S14})$$

where r1 := ro and r2 := rΘ. The augmented-Lagrangian change across the dual step is:


$$\begin{aligned} & L_{\rho}(\mathbf{o}_{t+1}, \Theta_{t+1}, \mathbf{y}_{t+1}, \hat{\Theta}_{t+1}, \mathbf{u}_{t+1}^1, \mathbf{u}_{t+1}^2, \mathcal{Q}_{t+1}) \\ & \quad - L_{\rho}(\mathbf{o}_{t+1}, \Theta_{t+1}, \mathbf{y}_{t+1}, \hat{\Theta}_{t+1}, \mathbf{u}_t^1, \mathbf{u}_t^2, \mathcal{Q}_{t+1}) \\ & = \sum_{i=1}^2 \frac{\rho_i}{2} \left( 2 \langle \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{u}_i^t, \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} \rangle + \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1}\|_2^2 \right), \end{aligned} \quad (S15)$$

and consequently:


$$\Phi_{t+1} = \Phi_{t+1/2} + \sum_{i=1}^2 \frac{\rho_i}{2} \left( 2\langle \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{u}_i^t, \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} \rangle + \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1}\|_2^2 \right), \quad (\text{S16})$$

because the two residual terms in Φ do not change during the dual step.

Proof. Only the quadratic penalty involving ui changes. For i ∈ {1, 2}, with r t+1 i fixed and u i t+1 = u i t + r t+1 i , we have:

$$\frac{\rho_i}{2} \left( \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{u}_{i+1}^i\|_2^2 - \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{u}_i^i\|_2^2 \right) = \frac{\rho_i}{2} \left( 2 \langle \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} + \mathbf{u}_i^i, \mathbf{r}_i^{t+1} \rangle + \|\mathbf{r}_i^{t+1}\|_2^2 \right). \quad (\text{S17})$$

This follows from ∥p + q∥ 2 2 − ∥p∥ 2 2 = 2⟨p, q⟩ + ∥q∥ 2 2 . Summing over both blocks gives Eq. S15; the unchanged residual terms then give Eq. S16.

Lemma 4 (Monotonicity, summability, and vanishing residuals). Under Lemmas 1–3 and the standing assumptions, there are positive constants C1, C2 such that

$$\Phi_{t+1} \leq \Phi_t - C_1 \|\Delta \mathbf{o}_t\|_2^2 - C_2 \|\Delta \mathbf{\Theta}_t\|_2^2, \quad (\text{S18})$$

#### Theorem 1 (Limit-point Stationarity).

Suppose the standing assumptions hold for the sequence generated by our optimization scheme. Then:

- 1. The sequence {Φt} is non-increasing and converges to a finite limit Φ ∗ .
- 2. Both successive model-parameter differences vanish: limt→∞ ∥ot+1 − ot∥2 = 0 and limt→∞ ∥Θt+1 − Θt∥2 = 0.
- 3. Both primal residuals vanish: limt→∞ ∥ot − yt∥2 = 0 and limt→∞ ∥Θt − Θˆ t∥2 = 0.
- 4. Any limit point satisfies the continuous KKT conditions and the discrete projection fixed-point conditions of Eq. S1. Specifically:
  - Stationarity for Model Parameters: ∇oL(o ∗ , Θ∗ ) + ρ1u ∗ = 0, ∇ΘL(o ∗ , Θ∗ ) + ρ2V ∗ = 0.
  - Optimality for Auxiliary Variables: 0 ∈ ∂g(y ∗ ) − ρ1 o ∗ − y ∗ + u ∗ , Θˆ ∗ = ΠQ∗ Θ∗ + V ∗ .
  - Optimality for the Codebook and Assignments: for every nonempty Q∗ j ,

$$q_j^* = \frac{1}{|Q_j^|} \sum_{\theta_i^ \in Q_j^} \theta_i^,$$

$$Q_j^* = \left\{ \theta_i^* : j \in \arg \min_{k=1, \dots, M} \|\theta_i^* - q_k^*\|_2^2 \right\}.$$

#### • Primal Feasibility: o ∗ = y ∗ , Θ∗ = Θˆ ∗ .

Proof. Items 1–3 follow from Lemma 4. For Item 4, let (o ∗ , Θ∗ , y ∗ , Θˆ ∗ ,u ∗ ,V ∗ , Q∗ ) be the limit of a subsequence {(otj , Θtj , ytj , Θˆ tj ,utj ,Vtj , Qtj )}j∈N. The opacity update is

$$\mathbf{o}_{t_{j+1}} \leftarrow \mathbf{o}_{t_j} - \boldsymbol{\eta}_1 \left( \nabla_{\mathbf{o}} L(\mathbf{o}_{t_j}, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{t_j}) + \rho_1 (\mathbf{o}_{t_j} - \mathbf{y}_{t_j} + \mathbf{u}_{t_j}) \right).$$

Item 2 makes the update direction vanish along this subsequence. Taking the limit and using Item 3 gives stationarity for o ∗ ; the same argument applies to Θ∗ . The auxiliaryvariable conditions and primal feasibility follow from their update rules and Item 3. Under Assumption 5, the centroid and assignment maps in Eq. S5 are fixed at the limit, giving the stated condition for Q∗ . Thus, the limit point satisfies all stated continuous and discrete stationarity conditions.
