The intersect.* units (layers 27–37) will port SMLib's surface/surface intersection 1:1. Before that work starts, I measured what the upstream C++ at c9c5979 gets right, on 119 surface pairs whose exact intersection is known by construction. The port will reproduce these results bit for bit. This issue proposes how the corpus can serve both the port and the fixes that come after it.
Setup
Build. source/SMLib and source/SM_API only, at -O3 with the upstream release defines, no TBB.
Entry points. SmSurface::GlobalSurfaceIntersect (what SmApiIntersectSurfaces calls) is the primary one. Also measured: SmAdvSurfaceIntersector, SmSurfaceIntersector, SmFace::FaceIntersect (20°) and SmApiIntersectSurfaces.
Tolerances. Approximation tolerances 1e-6 (primary), 1e-4 and the default. Global tolerances at their defaults (zone 1e-5).
Failure sweep. Every failure was re-run in 26 configurations: 4 intersectors × 3 tolerances × both surface orders, plus the API in both orders. A trace build (SM_DEBUG_CODE=1) reported no invalid-input errors.
Ground truth. Closed form for 116 cases. Three groups are numerically traced: the torus pairs, the tube arms and the appendix patch pair. All stored points lie on both exact surfaces to 1e-9 or better.
Scoring.
coverage both ways (one-sided Hausdorff);
exact distance to both surfaces;
connected components and free ends;
duplicated curves;
whether every singular point is a curve end or an output point.
Results
Primary run: 81 of 119 pass. 25 are incomplete, 11 have wrong topology, 2 return an error, and none crash. Every returned curve lies within 1.9e-6 of both exact surfaces, except two tangent curves at 3.4e-5 (y23_t2_ex4, y23_t6_ex1).

The reconstructions rebuild the paper's Fig. 1, Table 2 and Table 6 examples. The paper publishes no geometry for those, so the surfaces are ours. Each was fitted to the paper's figure through a camera recovered from the figure itself, keeping the stated degrees, topology and contact type, with the exact intersection known in closed form. An independent audit against the paper found 15 of 19 faithful in shape and topology and 4 matching in topology with a documented shape difference. The published-data cases are the Table 3/4 torus/ellipsoid pairs and appendix Example 2.
Gaps that no configuration closes (0/26 unless noted):
Isolated tangential contact points in a patch interior are mostly missed. The general path returns touching points reliably only for tangencies on patch boundaries; the sphere/plane and sphere/sphere special cases return interior contacts.
Missed: c3n_parab_apex, c3n_parab_parab_apex, c3n_four_points, c3_sphere_torus_outer, y23_fig1g (both tangent contacts missed; the shared cone point at the apex is found), y23_t2_ex6, y23_t6_ex4.
Found: y23_fig1b (a contact point between two transversal branches).
Tiny loops above the zone tolerance are missed. These are loops of diameter 2e-4 and 2e-5 on a model of size 2.
On a NURBS paraboloid: nothing is returned; 2/26 configurations find the 2e-4 loop.
On the analytic sphere: a single point is returned; adv/basic at 1e-6 find the 2e-4 loop (4/26).
Curves are not split at singular crossing points. The point set is complete, but curves run through the crossing without ending there.
Cases: c4_steinmetz (1 of 2 crossings), c4_viviani_figure8, c4n_viviani_nurbs, y23_fig1a, y23_t6_ex3, and the published y23_t3_ex4 torus/ellipsoid pair.
Tangent and high-order contact curves.
Open tangent curves come back twice, once in each direction: c2n_cylN_on_planeN, y23_t6_ex1 (also 3.4e-5 off the surfaces), y23_t2_ex4 (an inflected tangent curve, 58% overlap, 3.4e-5 off; 7/26 configurations pass).
Tangent curves collapse to their two end points: y23_t2_ex1 (straight second-order contact) and y23_fig1f (second-order contact where the sheets cross).
A closed tangent circle on a quartic is missed: c2n_quartic_circle.
Partial tangent curves: y23_t2_ex2 covered at 23% (6/26 pass, all at 1e-4); y23_t2_ex3, a loop and two curves meeting at four tangent nodes, covered at 8%; the tangential middle edge of y23_fig1d covered at 1%; one boundary curve of y23_t6_ex6 covered at 88%, with another returned seven times.
SM_ERR_NULL_POINTER from ReverseCurveDirection() (SmSurfaceIntersector.cpp:7278), called by DoIntersection (line 6555): c2n_quartic_contact_parabola (4th-order contact) and y23_t2_ex5 (two loops, one tangential along its whole length) at 1e-6; at 1e-4 and the default tolerance y23_t2_ex5 returns without error but incomplete.
Below the 1e-5 zone tolerance, the following are reported as such and not counted as defects: angles of 1e-6 to 1e-8, offsets of 1e-7, and loops of 2e-6 or less.
The constructor V-knot issue (SmBSplineSurface.cpp:87) also showed up; it is already in plan/findings.md under nurbs.b_spline_surface_p1.
Proposal
The corpus becomes fixtures for the intersect.* units. Each case is a JSON file holding:
the two surfaces (planes, quadrics, tori, NURBS);
the exact truth (implicit or parametric form);
the expected branches, isolated points, singular points and coincident regions, with a coverage tolerance.
difftest uses only the surfaces, so the gate stays Rust vs C++ at 1e-9. I would add the generator and the case inputs under fixtures/, with the sampled truth regenerated rather than committed (it is 28 MB).
A separate ground-truth acceptance test. It scores the port's output against the exact truth and reports per case. It does not gate a unit's green status, because a green unit must reproduce the gaps above.
Fixes come after parity. Once the SSI units are green, each gap gets its own change, recorded as a deviation in the unit header and in plan/findings.md. The fixed behaviour then gets its own fixture expectation instead of a C++ comparison. Each fix is gated by the acceptance test going from fail to pass, with no regressions elsewhere in the corpus.
Question: is (3) how you want beyond-upstream behaviour handled in solidcore, or should it live downstream in SolidCraft? If (3) works for you, I'll open a PR with (1) and (2).
