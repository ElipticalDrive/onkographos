Figure S shows a possible intersection configuration. The quadrilateral

Along with the 4 vertices of the Lagrangian cell, these internal and boundary intersections provide a 14-point multilateral intersection domain. From these 14
points, it is not hard to ste that 7 multilateral figures emerge, each In the same
Lagrangian cell but a different Eulerian cell. Each of these seven multilaterals

are then rotated about the 2-axis and sunned to give the total intersection volume
of the particular intersection multilateral. Indices tagging the parent Lagrangian
and Eulerian cells containing the multilateral are also computed and stored along
with the intersection volumes.
The ordering sequence for these Lagrangian and Eulerian zone indices is

a-values and increase continuously; first, with increasing r-values and then with
increasing z-values. The Lagrangian Indices, however, start with largest /.-values
and increase continuously, first, with increasing r-values and then with decreasing
a-values. For the case of a center converging web as shown in Fig. 8 one imagines
that lines of equal z-values are now passed through the origin so that the previously indicated numbering scheme is transformed as shown.

III. PROGRAM FLOW AND ANALYSIS
A .schematic flow chart for ESECT/EMAT is shown In Fig. 9. A listing of the
routines appears In Sec. IV. Briefly, the program logic and analysis proceeds
in the following manner.
The Lagrangfan grid point ZL.RL and Euletia,-* lines ££,RE are passed to
ESECT through a subroutine call statement. A preliminary edit and teat Is made
on the mesh data and all other arrays are zerced. A sweep Is made through the
Lagrunglan cell vertices and the Kuicrl.w line intersect ions with the Lagrangian
cell boundaries and the self-Intersection* of the Eulerian lines in Che interior
of the Lagrangian cell are computed and scored in arrays 2E,Rt. To all vertices
of the Lagrangian cell and to all boundary and internal intersections are also
assigned a set of two common line slopes (tagging the iip.es which intersect at
Che points) and & set of four integer Eulerian indices (denoting the possible
Eulerian '.-ells containing the points). The slopes are stored in arrays SI.SL
while the mapping indices are contained in the arrays MLZ.MLR. Sweeps over the
Eulerian mesh are controlled and determined by the- vertices of the Lagrangian
cell. Ti»e vertices of the Lagrangian cell define a maximum domain of Intersection and subsequent sweeps to compute Intersections and ranges of mapping
searches «lie controlled by the parameters MXZ, M1Z, MXii, and *"R. Coarse region
mapping parameters for the arrays 7.1 and RI are contained in arrays LZ.LR and
serve as pivots for computing ML2fMLR. The arrays 21,HI contain all possible
coordinates for construction of intersection multilateral;; formed by the overlay
of the Lagrangian cell on the Eulerian oeah. A sweep is then made through the
intersection domain and all points possessing the satire mapping indices MLZ,MLR
are placed Into the subarrays 27.1,RRl, while the corresponding line slopes are
placed into the subarrays SLI and SOI. The subarrays contain the actual vertices
and line slopes of the individual multilateral!*. A reordering algorithm first
sequentially joins the multilateral vertices through common line slopes
checks to make sure that connected points lie on the same segments and then triangulates the figure. The intersection volume of the triangulated multilateral
is finally computed using Eqs. (4), (5), and (6) and the results are given by VV
and stored in the array VV. The indices tagging the Lagrangian and Eulerian
cells common to the intersection multilateral, LM and IE respectively, are stored
in the arrays LLH and H E . The rotation volumes of the full parent Lagrangian
and Etilnrlan cells tagged by LM and IE are computed exactly and stored in arrays
OV and EV. Division of the intersection volumes W by the corresponding parent
cell volumes GV or EV produces a volume weighing fraction for mapping or averaging
between meshes, as indicated by Eqs. (8) and (9). All computed volumes are
normalized to 2v, that is, the assumed rotation angle about the z-axis Is 2TI.

In the event of default in the calculation due to overrun arrays, ordering
or mapping failures, etc., a listing of the large intersection arrays RI, ZI, SI,
SO, LR, LZ, MLR, and MLZ is first printed, followed by the smaller multilateral
arrays RRI, ZZI, SLI, and SOI, the ordering indices K£, KD, and the zone parameters
LM.IE.

IV. APPLICATIONS AND RESULTS
Timing runs for various mesh configurations are given in Tables I and II.
The total number of intersection volumes computed are also listed alongside run
times. In Table I, Eulerlan mesh spaclngs are uniform and radial and angular
segments of the center-converging Lagrangian mesh are equal. Geometrically, the
meshes compared in Table I are pictured as semicircular webs fully inscribed in
rectangles with the boundaries of the rectangles tangent at angles of 0°, 90°,
and 180°, The meshes compared in Table II are both rectangular with coincident
boundaries and equal spacing within each mesh. Run times are rounded off to next
highest integer seconds and intersection volumes are rounded off to next highest
hundred intersection number. It can be seen from these Tables that processing
times vary from a minimum of approximately 1 s/lk intersection volumes for simple
mesh configurations to a rough maximum of 3 s /Ik intersection volumes for large
Eulerian and small Lagrangian grids. Existing algorithms will process two orthogonal, rectangular grid patterns the fastest since line slopes and intercepts
need not oe computed, compared, and ordered.
