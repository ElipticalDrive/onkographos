Voxel-aware global illumination and ambient occlusion
Problem
With no AO or GI, voxel scenes lose the corner-darkening cues that make voxel geometry readable. The current single ambient term flattens caves and overhangs.
Goal
Add cheap but visually impactful indirect lighting for voxels: per-voxel screen-space AO plus optional one-bounce diffuse GI via voxel cone tracing or short ray bundles.
Option A — Raymarched AO (recommended first cut)
After primary hit, cast K short rays (default K=4) in the hemisphere around the surface normal.
Sample directions from a cosine-weighted hemisphere using blue-noise + Malley's method (sample disc, project to hemisphere). Seeded by (pixel.xy, frameIndex) to allow TAA convergence.
Each ray steps at most 4–8 voxels using the existing macro/brick traversal with an early-exit. Occlusion is the fraction of rays that hit.
Multiply diffuse by pow(1 - occlusion, 0.7); do not darken the specular/sky component.
Budget: K=4 short rays add ~0.7 ms at 1080p on a mid-range GPU.
Option B — Voxel cone tracing for one-bounce GI
Build a mipmapped voxel radiance volume: 3D texture (e.g. R11G11B10F at 256³ around the camera), updated each frame by injecting the lit primary-hit color into the corresponding voxel cell, then mipmapping.
For GI, trace 5–6 wide cones from the surface normal hemisphere into the mipped volume; LOD selection per step uses log2(cone_radius / voxel_size).
This is what The Tomorrow Children and various Sponza demos use. References: Crassin et al. 2011, "Interactive Indirect Illumination Using Voxel Cone Tracing".
Budget: significantly more memory (≈ 16 MB at 256³ with mips) and ~2–3 ms of GPU time. Make it opt-in.
Implementation notes
Both options reuse the existing brick texture as the occlusion source for AO. No new geometry pass needed.
For GI volume injection, run a compute shader over the brick pool: for each occupied voxel, splat its lit color (using current sun direction) into the radiance volume. Re-do every N frames (default 4) — GI is low-frequency.
Provide a RenderQuality.GI setting: Off, AO, VCT.
Combine with TAA so a small K still converges.
Specific GLSL gotchas
texture3D with linear filter on the radiance volume gives bilinear interpolation between voxels — exactly what cone tracing wants.
Never sample mip 0 of the GI volume — it's the injection target and only valid after the mip-down pass.
Clamp ray length to a multiple of voxel size to avoid stepping past the world AABB.
Acceptance criteria
AO toggle visibly darkens corners and concave geometry in the flycam-perlin example.
AO cost ≤ 1 ms at 1080p on mid-range GPU.
VCT GI produces visible color bleed (red wall → white floor turns pinkish) in a Cornell-box-style voxel test scene.
Golden-image BDD test for AO on / off comparison.
No NaNs in the radiance volume across 10 000 frames (fuzz-tested with random scene edits).
