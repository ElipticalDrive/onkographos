Related work and applications
The 1-Lipschitzness of implicit functions has long been exploited
in graphics and geometry processing. In rendering, the value of 𝑓
is used as a safe radius for ray-casting and cell pruning [Barbier
et al. 2025; Hart 1996; Kalra and Barr 1989]. These settings are in a
way the inverse setup involving the same value. To reject an entire
grid cell as not containing√𝑓 −1 (0), we can evaluate 𝑓 at the center 𝑐
and verify that |𝑓 (𝑐)| > ℎ 3/2. Naively flipping this around for our
setting, the query point becomes any probe point 𝑝 in the cell, so to
ensure that
√ 𝑓 (𝑝) > 0, we would require at each grid sample vertex
𝑓 (𝑣) > ℎ 3: loose by a factor of 1/2.
A sharp conservative offset is useful when marching cubes meshing occurs as a subroutine for tasks that require strictly maintaining
a containment invariance. The unsigned distance contouring of Hou
et al. [2023] begins with a marching cubes mesh of a small offset,
but makes no guarantee that the original 0-level set won’t poke
through if the offset mesh is too small. In the cage construction of
Sellán et al. [2021], high-resolution meshed levelsets are the initial
input to a decimation engine. In swept volume extraction, implicit
functions are particularly useful to aggregate signed distances of a
shape over its temporal motion [Schroeder et al. 1994; Sellán et al.
2021]. But if the final extracted mesh is meant for safe downstream
collision tasks, then a sharp and conservative offset is appreciated
(see Fig. 2).
