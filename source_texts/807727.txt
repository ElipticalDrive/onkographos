Abstract
Gaussian Splatting (GS) is widely used for efficient 3D scene representation and rendering by modeling scenes as continuous Gaussian distributions. However, GS struggles with high-frequency details and sharp transitions due to its low-pass filtering effect, often requiring multiple Gaussian stacking, which increases computational and memory costs. To overcome these limitations, we
propose Truncated and Tailored Gaussian Splatting (TNT-GS), a
novel approach that enhances shape complexity and preserves
sharp boundaries. Our method truncates Gaussians to generate
sharp edges and flexible shapes without excessive stacking, improving efficiency. We also introduce learnable parameters to dynamically tailor the receptive field of the primitives, optimizing
the balance between high-frequency details and smooth regions.
Furthermore, we employ specialized densification strategies to
further improve efficiency during tile computation. Experimental results show that TNT-GS outperforms state-of-the-art methods
in storage efficiency and rendering speed, offering a robust solution for real-time rendering. The code of TNT-GS is available at
https://github.com/GoogolplexGoodenough/TNT-GS.

Introduction
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [14] is a significant advancement in
3D rendering that enables realistic representations of 3D scenes by
projecting Gaussian primitives onto a 2D pixel plane and adjusting
them to fit the image.
Its ability to achieve high rendering efficiency and visual quality
in smooth and moderately complex scenes makes it valuable in

various applications, such as cinematic anatomy for medical visualization [2, 20], scenario representation in SLAM [10, 13, 18, 25], and
visual localization in augmented reality [26, 29]. However, Gaussian
primitives, due to their inherent smooth and continuous nature,
have limited representational capability [11, 17, 23], which reduces
their effectiveness in capturing sharp transitions, fine details, and
intricate shapes [7]. As Figure 1 illustrates, given the limited representational capacity of a single primitive, representing complex
shapes or high-frequency details in 3DGS often requires stacking
a large number of Gaussian primitives, which leads to significant
storage overhead and challenges in memory efficiency.
In recent years, various methods have been proposed to modify the splatted primitives, enhancing the diversity of Gaussian
primitives and improving their representational capability of high
frequency details and diverse shapes. 3D-HGS [17] represents sharp
edges by cutting the Gaussian kernel into two halves. Both DisCGS [23] and BG-Triangle [24] preserve sharp edges by integrating
Bézier curves with Gaussian primitives for discontinuity-aware rendering. DRK [11] represents complex shapes by interpolating multiple radial bases in polar coordinates. These advancements enable
Gaussian Splatting to capture fine geometric details or represent
complex shapes. Despite these improvements, a critical challenge
remains: achieving a balance between rendering efficiency and representational capability. Specifically, representing more complex
shapes often requires introducing numerous additional parameters,
e.g. additional anchor points for generating Bézier curves, which
significantly increases computational complexity and storage size
of the model.
We propose TNT-GS: Truncated and Tailored Gaussian Splatting. This approach intersects and truncates multiple Gaussians to
support flexible shape modeling and tailors their boundaries to enhance sharpness, enabling high-frequency detail preservation and
complex geometry representation without introducing extra anchor
points. To achieve this, we intersect truncated Gaussians within
a common tangent plane, using rotational alignment to construct
expressive shapes with sharp boundaries and minimal parameter
overhead. Then we introduce differentiable adjustable parameters
to apply continuous decay to the sharp boundaries. By controlling
the decay coefficient, we dynamically adjust the transition between
sharpness and smoothness for precise boundary refinement. Furthermore, to improve scene quality and resource allocation, we
introduce a densification criterion that selectively targets blurred
areas, guiding the model to split primitives where necessary. On the
DTU dataset, our method improves PSNR by 1.81, rendering speed
by 107%, and reduces storage to 16% of 3DGS, while outperforming
the second-best method by 0.49 in PSNR, 19.64% in rendering speed,
and requiring only 40% of the storage.

Related Works
Differential Primitive. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [14] uses
Gaussian primitives for smooth, continuous scene representation,
but struggles to capture sharp transitions and high-frequency details due to the low-pass filtering effect of Gaussians. GES [7] approximates higher-order Gaussian kernels with Generalized Exponential Functions, addressing some limitations of traditional
Gaussians. Mip-splatting [27] introduces a 3D smoothing filter and
a 2D Mip filter to reduce aliasing and dilation artifacts. Despite
these advances, these methods remain within the 3DGS framework
and do not fully resolve its inherent limitations. Improvements such
as 2DGS [9] and GSSurfel [3] provide spatial consistency across
views for surface reconstruction, but still rely on 2D Gaussian projections. To pursue sharp edges and diverse shapes, edge-cutting
and additional points are used to control the shape of the kernel.
3D-HGS [17] cuts normals for sharp edges, but its fixed cutting
direction limits flexibility. Additional shape-control methods like
anchor points in Disc-GS [23] and BG-Triangle [24] offer more
precise control, while DRK [11] defines shapes using radial bases.
However, these approaches increase storage and computational
costs due to the extra parameters.
Efficiency Optimization. Assigning an index score to each Gaussian and ranking the contribution is a general way to prune the
Gaussian primitives [4, 5, 21] along densification. Speedy-Splat [8]
introduces a prune score to each primitive based on the Hessian
during training and provides the closed form of the primitive boundary for fast rasterization. GOF [28] introduces a new metric that
guides the densification of Gaussian primitives in blurry regions,
enhancing the model’s ability to capture high-frequency details.

4.2
Tailored Gaussian for Sharpness Control
Direct truncation of the Gaussian kernel sharpens edges but introduces discontinuities, limiting the kernel’s ability to capture details.
This reduces adaptability, especially in complex scenes with both
sharp and smooth regions. Natural images often have dominant
low-frequency, smooth areas, while sharp edges are sparse. While
3DGS [14] struggles with high-frequency components, it effectively
models low-frequency regions. Other methods, like DRK [11], also
introduce low-pass filtering effects.

In sharp edge representation, conventional Gaussian kernels often
lead to excessive smoothing, resulting in blurred boundaries and
loss of detail. To address this issue, we introduce the Truncated

In practical applications, the interaction between just two truncated Gaussians already exhibits significant representational power,

our method requires fewer primitives to represent the same scene,
leading to a reduction in overall storage space.
By strategically incorporating the additional parameters for mutual truncation, our method achieves a balanced trade-off between
storage cost and model expressiveness, making it a practical choice

Experiment
To evaluate our method, we performed experiments on different
scales, starting with simple 2D geometric patterns, moving to 3D
indoor objects, and ending with complex outdoor scenes. Through
multi-view and full-angle evaluations, we highlight the advantages
of our approach over state-of-the-art methods (including 3DGS [14],
2DGS [9], GSSurfel [3], Mip-splatting (MipGS) [27], GES [7], 3DHGS [17], and DRK [11]). All experiments were performed on an
RTX 3090 GPU for efficient computational performance. Unless
otherwise specified, our training loss is consistent with that of
2DGS, and the model uses GSSurfel as the basic 2D building block.

To evaluate the geometric representation capability of the proposed
unit, we conducted experiments on various shapes using a limited

evaluate their representation capability. The training loss is Mean

using just one primitive results in lower relative error compared to

As mentioned in Section 4, our truncated and tailored strategies
are designed for 2D disk-based primitives. We evaluated their effectiveness by testing truncation and tailoring using 2DGS and GSSurfel as kernel components. For reliable assessment, we conducted
experiments on 5 randomly selected DTU scenes, maintaining consistent target parameters across scenes to minimize the impact of
variations in the number of Gaussian primitives.
We evaluated the truncation and tailoring strategies on two fundamental methods, 2DGS and GSSurfel. The composite truncated
Gaussian (CTG) and truncated and tailored Gaussian (TNT) are
created by integrating base kernels with truncation and tailoring
mechanisms, respectively. Both strategies improve PSNR and SSIM,
with TNT outperforming others while maintaining competitive FPS.
Compared to the original methods, TNT boosts PSNR by up to 8.3%
(2DGS) and 24% (GSSurfel), and SSIM by 1.7% and 5.5%, respectively.
Overall, TNT offers significant quality improvements without compromising computational efficiency. Furthermore, experimental
results show that accurate tile strategy improves rendering FPS
by an average of 249% (2DGS) and 50% (GSSurfel). As shown in
Table 4 and Figure 10, our densification strategy yields the best
visual quality across all metrics with comparable model size.
