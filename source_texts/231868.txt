# Neural Vector Fields for Chaotic Dynamics

**One conditional neural field represents 12 chaotic systems and is evaluated by the dynamics it produces—not only by pointwise fit.**

![Twelve learned chaotic attractors](assets/hero-attractor-grid.png)

> **Presentation-only showcase:** This repository is a presentation-only project showcase. Source code, datasets, notebooks, trained weights, and training artifacts are intentionally not included.

## Research question

> **Does the model only fit sampled geometry, or does it reproduce the underlying dynamics?**

A vector field can achieve excellent held-out error while still producing the wrong long-term flow when integrated. This project therefore treats pointwise regression as a necessary check—not the final result.

## Why pointwise validation is insufficient

Nearby samples from the same trajectory are strongly correlated. A random sample split can place near-duplicates in training and validation, making interpolation look like generalization. It also says little about the field away from the thin manifold traced by an attractor.

The final pipeline uses:

- trajectory-level train/validation/test separation;
- off-manifold supervision on perturbed shells and a surrounding box;
- learned-field integration from unseen initial conditions; and
- invariant-level comparison against the analytical systems.

## System architecture

![Architecture and validation pipeline](assets/architecture-pipeline.svg)

A shared **SIREN** trunk receives normalized 3D position. A learned system embedding is mapped to per-layer scale and shift parameters through **FiLM**, allowing one network to represent 12 distinct vector fields. Per-system space and time normalization balances systems with very different radii and speeds.

The learned velocity field is integrated with **RK4**. Its trajectories are then evaluated against the analytical field using the same numerical pipeline, including Lyapunov-exponent estimation.

## Physics-validation pipeline

1. Screen candidate systems with a Lyapunov-based chaos gate.
2. Generate analytical velocities with trajectory-level data splits.
3. Add off-manifold samples to constrain recovery behavior.
4. Train the conditional FiLM–SIREN vector field.
5. Measure held-out velocity accuracy by region.
6. Integrate analytical and learned fields through the same RK4 path.
7. Compare Lyapunov exponents, trajectory scale, and escape behavior.

## Key debugging discoveries

- **Validation leakage:** random sample splitting placed adjacent trajectory points on both sides of the split. Trajectory-level separation replaced it.
- **Incorrect chaotic parameters:** commonly cited parameters for Halvorsen and Thomas fell into periodic regimes under measurement. A chaos gate and parameter sweep caught them before dataset generation.
- **Numerical estimator error:** float32 state integration inflated Lyapunov estimates. Keeping model evaluation in float32 while promoting integration and renormalization to float64 removed the shared bias.
- **Off-manifold generalization:** fitting only the attractor surface left the vector field unconstrained elsewhere. Perturbed-shell and uniform-box supervision improved recovery behavior.
- **Training throughput:** grouped FiLM modulation avoided a costly per-sample gather/scatter path, producing an approximately **8.5×** training-step speedup.

## Known limitations

- Dynamical fidelity is not uniform: Rossler and Aizawa showed higher Lyapunov error than the median in the reported evaluation.
- FourWing converged to an attractor with a substantially larger RMS radius than its analytical reference.
- Far-field accuracy degraded for Dadras and Burke–Shaw relative to their on-attractor accuracy.
- The chaos gate rejects multistable systems rather than representing multiple basins with separate latent states.
- Results cover the 12 trained systems only; this is not a claim of zero-shot generalization.
