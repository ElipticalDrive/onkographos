ABSTRACT
This review paper presents a comprehensive analysis of 30 research papers on Unreal Engine
and real-time rendering systems, covering gaming applications and virtual production.
Developed by Epic Games, Unreal Engine has revolutionized the creation of interactive digital
content. Key technologies, including Nanite (virtualized geometry), Lumen (real-time global
illumination), MetaHuman Creator, and Ray Tracing are examined in depth. The paper also
explores virtual production pipelines for film and television, procedural content generation
(PCG) enhanced by AI and Large Language Models, and rendering optimization for local and
cloud-based gaming. Findings indicate that Unreal Engine 5 represents a paradigm shift in realtime graphics, enabling film-quality visuals at interactive frame rates. This paper serves as a
structured academic reference for researchers, developers, and filmmakers working at the
intersection of real-time technology and creative production.
Keywords: Real-Time Rendering, Unreal Engine 5, Nanite, Lumen, Ray Tracing,
MetaHuman, Virtual Production, Game Engine Optimization, Procedural Content Generation,
Cloud Gaming
I. INTRODUCTION
Unreal Engine, developed by Epic Games, has become one of the most powerful and widelyused game engines in the world. It has fundamentally changed how games are developed and
how movies use computer graphics. Originally built for first-person shooters in 1998, Unreal
Engine has evolved into a comprehensive real-time 3D creation platform used across gaming,
film, television, architecture, automotive design, and medical simulation. This review paper
examines 30 research papers about Unreal Engine and real-time rendering systems, focusing
on two primary areas: gaming applications and virtual production for films and TV shows.
This paper is organized into the following major sections: (1) Core Unreal Engine Technologies
covering Nanite, Lumen, MetaHuman, and Ray Tracing; (2) Gaming Applications including
engine comparisons and VR development; (3) Virtual Production for film, TV, and indie
filmmaking pipelines; (4) Rendering Optimization covering LOD, culling, and cloud gaming
techniques; and (5) Procedural Content Generation (PCG) with AI and LLM integration. Realtime rendering refers to generating computer graphics so rapidly that they appear
instantaneously on screen. Unlike traditional offline rendering—which can take hours or days
per frame—Unreal Engine has elevated real-time rendering to a level where it now competes
with, and in some cases surpasses, traditional movie-quality graphics.

II. RELATED WORKS
Recent literature demonstrates a clear and accelerating evolution in how Unreal Engine is
applied across gaming, film, and virtual production. Across all five thematic areas examined
in this review, a consistent pattern emerges: each major Unreal Engine 5 technology—Nanite,
Lumen, MetaHuman, Ray Tracing, and AI-driven PCG—delivers measurable improvements
in visual quality, development efficiency, or creative accessibility, while simultaneously
lowering the barrier to entry for smaller studios and independent creators.
A. UE5 Core Technologies (Nanite, Lumen, MetaHuman, Ray Tracing)

Nanite’s virtualized geometry system enables the use of film-quality assets—containing
hundreds of millions of polygons—without any perceptible performance degradation,
eliminating the need for manual LOD authoring [1, 7]. Lumen’s fully dynamic global
illumination system eliminates the reliance on pre-baked lightmaps, enabling lighting that
responds in real time to scene changes [4]. MetaHuman Creator has compressed digital human
creation from months of skilled technical work to under one hour for a single developer,
democratizing high-fidelity character production [3, 5]. Ray Tracing, while delivering
unmatched visual realism, carries a documented 20–30 FPS performance cost and
approximately 10°C increase in GPU temperature on current consumer hardware [6].
B. Gaming Applications
Comparative engine analysis consistently positions Unreal Engine ahead of competing
platforms for graphical fidelity, while Unity retains advantages in mobile deployment and
cross-platform reach [8, 10]. VR development research confirms that Unreal Engine’s forward
rendering pipeline and native VR template significantly reduce the development overhead for
immersive applications [9]. Third-person and open-world game development studies
demonstrate that World Partition and Level Streaming systems enable seamless streaming of
massive game environments without hitching, a capability previously limited to AAA studios
with custom engines [11, 12]. The primary limitation across gaming application research is the
steep learning curve associated with Blueprint Visual Scripting and C++ integration for
developers new to the ecosystem [13].
C. Virtual Production
Virtual production research reveals that the LED volume pipeline—pioneered on productions
such as The Mandalorian—allows directors and cinematographers to see final visual effects
live during principal photography, eliminating the historical disconnect between on-set capture
and post-production compositing [16, 17]. In-camera VFX studies confirm a significant
reduction in post-production time and cost compared to traditional green-screen pipelines [18].
Indie filmmaking research demonstrates that Unreal Engine’s virtual production tools are now
accessible to productions with budgets far below Hollywood scale, enabling small crews to
produce broadcast-quality output [19, 20]. The key remaining challenge is the calibration
complexity required to match LED panel output to real-world lighting conditions on set [21,
22].
D. Rendering Optimization and PCG with AI Integration
Rendering optimization research consistently identifies culling (reducing draw calls by up to
90%), instanced static mesh rendering (60% performance improvement), and adaptive LOD
systems as the most impactful techniques for achieving playable frame rates on mid-range
hardware [23, 24]. Cloud gaming studies confirm that adaptive streaming protocols, combined
with Unreal Engine’s Pixel Streaming feature, enable high-fidelity gaming experiences on
devices without dedicated GPUs [25, 26]. For Procedural Content Generation, the integration
of Large Language Models and Transformer-based architectures into Unreal Engine’s PCG

domains that are not incremental but transformational. For UE5 Core Technologies, Nanite’s

V. CONCLUSION
After reviewing 30 research papers published between 2022 and 2026, it is evident that Unreal
Engine 5 represents a paradigm shift in real-time graphics technology, enabling film-quality
visuals at interactive frame rates and reshaping creative workflows across gaming, film,
television, architecture, and education.
A. Nanite and Lumen — A Rendering Revolution
The core technologies of Unreal Engine 5—particularly Nanite and Lumen—represent a
watershed moment in real-time graphics. Nanite’s virtualized geometry system allows games
to use movie-quality 3D models with hundreds of millions of triangles without performance
degradation. Lumen’s fully dynamic global illumination transforms lighting from a static, precomputed process into a living, reactive system. Together, they eliminate the historical gap
between offline rendering and interactive real-time engines, making photorealism a standard
expectation rather than a distant aspiration.
B. Virtual Production — The Future of Filmmaking
Virtual production has emerged as a revolutionary approach to making films and television.
Unreal Engine allows directors and cinematographers to see final visual effects live during

The Mandalorian has validated virtual production at the highest level of the industry, and the
technology is rapidly democratizing—becoming accessible even to independent studios
without major Hollywood budgets. This represents a fundamental restructuring of the
filmmaking pipeline, where the boundary between principal photography and visual effects is
dissolved.
C. Ray Tracing — Visual Realism with a Hardware Cost
Ray tracing has become practical for consumer gaming, but carries significant hardware costs:
a 20–30 FPS performance drop and increased GPU temperatures of approximately 10 degrees
Celsius on current generation hardware. As next-generation GPUs mature and algorithms such
as MegaLights emerge to reduce sample counts, ray tracing will become a standard feature
rather than a premium option. The trajectory is unambiguous—photorealistic lighting is the
inevitable future of interactive media, and Unreal Engine’s hardware-accelerated ray tracing
pipeline places it at the center of that transition.
D. Optimization and PCG — The Foundation of Accessibility
Rendering optimization remains the critical bridge between advanced visual technology and
broad player access. Techniques such as Level of Detail (LOD) systems, culling (reducing
rendering workload by up to 90%), instanced rendering (60% performance boost), and cloudadaptive systems like Pixel Streaming ensure that even users on modest hardware can
experience next-generation content. Procedural Content Generation has simultaneously
evolved from simple random map generation to sophisticated AI-driven pipelines—integrating
Large Language Models allows developers to describe desired content in plain natural
language, with the engine handling implementation. This will drastically reduce game
development costs while enabling larger, more varied worlds than any manually-assembled
team could create.
E. Limitations and Future Research
Despite these advancements, several limitations persist across the reviewed literature. Ray
tracing’s hardware cost remains prohibitive for a significant portion of the gaming audience.
MetaHuman Creator, while dramatically faster than traditional pipelines, produces characters
that require additional customization to avoid the “uncanny valley” in close-up cinematic
contexts. Virtual production LED volumes require precise calibration to match digital and
physical lighting that demands specialist expertise not yet widely available. PCG systems
integrated with LLMs produce content that is structurally valid but may lack long-term
narrative coherence without additional human curation. Future research should prioritize
lightweight neural rendering for mobile and cloud deployment, cross-studio standardization of
virtual production calibration workflows, explainable AI modules for PCG creative
transparency, and accessibility tooling that lowers the Unreal Engine learning curve for
developers outside the AAA ecosystem.
