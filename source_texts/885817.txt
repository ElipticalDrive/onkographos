Abstract
To realize low-dose CT, many iterative reconstruction methods have been proposed, but
many iterations and high computational complexity are required for each scan. It is necessary
to reduce the number of iterations while effectively suppressing artifacts and noise caused by
reducing radiation dose for practical use. This study aims to accelerate the algorithm by
improving the Chambolle-Pock (CP) algorithm, the latest first-order primal-dual. First, we
introduce the Passty framework into the CP algorithm. The proposed CP becomes a rowaction type algorithm, enabling the incorporation of ordered subsets to accelerate the
algorithm further. Second, to step up with the latest trends, the regularization term (nonlocal
TV) is designed with a combined model of the first and second-order derivatives to preserve
smooth intensity changes. Third, we extend the proposed CP to the L1 and Huber datafidelity terms for more practical application. Most CT reconstruction studies employ the L2
data-fidelity term because of its good convergence. Even if the L1 data-fidelity term has
tremendous potential in noise immunity, it has a fatal drawback of poor convergence and is
not adopted at present. In the proposed CP, the L1 and Huber data-fidelity terms have
excellent image quality and convergence performance than the L2.
Keywords: Passty framework, Chambolle-Pock, nonlocal total variation, ordered subsets,
compressed sensing
1. Introduction
In low-dose CT, the more radiation dose is reduced, the more noise or artifacts are generated. The iterative reconstruction
method can improve image quality by building a precise mathematical model to minimize the cost function. Many iterative
methods have been proposed and applied to low-dose CT. Still, current iterative methods make the algorithm complicated to
handle the high degree of minimization and require many iterations 1–3. The main purpose of this study is to reduce the number
of iterations of the first-order algorithm that does not require inner iterations and to improve the performance of low-dose CT
reconstruction by designing a high-precision algorithm.
The Chambolle-Pock (CP) algorithm has been proposed as the latest algorithm of the first-order primal-dual 4,5. The CP
algorithm is an algorithm that uses proximal mapping to minimize both the data-fidelity term and the regularization term. It has
the great advantage of being able to flexibly deal with the minimization of the cost function, including the non-differentiable
ℓ1 data-fidelity term. However, the classical CP algorithm has an inevitable drawback. It was pointed out that the classical CP
algorithm assumes a standard iterative algorithm, such as the simultaneous iterative reconstruction technique (SIRT), which
requires a considerable number of iterations 6,7.
1
In this paper, we propose a new minimization framework for accelerating and improving the accuracy of CP algorithms.
Specifically, we focus on building a block-iterative type (row-action type) algorithm based on the Passty framework and
showing new possibilities for the non-differentiable ℓ1 data-fidelity term that is difficult to minimize with other major
algorithms such as the Split-Bregman 1,3,8 and FISTA 9–11.
First, we introduce the Passty framework 12 into the CP algorithm. To apply the Passty framework, the proposed CP algorithm
introduces distributed LASSO as a definition. The distributed LASSO-based cost function can be divided into multiblock
subfunctions in a one-direction unit of the projection data. The Passty framework allows the algorithm to converge by applying
proximity operators to multiblock subfunctions. This results in considerable benefits for optimization problems that appear in
CT image reconstruction. The structure of the final iterative algorithm becomes a row-action type algorithm concerning both
the data-fidelity and regularization terms, which enables the incorporation of ordered subsets to further accelerate the algorithm
2,6,7,9,13
.
Second, to step up with the latest trends, the regularization term (nonlocal total variation) is designed with a combined model
of the first and second-order derivatives to preserve smooth intensity changes. The first-order derivative is too sensitive to pixel
values, and even smooth intensity changes are falsely detected as edges, leading to staircase artifacts. Considering only the
second-order derivative, on the other hand, has the potential risk of image blurring problems due to the less ability to emphasize
edges. For these reasons, combined models of the first and second-order derivatives have recently become the mainstream to
overcome the image quality improvement limitations of regularization 7,8,14–19, which only considers the first 2,3,5,20 or secondorder derivatives 21,22.
Subsequently, we introduce a new weight that can be a perfect pair of second-order derivative nonlocal TV. Ordinary
nonlocal TV uses the weight of the nonlocal means filter (Buades et al., 2005), and the weights on the line centered on the pixel
of interest are assumed to be symmetric 24. However, in an actual image, the weights are asymmetric. This contradiction causes
errors in the optimization. The proposed weights are perfectly symmetric; thus, there is no such problem.
Third, we extend the proposed CP algorithm to the ℓ1 and Huber data-fidelity terms for more practical applications. In CT
image reconstruction studies using the CP 16,17,25, the ℓ22 norm is the most widely used for the data-fidelity term, which leads to
convergence of the overall algorithm. This is because the ℓ22 norm can be easily differentiated mathematically as the gradient
for convergence and produce a high-contrast image with good convergence. By contrast, although the ℓ1 data-fidelity term has
tremendous potential in noise immunity 26,27, it has a fatal drawback of poor convergence and is not adopted at present. However,
the proposed CP algorithm can overcome the convergence problem of the ℓ1 data-fidelity term by combining it with the Passty
framework. Furthermore, in this paper, we introduce the Huber data-fidelity term, which allows one term to compensate for the
weaknesses of ℓ22 and ℓ1 22,28.
In the ablation study, we design a particular experiment with impulse noise (salt-and-pepper noise) to further emphasize the
characteristics of the ℓ1 and Huber data-fidelity terms. The proposed CP (Passty CP) algorithm using the ℓ1 and Huber datafidelity terms can realize remarkable image quality improvement, especially for impulse noise.
2. Methods
The ordinary proximal splitting framework 29,30 can minimize the cost function by applying the proximity operator to each
subfunction only for a sum of two terms consisting of the data-fidelity and regularization terms. However, such an existing
framework cannot be applied to multiblock cost functions for the acceleration and refinement of the algorithm. To overcome
these limitations, in the proposed CP algorithm, we apply the Passty framework 12, which has a mathematical proof of
convergence for multi-operators. The proposed CP algorithm introduces a distributed LASSO based on its definition. The
distributed LASSO-based cost function can be divided into multiblock subfunctions in a one-direction unit of the projection
data. The Passty framework applies to multiblock subfunctions for primal and dual updates. Then, the proximity operators
corresponding to each multiblock subfunction are applied alternately, enabling a fast convergence 6,7.

2.3. Optimization for nonlocal TV
Nonlocal total variation minimization has been widely applied to the image reconstruction problem. Several nonlocal TV
regularization types (e.g., Tikhonov regularization and the Rudin-Osher-Fatemi (ROF) model) and algorithms for their
minimization have been introduced, showing impressive results in terms of image quality 3,7,8,20,24. Among them, the most
popular algorithms for nonlocal TV minimization are the Chambolle algorithm 31 and the Split-Bregman algorithm 1,32. Various
algorithms using the Chambolle or Split-Bregman approach have been proposed 33.
However, in CT image reconstruction, algorithms using the Chambolle or Split-Bregman approach have the following issues:
The Chambolle algorithm does not assume a data-fidelity term containing the system matrix for forward and back projection.
When the Chambolle algorithm is incorporated into the image reconstruction problem, the minimization of the data-fidelity
term should be considered separately, and additional inner iterations of the dual variable are needed to process the regularization
term 34. This result increases the complexity of the algorithm. The Split-Bregman was proposed as a second-order primal-dual
algorithm that enables a high degree of convergence. The Split-Bregman algorithm has an advantage over the Chambolle
algorithm in that it can be minimized empirically with a smaller number of iterations and is becoming a typical solution for
nonlocal TV minimization 3,8,33. However, in the case of the Split-Bregman algorithm, although it is possible to include the
system matrix in the data-fidelity term, the drawback is that the optimization becomes extremely complicated due to the Hessian
matrix, and additional inner iterations using Gauss-Seidel or CG methods are needed for minimization processing 1,2. For this
reason, a previous study 3 considered the minimization of the data-fidelity term separately, as in the case of Chambolle above.
The Chambolle-Pock (CP) algorithm was proposed as a first-order primal-dual that can overcome the aforementioned
problems 4,5. The CP algorithm is a first-order algorithm that considers the system matrix in the data-fidelity term and does not
require inner iterations, such as the Chambolle or the Split-Bregman algorithms mentioned above.

2.4. Applications to ℓ1 and Huber data-fidelity terms
In CT image reconstruction studies using CP 16,17,25, the ℓ22 norm is the most widely used for the data-fidelity term, which leads
to convergence of the overall algorithm. This is because the ℓ22 norm can be easily differentiated mathematically as the gradient
for convergence and produce a high-contrast image with good convergence. Under ideal conditions, such as sparse-view CT,
where the projection data are noise-free (i.e., 𝐴𝑢 = 𝑏), the ℓ22 data-fidelity term may be the best choice.
However, if the projection data are noisy (e.g., low-dose CT, 𝐴𝑢 = 𝑏 + 𝑛 ( 𝑛 is noise)), the ℓ22 data-fidelity term is
excessively sensitive to and emphasizes noise. By contrast, although the ℓ1 data-fidelity term is less sensitive to noise 26,27, it
produces a blurred image because the ℓ1 norm does not converge as well as the ℓ22 norm. Therefore, we introduce the Huber
data-fidelity term, which allows one term to compensate for the weaknesses of the other 22,28.

4. Discussion
By introducing the Passty framework and the ordered subsets into the CP algorithm, the proposed method converges within 30
iterations in both sparse-view CT and low-dose CT, as shown in Figs. 3, 4, and 5. Furthermore, we considered the Huber datafidelity term, which can compensate for the shortcomings of ℓ22 and ℓ1 . The difference in image quality is particularly clear in
the case of the impulse noise, as shown in Fig. 10. Additionally, we also confirmed the tendency of ℓ1 and Huber data-fidelity
term for Poisson noise and impulse noise, as shown in Figs. 6-11. Table 1 summarizes the properties of each data-fidelity term.
In addition to low-dose CT, the ℓ1 and Huber data-fidelity term can be helpful from a practical perspective, as there are many
cases in which the projection data contain noise or outliers 28.

5. Conclusion
We proposed the Passty framework of the CP algorithm to accelerate the algorithm. For more practical application, we extended
the proposed CP algorithm to the ℓ1 and Huber data-fidelity terms, and demonstrated its effectiveness in low-dose CT
(+impulse noise). Usually, the minimization process of the ℓ1 data-fidelity term is extremely complicated or impossible in other
major frameworks such as the Split-Bregman 1,3,8 and FISTA 9–11. However, using the Passty framework, the proposed CP
algorithm can be easily minimized as a first-order algorithm. As a future direction, we will consider its application to deep
learning. Recently, ADMM methods such as the Chambolle-Pock or Split-Bregman have been applied to deep learning 38,39.
We are also preparing for the next innovation.
