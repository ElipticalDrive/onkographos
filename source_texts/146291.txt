A BSTRACT
Feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction builds 3D scenes directly from input images
for novel view rendering, but learning Gaussian attributes from predicted geometry
for high-quality rendering under large viewpoint changes remains challenging. We
analyze limitations in Gaussian attribute learning and introduce InfiniSplat v2,
which improves rendering quality through architectural and training changes. Our
method decouples geometry and attribute prediction with a frozen geometry branch,
aligns predicted Gaussians using ground-truth camera poses during training, and
adopts large-angle target view supervision. Across six datasets, the feed-forward
outputs of the same trained model achieve state-of-the-art mean LPIPS and DISTS
among the evaluated methods in both pose-conditioned and pose-free settings.
In the pose-conditioned setting, mean LPIPS and DISTS decrease by 25.8% and
32.9%, respectively, compared with the strongest evaluated baselines. We further
find that opacity learned with only image reconstruction supervision suppresses
geometrically inconsistent points. Project page: https://zju3dv.github.
io/InfiniSplat2.

I NTRODUCTION
Image-based 3D scene reconstruction aims to recover a renderable 3D scene from input images to
support novel view rendering for applications such as simulation, content creation, and autonomous
driving. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al., 2023) enables efficient rendering through an
explicit scene representation. Building on this representation, recent feed-forward methods (Charatan
et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024a; Zhang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025b) directly predict Gaussian scenes
from input images, eliminating the need for per-scene representation optimization at inference time.
Pretrained multi-view geometry models (Wang et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025a;
Yang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026d) provide a useful starting point for constructing
renderable scenes from images. They predict depth maps and cameras for each input view. Backprojecting these depth maps with the corresponding camera estimates into a common coordinate
frame yields a predicted point cloud. This point cloud captures coherent scene geometry overall, but
typically also contains spatially isolated clusters or clusters that are inconsistent across views. Such
issues become particularly apparent after 3D aggregation, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
Existing feed-forward methods (Lin et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Team et al., 2026)
combine geometry prediction from multiple views with Gaussian attribute prediction to construct
renderable scenes from input images. For example, DA3-GS (Lin et al., 2025) learns Gaussian
attributes on top of the geometry predicted by DA3. However, as shown in Figs. 3 and 4, renderings
from DA3-GS and the other evaluated methods still exhibit blur, ghosting, and missing detail. Our
work focuses on using these imperfect geometry predictions to construct Gaussian scenes that support
high-quality novel view rendering.
As shown in Fig. 1, InfiniSplat v2 reconstructs Gaussian scenes from predicted multi-view geometry
and supports high-quality novel-view rendering at extreme viewpoints. We identify key factors that
affect Gaussian learning and introduce three corresponding architectural and training improvements.
First, we investigate why prior methods struggle to eliminate flying-point artifacts and find that jointly
optimizing geometry and Gaussian attributes leads to geometric degradation. We therefore redesign
the architecture to decouple geometry prediction from Gaussian attribute prediction while keeping the
geometry branch frozen. Second, we find that using predicted camera poses during training (Lin et al.,
2025) introduces pose errors. Under large-angle supervision, these errors can misguide the network
during training. We therefore use GT camera poses to align the predicted Gaussians. Third, inspired
by InfiniSplat’s strong performance under large viewpoint changes (Wang et al., 2026b), we introduce
large-angle target view supervision for feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction from arbitrary input
views. Our experiments show that these three improvements enhance novel view rendering quality
under large viewpoint changes, even when the predicted point clouds contain numerous outliers.
We evaluate the same trained model on six datasets, both with and without camera pose inputs. Our
method achieves the lowest mean LPIPS and DISTS across the six datasets among the evaluated baselines in both settings. With camera pose inputs, our feed-forward reconstruction reduces LPIPS and
DISTS by 25.8% and 32.9%, respectively, relative to the strongest evaluated baseline for each metric.
Additionally, we find that, with only image reconstruction supervision, the trainable attribute branch
learns to assign lower opacity to outliers that are geometrically inconsistent across views. These points
consequently contribute less to the rendered images, reducing visible artifacts in novel views (Fig. 7).
Our contributions are threefold:
• We introduce InfiniSplat v2, a feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction method for arbitrary
input views. It freezes the geometry branch to decouple geometry prediction from Gaussian
attribute prediction, aligns predicted Gaussians using GT camera poses during training, and
adopts large-angle target view supervision.
• We evaluate the method on six datasets in both pose-conditioned and pose-free settings. The
feed-forward outputs of the same trained model achieve the lowest mean LPIPS and DISTS
among the evaluated baselines in both settings.
• We find that opacity learned with only image reconstruction supervision selectively suppresses the rendering contributions of geometrically inconsistent points across views.

R ELATED W ORK
2.1
F EED -F ORWARD 3D G EOMETRY E STIMATION
Geometry foundation models recover scene structure from images and provide a geometric basis
for feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction. DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024), MASt3R (Leroy et al.,
2024), VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), Fast3R (Yang et al., 2025), and π 3 (Wang et al., 2026d) advance
learned geometric reconstruction. Depth Anything 3 (DA3) (Lin et al., 2025) reconstructs geometry
from varying numbers of images, with or without supplied poses. VGGT-Long (Deng et al., 2025),
VGG-T3 (Elflein et al., 2026), and Scal3R (Xie et al., 2026) extend learned reconstruction to longer
sequences and larger scenes. These predictions provide a useful starting point, but constructing
renderable Gaussian scenes also requires learning attributes such as opacity, scale, and appearance.
2.2
F EED -F ORWARD G AUSSIAN S PLATTING
Generalizable view synthesis learns to predict novel views or renderable representations across
scenes (Wang et al., 2021; Hong et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024). Feed-forward Gaussian methods
pursue this goal by directly predicting explicit Gaussian scenes. SHARP (Mescheder et al., 2026)
and InfiniSplat (Wang et al., 2026b) reconstruct scenes from a single image, while GS-LRM (Zhang
et al., 2024) combines multiple views. NoPoSplat (Ye et al., 2025) and FLARE (Zhang et al.,
2025) support reconstruction without supplied camera poses. Long-LRM (Ziwen et al., 2025) and
tttLRM (Wang et al., 2026a) further extend reconstruction to long input sequences. Work on Gaussian
representations also explores different ways to organize and predict primitives: C3G (An et al., 2025)
and TokenGS (Ren et al., 2026) use learnable queries or tokens, while VolSplat (Wang et al., 2025b)
predicts voxel-aligned Gaussians.
More directly related to our work is how geometry is estimated and used for Gaussian prediction.
pixelSplat (Charatan et al., 2024) establishes cross-view correspondences through epipolar attention,
while MVSplat (Chen et al., 2024a) estimates depth using multi-view cost volumes. DepthSplat (Xu
et al., 2025b) augments multi-view depth estimation with pretrained monocular depth features.
Learned geometric priors also support Gaussian prediction in AnySplat (Jiang et al., 2025), WorldMirror (Liu et al., 2025; Team et al., 2026), and DA3-GS (Lin et al., 2025). Building on such priors,
SplatAnything (Anonymous, 2027) scales up training with diverse synthetic and real-world data.
QuerySplat (Li et al., 2026) separately decodes geometry and appearance from frozen geometric
features. Our work focuses on learning Gaussian attributes from imperfect predicted point clouds to
support high-quality novel view rendering.
3
After the initial prediction, learned refinement uses rendering feedback to update the Gaussian scene.
G3R (Chen et al., 2024b) and the refinement module of Flux4D (Wang et al., 2026c) use explicit
rendering gradients to guide learned updates. ReSplat (Xu et al., 2025a) instead uses feature-space
rendering errors without explicit gradient feedback. Our refiner uses input-view rendering gradients
to learn updates to Gaussian attributes while keeping centers fixed.

M ETHOD
Overview. Given input images and optional camera conditions, InfiniSplat v2 constructs a point
cloud from predicted depth maps and corresponding cameras, then learns Gaussian attributes for
its points to regulate their rendering contributions. A frozen DA3 branch provides the depth and
camera estimates, while a trainable attribute branch adapts to rendering through image reconstruction
supervision (Fig. 2). We then refine the scene attributes using input-view rendering feedback.

3.2
F EED -F ORWARD G AUSSIAN R ECONSTRUCTION
Our goal is to improve Gaussian attributes through image reconstruction supervision without compensating for attribute errors by moving point positions. We therefore freeze the geometry branch and
keep Gaussian centers fixed at the back-projected point positions. Constructing points with predicted
input poses while supervising them from GT target poses can also introduce camera-induced residuals;
we use scale-aligned GT cameras for both operations during training. Finally, targets near the input
viewing directions may leave Gaussian shape insufficiently constrained. We favor large-angle target
views to constrain scales and orientations through projections from different directions. Figure 2
illustrates the resulting training pipeline.

attribute branch produces multi-layer patch features:

3.4
T RAINING AND I NFERENCE
Training. In Stage I, both branches receive input RGB and GT pose conditions. We supervise input
and sampled target renderings with equally weighted masked L1 and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018)
losses, updating only the attribute branch’s last K Transformer blocks and Gaussian decoder. Stage II
freezes the reconstructor and trains the feature projection and refiner. Input-view losses provide
feedback, while input and disjoint target views supervise the updated scene.
Inference. The same trained model supports pose-conditioned and pose-free inference. In the
pose-conditioned setting, we construct centers using the supplied intrinsics and poses, rescaling
their translations to match the normalized predicted depths. In the pose-free setting, we use the
geometry branch’s predicted cameras, normalizing their translations by snorm . In either setting, these
cameras also render the input views for refinement feedback. The refiner updates scene attributes
while network weights remain fixed.

losses, and training hyperparameters.

E XPERIMENTS
4.1
E XPERIMENTAL S ETUP
Datasets and baselines. We train InfiniSplat v2 on a mixture of Hypersim (Roberts et al., 2021),
Raw3D (Team, 2026), and ScanNet++ (Yeshwanth et al., 2023), using disjoint scenes for training and
validation. We evaluate on six datasets: DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024), ETH3D (Schops et al., 2017),
NRGBD (Azinović et al., 2022), Mip-NeRF 360 (Barron et al., 2022), RealEstate10K (RE10K) (Zhou
et al., 2018), and DTU (Aanæs et al., 2016). We compare with tttLRM (Wang et al., 2026a),
AnySplat (Jiang et al., 2025), C3G (An et al., 2025), DA3-GS (Lin et al., 2025), DepthSplat (Xu
et al., 2025b), QuerySplat (Li et al., 2026), ReSplat (Xu et al., 2025a), TokenGS (Ren et al., 2026),
VolSplat (Wang et al., 2025b), and WorldMirror 2.0 (Team et al., 2026).
Evaluation protocol. We use N ∈ {4, 8, 16} input views and four held-out target views per
example, with the same view splits for all methods. We evaluate methods separately in poseconditioned and pose-free settings. We use the same trained model for both settings. Ours denotes
the feed-forward output, and Ours∗ includes three learned refinement steps. Evaluation-camera
alignment is separate from reconstruction and uses only input-camera correspondences. Following
SHARP (Mescheder et al., 2026), we use LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) and DISTS (Ding et al., 2020)
as our primary metrics for perceptual quality. Within each dataset, scores are computed on the same
target views and valid regions. Per-dataset scores average equally over the three input-view counts,
and the overall mean weights the six datasets equally. The complete camera and scoring protocol is
provided in the appendix.

4.3
A BLATIONS AND A NALYSIS
We evaluate attribute-backbone fine-tuning (A), geometry freezing (G), GT-camera backprojection
(C), and large-angle supervision (V) on ETH3D and DTU, averaging pre-refinement results over
N = 4, 8, 16 input views. We additionally evaluate learned positional offsets (w/ O). Ours uses
K = 10; w/o A uses K = 0.
Under image reconstruction supervision, geometry fine-tuning (w/o G) and learned offsets (w/ O)
worsen both metrics on ETH3D and DTU (Table 2). Freezing geometry instead of fine-tuning
reduces LPIPS by 16.6% and 23.2%, respectively. We interpret this degradation as positional updates
compensating for attribute errors, a pathway blocked by fixed centers. With the geometry backbone
frozen, w/ O predicts 3D offsets to back-projected centers through the Gaussian head, producing
local blur and depth distortions in the NRGBD kitchen example (Fig. 5).
Fine-tuning the last K = 10 attribute-backbone blocks, compared with K = 0 (w/o A), reduces
LPIPS by 1.1% on ETH3D and 20.8% on DTU, with lower DISTS on both datasets. GT-camera
backprojection reduces DTU LPIPS by 36.1% relative to predicted cameras (w/o C). Large-angle
target view supervision improves both LPIPS and DISTS on ETH3D and DTU compared with local
interpolation-view supervision (w/o V).

DATA AND V IEW S AMPLING
Training data and inputs. Both stages sample Hypersim (Roberts et al., 2021), Raw3D (Team,
2026), and ScanNet++ (Yeshwanth et al., 2023) with probabilities of 60%, 10%, and 30%, using
disjoint training and validation scenes. Each sample contains four target views and N ∈ {4, . . . , 12}
input views. The processed image’s longer side is 1022 pixels for N ≤ 8, 756 for N = 9, 10, and
518 for N = 11, 12.
Target-view sampling. We rank non-input views by decreasing minimum optical-axis angle a(v, C)
(Eq. 5), assigning the upper and lower halves to large- and small-angle pools. Each sample selects
the large-angle pool with probability 0.7 or the small-angle pool with probability 0.3, then draws four
target views uniformly without replacement. The selected pool must contain at least four candidates.
Figure 8 shows the sampled angular distributions.
Training masks. Target supervision uses visibility masks constructed from GT depth. A target
pixel must have finite GT depth greater than 10−6 and pass the supplied validity mask. After backprojection, it is retained if at least one input view places it in front of the camera and within the image
bounds, has valid sampled depth, and satisfies
|zproj − dsrc | ≤ max(τabs , τrel dsrc ).
(8)
Here zproj is the point depth in that input camera and dsrc is the sampled depth. The mask function
defaults to τabs = 0.03 and τrel = 0.01.
Local-view control. The w/o V variant samples local interpolation views between input views,
retaining target supervision and the full model’s mask construction.
Evaluation views.

Initialization and rendering. Both branches are initialized from DA3NESTED-GIANT-LARGE1.1 with independent parameters. Feature projection and a DPT decoder predict Gaussian attributes.
Voxel merging uses a side length of 0.004 in the scale-normalized reconstruction frame. During
training and inference, Gaussians with opacity below τα = 0.01 are pruned after merging and before
refinement. All experiments use gsplat with eps2d=10−2 .
Scale alignment and point construction. Camera alignment applies only a scale factor, not the
full similarity transform. Predicted and GT poses are expressed relative to their respective reference
cameras, using the first input view in sorted order. Predicted depths and translations are normalized
by snorm > 0 from Eq. 3. Using only input-camera center correspondences, we fit an Umeyama
similarity transform (Umeyama, 1991) from the GT trajectory to the normalized predicted trajectory
and retain its scale sC . This scale applies to both input and target translations, preserving GT intrinsics
and reference-relative rotations:

convolution, merged using the Gaussian voxel groups and weights, and reused across steps. A linear

A.6
C AMERA P ROTOCOL AND E VALUATION M ETRICS
Camera inputs and rendering. Pose-conditioned and pose-free results are reported and ranked
separately. In the pose-free setting, reference input cameras are not reconstruction inputs; they only
establish the evaluation reference frame and estimate translation scale from input-camera correspondences. The scene is constructed before evaluation-camera alignment and remains unchanged.
Target poses define rendering queries without entering the fit. Alignment rescales reference-relative
translations only, rather than applying a full Sim(3) transform. For methods predicting intrinsics,
target focal lengths are the component-wise median of predicted input focal lengths, scaled to the
target resolution, with the reference principal point retained. In the pose-conditioned setting, reference
input cameras are available for reconstruction and target intrinsics are fixed to GT. Evaluation target
RGB is never used for reconstruction or refinement feedback.
Metrics and aggregation. We evaluate novel view rendering with PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS.
Both settings average equally at each successive level: target views within each example, examples
within each scene, scenes, the three input configurations, and the six datasets.

B.4
O PACITY AND G EOMETRIC C ONSISTENCY
We analyze feed-forward outputs on held-out Hypersim (Roberts et al., 2021) validation
scenes. Geometric labels are assigned independently of opacity using GT depth, with tolerance
max(0.03 m, 0.01d) and depth-edge samples excluded. A point is geometrically inconsistent if it
violates GT-observed free space in at least two views and agrees with no observed surface depth.
Surface-consistent points agree with at least two views and have no free-space violations; others
remain uncertain. This identifies 36,125 inconsistent points. Opacity distributions and pruning
statistics at threshold 0.01 are shown in Fig. 7.
