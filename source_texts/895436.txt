Implicit fields are now meshed by restricted Delaunay refinement instead of dual contouring, and constructive recipes can hold them as leaves.
The stack, bottom up:
exedra_math owns the exact-sign predicates (orient2d, orient3d, incircle, plane_side, moved from exedra_triangulate). It adds insphere and the weighted power test, both exact for every finite input, through one shared dyadic accumulator. orient3d now stays exact through underflow.
exedra_tetrahedralize (new, no_std) is an incremental 3D Delaunay and regular tetrahedralization built from an exact grid. It provides weighted insertion with hidden vertices, nearby walk starts, stars, spaced insertion, stats and validate.
exedra_implicit (new, no_std) defines each field once as a Formula over any Number. From that one formula come values, exact gradients, outward-rounded bounds, gradient bounds and crease ties. ScalarField is the consumer boundary, and Expr describes fields as data. It includes the built-in shapes, profiles, combinations, smooth blends and placements.
exedra_isosurface is now delaunay_mesh alone: DelPSC-style protection of traced creases and corners, and refinement until faces are well shaped, close to the surface, and each vertex's faces close into a disk. DelaunayReport accounts for passes, samples, refinements by reason, field queries, and anything left unmet. Dual contouring, the f32 fields, their ADRs and their benchmarks are removed.
exedra_constructive gains NodeKind::Implicit leaves. They are meshed at EvalPolicy::implicit resolution, with creases marked sharp, tagged regions carried as face regions, reports carried into GeometryReport::implicit_meshes, and an eval.implicit.incomplete warning. Leaves are fingerprinted and round-trip through text and JSON interchange.
exedra_fidget, the Fidget and render benchmarks, and the Boolean oracle move onto exedra_implicit. The facade gains an implicit namespace.
At 32 cells across:
A drilled and notched block meshes in 0.3 s, with no angle below 19°.
Four fins 0.4 of a cell thick mesh in 0.8 s, complete, with both edges of each fin sharp.
A gyroid sheet cut by a ball meshes in under a second.
All of these are closed and manifold.
Known limits, reported through DelaunayReport rather than hidden:
Knife edges of about 10° or sharper, and the rims of a cut gyroid can be left with unmet faces.
Crease spacing is conservative, so heavily creased scenes at fine resolution sample more than they need. A 64-cube lattice at 128 cells takes about 10 s.
TypeScript wiring of implicit leaves comes later. This replaces the dual-contouring work in #126.

```
The predicate notes move from the triangulation README to `exedra_math`, and
now credit Shewchuk's paper and public-domain `predicates.c` for the filter
bounds and expansion arithmetic.
```

math: keep orient3d exact through underflow
…
f5b618d
```
The exact fallback summed triple products of the original coordinates as
Shewchuk expansions, which are exact only while no product underflows. A
tetrahedron with subnormal edges therefore read as coplanar, contradicting
the exact-sign contract.

The fallback now has two stages. When subtracting the fourth point loses
nothing, as for grid-aligned or exactly coplanar input, Shewchuk's adaptive
middle stage expands six exact triple products of the differences: the
common and cheap case. Otherwise the 24 monomials of the original
coordinates are summed in the dyadic accumulator, which covers every finite
input. The filter defers whenever a product underflows or its bound is
subnormal, and non-finite input returns `Coplanar` explicitly rather than by
accident of NaN comparisons.
```
math: add an exact-sign insphere predicate
…
2456ffc
```
`insphere` classifies a point against the circumsphere of four others, the
test incremental 3D Delaunay construction decides every cavity with. It has
the same shape as `incircle`: Shewchuk's floating-point filter and error
bound from `predicates.c`, deferring whenever a product underflows, then an
exact fallback that expands the lifted 5×5 determinant into 360 five-factor
monomials of the original coordinates and sums them in the shared dyadic
accumulator. The sign is exact for every finite input, including subnormal
and overflowing scales. `insphere_evaluated` reports which path decided.

Tests cover one-ULP displacements around exact ties, extreme scales, the
non-finite sentinel, and agreement with an independent `i128` determinant.
```
math: add an exact power test for weighted points
…
a1e9bbc
```
`insphere_weighted` decides whether a weighted point has negative power
distance to the sphere orthogonal to four others, the conflict test of
regular (weighted Delaunay) tetrahedralizations. Weighted points let a
mesher place protecting balls on sharp creases, and power diagrams serve
volumetric representations as well.

`insphere` and the weighted test share one implementation. The filter is
Shewchuk's, with each lift reduced by its point's weight and each weight
entering the permanent by magnitude; the weighted bound carries half again
the slack for the extra subtraction. The exact path is the lifted
determinant with each weight subtracted from its point's lift, adding 24
monomials per point to the same accumulator.

Tests check how weights move the orthosphere, exact cancellation of equal
weights, agreement with `insphere` at zero weight, and an independent `i128`
power test.
```

tetrahedralize: add incremental 3D Delaunay tetrahedralization
…
2a2fb54
```
`exedra_tetrahedralize` builds a Delaunay tetrahedralization of a rectilinear
grid and grows it by Bowyer–Watson insertion, deciding everything with the
exact `orient3d` and `insphere` predicates. It is the 3D sibling of
`exedra_triangulate`: points in, tetrahedra with explicit adjacency out, no
knowledge of meshes or fields, `no_std` + `alloc`, deterministic.

Grid cells are exact axis-aligned boxes whose corners are exactly cospherical,
so their Freudenthal split is exactly Delaunay without jitter or symbolic
perturbation. Walks start at the nearest grid vertex through a per-vertex
tetrahedron table, which keeps location short in any insertion order. Points
on the hull split hull faces, coincident points return the existing vertex,
and points outside the hull are refused. Tetrahedra are positively oriented
and faces are wound outward, so a consumer can read a consistently oriented
surface between two classes of tetrahedra directly.

`stats` reports walk, cavity and predicate work; `validate` checks
orientation, adjacency, local Delaunay faces and the vertex table.
```
tetrahedralize: insert weighted points
…
79015da
```
`insert_weighted` gives a point a weight, a squared radius, and keeps the
tetrahedralization regular (weighted Delaunay) with the exact power test.
Unweighted tetrahedralizations keep the cheaper `insphere` until the first
nonzero weight arrives.

A point whose power cell would be empty is refused as `TetError::Hidden`. A
heavy point can hide existing vertices; they leave every tetrahedron, keep
their `VertexId`, and report `is_hidden`, and walks that would start from a
hidden grid vertex fall back to the last insertion. `stats` counts hidden
points and vertices, and `validate` checks the weighted Delaunay property and
that hidden vertices belong to no tetrahedron.

Weights let a mesher place protecting balls on sharp features, where
refinement must not cut between neighboring feature samples.
```

```
Refinement inserts points it computes, and a point almost on an existing

`insert_spaced` inserts like `insert_near` unless a vertex lies closer
than a given spacing, and then returns `TetError::Crowded` with the

found; the cavity is now grown before the point is recorded, and a
refused point leaves no trace but a count in `stats().crowded_points`.
With weights the nearest vertex need not be a neighbor, and only
neighbors are checked.
```

implicit: add fields written once as formulas
…
a0d7107
```
A field is a function of space, negative inside a solid, whose zero set
is its surface. Consumers ask a field several questions: values,
gradients, bounds over a box, bounds on the gradient, where its creases
lie, and how its values relate to distance. Writing each answer by hand
for every shape multiplies the work by every new question, and a
composite only answers what all its parts answer.

`exedra_implicit` defines a field once, as a `Formula` over any `Number`:
`f64` for values, `Dual` for exact gradients by forward differentiation,
`Interval`, rounded outward, for bounds over a box, `Dual<Interval>` for
bounds on the gradient, and an internal number that follows each `min`,
`max` and `abs` to the creases they mark. `Number` has no comparisons,
so a formula cannot branch on a value; `select` joins pieces that meet
smoothly without marking a crease, and `hypot` keeps gradients bounded
where a length vanishes.

`ScalarField` is the boundary consumers use, in `f64`: batched values and
gradients, value and gradient bounds over a region, the two creases
nearest a point, and claims. Every formula is one, with value bounds the
tightest of the natural interval form, the mean-value form and its
Lipschitz claim. Fields that cannot be formulas implement it directly,
the defaults answering the rest soundly from their claims, and join
formulas through `Opaque`, which answers each number from the field's
own queries, through the chain rule where the formula moves it.

A crease is reported as a `Tie`: a function zero where two pieces meet,
with how far away it counts as. A side a `min` or `max` does not choose
keeps its own ties, counted as no nearer than the tie to the side that
was chosen, so at a corner where three faces meet both creases nearest
it are found from any face.

`Claims` state a `Metric` and a `Support`. Shapes state theirs from
their checked parameters, and combinators derive theirs from their
parts'. The built-ins are the shapes `Sphere`, `Cuboid`, `Cylinder`,
`Torus`, `HalfSpace`, `Capsule` and `RoundCone`; the profiles `Circle`,
`Rectangle` and `HalfPlane`, lifted by `Extrude` and `Revolve`; the
combinations `Union`, `Intersection`, `Difference`, `SmoothUnion` and
`SmoothUnionN`, a symmetric blend of any number of parts that skips
those whose claims put them out of reach; and `Translate`, `Scale` and
`Placed`. The crate is `no_std` and depends only on `exedra_math`.
```

isosurface: mesh fields by restricted Delaunay refinement
…
99e2f2e
```
Dual contouring places one vertex per cell, so its triangles follow the
grid: slivers where the surface grazes a cell, folds where cell sizes
meet, and creases cut short wherever two surfaces meet at an acute
angle. `delaunay_mesh` meshes an `exedra_implicit` field from its
restricted Delaunay triangulation instead, refined until every triangle
is well shaped and close to the surface.

A grid of signs over the bounds seeds points where cell edges cross the
surface, points on creases, and points on features thinner than a cell,
found by probing cells the field's bounds cannot prove empty; blocks of
cells whose bounds exclude zero are skipped whole. Creases are traced
from their seeds with steps as long as the crease stays within the
distance tolerance and turns less than the turning limit, stopping at
corners, which are solved where the field and two ties are zero.
Tracing stops only at samples of the same crease, told apart by their
ties, so both sides of a fin thinner than a ball are traced; probes
around each corner then find every crease leaving it, so an edge too
short for the grid to seed is traced from corner to corner too. As in
Cheng, Dey and Levine's DelPSC, each crease sample becomes a weighted
vertex protected by a ball overlapping its neighbors, spaced so that no
ball reaches another crease and neighboring balls differ by at most half.

In the regular tetrahedralization of crease samples and seeds, each
tetrahedron takes the field's sign at its orthocenter; the faces between
inside and outside tetrahedra are the surface. A face too large, too
badly shaped, too far from the surface or folded is refined where its
dual edge meets the surface, found by one Newton step along the face
normal and confirmed by the gradient there, falling back to a bracketed
search; points are kept apart with `insert_spaced`. Faces bad in size or
shape alone are judged in full only when their turn comes, and a face's
surface point is kept while its dual edge still reaches it. Every
vertex's faces must close into one disk. A face whose point falls in a
protecting ball, or a protected vertex whose faces do not close, shows
the ball is too large: that stretch of crease is split, or a corner's
gap shrunk, and the pass starts again; the last pass refines to the end.

`DelaunayParams::new` derives every length from the cell size.
`DelaunayResult` holds `f64` positions and outward-wound triangles, with
`mesh` building an Exedra mesh, and `DelaunayReport` counts passes,
crease samples and corners, refinements by reason, field queries, and
anything left unmet, with `is_complete` summarizing. Identical inputs
give identical meshes.

At 32 cells across, a drilled and notched block meshes in 0.3 s with no
angle below 19 degrees; a gyroid sheet cut by a ball in under a second;
four fins 0.4 of a cell thick in 0.8 s, complete, with the edges on both
sides of each fin sharp; all closed and manifold.
```

```
`exedra_fidget` implements `exedra_implicit::ScalarField`. Fidget
evaluates in single precision, so points round to `f32` and interval
bounds are taken over the box rounded outward and widened by one `f32`
step. Specialization stays as `FidgetField::specialize`, since no
extractor consumed the trait it implemented.

The Fidget and render benchmarks mesh the toothed torus with
`delaunay_mesh`. The oracle's field witness composes `exedra_implicit`
fields, now in `f64`, and its fixed suite meshes `Tagged` box and
cylinder combinations, requiring every vertex where the two parts' faces
meet to lie on both surfaces.

The facade gains an `implicit` namespace, which `isosurface` selects.
```
isosurface: retire dual contouring and the f32 fields
…
a7d87b9
```
Fields now live in `exedra_implicit`, and restricted Delaunay meshing
replaces dual contouring as the way to mesh them: it keeps creases and
corners sharp without fitting QEFs or projecting onto known primitives,
closes a disk around every vertex, and labels faces with the regions of
tagged parts.

Gone are the f32 `ScalarField` and `ScalarField2d` with their
specialization and provenance traits, the analytic, profile, skeletal
and transform fields, dual contouring with its octree, Hermite data, QEF
solver, transitions and semi-analytic projection, their ADRs, and the
isosurface wind tunnel and QEF benchmarks that measured them.
`exedra_isosurface` is now the Delaunay extractor alone.

The open ticket on manifold dual contouring closes as superseded.
```
implicit: describe fields as data
…
8b5014b
```
`Expr` is a field as a value: a tree of the built-in shapes, `ProfileExpr`
profiles, combinations, placements and tags, each variant holding the
type it names over boxed sub-expressions. It evaluates exactly as those
types do, so values, gradients, bounds, creases, regions and claims carry
over, and it can be built at run time, compared and serialized by a
consumer.

Rebuilding a field from its own parameters now gives the same field: a
unit direction and an already rigid placement are kept exactly, rather
than normalized again, which could move them by an ulp.
```
constructive: add implicit-field leaves
…
ef0cb7f
```
`NodeKind::Implicit { field, bounds, placement }` holds a solid as an
`exedra_implicit::Expr` in its local frame. Evaluation meshes it by
restricted Delaunay refinement within `bounds`, or the box the field's
claims bound it by, at `EvalPolicy::implicit` resolution: `cells` along
the longest side, 32 by default, so one policy suits leaves in any units.
The mesher's `f64` positions are placed before the mesh narrows them, so
a field far from the origin placed near it keeps its precision. Creases
are marked sharp, and faces carry the regions of `Tagged` parts as face
regions and `Feature::ImplicitRegion` provenance.

The builder refuses bounds that are not a finite, ordered box, and fields
nesting deeper than `MAX_IMPLICIT_NESTING`, which keeps every accepted
leaf within what the interchange readers take.

Each leaf's meshing report rides with its body, so cache hits replay it,
and appears in `GeometryReport::implicit_meshes`; an incomplete mesh adds
an `eval.implicit.incomplete` warning. Fingerprints encode the field's
tree and parameters, the policy fingerprint covers the new policy, and
text and JSON interchange round-trip the field through its constructors.
The frozen JSON corpus gains the leaf, the hostile-input suite builds and
meshes hostile fields, and the gallery adds a bracket with a blended boss
and a bore.
```
