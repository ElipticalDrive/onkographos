Lets a scene be lit by an HDR panorama, a real photographed sky or one drawn in code, with importance sampling so a bright sun in it doesn't make the image noisy.
Library
EnvironmentMap: an equirectangular panorama of linear radiance, centred on −z.
EnvironmentMap.procedural(w, h, (direction) => radiance) draws one in code.
fromHDR(bytes) and load(url) read Radiance .hdr files, flat or run-length encoded.
sample(d) is the bilinear lookup.
Sky.environment(map, strength, rotation), plus options for with(). The environment replaces the gradient, and a separate sun can still go on top. sky.radiance(d) gives the sky's radiance on the CPU.
Upload: the map goes up as an rgba16float texture, sampled with wrapping round the horizon and clamping at the poles. Its importance table goes up as an r32float texture: the panorama in at most 512 × 256 cells, weighted by luminance × sin θ, with a cumulative distribution across each row and one down the rows. Neither counts against the 8-storage-buffer limit.
Shader: the environment is one more choice for next event estimation. sample_env() binary-searches the table, and env_pdf() gives the density per steradian. BSDF rays that escape are MIS-weighted against it.
Uniforms: env_strength and env_rotation take the two spare slots, inside the block that restarts the image.
Demo: three HDR environments drawn in code, each with its own scene:
Studio: chrome and glass (Classics): softboxes reflected all over a chrome trefoil knot, glass, gold, red and white balls, on a dark glossy floor.
Alpine meadow (Places): sun, cumulus and snowy peaks, reflected in a chrome ball and seen through a glass one.
Sunset on the water (Places): a low sun through a glass vase, and its path across the water.
The demo can't download real HDRIs from this environment, but EnvironmentMap.load() takes any .hdr file.
Tests
Unit:
texture coordinates and directions round-trip;
.hdr files written by the test read back, flat and run-length encoded;
the importance table's pdf integrates to 1, its samples cluster where the light is, and it reports its own pdf;
a black panorama is safe;
Sky.radiance turns and scales the map.
Browser:
the GPU's view of the bare sky matches Sky.radiance with the rotation;
a diffuse floor's brightness matches albedo/π × the irradiance integrated from the panorama, within 1.5%. Breaking the environment's pdf on purpose makes this test fail by 19%.
Uniform offsets and scene checks are updated, and the type usage test covers the new API.
npm test: 155 passing locally.

EnvironmentMap holds an equirectangular panorama of linear radiance,
drawn by a function or read from a Radiance .hdr file (flat or run-length
encoded). A Sky can light the scene with one, scaled and turned, in place
of its gradient. The renderer uploads it as a half-float texture with a
table of cumulative distributions, by luminance times sin(theta), and
next event estimation picks directions from it in proportion to their
light, MIS-weighted against BSDF rays that escape to it, so even a small
bright sun in a panorama lights the scene with little noise. The CPU
mirrors the lookup, sampling and pdf, and tests check them against each
other and the GPU, and a floor's light against the exact irradiance.

The demo gets three environments drawn in code (a studio, an alpine day
and a sunset) and a scene for each.
