Plan after walking a long way and back (12878 chunks held, 6233 rendered, 32.6 M triangles, 19 fps; ~5k triangles per chunk, mostly alpha-masked canopy voxels).
Bands by distance from the camera per chunk column, with hysteresis:
near (to ~12 blocks): current full detail;
mid (to ~40): terrain as a one-vertex-per-tile heightfield coloured from the top voxel; trees re-rasterised at a quarter of the voxel resolution, greedy-merged, opaque leaves (no cutout prepass or overdraw); plants and tufts dropped;
far (to the retention edge): four-tile heightfield with a canopy layer (surface raised by tree height, foliage colour where trees stand);
beyond: the horizon.
Instancing: at mid range drop the seed, so a tree is one of a few canonical crowns per species scaled to DF's height and radius; identical mesh+material entities batch in Bevy. The cache holds tiles, not meshes, so every band is re-meshed on demand from what is on disk.
First step, cheapest and largest: canopy raster resolution and opaque leaves by distance, canopy chunks only. Measure triangles and fps at the same framing before and after.

Engine-side pieces to use rather than build: Bevy's GPU occlusion culling (OcclusionCulling on the camera with the DepthPrepass we already have; two-phase depth-pyramid test per mesh) and VisibilityRange per entity for the near/mid/far swap with dithered crossfade. Choose the band by projected tile size on screen (pixels per tile at that distance), not raw distance, the Nanite rule at chunk granularity. Quake-style precomputed visibility does not fit an open, growing world.

Tree LOD chain, chosen by projected size per tree or chunk: (1) full voxels, cutout leaves; (2) quarter-resolution voxels, opaque leaves (this issue's first step); (3) one canonical crown per species, a few dozen triangles, instanced (#31 builds it for the horizon; the fine bands reuse it); (4) a green box, twelve triangles, instanced; (5) nothing per tree: canopy colour and height baked into the coarse heightfield. Visibility ranges carry stages 1 to 4 as meshes on the same chunk or instance; stage 5 is the heightfield's own.

Design (user): LOD as one detail scalar on the L-system rather than hand-built stages. detail in 0..1 maps to raster resolution (4..1 voxels/tile), grammar depth (full..trunk plus one or two crown clusters), leaf cluster count and porosity, with porosity compensated so mean colour and see-through fraction hold along the ramp. The band list becomes samples along the ramp with crossfades between neighbours; the bottom sample at 1 voxel/tile greedy-merged is the box crown, so the manual box builder (crown.rs) becomes a fallback or goes. Every sample is a cached mesh per tree (window) or per preset variant (horizon).

VRAM (user): a finer ramp costs resident meshes per band. Mitigation is residency by distance rather than fewer samples: build a chunk's band mesh lazily when the camera comes within that band's range plus a margin, evict it when the camera leaves (hysteresis), regenerate from the cached skeleton on return. The near band is ~5k triangles per tree against ~1k for the half band and a few hundred for the rest, so far chunks holding only their coarse bands is most of the saving. Horizon variants are per preset and small.
