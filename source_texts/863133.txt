On WebGPU devices limited to the 128 MiB maxStorageBufferBindingSize that WebGPU guarantees, GPU-sorted gaussian splats stopped rendering past about 4.19 million active splats. The projection cache (32 bytes per splat) no longer fit a single storage buffer binding, so every bind group using it failed validation and nothing was drawn. This was reported on the forum (https://forum.playcanvas.com/t/42657, Tab S8 Ultra), and it reproduces on any device with the webgpu:bare device type.
Changes:
GPU sorting (GSPLAT_RENDERER_RASTER_GPU_SORT, the WebGPU default) now falls back to GSPLAT_RENDERER_RASTER_CPU_SORT for a camera and layer whose splats need a larger projection cache than the device can bind, instead of rendering nothing with a flood of validation errors. A single debug warning reports the sizes.
The fallback stays in place while the camera renders splats on that layer, also when their number drops back under the limit, so a streamed scene hovering around the limit does not rebuild on every crossing. It resets once the layer has no splats left or the camera stops rendering it.
Scenes under the limit, and devices whose limit fits the cache, keep GPU sorting. Full WebGPU requests the adapter's limits, for example 4 GiB on Apple GPUs.
API Changes:
No new API. GSplatParams#renderer documents the fallback, and GSplatParams#currentRenderer notes that it does not reflect it, as the fallback is per camera and layer.
Performance:
CPU sorting is slower than GPU sorting at these splat counts, but it only takes over where GPU sorting could not render at all.
