Tiled matrix multiplication is a core operation in high-performance computing and deep learning, where optimal selection of tile sizes is critical to maximize computational efficiency and memory throughput. Traditional methods typically rely on fixed heuristics to determine tile sizes, which may not perform well across various matrix dimensions and hardware configurations. In this paper, we propose a reinforcement learning-based approach to dynamically select optimal tile sizes for CUDA-based matrix multiplication. Our method employs Q-learning to explore and learn effective tile size decisions by adaptively choosing between static and dynamic tiled kernel implementations based on observed performance metrics. This allows the system to generalize well across a wide range of input sizes and optimize execution at runtime. We evaluated our approach across several matrix sizes and observed that the Q-learning agent learns optimal tile configurations over time. Empirical results show that our adaptive reinforcement learning method highlights its promise for automated performance optimization in GPU-based matrix multiplication, since it considerably lowers execution times when compared to static heuristics.
