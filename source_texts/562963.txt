Problem
Since the raymarcher replaced the instanced-box renderer, 3D MRMS weather has become very transparent. Light and moderate echo is barely visible at the default opacity.
Two changes aimed at the same problem had compounded:
The floorless cubic extinction ramp (later a 2.75 power) kept light echo nearly transparent so shells wouldn't bury cores.
The intensity-scaled opacity ceiling (opacityCap) solved that same problem again.
Together they left light and moderate echo with a fraction of the old renderer's opacity. At the default 35%, per 0.5 NM cell:

The 2.75 recalibration after the sparse-brick change matched the dense grid, which was already this faint.
Changes
Extinction ramp is now 0.1 + 0.9·t², so light and moderate rain has a visible body. Keeping shells from burying cores is left to the opacity ceiling alone.
Light-echo ceiling is linear in the slider: ~0.33 at the default, up from ~0.23. The ceiling's shape (full at 60 dBZ, pow 2.2) is unchanged.
Near-camera fade: while the camera itself sits in echo, extinction fades in over the first 5 NM from the camera, so in-storm views see out of the rain. A camera in clear air gets no fade, so storms right beside it keep their full body.
Heavy rain shows from above: stratiform columns put 10–20k ft of 15–25 dBZ over the 30–40 dBZ rain beneath, and front-to-back compositing spent the opacity ceiling on that green canopy. Two changes:
MIDA (after maximum intensity difference accumulation, Bruckner & Gröller 2009): a sample stronger than any the ray has met scales the color weight accumulated in front of it by 1 − jump / 10 dBZ. Opacity keeps its plain accumulation, so a stronger sample can never make a pixel less opaque.
Importance-weighted color: a sample's color weight doubles every 5 dBZ while its opacity weight doesn't. Hue follows the strongest echo, and opacity still follows how much precipitation the ray crossed.
Smoke runner fix: the runner now waits for Chromium to exit and retries removing its profile. Before, an ENOTEMPTY race intermittently replaced the test verdict.
Updated the docs, overview page, and sparse-brick design note.
Validation

- MIDA now reweights color only. Scaling accumulated opacity let a thin
  strong sample behind capped light rain leave the pixel less opaque than
  the rain alone; opacity keeps its plain accumulation, so coverage can
  never drop. The range tightens from 20 to 10 dBZ, which keeps the
