Problem
create_bounding_boxes (nukexc/octree.hpp:39) Morton-sorts the grid points and then chops the sorted list into fixed-size chunks of max_points_per_bb. Morton order is cheap and gives reasonable locality, but fixed-size chunks of a space-filling curve produce batches with poor aspect ratio — a chunk that straddles a Morton discontinuity has a bounding box far larger than its point count justifies, which inflates nbe for that batch and therefore the cost of every kernel downstream.
Proposed fix
Recursive cut-plane bisection, per Blum et al., Comput. Phys. Commun. 180 (2009) 2175, section 4.1 step 1:
split the full grid by finding a cut-plane through the centre of gravity of all points which maximises the sum of distance squares of all grid points to that plane; apply the same operation to the two resulting groups, until a predetermined maximum batch size is reached.
The plane normal that maximises the sum of squared distances is the principal eigenvector of the point covariance matrix, so each split is a 3x3 eigenproblem on the centroid-shifted second moments — cheap, and parallelises as a tree. Target ~100-200 points per batch.
This produces compact, roughly isotropic batches, which directly reduces nbe per batch and hence the work in every screened kernel. Worth measuring nbe histograms before/after — print_neighbor_list_histogram in nukexc/octree.hpp already produces exactly that plot.
Note: create_bounding_boxes currently reaches into ArborX::Details:: (computeSpaceFillingCurvePermutation, applyPermutation, PredicateIndexables) and builds a throwaway ArborX::Nearest query view purely to satisfy a geometry type. A self-contained bisection would remove that dependency on ArborX internals.
