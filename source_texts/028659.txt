The panel explained the DICOM → 3D pipeline and ran none of it. I wrote that sentence myself in #1562, called it "engineering work, not wording" — and then went back to wording. This is the engineering.
What is new

what it does

src/components/VolumeDicom3D.tsx

Two things that make such a render convincing and wrong
Voxels are not cubes. CT slice spacing is commonly millimetres while pixel spacing is under one, so a 64×64×24 stack drawn as a cube flattens the body along the head–foot axis. The box is proportioned by millimetres — in the test series the slice axis has the fewest voxels (24) and is physically the longest (72 mm).
One byte is not a thousand Hounsfield units. What is mapped to 0–255 is the chosen window, and the window travels with the texture, so a slider labelled HU means HU.
Ray-casting rather than isosurface, deliberately: an isosurface discards the volume, and a hole in it cannot be told apart from a hole in the threshold you picked.
Measured, not asserted
I built a synthetic uncompressed CT series — 24 slices, 64×64, a 700 HU sphere in −1000 HU air, explicit VR little endian — and drove the real page at 390×844.

```
The sphere renders. The rings visible on the screenshot are the 3 mm slice spacing — an artefact of the data, not of the renderer.

Adds the second half of the reference behaviour: surface rendering next
to volume rendering, sharing the threshold settings so the two can be
compared or overlaid rather than trusted separately.

Surface mode stops at the FIRST voxel inside the threshold and shades it
with a normal taken from the value gradient -- no mesh, no triangles, so
no shape is invented between voxels.

Measured at 390x844 on the same synthetic 700 HU sphere: Volume lights
9.3% of the canvas at peak 238, Surface 9.8% at 255, Both 9.8% at 245 --
three visibly different images from one threshold pair. The terracing
visible in surface mode is the 3 mm slice spacing: volume mode
integrates over it, surface mode exposes it. That difference is the
reason for having both, and the UI says so.

Gate extended, sabotage-proved four more ways, including giving the two
modes separate thresholds -- which would make the comparison meaningless.

feat(body-exposure): simulated radiograph from the volume (Beer-Lambert)

```
Fourth mode: a digitally reconstructed radiograph. Attenuation is
integrated along each ray from the ACTUAL Hounsfield values --
mu = mu_water x (1 + HU/1000), straight from the definition of the
Hounsfield scale -- with path length in millimetres, then
I = I0 exp(-integral(mu dl)). mu_water = 0.0193 /mm at ~70 keV.

Measured at 390x844 on the synthetic 700 HU sphere: exposure x0.4 gives
mean 7.7 peak 108, x1 gives 14.6 / 191, x3 gives 22.2 / 251 -- monotonic,
as the exponential requires.

Controls now disappear where they mean nothing: thresholds are hidden in
radiograph mode, and the opacity-per-step slider is hidden in both
radiograph and surface mode, since it is a volume-compositing factor. A
slider that does not affect the image teaches its user that numbers on
screen need not relate to what they see.

Stated, not glossed: single energy, no scatter, no beam hardening, no
detector response. Exposure stands in for tube output and detector gain
together and is NOT a dose in milligray; no dose is computed anywhere.

Gate extended, sabotage-proved six more ways: attenuation no longer
derived from HU, the exponent sign flipped, the millimetre step dropped
from the integral, the water coefficient silently changed, the
"not a photograph" limit removed, and the opacity slider shown where it
does nothing.

One of those sabotages passed the first time: I had asserted that the
uniform uMmPerLangkah EXISTS, not that the integral uses it. Removing
the multiplication left the uniform declared and the gate green.
Tightened to match the integral line itself.
