Destination
Produce a refreshed rolling roadmap of three to five one-sentence outcome candidates after the completed Milestone: Dense raster Voxel Scene proof, and select exactly one next milestone ready to hand to to-spec. Re-evaluate the provisional editable-raster outcome against the implemented engine and retained evidence rather than assuming the prior order.
Notes
Planning and decisions only: do not create implementation tickets, write engine code, publish a draft milestone, apply devlog, or activate a milestone.
The provisional next payoff is a user-triggered predetermined voxel edit followed by spatially localized, revision-correct raster replacement.
Greedy meshing is deferred; do not make it a requirement of the selected milestone.
The provisional concurrency proof is a burst of edits whose overlapping derived work converges only to the newest Voxel Scene Revision while the desktop remains responsive.
Retain portable compute-ray rendering and editable-SVO storage independence as provisional later roadmap anchors, while allowing their order and milestone boundaries to change from evidence.
Start from the completed milestone and its correction in Render outward voxel faces instead of back faces. Current implementation has an immutable one-revision Voxel Frontend, whole-volume exposed-face raster artifact, one-shot revision gate, background preparation, and deterministic camera control; it does not yet provide edit publication, Voxel Change Sets, spatial raster artifacts, supersession, or voxel picking.
Use grilling for human decisions, domain-modeling to protect the canonical language in CONTEXT.md, and codebase-design when placing edit, invalidation, derived-artifact, and installation seams.
A selected milestone is ready only when its goal, viewer payoff, completion demo, success criteria, technical shape, risks and rabbit holes, non-goals, and capture plan are supported by resolved evidence.
Keep every later outcome at one-sentence roadmap resolution and treat it as a hypothesis rather than a commitment.
Decisions so far
Define edit publication and Voxel Change Set semantics — A serialized single-coordinate command classifies against current state, then publishes an atomic successor view plus scene-and-revision-bound conservative invalidation; errors and semantic no-ops publish nothing.
Choose the spatial raster replacement boundary without greedy meshing — Stable finite Raster Regions use face-neighbor halos, Render Path-owned derivation and per-region GPU resources, and whole-scene-revision atomic replacement.
Define edit-burst supersession and visible convergence — Newest-only Raster Convergence eagerly accumulates bounded invalidations, cooperatively supersedes CPU work, rejects stale candidates at upload and commit, and classifies failures on the UI thread.
Revalidate the editable raster demonstration and evidence contract — A fixed three-command, two-barrier proof uses exact semantic and untouched-region oracles, deterministic resilience checks, objective extent selection, descriptive measurements, and a versioned auditable bundle.
Shape the localized editable raster milestone contract — One milestone contract joins a fixed three-edit localized raster proof with newest-only convergence, mandatory resilience evidence, and correctness-first measured Raster Region selection.
Select the next milestone and refresh the post-raster roadmap — Localized editable raster convergence is selected for to-spec in a three-outcome order; portable compute ray precedes editable-SVO storage independence as a provisional later hypothesis.
Not yet specified
None. The refreshed rolling roadmap and selected next milestone are resolved in Select the next milestone and refresh the post-raster roadmap.
Out of scope
Engine implementation, source edits, implementation tickets, and file-level implementation plans.
A draft milestone issue, devlog activation, or a planned-work checklist.
Detailed specifications for later roadmap candidates.
Mouse picking, general editor UX, free-form voxel tools, gameplay, networking, physics, and world streaming.
Greedy meshing, progressive streaming, production performance targets, and runtime claims beyond retained evidence.
Hardware ray tracing and sparse-DAG work.
