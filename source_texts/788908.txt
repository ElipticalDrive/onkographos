I am not 100% sure how blender would handle this just because volumes can be a bit of a rough patch for a lot of engines, but my vote, as always, is view layers. View layers can interact with each other as if they are in the same space but can have their own independent objects. What you could then do is use an object ID to mask your depth or Fog pass and then use your depth value of the cubes to mask the depth of the fog over the cubes. I would start just by getting used to render layers as a whole because they can be a little bit of a chore to wrap your brain around in blender. It handles them kinda strange compared to some other programs but once you get it its not that hard. Just a bit tricky at first.

Forget the holdout. Use a depth pass of a fog pass to get the depth of the object in the fog and then use the fog pass to mask the cubes. A cube that is farther away will be more transparent then, and it will retain any information from shadows and scattering as well then (which I am not sure there would be a whole lot of in cycles TBH but they will be there). Then use object IDs on each cube so if you need to manually tweak them in post you can without using a holdout.

Yeah, at least none that I know of. Maybe someone else will come on here and have a better idea but, standard practice from my own personal experience has always just been manipulation of render passes. I know some really amazing compositors who can throw together a 400 node group in nuke in 30 minutes or an hour just quickly busting out a good base comp. it’s job title of its own for a reason but that shouldn’t intimidate anyone just trying to get a good render. You can certainly do quite a bit with quite a lot less and blender is by no means an amateur tool. In my experience, things like this just require a degree of repetition and doing it multiple times until it becomes second nature. Unless you are trying to get a job as a compositor I don’t think anyone is expecting you to know off the top of your head how to do complex AOV composites to the extremes that people making 70-110k a year are who specialize in just that but of course, again, that’s when you have the help of pipeline. When you are doing things on your own like this it’s just about critical thinking and Breaking down big tasks into smaller ones, one by one in order of start to finish. The quickest way to overwhelm yourself is by trying to do the end before you have seen what the beginning even looks like and then you just end up getting confused in a big web of steps. In all actuality, that big web of steps can usually be done in five or 10 minutes if you take those additional five minutes before hand to just sort of go over everything and try and infer how something happens in real life.
The way I can come up with the passes is my thinking, OK, if this is behind the fog volume and something else is in the front center ish area, and the fog has an absorption of 5 m then what do I know?:
the fog volume is 10 m long so the cube behind the fog should be almost invisible since it absorbs at 5m
the object near the front is about 1/4 of the way see so it should be pretty visible.
So I ask what can hide these objects based on depth in my passes?
fog pass, but fog is not sized to the volume so I can clamp it until absolute black is around 5m into the volume and white is at the front of the volume.
Cool, but now that will effect everything so how can I tell it to JUST effect the volume where the cubes are?
2 options, object ID and Alpha, does my object pass have an alpha? No? Ok we use object IDs.
make the objects in the object ID into a mask
Screen my fog pass by the mask so the cubes have fog shading and all else is white.
Alpha over the fog over the cubes and use the adjusted fog pass as the blend amount so the fog pass will make the fog slightly transparent where the cubes are based on depth, keeping part of the texture now from the fog volume depending on the objects depth.
Boom, done. And you could probably do it faster than I wrote it.

Thanks again for your help. I’m realizing now that we’re dealing with two different problems. One is the masking issue you are talking about, and the other was a different issue with color management.
After doing more tests, I realized that the other problem is that the Add blend mode in Blender acts very differently from Photoshop. After re-rendering the test scene as 32bit exr and creating a new Photoshop file I now get identical results to the composite in the test scene.
However, that wasn’t the case in my other other scene I’m working on, even when re-exporting everything as exr. I did eventually find a setting in the Exr-IO plugin that fixed the issue.
Fix transparent light emitters
OpenEXR allows for pixels to have color information with no (or very low) opacity. Photoshop does not support this for layers with integrated transparency and will discard color information. When the option is enabled Exr-IO will adjust the transparencies to avoid this issue and display colors in Photoshop as they exist in the file. This will modify the opacity of affected pixels.
So that fixes one problem …but creates another. The exr export does not contain the Filimc color grading from Blender.
