Abstract:
1 Neural Volume Rendering (NVR) delivers photorealistic results but suffers from prohibitive multilayer perceptron (MLP) computation, making real-time deployment impractical on graphic processing units (GPUs) or conventional accelerators. To address this challenge, we propose HiSparse, a hierarchical sparsity framework that unifies two complementary schemes: R-Sparse Skipping for majority-zero rows and T-Sparse Encoding for near-zero truncation. By combining coarse- and fine-grained sparsity exploitation, HiSparse minimizes redundant MLP operations while preserving reconstruction fidelity. Built upon this framework, we design ZeroBlade—a spatial similarity-aware sparse MLP engine that implements HiSparse in hardware, enabling both lossless and lossy acceleration with tunable accuracy–efficiency trade-offs. ZeroBlade adopts an outer-product computation flow and lightweight control logic to efficiently utilize hierarchical sparsity across NVR workloads. We implement and validate ZeroBlade on the Synopsys HAPS-80 FPGA prototype system. To evaluate its performance, the design is further synthesized using Synopsys Design Compiler under a 28 nm CMOS process. ZeroBlade achieves up to 3.4× speedup with only minor quality degradation. The synthesized core occupies 1.284 mm2 and consumes 102.8 mW at 200 MHz, achieving an energy efficiency of 24.55 TOPS/W and surpassing previous 28 nm sparse accelerators in both power and area efficiency.

I. Introduction
NEURAL Radiance Fields (NeRF) [1] have fueled the rapid rise of Neural Volume Rendering (NVR), establishing it as a dominant paradigm for photorealistic view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. In recent years, NVR has been applied in virtual and augmented reality (VR/AR) [2], autonomous driving [3], and digital content creation [4], delivering unprecedented fidelity even from sparse observations.
