Two objects that build a mesh2 at parse time from a slower shape, and the documentation for both. This replaces #35 and #39: their commits are here unchanged, rebased onto current performance, followed by one documentation commit.
skein_mesh { ...a skein... min_size S max_angle A } tessellates the skein over (u, v): exact vertices, normals and uv, adaptive splitting by normal turn, watertight across seams, poles, caps and envelopes.
isosurface_mesh { ...an isosurface... min_size S max_angle A } builds an octree over contained_by (interval culling where the function allows, sampling with the max_gradient margin otherwise) and extracts the surface by dual contouring, keeping sharp edges; closed by the container unless open.
A declared mesh is built once and every copy shares it. Both build straight into the compact mesh storage (about 24 to 32 bytes a triangle) and stop with an error above 2 million triangles.
Documentation
The mesh2 page gains "Meshes Built From Other Objects": when a mesh pays (many copies, large images, antialiasing and secondary rays, iteration, distant copies, isosurface CSG without all_intersections) and when to keep the original (shapes that change every frame, close-ups, one small render, memory, uv on an isosurface, open containers). Each mesh section links to it and shows renders made while testing. One example: thirty copies of a heavy isosurface at 960x720 with antialiasing and shadows take 5 minutes 4 seconds as isosurfaces and 40 seconds from one shared mesh.
Verified
The combined branch builds; skein_mesh_check.pov and isosurface_mesh.sh pass with the same numbers as on their own branches (closed meshes have 0 open edges; chord errors within their limits; inside agrees at every test point).
Separate pieces, and CSG with them, match the isosurface.
The documentation's link check is clean apart from a missing anchor in t2_5.html that predates this.
Follow-ups
Reusing a built mesh across parses and animation frames (a mesh cache); clipping open rims at the container; interval plans for sqrt and pow.

Skein: skein_mesh, a mesh2 built from a skein at parse time

c62edbe
```
skein_mesh takes everything a skein takes, plus min_size and max_angle,
and builds a triangle mesh when the scene is parsed. The result is a
Mesh, so it declares, copies (sharing one set of triangles), transforms,
textures and works in CSG like any mesh2.

The mesh starts from the skein's patch grid rounded down to a power of
two and halves a cell along u or v while the normal or an edge turns
more than max_angle across it (default 10 degrees), unless its edges
would fall below min_size (default a thousandth of the skein's size).
Leaves with smaller neighbours fan from their centre over every vertex
on their edges, so there are no cracks. Vertices, normals and uv are the
skein's own; a corner on a crease takes the normal from inside its
triangle, and a triangle spanning a crease takes its face normal.
Wrapped seams, poles and sealed ends share positions, flat ends are
fans from the end ring, and a closed skein sets the inside vector.

One vertex per distinct position, normal and uv, so the mesh needs no
index lists and stores exactly what an equal mesh2 would. More than two
million triangles is a parse error. Each build writes its triangles,
vertices, open edges, bytes and time to the debug stream.

tests/render/skein_mesh_check.pov compares a torus, a hexagonal column,
a sphere with poles and an envelope slab with their skeins (chord error,
normals, inside) and checks a sealed envelope is closed; skein.sh checks
the torus counts and that all five meshes have no open edges.
```

```
isosurface_mesh takes everything an isosurface takes plus min_size and
max_angle, and builds a triangle mesh by dual contouring on an octree over
contained_by. Cells that range arithmetic proves empty stop; functions
without a range plan fall back to one centre sample and max_gradient. A
cell splits while the gradient turns more than max_angle across it, unless
the turn is a crease or corner its vertex can sit on, so sharp edges stay
exact at coarse sizes. Vertices sit where the crossing planes meet, then on
the surface; normals are the gradient. The container closes the mesh unless
open. Copies share one mesh; more than two million triangles is an error.

MeshBuilder fills a mesh's own arrays as triangles arrive, one vertex per
distinct position, normal and uv, so no index lists and no second copy.
```
