Enhancing Energy Efﬁcient Task Caching and
Ofﬂoading in Mobile Edge Computing
Fabiano Lorusso∗ , Frédéric Giroire∗ , Joanna Moulierac∗ , Guillaume Urvoy-Keller∗
∗ Université Côte d’Azur, CNRS, Inria, I3S. ﬁrstname.name@univ-cotedazur.fr
Abstract—Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) enables both to
prolong the battery life of mobile devices and support the
execution of computationally intensive applications at the edge.
This can be achieved by ofﬂoading these tasks to a server
deployed near the base station and/or by directly caching them.
Previous works focus on only one of these two strategies or
formulate optimization problems that are hard to solve and
propose a suboptimal solution.
In this paper, we propose a linear model for the joint task
caching and ofﬂoading optimization problem. Moreover, we
present two efﬁcient heuristics which provide close-to-optimal
results in terms of energy efﬁciency with a low execution time. We
further prove that the ofﬂoading subproblem can be solved with
an optimal algorithm. Finally, we demonstrate the performance
and scalability of our propositions by extensive simulations on a
large number of 105 mobile devices
I. I NTRODUCTION
Mobile devices and sensors need to support complex applications which combine a high computation intensity and real
time constraints. Typical examples include image recognition
or augmented reality [1], [2]. These requirements either exceed
the computing capacities of the mobile devices hardware or
can rapidly exhaust their battery.
To enable a correct execution while preserving the battery
level of the mobile device, a strategy is to execute part of
the application operations in a server in the cloud. However,
the stringent delay constraints of these applications call for
locating this server close to the mobile device. This is the idea
behind Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) [3]. MEC enables the
deployment of an edge cloud directly at the base station. It
can exploited by ISPs and third party application providers.
By bringing computing resources closer to the users, MEC
signiﬁcantly reduces latency, ensuring that the requirements
of delay-sensitive tasks are met effectively.
Availability and deployment of MEC has fostered research
on task ofﬂoading techniques, leading to a lot of models and
approaches proposed to reduce energy and task execution [4],
[5], [6], [7]. We consider in this work, similarly to previous
studies, e.g. [8], a generic model where the nearby and grid
powered computing and storage facility can be used in two
different manners. First, the mobile applications can decide
to ofﬂoad part of its computation tasks, in terms of code and

Second, the control plane of the applications, likely deployed

Unlike traditional cloud computing, MEC features limited

algorithms and show that the ofﬂoading subproblem can be
tackled with an optimal algorithm.
The paper is structured as follows: in Section II we present
the related work on achieving energy efﬁciency using task
caching and task ofﬂoading schemes. The modelling of our
MEC network is illustrated in detail in Section III with also
a thorough description of the linear optimization problem
and the two heuristic algorithms derived from it. Section IV
presents the simulation results and compares the performances
of the different algorithms. Lastly, Section V provides conclusions and future work directions.
II. R ELATED W ORK
An extensive standardization and research effort in the ﬁeld
of Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) has been conducted in the
last years, see [3] for a survey on the beneﬁts and challenges
of MEC. One of the challenge is to proﬁle applications, so as
to isolate which application fragments can be ofﬂoaded [9].
Once the application fragments candidates for ofﬂoading are
identiﬁed, the next key step is to decide whether to fully or
partially ofﬂoad the fragment. Our work focuses on this crucial
problem.
A ﬁrst series of papers tackles the task ofﬂoading problem.
In [10], the author considers a device-edge-cloud fusion scenario, where the task can be scheduled either on the mobile
device, an edge cloud or a central cloud. Several heuristic
algorithms are proposed and compared to an asymptotic lower
bound of the optimal solution. Similarly, [11] formulates
the problem of minimizing the total energy consumption as
a mixed integer non-linear program and proposes a metaheuristic optimization algorithm, as the authors in [5]. [4] proposes a stochastic optimization approach to ensure a bounded
average queue length at the IoT devices in a MEC scenario.
A second series of work then considers the joint optimization of task caching and ofﬂoading.
A reference paper for this is [8]. Their model considers
computing and storage resource constraints on the edge cloud
and delay constraints on the mobile devices. Tasks can be
partially or fully ofﬂoaded at the MEC server. The authors
ﬁrst formulate this problem as a mixed integer programming
problem that is hard to solve. Then they propose an approximate algorithm named Task Caching and Ofﬂoading (TCO)
based on an alternating iterative approach. [12] formulated
task caching and ofﬂoading as a non linear problem, similarly
to [8] and propose a reinforcement learning approach to
obtain an approximate solution for large-scale scenarios. [13]
and [14] proposed Lyapunov optimization techniques to solve
ofﬂoading and caching to minimize execution time with ﬁxed
energy budget. [6] proposes a linear version of a variant of the
problem in which partial ofﬂoading and sequential dependent
tasks are considered.
We used the general model in [8] as a starting point and we
proved that it can be linearized. We then proposed a heuristic
alternative to reduce the execution time. Moreover, we showed
that the ofﬂoading sub-problem part of their solution can be
tackled with an optimal algorithm.
III. S YSTEM M ODEL AND P ROBLEM F ORMULATION
Similarly to [8], we consider a MEC system composed
of one Edge Server deployed in proximity of a base station
providing services to a set N = {1, 2, ..., N } of mobile
devices (or users) through a wireless channel. To each device
is assigned one computational task among the set K =
{1, 2, ..., K} with the assumption that the total number of
devices is larger than the number of tasks (N ≥ K). Moreover,
any task can be decomposed into smaller components that can
be either processed locally on the mobile device or ofﬂoaded
and processed on the edge cloud. Additionally, a task can be
cached in the edge cloud to avoid computing it locally or
ofﬂoading it. As a consequence, all the mobile devices that are
assigned a task cached in the edge cloud will receive directly
the results of the computation of such task and, therefore, will
have no energy cost1 .

capacities constraints of the edge cloud. In particular, the ﬁrst

D. Algorithms: H-TCO’24 and Fastened It-TCO[8]:
Fast-It-TCO’24
Based on the discussion of the previous section, we propose
two heuristic algorithms capable of achieving a sub-optimal
solution for the joint optimization problem in a much shorter
time than the linear programs presented before.
The ﬁrst one, H-TCO’24, ﬁrst solves the ofﬂoading problem with Algorithm 1 and then solves the caching problem
with the method described in Section III-C1.
The second one, Fast-It-TCO’24, is an improvement
in time of the heuristic presented in [8]. The upgrade consists
in implementing our optimal task ofﬂoading algorithm instead
of the fractional linear ofﬂoading sub-problem of the original
model. The caching is done optimally with a linear program.
We show in the numerical evaluation that Fast-It-TCO’24
is a lot faster than It-TCO[8], while achieving the same
performance, and that H-TCO’24 is even faster with similar
performance.
IV. N UMERICAL E VALUATION
A. Experiment setup
In this section, we present the inputs for the experimentation and evaluation of the four different approaches : (1)
our linearization of the mixed integer programming problem
(Opt-TCO’24), (2) our heuristic H-TCO’24 proposition, (3)
the literature algorithm [8] (It-TCO[8]), and (4) our improvement in execution time of this approximation algorithm
(Fast-It-TCO’24).
We are focusing on an edge computing system with an edge
cloud, a set of N mobile devices, N varying from 100 to
105 , each of them assigned with one computation task chosen
among a set of K = |N5 | tasks. The edge cloud, deployed
near the wireless access point, has a computing capacity of
25 GHz (cs = 25 × 109 ) and a cache size that is proportional
to the number of devices in such a way that 20% of the tasks
are cached in average. The data size of each task is generated
uniformly within the range of 5 MB to 15 MB. Furthermore,
each mobile device has a uniform transmission power of 0.5 W
(Pn = 0.5) and a consistent computing capability of 0.7 GHz
(fl = 0.7 × 109 ). The data rate of each device is modeled by
a normal distribution with an average of 2.3 MBps, sourced
from the average upload speed provided by Orange in France
according to [18]. The completion deadline for each user is
set to 2 s (Dn = 2), and the computational resource allocated
to each user n at the edge cloud is 2 GHz (fnc = 2 × 109 ).
Moreover, the amount of computing resource required for each
task is generated by a normal distribution with mean that is
eight times bigger than the average task size and a standard
deviation four times smaller than the average task size.

D. Energy consumption with varying parameters
a) Impact of the task popularity: Figure 5 shows how the
Zipf distribution parameter γ impacts on the average energy
cost of each mobile device. When γ is high, it means that
some tasks have a really high popularity and are assigned to a
large number of mobile devices. With a lower γ, the popularity
distribution is ﬂatter. As a consequence, as predicted, caching
really popular tasks allows to obtain large energy savings.
When γ is small, there is less impact on the chosen tasks
to be cached (as all the tasks share a similar popularity).
b) Impact of the cache size and of cloud ofoading
capacity: Figure 4 shows that, when the cache size increases,
the average energy cost per mobile decreases superlinearly, as
we are ﬁrst caching very popular tasks and then less and less
popular tasks, as discussed above. In parallel, the energy cost
decreases almost linearly with an increasing cloud ofﬂoading
capacity, as the energy savings for a the task of a user is linear
with the ofﬂoaded amount.
V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we formulate a linear formulation solving
optimally a joint task caching and task ofﬂoading problem.
This allows to solve optimally medium sized instances. We
also show that the caching and ofﬂoading sub-problems both
have a special structure allowing to solve them efﬁciently for
the ﬁrst one and optimally for the second one with simple
algorithms. We then further propose a heuristic alternative that,
while yielding results close to the optimal results, signiﬁcantly
reduces computational time, allowing to solve large instances
very quickly. In future works, we plan to extend our current
model, e.g. by adding the energy of the cloud in order to
propose a global energy reduction scheme.
