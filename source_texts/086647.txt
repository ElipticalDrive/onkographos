True-to-life three-dimensional (3D) video display via computer-generated holography is highly sought-after in virtual reality, augmented reality and human–computer interactions. However, high crosstalk fundamentally limits 3D projection continuity. Most methods reconstruct 3D scenes by stacking widely spaced axial or lateral planes, yielding discontinuous reconstructions that diminish the realism. State-of-the-art techniques still balance crosstalk against plane spacing, preventing fully continuous 3D projection. Here we propose a holistic design paradigm based on a cascaded light-field propagation mechanism that reuses outgoing light as constructive input for subsequent planes, thereby transforming the crosstalk into a useful signal. To address the computational challenges associated with our method, we develop a physics-embedded, untrained holographic diffractive prior neural network framework (Holo-Prior-Net) that serves as a computational realization tool. Without pretraining, it can efficiently design 3D holograms through its architectural prior. We demonstrate free-space 3D light-field projection that is both axially and laterally continuous using a spatial light modulator. Beyond volume-rendered reconstructions, the 3D continuity is directly observable across wide range of viewing angles, confirming the physical realization. By reconstructing the underlying design logic rather than incrementally improving image metrics, this work provides an alternative route towards projecting volumetrically continuous 3D objects. Cascaded light-field propagation enhances three-dimensional holography by transforming crosstalk into constructive signals. Using an untrained neural framework, the method achieves continuous projections along with improvements in contrast and spatial resolution without extensive computational demands.

Supplementary Video 1 (download MP4 )
Simulation of Fresnel holography, RV-CGH and the proposed method. The variations of the light field at different depths within a 150-mm axial range are shown. The projection planes are at 50 mm and 100 mm. The propagation process from letter A to letter B illustrates how outgoing light from the preceding plane is used rather than discarded.
Supplementary Video 2 (download MP4 )
Three-dimensional volumetric rendering of experimentally reconstructed light fields for a continuous DNA double helix across different viewing angles. The 3D hologram was optically reconstructed and projected within an 80-mm axial range. The lateral spatial dimension of the helix is 0.6 mm, with a linewidth of approximately 50 μm.
Supplementary Video 3 (download MP4 )
Three-dimensional volumetric rendering of experimentally reconstructed light fields for a continuous hollow cylinder across different viewing angles. The 3D hologram was optically reconstructed and projected within a 40-mm axial range. The hollow cylinder has a diameter of 5 mm and a wall thickness of 100 μm.
Supplementary Video 4 (download MP4 )
Three-dimensional volumetric rendering of experimentally reconstructed light fields for a continuous hollow light sphere across different viewing angles. The sphere has a lateral range of 1 mm and an axial range of 40 mm.
Supplementary Video 5 (download MP4 )
Rapid dynamic hologram design. A sequence of 14 target light-field distributions was dynamically designed, and the corresponding holograms were loaded onto the SLM. The experimental results were captured using a CCD camera with a response time of less than 0.5 s per frame.
Supplementary Video 6 (download MP4 )
Free-space volumetric visualization of a dynamically rotating 3D continuous double-helix light field in scattering smoke. Twenty 3D holograms corresponding to different spatial orientations of the double-helix structure were sequentially and continuously loaded onto the SLM, producing a volumetric display of the dynamically rotating 3D double-helix light field.
Supplementary Video 7 (download MP4 )
Projection of a continuous 3D volumetric light field, directly observable by the naked eye and viewable from several angles, without relying on postprocessing or screen rendering.
Supplementary Video 8 (download MP4 )
Axial continuous-scanning experimental video comparing different design paradigms. The translation stage moved over a 50-mm range with 0.1-mm steps, producing 500 raw camera frames. The letters A and B were projected with a 30-mm axial separation. The videos compare the layer-wise independent design paradigm (represented by RV-CGH) and the proposed cascaded-propagation design paradigm. As the reconstruction planes move axially, the layer-wise design shows defocused output light and interplane crosstalk, whereas for the cascaded-propagation design, the output light from one plane is constructively used as the imaging light for the next plane rather than being scattered into random speckles.
Supplementary Video 9 (download MP4 )
Axial continuous scanning of the 3D double-helix structure. The translation stage moved over a 140-mm range with 0.2-mm steps, generating 700 raw camera frames. The scan includes intermediate depths not explicitly sampled during the design process, confirming the continuity of the projected light field.
