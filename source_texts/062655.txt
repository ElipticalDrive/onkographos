Abstract
Volumetric video streaming promises immersive experiences by allowing 6 degrees of freedom movement through dynamic 3D scenes. Volumetric videos, however, still lack a universally adopted representation format. Gaussian Splatting based methods offer high-quality appearance reconstruction at the cost of size and rendering speed. Point clouds are easy to store and render but struggle to achieve the same visual quality. We present Δ Splatting, a dynamic scene reconstruction method to bridge the gap between the simplicity of point clouds and the visual quality of Gaussian Splatting. Our method separates a dynamic scene into one appearance keyframe and subsequent motion delta frames. By using triangle primitives for appearance, Δ Splatting remains compact and renderable with mesh-like primitives, while its geometry-oriented motion representation is compatible with off-the-shelf point-cloud compression methods. Experimental results show that Δ Splatting achieves competitive visual quality and compression rates while providing a streaming- and rendering-friendly keyframe/delta frame representation.
