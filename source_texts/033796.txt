Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting has recently emerged as a novel 3D representation method,
gaining recognition for its exceptional rendering speed and visual fidelity. However, these advancements come at a cost: this technique exhibits substantial
memory demands, with optimized models often containing millions of Gaussian
primitives that can exceed 1 GB of storage. We attribute this inefficiency to
inherent redundancy in primitive allocation, where overlapping or non-essential
elements remain unoptimized in current implementations. In this paper, we propose a novel memory-efficient Gaussian compression method named MiniGS,
featuring Full Factors Weighted (FFW) Importance Score Pruning and Variational Densify Threshold (VDT) components. On one hand, we construct several
importance score to selectively combine pixel coordinates, Gaussian distances
and opacity on the set of Gaussian primitives, and utilize them to prune out
redundancy while preserving a small number of highly contributive primitives,
achieving an impressive 90% compression ratio. On the other hand, to compensate for the quality loss of pruning Gaussians, we utilize the plug-and-play
Variational Densify Threshold (VDT) Component to recover fine details of
the 3D scenes, effectively compensating for quality losses with primitives at a
1
low scale. We demonstrate the performance of MiniGS with extensive experimental results on classic dataset and our self-constructed synthetic dataset.
For example, our proposed method MiniGS can achieve 29.63 PSNR with
303.271 thousand Gaussian primitives, while the vanilla Gaussian splatting algorithm achieves same-level PSNR(29.73) with one order of magnitude higher of
Gaussian primitives on the DeepBlending dataset.

1 Introduction
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) Mildenhall et al. (2021),Barron et al. (2021),Barron
et al. (2022),Barron et al. (2023) have transformed photorealistic 3D reconstruction, opening new possibilities for boosting visual fidelity across applications like
human avatar reconstruction Hong et al. (2022); Gafni et al. (2021); Zheng et al.
(2022a,b); Yang et al. (2024a), SLAM Rosinol et al. (2023); Zhang et al. (2023), and
extended reality (XR) Yang and Zhang (2022); Yang et al. (2023). NeRF’s power
comes from its capacity to encode complex 3D scenes—including view-dependent
effects—within a network of multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) and to generate new
views via alpha-compositing using a sophisticated volumetric rendering strategy. However, its dependence on deep MLP architectures and the high computational cost of
volumetric rendering impose significant overheads, thereby limiting real-time usability. This challenge has spurred research into alternative data structures Chen et al.
(2022); Fridovich-Keil et al. (2022); Müller et al. (2022) aimed at improving rendering
efficiency.
In contrast, the recent development of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Kerbl et al.
(2023) has made significant strides in achieving high-quality, real-time 3D scene reconstruction. Departing from traditional NeRF techniques—which use implicit scene
representations via MLPs and specialized data structures Mildenhall et al. (2021);
Müller et al. (2022); Yu et al. (2021)—3DGS models scenes explicitly with parameterized 3D Gaussian primitives. These primitives are quickly projected and rasterized
onto the image plane through a process known as Splatting. By leveraging a tile-based,
differentiable renderer with alpha-blending, 3DGS not only delivers photorealistic
results but also efficiently optimizes the Gaussian primitives. This approach marks a
notable advance in balancing rendering quality with computational speed, and it has
been successfully applied to domains such as SLAM Keetha et al. (2024), XR Dongye
et al. (2024a), and human modeling Dongye et al. (2024b).
Yet, despite its efficiency gains, 3DGS encounters issues related to primitive overgrowth. During the densification stage—when primitives are cloned and subdivided
to address reconstruction inaccuracies—their numbers can surge, leading to increased
memory usage and slower rendering, especially on lower-end devices. As Fan et al. Fan
et al. (2023) have pointed out, many of these additional primitives contribute little to
the final rendered output and can be pruned without compromising visual quality.
2
To address this, several recent studies Fang and Wang (2024); Fan et al. (2023);
Niemeyer et al. (2024); Navaneet et al. (2023); Lee et al. (2024); Liu et al. (2024); Yang
et al. (2024b); Niedermayr et al. (2023) have proposed various Gaussian primitive
pruning techniques that assess the significance of each primitive and remove those
deemed unimportant. However, these strategies have not been exhaustively validated,
and improper tuning of pruning parameters can result in substantial quality loss. This
underlines the need for a more robust, thoroughly investigated approach to balance
efficiency with rendering quality.
In this paper, we present MiniGS, a novel method that inherently enforces a natural
cap on pruning parameters to prevent severe scene degradation. Our approach demonstrates superior performance under extreme pruning conditions compared to existing
methods. Specifically, our experiments show that the Importance Score combining
all factors as view-independent color, opacity and pixel coordinates with Variational
Densify Threshold is highly effective for discerning and preserving critical primitives,
thereby recovering fine-grained details and significantly enhancing the final rendering
quality in post-pruning reconstruction.
We summarize our contributions as follows:
• We introduce a novel and efficient 3D Gaussian primitives pruning method MiniGS.
In this method we procedurally construct the full factors importance score with
pixel coordinates, Gaussian position distances, opacity and transparency to effectively prune out redundancy while preserving a small number of highly contributive
primitives.
• We present Variational Densify Threshold (VDT) for postprocessing quality compensation, a gradient optimization mechanism applied during the backward process.
VDT enhances the preservation of fine-grained details and significantly improves
rendering quality in pruned Gaussian representation.
• We specifically constructed a novel synthetic dataset to rigorously evaluate the
performance of our proposed method, MiniGS, across various compression ratio
configurations. The experimental results conclusively proved its stability and
efficiency.
• We perform comprehensive qualitative evaluations and quantitative analyses to
benchmark MiniGS (MG) against state-of-the-art methods. Extensive experimental
results highlight the superiority of our approach, particularly under extreme pruning
conditions.
2 Related Works
2.1 Efficient Neural Radiance Fields
Over the past several decades, a diverse array of methods for 3D scene reconstruction and representation has captured the attention of both the computer vision
and graphics communities. Traditional techniques—such as structure-from-motion
(SfM) Snavely et al. (2006) and multi-view stereo (MVS) Goesele et al. (2007)—have
long been used to estimate camera parameters from image collections and to generate
dense 3D point clouds of object shapes. The integrated framework of SfM and MVS,
3
exemplified by COLMAP Schonberger and Frahm (2016), has become the de facto
standard for producing virtual 3D scenes.
At the same time, the advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) Mildenhall et al.
(2021) has pushed rendering quality toward photorealism. NeRF encodes scene colors
and geometry via features learned by multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) and synthesizes
new views through volumetric rendering. This breakthrough has led to applications
spanning SLAM, generative modeling, and human modeling.
Despite these advances, NeRF faces challenges such as reconstructing unbounded
scenes Zhang et al. (2020), managing noisy or inconsistent data Martin-Brualla et al.
(2021), and handling temporal variations Gao et al. (2021); Li et al. (2021). Of these,
improving rendering efficiency and memory usage has emerged as a critical concern.
Notably, Sun et al. Sun et al. (2022) identified MLPs as a major bottleneck and
introduced a hybrid approach that combines feature voxel grids with lightweight MLPs
to mitigate this issue.
Additional grid-based methods have pursued reduced memory demands by employing more efficient data structures—such as tensor decomposition Chen et al. (2022),
hash tables Müller et al. (2022), and octrees Yu et al. (2021). While these approaches
have achieved faster scene convergence and enhanced image quality compared to conventional MLP-based NeRFs, they still face limitations. In particular, the choice of grid
data structure can constrain image quality, and the intrinsic multi-sampling nature
of volumetric rendering can slow down forward-pass performance, preventing these
methods from serving as perfect solutions.
2.2 Efficient 3D Gaussian Splats
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Kerbl et al. (2023) has set a new standard by
striking an impressive balance between rendering quality and speed. Its success is
primarily due to an MLP-free 3D representation that leverages parameterized 3D
Gaussian primitives for real-time, high-fidelity rendering. Additionally, its explicit
scene representation dovetails seamlessly with conventional graphics pipelines, taking
full advantage of GPU parallelism for efficient processing.
However, the densification algorithm in 3DGS Kerbl et al. (2023) presents its own
challenges. By populating new Gaussian primitives in under-reconstructed regions,
the algorithm can generate millions of primitives, significantly increasing memory
usage. To combat this, the original work Kerbl et al. (2023) proposed periodically
discarding primitives with opacities below a preset threshold. Yet, research such as
LightGaussian Fan et al. (2023) has shown that this approach is insufficient, introducing a Gaussian importance evaluation metric that indicates up to an additional 60%
of primitives can be pruned without degrading rendering quality.
Similarly, various studies Liu et al. (2024); Fan et al. (2023); Niemeyer et al. (2024);
Fang and Wang (2024); Lee et al. (2024); Yang et al. (2024b) have suggested different
pruning strategies based on distinct importance metrics to remove less critical primitives. However, the fundamental principles and key design factors for such metrics
remain underexplored, underscoring the need for further research to optimize 3DGS
for memory efficiency while maintaining its outstanding rendering capabilities.

3.1 Background
3D Gaussian Splatting 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is a point-based method
for representing 3D scenes. It starts by initializing from sparse point clouds generated
using Structure-from-Motion (SfM) Snavely et al. (2006). Each 3D Gaussian, denoted
as g , is characterized by several parameters: its mean (position), covariance (shape and
scale), opacity, and view-dependent color, which is typically represented using spherical
harmonics (SH). A scene in 3DGS is modeled as a collection of these Gaussians.

4 Experiments
4.1 Implementation Details
Source Code Leveraging the open-source code of 3DGS Kerbl et al. (2023), we established our experimental platform with ease. We implemented the primitive pruning
algorithm on top of vanilla 3DGS’s Python framework and extended the CUDA-based
differentiable renderer to incorporate various score functions. To understand the implications of different pruning techniques, we integrated pruning implementations (i.e.,
pruning settings and score functions) from prior works. The pruning techniques evaluated include LG, MS, RS, and EG, derived respectively from LightGaussian Fan et al.
(2023), Mini-Splatting Fang and Wang (2024), RadSplat Niemeyer et al. (2024), EfficientGS Liu et al. (2024) and our proposed method. As of this paper’s submission,
9
only MiniGS, LightGaussian and Mini-Splatting have open-sourced their code. Therefore, we manually implemented the pruning techniques for RadSplat and EfficientGS
based on the descriptions provided in their respective papers.
Training Each technique was trained for 30,000 iterations on 8-card Nvidia H100
server. To ensure a fair comparison, pruning was applied once at the same time point,
which is 15,000 iterations during training. For other hyperparameters, such as learning rate scheduling, spherical harmonics (SH) degree, and the opacity reset point,
densification operation, we adhered to the vanilla 3DGS configuration to maintain
consistency across all pruning techniques and datasets. For other comparison works,
some amount controlling parameters were added to balance the final primitive amount
into similar scale as close as possible.
Rendering The inference and rendering of all methods for benchmark was executed on one single NVIDIA H100 GPU with 80 GB HBM respectively. It’s ensured
no any additional CPU/GPU resources occupied meanwhile.
4.2 Datasets
To comprehensively assess the effectiveness of pruning techniques and importance
score functions, we trained each method on a diverse set of datasets, including MipNeRF360 Barron et al. (2022), Tanks&Temples Knapitsch et al. (2017), and Deep
Blending Hedman et al. (2018). For dataset usage, we adhered to standard practices. For the unbounded, real-world scenes in the MipNeRF360 dataset (comprising
four indoor and five outdoor scenes), Tanks&Temples (two outdoor scenes), and Deep
Blending (two indoor scenes), we used every 8th image for testing and the remaining
images for training, all at their original resolutions. Additionally, we constructed a
Synthetic dataset named gym scene, it’s a basketball gym scene, with rich textures on
the walls and floor. The dataset contains multiple different views from 101 cameras.

4.4 Quantitative Comparison of Compression Methods
We conducted systematic quantitative evaluations to compare our proposed MG
method with existing state-of-the-art approaches, including vanilla 3DGS Kerbl et al.
(2023), LG Fan et al. (2023), EG Liu et al. (2024), and MS Fang and Wang (2024).
All methods were configured to represent scenes using a range of [200, 500] thousand
primitives, representing a pruning of approximately 90% compared to vanilla 3DGS.
Note that RS Niemeyer et al. (2024) was excluded from the comparison due to its lack
of a controllable mechanism to achieve a specified number of primitives.
Evaluation Metrics Rendering quality was assessed using PSNR, SSIM Wang
et al. (2003), and LPIPS Zhang et al. (2018) metrics. Additionally, we measured the
number of primitives (in thousands) to ensure a fair comparison across methods with
equivalent levels of information density.
As shown in Table 3, the experimental results demonstrate that our proposed
MG method consistently outperforms other state-of-the-art pruning techniques across
various scenarios. Specifically, MG achieves the best performance in 78% of cases and
secures second place in 22% of instances across all datasets. A particularly notable
result is observed on the DeepBlending dataset Hedman et al. (2018), where MG
achieves a PSNR score of 29.63, significantly surpassing the results of the other three
methods, which range between 24.05 and 25.28. This substantial performance gap can
be attributed to the complex textural characteristics of the drjohnson scene. The MG
method excels in handling such complexity, accurately reconstructing intricate details
with exceptional fidelity.
Furthermore, the results on the Tanks&Temples dataset Knapitsch et al. (2017)
exceed expectations. MG achieves a PSNR score of 24.16, outperforming vanilla 3DGS
(23.77) while using only 14.1% of the primitives. Similarly, MG achieves results that
are very close to vanilla 3DGS across all three evaluation metrics and significantly
higher than other state-of-the-art pruning methods.
Extensive experiments confirm the superiority of our proposed MG method over
current state-of-the-art approaches, as demonstrated by quantitative benchmarks.
This advantage is both stable and generalizable across diverse datasets and scenarios.

5 Limitation
The densification and pruning processes in 3D Gaussian Splatting constitute a complex systematic engineering task, with multiple factors influencing the accuracy of the
final results. This paper aims to analyze the impact of each element and identify the
optimal solution. However, automating the selection of densification and pruning is
also essential to enhance efficiency and consistency. By systematically evaluating different parameters and strategies, we can better understand how each factor affects the
final rendering quality. Automated methods not only reduce human intervention but
also enable more precise control in complex scenarios. Future research could explore
intelligent algorithms and machine learning techniques to further optimize this process, leading to more efficient and accurate 3D reconstruction and rendering. This
advancement will contribute significantly to the fields of computer graphics and vision.

6 Conclusion
This paper proposes MiniGS, a memory-efficient Gaussian compression method featuring Full Factors Weighted (FFW) Importance Score Pruning and Variational Densify
Threshold (VDT). FFW selectively combines pixel coordinates, Gaussian distances,
and opacity to prune redundancy, achieving a 90% compression ratio while preserving critical primitives. Comprehensive experiments were conducted to determine the
optimal importance score option. VDT compensates for quality loss by recovering fine
details with low-scale primitives. We analysis the distribution of the different backward
gradient threshold and give the best value. For the overall method. Finally, Systematic
experiments on classic and synthetic datasets show MiniGS achieves the best result
in all evaluation metrics comparing with existing state-of-the-arts. In future research,
we will further explore the key factors influencing pruning efficiency and refine the
Gaussian pruning methodology for enhanced performance.
