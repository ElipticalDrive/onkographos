In Skeleton.refresh, computeBoundingSphere() is called for all buffer geometries (given that at least some tree changed somehow). For very large tracings (e.g, 2M nodes), these computations can consume ~100ms in total. However, often it is not needed to re-compute the bounding sphere.
One optimization possibility would be to only re-compute the sphere for affected buffers (for example, in the createNode() method we could mark that current buffer as affected).
Additionally, I noticed that loading a very large tracing will initially create a very big buffer geometry (due to Math.max(initialCapacity, MAX_CAPACITY) in initializeBufferCollection). However, changing that to Math.min(initialCapacity, MAX_CAPACITY) could also have a negative impact (we should measure this).
My gut tells me to at least split this large buffer into ten pieces or so. Then, the above optimization suggestion would at least cut the bounding sphere calculation to 1/10 on average.
