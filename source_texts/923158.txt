Summary
Replaces the scene's lighting system with a unified, physically-motivated approach: a procedural sky that serves as both the visible dome and the source of image-based lighting, a directional sun with a shadow frustum that follows the player, and screen-space ambient occlusion to restore contact shadows and depth cues to untextured geometry.
Key Changes
New Graphics Modules:
graphics/sunSky.ts: Centralized sun/sky math (direction, intensity, sky gradient, shadow frustum) with unit tests. Eliminates three independent, silently disagreeing light sources.
graphics/SkyEnvironment.tsx: Procedural sky dome and PMREM environment map baking. The sky gradient is tinted by weather fog color and responds to sun elevation, ensuring ambient lighting always matches the visible sky.
graphics/AmbientOcclusion.tsx: Screen-space ambient occlusion with bilateral blur. Darkens the ambient term in contact shadows, crevices, and underside geometry without affecting directly-lit surfaces. Takes over the render loop with a multi-pass approach (beauty pass → half-res AO → bilateral blur → composite).
Updated Scene Lighting:
scene/SunLight.tsx: Directional light with a following shadow camera. The frustum is centered ahead of the player's view direction and snapped to the shadow-map texel grid to eliminate edge crawl. Shadow density increases as the frustum shrinks, keeping shadows sharp where they matter.
scene/GameWorld.tsx: Removes the old <Sky>, ambientLight, hemisphereLight, and fixed directionalLight. Integrates the new sky, sun, and AO components.
Render Quality System:
app/renderQuality.ts & renderQuality.test.ts: Added AO preference toggle (defaults on for non-touch devices, gated by PRETTY tier). Persists to localStorage alongside shadows and tier preferences.
city/CityStatsOverlay.tsx: Exposes AO toggle in the stats overlay for A/B testing.
Implementation Details
Unified Sky Description: All lighting (dome, IBL, sun color, intensity) derives from sunSky.ts functions, eliminating the previous mismatch where the sky dome, shadow light, and fill light disagreed on sun position.
Depth-Aware AO: Samples depth with NEAREST filtering and snaps to full-res texel centers to avoid reading invented geometry at silhouettes. Bilateral blur weights by view-depth difference to prevent halos across edges.
Bright Pixel Protection: The AO composite uses luminance to detect directly-lit surfaces and protects them from darkening, ensuring AO only affects the ambient term.
Shadow Frustum Following: The shadow camera snaps to its own texel grid in light space, not world space, to hold shadow edges still as the player moves. Half-extent shrinks with distance to the player, doubling shadow-map density for the same texel budget.
Linear Rendering Path: The AO composite applies sRGB tone mapping explicitly (matching three's piecewise curve) so toggling AO cannot shift overall image brightness—only the occluded parts.

/practice, /play and /city all render through GameWorld, whose lighting was
three suns that never agreed -- a drei <Sky> drawing its disc at [120,28,40],
a shadow light at [48,42,18], a blue fill light from the opposite corner --
over an ambientLight + hemisphereLight pair that lit every surface in the
world to the same value regardless of which way it faced. Untextured geometry
read as cardboard, and because the shadow camera was pinned to the world
origin with a +/-90 m frustum, shadows in the city simply stopped a block out.

- graphics/sunSky.ts: one description of the sky. Sun direction (defaulted to
  the direction the old shadow light already had, so the key light does not
  jump), a weather-tinted sky gradient, sun intensity by elevation, and the
  shadow-frustum math. Pure, so it is unit-tested.
- graphics/SkyEnvironment.tsx: procedural sky shader used both as the visible
  dome and, via PMREM, as the scene environment map. Real directional skylight
  replaces the flat fill: up-facing surfaces catch sky, down-facing ones catch
  ground bounce, and a dust storm now lights the world brown.
- scene/SunLight.tsx: the sun follows the player, so shadows exist everywhere
  in the city instead of near the origin, with a frustum tightened to 48 m for
  4x the shadow-map density. Quantised to the shadow camera's own texel grid
  so the tighter, moving map does not crawl.
- graphics/AmbientOcclusion.tsx: SSAO restoring the contact cues a skylight
  cannot -- corners, undersides, the seam where debris meets the ground.
  Snapped NEAREST depth taps, a bilateral blur, and a composite that protects
  sunlit pixels so AO reads as occlusion rather than dirt.
- renderQuality.ts: SSAO gets its own persisted toggle (PRETTY + opt-in),
  alongside a button in the city overlay, for the same reason shadows have
  one -- it is a real fill cost and A/B-ing it should be one click.

Caught by screenshotting /practice with SSAO on and off: the same sky came out
at two different brightnesses. A raw ShaderMaterial gets three's tone-mapping
and colour-space helper functions in its shader prefix but not the steps that
call them, so the dome was writing linear radiance straight into an sRGB
canvas -- far too dark -- while the SSAO composite path, which does its own
encode, rendered it correctly. Adding both steps to main() makes the two paths
agree (measured sky luminance within 7%, ground within 2%).

Including the matching `_pars_` chunks is what does NOT work: those helpers are
already in the prefix and a second copy fails to compile. The PMREM bake wants
the raw radiance instead, so its copy of the material compiles both steps out.
