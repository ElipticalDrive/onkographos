ABSTRACT
In this dissertation attention is focused onto the problem of
image reconstruction from a limited (generally inadeguate) number of
para IleI-beam projections.
problem.
This is the standard tomograph i c imaging
Two series expansion approaches are developed with the
intent of obtaining approximate (rather than exact)
image recon­
structions.
One series expansion is based on the inverse Radon transform
(IRT) representation of an image.
A second approach - Hilbert-space
approximation (NSA) - is based on classical
theory.
linear L2~approximation
The key to the particular HSA approach is the use of the
measurement eguation kernels as the set of basis functions.
Within each series expansion approach, three different criteria
of optimal ity are used to solve an underdetermined (due to undersamp li ng in projections) vector measurement equation:
(1)
a minimum­
norm least-square (MNLS) criterion, (2) an anti-aliased (AA) recon­
struction criterion, and (3) a minimum-variance (MV) criterion.
Based
on a set of simulations and a reconstruction SNR performance measure,
the IRT estimator ranking from best to worse was found to be MV, AA
and MNLS.
Whereas for the HSA approach, the MV and MNLS estimates
were similar and the AA estimate was worst.
Furthermore, the best
IRT estimate was generally superior to the best HSA estimate.
A
significant feature of the AA and MV IRT estimates is that they yield
a variable resolution reconstruction.

INTRODUCTION
During the past several years, 3-D imaging problems in many
different fields have been successfully solved by taking a tomographic
imaging point of view.
With this viewpoint, a certain quantity
(function) which varies over a volume (R^) is imaged by stacking a
set of 2-D images associated with contiguous parallel planar regions
(slices) in the volume.
Each 2-D image is typically obtained by an
algorithm which processes measurements of the quantity (function) made
over a slice, i.e. a tomogram (from the Greek word tomo (meaning
section or slice) and graphy (meaning representation)).
Tomography is
best known for its application in diagnostic medicine where it is used
in radiographic (x-ray)

sonic (8-101 and recently in nuclear magnetic resonance (11-121
to obtain pictures of the body or brain.
