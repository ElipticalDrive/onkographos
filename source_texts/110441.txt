ABSTRACT
The advancement in fields like multimedia, medical imagery and emergence of
high resolution digital cameras has necessitated the acquisition, storage and transmission
of high resolution digital images. Storage and transmission of such images are expensive
in terms of bytes and bandwidth. There is a need for compressing these images to curtail
the storage and transmission budget. A bewildering variety of image compression
schemes featuring new concepts and techniques have been proposed to yield superior
compression quality.
Hybrid Vector Scalar Quantization (HVSQ) is one such novel compression
scheme used for compressing high resolution cervigram image archives of the US
National Library of Medicine. It is a lossy compression scheme comprising of two of the
well known quantization techniques namely Vector and Scalar Quantization in the
wavelet domain. This thesis focuses on implementation and optimization of the Vector
Quantization module of HVSQ to yield higher image quality in terms of Peak Signal to
Noise Ratio (PSNR) and lower encoding time. The thesis also focuses on evaluating the
performance of Vector, Scalar and Hybrid Vector Scalar Quantization. The superior
performance HVSQ is then verified by compressing a few high resolution natural images.

CHAPTER I
INTRODUCTION
The famous Moore's law, which states that every 18 months the speed of
computer doubles, has an evil twin: every 18 months software becomes twice as slow. A
similar relationship can be formulated for images: no matter how big the memory budget
of the images may seem to be, memory could be filled up much faster than expected. The
appetite for bytes grows faster than the rate at which the memory budget grows.
The total number of bits required to store digital images is extremely large.
Often this number exceeds the number of bits intended for efficient storage and fast
transmission. Images contain statistical redundancy and perceptual irrelevancy which
contribute to the total number of bits required to represent them. The science of obtaining
a compact representation of an image while maintaining all the necessary information is
referred to as image compression.
Image compression can be broadly classified into two types, namely Lossless
compression and Lossy compression. Lossless compression is also referred to as
reversible, entropy or noiseless coding [2] as it enables complete recovery of the original
image from the compressed data. The main draw back of this method is low compression
ratio, Especially in the case of images, the compression ratio could be as low as 2:1.
Some of the common Lossless coding schemes are Run-Length coding, Huffman coding
and Arithmetic coding [4]. Lossy compression is also referred to as irreversible
compression. Its main advantage is higher compression ratio, however, lossy
compression introduces some error in the data by eliminating some information and the
1
original data cannot be recovered completely. Quantization forms the heart of any lossy
compression scheme. Quantization involves representation of a set of fine resolution data
by a coarse resolution approximation.
Lossy compression can be broadly classified into 2 types, namely Scalar
Quantization (SQ) and Vector Quantization (VQ). SQ involves processing the input
samples individually whereas VQ involves processing the input samples in groups. An
SQ involves mapping of each individual input to an output using some distortion
measure. VQ involves mapping a group of inputs into a set of well defined vectors using
some distortion measure. According to Shannon’s Rate Distortion Theory [4], it has been
proved that better results would be obtained by processing a block of data as vectors
instead of processing the data individually as scalars because vectors makes good use of
the statistics of the signal.
Hybrid Vector Scalar Quantization (HVSQ) is a novel lossy image compression
scheme developed for compressing high resolution images at high compression ratios.
HVSQ involves a two pass hybrid quantization scheme in the wavelet domain [8].
Quantization is an important and key step that distinguishes HVSQ from other
popular compression schemes. Quantization in HVSQ involves a hybrid combination of
VQ and SQ. The data is first quantized using VQ to capture only the significant
information. SQ is then applied on the VQ residuals to further quantize the residual data
and thereby improving the quality of the reconstructed images.
2
1.1 Motivation
The VQ module of HVSQ features online codebook training unlike offline
training in traditional VQ techniques. A codebook is generated on the fly for every image
which is then transmitted along with the vector indices. The main drawbacks of
traditional VQ techniques are the timing overheads introduced during a long codebook
training process which is generally dependent on the number of codewords, size and
number of input vectors and poor selection of the initial codewords leading to a codebook
that is not optimal. If the codebook is not optimal the reconstructed image will be poor in
quality. In order to overcome these problems, a fast and efficient codebook training
algorithm generating an optimal codebook on the fly during the encoding process of each
image is desired. The reduction in VQ overheads together with the functional advantages
of VQ makes HVSQ superior in performance to mere SQ techniques like JPEG 2000 and
SPIHT [8].
1.2 Objective
This thesis focuses on the design, implementation and optimization of the vector
quantization module in HVSQ .The thesis also focuses on extensive analysis of VQ and
SQ modules individually and the behavior of overall HVSQ. The VQ module is
implemented for 3 algorithms namely the classical LBG Algorithm [5] referred to as
OLBG, Enhanced LBG Algorithm (ELBG) [6, 7] and Simplified ELBG (SELBG) and a
qualitative comparison is obtained in terms of Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR) and
encoding time.
3
1.3 Organization
The thesis is organized as follows. Chapter 2 discusses the basics of the wavelet
transform and how it is useful for image compression. This chapter is presented because
quantization is performed on the wavelet coefficients. Chapter 3 gives the basic frame

Chapter 4 gives a detailed explanation of VQ and the implementation details of the

WAVELETS IN IMAGE COMPRESSION
The wavelet transform is more efficient than Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) in
re organizing signals/images prior to image compression. DCT based compression
algorithms are optimal for stationary signals whereas wavelet transform due to the
localization of basis functions in both space and frequency domains are optimal for
representing and compressing non-stationary signals [16]. Mathematical representations
of wavelets have been developed by a number of scientists. However, Ingrid Daubechies
(1998) first showed the link between wavelets and FIR filters thus initiating the
application of wavelets to signal processing issues. Mallat (1989) developed the theory of
multi-resolution signal and image decomposition using wavelet representation.
Some of the essential quality criteria of wavelet bases for optimal performance in
image compression [16] are
•
Wavelet Regularity
•
Number of vanishing moments
•
Spatial characterization of the scaling function
•
Characterization of the associated filters
These are briefly described in this chapter together with the basic definition of
wavelets.

2.6 Quality criteria for wavelets used in image compression
Wavelet regularity
Regularity refers to the smoothness of the wavelets. Since most natural images are
perceptually smooth except for a few edges it would be favorable to use regular wavelets
[16]. This would ensure that the reconstructed image exhibits lesser visible artifacts.
8
Number of vanishing moments
Vanishing moments refers to the oscillatory nature of the wavelets. The regularity of
wavelets is determined by the number of vanishing moments. This directly impacts the
compression rate [16] as it enables the cancellation of wavelet coefficients of a signal
having a degree less than the number of vanishing moments.
Spatial characterization of the scaling function φ (x)
The spatial characterization enables the determination of energy concentration, spatial
length and localization of the scaling function. This criteria is applicable even for the
wavelet function. This is achieved using a parameter m k [16] such that:

Characterization of associated filters
The filters used in the wavelet transform must possess a linear phase because it affects
the quality of the reconstructed image as a non linear phase would degrade the edges in
the reconstructed image [16]. The impulse response of the filter must be finite and
contain few elements as it would make the convolution operations computationally less
expensive. HVSQ supports 3 wavelet filters namely Haar, Daub 4 and Daub 9/7. Daub
9/7 has been used for all the experiments in this thesis.
9
Daubechies wavelet transforms developed by Ingrid Daubechies have very good
orthogonal properties. They are also called max flat filters as the frequency response of
these filters has maximum flatness at frequencies 0 and π. The coefficients of orthogonal
filters are real numbers. The filters are of the same length and are not symmetric.
The Daubechies 9/7 filters are also called Cohen Daubechies Feauveau filters and
are the most widely used bi-orthogonal wavelets. These wavelets have symmetric scaling
and wavelet functions, i.e., both the low pass and high pass filters are symmetric. In the
case of the biorthogonal wavelet filters, the low pass and the high pass filters do not have
the same length. The analysis filter has 9 coefficients and the synthesis filter has 7
coefficients. The filter with 7 coefficients is chosen as synthesis filter as it gives a
smoother scaling function than the 9 coefficient filter. The rational filter length of
Daubechies 9/7 filter yields very good performance for applications such as compression
as it separates the low frequency and high frequency information very well with less
aliasing redundancy. Experiments have proven their superior performance with lossy
compression schemes [15]. It is widely used in the JPEG 2000 image compression
Standard. Daub 9/7 has been used for all the experiments in this thesis.

The idea of applying wavelet coding for image compression emerged due two
main reasons. Firstly, since the wavelet coefficients have a generalized gaussian
distribution it convenient to mathematically model the wavelet coefficients. Secondly, the
11
efficiency of coding an image using wavelets is higher than coding directly with the
intensity values [10]. This is because wavelets pack more information into fewer
coefficients and thus decorrelates the data. Wavelets also retain the spatial information
allowing the compression codec to exploit both spatial and frequency information. The
remaining coefficients can be coarsely quantized or zeroed out. As the number of filter
taps increases the information packing ability increases as well. The Haar wavelet
enables 46 % of the total transform to be zeroed out; biorthogonal wavelets like 9/7
enable 55 % of the transform to be zeroed out.
The wavelets are constructed using the lifting scheme which has the following
advantages. It enables faster implementation of the wavelet transform. Wavelets
calculated using the sub band transforms and the lifting scheme makes optimal use of the
similarities between the lowpass and highpass filters thereby reducing the computational
complexity. It also enables in-place computation where the image is replaced with its
wavelet coefficients. No additional memory is required. The transforms built using the
lifting scheme are invertible and the inverse transforms have almost the same
computational complexity as the forward transforms. The number of decomposition
levels affects the reconstruction quality. Experiments have proven that as the number of
wavelet levels increases the coding efficiency increases thereby giving superior
reconstruction [15]. It saturates after a certain number of levels. This has been verified
experimentally in chapter 5. After a certain number of levels the quality saturates or even
drops slightly. For optimal performance 4 or 5 level decomposition is a good choice.

3.1 Image Compression Preliminaries
Before arriving at a model for image compression, various types of redundancies
present in images [10] are discussed below. They are
•
Coding Redundancy.
•
Interpixel Redundancy.
•
Psychovisual Redundancy.
Coding Redundancy occurs due to the assignment of code symbols to different
pixels of the image which does not take into account the probability of occurrence of gray
levels. This can be eliminated by assigning variable length codes to the pixels depending
on the frequency of occurrence of gray levels. The higher the frequency, the shorter the
length of the code symbols. This technique is called Huffman coding.
Interpixel redundancy occurs due to the high correlation present in images. This
happens due to the objects or the geometry of the image, resulting in highly correlated
pixels.

Psycho visual redundancy is due to the sensitivity with which the human eye
observes an image. A lot of information is perceptually masked due to the biological
nature of the human visual system. Since all the information within an image does not
contribute to the visual perception, the psycho visually redundant data is eliminated using
quantization techniques resulting in a reduction of the amount of data representing the
image.

In figure 3.2, the source encoder is involved in removal of all the image redundancies
discussed above. It comprises of a mapper which maps the image into an array of
coefficients and a quantizer that approximates the mapped coefficients using some
fidelity measure. A symbol encoder then performs fixed or variable length coding like
Huffman coding [10] on the quantized data for reducing the total number of bits used to
store the quantized coefficients.
The Channel encoder adds noise immunity to the output of the source encoder.
This is essential as the output of the source encoder has very little or no redundancy and
if the channel is noisy then the source encoder data would be very sensitive to channel
noise, so the channel encoder would introduce a small amount of redundancy in a
controlled manner. One of the common channel coding techniques is called Hamming
code [Hamming 1950]. It is based on appending some bits to the data to ensure that a
minimum number of bits are changed between valid codewords. The source decoder
comprises of a symbol decoder and an inverse mapper and performs the reverse operation
of the symbol coder and mapper.

3.2.1 Pre Processing
The input image is subjected to a sequence of preprocessing steps. The first step is
to read the image specified by the user. Currently HVSQ Supports 5 different color
spaces namely RBG, YCbCr (Reversible Color Transform), YCbCr (Irreversible Color
Transform), YIQ and HSV. It is to be noted that in this thesis all the work is done in
YCbCr plane. This is because best compression results are achieved if the color
components are un correlated and YCbCr is ideal for this as most of the information is
concentrated in the luminance plane and very little in the chrominance plane. Hence, the
luminance components and chrominance components can be allocated bits in the ratios of
4:1:1, 2:1:1 or 1:1:1. A linear transformation is done for converting from RGB to the
YCbCr plane. The DC content of each channel is removed by subtracting the mean of all
16
the pixels. This helps in eliminating the inter pixel redundancy. The mean is added back

The lossy compression scheme can be analysed using Rate distortion theory [4].

distortion measure D there is a function called R(D) referred to as the rate distortion
function [4]. If D is tolerable then R (D) is the best possible compression rate.

VQ is a very powerful method for lossy compression of data such as images and
speech. A vector quantizer can be considered as a generalization of a scalar quantizer.
In the case of VQ a set of symbols is grouped /combined to represent the input
and is referred to as an input pattern or input vectors. A vector can describe almost any
type of signal such as speech or image.
A vector quantizer Q, of dimension k and size N is a mapping from a vector in k

points called code vectors or codewords. Mathematically it is defined as:

4.2 Advantages of VQ over SQ
VQ can be considered as a pattern recognizer where an input pattern is
approximated by a predetermined set of standard patterns. Experiments have shown that
VQ produces superior performance over SQ even when the components of the input
vectors are statistically independent [4].VQ exploits the linear and nonlinear dependence
among vectors. It also provides flexibility in choosing multi dimensional quantizer cell
shapes and in choosing a desired codebook size. If SQ is extended to k dimensional
vectors using N levels, then the codebook would contain N k codewords. In the case of
VQ there could be arbitrary partitions with integer number of codewords N. Another
advantage of VQ over SQ is the fractional value of resolution that is achievable. This is
very important for low bit rate applications where low resolution is sufficient.

The encoder is responsible for mapping the input vectors into a set of predefined

proves the need for generating codewords that best represents the input data set. All that
needs to be transmitted to the decoder are the indices and codewords. Thus VQ results in

4.4 Optimality of a Quantizer
The quantizer is considered to be globally optimal if there is no other codebook that gives
a lower value of the distortion D. The quantizer is considered to be locally optimal if a
26
small perturbation of the codewords does not decrease the distortion D. This shows that
the necessary conditions are sufficient to prove the local optimality because a slight
distortion of codewords will not vary the boundary. If the boundary stays fixed then
distortion causes the centroid condition to be violated and the average distortion could
increase.
The necessary conditions of optimality stated above indicate that a vector
quantizer converges to optimality iteratively. The process starts with an initial codebook
then a new codebook is found in the next iteration. This forms the new vector quantizer
with a statistical distortion less than the previous quantizer. This process repeats until the
distortion decreases to a set threshold. Although the steps mentioned for optimization
appear to be simple, there is no closed form solution to the optimal quantization problem.
The selection of the initial set of codewords is a very tricky problem in any VQ
design. A variety of techniques are available in the literature for the initial selection of
codewords [4].
A few of them are explained below
•
Random Coding: Here a set of random vectors are chosen from the input vectors
and this serves as the codewords. This may result in the selection of a set of
codewords which poorly represents the input vectors.
•
Pruning: The entire training set is considered for the codebook. The training
vectors are selectively eliminated leaving behind a final set of vectors which serve
as the codewords. The first training vector is entered into the codebook. Next, the
distortion between this codeword and the next input is found. If it is below a
certain threshold we discard (prune) it, else it is entered into the codebook as the
27
second codeword. This is done for each training vector where the distortion with
each codeword is found, if it is not above a certain threshold we discard it else it
is entered as a codeword.
•
Pair wise Nearest Neighbor Design: This method begins with the entire training
set (with N vectors like in the previous method and ends in a smaller set of L
vectors) which serve as codewords. But unlike the pervious method the final
vectors need not be a subset of the training sequence. The process starts by
computing the distortion between each pair of input vectors. The pair yielding
minimum distortion is combined into a single cluster. Now there are N-1 vectors
remaining. Next, two more vectors are found from the remaining N-1 vectors
which upon merging give a decrease in the average distortion. This process is
continued until we are left with L vectors [4].
•
Splitting: This method was proposed by Linde et al. The initial codeword C is
chosen by taking the centroid of the entire training sequence. Then, this codeword
is split into two namely C+ ε and C- ε , where ε is any Euclidean norm (it is an
indicator of optimization precision). This process of iteratively splitting each
codeword into two continues until the desired resolution of the codebook is
obtained [4]

4.8 Simplified Enhanced LBG (SELBG) Algorithm
The SELBG Algorithm [8] is based on the ELBG algorithm proposed by [6, 7]
but it is a simplified version of the algorithm as it combines the splitting and merging of
codewords in the main LBG algorithm itself. There is no separate module like in the
ELBG algorithm which takes care of the splitting and merging. In SELBG during the
main LBG’s process of binary splitting and codebook optimization, the codewords are
split or eliminated (instead of merging as in ELBG) based on their utility values thereby
resulting in equalization of codeword distortion. Ideally SELBG produces superior results
than ELBG as it gives a globally optimum solution unlike the sub optimal solution
produced by ELBG and locally optimum solution produced by LBG.
Description of the algorithm
1. Initialization
Yo is the initial codeword formed by taking the mean of the entire set of input vectors, the
value of ε is fixed, the total distortion D −1 is initialized, the utility of the first codeword
is initialized to 1, the codeword count Nc is set to 1, the desired number of codewords is
Ncmax (specified by the user) and Nctarget is the number of target codewords in the
current iteration, the iteration number n is set to 0.
2. Calculation of Codewords
New codewords are formed by splitting the current codewords starting from a
high value of utility using the binary splitting technique until Nctarget number of
codewords are formed. After splitting, there is no local rearrangement of codewords like
in ELBG instead the codebook optimization step follows and the vectors are reassigned
globally. Thus SELBG is considered to produce a globally optimal solution.
46
3. Codebook Optimization
The new codebook calculated in step 2 is optimized using the centroid condition. If D n−1
is the distortion before the optimization begins and D n is the distortion after the
optimization then we assign r= (D n −1 -D n )/ D n . Step 3 repeats until r ≤ ε . In this stage,
codewords are updated using the centroid condition. For each codeword the utility is
evaluated similar to the ELBG Algorithm (refer to section 4.6).
4. The merging step in SELBG is implemented by simply eliminating the codewords that
have a low value of utility. When codewords are eliminated the codeword count reduces.
Step 2 is repeated to form new codewords until Nctarget is reached and then steps 3 and
4 follow. When there are no codewords with low utility value there would be no
elimination of codewords. n increments, Nctarget is doubled and the steps 2,3 & 4 repeat.
5. Termination Condition
The algorithm would be terminated if Ncmax number of codewords is formed. The flow

4.9 Implementation of VQ in HVSQ
The VQ module of HVSQ supports all the 3 algorithms discussed in the previous
sections. The quantization module follows the pre processing block. The user has to
specify the codeword size and codeword dimension along with other parameters like the
bit rate ratio for each channel of the image and number of wavelet levels. All the pre
processing steps mentioned previously are applied to the input image. It is to be noted
that for the current application a low codeword count is sufficient as VQ essentially
captures only the significant information from the image and SQ efficiently captures the
residual data. A high codeword count would result in a better vector quantizer but it
increases the computational complexity and embeds a large codebook which is undesired.
Once the input vectors are generated codebook training is started. In all the three
algorithms the binary splitting technique is used for iteratively generating the desired
number of codewords. The mean of all the input vectors serves as the first codeword. It is
then split into two codewords, if C is the initial code word, C (1+ ε ) and C (1- ε ) are
obtained after splitting. ε is fixed at 0.01 throughout this thesis. In the case of OLBG
after the codewords are generated the partition using Nearest Neighbor condition is
calculated and the optimized codewords using the centroid condition is calculated. If the
desired number of codewords is not generated then each codeword is split into two and
the whole process repeats.
In the case of the ELBG Algorithm, after the calculation of optimized codewords
using the centroid condition, the MQE of the entire codebook is calculated and it is
compared with the MQE of the previous iteration. If there is a significant difference, then
the codebook is not optimum and the ELBG block discussed is executed. SELBG also
49
generates the desired number of codewords using binary splitting. The SELBG algorithm
is implemented as mentioned in section 4.8.
After the codewords are generated, each element of the codeword contains a 32
bit floating point number. This contributes significantly to the bit budget, so each element
is quantized to an 8 bit number by retaining the significant bits. Hence the codebook size
reduces by one fourth. The remaining three fourth of the bits are allocated for scalar
quantization where the VQ errors are compensated.
The design of the scalar quantization module is beyond the scope of this thesis,
the thesis involves a qualitative comparison between the implemented VQ and SQ
techniques and how they behave when combined.
4.10 Bit Budget in HVSQ
One of the most difficult issues in any compression scheme is to fit in all the
compressed data within the available bit budget. Typically, a color image has 3 channels
and each pixel typically takes 8 bits per channel for representation. Therefore a pixel
takes 8 X 3 = 24 bits. So the bit rate is 24 bits per pixel (bpp). An image of resolution 512
X 512 would occupy 786432 bytes or 786 KB. Assume that an 80:1 compression of this
image is required. The bit rate for this compression ratio would be 0.3 bpp. The size of
the compressed image would be 9.8 KB.
In HVSQ the available bit budget is shared between VQ and SQ. First VQ is
executed and the bit requirement in VQ is dependent on the codeword size and number of
codewords. The rate of VQ with out including the codebook is given by R= log 2 cwc/cws
50
[8] where cwc is the number of codewords, cws is the codeword size. If the codebook
size is included then the total rate would be R’=R + Rc,
where Rc= (cwc X cws X rc)/ (L X cws) = cwc X rc /L
(4.21)
and L is the total number of vectors. rc is the number of bits taken by each element of
the codeword. In order to reduce the number of bits taken by the codewords rc is kept
low (it is quantized to 8 from 32 bits as discussed before). After VQ the remaining bits
are allocated for the scalar quantized residual data.

CHAPTER VI
CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK
Lossy image compression techniques such as VQ and SQ have proven to produce
very high compression ratios and superior reconstruction quality that are essential for
storage and transmission of high resolution images such as medical images. Since HVSQ
is a hybrid combination of VQ and SQ, it provides the advantages of both vector and
scalar quantization. The drawbacks of the traditional VQ techniques in terms of timing
over heads and poor quality codebooks due to poor initialization were overcome by
ELBG and SELBG. The drawbacks of VQ in terms of long offline training of a global
codebook were overcome by generating a local codebook on the fly for each image that
best represented the input vectors.
The performance evaluation plots have clearly given the distinction between
OLBG, ELBG and SELBG. ELBG and SELBG consistently performed better than
OLBG in terms of PSNR and encoding time for all the images tested. The qualitative
comparison between HVSQ and SQ revealed the superior performance of HVSQ and this
can be attributed to VQ. ELBG and SELBG have helped in narrowing down the encoding
time difference between HVSQ and SQ by converging the algorithm faster by a smart
migration of codewords. Apart from the improvement in quality measures such as PSNR
and Encoding time, as shown in figures 5.7 (e),(f),(g) the reconstructed images obtained
using ELBG and SELBG have shown lesser blocky artifacts than that of OLBG.
Even though the high performance of HVSQ was experimentally verified, there
are a few shortcomings and some of them are worth getting some attention. Currently for
all the three algorithms namely OLBG, ELBG and SELBG the number of codewords is

fixed initially. It is desirable to have a fully automatic clustering algorithm that decides
the number of codewords to be chosen based on the statistics of the input vectors to
obtain the highest possible reconstruction quality. Vectors from the approximation band
were considered for quantization. It is worth evaluating the performance by considering
the high frequency bands for quantization. SELBG did not attain a globally optimal
solution practically and it is worth looking into this issue. In this thesis, the bit rate ratio
for each channel is fixed at 1:1:1.The process of selecting an optimum bitrate ratio for
