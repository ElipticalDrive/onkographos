## Why

Extend continuous collision detection from finite convex pairs to finite
convex bodies against infinite planes, including rotating finite bodies
and translating kinematic planes.

## Convex-plane TOI
- exact deepest finite-shape support point against the current plane
normal
- predicted finite-body transform under linear + constant world angular
velocity
- translating plane support
- signed support distance
- conservative advancement with finite-body angular sweep bound
- binary refinement of first threshold crossing
- A->B normal semantics where A is the finite convex body and B is the
plane
- witness point on the finite shape and projected witness on the plane
- initial-overlap contract
- bounded iteration/tolerance validation
- rotating infinite planes fail closed because no finite global sweep
radius exists

## Relative pair admission
General CCD admission now uses relative pair motion rather than only the
continuous body's own translation:
- relative linear travel
- bounded angular travel for both finite bodies
- threshold radius from continuous participants
- one continuous dynamic body can protect a pair even when the other
body supplies most relative motion

This covers stationary continuous characters struck by fast moving
platforms/kinematic planes.

## Global CCD
- existing analytic sphere/static-plane path remains authoritative when
available
- general convex-plane path fills unsupported finite-convex cases
- zero-time separating overlaps remain with the discrete solver
- canonical lexical TOI ordering preserved
- world atomic rollback remains the failure boundary

## Validation
Canonical regressions cover:
- exact box/plane linear TOI
- rotation-only long-box contact
- translating kinematic plane
- moving-away rejection
- rotating-plane fail-closed behavior
- initial overlap
- global CCD event generation
- stationary continuous body hit by moving plane
- noncontinuous exclusion
- zero-time separating suppression
- lexical normal flipping
- full-world fast box anti-tunneling through plane
- full-world stationary box hit by kinematic plane

## Why

Add authored finite-convex collision assets on top of #1131.
Primitive-only physics is not enough for serious game scenes; hulls need
deterministic topology, physical mass properties, queries, collision,
and CCD without introducing import-order identity drift.

## ConvexHullShape
- canonical lexical vertex ordering
- triangle index remapping and canonical cyclic rotation
- deterministic triangle ordering
- unique-vertex and unique-triangle validation
- non-degenerate triangles
- closed-manifold edge validation
- consistent directed winding validation
- global winding canonicalization by signed volume
- per-face convexity validation
- deterministic support mapping
- transformed AABB
- support-radius bound for angular CCD

## Physical properties
Exact signed-tetrahedron integration:
- enclosed volume / mass
- centroid
- second moments
- full inertia tensor
- parallel-axis shift to COM
- roundoff-scale centroid stabilization only for numerically symmetric
hulls

Centered authored hulls can be dynamic today. Genuine offset centers of
mass still fail closed until compound-body COM support lands.

## Engine integration
- complete topology bound into PhysicsWorld configuration/state identity
- GJK/EPA finite-convex collision against
sphere/box/capsule/cylinder/hull
- support-plane contacts
- general moving-convex CCD and convex-plane CCD participation
- deterministic triangle raycasts
- inside-origin ray semantics
- support-mapped sphere casts via conservative TOI
- public ConvexHullShape API

## Validation

- source vertex/triangle permutation invariance
- global winding reversal canonicalization
- exact cube mass, COM and inertia
- centered dynamic hull construction
- offset-COM fail-closed contract
- support/AABB transforms

- duplicate vertex rejection
- open mesh rejection

- non-convex closed mesh rejection
- hull/sphere contact
- hull/hull separation and overlap
- plane/hull penetration
- exact face raycast and inside-origin ray
- hull sphere cast
- continuous hull global TOI
- world solver response against a plane
