Universal per-pixel occlusion, proven by a mesh z-buffer oracle
Supersedes #136 (structure slices) and #128 (Canvas2D parity). Visible change. Do not self-merge; ready for coordinator review.
The model (one rule, both renderers)
The projection sy = 16·(rx+ry) − 16·z keeps a pixel fixed along the view ray (1, 1, 2) (tiles, tiles, levels). So every point on one pixel's ray has a distinct world height, and per-pixel occlusion reduces to: the highest surface point at that pixel wins.
Sprites. Every sprite is rasterised from a mesh (tools/sprites). The rasteriser now also bakes each pixel's height above the anchor. The packer writes a parallel RGBA8 height page per atlas page. Pixel height = anchor height + baked height × (drawn / frame height).
WebGL2.
Sprites draw with DEPTH_TEST and write gl_FragDepth = 0.5 − h/1024.
Ground depth comes from a depth-only pass over the visible terrain and cliff meshes: an empty fragment shader, vertex-derived depth (exact at any zoom), and a polygon offset so ground loses exact ties. The zoom-1 ground cache now paints colour only.
Water, patrol overlays and previews draw in a translucent pass afterwards, depth-tested with depthMask(false).
Canvas2D.
Ground is painted first.
Then a software depth composite (depth-surface.ts, depth-composite.ts) z-tests every atlas drawImage by height, against span-rasterised terrain heights cached per view.
It uses the same sampling, fill rule and tie rule as WebGL2.
Deleted. SOLID_BIAS, PERSON_BIAS, GROUND_COVER_BIAS, the queue-rail and litter biases, SurfaceDepths/personDepth, pathSlabDepth, queueRailDepth, terrain-occlusion.ts (occluder redraw keys), the water/bank key offsets, adjustDepthForGroundZ, and ride-floor tracking.
depth-key.ts is now one unbiased anchorKey. It only orders the translucent pass and settles exact ties.
Multi-tile rides, entrances, exits, gates, track, vehicles and people get no special cases.
Placement fixes, not biases.
People, litter and path props stand on the path mesh's walking surface (PATH_SURFACE_Z, 1/16 level) instead of inside the slab.
Flowerbed soil sits 1px above the terrain; it was exactly coplanar and z-fought.
Found along the way: a Canvas2D bug that also exists on main. Tile culling kept no margin below the view, so raised paths and track over off-screen terrain vanished.
src/render/docs.md has an "Occlusion model" section describing all of this.
Park boundary and land overlay (owner report 2026-10-27)
Boundary fence. The park boundary is now real geometry: a generated rail-and-post fence (park-fence wall sprites with baked heights) on every edge of owned land that faces unowned land or the map border. Both renderers draw it through the wall path, so track, supports, trees and rides in front of it hide it.
Land overlay. The overlay (the wash over unowned land, plus the dashed land and construction-rights sale borders) lies on the terrain surface:
WebGL2 draws it as translucent tile meshes in the depth-tested translucent pass;
Canvas2D paints it per tile during the terrain walk, before sprites are composited over it.
Shown only with land tools. setLandOverlay turns it on only while the land, buy-land or buy-rights tool is selected, as in RCT2.
The screen-space OwnershipOverlay canvas is gone.
Scored. Both have object kinds, the overlay is in the oracle as terrain-surface triangles, and fuzz parks cede the land past the coaster's middle so the boundary crosses track and supports.
Fix found while scoring. The oracle and Canvas2D compared double-precision heights against Float32-stored ones, which broke exact coplanar ties into row stripes.
Full matrix with fence and overlay: WebGL2 0.059% of 6.5M object px, Canvas2D 0.000%, no pair over 2%. The fence and overlay pairs are all at or below 1.26% (support>land-overlay).
Proofs: boundary-* in pr-139/ (see comment).
Oracle
occlusion-oracle.ts z-buffers the placed sprites (anchor + baked height) and the terrain/water triangles into an object-ID image. occlusion-oracle.test.ts proves this equals one joint z-buffer over the meshes.
occlusion-metric.ts scores a renderer's ID capture against it, with a per-object-pair breakdown. Both renderers have captureObjectIds.
src/dev/occlusion-fuzz.ts builds dense random parks through real sim commands: flat, sloped and terraced ground; paths, queues and additions; scenery and walls; flat and multi-tile rides; a coaster with supports and train; stalls, gate, entrances and exits; people at sub-tile positions, including at gates, entrances and exits, in queues, under shelters and on ramps; litter.
Ambiguous samples. Samples within 1/100 texel of a covered/clear texel boundary or a sprite edge are marked ambiguous and left out of the score, because GPU interpolation precision decides them. The report counts them.
CI gate. smoke/occlusion-oracle.spec.ts runs seed 1 × 4 rotations × zoom 1 and 2 × both renderers in about 2.6 min. e2e/occlusion-oracle.spec.ts runs the full matrix (seeds 1-3).
Caveat. The oracle scores ordering given the renderer's own placements. For Canvas2D it is close to a tautology, because the composite uses the oracle's rule. The independent Canvas2D check is the WebGL2-vs-Canvas2D object-kind parity in e2e/people-depth.spec.ts.

Tests
e2e/people-depth.spec.ts, rewritten as an oracle test. Same scenes and guest positions as before, plus:
a hill scene;
guests all around a tree, a stall and a bench;
guests under a raised path (the old bridge-deck case).
It checks every pixel against the oracle in both renderers and caps guest-involving mismatches at 8 px per position. It also checks WebGL2-vs-Canvas2D kind parity (≤1% of pixels both show as objects). 36/36 pass.
The old painter assertions ("a guest is never covered by its path tile", "a tile object beats a guest inside its tile") contradict the meshes. A guest standing 0.1 tile behind a kerb really has its feet covered. They are replaced, which acceptance (b) allows.
e2e/renderer-depth.spec.ts asserted painter rules with heightless solid-colour atlases. It is superseded and removed.
Unit tests:
depth-composite.test.ts: id-for-id equality with the oracle on 20 random scenes with ground;
fill-rule and ambiguity tests;
the anchor-key test.
e2e/rotation-ownership.spec.ts passes. Three fixture fixes, each failing the same way with main's ordering:
the ride's entrance and exit huts sat on the ride's own footprint, which the sim refuses;
the solid-colour atlas had no height pages; it now does, so it exercises the real depth path;
litter is a few pixels tall, so it is now sampled just above its pivot.
smoke/ui-icons.spec.ts fixed (it also failed on main's CI). The second click on Park toggled the already-open window closed, and the Actions lookup also matched another button.
Full unit suite (1,675 tests), lint, format and typecheck pass. The smoke suite passes locally, including the new occlusion gate.
Performance
Pixel 8 numbers are not real yet. This runs in headless Chromium on SwiftShader (a CPU rasteriser, no GPU), so nothing here is a real Pixel 8 measurement.
pnpm bench --guests 5000: p99 1.31 ms/tick, PASS. The sim is unchanged.
WebGL2 draw wall time, dense fuzz park, 1280×800, warmed, median of 9 × 20 frames: before 2.56–2.81 ms, after 3.34–3.51 ms. That is +25–30% on SwiftShader, almost all of it the full-screen depth-only terrain pass.
That pass is the price of exact ground depth. Limiting it to raised tiles made track and supports below the ground surface show through (ground>track 11%), so I reverted that.
On a GPU a depth-only pass with an empty fragment shader is cheap. A fragment-bound CPU rasteriser is the worst case.
Needs a real Pixel 8 profile before merge. If it misses the 10% budget, the follow-up is to draw the depth pass only under tiles that some sprite overlaps.
Canvas2D: dense park at 1280×800, median 38–44 ms after vs 46–56 ms before. Removing the painter-era work offsets the composite.
smoke/mobile-fps (Pixel 8 emulation, 4× CPU throttle): soft budget missed both before and after on SwiftShader (4–7 FPS). Adaptive resolution settled at different scales (0.60 vs 1.00), so these numbers aren't comparable.
