What
This adds motion blur from the camera's own movement. It runs only in the pastel look and only while the camera moves.
Each pixel's point is rebuilt from depth, moved into the last frame's view and projected there. Half of the way between the two positions is averaged over 8 reads of the scene target (app/_components/motion-blur.ts). That half is a 180° shutter, capped at 32 px.
The CPU measures the frame's motion on a 3×3 grid at 3 m and 1 km (lib/city/motion-blur.ts, unit-tested). It blurs only while the lens blur is already off (regressed):
Desktop: once half of the samples move 2 px.
Phone: once all of them do. There the blur replaces the FXAA, so a far city that barely moved would otherwise get neither blur nor antialiasing.
Result: turns, strafes and flights blur; walking straight ahead stays sharp and antialiased.
When it is drawn sharp:
a jump of more than 600 px, or a point behind the last camera;
a new size or projection;
the first frame after the page comes back to the front.
Where it is off: the graphic styles (Comic, Strich, Sin City, Papier, Schwarzplan, Film noir) and Modell. Film noir runs through the styled pipeline, and adding the blur there would mean another pipeline per style.
No velocity output and no frame history: the clay has no inter-stage variables left, and either one would need a screen-sized target.
Cost
Memory
GPU while moving
Phone (FXAA)
+0: no target. Its last pass is warmed once on the canvas under the first frame.
Runs in place of the FXAA: 8 colour reads + 1 depth + 1 AO, against FXAA's up to ~20
Desktop (SMAA)
+0: draws into the lens blur's input copy, which is idle while regressed; SMAA reads it there
One full-resolution pass more than walking today, still well under a standing frame with its depth of field
Not measured on a GPU, because the container only has SwiftShader. Real timings need ?trail=1 on the iPhone.
Checks
bun run verify is green. New unit tests cover a still camera, a turn (blurs everywhere), a forward step (blurs nowhere), a teleport, and a point that was behind the camera.
SwiftShader plates at the start view, desktop and phone profile, 1.5° per frame turn and 1 m forward step:
Pastel turn: a horizontal smear.
Forward step: sharp.
Back to rest: sharp, with depth of field on desktop.
Comic turn: sharp.
Code review: 8 findings worked in. These cover antialiasing on the phone, size and projection changes, warm-up, the view lens, the regressed gate, allocations and one table entry for the blurred frame.
Snapshots
Open the preview at /dresden, then the sidebar Erweitert → Snapshot, paste one JSON and press Anwenden. The blur only shows in motion, so turn with the mouse or the arrow keys, or start a flight. Compare with Comic at the same spot, where the blur stays off.
Brühlsche Terrasse from the Elbe, Pastell: turning smears the Altstadt horizontally. When you stop, the picture is sharp again, with depth of field.
