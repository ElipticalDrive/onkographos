Contributor
findProjection and ray casting spent most of their time in the shape of the binary AABBTree (one triangle per leaf, siblings far apart in memory) and in walking the topology (edgeWithLeft -> prev -> org) to get the corners of every tested triangle.
What changed
New AABBTreeWide (MRAABBTreeWide.h): up to 4 children per node stored next to each other, up to 4 triangles per leaf; every leaf keeps the face id and the 3 vertex ids of its triangles (16 bytes per triangle), so a query reads corners straight from mesh.points.
It is built in MRAABBTree.cpp by the same median splits as AABBTree (two binary levels per node, in parallel); the median partition of AABBTreeMaker became the free function partitionBoxedLeaves shared by both.
Mesh::getAABBTreeWide() / getAABBTreeWideNotCreate() cache it like the other trees; invalidateCaches, updateCaches, updateCachesAfterSplits and packOptimally drop it (no refit yet), heapBytes counts it.
findProjection searches this tree, with a fast path when no transformation, region, validFaces or validProjections is given (their checks cost 13% in the hot loops). findProjectionSubtree and the binary tree are unchanged.
rayMeshIntersect (and so multiRayMeshIntersect, rayMultiMeshAnyIntersect) and rayMeshIntersectAll traverse it too: the closest-hit search pushes the crossed children with the nearest entered last. The float version passes the stored boxes to rayBoxIntersect by reference; the copy of every box made before (Box3<T>{ box }) cost 20% of the ray time.
findTrisInBall searches this tree too, testing the triangles of the leaves in the ball by their exact distance (offsetVerts and MeshThickness use it). findBoxedTrisInBall stays on AABBTree: it reports triangles by their own boxes, and rebuilding those from the vertices made it 1.4-2x slower than reading them from the binary tree's leaves.
The places that built the binary tree before parallel projections or rays now build the wide one: replicateZ, spline projection, MeshOrPoints::cacheAABBTree, the voxel volumes of distance, closeness and inside (MRMeshToDistanceVolume.cpp) and the screen lasso. The binary tree is still built for winding numbers by getDipoles. Line3Mesh::tree was not used by the rays even before; its comment says so now.

Tree
Build, 1M: AABBTreeWide 80 ms vs AABBTree 72 ms on 24 threads (the first two levels are serial), 581 vs 607 ms on 1 thread. Memory: 28 MB vs 66 MB.
Results
Closest point: squared distances are identical to the old path for all queries on 58K, 244K and 1M triangles with every option: plain, region, upDistLimitSq, xf, validFaces, validProjections and all together; with loDistLimitSq the answer stays within the limit whenever the old one is. The returned face differs in about 35% of the queries: these are ties, 99.7% of them the same point on an edge or a vertex shared by both faces, the rest equidistant points.
Rays (closest in float and double, from -FLT_MAX, with a region): the same rays hit, at the same distance, except at most 9 rays per mode hitting another face at the same distance and 1-2 rays on 1M where the new search finds a closer hit that the old one missed (a float-rounded box of a single triangle). Any-hit rays may return another hit, as before. rayMeshIntersectAll reports exactly the same faces for every ray in float and double, in another order.
findTrisInBall finds the same triangles, possibly in another order.
Callers relying on which face wins a tie, or on the order of rayMeshIntersectAll callbacks, may see different, equally correct results.
A mesh that also needs AABBTree (Boolean, collisions, winding numbers, findBoxedTrisInBall, ...) keeps both trees: the wide one costs 80 ms and 28 MB for 1M triangles.
Testing
122 MRTest files that depend only on MRMesh, built against this branch: 548 passed, 6 skipped, 2 failed only because that local test executable has no mimalloc redirect and no logger set up (MimallocRedirectActive, Spdlog).
clang-cl syntax check of the changed sources; the full MRTest and the generated bindings are left to CI.
