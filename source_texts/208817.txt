Implementation
New crates/bsc-cad/src/qef.rs: per-cell hermite data (crossing point + SDF gradient), solving min Σ (nᵢ·(x-pᵢ))² via AᵀA accumulated incrementally and inverted by pseudo-inverse through a cyclic-Jacobi symmetric eigen-decomposition.
The pseudo-inverse is the load-bearing detail. AᵀA is singular in exactly the common cases — a flat patch leaves two directions unconstrained, an edge one — so a plain solve flings the vertex to infinity. Dropping near-zero eigenvalues and solving relative to the mass point keeps those directions in-cell and makes flat regions degrade gracefully back to surface-nets behaviour. The solved vertex is then clamped into its own cell: DC's textbook failure is an ill-conditioned solve spiking a vertex outside its cell and self-intersecting the mesh.
Topology is untouched — both methods share the dual quad stage, so triangle counts match exactly.
--method dual|surface-nets added (dual is default). The old placement is kept only so the difference stays measurable.

The polygonizer rounded every sharp feature: surface nets places a cell's
vertex at the mean of its edge crossings, and the mean of points scattered
around a cube's corner sits strictly inside the cube. Both mesh.rs and the
crate README already carried aspirational comments promising dual contouring
with no implementation behind them; the implementation now exists and those
comments are true.

Each surface-straddling cell keeps hermite data — every edge crossing plus
the SDF gradient (surface normal) there — and its vertex is placed by solving
the QEF min Σ (nᵢ·(x-pᵢ))² for the point best satisfying all those tangent
planes at once. New qef.rs accumulates AᵀA incrementally and inverts it by
pseudo-inverse via a cyclic-Jacobi symmetric eigen-decomposition.

The pseudo-inverse is the load-bearing detail: AᵀA is singular in exactly the
common cases (a flat patch leaves two directions unconstrained, an edge one),
so a plain solve would fling the vertex to infinity. Dropping the near-zero
eigenvalues and solving relative to the mass point keeps those directions at a
sane in-cell position, which also makes flat regions degrade gracefully back to
surface-nets behavior. Edge crossings are refined by regula falsi against the
real field rather than one linear interpolation, so curved and smooth_union
surfaces get accurate hermite data too. The solved vertex is clamped into its
own cell — dual contouring's textbook failure is an ill-conditioned solve
spiking a vertex outside its cell and self-intersecting the mesh, and the clamp
bounds that to "no worse than surface nets". Topology is untouched: both
methods share the same dual quad stage, so triangle counts match exactly and
output stays watertight.

Measured on a 20 mm cube at --res 32: max vertex deviation 0.0000 mm and
volume 8000.00 mm³ (both exact) vs 0.4167 mm and 7932.05 mm³ for surface nets.
Surface nets' error is a fixed ~2/3 of a cell at every resolution, so refining
never sharpened the feature. On a cube rotated off the grid axes, dual
contouring reproduces the analytic bounding box to 4 decimals while surface
nets comes up 1.72 mm short, having shaved the corner away.

bsc cad mesh now reports a deviation pair (max/mean |SDF| over the mesh's
vertices) so sharpness is a number a caller can branch on, and
--method dual|surface-nets selects the polygonizer (dual is the default; the
old placement is kept only so the difference stays measurable). New worked
example examples/sharp-block.json — a 45°-rotated octagonal prism with a
diagonal through-slot and two bolt holes, so nothing lines up with the grid.
