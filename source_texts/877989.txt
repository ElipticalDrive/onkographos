What and why
bx_audit.deliverable() reported parts that cross face to face as floating: a rail fixed across a
post's side, a beam resting across a post's top. _attached() joined two parts only when their
triangles intersect or a vertex of one lies within tol of the other. Where two parts cross, no
vertex of either lies near the other, and the hairline gap float rounding leaves between faces
that meet hides the contact from BVHTree.overlap. The gate then fails an asset whose parts touch.
_attached() now also takes points along each part's edges, clipped to the overlap of the two
bounding boxes grown by tol (the ends and middle of each clipped piece), and tests them against
the other part within tol. The pass runs only for pairs the triangle and vertex tests did not
join, and a point within tol is a contact by the gate's own definition, so parts farther apart
than tol stay apart.
Found while gating a batch of about 100 procedurally generated environment assets (buildings,
street props, modular kits), where parts that touch their neighbors face to face read as floating.

Note
Medium Risk
Changes contact detection for the floating gate only, with bounded extra BVH queries on edge samples; wrong tolerance could merge distant parts or still miss edge cases.
Overview
The delivery gate’s floating check no longer false-flags assemblies where parts meet face-to-face (e.g. a rail on a post) but share no nearby vertices and BVH overlap misses a sub-tolerance gap from float rounding.
_attached() still uses triangle overlap and vertex proximity first; if those fail, it samples end/mid points on each part’s edges (clipped to the two parts’ overlapping bounds, expanded by tol) and treats a hit within tol on the other mesh as contact. Island extraction now records edge pairs to drive that sampling via new _clip (Liang–Barsky) and _edge_samples helpers.
A live crossing fixture (post + rail + beam with ~0.1 mm intentional gaps) and test_parts_crossing_face_to_face_touch lock in the behavior.
