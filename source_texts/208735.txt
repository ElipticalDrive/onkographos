Introduction
Optical holography, the technique for realizing thri?e dimensional
images with the aid of coherent light beams has given rise to a new form
of holography in which a hologram is made by shadow casting a Fresnel
zone plate (FZP) with incoherent radiation.
The fundamental notion
behind the technique rests on the observation that an ordinary
coherently
2
made optical hologram of a point source is nothing but an FZP pattern.
The first successful experimental results with 140 keV (d.c.) gamma
3 4
ray sources were reported ' in which ~ 1 mm thick lead FZPs were
employed.
In this paper we report the use of an FZP imaging system for a pulsed
x-ray source generated by an intense electron beam, having a maximum
electron energy of 600 keV and a pulse width (FWHM) of 3 nanoseconds.
We also have calculated point response functions of an FZP from
Kirchhoff's diffraction theory.
The calculation shows that for a given
geometry of the imaging system there exists an optimum number of rings of
an FZP.
This optimum number will increase the limiting resolution and
enhance contrast.
The calculations were experimentally verified with
optically made FZP holograms.

A Proposed Real-Time Imaging System
There are two schools of thought regarding real-time imaging applications
to «n Incoherent holography.
The first one is to digitize A hologram obtained
on-line by using a vidlcon camera system.
The video signal can be easily
digitised for computer processing, end the data can eventually be displayed
on a monitor*
The second method is to make use of an on-line Fourier
transform system.
We prefer the latter since it provides a relatively easy
spatial filtering capability over a broad range in addition to real time
image reconstruction.

The information In a hologram will be transferred onto the crystal

Summary
He Jieve examined the quality of the reconstructed image of an FZP
aperture from both theoretical and experimental viewpoints, and have found It
to be very geometry sensitive.
Our preliminary data imply that reconstructions
from holograms containing shadow casts with a given radius of the innermost
circle, r., can be optimized by adjusting the number of rings, n.
Bqulvalently,
In hologram construction with an FZP having a fixed number of rings, there
is a mlnification factor which will result in the most favorable combinstion(s)
of r« and n.
The FZP aperture has been applied to a pulsed x-ray source to increase
the absolute efficiency compared with that of a pinhole aperture and to gain
depth information,
Finally, an on-line imaging scheme that consists of a
vidicon camera system and an on-line Fourier transform system is being
proposedf
