Summary
TSDF fusion and surface-nets extraction on the GPU for the CUDA meshes: gsplat_mesh_cuda --gpu-tsdf, mesh::extract_mesh_gpu, CudaGsplat::tsdf_surface. It uses the CPU Tsdf's maths, options and filters. Stacked on #409.
Allocation first: every view is rendered once, and the 8^3-voxel blocks of its truncation band go into an open-addressing set on the device. So every block sees every view; the CPU allocates as it fuses, and a block first touched by view k misses views 0 to k-1.
Slabs: the sorted blocks are fused in slabs along x of at most --slab-blocks blocks (default 65536, 0.67 GB of voxels). Each slab also holds the next layer up for its top face's cells. Each slab emits its vertices and its edges with a sign change, and the host assembles the quads across slabs with a parallel sorted lookup (rayon). Memory stays bounded at any voxel size.
View cache: the views' depth and colour stay on the device up to 2 GiB, instead of being rendered again for every slab.
CPU path: Mesh::remove_small_components counts per component in a vector instead of a hash map, which speeds up the CPU path too.
ABI: CUDA ABI 7.
Validation (tests/tsdf.rs)
One view: both sides allocate before fusing, so the meshes must match. Every vertex is within 4e-4 voxels of the CPU's, colours are within 1 of 255, and the triangles are the same.
Four views: the surface is bit-identical in 1 slab or 30, with or without the view cache, and 99.8% of its vertices lie within 0.01 voxels of the CPU's.
Lints and tests: cargo fmt, clippy -D warnings and rustdoc -D warnings are clean, and the gsplat-cuda and gsplat-core tests pass.
ETH3D (baseline splats, ETH3D-oriented options, 1 cm voxels, depths up to 20 m; another training run shared the GPU)

`gsplat_mesh_cuda --gpu-tsdf` (`mesh::extract_mesh_gpu`,
`CudaGsplat::tsdf_surface`) fuses the median depth and extracts the mesh on
the device (native/gsplat_cuda/tsdf.cuh), with visloc_gsplat_core's `Tsdf`
maths, options and filters.

- Allocation first: every view is rendered once and the 8^3-voxel blocks
  of its truncation band go into an open-addressing set on the device, so
  every block sees every view (the CPU allocates as it fuses).
- Slabs: the sorted blocks are fused in slabs along x of at most
  `slab_blocks` (default 65536), each with the next layer up for its top
  face's cells; each slab emits its cells' vertices and the edges with a
  sign change, and the host assembles the quads across slabs by a parallel
  sorted lookup (rayon).
- View cache: the views' depth and colour stay on the device up to 2 GiB,
  instead of rendering every view again per slab.
- `Mesh::remove_small_components` counts per component in a vector instead
  of a hash map (the CPU path gets it too).
- Tests: one view gives the CPU's mesh (vertices within 4e-4 voxels,
