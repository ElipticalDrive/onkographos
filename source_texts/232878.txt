Requested by Dan · project thread
Before: every mesh in front of the camera drew, even ones hidden behind a wall.
After: OccluderInstance3Ds hide the meshes behind them, as Godot 4.6's raycast occlusion culler does, and occluder.bake makes an occluder from the scene's meshes as Godot's Bake Occluders does.
```
"wall": {"components": {
  "MeshInstance3D": {}, "BoxMesh": {"size": [12, 4, 0.5]},
  "OccluderInstance3D": {"shape": "box", "size": [11.8, 3.8, 0.3]}
}}
// project.json
"rendering": {"occlusion_culling": {"use_occlusion_culling": true}}
```
How:
Occluders (engine/scene3d/src/occlusion.cpp, occluder_mesh): OccluderInstance3D (new component) with Godot's quad, box, sphere, polygon and array shapes, made as Godot's Occluder3D subclasses make them. Polygons are triangulated as Godot's, and their winding is fixed as Godot's.
Ray caster: OccluderScene is a bounding volume hierarchy over every visible occluder's triangles, with a median split, leaves of up to 4 triangles and two-sided hits. It stands in for Godot's Embree scene; the sort is stable, so it is the same on every machine. It is rebuilt only when the triangles change.
Buffer (OcclusionBuffer, ported from RaycastOcclusionCull and HZBuffer):
It is sized as Godot's from occlusion_rays_per_thread times 8 threads, clamped to between 1/1024 and 1/4 of the view's pixels.
Rays go through texel centers from the near plane, for perspective and orthogonal cameras alike, and reach to 1.05 times the far plane.
Mipmaps keep the farthest depth.
occluded is _is_occluded line for line: the bounds' closest point, the corners' rect, the mip level from ceil_log2, and at most 512 samples.
jitter_projection cycles Godot's 9-frame pattern, and a mesh that was seen keeps a timeout of 9 frames.
Renderer (scene3d::Renderer::cull_occluded, in prepare before LODs are picked):
Each draw is tested by its own placed bounds.
A hidden draw becomes ShadowsOnly when it casts shadows, and is dropped otherwise.
double_sided and shadows_only casters, and MeshInstance3D.ignore_occlusion_culling (new, Godot's), are never hidden.
Reflection probes and shadow maps are unaffected, as in Godot.
Bake (bake_occluder, the new occluder.bake command), after OccluderInstance3D::bake_scene and _bake_surface:
It takes visible meshes on bake_mask whose material isn't transparent: primitives and models' surfaces.
Surfaces are simplified with meshoptimizer's meshopt_simplify to bake_simplification_distance, with locked borders.
Vertices are remapped, and duplicate triangles are removed with Godot's triangle hash.
It sets an array shape, reports unchanged and records undo, and fails with nothing to bake as Godot does. meshoptimizer's index generator is now built too.
Settings: project.json's rendering.occlusion_culling (use_occlusion_culling, occlusion_rays_per_thread, jitter_projection) is in the schema, runtime::Project, canvas::Settings and the session's settings.
Agents:
frame.capture's drawn counts occluded, and CanvasRenderer::meshes_occluded() gives the same count.
scene validate reports polygon and array occluders that can't make triangles.
Godot import: OccluderInstance3D and its occluder types, ignore_occlusion_culling and the three project settings are imported. Occluders in binary .occ files are reported with a hint to run occluder.bake.
Tests:
New tests/test_occlusion.cpp:
every shape;
ray casts;
buffer sizes and mip levels;
hidden and visible boxes;
jitter timeouts;
culling through canvas::capture;
the settings;
the bake;
the checks.
test_command.cpp covers occluder.bake, and test_import_godot.cpp covers the import.
Example: new examples/occlusion-culling, three houses behind a wall, a tower and a bake.
Docs:
scene-format.md gains "Occlusion culling" and ignore_occlusion_culling.
project-format.md gains "Occlusion culling".
commands.md covers occluder.bake and frame.capture, and godot-import.md covers the import.
AGENTS.md and examples/README.md are updated.
The roadmap ticks occlusion culling, finishing milestone 7's LOD and culling row.
Bench: the baseline is updated for the new command's definition.
