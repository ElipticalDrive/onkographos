Abstract
3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) has become a popular method for efficient and high-quality novel view synthesis. However, its training process demands excessive GPU memory, primarily due to uniform storage of high-order spherical-harmonic coefficients and rapid increases in the number of Gaussians. To address this, we introduce a memory-efficient approach that dynamically adjusts spherical-harmonic orders based on the expressive needs of each Gaussian and employs a saliency-based pruning strategy to remove redundant Gaussians. Our method reduces the peak GPU memory consumption during training by 29.6%, 29.8%, and 22.4% on the Mip-NeRF360, Tanks&Temples, and Deep Blending datasets, respectively, while maintaining rendering quality comparable to the original approach.

Ablation of sh order adjustment interval
When the dynamic adjustment interval \(D_N\) for SH order is smaller than the densification interval, the order of Gaussians is frequently adjusted. Short intervals lead to more Gaussians being briefly judged as needing a higher order from certain perspectives during the training process, resulting in an overall increase in the number of Gaussians upgraded.
In contrast, when the adjustment interval is greater than the densification interval, the assessment of whether a Gaussian needs to be upgraded becomes more robust. Longer intervals allow the necessity of that Gaussian to be validated from more perspectives, thereby reducing the probability of misjudgment. However, this also brings a side effect: during the densification phase, since most Gaussians remain at zeroth order, the system generates more new zeroth-order Gaussians to supplement perspective-dependent rendering.

The rendering quality shows a declining trend. In contrast, the Deep Blending [49] dataset exhibits different behaviors. Here, \(D_N\) = 100 achieves the best balance, compared with \(D_N\) = 50 and \(D_N\) = 200, the rendering quality is slightly improved, and the peak memory usage is the lowest.

Overall, these results confirm that although larger adjustment intervals typically reduce memory consumption, their impact on reconstruction quality depends on the dataset. In practice, choosing \(D_N\) = 100 can provide a robust balance in most scenarios.
Ablation of threshold
We tested different color gradient thresholds \(\mathrm {grad\_threshold}\), the results are shown in Table 6. In most cases, as the threshold increases, the number of Gaussians with third-order SH coefficients gradually decreases, which causes the rendering quality to gradually decline, while the GPU memory usage also decreases. When the threshold is small enough, the rendering quality is already close to 3DGS, but this does not reduce GPU memory usage.
We tested different pruning ratios under the SP-only configuration, the results are shown in Table 7. In most cases, the higher the pruning percentage, the lower the rendering quality. To avoid removing too many Gaussians, we generally choose less than 2%.
In practice, Gaussians with opacity lower than \(\operatorname {sigmoid}(-5.3)\) are removed. We do not impose particularly strict restrictions on opacity, but only limit Gaussians with opacity lower than \(\operatorname {sigmoid}(-5.0)\) to keep low-order SH coefficients to prevent nearly-transparent Gaussians from using more GPU memory. Table 8 shows comparisons of different opacity thresholds on the Deep Blending [49] dataset, and even if the threshold is slightly raised, the change is not significant.
Table 9 shows different T_thresholds on the Mip-NeRF360 [45] dataset. As the threshold increases, fewer Gaussians retain third-order SH coefficients, reducing both rendering quality and GPU memory usage. Since Gaussians with transmittance below 1e-3 do not participate in pixel shading, we adopt this threshold. Combined with Table 6, the strict upgrade criteria ensure that Gaussians with third-order coefficients carry relatively high weights during pixel shading.
Orthogonality study
We apply ASH on top of Taming 3DGS [24] on the Deep Blending [49] dataset. As shown in Table 10, Taming 3DGS [24] with ASH reduces peak GPU memory from 10,552 MB to 8,676 MB (a 17.8% reduction), while maintaining rendering quality.
