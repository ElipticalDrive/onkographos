High-resolution novel view synthesis from low-resolution (LR) multi-view observations is challenging for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), because super-resolution guidance can introduce unstable high-frequency gradients for density control. Existing Gaussian super-resolution methods exploit pseudo-HR supervision to recover fine details, but they often use the resulting gradients directly for densification. Such unstable densification signals can promote transient SR artifacts into persistent redundant Gaussians, degrading the quality-efficiency trade-off of HR rendering. High HR-space gradients should trigger densification only when they agree with LR geometry and persist across views. Based on this insight, we propose RD-GSR, a compact Gaussian super-resolution framework that regulates topology growth with cross-scale gradient agreement and cross-view persistence. RD-GSR decomposes reconstruction into two stages. Cross-Scale Agreement (CSA) validates early densification by requiring directional consistency between LR and HR positional gradients, suppressing growth caused by conflicting cross-scale gradients. Persistence-Aware Clone Selection (PACS) furtherfilters SR-guided clone operations by requiring high-gradient responses to persist across visible views, reducing expansion induced by view-inconsistent SR artifacts. Across nine SwinIR-supervised benchmark settings, RD-GSR improves PSNR by 0.31 dB over SRGS on average, while using 25.4% fewer Gaussian primitives and achieving a 17.3% relative FPS gain.</jats:p
