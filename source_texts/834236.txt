Before: each loaded chunk waited in the mesher's queue for its own job. The mesher runs at most cores/2 jobs, and each result lands a frame later, so it meshed at most about cores/2 chunks a frame. While flying, thousands of chunks waited and the fog held back for them.
After: streamed chunks (full detail and every coarse level, generated or saved) are meshed on the loading worker right after they're made, so they arrive meshed and wait only for their upload. The mesher's own jobs are left for edits, ships, and chunks whose neighbour changed. Declan tested in game: startup to full distance went from 60 s to 45 s, and loading now stays ahead while flying.
How: the loader's single preparer becomes an ordered list of stages (IChunkPreparer). Each stage runs over all of a job's chunks before the next starts, and it gets the job's chunks so it can look across borders. Today's stages are opacity packing, then meshing (ChunkMeshSystem.Stage). The mesh rides on the chunk as PreparedMesh, and ChunkMeshSystem uploads it within its existing 4 ms budget, nearest first, once the chunk is drawn.
Only see-through faces are culled across borders in the streamed world, so the stage records which neighbour each face was meshed against. Chunks from the same job don't mark each other for a remesh. A see-through face beside a chunk from another job gets remeshed after the upload.
A solid chunk with solid chunks above and below it isn't meshed while loading. About 4 in 10 island chunks are solid, and most end up buried. When it's uploaded it's cleared if buried, or gets a mesh job if not.
Light-map bakes, which the faster meshing exposed:
A chunk with light maps isn't drawn until they've been baked once. The budget (BakeQuadBudget, 65,536 quads a frame) now covers every static volume, the world's chunks as well as each coarse level's. Whatever is left of it each frame goes to chunks that have never been baked, finest level first, including chunks out of view. Each baked chunk gets LightmapsBakedFlag.
The swaps treat an unbaked chunk as not ready, so a level doesn't fade out before the level replacing it can be drawn.
The Chunk Rendering panel shows how many chunks in view are still waiting for their first bake.
