GamerKraft gets its own path tracer, written against raw WebGL2 rather than
three.js. It runs entirely in the browser, so rendering is free and nothing
is uploaded.
Renderer (src/render/rt-scene.js, pathtracer.js):
Voxels are packed into a 3D texture and traversed with a two-level DDA that
skips empty 4^3 bricks. Actor meshes, including instanced foliage, go into
a binned-SAH BVH.
Lighting: multi-bounce global illumination, soft sun shadows, GGX
reflections, and refraction and absorption through glass, water and slime.
Water scatters light. Torches and point lights are importance-sampled, and
glowing blocks act as cube area lights.
Camera and image: depth of field, the level's sky and fog, and tiled
running-average accumulation throttled by GPU fences. An a-trous denoiser
is guided by normals, depth and albedo, followed by ACES tone mapping.
Block surfaces and the sky reuse the raster renderer's GLSL, so both
renderers match.
Editor (src/editor/render-studio.js):
A Path Traced viewport mode refines progressively and restarts on edits.
It drops to half resolution while the camera moves.
The Render window (Alt+R, Render menu and toolbar) renders stills up to 8K
and saves them as PNG or copies them to the clipboard.
Animation rendering covers turntables and day-cycle time-lapses. Output is
WebM through WebCodecs with exact frame timing, with a MediaRecorder
fallback, and/or a ZIP of PNG frames.
Render settings live in settings.render and save with the project. New
console commands: render [samples] and r.pathtrace 0|1.
Also fix loading a project keeping the previous project's sky in the viewport:
the world view now re-applies the environment on reset.
Tests: unit tests cover voxel packing, BVH traversal against brute force,
triangle extraction from a real WorldView, lights, and the ZIP and WebM
writers. A new browser smoke test renders a still, an animation and the
viewport. Chromium is launched with --enable-unsafe-swiftshader so WebGL keeps
working on CI now that the automatic software fallback is deprecated.
