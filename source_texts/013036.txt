Summary
Implementation of a primary Raytracing kernel using GLSL Compute Shaders. This shader serves as the core rendering engine for the voxel world, responsible for casting rays from the camera through a screen-space grid, traversing the voxel data structures, and calculating final pixel colors based on hit information and lighting logic.
Functional Requirements
Screen-to-World Ray Generation: The shader shall calculate a primary ray direction for each global invocation (pixel) based on camera matrices (View/Projection) and normalized screen coordinates.
Voxel Grid Traversal: The shader shall implement a robust traversal loop (utilizing the DDA utility) to step through the voxel world data until an intersection with a non-air voxel is detected or the maximum render distance is reached.
Acceleration Structure Integration: The system shall support hierarchical lookups (e.g., checking high-level chunk bounds before individual voxels) to skip empty space efficiently.
Surface Shading & Lighting: Upon a hit, the shader shall calculate the surface normal based on the entry face and apply a lighting model (e.g., Lambertian, hard shadows, or basic ambient occlusion).
Write to Storage Image: The final computed color for each ray must be written to a writeonly storage image (RGBA8 or RGBA16F) to be displayed or post-processed in subsequent render passes.
Safe Termination: The kernel must handle "miss" scenarios (rays that exit the world or hit the skybox) by returning a background color and terminating the traversal loop to prevent infinite execution.
Acceptance Criteria
Verified that a 3D voxel scene renders correctly from a perspective camera view.
Verified that movement of the camera matrices correctly updates the rendered perspective in real-time.
Verified that the shader correctly identifies the material of the hit voxel and applies the corresponding color.
Verified that rays hitting the edge of the defined world do not cause memory access violations or hangs.
Verified that performance remains stable when rendering empty space versus dense geometry (efficient culling).
