#### ABSTRACT

3-D interactive volume rendering can be rather complicated through conventional ray casting with simple sampling and texture mapping. Owing to the limitation of hardware resources, volume rendering algorithms are considerably time-consuming. Therefore, adaptive sampling technique is proposed to tackle the problem of excessive computational cost. In this paper, considering an optimization in parallelized ray-casting algorithms of volume rendering, we propose an adaptive sampling method, which mainly reduces the number of sampling points through non-linear sampling function. This new method can be rather effective while making a trade-off between performance and rendering quality. Our experimental results demonstrate that the proposed adaptive sampling method can result in high efficiency in computation, and produce high-quality image based on the MSE and SSIM metrics.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Volume Rendering [1] is a set of specific methods or techniques, which comprise extracting constructive information from a 3-D volumetric data and accumulating the information to a displayable result, to draw a 2-D projection out of a 3-D volumetric data. There are many methods proposed towards solving this issue, however, complexity for such methods are relatively high. Although programmable processors on a single desktop PC is abundant currently, it is still messy to take this superiority, since the structure of GPGPU determines that a computation task can be efficaciously accelerated only if the task is implemented in parallel.



OpenCL, short for Open Computing Language, is a standard of GPGPU that allows programmers to do cross-platform C/C++ programming without any deep understanding in assemble languages among different calculation hardware. This feature enables us to implement parallel programming with diverse processors, which are nowadays prevailingly advantaged in our desktop PCs and Macs.

Ray-casting [2] algorithm, shown in Figure 1, is one of the rendering methods that are easily parallelizable, and directly implementable through OpenCL. For every pixel on the image plane, there is a ray that goes through the whole box of 3-D volumetric data at some specific direction from the view point. Through weighted sum and transfer function lookup, a pixel of result is accumulated.

Performance of volume rendering through ray-casting can be limited by the computational speed and the onboard memory of computational hardware. While the approach to do optimization by sampling view rays, or doing sampling on the image plane, is hard to implement in OpenCL, it is still possible to skip rays that are superfluous. This optimization is called adaptive sampling. Since it avoids scanning all details of the volumetric data, it is crucial that the method of implementation will not compromise the quality of rendering result. MSE (mean square error) and SSIM [3] metrics are used to compute the qualitive losses between our implementation and the source image, therefore define the feasibility and the degree of optimization in our rendering algorithm.

In this paper, our way of adaptive sampling is provided to:

- Accelerate the rendering on multiple parallelizable computational processor.
- Generate a result image with a SSIM value of 0.95 to the source image.
- Directly implement through OpenCL programming.

#### 2. RELATED WORK

Early texture-based volume rendering through hardware texture mapping using CPUs can be found published by Brian Cabral et al. [4], which the implementation of rasterization through texture slices to render through CPU cores is presented. Contemporary advanced framework for direct volume rendering of uncertain volumetric data can be found published by Elham Sakhaee and Alireza Entezari in [5]. Researches on intuitive volume rendering, which focuses mostly on mobile devices using OpenGL ES can be found published by Yuchen Xin and Hon-Cheng Wong in [6]. Sang-Soo Jun et al. [7] proposed a GPU-level optimization with branch distribution. Optimizations in texture mapping algorithms, such as the method that published by Tao Ling and Qian ZhiYu [8], encounters a severe challenge on causing excessive noise while performing mapping, which affects the quality of a rendering result. Finally, we stick onto the ray-casting.

The sampling method used in ray-casting volume rendering is crucial to the computational performance. Some useful and easily implementable methods can be found published by Huawei Wang et al. [9]. When a ray hits nothing, or the alpha value of frontal opacity is already saturated, there will be no demand to do the intermediate sum anymore. Simultaneously, it is essential to terminate when the alpha value reaches top opacity-level. The method to decide whether a ray hits can be executed in CPU by a simple intersection processing. However, doing optimization on the choice of pixels can be hard to implement in parallel. Another problem is that, the GPU must additionally compute the intersection of the rear plane, which results in a larger time complexity of the whole rendering system.

There are a variety of methods that do optimization on sampling method such as transfer the 3-D data into several workgroups and then do parallelized computation through topological sorting, which can be found published by Jun Yao et al. [10]. Another method which samples through creating priority queue with treelike algorithm can be found published by Yuchi Huo et al. [11]. Those algorithms leave us an intuition to do sampling in every workgroup or tree and then sum the result up. However, owing to the memory architectural feature of the OpenCL standard, it is hard to do topological sorting among threads of workgroups without increasing the time complexity. Finally, the idea to utilize the 3-D index space of architecture is abandoned.

Compared with sampling on the image plane, adaptive sampling in a view ray is easier to implement. Theoretically, adaptive sampling is to sample only the key points, according to the work of Huawei Wang et al. [12]. Reducing redundant sampling points in a view ray is an optimization of rendering. Nevertheless, the algorithm used to implement the computation on identifying the sampling points become another problem to increase the time complexity and memory usage. Skipping sampling points through a specific function or algorithm is another kind of adaptive sampling method. One similar and distinct method can be found published by Martin Kraus et al. [13], which does sampling in three directions, skipping slices of sampling points that are too far from the view plane of every view ray, and then do pyramidal synthesis on every level of slices to skip sampling points.

However, the computation of three direction sampling is hard to be parallelized without increasing time complexity through OpenCL coding. Later, Huw Bowles et al. [14] proposed a novel approach which utilizes a specific density function for sampling through every ray of the image plane. Inspired by that, a new method of adaptive sampling algorithm in doing volume rendering is eventually found out and researched in our work.

In our work of research in this paper, our contributions are regarded as follows:

- Unnormalized coordinate is designed and implemented to read the volumetric data.
- Adaptive sampling method is researched and implemented.
- All methods are approachable through OpenCL to fit for cross-platform architecture.

#### 3.1 Ray-casting

Ray-casting algorithm [2], which projects 3-D volume data into a 2-D image, is implemented in the OpenCL kernel and runs on the GPU. There're three steps to implement the ray-casting algorithm:

- Construct rays from eye position and then do ray-box intersection [15] to mark an entrance and an exit for each ray.
- Utilize the unnormalized coordinates to load voxel texture for subsequent sampling algorithm.
- Carry out adaptive sampling algorithm and then weighted sum the opacity values estimated from the sampling points through transfer function.

The homogeneous coordinates [16] is generally used in various fields including OpenGL coordinate system. Therefore, it is convenient to find the ray direction and the distance between any point of 3-D volume to any pixel on the image plane through vector calculations. The definition of the casting vector which starts from the view point of image plane and ends at the sampling target point is shown in Eq. (2) [16] (1).

$$\mathbf{d} = \frac{\mathbf{x}_{eye} - \mathbf{x}_{pixel}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{eye} - \mathbf{x}_{image}\|}$$

#### 3.2 Unnormalized Coordinate

Similar to the section 3.1, an unnormalized coordinate is utilized to load and read image texture from the image buffer. Instead, the length of the longest edge will practically be scaled into 1. E.g., a volumetric cube size of 128\*128\*64 will be scaled into a cubical box that size of 1\*1\*0.5. To scale the longest edge to 1 instead of 2 helps in calculating texture coordinate without changing the center of the world coordinate into the cubical box. Since there is no negative coordinate exists, the texture loading method is uniformed and optimized. Comparing to ray-box intersection, the use of unnormalized coordinate is rather indispensable. Since the length of three dimensions in a volumetric data is dissimilar, the hastily use of normalized coordinate may cause an out-of-bound buffer reading. Nevertheless, the selection of unnormalized coordinate is optional, for reaching a voxel that is out-of-bound will not seriously affect the finally result of one ray.

At each pixel in the plane of 2-D image, the following function [2] for accumulating the result of every image voxel read from buffer through transfer function implementation is used in (3) [17]

$$C_i = C_{i-1}(1 - \alpha(x_{i-1})) + c(x_{i-1})\alpha(x_{i-1}) \quad (3)$$

, where Ci is iterated to the result opacity value for one pixel in the image plane.

#### 3.3 Adaptive Sampling .

To introduce the proposed algorithm of adaptive sampling, a comparison to the linear sampling algorithm is presented as follows:

In the linear sampling as shown in Figure 2, the voxels are sampled according to an interval of fixed distance. Usually, the sampling distance tstep is user-defined or experimentaldetermined. At each sampling position along the tracing ray, a weighted sum based on transfer function is calculated and accumulated by Eq. (3). Practically, a maximum number of sampling steps maxstep will also be set. Both parameters tstep and maxstep are used to control the image quality and the execution time.

In linear sampling, the parameter tstep is fixed. Therefore, the implementation of linear sampling method confronts a dilemma, whether to provide a high-quality rendering result or to achieve a high efficiency at relatively low rendering quality. The proposed sampling algorithm is inspired by the method described in [15], where a specific monotonically decreasing function is used. When the independent variable x is near zero, the dependent variable should be large enough. The goal of our approach is to simulate photographing, where objects too far away are vague and those close enough are clear. In reality, it follows the simple Eq. (4), where f(x) is the clarity (in volume rendering, the density of sampling points) and x is the distance between the eye and the object. In the algorithm [15], tstep is not chosen to be necessarily nonlinear, instead, it gradually exponential increases in power of 2 in the whole range, even fixed in the local region of sampling intervals. Differently, in the proposed sampling algorithm, tstep changes all the time along the tracing ray resulting in nonlinear sampling.

Inspired by the linear sampling algorithm, our algorithm takes every step of sampling density as the prior knowledge to the next sampling point, in other words, every continuous two sampling points have the same difference in the sampling density. Since the mathematical function is nonlinear, even though the distance of sample density preserves identical, tstep differs, resulting in ignoring relatively more redundant sampling points compared with the linear sampling algorithm.

#### 4. IMPLEMENTATION AND RESULT

To make our algorithm more convincing and experimentally solid, the uniform linear sampling algorithm is firstly implemented to render a 256\*256\*256 3-D volumetric data into a 512\*512 2-D image plane. The image data comes from ultra-sound biomicroscopic (UBM) volumetric images of monkey ciliary muscles exported from medical equipment is used during experiment. Different sets of maxsteps and step length tstep is built in pairs. For each pair, a comparison to a specific pair that is of 200 maxstep and 0.01 tstep (considered as the original picture) is made for a better viewing of rendering efficiency. The result is shown in Table 1.

In our implementation of the adaptive sampling algorithm of [15], the result is all but satisfying. Since the algorithm that generates only 32 sampling points, the 2-D result of SSIM to the original picture is too low, only about 0.5575 when the executing time is about 2.2ms on average, since there's too much visible aliasing in the result. We tried our best to increase the SSIM value to 0.95, but the execution time of OpenCL kernel is relatively too long, about 15 milli-seconds on average.

We implement the adaptive sampling algorithm with the same input volumetric data and the same output image requirement as the aforementioned linear sampling experiment. We set different k values of Eq. (6) in different experiments, while we fix the initial step length to 0.01 and the maximum number of sampling point to 100. This result is shown in Table 2.

### 5. CONCLUSION

The proposed adaptive sampling algorithm is efficient in computational complexity, while achieve satisfying quality result. Our approach of sampling algorithm outperformed uniformed linear sampling algorithm by a great margin in MSE. In our approach, when k value is set to near 4, both the MSE and SSIM come to an acceptable satisfactory. A higher k value may increase the SSIM value, but the MSE value will simultaneously increase, too.

In our work of adaptive sampling, no change has been made in the maxstep and the first step length. Instead, we fix them to an arbitrary value to mainly consider the change of k value. In future work, the research on which pair of step length and maxstep optimizes best on this sampling algorithm is one aspect that worth carrying out. Furthermore, only one type of mathematical function is manifested, since it solves the under-sampling problem we found in Eq. (4). There may be a better function and that is another interesting aspect for further exploration.
