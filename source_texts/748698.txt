Abstract
Immersive video communication requires photorealistic, compact dynamic scene
representations that support efficient rendering. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
is a promising approach, but dynamic 3DGS remains difficult to compress due to
its dense primitives and spatiotemporal redundancy. Anchor-based formulations
improve compactness through sparse scaffolds that share geometry and appearance
across primitives. However, existing designs typically deform a single canonical
scaffold and condition each primitive solely on its associated anchor, limiting their
ability to model non-local dynamics and disocclusions and exploit inter-anchor
correlations. To address these limitations, we propose SAGA, a volumetric video
codec built on Sparse Anchor-assisted GAussian splatting representations. SAGA
40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).
represents a dynamic scene using hierarchical sparse anchors in 4D space-time.
Coordinate-based INR decoders generate fine anchors and Gaussian primitives from
features obtained through kernel interpolation among nearby anchors, enabling
parameter sharing across spatiotemporal structures. A fixed-size memory with
a novel orthogonality-informed slot-update strategy further captures long-range
dependencies among unstructured anchors for entropy coding. Empirically, SAGA
achieves PSNR-based BD-rate reductions of 90.88%, 64.30% and 65.14% relative
to GIFStream, TED-4DGS, and OMG4, respectively, on the Neu3D testset.

Introduction
Immersive visual content is increasingly important for applications such as telepresence, freeviewpoint video, virtual and augmented reality, and digital media production [1, 6]. It provides
a stronger sense of presence, richer spatial understanding, and greater viewpoint flexibility than
conventional 2D media. However, these benefits introduce substantial challenges. While 2D video
mainly records view-specific projected appearance on the imaging plane, free-viewpoint volumetric video requires dynamic scene representations that support rendering from multiple viewpoints,
further increasing the demands on storage, transmission, and rendering. Consequently, compact
dynamic scene representation has become central to scalable immersive video systems, where fidelity,
rendering efficiency, and compressibility must be jointly optimized.
For compact representation and compression of volumetric visual content, prior work spans traditional
geometry- and video-based pipelines [40, 39, 1, 6] and neural scene representations [34, 85, 48].
Among neural methods, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have advanced novel-view synthesis [56] and
compression [74, 83, 25, 80] by modeling scenes as continuous volumetric radiance fields, but their
reliance on volume rendering remains costly for real-time immersive applications. In contrast, 3D
Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [32] uses an explicit, rasterization-friendly representation with efficient
training, high visual fidelity, and substantially faster rendering. Consequently, 3DGS compression
(e.g., [54, 11]) has become an active research direction for reducing storage costs and enabling
practical immersive visual communication.
Following advances in static 3DGS compression [54, 11, 12, 10, 72, 58], GS-based volumetric video
coding has attracted increasing attention [67, 24, 41]. Compared with static scenes, dynamic 3DGS
must represent changes in geometry, appearance, and visibility over time, introducing additional
parameters and spatiotemporal redundancy. Existing methods broadly fall into frame-by-frame
approaches [67, 24, 38, 78], which encode temporally correlated Gaussian frames, and canonicalto-deformation approaches [13, 41, 23] that model video- or chunk-wise temporal variations from a
shared canonical representation. While effective, frame-wise methods exploit temporal redundancy
via predictive or correlated coding, which can complicate random access and accumulate drift under
long prediction chains; canonical-to-deformation methods rely on a single reference that can be
affected by abrupt motion, disocclusion, or topology changes.
To address these limitations, we propose SAGA, a Gaussian Splatting-based volumetric video
compression framework using a hierarchical sparse 4D anchor scaffold. SAGA organizes anchors
directly in spacetime, allowing features to be shared across spatial and temporal neighborhoods
without deforming a single canonical scaffold. Fine anchors and Gaussian primitives are generated
through a unified interpolation-and-decoding mechanism: neighboring parent-anchor features are
aggregated at each target coordinate and fed, together with positional embeddings, into shared
coordinate-based implicit neural representation (INR) decoders. This enables parameter sharing
at multiple granularities while retaining local flexibility to represent dynamic scene content. A
lightweight local hash-based residual branch further refines primitive attributes in normalized anchor
coordinates to recover high-frequency details. To complement this local feature sharing, we introduce
a long-context entropy model that summarizes previously decoded anchor information in a fixed-size
memory bank. Orthogonality-guided slot updates incorporate new context while limiting repeated
writes of information aligned with the previous slot state. Our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose a novel GS-based volumetric video compression framework with hierarchical sparse
4D anchors. By organizing anchors directly in spacetime, our formulation combines local adaptability to geometry and visibility changes with long-range temporal sharing, enabling bidirectional
redundancy reduction and flexible non-canonical modeling of complex dynamics.
2
• We leverage coarse-to-fine and inter-anchor correlations through position-interpolated hierarchical features. These features condition both fine-anchor and Gaussian decoding, allowing them to
aggregate neighboring-anchor context rather than relying on isolated anchor features.
• We introduce an efficient long-context entropy model for unstructured 4D anchors. It distills
decoded spatiotemporal contexts into a compact memory bank whose slots are updated with an
orthogonality-guided rule, capturing long-range dependencies under bounded complexity.
Experiments on Neu3D [45], Panoptic Sports [29], and MPEG MIV Common Test Condition (CTC)
[6] datasets show that SAGA achieves favorable rate-distortion performance against existing GS-based
and MPEG-standard volumetric video codecs.

Related Work
Volumetric video compression has traditionally focused on explicit scene data and multi-view
signals, such as point clouds and multi-view videos. Representative standardization efforts include
V-PCC [63, 26], G-PCC [39], and V3C [1], covering video-based and geometry-based coding of
volumetric content. For immersive multi-view video, TMIV [1, 6] provides reference software that
exploits inter-view redundancy and packs texture and geometry into video atlases. Video-based
volumetric codecs can further leverage mature 2D coding tools and ecosystems, such as HEVC [66]
and VVC [7], with performance typically assessed under common test conditions and view-synthesis
evaluation protocols. Beyond standardized pipelines, learning-based methods have also been explored
for explicit 3D data, especially point clouds, using learned geometry transforms, entropy models,
temporal prediction, motion compensation, and residual coding [59, 16, 28, 64]. More recently,
coordinate-based and implicit neural representation (INR) codecs have emerged for multi-view and
immersive video compression, such as MV-HiNeRV [34], MV-IERV [85], and MV-MGINR [48].
Compression for NeRF-based methods has evolved along a clear methodological progression.
Early work improved compactness through end-to-end optimization [4] and more structured radiancefield parameterizations, including sparse grids, explicit voxels [81], tensor decompositions [68, 9],
multiresolution hash encodings [57], and plane-based factorization [8, 18]. Later works treat NeRFs
more directly as compressible representations, introducing transform coding [60, 37], quantization
[42, 31], entropy-constrained coding [37, 43], and learned codec regimes [43, 31, 71, 17, 83, 25].
These studies show that compactness strongly depends on structured field parameterization, but they
remain primarily designed for volumetric radiance fields, whose rendering is generally less direct for
real-time immersive applications than explicit Gaussian rasterization.
Compression for GS-based methods extends compact static 3DGS representations to dynamic
volumetric video, driven by advances in GS compression [54, 11, 12, 10, 67, 24, 41] and multiview
datasets [29, 62, 45, 19, 2]. For static 3DGS [32], existing methods reduce redundancy through
compact parameterization [79, 14], pruning [61, 22, 35], quantization [70, 58, 15], context modeling
[49, 12, 72, 50, 76], and rate-distortion optimization [54, 11, 12, 10]. Feed-forward methods [53,
10, 78] further avoid extensive per-scene overfitting. For dynamic scenes, GS-based codecs can
be broadly grouped into frame-wise predictive approaches and temporally shared representations.
The former encode timestamp-dependent Gaussian sets using online updates, motion compensation,
GoF-based inter-frame coding, residual prediction, or temporal pruning and quantization [67, 20, 24,
78, 21, 52, 27]. The latter model temporal evolution with canonical deformation, feature streams,
motion-decoupled trajectories, or structured UV/attribute atlases [41, 82, 84]. Beyond volumetric
video, generative GS-based approaches have also been explored for 2D video compression [51, 69].

3.5
Rate-Distortion Optimization
After the proposal warm-up and hierarchy construction, the provisional flat anchors are discarded,
and only the hierarchical 4D anchor scaffold is optimized for compression. We train SAGA with a
rate-distortion objective over the quantized transmitted symbols Ẑ:
L = Drender + λr R(Ẑ) + λA LA + λmask Lmask .
(11)
Here R(Ẑ) is the entropy-estimated bitrate under the hierarchical coding order, and λr , λA and λmask
control the relative strengths of the bitrate penalty, anchor regularization, and mask regularization
terms, respectively. Quantization and the rate term are enabled after an initial reconstruction stage,
with λr ramped up linearly. The rendering loss follows common 3DGS practice, with Drender =
(1 − η)∥I˜c,s − Ic,s ∥1 + η(1 − SSIM(I˜c,s , Ic,s )), where I˜c,s and Ic,s are the rendered and ground-truth
frames; during training, I˜c,s passes through a per-camera affine color correction that is the identity
at test time. The anchor regularization constrains local offsets to remain within their parent-anchor
coordinate ranges. We define OAP= {ōmi } ∪ {ōmij } for the coarse-to-fine and fine-to-primitive local
offsets, and use LA = |OA |−1 ō∈OA ∥ ReLU(|ō| − ρA )∥22 , where ρA is the allowed normalized
boundary. This prevents the hierarchy from degenerating into unconstrained flat anchors.

4.2
Experimental Results
Novel View Synthesis. As summarized in Table 1, we first evaluate SAGA as a compact dynamic GS
representation before quantization and entropy coding. Across Neu3D and Panoptic Sports, SAGA
provides a favorable fidelity-size trade-off, with its small variant achieving the smallest reported raw
model size among compared methods. On Neu3D, the medium variant already surpasses all evaluated
baselines in PSNR with a 17.37 MB model, while the large variant further achieves the best PSNR and
LPIPS at 32.81 dB and 0.019, respectively. On Panoptic Sports, even the small variant outperforms
all evaluated baselines in PSNR and SSIM, using only 8.7 MB. Increasing model capacity further
improves reconstruction quality, although the LPIPS remains higher than that of 4DGS and E-D3DGS.
These results show that the proposed spacetime anchor hierarchy and inter-anchor decoding provide
a compact representation with competitive reconstruction quality before entropy coding; additional
gains from quantization and entropy modeling are evaluated in Table 2.
Volumetric Video Compression. As summarized in Table 2, SAGA achieves consistent ratedistortion improvements over the evaluated GS-based volumetric codecs across the available comparisons. Compared with GIFStream [41], SAGA obtains large BD-rate reductions across all three
datasets, with at least 67.09%, 67.10%, and 45.87% savings measured by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS,
respectively. The gains also extend to recent baselines on Neu3D, with PSNR BD-rate savings
exceeding 60% over both TED-4DGS and OMG4, indicating that the compression advantage is
not limited to a single baseline. Beyond GS-based methods, SAGA also outperforms both TMIV
configurations on MPEG MIV. Even against the VVEnc configuration, it achieves a PSNR BD-rate
reduction of 18.41%, with larger savings of over 70% under SSIM and LPIPS. Together, these
results demonstrate improved compression efficiency under both distortion-based and perceptual
quality metrics. The corresponding rate-distortion curves and qualitative comparisons are provided in
Figure 4 and Figure 5, with the latter further illustrating that SAGA preserves finer texture details at
lower bitrates than GIFStream and OMG4.

4.3
Ablation Study
To investigate different design components in SAGA, we created the following variants: V1 removes
the coarse-to-fine scaffold and uses flat anchors; V2 disables inter-anchor feature interpolation and
decodes finer representations only from its own anchor; V3 removes the residual hash grid; V4
disables MLP compression; and V5 replaces the proposed orthogonality-guided memory update with
a non-orthogonal gated residual update. The full model is used as the anchor for BD-rate computation.
As shown in Table 3 (b), each ablated variant incurs BD-rate loss, demonstrating the effectiveness of
the corresponding component. Removing the hierarchy or inter-anchor interpolation substantially
degrades RD performance, supporting the importance of both components, while the 8.17% loss of
V5 further confirms the contribution of the orthogonalized memory update.
Impact of Memory Slot Size. We also ablate the impact of memory slot size on compression
performance and decoding speed, as shown in Table 3 (c). It can be observed that naively increasing
the memory slot size offers diminishing returns in compression efficiency; this indicates that the
long-context spatiotemporal dependency is already well captured with fewer memory slots, i.e., no
more than 256. We hence choose 64 slots as the default for real-time decoding, while larger memories
provide better compression at a substantial throughput cost.
5
Conclusion
In this paper, we presented SAGA, a GS-based volumetric video compression framework built on
hierarchical sparse 4D anchor scaffolds, which exploit inter-anchor redundancy and model local
dynamics at variable granularity. Sparse coarse anchors decode fine anchors, which instantiate
Gaussian primitives via learned offsets, and learnable masks prune redundant primitives. A longcontext entropy model further summarizes decoded contexts into bounded memory for efficient
entropy-parameter prediction. Experiments on Neu3D, Panoptic Sports, and MPEG MIV CTC
benchmarks demonstrate that SAGA outperforms existing GS-based codecs in compression efficiency,
with average BD-rate reductions (measured by PSNR) of 64.30%, 65.14%, and 90.88%, over TED4DGS, OMG4, and GIFStream on the Neu3D testset, respectively.
Limitations and Future Work. SAGA still requires per-scene optimization, which may incur notable
encoding time for long or complex sequences, and fast motion, topology changes, and sparse camera
coverage may also challenge reconstruction. Future work will explore faster encoding, adaptive
anchor allocation, and standardized bitstreams for scalability, random access, and deployment.

A
Broader Impact
This work improves the efficiency of volumetric video compression, with potential benefits for
telepresence, remote collaboration, education, cultural preservation, digital media production, and
immersive communication. By reducing storage and transmission costs while retaining real-time
decoding, SAGA may make high-quality immersive media more accessible under limited bandwidth
and compute budgets, and may reduce the infrastructure and energy costs of large-scale volumetric
video delivery.
At the same time, more compact and realistic volumetric representations may increase risks related
to privacy, unauthorized capture, surveillance, and deceptive or non-consensual synthetic media,
especially when the content contains identifiable people or private environments. Responsible
deployment should therefore consider consent, access control, provenance tracking, watermarking or
authentication, and dataset governance. Our work focuses on compression and reconstruction quality
on established research datasets, and does not develop tools for identity manipulation, text-driven
content synthesis, or surveillance.

Additional Experimental Results
D.1
Bitstream and Inference Speed Breakdown
Inference speed breakdown. As shown in Fig. D.1, rasterization (28%) and anchor/offset decoding
(26%) dominate the inference time, accounting for more than half of the total runtime. In contrast,
entropy decoding and other lightweight operations introduce relatively limited overhead.
Bitstream breakdown. As shown in Fig. D.2, Gaussian-related parameters account for the majority
of the bitstream across different datasets, with Gaussian offsets contributing the largest share (approximately 41–46%). This suggests that Gaussian parameter representation, particularly offsets, remains
the primary bottleneck for further compression.
