Context\n\nAfter regional loading is bounded, the CPU renderer still projects individual voxel corners, creates one UI shape per exposed face, and sorts all generated faces every frame. Dense local regions can therefore remain expensive even when the total world is streamed correctly.\n\n## Outcome\n\nUnchanged resident chunks reuse render data, and dense chunks render exposed surfaces rather than independent voxel cubes.\n\n## Scope\n\n- Introduce render-cache entries keyed by world identity, chunk coordinate, overlay revision, and geometry-affecting render settings.\n- Rebuild a chunk only when it becomes resident, its overlay changes, a relevant setting changes, or a neighboring boundary changes.\n- Extract only exposed surfaces.\n- Benchmark simple face extraction first; add greedy coplanar face merging if measurements justify it.\n- Reuse cached geometry across camera movement.\n- Invalidate both sides of a chunk boundary when occupancy changes there.\n- Preserve agent, goal, trail, waypoint, start, and selection overlays as separate dynamic layers.\n- Keep picking correct against cached geometry.\n\n## Acceptance criteria\n\n- [ ] Static camera motion does not rebuild unchanged chunk geometry.\n- [ ] Interior faces and cross-chunk hidden faces are not emitted.\n- [ ] Boundary mutations invalidate the affected chunk and neighbor.\n- [ ] Overlay semantics match the direct regional world view.\n- [ ] Dense-region benchmarks report build time, rendered faces/triangles, frame-time p50/p95, and cache effectiveness.\n- [ ] Profiling evidence records whether greedy meshing is required.\n- [ ] Structural rendering and picking tests cover chunk boundaries and overlay changes.\n\n## Relationships\n\n- Depends on bounded agent-local compiled-world visualization.\n- Performance increment under #22.\n- GPU buffers may consume this cache later, but GPU work is not required here.
