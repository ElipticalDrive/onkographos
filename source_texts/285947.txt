Assessing swelling is crucial for the surgical treatment of lower limb lymphedema and is often conducted using 2-dimensional (2D) imaging, which is available in most outpatient settings. Advanced methods, such as taping and computed tomography (CT), are limited by time and equipment constraints. The neural radiance field (NeRF) uses deep learning to reconstruct 3-dimensional (3D) scenes from 2D images, overcoming the challenges, particularly reflections and refractions, associated with photogrammetry. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of Luma 3D, an iPhone application that utilizes NeRF, for volumetric assessment in an outpatient setting. An 88-year-old woman with chronic lymphedema was evaluated over a period of 12 months using NeRF-based 3D modeling. She presented with walking difficulty, owing to congestive dermatitis and persistent exudate, and was treated nonsurgically using topical applications and compression therapy. Initial CT scans showed a bilateral volume of 19,500 mL from the knee to the foot, whereas 3D measurements indicated a total volume of 20,100 mL. After 12 months of treatment, a CT scan showed a volume of 19,900 mL, whereas the 3D application measured a volume of 20,300 mL, demonstrating the capability of NeRF in volume change assessment, which could not be achieved by 2D assessments. Therefore, NeRF offers a promising solution for the 3D assessment of lymphedema in outpatient settings, providing accurate and noninvasive volumetric measurements. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional imaging methods and has potential applications in the fields of medical equipment and telemedicine.

Takeaways
Question: What is the practicality and efficacy of using the neural radiance field (NeRF) technology in daily practice?
Findings: NeRF-based 3-dimensional modeling demonstrated that volumetric changes can be monitored noninvasively with considerable accuracy.
Meaning: NeRF provides a promising, noninvasive method for 3-dimensional volumetric assessment and has the potential to change diagnostic and monitoring practices in outpatient settings.
INTRODUCTION
Accurate swelling assessment is crucial for the treatment of lower limb lymphedema. However, assessment in outpatient settings is often conducted in 2-dimensional (2D) spaces using cameras. Objective evaluation methods for lower limb lymphedema, such as taping and computed tomography (CT), are limited by time and equipment constraints in clinical practice, hindering their application. Thus, a more simple, cost-effective, and accessible volumetric measurement method is required. Neural radiance field (NeRF) is an innovative technology that generates 3-dimensional (3D) scenes from multiple photographs, allowing for object reconstruction. Although its application in the medical field is in its early stages, NeRF-generated 3D models have the potential to replace physical models in medical and engineering education.
METHODS
We evaluated leg edema progression in an 88-year-old woman with chronic lymphedema using NeRF 3D modeling over approximately 12 months (Fig. 1). The patient presented with walking difficulty owing to congestive dermatitis and persistent exudates. As she declined surgical treatment, she was treated with ointment application and compression therapy using elastic bandages. The application, Luma 3D (Luma AI, Inc., San Francisco, CA), is free to use and requires 3 complete rotations around the patient for scanning, a process that takes approximately 60–90 seconds. Artificial lighting was exclusively used, completely eliminating natural light at the imaging site. The patient stood in a stable position without tremors, enabling consistent scanning from a fixed distance. The scanner was set to a medium resolution throughout, and a standardized procedure was followed for postscan data processing. At both the initial visit and 12 months posttreatment, CT scan and 3D modeling were performed. The resulting stereolithography data were segmented and volumetrically analyzed using Fusion360 (Autodesk, Inc., San Francisco, CA). The entire procedure, from scan measurement to sectioning and volume data determination, was conducted by the same individual. Scanning was performed twice, and the scan data from the session that exhibited no obvious skin edge irregularities were selected for segmentation. The region of interest was delineated from the suprapatellar border to the plantar region of the foot. This study was conducted with the approval of the ethics committee of Nippon Medical School Hospital.

RESULTS
After 12 months of treatment, the patient’s skin problems resolved, and she resumed her routine activities. The initial CT scan showed a bilateral volume of 20,100 mL from the knee to the foot, whereas the initial 3D NeRF measurement showed a total volume of 20,106 mL (right side: 9903 mL; left side: 10,203 mL). After 12 months of treatment, the volume of the CT was 19,879 mL (right side: 9673 mL; left side: 9906 mL), and the volume of the 3D application was 20,273 mL (right side: 9902 mL; left side: 10,371 mL), demonstrating that NeRF could assess the volume, which was not possible using 2D assessments (Fig. 2). Additionally, we performed water displacement tests, revealing a volume of 20,050 mL (right side: 9800 mL; left side: 10,250 mL).

DISCUSSION
Given the limited subject data, we included a second set of volumetric comparisons derived from 2 separate CT scans and NeRF-based assessments performed for the same patient. This allowed for reproducibility validation of our methodology. To address overall reliability concerns, the water displacement method was conducted alongside NeRF; the results were almost consistent with those of the CT scans. NeRF achieved comparable accuracy to that of traditional CT scans and the water displacement method, highlighting its potential as a novel noninvasive tool for accurate volumetric assessment.
CT is widely used for measuring human body volume. However, access to medical facilities is often limited owing to high costs, unavailability, and lack of immediate accessibility. Photogrammetry and infrared light have been widely applied in the medical field owing to their capability to generate precise 3D data from images, which are essential for accurate assessment and treatment. Because of their practicality and precision, these technologies are being incorporated in various medical applications including volume scanning and surface measurements. Devices such as Perometer (Pero-System, Wuppertal), which uses infrared light, and VECTRA (Canfield Scientific, Inc., Parsippany), LifeViz (QuantifiCare, Biot), and RealFoot (DreamGP, Osaka), which employs photogrammetry, exemplify the utility of these technologies in enhancing diagnostic capabilities and treatment planning by providing detailed and noninvasive 3D visualizations of the human body. However, both types of measuring instruments are expensive and have poor accessibility for use.
Here, we used NeRF, a neural network capable of reconstructing intricate 3D scenes from a series of 2D images.1 This system employs deep learning to infer 3D shapes from image data captured from multiple perspectives.2 This technology uses images of a single object taken from various angles to create a comprehensive 3D model, including parts not fully captured in the images. Consequently, these generated 3D models exhibit minimal missing parts. This approach is expected to address the challenges associated with scanning technologies. Although photogrammetry has been a conventional technique for 3D modeling, NeRF serves as a superior 3D modeling tool. Photogrammetry relies on geometric calculations to estimate an object’s 3D shape, whereas NeRF utilizes deep learning for 3D reconstruction. Photogrammetry struggles to accurately generate scenes due to transparency, reflections, and refractions; in contrast, NeRF is garnering worldwide attention for its proficiency in these areas.3
Regarding the efficacy of reduced swelling measurement, NeRF enables straightforward and minimally invasive volumetric assessment by scanning the target lower limb for a fraction of a second in an outpatient setting. However, NeRF has several potential challenges for human application. Unless the patient is completely stationary, 3D reconstruction is often ambiguous. If the patient cannot maintain a steady posture, using the device for imaging purposes may prove difficult. Additionally, generating a 3D model is time-consuming and requires the patient to wait for the 3D image to be processed and shared. Recent research has focused on these issues, and solutions are likely to emerge.4
CONCLUSIONS
NeRF is expected to be used in future medical equipment, overcoming the limitations of traditional magnetic resonance imaging and CT scans, and may also have applications in telemedicine. Despite these advancements, NeRF still requires improvements in data volume and computational complexity. Furthermore, it holds significant potential for remote diagnostics and real-time consultations, offering enhanced visual data.
