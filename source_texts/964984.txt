Adds a mode to the finish's light_samples. It is the default from #version 4.0 (and in .pov4 scenes); older versions keep classic full sampling:
```
finish { light_samples M [full | by_contribution] }
#default { finish { light_samples 1 full } }   // classic sampling for a whole 4.0 scene (default { ... } in .pov4)
```
With by_contribution, a lit hit takes light samples in proportion to its contribution to the pixel. A hit seen through a faint reflection or through several clear layers takes few samples. Every surface the camera sees directly, clear ones included, takes them all. full is the classic sampling.
Syntax and naming
Per finish, extending light_samples M. The mode belongs to the receiving surface, like the multiplier it modifies. light_samples M alone keeps the finish's mode, so raising the multiplier never changes the mode.
Version default, no global setting. A finish that states no mode samples by contribution from #version 4.0 and in full before it, resolved when parsing ends, as interface_texture is. #default { finish { light_samples 1 full } } (default { } in .pov4) restores classic sampling for a scene, and an explicit mode in a finish overrides either default. No new keyword was needed beyond the two values.
Name: by_contribution. Alternatives considered:
by_contribution (chosen): says what the sample count follows, the hit's contribution to the pixel. It reads naturally after the multiplier: light_samples 1 by_contribution.
throughput: names the quantity used, but it is renderer jargon, and the weight also includes the light reaching the layer through the layers above.
importance: conventional in rendering, but "importance sampling" usually means where samples go on a light, not how many a hit takes, so it would mislead.
The word is easy to change if the owner prefers another. It appears in one table in parser_materials.cpp and one comparison in pov4.cpp, plus the docs and tests.
full and by_contribution are values of light_samples, not finish items. The classic parser reads it from a table, so it is not mined into the 4.0 block schema. The 4.0 evaluator takes it only right after light_samples' value; a bare finish { by_contribution } is an unknown name. Both words stay free as identifiers.
Definition
Contribution w: the weight of the ray that found the hit (the product of reflection and transmission factors from the camera, the same weight adc_bailout tests) times the largest channel of the light reaching the texture layer through the layers above it, capped at 1.
No opacity factor. The layer's own opacity is deliberately left out, so a clear surface the camera sees directly has w = 1. An earlier revision multiplied by opacity: clear sheets seen directly fell to one or two samples, and their lighting noise showed even though it is a small share of the pixel (see Evidence).
Surface and volume lights: round(N x M x w) draws, at least 1.
Classic area lights: a grid of 1 + round((n - 1) x sqrt(w)) points per side, at least 2; sides of 1 or 2 are unchanged. Applies to adaptive, jitter, circular, orient and area_illumination alike.
Ceiling: never more samples than full.
Layered textures: each layer has its own mode and weight. A classic area light's shadow is cached per hit and shared by its layers. It is sampled again when a later layer weighs more than the one it was sampled for, so the heaviest layer sets the grid.
Unbiased: fewer samples add noise, not a brightness change.
Deterministic: the weight depends only on the ray's path, so images are identical for any thread count.
Anti-aliasing: every camera ray starts at w = 1. Judge the mode at -A, because adaptive AA reacts to noise.
Only surface lighting is scaled, which is deliberately conservative. Media lighting (every method), subsurface scattering (both methods), photons and radiosity gather rays never read the weight and keep their full counts. Subsurface runs after the weight is restored, and the media sample counts don't see it.
Deferred
Media light sampling: the weight would have to be passed into MediaFunction, with a per-path decision for the classic, fixed-path and prepared method 4 counts.
Subsurface: method 2 irradiance is cached and shared across pixels. Method 1's per-hit budget could be weighted.
Radiosity gathers (radiosityShare): the cached results are reused across pixels, so this needs its own evaluation.
Evidence
Scenes: generic, 320x240, every render -A. Cost is user-space instructions (perf counters, all threads). RMSE is on the 0-255 scale, against a full-sampling reference at S256/G33 (S = surface/volume light samples, G = classic area light grid side).

Tests
tests/render/light_samples_by_contribution.sh (in check-quick) checks:
A #version 3.8 scene with no mode samples in full (identical to explicit full and to plain light_samples 1).
A #version 4.0 scene with no mode samples by contribution (identical image and draw count to explicit by_contribution, and fewer draws than explicit full); plain light_samples 1 keeps that default.
The mode takes fewer surface draws and fewer classic shadow rays.
A finish's own light_samples 1 full opts out of a default that has the mode.
Images are identical at 1, 2 and 4 threads.
A directly seen floor gives an identical image with identical counts.
A directly seen clear slab draws exactly what full sampling draws.
A layered floor (a light clear layer over an opaque one) traces at least the opaque floor's shadow rays.
Means agree within 0.5% at 2048 samples.
A .pov4 scene with no mode samples by contribution and lowers to the same render.
A bare by_contribution and an unknown word after light_samples are refused.

surface and volume light's samples, as before. by_contribution scales each
lit hit's samples by its contribution to the pixel: the ray's weight (the
one adc_bailout tests) times the largest channel of the light reaching the
texture layer through the layers above, capped at 1. Surface and volume
lights draw round(N x M x w) points, at least 1. Classic area lights sample
a grid whose sides shrink to 1 + round((n - 1) x sqrt(w)) points, at least
2, for shadows and area_illumination alike. A layer's own opacity is not
part of w, so every surface the camera sees directly, clear or not, keeps
full samples. full is the classic sampling.

by_contribution is the default from #version 4.0 and in .pov4 scenes; full
is the default before. light_samples M alone keeps the finish's mode, and
a whole scene.

A hit's layers share a classic area light's cached shadow, so the cache
records the weight a shadow was sampled at and samples it again for a
heavier layer. Radiosity gather rays, media, subsurface and photons keep
their full counts. The weight follows the ray's path, so images do not
depend on the thread count.

they are not finish items in the 4.0 block schema; the 4.0 evaluator takes

the r3_3 keyword index, contextual words and version defaults,

shadow rays, thread identity, an unchanged directly seen floor and clear
slab, layered textures, many-sample means, the 4.0 language and refused
