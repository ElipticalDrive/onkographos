Part of #71
Question
Which conservative visibility tests can cut client work in BhayanakVoxel without changing a pixel, and what does each actually save in a radiance-cached voxel path tracer?
Cover at least
Frustum culling at brick, region and LOD-node granularity: test cost, false-positive rate on a sphere, and whether it belongs on the CPU scene selection, the GPU traversal, or both.
Horizon capping on the 16 km Planet: visible-cap half-angle as a function of camera height, from the ground to 20–50 R; how to intersect it with the frustum; how it behaves in caves and under overhangs; how it interacts with the continuous Zoom.
Occlusion: Hi-Z or depth-prepass, portal/cell for caves, coarse-occupancy tests. Does it pay for primary rays when the tracer already DDA-steps empty bricks? Does it pay for shadow and GI rays, given the radiance cache? Is the real win in tracing, generation, upload, VRAM or BVH updates?
Conservative correctness: what bounds make each test exact (never removes a surface that could contribute a pixel), and how to prove it — culling-on vs culling-off image equality, plus a no-hole camera path.
Contract impact: what each test needs from LOD rings on the GPU #48's ring layout and the scene tree, and what it would change in LOD rings on the GPU #48, Streaming generation #49, Hardware RT trace() #50 and Half-res tracing and TAAU upscale #51.
Output
A ranked shortlist. Per technique: what it buys (ms / bytes / hitches, as a range with confidence), implementation cost, risk, fidelity impact, dependencies, and the cheapest experiment that would validate it on the 4090.
Cite primary sources for external claims. This ticket is evidence for the catalogue; do not design the final architecture.

Resolution
Findings: https://github.com/theHimanshuShekhar/bhayanakvoxel/blob/research/visibility-and-culling/docs/research/visibility-and-culling.md
Culling cannot cut rays here, and that reframes the whole ticket. trace() is a DDA that only visits what a ray crosses, a depth prepass would duplicate the primary pass, and a cull cannot remove a camera ray. Only about 2.0 of the 8.6 ms is even addressable (primary 1.0, edits/BVH 1.0), so the realistic frame-time win is 0.05–0.25 ms (1–3 %). The wins are hitches (2–3.5× fewer new bricks/s) and VRAM (2–8×); generation > upload > VRAM > BVH size > tracing.
A frustum test without the horizon test is close to useless on a sphere. Measured (40 M sampled nodes/camera): at ground level the cone keeps 7.7 % of all surface bricks while 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ are visible — 4,650× waste, and 1.7 × 10⁷× looking 60° down, because the cone passes through the Planet and takes the far side. Frustum-only is 8.5× at 1 km, 27× looking 30° down.
The horizon cap is the one must-have, and the only cull with no look-around risk. One dot product per node (3.7 ns measured), view-independent, exact for a convex Planet: 2.0–2.1× of the coarse set at 20–50 R (the visible fraction never passes ½), 4× at 1 R, 30–3,000× between 10 m and 1 km. Combined with the frustum it leaves 1.0–2.1× of the truly visible bricks. Cap geometry table, Zoom behaviour and the cave gating are in §2.
Frustum culling belongs on the CPU as scene selection on a camera change, at ring-tile/region granularity — never per ray. 12.5 ns/node measured: 10⁵–10⁶ bricks is 1.3–13 ms (too slow), 10³–10⁴ regions is 13–125 µs, 10²–10³ tiles is 1–13 µs. Selection is a property of the camera, not the frame, so it costs ~0 µs on still frames.
Occlusion does not pay. Hi-Z is circular for camera rays, faces the wrong way for shadow rays (a shadow ray leaves away from the depth buffer), and is non-conservative for GI rays — Lumen's screen traces are an accelerator with a fallback, not a cull. Coarse occupancy mips can't be conservative: "any-solid" over-culls, "all-solid" just reproduces Core. Portals/cells for caves stay on the watchlist.
Two cheap wins hide in the existing trace(). It clips to the occupied-region AABB; a corner ray walks 0.73 R ≈ 11.7 km of empty space (1,828 region probes at level 0) before reaching the Planet, so a bounding-sphere clip is free and exact — but Scene has no per-node bounds and one global AABB, which is the real contract gap.
Proof is three gates, not one: bit-exact culling-on vs culling-off PNGs (the tracer is already deterministic), a CPU ray-incidence oracle (visited ⊆ retained over millions of rays — the only test that finds the cave case), and a no-hole path (180° turn, cave dive, ground↔50 R Zoom, teleport).
Contract asks: LOD rings on the GPU #48 — per-tile bounds, a per-level origin/extent, an angular tile index (makes the horizon a dot-product threshold and the frustum a per-face range), a visible/instance list, and bound_centre/bound_radius in SceneHeader. Streaming generation #49 — priority visible ∩ horizon, prefetch at h(t+1–2 s), "no holes" asserted with culling on. Hardware RT trace() #50 — TLAS built from the visible list, rebuilt on cull change. Half-res tracing and TAAU upscale #51 — conservative over the jittered frustum and the history window; a cull change must not invalidate TAA history.
Confidence: high on the geometry, the test costs and the kept fractions (computed/measured here); medium on the byte and hitch ranges; low–medium on the frame-time numbers, which no one can know without the GPU.
Open questions: the cave width and lateral reach (#46) are the one blocker for the horizon cap; whether #48's rings are 4π shells or a tangent-plane clipmap changes every denominator; nothing measures region probes per ray yet, so the sphere clip's value is an estimate; and #54's cache-update policy decides whether view-scoped cache updates are a real second-order win.
Five experiments for the 4090 are in §7, each with a pass/fail that does not need a human to squint. The gating one is a --cull on|off flag plus a cave-orbit-v1 path: byte-identical PNGs or the technique is out.
