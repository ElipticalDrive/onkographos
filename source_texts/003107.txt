Abstract
Approaches to achieve high performance for volume visualization via the popular Marching Cubes algorithm on commodity x86 personal computers are described. The approaches utilize Single Instruction Multiple Data (SIMD) Within A Register (SWAR) capabilities available in the streaming media extensions of recent-generation x86 CPUs. The SWAR capabilities are exploited in a manner that enables simultaneous processing of multiple volume data points, edges, and cells. In addition, the approach maintains the rendering quality of a standard serial implementation of the Marching Cubes. An average overall speedup of nearly four times is achieved (compared to an unoptimized standard implementation that uses conventional serial processing).

Introduction
Many application domains make use of volume visualization to discover information in 3D datasets. However, visualization of most datasets requires a large number of computations which, historically, have not been computable at real-time rates in uni-processor environments. In addition, it is common for users of volume visualization techniques to conduct multiple applications of a given technique, typically through variance of one or more parameters of the technique, until either a satisfactory rendering is achieved or until all needed information is discovered. The iterative nature of the discovery process makes user-creators of visualizations quite sensitive to computational delays.
In order to provide improved performance for volume visualization, many parallel approaches have been presented over the past decade. The majority of such work to date has involved use of multi-processor computers, especially in multiple instruction multiple data (MIMD) computation modes. Some past works (e.g., by our group [1] and others (e.g., [2])) have demonstrated the utility of single instruction multiple data (SIMD) computation (either using massively parallel or vector computation) for certain visualization and/or graphics tasks. However, only a few methods for efficient desktop SIMD (i.e., SIMD Within A Register (SWAR)) computation of volume visualization methods have been reported in the literature. (For instance, a very small number of papers have considered exploitation of the SIMD capabilities of commodity Intel Pentium III/4 or AMD Athlon CPUs (e.g., [3]).) In this paper, we describe methods that efficiently exploit desktop SIMD capability in the Pentium III/4 and AMD Athlon/Duron CPU lines to compute one volume visualization technique, the popular Marching Cubes [4] isosurface extraction.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, related works are discussed. In Section 3, a brief introduction to vector parallelism and desktop SIMD is presented. In Section 4, the Marching Cubes isosurface extraction is discussed. In Section 5, details of our SWAR-exploitive approach to isosurface extraction are presented. In Section 6, results are described. The paper concludes in Section 7.

Conclusion
In this paper, approaches that exploit small-scale SIMD capabilities of commodity x86 CPUs for efficient isosurface extraction have been described. The approaches reorganize the processing steps of the standard Marching Cubes to allow for, on average, approximately twice the performance of a compiler-optimized C code and approximately four times the performance of a compiler-unoptimized C code.
