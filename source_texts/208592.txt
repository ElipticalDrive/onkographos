What this adds
A precomputed volume gradient that both backends build the same way, and two
new render settings that consume it:
volumeGradientOpacity modulates the background ray-march alpha by gradient
magnitude, so flat interiors fade and material boundaries stay.
volumeSilhouette adds a Fresnel rim term.
volumeLayerGradientMode selects the in-shader normal estimator for the
overlay and drawing passes, which carry no gradient texture of their own.
All three default to 0 / off, so an existing scene renders exactly as before
and no volume pays for a gradient pass it does not use.
Figures
Kingsnake OME-Zarr in range.html, WebGPU, same window (30-110), same camera.
Before - illumination, gradient opacity and silhouette all 0. The specimen
is an opaque resin cylinder and the snake inside it is not visible at all.
After - illumination 0.55, gradient opacity 0.65, silhouette 0.40. Gradient
opacity clears the flat resin, the Fresnel rim picks out the scales, and the
coiled body and vertebral column read directly.
The parity work behind it
The two backends disagreed in three ways. Each was found by measuring the
readbacks, not by reading the code:
Magnitude was WebGPU-only. The GL pass wrote a constant 1.0 into
alpha, which made gradient opacity a silent no-op on WebGL2. Both now write
a logarithmic magnitude; a linear one sits near zero through most of a
volume and drives the whole render to black around amount 0.7.
They differentiated different channels. WebGL2 used the colormapped
alpha, WebGPU the red. On hot, red saturates at 37% of the range and is
flat above it, so WebGPU returned zero gradient across the top 63% of the
data. Alpha is monotonic in intensity for every LUT, so both read alpha now.
Different estimators. WebGPU ran an 8-corner Sobel plus a 27-tap blur
against WebGL2's three central differences, and the blur ran on the already
encoded texture, averaging across the encoding rather than the data. The
shared estimator in view/NVGradient.ts now pins the constants for both
sides, and the linear sampler supplies the smoothing.
Cost
The gradient build is deferred: an all-zero lighting state uploads no gradient
texture at all. Turning a consumer on evicts and re-streams the working set
with gradients built. On the range streaming demo that re-stream is served
from the decoded-chunk cache and touches the network not at all (resident 38,
decoded-tier hits 0 -> 37 with misses unchanged at 38). Moving a slider within
the non-zero range after that costs nothing.
Testing
NVGradient.test.ts pins the shared encoding constants under the Bun runner.
e2e/gradient-parity.spec.ts runs both backends' passes over one volume in
real Chromium and diffs the readbacks.
e2e/gradient-deferred-build.spec.ts asserts the all-zero state uploads no
gradient, and that enabling a consumer produces one.
Examples
vox.gradopacity - the feature demo over mni152 and two CT volumes, whose
air/tissue and tissue/bone steps give the gradient sharper material edges.
vox.gradopacity.explode - the same settings on force-tiled blocks, where
the halo is what keeps a cut face from rimming like a real surface.
vox.blobgrad - an A/B of the in-shader layer estimators across two linked
instances with a draggable seam.
range.html gains the three sliders, so the settings are exercised on real
streamed multi-LOD bricks. Two notes documented in the file: they act on the
3D ray-march only and the page opens on multiplanar, so the sliders look
dead until Layout is render; and the default synthetic shard source is
halo 0 by construction, so its exploded bricks rim on their cut faces. That
is the negative control for the halo, not the positive case.
Every measurement here was taken in Chrome on an M5 Max, not headless
SwiftShader, which inflates these numbers badly (the shader compile by two
orders of magnitude).

Both backends now build the precomputed gradient texture with the same
estimator, and two new consumers read it: `volumeGradientOpacity` modulates
the background pass alpha by the gradient magnitude, `volumeSilhouette` adds
the Fresnel rim term. `volumeLayerGradientMode` selects the in-shader normal
estimator for the overlay and drawing passes, which carry no gradient texture.

Three fixes brought the two backends together, each found by measuring rather
than by reading the code:

- Only WebGPU ever stored a magnitude; the GL pass wrote a constant 1.0 in
  alpha, which made gradient opacity a silent no-op there. Both now write a
  LOGARITHMIC magnitude, since a linear one sits near zero through most of a
  volume and drives the whole render to black at amount 0.7.
- The two backends differentiated different channels: WebGL2 the colormapped
  alpha, WebGPU the red. On `hot` red saturates at 37% of the range and is
  flat above it, so WebGPU returned zero gradient across the top 63% of the
  data. Alpha is monotonic in intensity for every LUT, so both read it now.
- WebGPU ran an 8-corner Sobel plus a 27-tap blur against WebGL2's three
  central differences. The blur ran on the ENCODED texture, averaging
  already-normalized normals and already-log-compressed magnitudes, so it
  biased the magnitude field rather than smoothing it. WebGPU now runs
  WebGL2's estimator and gets its smoothing the same way, free from the
  linear sampler at a fractional radius; blur.wgsl is gone.

The shared constants live in the new src/view/NVGradient.ts: sobel.wgsl takes
them as pipeline-overridable constants and gl/gradient.ts interpolates the
same values into its GLSL, so the two encodings cannot drift.

Building a gradient is not free (measured 8.8 ms on WebGL2 and 10.0 ms on
WebGPU for an 11.4 Mvoxel volume, plus 45.6 MB of VRAM), so it is now gated
on a consumer actually wanting one. A chunked stream whose lighting is all
zero uploads bricks UNLIT and skips the per-brick gradient pass entirely;
turning any consumer on re-streams the working set once with gradients built.
