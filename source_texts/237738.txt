Part of #346.
Add an isosurface (triangle-mesh) representation for volumetric data, alongside the point cloud (today) and the planned voxels (#351). For a volumetric scalar field, the natural mesh is a marching-cubes isosurface at a threshold — and for radar these map onto operational products.
Why it's compelling for radar
Storm-cell shells — the 40/50 dBZ surface shows convective cores as solid 3D objects.
Echo tops — the height surface of a reflectivity threshold (a standard nowcasting product).
Hail / ZDR-column and bright-band / melting-layer surfaces — dual-pol signatures as 3D shapes.
CAPPI — a textured mesh at a constant altitude.
Why a mesh, vs the other representations
Surfaces read as solid 3D; point clouds don't. A mesh is lit, occluding, depth-sorted — a supercell rendered as a shaded isosurface looks like a 3D object; the point cloud is unlit speckle with no occlusion.
Small payload — a surface is far less data than a volume of points (~13 MB .pnts → a few hundred KB of mesh).
Standards-clean, non-deprecated — a triangle mesh is glTF 2.0, first-class 3D Tiles 1.1 content, with none of the glTF-POINTS rendering gotcha. So this also sidesteps the .pnts deprecation (3D Tiles: migrate point-cloud content from deprecated .pnts to glTF (or retire in favor of voxels #351) #356) for this representation.
How it fits the three representations (complementary, by question)
Voxels (3D Tiles voxels: octree LOD and national mosaic after single-tile support #351): the full continuous field — "what's the reflectivity everywhere" (ray-march/transparency).
Isosurface mesh (this issue): discrete features — "where are the storm cores and how tall" — legible, shareable, lit, small.
Point cloud (today): the simplest faithful dump; good default, least communicative.
Tradeoffs / risks
It's a reduction: one surface = one threshold; you lose the continuous field. Nested translucent shells (e.g. 35/45/55 dBZ) bring transparency-sorting fiddliness.
Needs structured input + extraction: resample the polar volume to a regular (radius, azimuth, height) lattice (the same one voxels need), then marching cubes — more server compute than dumping points, but smaller output.
Gaps/noise: radar is holey (cone of silence, beam spreading, attenuation). Isosurfaces can be jagged, and the mesher must not fabricate surface across unscanned gaps — respect the coverage envelope, like the current sampler.
Scope (sketch)
ds-3dtiles: a marching-cubes isosurface → glTF (TRIANGLES) encoder; per-shell colour/material; optional nested levels with transparency.
Reuse the (radius, azimuth, height) resampling shared with 3D Tiles voxels: octree LOD and national mosaic after single-tile support #351, and the existing 4/3-Earth geometry; reuse the envelope guard so gaps aren't bridged.
api-3dtiles: serve the mesh tileset (a ?style=isosurface&levels=35,45,55 content variant, or a separate route); tileset content.uri → .glb.
Likely a new domain type in ds-core::volume (a regular scalar grid, or a mesh) rather than VolumePointCloud.
Relationship to siblings
3D Tiles voxels: octree LOD and national mosaic after single-tile support #351 (voxels) shares the resampling-to-regular-grid step.
3D Tiles: migrate point-cloud content from deprecated .pnts to glTF (or retire in favor of voxels #351) #356 (glTF migration) — this is firmly in the glTF / Tier-A direction; pairs naturally.
