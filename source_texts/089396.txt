Background
Real-time voxel destruction (think Teardown, Minecraft, Voxel Doom, MagicaVoxel) is its own subdomain. The goal: any analytic-primitive or CSG-mesh object can be "fractured" into voxel chunks on impact. Each chunk becomes an independent rigid body. Particles + audio + screen shake make it feel right.
This roadmap supersedes the destruction half of #52 (which originally bundled soft-body + destruction). Soft body is now covered by #131 physics roadmap. Voxel destruction gets its own track because it has different concerns: voxelization, connectivity graphs, fracture patterns, chunk merging — none of which are physics solver work.
Hard dependency
Cannot ship until [physics Phase 2 (rigid body) #(this batch's rigid body issue)] lands — voxel chunks become rigid bodies, no rigid body = no destruction.
Phases
Phase 1: voxelize CSG mesh + render as voxels (1 PR)
Convert any CSG or analytic primitive to a discrete voxel grid (default 0.1 m voxel size, configurable)
SDF query per voxel center → "inside/outside" classification
Each "inside" voxel becomes an analytic box in the renderer's primitive list
No simulation yet — just visualize the voxelized form
Cvars: r_voxelize_demo (0/1), r_voxel_size (metres, default 0.1)
What this LOOKS like: any mesh re-rendered as a blocky Minecraft-style chunk pile
Phase 2: fracture trigger + voxel rigid bodies (1-2 PRs)
Connectivity graph: 6-neighbor adjacency for each voxel
Impact trigger: when a physics body collides with the voxelized object above a force threshold:
Compute hit voxel + radius of fracture
Sever connections between fractured voxels and the rest
Free voxels become rigid bodies in the physics system (use physics Phase 2 rigid body)
Static-connected voxels remain as the "remaining" object
Cvar: r_destruction_threshold (impact force in N to fracture)
What this LOOKS like: shoot a wall, chunks fly off, rest of wall stays standing
Phase 3: chunk merging optimization (1 PR)
Groups of contiguous voxels that aren't separately moving get treated as ONE big rigid body (not N small ones)
Recompute aggregated inertia tensor on chunk merge
Why this matters: 1000 individual voxel bodies = solver explosion. 30 chunks of 30 voxels each = manageable.
Phase 4: material strength + fracture patterns (1-2 PRs)
Per-material fracture toughness (steel hard, glass brittle, wood splintery)
Crack propagation: when fracturing, cracks prefer to travel along the weakest material axis
Voronoi fracture patterns for non-grid-aligned breaks (think: glass shattering with radial cracks)
What this LOOKS like: glass shatters into shards radiating from the impact point; concrete fractures into chunky blocks; wood splinters along the grain
Phase 5: VFX coupling (1 PR)
Particle burst at fracture point (uses engine: particle / VFX system (GPU particles, PT-compatible) #82 particle system)
Audio: impact sound + falling-debris sound (uses audio: ray-traced spatial audio (BVH-shared occlusion + reverb) #80 audio system)
Camera shake on heavy impacts
Material-specific dust color
What this LOOKS like: AAA-feeling destruction
Honest scope total
5 phases, 5-7 PRs total depending on Phase 2 and 4 split. Plus the hard dependency on physics Phase 2 means voxel destruction can't start until rigid body is solid (so Wave-4 or Wave-5 of the parallel-batch effort).
Critical perf consideration
100k voxels in a wall = a LOT. A 1m³ wall at 0.1m voxel size = 1000 voxels per cubic metre. A 10m × 3m × 0.3m wall = 9000 voxels. Even with chunk-merging optimization, this is a meaningful load. Mitigations:
Variable voxel resolution (coarser interior voxels, finer surface voxels) — Phase 3 polish
GPU broadphase (covered in physics Phase 5)
Aggressive sleeping (chunks not currently moving don't simulate)
What this WON'T do
Deformable / dent-able geometry (not destruction, that's plasticity)
Smoke / dust simulation (uses [Roadmap] Fluid / smoke simulation — emitters, Eulerian grid, SPH, hybrid #135 fluid roadmap, parametric Phase 1 only)
Persistent destruction across game saves (separate save-game work engine: save game system (player state, settings, progress) #89)
Procedural rebuilding (clean-up the mess — that's gameplay, not engine)
