Summary
Implementation of a volumetric lighting stage to simulate "participating media" like fog, dust, and light shafts (god rays). By integrating a scattering model into the existing raytracing and temporal accumulation pipeline, the engine can visualize how light interacts with the air itself, adding significant depth and atmosphere to the voxel world while masking distant level-of-detail transitions.
Functional Requirements
Participating Media Model: The system shall implement a scattering and extinction model (e.g., Beer-Lambert Law) to calculate how much light is absorbed or diverted as it travels through a volume.
In-Scattering Calculation: The compute shader shall sample light sources along each camera ray to determine the amount of light scattered toward the eye, utilizing the Henyey-Greenstein phase function for realistic directional scattering (anisotropy).
Voxel-Aware Density: The fog density must be configurable and support spatial variation, allowing for height-based fog (denser at lower altitudes) or localized "fog volumes" within specific voxel regions.
Ray-Marched Integration: The system shall perform a coarse ray-march through the view frustum to accumulate volumetric lighting, using the Denoising & Temporal Accumulation system to smooth out the results of a low sample count.
Volumetric Shadowing: The system shall account for occlusion within the fog, ensuring that solid voxels cast shadows into the participating media to create light shafts (god rays).
Acceptance Criteria
Verified that light shafts are visible when a strong light source is occluded by voxel geometry.
Verified that fog density increases correctly with distance, eventually obscuring distant voxels.
Verified that the anisotropy parameter successfully controls the "halo" effect around light sources.
Verified that the volumetric pass is correctly jittered and smoothed by the existing temporal accumulation logic.
