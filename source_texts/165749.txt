Recent advancements in reconstructing 3D dynamic scenes from monocular videos have created new possibilities for space-time view synthesis. However, real-world scenarios often involve challenges such as video blurriness caused by de-focus or motion, which degrade synthesis quality. To address this issue, we propose a novel approach that incorporates a deformable blurring kernel and ray origin prediction within neural scene flow fields, effectively modeling the inherent blurriness in imaging. Building on this motivation, we introduce the Adaptive Neural Radiance Field (AdaNeRF), which explicitly accounts for defocus blur by integrating bokeh rendering within a physically grounded blurring model. Extensive experiments on six synthetic datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing approaches, delivering superior quantitative results. AdaNeRF not only enhances synthesis quality but also improves the practical feasibility of 3D reconstruction in real-world applications.
