ABSTRACT
This paper presents a novel methodology for low power
implementation of one and multidimensional discrete wavelet
transform. The basic computation performed by forward and
inverse wavelet transform is the computation of inner products
between vectors of data (either input data or wavelet coefficients
of previous stages) and filter coefficients. The proposed
methodology aims at reducing the switching activity of the inner
product computations required by the wavelet transform, by
reordering the sequence of evaluation of the partial products. The
total hamming distance of the sequence of coefficients (sum of
hamming distances between successive coefficients) is used as the
cost function driving the reordering. Minimization of this cost
function leads to switching activity reduction at the inputs of the
computational units. The proposed methodology is applicable to
both custom hardware and instruction set architectures.
Experimental results show that the proposed methodology leads to
significant switching activity and thus power consumption
savings.
1. INTRODUCTION
Portability as well as cooling and packaging concepts make low
power consumption an important design issue in modern CMOS
design [1]. Especially the need for portable battery powered
wireless communication systems that make extensive use of digital
signal processing such as cellular phones, wireless modems and
multimedia terminals has made low-power DSP a research area of
great importance. The current research for low power design, has
two main directions: estimation and optimization. As far as power
optimization is concerned the most significant power savings can
be achieved in the higher levels of the design hierarchy [1].
Extensive research in the area of data path synthesis for power
optimization has been carried out especially for digital signal
processing systems. Synthesis techniques aiming at power
optimization of digital signal processing systems are described in
[2], [3].
In the last decade wavelet transforms are widely used in signal [9]
and image [10] processing because they offer a wide variety of
useful features in contrast to the Discrete Cosine Transform and
the Fourier Transform. Low-power implementation of discrete
wavelet transforms is very important since wavelets can be
incorporated in a number of portable applications, for example for
image and video compression in portable multimedia devices such
as wireless videophones.
The proposed methodology aims at reducing the switching activity
and thus the power consumption, in Multiply-Accumulator
(MAC) based implementations of wavelet transforms through
reordering the required inner product computations between data
and coefficient vectors. Inner product computation in wavelet
transforms forms the major part of the total computation. The
filter coefficients are stored in memory and are static i.e. known
before implementation. Switching activity reduction is achieved
by reordering the sequence in which the partial products (forming
the inner products) are evaluated and thus the sequence in which
the data and coefficients appear at the inputs of the functional
units. The cost function driving the reordering procedure is the
total hamming distance of the sequence of coefficients i.e. a metric
of bit level transitions in the coefficient inputs of the
computational units. Minimization of this cost function during
reordering leads to reduction of the switching activity and power
consumption on the inputs of the computational units. The
switching activity of a combinational circuit depends on the
internal node structure and on the switching activity at the inputs.
At the architectural level the detailed gate level implementation of
the computational units is not yet known and the power
consumption is estimated based on the total input switching
activities [11]. Thus it can be safely assumed that the reduction of
the switching activity at the inputs of the computational units
leads to reduction of the switching activity and power
consumption inside the units as well.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In section 2 the
mathematical model and the formulation of the proposed
methodology are presented. In section 3 the target architecture
model is presented. Experimental results are given in section 4
while conclusions are offered in section 5.

The problem of computation reordering is formulated as a
Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP). The graph G (V, E) of the
problem consists of the set V of N vertices corresponding to the
partial products required for the computation of an inner product,
and the set E of edges which model the unconstrained transition
from one partial product to another. The problem’s graph is
complete i.e. each vertex pair is connected by an edge. To each
edge of the graph a cost, which is the HD between the two partial
products that the edge connects as defined by eqn. 3 for a specific
ordering function, is assigned. A closed path over all vertices
(partial products) must be found, without passing from a vertex
more than once, resulting in a minimum HD cost. The costs of the
edges are well-bounded positive integer numbers. Their lower
bound is zero and the higher bound equals to the number of bits
used for the representation of the coefficients.
Several algorithms have been proposed for the solution of the TSP
problem. If the size of the problem is relatively small, an exact
solution can be found in short time. For larger problem sizes the
NP-complete class of the problem motivated the research for
heuristic algorithms. Christofides in [4] proposed a heuristic that
requires O(n4) time to find a nearly optimal solution.
3. TARGET ARCHITECTURE MODEL
The proposed methodology can be applied in both custom
hardware and instruction set [5] architectures. A custom hardware
multiply-accumulator based implementation of discrete wavelet
transform is described in [6]. The simplified structure of the target
architecture is shown in figure 1. The data are stored in a
background memory. (This is especially true for multidimensional
signal processing applications). From the definition of the
convolution it is obvious that an overlapping by N-1 terms of the
data vectors, required for the computation of successive output
terms of the convolution, exists. This data reuse [12] may lead to
significant power savings if the appropriate memory hierarchy is
introduced. To exploit the data reuse a memory hierarchy is
created by introducing a set of N foreground registers for storing
the data terms required for the evaluation of one output term of the
convolution. The use of the foreground registers favors power
consumption reduction by replacing the background memory
accesses by register accesses. After the n-1th output term of the
convolution is computed the data term in each register is shifted to
the previous register, the new data term is transferred from the
background memory to the first register (register[n]) and the
computation of the nth output term of the convolution may start.
Since the data are read from the foreground registers according to
the derived ordering function no switching penalty in the address
lines is introduced.
The coefficients are usually stored in a buffer (usually a ROM).
The computation of the output terms of the convolution is
performed on a multiply accumulator based computational unit.
The number of multiply accumulators used is in general
determined by the performance requirements of the application.
The main effect of the proposed methodology on the power
consumption is the reduction of the switching activity at the inputs
of the computational units by deriving an activity optimal
schedule for the evaluation of the partial products. The switching
activity inside the computational units and thus the power
consumption is proportional to the input switching activity [11].
Thus the computation reordering transformation leads to
important power reduction in the computational units.
4. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
The typical 3-level 2-D wavelet decomposition was used as test
vehicle for the demonstration of the proposed methodology. The
simulation results for two grayscale test images (Lena and Man)
and different set of filters (high-pass and low-pass) are included in
tables 1, 2. The EPIC filters are described in [7], the Daubechies
filters are described in [6] while Johnston’s filters are described in
[8].
The second and fourth columns include the changes in the
numbers of transitions in the coefficient inputs of the
computational units after the application of the coefficient
reordering. The third and fifth columns include the percentage
changes, where – denotes reduction of switching activity (savings
from the application of the reordering), while + denotes increase
of the switching activity (penalty from the application of the
computation reordering). For the coefficients, a 16-bit two’s
complement fixed point representation with 15 bits dynamic range
was assumed. The high dynamic range is a worst case assumption
for the proposed methodology since in general it reduces the
possibilities for effective reordering. For the representation of
wavelet coefficients 16 bits were used while for the input data 8
bits were used. The simulation results included in tables 1,2 were
produced assuming that each basic computation as defined in
section 2 is performed on a single multiply accumulator. In such a
case the number of the available computational hardware
resources does not affect the savings achieved by the computation
reordering. If the basic computation is assigned to a number of
different resources the savings are reduced as the number of
resources increases.
The effect of the proposed methodology is obvious. The switching
activity in the inputs of the computational units is significantly
reduced (13.3% worst case, 51.8% best case). It must be noted
that identical savings are achieved in the coefficient memory I/O
unit and in the bus connecting this buffer with the computational
units. The effect of the computation reordering is different at the
data inputs of the computational units. In general the computation
reordering introduces a penalty (worst case 12%) in the switching
activity at these inputs. This is because the reordering destroys
data correlation. Specifically the reordering of computation and
thus of data, introduces switching activity penalty in the inputs of
the three levels of the wavelet decomposition because at these
points the correlation of data is high. In the intermediate and
output stages of the decomposition levels data are less correlated
and thus the result of the reordering is more random (introduces
either small savings or small penalties in switching activity).
However, the penalty in data activity introduced is much smaller
in comparison to the savings achieved, making the use of the
proposed methodology advantageous.
Two basic parameters affecting the result of the reordering on the
switching activity on the inputs of the computational units are: a)
The filter length and b) The symmetry of the filter. From tables 1,
2 it is obvious that as the length of the filter increases the savings
in coefficient activity increase but the penalty in data activity
increases also. In general as the length of the filter increases the
total activity required for the wavelet decomposition increases
either with or without computation reordering. In cases of
symmetrical filters ( h[n]=h[N-1-n] , N: filter length ) the savings
in coefficient activity by the application of computation reordering
are higher.
The proposed methodology does not reduce the switching activity
only in the inputs of the computational units but inside the
computational units as well. As already stated it can be safely
assumed that reduction of the activity on the inputs of a
combinational unit leads to activity reduction inside the unit as
well. In the sixth collumns of tables 1, 2 the total activity savings
on the inputs of the computational units are presented. The
switching activity savings within the computational units are
analogous to these savings. To demonstrate this fact the activity
on the outputs of the multipliers was monitored. This activity was
reduced after the computation reordering (17.5% best case, 4%
worst case).
5. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper a novel methodology for low-power implementation
of forward and inverse discrete wavelet transform was presented.
The proposed methodology derives in the first step a low power
target architecture for the algorithms. Introduction of foreground
registers as a form of memory hierarchy is proposed to allow data
reuse and reduce the background memory related power
consumption. In the second step an architectural transformation
the reordering of computation is proposed to reduce the switching
activity at the inputs and inside the computational units.
Reduction of the switching activity at the inputs and inside the
computational units is equivalent to power consumption
reduction. The reordering of computation aims at deriving an
optimal, in terms of switching activity at the inputs of the
computational units, scheduling (and assignment in specific cases)
of the partial products constituting the inner products required by
the algorithms on the computational units. Information related to
algorithm’s coefficients (which are statically determined i.e.
known before run time) is used for this reason.
The proposed methodology was applied to several 2-D 3-level
forward wavelet transforms. Experimental results prove that the
reordering of computation leads to significant activity savings in
the computational units (data paths) even when the length of the
inner product computation required by the algorithm is relatively
small. The application of the proposed methodology is feasible in
both custom and instruction set architectures.
