Abstract – The volume rendering algorithm is used for the conversion of three-dimensional data into images that are coherent for visualization, where the phase function has a pivotal position in controlling the scattering of light in the medium. This research outlines the development of phase functions from the fundamental ideas proposed by Mie's scattering theory to the most recognized Henyey-Greenstein phase function and its extensions, which are used for educational purposes by students interested in optical physics and biomedical engineering. We highlight the inherent limitations of these functions, especially when applied to complex scattering scenarios like biological tissues or non-spherical particles. Our article will further assess the existing phase functions for rendering quality using perceptual parameters like SSIM and CIEDE2000 particularly for biomedical datasets.
