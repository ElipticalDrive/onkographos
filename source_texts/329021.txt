Summary
automesh hausdorff (#761, PR #803) compares a volume mesh with a tessellation. -a, --mesh must hold hexahedra or tetrahedra, and automesh extracts its boundary surface. -b, --tessellation must hold triangles, and #801 (PR #808) lets it come from any of the six mesh formats.
This issue lets -a be a tessellation as well. The command then measures the Hausdorff distance between two triangle surfaces directly. The comparison is tessellation to tessellation, not only STL to STL: each side can be exo, inp, mesh, off, stl, or vtu.
Implementer: @hovey
Reviewer and approver: @mrbuche
Motivation
Several automesh commands turn one triangle surface into another. None of them can measure how far the result moved from its input today:
automesh remesh uniform and remesh adaptive: the remeshed surface against the input surface.
automesh smooth on a triangle mesh: the smoothed surface against the original. Laplace smoothing shrinks the surface, and Taubin smoothing is meant to avoid that. The distance would quantify both.
automesh mesh tri: the isosurface against a reference STL of the same object.
Any surface from another tool, such as a decimated or repaired scan, against the original.
Proposal
-a accepts a mesh whose blocks are all triangles, in any of the six formats, with the same triangles-only check that Hausdorff: expand triangle surface input types beyond .stl #801 applies to -b.
For a triangle input, -a is its own surface $\partial\Omega_h$. The command skips the exterior-face extraction and the quadrilateral split.
Everything downstream is unchanged:
the closed, consistently wound, and outward checks on both surfaces
pseudonormal signs
both CSV files with their # summary lines
$H_h$
The A_to_B file samples the vertices of -a that a triangle uses. This matches how B_to_A samples -b after Hausdorff: expand triangle surface input types beyond .stl #801. reference node keeps the file's node labels, or the 1-based index.
The tangled-element warning does not apply, because a triangle surface has no volume elements.
Questions for review
Flag names. --mesh reads oddly for a triangle surface. Options:
keep -a, --mesh and document that it may hold a surface
rename the pair to something neutral, such as -a, --surface-a and -b, --surface-b
keep the names and add an alias
Open surfaces. A remeshed or smoothed patch may be open, such as a scan with a hole. The current checks reject open surfaces because the sign needs an inside and an outside. Should an open pair be allowed with unsigned distances only? That would be a larger change, touching the sign column and the summary.
Mixed blocks. Should -a reject a file that mixes triangle blocks with volume blocks, as proposed? Or should it take the triangles and ignore the rest?
Tests
Identity: a tessellation against itself gives $h_h = 0$ in both directions, in every format.
Spike: hausdorff_cube.stl against hausdorff_cube_spike.stl gives $h_h(A \to B) = 0$ and $h_h(B \to A) = 0.3$, and the swap gives the reverse.
Format independence: the spike pair converted to off, exo, inp, mesh, and vtu gives identical results within 1e-12.
Remesh: automesh remesh on a unit sphere STL, compared with its input, gives a finite $H_h$ that falls as the target edge length falls.
Dependencies
Hausdorff distance - surface similarity #761 (PR Hausdorff: book pages, CLI definition, cube fixtures, and tests (part of #761) #803), which creates the subcommand.
Hausdorff: expand triangle surface input types beyond .stl #801 (PR Hausdorff: read --tessellation from every mesh format (#801) #808), whose triangles-only check and six-format reader this issue reuses for -a.
