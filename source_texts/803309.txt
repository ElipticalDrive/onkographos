💡 What:
Replaced the expensive trigonometric operations (tan, atan, cos) in hash12, hash13, hash14, and hash(float) with pure ALU-based hash functions (Dave Hoskins' method).
🎯 Why:
In GLSL, trigonometric functions execute on the Special Function Units (SFU) which are much slower than pure arithmetic (ALU). When these functions are used in pseudo-random number generators (PRNGs) inside iterative noise algorithms (like fbm3D or noised), the SFU usage skyrockets, becoming a severe bottleneck. Avoiding trig drastically speeds up these heavy math loops.
📊 Impact:
Significant reduction in shader execution time for any system relying heavily on procedural noise or hashing (e.g. water rendering, volumetric clouds, procedural terrain, or ambient occlusion).
🔬 Measurement:
Frame time reduction can be measured in heavy scenes relying on fbm3D or noised(), monitoring the shader execution time. The change can be manually verified by reviewing shaders/lib/common/hash.glsl and noting the removal of tan, atan, and cos.
