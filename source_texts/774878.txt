The texture atlas is mip_level_count: 1 with FilterMode::Nearest for mag, min and mipmap (src/renderer/mod.rs:514, 546-553), and every sample is textureSampleLevel(..., 0.0). Two consequences: distant textures alias and shimmer, and incoherent bounce rays thrash the texture cache because every one of them reads from the full-resolution level.
Two real blockers make this a feature rather than an optimisation, and they are why it has not been done.
LOD selection needs a footprint, and nothing tracks one
There are no ray differentials and no ray cones anywhere in the renderer. Any LOD chosen without them is a guess that trades sharpness for cache hits.
Ray cones are the tractable option here (Akenine-Möller et al., "Texture Level of Detail Strategies for Real-Time Ray Tracing"). Track a width and a spread angle per path: initialise the spread from the pixel's angular size at the camera, grow the width by spread * t at each hit, and add a curvature term at each bounce (zero for a flat triangle is an acceptable first approximation). Then the texel footprint gives a mip level directly. It is two extra floats of path state, which matters in a megakernel but not much.
This is also the piece that would let FilterMode::Linear be turned on, at which point #39's shader-side sRGB decode becomes wrong — decoding after filtering is not the same as filtering after decoding — and the atlas should move to the two-view arrangement that issue describes.
Mipmapping a packed atlas bleeds neighbours together
TexturePacker::pack (src/util/texture_processing.rs:61-141) is a shelf packer with no gutters: placements are adjacent. At mip level 1 each texel averages a 2x2 block, at level 4 a 16x16 block, so neighbouring textures bleed into each other progressively as the level rises. Fixing it means growing gutters between placements — ideally replicated edge texels rather than empty space, so the gutter itself filters correctly — and re-deriving the per-material albedo_offset/scale and normal_offset/scale around them.
There is also the manual fract(abs(uv)) wrap in surface_at (:648, :655), which exists because the sampler's Repeat mode would wrap across the whole atlas. That interacts with gutters and with filtering, and needs thinking through rather than leaving as-is.
What to measure
Texture aliasing is a look, not a number — a golden at 100x50 has already averaged it away. The honest measurement is a visual pair from a #[ignore]d diagnostic on a scene with a receding textured floor, plus the texture-cache side as a cargo bench -- render delta on a texture-heavy scene. There is currently no texture-heavy bench scene, so one is needed.
