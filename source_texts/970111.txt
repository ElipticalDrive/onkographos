Blocker 1 of the four MarchingCubesConfig's TODO(mesh) enumerates. Stacked on #47 — review that first.
The hazard
The arena is one grow-only buffer reused in place. The next extract overwrites the memory an in-flight draw is reading, and a grow frees it — vmaDestroyBuffer runs immediately, no fence wait, because this tier's own work is fence-blocked inside submit_single_time and never needed one. DeviceMesh::generation guards only the host download() path.
The contract
MarchingCubesConfig::slot_count gives each outstanding extract its own arena and index run.
1 (default) — exactly the old behaviour. A DeviceMesh dies at the next extract; generation enforces it.
>1 — turns on the release contract. The consumer calls release_through(generation) as its frames retire, and an extract only ever writes, grows, or frees a slot that has been released.
That's what makes the immediate destroy safe again, and it's why this needs no fence queue in the library and no semaphore across the seam. A cross-library GPU wait is the one thing the shared-queue arrangement forbids — waiting on a value the sibling hasn't signalled deadlocks against a swapchain rebuild, which drains the queue while holding the submit mutex. Reporting completion after the fact can't.
Two things I got wrong first, both found by running it
The slot must be claimed once per extract, not once per call. A call that plans its capacity low returns through ensure_output_buffers to refit against the measured count. Advancing again would strand the first dispatch's output in the previous slot. Gating on "the current slot already carries this generation" fixes it — and makes the refit's grow safe for free, since the buffer it frees is one only this extract has written.
The slots are a fixed array, not a std::vector. This class promises a self-move leaves it intact, and marching_cubes_sparse_test exercises mc = std::move(mc) directly. Every other member keeps that promise; std::vector doesn't — self-move-assignment is valid-but-unspecified, and libc++ empties it, so the extractor passed valid() and then indexed nothing. Segfaulted on the first run.
The test
Pins what the feature is for: consecutive extracts land in different buffers, a third with both slots outstanding is refused rather than corrupting a live read, releasing frees one, and a stale release never un-releases. Disabling the ring fails it; restoring passes.
It deliberately does not assert the ring reuses gen1's buffer. A slot may grow when the ring comes round, so pinning the handle would test the growth policy while claiming to test the ring — I had that assertion, it failed, and it was wrong rather than the code.
26/26 green, pre-commit clean.

Review of the ring found one behavioural bug and four places the prose had
fallen behind the code.

The refusal was not free. claim_output_slot ran inside ensure_output_buffers,
which is after ++generation_ -- so an extract refused because every slot was
still outstanding, the one path whose entire purpose is to protect a slot the
consumer is still reading, advanced the generation and made download() retire
the very mesh it had just protected. Probed before the fix: download(gen2) is
OK before the refused extract and REFUSED after, with gen2's slot untouched
either way. The claim is now its own method, called once at the top of each
extract and before the bump.

That also makes "once per extract, not once per call" structural instead of a
generation-equality trick. A call that plans its capacity low re-enters
ensure_output_buffers to refit, and the old guard leaned on the slot already
carrying this generation to avoid advancing twice; with the claim hoisted to
the extract entry point there is only ever one visit to advance from, and
ensure_output_buffers just stamps (idempotently).

release_through is not atomic and not internally synchronized, while the
consumer it is written for is on another thread -- fuse_viewer fuses on a
background thread and retires frames on the render thread, which is the exact
pattern the doc invites. Documented as a caller obligation rather than made
std::atomic: an atomic is not movable and this class's rule-of-zero defaulted
moves are load-bearing, and the consumer already holds a mutex to hand the
DeviceMesh across, so a uint64_t store under it is free. Silence was the one
option that was not safe.

download() reads the current slot unqualified, which is correct only because
the generation check and the slot claim together make "current generation" and
"current slot" the same statement. Its comment was still justifying the check
with the single-arena argument -- that a superseded view names the *same*
VkBuffer -- which a ring inverts. Restated, including that widening it to
older generations means indexing slots_ by generation rather than relaxing a
comparison.

Also: the seam-B TODO(mesh) and CLAUDE.md now record blocker 1 as settled and,
more to the point, that this takes the ring DESIGN.md specifies and
deliberately declines its timeline semaphore; the empty-mesh early return says
why it may advance the generation without claiming a slot; and kMaxSlots notes
what the unused slots cost.

Verified on MoltenVK (M5 Max): 26/26 ctest, 26/26 under ASan+UBSan,
pre-commit clean, and 100-frame room0 fuses to 793,473 vertices / 264,491
triangles -- unchanged from main. Restoring the old claim ordering fails the
new assertion; nothing else in the suite notices it.
