Summary
docs/scaling/tiling.md — the blocked-matmul worked example for the
Scaling & Distribution epic (#68).
The central point: tiling is a partition of the same index space, not a
rewrite. Because matmul.sure is uniform, its a/b/c
dependences are constant offsets (0,−1,0), (−1,0,0), (0,0,−1). Cut the
(i,j,k) cube into T×T×T blocks and the recurrence inside every block is
untouched — "blocked matmul" is a schedule of matmul.sure, not a new spec.
Every dependence a tile cut severs is therefore a halo — an O(T²) surface
exchange with the adjacent block — never a collective. Even the
C-accumulation across K-blocks, which looks like a reduction, is a ±1
pipelined hand-off. matmul tiles linearly — the best-case baseline for the
halo-vs-collective dichotomy.
The page covers index-set partitioning, the two-level (across-tile + within-tile)
schedule, matmul's self-similarity under tiling (the block grid runs the same
multiply-accumulate recurrence as the inner array), and a 4×4-as-2×2-blocks
numeric example.
The tiled animation
The schedule-animation viewer gains a data-tile overlay mode
(data-tile="T" or "Tx,Ty,Tz"):
colours each activation by its tile block (one golden-angle hue per block),
draws the tile-boundary wireframes,
keeps the firing-wavefront highlight, so you watch the τ=[1,1,1] sweep cross
block boundaries — each crossing is a halo exchange.
Embedded on the page as matmul at 15³ partitioned into a 3×3×3 grid of 27
tiles, reusing the existing matmul-linear.json (tiling is a pure viewer
overlay — no new asset). Verified headless: all 27 tile ids assigned, 0 NaN over
10 125 activations.
