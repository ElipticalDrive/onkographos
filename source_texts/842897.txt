Dear authors, thank you for open-sourcing your work and for providing such a powerful baseline.
I have a question regarding your code implementation. I noticed that you performed per-scene parameter tuning (which is totally reasonable), but I also found that your hyperparameters include an absolute-gradient threshold. Your implementation appears to follow the official AbsGS implementation as well—using abs for split but not for clone.
Therefore, I was wondering whether you could share some insights on how the results might change if densification were performed without the FastGS score and relied solely on the absolute gradient. This aspect was not explicitly discussed in the paper, and during conversations with colleagues, several people independently brought up a similar curiosity—namely, to what extent the improvements may be attributed to AbsGS versus the proposed Multi-view Consistent Densification.
By the way, I did not find the supplementary material on arXiv. I am quite curious about the per-scene PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS scores. I look forward to your response!

Hello, that's a very good question. Our supplementary materials are still being prepared. The Gaussian gradient in abs-gs is actually only used to add Gaussians to the original densification strategy, and its role is very minor. In fact, our multi-view consistency metric is used to constrain the added Gaussians, selecting those that meet the multi-view consistency criteria for densification. Abs-gs is widely applied in various GS-related works, such as PGSR, GSplat, and omniRE. So we treat it as a default setting. The ablation results of abs-gs are as:
