RealDust adds the sense that time has passed: a film on every top face, real grains and fibres
near the camera, dust bunnies along the walls, motes drifting where the light falls, and the story
you can tell with it (a finger line, the ghost of a box that was moved, footprints).

It is the second layer of a realism toolkit. [RealWorn](https://github.com/nabrahma/RealWorn)
handles materials, wear and damage. RealDust goes on top of whatever materials are there, and it
never edits your own nodes.

## Features

- **One dust mask, two consumers.** The same formula places dust in the shader and in the
  particle scatter, so specks never float on clean areas.
- **Settled dust.** A film shaded with Sheen, with the following particles near the camera:
  - grains, fibres and clumps;
  - dust bunnies on floors, along walls and under furniture.

  Coverage depends on how a surface faces, what shelters it from above, and how close it is to a
  corner.
- **Air dust.** Motes in near, mid and far layers inside a room, a selection or the camera's
  view. Optional haze.
- **Six dust types.** House, Sawdust, Plaster and Concrete, Soot and Ash, Sand and Grit, and
  Pollen. Any object can override the scene's type.
- **Age you can keyframe.** Weeks, Months, Years or Abandoned. A timelapse adds particles one by
  one, without popping.
- **Story tools.**
  - Wipe brushes: Finger, Cloth, Hand and Sleeve.
  - Leave Ghost and Bring Back: a moved object's clean outline stays in the dust.
  - Trails: footprints, bare feet, drag marks and wheels.
  - Cobwebs in the inside corners of the selection.
- **Moving dust.**
  - Falling dust from a crack, in Steady, Bursts or Triggered mode.
  - Puffs placed by hand, or from an animation (footsteps, a dropped box).
  - Swirl around moving objects.
- **Glass, screens and felt.** These get their own dust look, and window sills get extra dust.
  Optional felt depth for close-ups.
- **Clean in, clean out.** Remove Dust restores materials, UV maps, modifiers and properties
  exactly. RealWorn keeps working before, after and alongside.
- **Renders without the add-on.** Scene values reach the nodes through drivers and Attribute
  nodes, and every node group, shape and image is stored in the .blend.

## Requirements

- Blender **5.2 LTS** or later.
- For EEVEE finals, **Bake Dust Mask** (it needs a GPU or a few minutes of CPU).

## Install

1. Download `realdust-<version>.zip` from the
   [latest release](https://github.com/nabrahma/RealDust/releases/latest), or build it yourself
   with `python scripts/package.py` (it writes `dist/realdust-1.0.0.zip`).
2. In Blender: **Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions**, the drop-down at the top right,
   **Install from Disk**, and pick the zip.
3. RealDust lives in the 3D Viewport sidebar (**N**), tab **RealDust**.

## Five minutes to an abandoned room

1. **Texture the room** with RealWorn or with any materials.
2. **Select everything and click Add Dust** (Objects panel).
   - Every object gets dust on its top faces, less under shelves and tables, more in corners.
   - Within 1.5 m of the camera, real grains and fibres appear.
   - The lowest large floor is marked **Floor** and grows dust bunnies along the walls and under
     the furniture.

   Then pick an **Age** in the Scene panel: Weeks, Months, Years or Abandoned.
3. **Pick a Dust Type** if this is not a house. Placement stays the same, while colours, particle
   shapes and sizes change. An object can override the scene's type, for example a workshop bench
   with sawdust next to shelves with house dust.
4. **Click Add Air Dust** (Air panel). Choose Fit Room and the collection holding your walls,
   floor and ceiling. Motes only show where light hits them. Tick **Haze** for thicker air; it is
   slower to render.
5. **Tell the story.**
   - **Wipe** paints dust away with Blender's own texture paint. Hold **Ctrl** to bring dust back,
     and click **Done Wiping** when finished.
   - **Leave Ghost** hides the selected object and leaves its clean outline in the dust, with a
     little pile around it. **Bring Back** returns the object; the outline stays.
   - **Trail**: draw a curve on the floor with the curve draw tool (surface projection), choose
     Footprints, Bare Feet, Drag Trail or Wheels, then click **Trail...**. After editing the
     curve, click **Update Trail**.
   - **Cobwebs** finds inside corners in the selection and spans dusty webs across them.
6. **Animate** (optional).
   - **Falling Dust** from the selected edges of a ceiling crack.
   - **Puff** at the 3D cursor, or **Puffs from Animation** for footsteps or a dropped box.
   - **Swirl** around a moving object, then **Bake Air**.
   - Keyframe **Age** for a timelapse of dust settling.
7. **Render.**
   - In Cycles everything works live.
   - For EEVEE, save the file, select the dusted objects and click **Bake Dust Mask** once the
     layout is final. EEVEE then matches Cycles.

**Remove Dust** takes all of it out again.

## How it works

One mask decides where the dust goes:

```text
D = clamp(Up x Exposure x (1 + c Corner) x Breakup x A) x (1 - Wipe)   + an edge pile
```

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Up | How much the surface faces the sky: a faint trace on walls, none underneath |
| Exposure | Rays cast upward in a narrow cone; only surfaces overhead give shelter |
| Corner | Short rays around the normal, so dust gathers where surfaces meet |
| Breakup | Soft patches times a fine dust texture, so coverage is never uniform |
| A | Age x Dustiness x the object's Amount |
| Wipe | The painted wipe image plus the ghost and trail stamps |

[`realdust/builders/mask.py`](realdust/builders/mask.py) writes this formula into two node trees,
using the constants in [`realdust/core/params.py`](realdust/core/params.py):

- **`RD_Dust`**, the shader layer. It is inserted between whatever feeds a Principled BSDF and
  the Principled itself.
- **`RD_Scatter`**, the Geometry Nodes scatter.

A test keeps the two trees in agreement (correlation 0.97). Particles exist only near the camera,
and only inside the frame. Everything in the air is an analytic function of time, so you can
scrub, render on a farm and change settings freely. Only Swirl runs a simulation, and it has to be
baked.

| Feature | Cycles | EEVEE |

| Dust shader, baked mask | Full | Full, matches Cycles (correlation 0.999) |

| Motes, falling dust, puffs, swirl | Full | Full |
| Haze | Full | Full |
| Wipe, ghosts, trails, cobwebs | Full | Full |

## Performance and known limits

Measured numbers, with the machine they ran on, are in [docs/perf.md](docs/perf.md). Budgets are
never relaxed; a miss is listed with its real number.

On a GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU, in the reference attic:

- **Within budget:**
  - Add Dust on 20 objects: 1.1 s.
  - Scrubbing: 7 ms per frame.
  - A close-up: 27k instances.
  - Motes: +12% render time.
- **Over budget:**
  - The shader with a baked mask: +44% render time.
  - The shader plus particles: +65%.
  - Haze: +128%.
  - Bake Dust Mask on 20 objects: 259 s.
  - The live-mask viewport: 14 fps.

The baseline is a flat-colour test room, so these percentages are worst case. In a fully textured
scene the same dust cost is a smaller share. Ideas for closing the gaps are in
[docs/perf.md](docs/perf.md) and [docs/feedback.md](docs/feedback.md).
