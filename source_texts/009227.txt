Shadows
• Shadow is the “absence” of light
• Basic algorithm: for each point of intersection P, check if P
can see light Li
• If it can, calculate shading for Li else continue onto next light
• Shadow ray: from P in the direction towards the light
• Use ray cast to see if shadow ray hits something between P
and the light
• Fudge shadow ray a bit in its direction to avoid precision issues
Reflections and Transparency
• To give material reflectivity and transparency, consider
coefficients a, r , t
• a: Absorption (0: not absorbent, 1: fully absorbent
• r : Reflection (0: not reflective, 1: fully reflective
• t: Transparency (0: not transparent, 1: fully transparent
• Constrain a + r + t = 0
• Final color = aCa + rCr + tCt
• Ca : Color from the material itself (shading + texture mapping)
• Cr : Color reflected by the object at point of evaluation
• Ct : Color due to light passing through the object at point of
evaluation

Extensions of ray tracing: Anti-aliasing
• Spawn several rays from the camera through the pixels (at
sub-pixel levels)
• Compute the average of colors from rays
• Random vs rectilinear sampling for a pixel for rays
Extensions of ray tracing: Soft shadows
• Happens due to area lights
• Approximate area lights as several point lights (need a large
sample to look believable)
Extensions of ray tracing: Depth of field (Bokeh)
• Objects only in a narrow range are in focus, everything else
appears out of focus
• Happens due to the size of aperture in camera (or eye). For
ray tracer, it is a pinhole camera
• Algorithm:
• Choose plane of focus as the near plane.
• Replace camera from a point to a quad of small size.
• For each pixel on near plane, spawn various rays going through
it starting somewhere in camera quad. Average colors.
Speed of ray tracing
• Most time is taken by the ray-casting operation:
• Too many objects in the scene. Most rays miss most objects.
• Every object has lots of triangles. Most rays miss most
triangles.
• Adjacent rays have very similar ray-casting results. Must
leverage.
• Possible solutions:
• Bounding volumes
• Spatial subdivision data structures
• Algorithms to leverage ray coherence
Bounding Volumes
• Surround each object with a simple bounding volume
• Intersect ray with bounding volume first. If no intersection,
skip object
• Types of bounding volumes:
• Sphere (simple ray intersection, may not be good fit for
objects)
• Axis-aligned box (simple ray intersection, better fit for many
objects)
• Oriented box (complex ray intersection, even better fit for
many objects)
• Object grouping: group objects along with their bounding
volumes
• Scene graphs ↔ bounding volume hierarchies (BVH)
Space Subdivision
• Divide triangles (polygons) in space
• Ray determines order in which triangles are checked (check
near-to-far)
• Binary space partitioning
• Repeatedly divide space into two half-spaces with a dividing
plane
• Creates binary tree
• Incoming ray: check “near” side before checking “far” side for
each plane
• “Reverse” painter’s algorithm
K-d tree: special case of BSP
• Dividing planes align with coordinate planes (easier to
implement)
• Trivial to determine which “side” of plane ray is on
• Most popular variant of BSP used in ray tracing
• Efficiency depends on dividing plane selection, but most
implementations are significantly faster than no acceleration
K-d tree ray intersection
• Enclose entire scene in a box. If ray does not hit box, quit.
• If ray hits box, find (tmin , tmax ). Start at root.
• For current node c:
• If leaf, check all triangles in it and report intersection if any
• If non-leaf, recur to the “near” side, then check triangles on
plane, then recur to “far” side in that order
K-d tree ray intersection: Improvements
• Observation: call stack is used only to know where to go
climb up and go next.
• Improvement: If leaf, determine the value of t for ray with the
dividing plane. Restart at root with (t, tmax ) instead of
recursion/stack
• Improvement: At node, use new tmin to climb up and
determine next ancestor to explore
• Result: Stack-less, “smart” k-d tree traversal (this is used in
many GPU implementations
Improvements from: KD-Tree Acceleration Structures for a GPU
Raytracer, Foley and Sugerman, Proc. Graphics Hardware, pp.
15-22 (2005)
K-tree subdivision schemes
• Alternate X,Y,Z spans, choose average of span (both nodes
are equal sized)
• Simple to implement
• Performance is (relatively) average
• Alternate X,Y,Z spans, choose median of span (triangles are
equally sub-divided)
• Requires sorting of vertices for median computation
• Binary tree is balanced
• Performance may be (relatively) average or worse
K-tree subdivision schemes
• Surface area heuristic (SAH)
• Intuition: pack many triangles into small space. This creates
larger, emptier spaces
• Larger,emptier spaces = many rays can be quickly eliminated,
fewer ray-triangle intersections
• Requires additional time to compute
• Performance is (relatively) pretty good
• Finding “optimal” plane is a hard problem
