What. global_settings { subsurface { method 2 } } (or method 2 in a finish's subsurface block) sums a cloud of surface points, lit once and shared by all pixels, instead of casting sample and shadow rays under every shading point. method 1 stays the default, bit for bit unchanged. Knobs: spacing (default 1) and error_bound (default 0.1); all three reuse existing keywords. Docs: doc/html/r3_6.html; design and numbers: doc/PERF.md.
Why. Method 1 is slow, and grainy unless its sample count is high.
Results (+WT4 user-space cycles; error in levels of 255 against method 1 at 8 to 16 times the samples):

At 320x240: 1.6 and 2.9 times faster. tools/bench/sslt-open.pov (320x240): slab and clipped shell within 0.25 levels; Case 3, a block with a hollow cut by difference, rms 2.01 against method 1's 2.83; Case 4, the block as an inward-wound mesh with inside_vector, rms 1.85 against 2.32.
Visible changes. Only with method 2: wax and marble lose their grain.
Risks.
Edges, thin parts, creases, unbounded objects, open or unorientable shapes (e.g. meshes without inside_vector) and subsurface radiosity still go to method 1, as does any cube of space over 262,144 points.
Once the shared budget of about a million points is spent, which cubes fall back to method 1 depends on thread order and can vary between renders.
A few 8-pixel blocks at 800x600 are 2 to 4 levels off the reference (candle foot and drip, wax sphere); method 1's are within 1.2.
Memory: up to about 120 MB of points, plus 12 bytes per point per light.
Threads needing a cell share its building (per-cell mutex, condition variable).

Subsurface: a shared cache of irradiance point clouds
…
3618518
```
Cells of surface points per object and cube of space, each with a point
hierarchy (area-weighted irradiance, normal cone) for summing far groups
as one, built on demand under a point budget shared by all objects. Build
stages are split into jobs so that threads needing a cell while it is
built help build it; a cell that fails ends ready but unusable. The
cache also holds the view's camera, which cells space their points for,
so a cell comes out the same whichever thread builds it. Nothing uses it
yet.

Subsurface: a point-cloud method for the diffuse term
…
58f53a5
```
With method 2, a shading point sums the object's point cloud instead of
casting diffuse sample rays: a core around the exit point lit as the exit
point is (its own shadow test where nearby points disagree), a disc and a
ring averaged by the diffusion profile and scaled by its integral in
closed form (the ring's corrected across folds by how much nearer its
points are than unfolded), and farther points summed in groups. Points the sampled
method's base point sees from behind are skipped. Where the surface near
the exit point bends or runs out (edges, thin parts, open borders), on
unbounded objects, past the point budget and with subsurface radiosity,
the sampled method is used.

Points lie where lines along the axes through a jittered grid cross the
object, spaced by the diffusion length or by a pixel where that is
coarser; every crossing of a line is found by testing again past the
farthest found, since one blob test returns only the nearest interval.
Normals are turned out of the object by testing which side is inside,
since primitives ignore a difference's inversion and meshes follow their
winding; cells with many crossings that cannot be told apart, or with
over 262144 points, go to method 1, as does a cell whose build throws
(which fails instead of stalling the threads waiting on it).

Single scattering from a light in front of a flat surface, whose shadow
nearby points agree on, is taken in closed form; other lights are
sampled, with shadows from nearby points where they agree.

Method 1, the default, renders bit for bit as before.
