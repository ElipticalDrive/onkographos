Change
Three commits, in Simplex.inl and Utils.inl. No node, setting or public API changes.
1. Simplex 2D, 3D and 4D.
Cell base: the integer cell comes from part 6's FloorToInt( skewed, floored ). On AVX-512 that is one round-down conversion of the skewed coordinate, so the hash chain doesn't wait for the floor. Other feature sets convert the floored value, as before.
Vertex hashes: HashPrimes XORs the seed with the primed coordinates and multiplies. Master builds each vertex's primed coordinates with masked prime adds and hashes them from scratch. Now seed ^ xPrimed ^ yPrimed ^ ... is computed once, with one delta per axis, xPrimed ^ ( xPrimed + Primes::X ). A vertex's hash input is that base XOR the deltas of the axes it moves (picked with a select, or masked), which is the same integer. Simplex 2D on AVX-512 keeps the masked prime adds: there they are single instructions, the XORs fuse into vpternlogd, and the deltas measured 1.7% slower.
4D, one vertex at a time: vertices 1 to 3 each go through one helper (VertexGradientRampValue: offsets, falloff and gradient of one vertex), so the offsets and masks of the three vertices are not all live at once.
4D offsets: the offsets of vertices 1 to 3 are d - ( moved ? unskew + 1 : unskew ), one blend of two constants and one subtraction (OffsetFromVertex). Master subtracts 1 under a mask and then the unskew. SSE2 has no blendv and keeps master's two steps.
AVX-512 4D ranks: master counts each axis's rank with 12 masked increments, which on AVX-512 are a mask-to-vector conversion and a subtraction each, after six compares and their six inverted copies. Now the six comparisons are -1/0 integers, and each is added to one rank and subtracted from the other (rankY = 1 + ( x >= y ) - ( y >= z ) - ( y >= w ), with true = -1). The same change measured 1.02 on SSE4.1, so only AVX-512 uses it.
2. SuperSimplex 2D, 3D and 4D.
Cell base: FloorToInt as in Simplex. In 3D and 4D the move to the closest lattice vertex adds the primes to the integer base (masked add). Master increments the float base and converts it afterwards, so the conversion and the prime multiplies had to wait for the closest-vertex search. Now they run alongside it.
Vertex hashes: base XOR deltas as in Simplex, with a positive and a negative delta per axis.
Direction from a sign bit: each 3D/4D vertex goes in the + or - direction depending on a comparison such as xyzNormal < zNormal. Master compares into a mask, selects +/- primes with it and builds the offset sign from it. Now the comparison is a subtraction (xyzNormal - zNormal, or xNormal + yNormal for xNormal < -yNormal). The result is negative exactly when the comparison is true (equal values give +0), so its sign bit selects the deltas with blendv, which only reads the top bit (SelectSign), and the sign bit masked out is the offset sign (SignBit). AVX-512 keeps comparing into a k mask, which is cheaper there.
Vertex skips: the vertices at the larger lattice distance, which are in range for fewer points (the three 2-axis vertices of 8 in 3D, the four 3-axis and six 2-axis vertices of 16 in 4D), compute their falloff first and skip the hash, the gradient and the accumulation when it is 0 in every lane. Out-of-range lanes are exactly +0 after the clamp, so on SSE4.1 and AVX2 the test ORs the two native halves and checks for any set bit with one ptest, instead of a compare and a mask extraction per half (AnyInRange). 4D skips on every feature set. 3D skips below AVX-512 (kSkipOutOfRangeVertices3D): a 32-lane AVX-512 vector rarely misses a vertex in every lane, and the skips measured 0.98 to 1.01 there.
4D at full width: the 4D kernel is a template, GenSuperSimplex4D<F, I>, called at full width. Evaluating each native half separately measured slower on every feature set: the two independent halves overlap better than their spills cost. The closest-vertex search first reduces the candidates that share a score offset in independent pairs, then merges them. Max and "the first strictly greater candidate wins" are associative, so it selects the same vertex with a shorter dependency chain.
4D skew sum: x + y + z + w on every compiler again. Part 2 reordered it for MSVC 19.50 and later, which miscompiled master's 4D function once ScalePositions was inlined. The new function doesn't hit that: MSVC 19.51's SuperSimplex 4D matches master within its usual rounding (see Output).
3. AVX-512 Simplex 3D masks from masked compares (MaskedGreater and kUseMaskedGreater in Utils.inl). Four of the 3D vertex masks are an AND or AND-NOT of two comparisons. AVX-512 can compare under a mask (_mm512_mask_cmp_ps_mask), so each is now one compare that takes the other comparison as its mask. In clang-cl 23's AVX-512 Simplex 3D GenFn this removes 4 kandd and 4 knotd (k-mask instructions use the same Zen 4 pipes as FMA). The masks are the same. MSVC doesn't use it: in earlier measurements this form was 0.93 with MSVC 19.44 but 1.25-1.30 with MSVC 19.51. kUseMaskedGreater is false for MSVC, and MSVC 19.51's objects are instruction-identical with and without this commit.
Part 14 (perf-series/aarch64) turns the 3D skips off on ARM and swaps in its explicit FMA macros. Until then ARM builds take the 3D skips like SSE and AVX2, which on an Apple M1 measured 3% faster on single-octave grids but 11% slower on the MountainTerrain tree in 2D, where the branches mispredict.
Why
These kernels mostly wait on dependency chains and spills, not on instruction throughput: earlier profiling on Zen 4 had the AVX2 Simplex and Value bodies at 1.7 to 2 times their instruction-throughput bound. Each change shortens a chain or frees registers:
Hash chains: in master's SuperSimplex 3D, a vertex hash is a compare, a +/- prime select and an add per moved axis, XORs with the seed and the other axes, then the multiply, all after the closest vertex is known. Now the base and the deltas are ready before the directions are decided, and a vertex hash is one blendv per moved axis (straight from the sign bit), XORs into the base and the multiply.
Cell base: the integer cell no longer waits for the closest-vertex search (SuperSimplex) or, on AVX-512, for the floor.
Registers: stack accesses in clang-cl 23's Simplex 4D GenFn go from 379 to 246 on SSE2, 370 to 326 on SSE4.1 and 129 to 56 on AVX-512 (AVX2 276 to 280), and SuperSimplex 4D from 899 to 766 on SSE4.1 and 899 to 834 on AVX2. MSVC 19.51's AVX-512 Simplex 4D goes from 856 instructions to 732, its AVX2 SuperSimplex 4D from 2480 to 2328.
Vertex skips: on a grid the lanes of a vector are neighbours, so often none of them is in range of a given far vertex. A skipped 4D vertex saves its hash, its gradient decode and the multiply-adds of its contribution. On grids the 4D skips alone take SuperSimplex 4D to 0.73-0.79 of the time with clang-cl 23 and 0.73-0.84 with MSVC 19.51 (see Effect).
Effect
Measured on a Ryzen 9 7950X, one pinned core, nothing else running: clang-cl and MSVC under Windows, GCC and Linux clang under WSL 2 on the same machine. Both builds use FastSIMD 7aff025 and run interleaved in one process, in 3 processes with the A/B order alternating; each figure is the median of the per-process new/old time ratios (lower is faster). Two identical builds give feature-set geomeans of 0.998-1.001 and 99% of single rows within 1.5% (worst 9.1%) under Windows, and 0.999-1.005 and 2.4% under WSL, where a few single rows were 8-26% off in every process, so a lone Linux row is not evidence on its own.
