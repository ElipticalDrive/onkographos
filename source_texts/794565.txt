<h2>Structure from Motion Lab</h2>

<p>
This lab focuses on reconstructing 3D geometry from images using a Structure-from-Motion (SfM) workflow and evaluating reconstruction quality against ground truth.
</p>

<h3>What we did</h3>

<ul>
  <li>
    <b>Photogrammetry with Meshroom:</b> Reviewed core pipeline concepts (camera distortion models, texture formats, and mesh export formats) and discussed when each option is appropriate.
  </li>
  <li>
    <b>Monocular camera calibration (OpenCV):</b> Performed chessboard-based intrinsic calibration and analyzed calibration stability by restricting higher-order distortion parameters.
  </li>
  <li>
    <b>Intrinsics and reprojection error comparison:</b> Extracted Meshroom intrinsics from <code>cameras.sfm</code> across multiple dataset sizes and compared reprojection errors against the OpenCV calibration baseline.
  </li>
  <li>
    <b>Extrinsics evaluation (poses):</b> Compared reconstructed camera poses to ground truth using relative position error (scale-normalized) and orientation error (quaternion angular difference), and visualized camera trajectories in 3D.
  </li>
  <li>
    <b>Mesh-to-mesh evaluation with ICP (Open3D):</b> Sampled point clouds from reconstructed meshes and the ground-truth mesh, performed ICP registration, and reported fitness (overlap/completeness) and inlier RMSE (alignment accuracy).
  </li>
</ul>

<h3>Key outputs</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Estimated camera intrinsics and distortion coefficients (OpenCV calibration).</li>
  <li>Reprojection error comparison between OpenCV calibration and Meshroom-derived intrinsics.</li>
  <li>Relative position and orientation error statistics versus ground truth poses.</li>
  <li>3D camera trajectory plots for qualitative comparison (ground truth vs reconstruction).</li>
  <li>ICP fitness and inlier RMSE scores to quantify reconstruction fidelity.</li>
</ul>
