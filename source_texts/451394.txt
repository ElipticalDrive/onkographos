Summary
SSR ray-marches through a shared per-eye Hi-Z pyramid instead of the fixed-step linear march, behind an opt-in setting (EnableSSRHiZ, off by default). The technique follows Wu, Xu and Wang, "Stereo-consistent Screen Space Reflection" (EGSR 2024): min and max Hi-Z chains per eye built from the existing depth buffer, with the walk continuing in the other eye's chain once the max chain shows the ray has passed everything in a tile.
Shared pyramid (src/HiZPyramid.*, Common/HiZBaseCS.hlsl, HiZReduction.hlsli, SPD.hlsl): the Hi-Z pyramid moves out of Grass Optimizations into a shared class with min or max reduction and optional per-eye chains, so no level blends the two eyes' depth. Grass Optimizations builds the same max chain through it as before.
SSR Hi-Z march (ISReflectionsRayTracing.hlsl, DynamicCubemaps): walks the chains from the finest level, ascending past empty tiles and descending toward the crossing, with the same ray-versus-depth test as the linear march. In VR the walk moves to the other eye's chain when it leaves the viewport or passes behind a tile. Chains are built at the SSR draw.
Devbench diagnostics: ssrHiZAvailable, chain size and level count through openshaders.feature diagnostics.
Testing
Built as ALL-TRACY, run in game on VR (Riften) and SE with the setting on.

Cost
SE, Dragonsreach, Tracy at the 60 fps cap: no GPU or CPU zone moves, GPU-span delta +0.19 ms against a noise sd of 0.41 ms. VR, Tracy zones, Guardian Stones: about +0.06 ms of GPU in total, within noise; the SSR draw itself was about 2.5x slower in that short-ray scene, where the linear march already exits early. The expected gain on long empty-space rays is not shown yet. Measured with unoptimized (Developer Mode) shaders.
Notes
The min and per-eye chains have no consumer other than SSR.
A shader compile failure in the pyramid is cached by LazyShader until it is reset; the old grass code retried every frame.
Grass Optimizations' GPU passes are now named HiZPyramid::Base and HiZPyramid::Mips.

Screen-space reflections step a fixed-count linear march, which spends
most of its budget on empty space. Add an opt-in Hi-Z walk over the
shared min/max pyramid, VR stereo-aware: per-eye chains, with the other
eye's chain and slope taking over once the ray passes through an
occluder.

The chains and their settings are built and bound together at the SSR
draw from the live depth target. EnableSSRHiZ defaults off, and the
shader falls back to the linear march whenever the chains are
unavailable. Hit refinement is shared by both paths.

Closing: Hi-Z did not pan out for SSR here.
Speed: neutral on SE, slightly slower on VR (SSR draw 0.0255 to 0.0591 ms, about +0.07 ms/frame with the pyramid builds).
Fidelity: a RenderDoc VR capture of the Riften railings showed the walk dropping about 75% of the hits the linear march finds. A frame-exit bug caused that (fixed in 43ef4d2, pushed here). After the fix it matches linear about as well as linear matches its own 4x-step result, and more steps do not close the gap.
Stereo consistency, the reason for the change: left/right hit agreement on Riften was no better than linear (97% of linear hits already have a matching hit in the other eye at 3x3 tolerance; Hi-Z with the fix is 96%).
The linear march already has the cross-eye sample resolve and the stereo-aware fade from the same paper. Hi-Z only added the pass-through test, which did not help.
Reopen if a scene turns up where the eyes visibly disagree.
