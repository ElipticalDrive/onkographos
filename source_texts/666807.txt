Follow-up to #45: the 100 km+ side finding. Three commits:
1. The AP volume starts at the top of the atmosphere
When the camera is above the atmosphere, Hillaire's RenderCameraVolumePS starts each froxel's march where the ray enters it. He also shortens the march by the distance skipped (tMaxMax -= LengthToAtmosphere, RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl:685–701).
What we did before: every voxel marched its full camera distance starting from the entry point. Voxels still in vacuum stored haze, and the ground looked washed out, with a seam at the coverage edge.
The port: faithful, except for one deviation. A voxel that ends before the ray reaches the atmosphere stores "no haze" (0, 0, 0, 0) rather than his fully opaque (0, 0, 0, 1).

2. The raymarch fallback measures depth from the top of the atmosphere
The raymarch passed the camera-relative distance as the march length, but started the march at the atmosphere entry. Geometry above the ground seen from space got haze from beyond itself. The fix matches Hillaire's tDepth, measured from the moved start.
On a 15 km cone seen from 150–300 km, the raymarch had over-hazed by about 70%. It now agrees with the AP path to within 0.006–0.009 alpha:

… camera is above it

Port of RenderCameraVolumePS (RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl:685-701). When the
camera is above the atmosphere, the march starts where the ray enters it
(moveToTopAtmosphere, as before), and now also:
  - shortens the voxel's march by the skipped distance
    (tMaxMax = max(0, tMaxMax - LengthToAtmosphere));
  - stores no haze for voxels that end before the ray enters the atmosphere.
Before, every voxel marched its full camera distance from the entry point,
so voxels still in vacuum held haze and every slice past the entry held
roughly the whole-path value. Inside the atmosphere the skipped length is
exactly 0, so the LUT is unchanged there.

Deviation: his two early-outs return (0, 0, 0, 1), i.e. opacity 1. With
his premultiplied blend that would black out anything in front of the
atmosphere. We store (0, 0, 0, 0), as we already do for rays that miss it.

Above 100 km in 'ap' policy, mean |8-bit diff| to the per-pixel raymarch,
1280x720, before -> after:
  - 150 km, pitch -30: 16.9 -> 3.4
  - 300 km, pitch -30: 15.3 -> 3.1
  - 300 km, pitch -70: 31.3 -> 6.0
  - 400 km, pitch -20:  8.4 -> 1.8
The old LUT over-hazed: washed-out ground plus a seam at the 1024 km
coverage edge.

Two caveats, both measured:
  - Slice bands of 0.009 (150 km) to 0.014 (300 km) in alpha now show in
    'ap'. The bug had flattened each froxel ray's haze profile, which hid
    the #5 interpolation error up there. The refinement's four uniform
    steps under-sample the dense end of each descending segment: 8 steps
    give 0.009 and 16 give 0.006 at 300 km. 8x build steps change nothing.
  - With apRefineSteps: 0, 'ap' above 100 km reads worse than before
    (2.5-4.3 -> 6.5-18.7), because the correct profile exposes the
    plain interpolation error.
Default 'auto' raymarches above 100 km and is pixel-identical before and
after (150, 300, 400 km).

Verification:
  - Ground-level and sub-100 km views are pixel-identical: demos 03, 05,
    C2, C3, 22, and the planet page at 200 m and 75 km. Demo 07 is a timed
    cinematic: its run-to-run noise (max 28) exceeds the before/after diff.
  - verify-haze-shadows passes. The only flag is 2 px in the inspector
    overlay row, as on main.
  - ref:verify passes. Sections 1-4 are byte-identical; only the
    path-tracer table moves, by its own noise.
  - Parity pages pass.
  - Cost: not measurable. Frame time at 300 km 'ap' and at ground level is
    within burst noise either way.

…atmosphere

From above the atmosphere, the haze raymarch starts at the atmosphere top
but marched the camera-relative distance, so it ran past the surface by
the vacuum it had skipped. Ground pixels hid this because the march stops
at the ground sphere; anything standing above the ground got the air
behind it.

Hillaire's RenderRayMarchingPS moves WorldPos to the atmosphere top (:377),
and IntegrateScatteredLuminance measures the depth-buffer distance from
that moved position (tDepth, :67). The march length is now the
camera-relative distance minus the skipped length.

That exposes depth-buffer precision. The old overshoot always ended at the
ground sphere, so it never depended on it. Bruneton's space views in
ref:verify (2,700 and 12,000 km, standard depth) read 11-12 % less haze on
the ground with the plain subtraction (probes 0.964 -> 0.844 and
1.013 -> 0.903). So above the atmosphere, a surface within 0.2 % of the
ground sphere's distance is taken to be the ground. That restores those
views exactly, and is only 0.6 km at 300 km. Inside the atmosphere the
skipped length is exactly 0 and the snap is off, so nothing changes below
100 km.

Measured on a 15 km cone (demo 05, hooked page), mean alpha over the
cone's pixels:

  camera               raymarch before   raymarch after   AP path (LUT + refinement)
  150 km, pitch -30        0.248             0.148             0.141
  300 km, pitch -30        0.253             0.150             0.144
  300 km, pitch -70        0.151             0.086             0.077

The raymarch over-hazed the cone by ~70 %. It now agrees with the
independent AP estimate within 0.006-0.009. In 'auto' above 100 km only
the cone's pixels change (0.2-1.1 % of the frame); the ground is
unchanged, including a straight-down view from 12,000 km.

Ground level, and 'auto' and raymarch at 10 and 75 km, are pixel-identical.
Cost: within burst noise (5.59 -> 5.70 ms median at 300 km, 1920x1080).

The AP slice refinement marches the last stretch of each pixel's ray. Seen
from above, the dense air sits at the far end of that stretch, by the
surface. Its steps now follow a new 'quadraticEnd' distribution on the TSL
integrator: segment ends at (1 - (1 - s/N)^2) * tMax, the mirror image of
Hillaire's quadratic spacing. Same step count, same cost. It stays
continuous across slices, because the two estimates are still blended by
slice phase.

Slice-locked error, peak-to-peak in AP alpha against the per-pixel
raymarch, binned by slice phase, at the default 4 steps:

  altitude   equal steps   end-packed
  10 km        0.0025        0.0025
  25 km        0.0026        0.0024
  50 km        0.0030        0.0025
  75 km        0.0051        0.0044
  150 km       0.0090        0.0076   ('ap' policy)
  300 km       0.0140        0.0109   ('ap' policy)

This does not make the 150/300 km bands go away at 4 steps. They are not
just step spacing:
  - end-packed 8 steps gives 0.0046 / 0.0069; 12 steps gives
    0.0036 / 0.0054;
  - 8x LUT build steps change nothing;
  - anchoring the refinement one slice further back made it worse.
8 steps costs +0.5 ms at 300 km (1920x1080), so the default stays 4. Above
100 km the default 'auto' policy raymarches and never reads the volume; the
haze guide says how to trade cost for those rings with apRefineSteps.

Verification:
  - Ground level is pixel-identical: demos 03, 05, C2, C3, 22, and the
    hooked planet page at 200 m in 'auto' and 'ap'.
  - verify-haze-shadows passes, apart from 9 px in the inspector overlay
    row (as on main).
  - ref:verify passes. Sections 1-4 match the previous run except one
    view-5 ground probe, 0.968 -> 0.967 (its camera is above 1 km, where
    the refinement runs).
  - Parity pages pass.
  - Cost: unchanged (1.76 vs 1.78 ms median at 300 km 'ap'; 1.28 vs
    1.31 ms at 25 km).
