Abstract:
The ray-driven projection and back-projection methods, frequently represented as calculating the path of line integration through a pixel or voxel space, are widely applied in various imaging research fields such as positron emission tomography (PET), computerized tomography (CT) and other volumetric ray tracing studies. For high resolution iterative image reconstruction with large amount of sample events, computing the projections event-by-event is an extremely time consuming task. This work presents a novel ray-tracing calculation technique applying to a list-mode EM image reconstruction algorithm as a part of the system model to accelerate its projection operations. Compared to both the conventional Siddon algorithm and its accelerated methods, the new algorithm has less average computation operation time on each voxel. The results show that the new algorithm significantly improves the calculation speed up to twice as fast than the incremental Siddon algorithm.

I. Introduction
IN computerized tomography and other volume imaging application studies, there exist a large amount of calculations related to inter-transforming between the image domain and the projection domain through a straight line. Such operations are called ray-driven forward-projection and back-projection operations. An example is the iterative list-mode 3D poisson emission tomography (PET) image reconstruction algorithm [2] using these operations as a part of the system model. In this algorithm and other many applications, the forward projection can be written as:f_{n}=\sum\limits_{i}\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{k}l_{n}(i, j, k)\rho(i, j, k)\eqno\hbox{(1)}
where is the intersection length between a ray and the pixel or voxel array, and is the density weights of each pixel or voxel of the array. The value presents the sum of the density weights though the radiological path of the ray. The back-projection is connected with its transpose operation: every back-projection value is distributed into the array though the path.
