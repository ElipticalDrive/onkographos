Abstract
In this paper we propose SPaGS, a high-quality, real-time free-viewpoint rendering approach from 360-degree panoramic images. While existing methods building on Neural Radiance Fields or 3D Gaussian Splatting have difficulties to achieve real-time frame rates and high-quality results at the same time, SPaGS combines the advantages of an explicit 3D Gaussian-based scene representation and ray casting-based rendering to attain fast and accurate results. Central to our new approach is the exact calculation of axis-aligned bounding boxes for spherical images that significantly accelerates omnidirectional ray casting of 3D Gaussians. We also present a new dataset consisting of ten real-world scenes recorded with a drone that incorporates both calibrated 360-degree panoramic images as well as perspective images captured simultaneously, i.e., with the same flight trajectory. Our evaluation on this new dataset as well as established benchmarks demonstrates that SPaGS excels over state-of-the-art methods in terms of both rendering quality and speed.
1. Introduction
360° free-view synthesis promises immersive visual experiences by rendering complete surroundings from arbitrary viewpoints for applications like virtual reality, telepresence, and robotic navigation. In contrast to an image taken with a normal perspective camera, a 360° panoramic image contains more complete information about the environment due to its omnidirectional visual perception, which leads to better 3D scene reconstruction in terms of completeness. Using full 360° imagery instead of perspective crops preserves global scene context and avoids rectification using perspective reprojection, enabling more accurate and efficient panoramic reconstruction. At the same time, it creates new challenges for high-quality novel view synthesis from native panoramas in real-time.

There are already several works that try to address this challenge. Recent free-viewpoint approaches from panoramic images built on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [MST*20] have demonstrated impressive quality, but lack the capability to render images in real time. For example, 360Roam [HCZY22] incorporates geometry-aware sampling for faster convergence, and PanoGRF [CCG*23] leverages learned scene priors to improve novel views. EgoNeRF [CKK23] uses a balanced spherical grid, which effectively avoids unnecessary sampling by discretizing space in latitude-longitude form and achieves significant speed-ups and quality gains for egocentric scenes. Overall, current NeRF-based methods are fundamentally limited by their volumetric ray-marching. Accumulating densities and colors along each ray via a neural network remains computationally expensive, resulting in slow training and inference, making it unsuitable for interactive and other real-time applications.
A recent paradigm shift in novel view synthesis addresses these challenges by replacing heavy neural ray sampling with explicit 3D point-based representations. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [KKLD23] popularized this approach, representing the scene as a set of 3D points with anisotropic Gaussian extent (3D Gaussian splats) as primitives instead. To render an image, 3D Gaussians are projected onto the image plane using a local affine approximation [ZPvBG01] to acquire 2D Gaussians for subsequent rasterization. The final color of each pixel is calculated by alpha blending all contributions. As this process is fully differentiable, it enables fast gradient descent-based optimization, which significantly reduces training and rendering times while still retaining photorealism. However, the local affine approximation fails to accurately account for perspective distortion, which leads to artifacts when rendering from certain viewpoints [HFW*25]. The original 3DGS method also supports only undistorted camera models, and introducing a spherical camera model poses additional challenges. Due to the spherical representation of the image plane, the projection of 3D Gaussian splats onto the equirectangular image plane is no longer trivial. Some concurrent works have addressed this problem [LCHL24, BHG*24] through intermediate projection and layout priors. However, they are either limited to specific scenarios or achieve suboptimal rendering quality. OmniGS [LHYC24] introduced a fully analytical solution that directly projects Gaussian splats onto the equirectangular image plane and completely avoids intermediate projections, thereby achieving state-of-the-art rendering quality and speed. Nevertheless, it is still based on the local affine approximation, which prevents accurate rendering [HBG*24] and leads to projection artifacts (see Fig. 2). Furthermore, the available 360° panoramic datasets for novel view synthesis hinder the development of panoramic view-based free-viewpoint rendering. Current datasets are limited in various ways: OmniPhotos [BYLR20] provides 360° panoramic images from real-world scenes. OmniBlender and Ricoh360 datasets from EgoNeRF [CKK23] contain both real-world and synthetic scenes that allow for a more complete evaluation. Nevertheless, these datasets are predominantly egocentric, characterized by a limited baseline between cameras. 360Roam [HCZY22] provides several real-world scenes with roaming camera trajectory, which offers larger camera movement within the scenes. However, it is exclusively comprised of indoor scenes.

In this paper, we present SPaGS, a novel fast and accurate free-viewpoint rendering method using 360° panoramic images. The key insight behind SPaGS is to use ray-based pixel color calculation and bounding box-based rasterization, exploiting accurate ray casting-based rendering while significantly accelerating rendering.
We push the boundary of the real-time photorealistic 3D scene reconstruction from 360° panoramic images further with the following three key contributions:
We propose a novel spherical panoramic view based free-viewpoint rendering approach using omnidirectional ray-splat intersection, thereby facilitating precise 3D scene reconstruction with 360° panoramic images.
Furthermore, we employ a novel tangent plane-based splat-bounding-box projection from camera space onto the equirect-angular image plane, with the objective of achieving substantial acceleration of the rasterization process. In addition, we conducted a mathematical and geometrical analysis to ascertain its validity.
We construct the first dataset for novel view synthesis using drone, named Roaming and Rounding (RaR), encompassing ten scenes that collectively comprise perspective images and 360° panoramic images.
In addition, the efficacy of our approach is evaluated by comparing it to other state-of-the-art methods on established benchmarks and the constructed dataset. Furthermore, the impacts of using images captured with different camera types are analyzed.
2. Related Work
2.1. 3D Gaussian Splatting Methods
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for novel view synthesis. Kerbl et al. (2023) introduced 3DGS as an explicit radiance field representation using 3D Gaussian primitives. They achieve state-of-the-art visual quality and real-time 1080p rendering [KKLD23]. Teir approach avoids expensive volumetric sampling and achieves interactive framerates without sacrificing quality by optimizing anisotropic covariance and using a fast, visibility-aware splatting renderer. Subsequent improvements have focused on rendering correctness and robustness. Hahlbohm et al. (2024) addressed perspective distortions and transparency artifacts by proposing a fully perspective-correct Gaussian splatting pipeline [HFW*25]. Their method evaluates splats at the true perpixel contribution point, eliminating matrix inversions, and blends them with a hybrid transparency scheme. This yields up to 2 times faster rendering and optimization with fewer popping artifacts compared to the original 3DGS.
Other works extend 3DGS to non-perspective imagery, e.g. omnidirectional Gaussian splatting systems that project Gaussians directly onto spherical screens. Adapting 3DGS to panoramic domains is non-trivial: native perspective splatting causes severe distortions. OmniGS by Li et al. employs a GPU rasterizer that splats Gaussians onto an equirectangular panorama without cube-maps or tangent-plane approximations [LHYC24]. This enables end-to-end optimization from 360° inputs and achieves high-quality, real-time reconstruction of full surround scenes. Concurrent works such as 360-GS [BHG*24] enable 3D scene reconstruction with supplementary layout information for indoor roaming scenes. Also, OmniGS [LHYC24] reconstructs 3D scenes from 360° images using a direct mathematical derivation. OmniGS employs a local affine approximation to project 3D Gaussians onto the image plane and still has superior speed and rendering quality compared to prior NeRF-based methodologies [BMV*22,CXG*22,HCZY22,CKK23], and better extensibility than 360-GS [BHG*24]. DroneSplat introduces a pipeline for drone-recorded scenes that integrates local-global segmentation to mask out moving objects and augments 3D Gaussian Splatting with multi-view stereo and voxel-guided refinement [TGY*25]. This enables reconstructions from limited drone viewpoints, filters dynamic distractors and yields superior results on real-world datasets compared to baseline NeRF and 3DGS approaches. EVER replaces the rasterized splats with exact volumetric ellipsoids to eliminate the popping artifacts and view-dependent density issues of standard 3DGS [MHK*24]. Each Gaussian is ray-traced as a volume, adding effects like depth-of-field blur and fisheye lens distortion at real-time rates. It achieves the sharpest reconstructions on large-scale scenes (e.g. Zip-NeRF) among current real-time methods. 3DGUT retains the efficiency of 3DGS but applies an Unscented Transform to each Gaussian to handle exact projection under arbitrary camera models [WMEM*25]. This makes it compatible with distorted panoramic inputs (e.g. 360° fisheye or rolling-shutter cameras) and also permits secondary ray effects like reflections and refractions within the splatting framework. 3D Gaussian Ray Tracing pushes towards full ray tracing and builds a GPU-accelerated BVH for Gaussian splats [MLMP*24] and uses special ray–triangle intersections and depth-sorted shading to render complex, semi-transparent scenes with minimal speed penalty. Opposed to raster splatting, this approach supports advanced lighting (shadows, global reflections) and extreme camera distortions (panoramas). Approaches like ODGS define local tangent planes for each Gaussian or directly incorporate spherical camera models into the splatting equations [LCHL24], but reach only suboptimal rendering quality and speed. Despite this progress, challenges remain for 360° images. The standard 3DGS projection uses a first-order affine approximation because Gaussians are not closed under true projective transforms. This leads to residual errors and noticeable rendering artifacts in wide-FoV views. To address this, Huang et al. (2024) and Bai et al. (2024) present an in-depth error analysis and an optimal projection strategy for 3DGS [HBG*24, BHG*24]. Bai adapts per-splat projection rules to different camera views to minimize distortion. Huang shows that projecting each Gaussian along the ray from its mean to the camera center onto a tangent plane reduces blurring and cloud artifacts. Their method involves minimal code changes and retains real-time performance while consistently improving image quality across various camera models.
2.2. NeRF-Based Methods for 360° Panoramic Imaging
Mip-NeRF 360 (Barron et al., 2022) [BMV*22] introduced a distortion-aware regularizer and a multi-scale mipmap ray-sampling method to address aliasing and the extreme depth ranges of 360° scenes. This approach significantly improved NeRF's accuracy on full panorama images, reduces error by 57% and produces realistic detail, but requires long training times. 360Roam builds an omnidirectional NeRF (360NeRF) from sparse 360° images and then integrates a geometry-aware occupancy grid to skip empty space during rendering [HCZY22]. It progressively refines a 3D occupancy map, splits the scene into sub-fields and accelerates ray marching with rendering speeds orders of magnitude faster than standard NeRF.
EgoNeRF employs a balanced spherical grid to model large-scale egocentric 360° scenes [CKK23]. It avoids pole singularities and handles unbounded scene extent by replacing the uniform cartesian voxel grid with a quasi-uniform angular grid, formed by two combined sphere tessellations, and an exponentially scaled radial depth partition. This design aligns with head-mounted 360° video input and yields state-of-the-art novel-view synthesis quality on synthetic and real egocentric 360° data. The core contribution adapts the NeRF framework to 360° egocentric imagery via a space-efficient, view-aligned coordinate system, adding a small multi-layer perceptron for color regression. A key limitation is that EgoNeRF assumes the camera follows a quasi-stationary, egocentric motion pattern and thus cannot directly handle freely roaming panoramas [LHYC24]. It does not support real-time rendering with only a few frames per second because of the volume integration through the feature grid.
Instant-NGP introduced a substantial improvement in NeRF acceleration by replacing heavy MLP computations with a multi-resolution hash-table encoding of the scene [MESK22]. Its core idea is to use a sparse trainable feature grid and a small neural network that reduces memory access and computation per ray sample. It also uses custom fused CUDA kernels yielding orders-of-magnitude speedups. Instant-NGP enables real-time novel-view rendering and quick scene reconstruction, raising the bar a in efficiency. However, the method assumes standard pinhole camera inputs and does not explicitly account for the distortions and extremely wide fov panoramic cameras. In very large or full panoramas, the fixed-resolution hash grid can lead to aliasing or blur, as it lacks a mechanism for scale-dependent detail (addressed by Zip-NeRF). Moreover, alhtough being memory-efficient, it represents the scene implicitly via a neural network.
Zip-NeRF is an anti-aliased grid-based NeRF that combines the efficient hashed feature grids with the multi-scale rendering of mip-NeRF 360. Each ray is sampled as a conical frustum like in mip-NeRF 360. It mitigates aliasing artifacts such as jagged edges or missing thin structures in wide fov scenes with this technique, together with a grid-based backbone like Instant-NGP [BMV*23]. This hybrid approach significantly improves quality and leads to a 8%–77% lower error than either mip-NeRF 360 or Instant-NGP separately and accelerates training by around 24 times compared to mip-NeRF 360. Zip-NeRF shows that also unbounded 360° scenes can be reproduced with high quality and fast convergence using an implicit grid representation with anti-aliasing. Its strengths are image quality and training speed, but it still assumes a perspective-camera and relies on a small neural network at render time. Therefore, applying it to 360° panorama images would require converting the 360° photos to multi-view perspective batches. Also, although the the inference speed is faster than standard NeRF, is capped to the neural sampling.
TensoRF represents a different trade-off, focusing on memory efficiency and fast convergence through tensor factorization [CXG*22]. It models the radiance field as a 3D grid of features with an additional feature dimension, that is factorized into compact low-rank components. By applying CP decomposition and a novel vector-matrix (VM) decomposition, TensoRF heavily compresses the scene representation. It reduces space complexity from O(n3) to O(n) or O(n2) for each dimension. This leads to small model sizes of a few megabytes and only a minor quality loss. TensoRF can exceed NeRF's visual quality while reconstructing the scene in under 10 minutes of training. its strength is in delivering high qulity with a lightweight representation that is attractive for memory-constrained scenarios. On the other hand, the factorized volume still requires combining multiple components per ray during rendering making it computationally costly. Its inference speed is improved over standard NeRF but does not achieve real-time speed. Additionally, like other grit or voxel methods, it assumes perspective images, so for using 360° panoramas, the tensor needs to be sampled with distortion or the data converted into many planar views.
These NeRF-based panoramic methods show high reconstruction quality and can capture fine details in all directions, but normally use large neural networks like multi-MLP radiance fields or dense feature grids and brute-force volume rendering, making them memory-intensive and slow at runtime.
2.3. Other Relevant Works
360° Scene Reconstruction. Other approaches outside radiance fields have worked on panoramic scene reconstruction. Zhang et al. (2024) present a method that combines panoramic imagery with additional sensing (gyroscope, satellite navigation, LiDAR) to improve 3D reconstruction completeness [ZHH*24]. Other recent works combines LiDAR scans with 360° images using Gaussian splat-ting to generate dense point clouds of driving scenes, outperforming NeRF-based pipelines in accuracy and speed [JGCZ25]. PatchMatch Stereo Panorama takes a purely image-based approach with adapting MVS algorithms to equirectangular videos [STS22]. Their system extends a feature-based SLAM pipeline with a parallel PatchMatch stereo module that uses 360° frames, producing real-time dense depth maps for UAV-based exploration. They explicitly support the equirectangular camera model and achieve higher completeness than pinhole MVS on panoramas. These works highlight the importance of handling spherical distortion in traditional reconstruction.
Datasets for Panoramic Understanding. Progress in 360° vision was achieved using annotated datasets. Sun et al. (2021) introduced a large-scale collection of indoor panoramas with semantic labels (Aerial-PASS), for benchmarking omnidirectional scene understanding. Similarly, Lyu et al. (2020) released a 360° dataset incorporating diverse environments for panoramic depth estimation and segmentation [LVX*20]. These datasets provide real-world 360° footage, useful for training and evaluating methods like SPaGS to ensure generalization across a variety of scenes.
Spherical Camera Models and Panoramic View Synthesis. Modeling the optics of 360° cameras is crucial for methods like ours. Sun et al.'s (2017) foundational work on unified spherical camera models extended two-view geometry to omnidirectional cameras [SLY17]. They treat a panorama as a projection onto a sphere, enabling accurate calibration and reasoning about epipolar geometry in 360° images. Prior view synthesis approaches for panoramas also inform our work. Chen et al. (2023) proposed a panorama novel-view interpolation (PNVI) method that uses multi-sphere image representations to synthesize new 360° views [CCG*23], and Choi et al. (2023) developed an egocentric view synthesis system (EgoNeRF) using a balanced spherical grid to overcome distortions at the poles of the panorama.
These methods achieve compelling results but often require complex network architectures or multiple stages (depth estimation, flow warping, etc.) to handle spherical data.
3. Method
Recently, Hahlbohm et al. [HFW*25] introduced an efficient and numerically stable approach for ray-splat intersection to replace the local affine approximation-based rasterization used by traditional 3DGS methods, which enables perspectively and geometrically accurate rendering. This approach, in conjunction with hybrid transparency-based blending, achieves better rendering quality and faster rendering speed compared to previous methods [KKLD23,RSP*24,HYC*24,HBG*24, YSG24]. However, it currently only supports undistorted perspective camera models. This limitation significantly restricts the potential for further development and application of the method.
In this work, we apply the fundamental approaches of HTGS and extend it to a spherical camera model. All preliminaries are clarified in Sec. 3.1. We introduce our omnidirectional Ray-Splat Intersection approach in Sec. 3.2. In Sec. 3.3 we present a detailed mathematical and geometrical analysis on spherical projection and the calculation of a 2D bounding box for each splat on an equirectangular image plane for significant acceleration of the rendering process.

4. Dataset
As stated in the introduction (Sec. 1), the evaluation on available datasets remains incomplete. To address these limitations, we propose our novel drone-perspective dataset, designated as Roaming and Rounding (RaR), that incorporates both roaming and object-centered real-world scenes. These scenes were captured simultaneously using 360° panoramic and perspective camera systems.
4.1. System Setup
In consideration of the prevailing conventions of the applications in this field and the convenience, a continuous video is recorded by each spherical and perspective camera for every scene, rather than individual images. Therefore, we use a comprehensive video capture system (see Fig. 4) that includes the capacity to record 360° panoramic videos and perspective videos simultaneously during a single flight. The hardware components of our video capture system consist of a DJI Mavic Air 2 drone with integrated perspective camera, and an Insta360 Sphere camera, specifically developed for the DJI Mavic Air 2 drone model. For the spherical camera, the recording resolution is set to 3840 × 1920 with a frame rate of 50 fps. For the perspective camera, the recording resolution is set to 3840 × 2160 with a frame rate of 60 fps. Moreover, the auto-exposure of both cameras has been deactivated to ensure consistent camera parameters throughout the video recording, thereby facilitating the acquisition of a unified illumination condition for the entire scene. Due to the varying light conditions across scenes, we intentionally employed different yet constant camera exposure settings to ensure satisfactory video capture for each scene.

4.2. Data Processing
For 360° panoramic videos, we use the original pre-processing software from the camera manufacturer Insta360 to obtain the final videos from RAW data. Considering the low flight velocity and the frame rate of the video, one frame per second is extracted from the original video of each camera. Furthermore, the majority of current 3D Gaussian Splatting-based works for 3D scene reconstruction necessitate a Structure from Motion (SfM) reconstruction to supplement the images. Consequently, we perform SfM reconstruction for both types of camera on all scenes. For perspective cameras, we employ the conventional SfM pipeline Colmap [SF16,SZPF16]. However, it should be noted that Colmap does not officially support the spherical camera model. Therefore, we have chosen to utilize OpenSfM [map25] for the spherical camera, as it natively supports omnidirectional imagery and is better optimized for drone perspective images and large-scale reconstructions.
4.3. RaR Dataset
Our dataset contains a total of ten scenes, including five roaming scenes denoted as alley, avenue, bridge, bypath, garden and five object-centered scenes (rounding) denoted as car, lion, statuary, stone, windmill. Each scene comes with 360° panoramic images and perspective images. Our dataset encompasses a broad range of scales and styles of environments and objects, ensuring a diverse range of visual content. Each scene includes between 86 and 546 images. Furthermore, in rounding scenes, the perspective camera focuses consistently on the central object, while in roaming scenes, the perspective camera maintains its focus along the flight trajectory. Our RaR dataset provides both panoramic and perspective images. However, our method focuses on the reconstruction with panoramic images.
5. Evaluation
We evaluate SPaGS on existing benchmark datasets and our new RaR dataset to confirm the effectiveness of our method.
5.1. Implementation and Setup
We implement our approach in PyTorch inside the NeRFICG framework [KHS25] and implement the rendering routines inside custom CUDA kernels as an extension to the framework of Hahlbohm et al. [HFW*25] Furthermore, we also adapt the 3D filter from Mip-Splatting [YCH*24] to work with the spherical camera model under the consideration of varying sampling frequencies at different latitudes (θ) to achieve a better anti-aliasing effect. We provide an overview of our hyperparameter settings in the supplementary material.
We evaluate our method on the two datasets by Choi et al. [CKK23] (OmniBlender and Ricoh360) as well as on our new RaR dataset. OmniBlender contains four synthetic indoor scenes and seven synthetic outdoor scenes whereas Ricoh360 consists of eleven real-world scenes. For both, we use the original resolution, which is 2000 × 1000 and 1920 × 960 for OmniBlender and Ricoh360 respectively and use the provided train/test splits. For our new RaR dataset, we evaluate all methods at a resolution of 1920 × 960 and use the established 7:1 train/test split.
As baselines for our comparisons, we use the recent NeRF-based methods Instant-NGP [MESK22], TensoRF [CXG*22], ZipNeRF [BMV*23], and EgoNeRF [CKK23] as well as the 3D Gaussian Splatting-based methods OmniGS [LHYC24] and ODGS [LCHL24]. To evaluate image quality, we use PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS [ZIE*18]. All experiments were conducted on a single Nvidia RTX 4090 GPU.
5.2. Results
Quantitative Comparisons. The quantitative results are shown in Tabs. 1 and 2. On the real-world dataset Ricoh360, our SPaGS outperforms all other methods in both rendering quality and speed. On the synthesis dataset OmniBlender, our approach achieves significantly faster speeds compared to the second fastest method INGP [MESK22] for training and OmniGS [LHYC24] for rendering, while maintaining similar quantitative metrics. Furthermore, the evaluation on our custom RaR dataset also confirms the superiority of our SPaGS method. In terms of quality metrics, the PSNR and SSIM of our method are slightly lower than those of the state-of-the-art 3DGS-based OmniGS approach. However, our method requires fewer splats than employed by OmniGS, making it more than 2.5× faster during both training and rendering. In summary, the evaluations on the datasets verify that our approach SPaGS exhibits superior performance in both rendering quality and speed.
Qualitative Comparisons. We perform the qualitative comparison across both NeRF-based methods and 3DGS-based methods on all datasets. The results are shown in Fig. 5. On the EgoNeRF [CKK23] datasets OmniBlender and Ricoh360, our SPaGS is able to reproduce fine details well e.g., grass while some artifacts to be observed in some scenes by the OmniGS [LHYC24], and other methods have issues in rendering sharp edges. On our custom RaR dataset, our approach could provide comparable rendering quality in comparison to the state-of-the-art 3DGS-based method OmniGS [LHYC24] while requiring much fewer splats.

Using Panoramic vs. Perspective Images. We perform an experiemnt on our RaR dataset to analyze the difference of using a perspective camera and a spherical camera. The quantitative and qualitative results in Tab. 3 and Fig. 6 show that in areas with insufficient coverage, the reconstruction with the panoramic images achieves higher quality and contains less artifacts than the reconstruction with perspective images. We further observe that the reconstruction from perspective images works much better with the object-centric capture style than with the roaming capture style. On the contrary, the capture style has a much lower impact on the quality of reconstructions when using the panoramic images.

5.3. Discussion
Limitations. Despite our method achieving state-of-the-art rendering quality and speed, it has some limitations. Notwithstanding the fact that the adapted 3D filter substantially improves anti-aliasing, it is still not as effective as the screen-space low-pass filter in EWA splatting [ZPvBG01], which is not applicable in our method. It leads to a suboptimal representation for high-frequency regions, such as densely paved brick roads, that could be reconstructed more coarsely than smoother surfaces. It could also be the reason why our SPaGS gets slightly lower PSNR in some scenes compared to other approaches, as mentioned by HTGS [HFW*25]. Moreover, the current axes-aligned bounding boxes on the equirectangular image plane are not optimal. There is scope for further minimization that could further accelerate the rendering speed. Furthermore, due to the suboptimal original pre-processing software from the camera manufacturer Insta360, we have noticed some stitching artifacts from the original video as shown in Fig. 7, which could affect the image quality of our custom dataset.

Future Work. To further improve rendering of high-frequency details, future work could develop a specialized anti-aliasing strategy for rendering panoramic images. Our experiments also suggest that a more robust approach for densification is likely to improve the overall reconstruction quality of our approach. Regarding rendering speed, future work could further analyze the spherical projection of 3D Gaussians onto the equirectangular image plane or use non-axis-aligned bounding boxes to reduce the number of Gaussian evaluations during blending. Lastly, we argue that future work towards joint optimization with images taken by multiple cameras – each with a different camera model – is important because it simplifies the capturing and 3D reconstruction process for large scenes.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced a novel approach SPaGS that achieves fast and accurate real-time 3D scene reconstruction with native 360° panoramic images based on 3D Gaussian splatting. In comparison to other state-of-the-art methods in this field, our SPaGS employs an omnidirectional ray-splat intersection mechanism that avoids local affine approximation to achieve accurate rendering. Furthermore, we perform a tangent-plane based axes-aligned bounding-box calculation on the equirectangular image plane to significantly improve the rasterization, and apply a mathematical and geometrical analysis to verify its validity. Thereby, we achieve state-of-the-art performance in both rendering quality and speed. Concurrently, we propose the first dataset for novel view synthesis with both perspective and 360° panoramic images that are captured simultaneously. Our new dataset provides a more extensive camera baseline and a wider range of scenes, thereby providing a more comprehensive and challenging evaluation opportunity. The evaluation on our novel dataset also verifies the performance of our method.
