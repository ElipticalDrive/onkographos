SSAO/SAO can be viewed as an equivalent to AOmaps and can be used to dim the indirect diffuse. In order for this to work, we need to compute the SSAO pass before the beauty render and then pass in the SSAO map to each material so the material shaders can access it and dim their "indirect diffuse" contributions.
This should greatly increase realism without the need to precompute aoMaps on a per mesh basis -- which is just a lot of prep work.

It wouldn't be an AO UVMap, rather you would figure out where the final pixel is on screen and get it directly from the SS AO. It is basically the same quality, hemispheric visibility ratio per pixel, just that an SSAO/SAO is in screenspace, rather than UV-mapped. It would require us to compute a normalized screen UV coordinate to get it from the SSAO map. But it would look nearly as good as aoMaps without the effort.

you'd need a zprepass for this approach to work in a forward renderer, you need the depth to calculate ssao. So you'll have to bear the cost of submitting geometry twice but on the other hand you'll gain from minimizing overdraw in the final render + proper ssao which indeed should only be applied to ambient for accuracy. I personally don't think that ssao looks as good as prebaked ao though. it tends to be unstable under motion especially at low sample counts.
