Clouds currently look like rotated faceted solids; repeated flat silhouettes also fail to hold up when banking or flying above them. This change renders cumulus clouds from stable 3D density, with irregular outlines, brighter sunlit surfaces, and darker interiors. Cloud shape stays fixed in the world through camera changes and close passes.
Closes #12. Issue #66 remains excluded.
The implementation shares four original, CPU-generated density variants in a 450 KiB 3D texture. Sixty-three instanced bounds use one cloud draw call. Each ray integrates at most 24 samples and stops once opaque; occupied samples estimate directional self-shadowing. Existing sunlight, ambient light, exposure, airport offsets, and departure corridor placement remain connected. Sorting occurs before instance-buffer upload. Cloud haze accounts for the elevated sightline without changing terrain/water materials.
Validation:
Syntax/action checks, all 247 Node tests, and production build pass.
Chrome/Intel ANGLE Vulkan at an actual 1280×720 viewport: eight matched baseline/candidate views (overview, near, banked, overhead, above, inside, sunset, night), plus simulator cockpit, wing, and chase screenshots. No captured browser exceptions or console errors.
GPU timer queries measure actual rendering separately from CPU submission. The background simulator is paused during isolated comparisons; disjoint or missing results cannot silently become a pass.

The volume renderer costs roughly 5–7ms more GPU work than the flat baseline on this device. This is a visual-quality tradeoff; no mobile or full-simulator FPS gain is claimed. Shapes remain stylized, close views can show sampling grain, and overlapping bounds use approximate center sorting. Opaque geometry intersecting cloud interiors is not integrated with a scene-depth buffer.
Reproduction, provenance, and limitations: cloud rendering notes. Raw GPU samples and all screenshots: review artifacts.
