Although Volume Rendering has gained reputation as&lt;br&gt;a powerful tool for visualizing complex structural and&lt;br&gt;stratigraphic features embedded in 3-D seismic data,&lt;br&gt;the complexity of the parameters involved still requires&lt;br&gt;a lot of work in order to produce an informative&lt;br&gt;image. One of such parameters assigns opacity&lt;br&gt;levels to data-amplitude values to highlight the features&lt;br&gt;of interest. The design of the opacity function&lt;br&gt;usually follows a non-intuitive trial-an-error approach.&lt;br&gt;The strong dependence of the rendered image&lt;br&gt;on other optical parameters also contributes to make&lt;br&gt;it a time-consuming task. Furthermore, some particularities&lt;br&gt;of seismic data contribute to make such approach&lt;br&gt;more suitable for the visualization of highamplitude&lt;br&gt;anomalies. This work describes a method&lt;br&gt;for generating two-valued (amplitude and gradient)&lt;br&gt;opacity functions that is able to better discriminate&lt;br&gt;mid-amplitude events without obscuring them with&lt;br&gt;other seismic features. Preliminary results using synthetic&lt;br&gt;data are presented. Examples using real datasets&lt;br&gt;will be presented at the conference.
