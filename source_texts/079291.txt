Differentiable rendering focusing on estimating derivative of forward-rendering result, which capable of differentiating with respect to a wide range of scene parameters including material properties and object geometries. In this thesis, we explore different types of variance in differentiable rendering: variance in gradient estimates in differentiable rendering and inverse rendering, and differentiating rendering variance.First, we introduce an efficient technique for estimating boundary integrals in path-space differentiable rendering, which significantly reduces estimation variance, enabling robust inverse rendering with complex geometry and lighting.Second, we introduce an image-space adaptive sampling technique that accelerates inverse rendering by dynamically adjusting pixel sampling probabilities based on gradient variance during inverse rendering.In the end, we establish a framework for differentiating rendering variance with respect to both scene parameters and sampling probabilities. While traditional differentiable rendering differentiates pixel values, we differentiate their variance. This introduces the concept of Variance-aware inverse rendering, which alters a virtual scene and/or an estimator in an optimal way to offer balance between rendering bias and variance
