What this is
docs/ARBITRARY-SHAPES.md Phase 2 landed the traversal in PR #6 and left it unused: the mesh chunk could find a triangle, and nothing called it. Both renderers still asked raySphereIntersect what the light lands on. This routes them onto the surface they were given.
The gate is unchanged and holds throughout: the sphere path stays byte-identical — bench --scenarios 12 --seed 1234 at 5,563,347 characters, env and per-scenario timings removed.
The blend had to cross first, and that made it a texture
A mesh's blend weight is not a closed form on the angle from a limb. It is Phase 3's per-vertex geodesic field, which footprintDistanceAt interpolates across the face a hit landed on — and a shader cannot follow a Float64Array any more than it can follow the hierarchy.
So pack.ts gains a third texture: three texels per triangle, one per corner, four channels, because a framebuffer holds four viewports (PARAMETERS.md §3.4) and both shaders declare MAX_PROJ = 4. Per corner rather than per vertex, which is the trade the positions already make — a dependent fetch in the blend would sit in the same innermost loop the traversal is trying to keep prefetchable. A fifth field is refused rather than dropped into a channel that does not exist.
packBvh(bvh, mesh, fields) is measured against footprintDistanceAt at six barycentric points on every seventh face: worst departure 3.46e-8 m, which is float32 on a 0.86 m sphere.
That field belongs to the COMPOSITOR, which took a second look to see
buildDisplayUniforms already states the rule and follows it for three uniforms:
The blend and mask are the COMPOSITOR's: the weights belong to the calibration the content was generated against, not to where the light physically landed. Reading them off physical would be a subtle and very convincing bug — the picture would still look like a sphere.
uRampShape, uWidthDeg and uRampGamma come off content.blend for that reason. On a mesh the ramp needs two more terms — the per-corner field and the blend width as an arc — and both came from whichever rig was handed to packMesh, which is the physical one. The shader ramped on where the light really goes while coverageAndWeights ramped on where the compositor thought it went. Code stating a rule and then not doing it, the same shape as the placeholder-texture finding below.
This is not a second field texture, which is where it was heading. There is exactly one blend in the display shader and it is the compositor's: the compositor computes the weights and bakes them into the pixels it sends, a projector emits those pixels, and where they land is physics. rampWeight is called twice, both inside contentWeight; bvhFieldAt is called once, feeding it; meshIlluminated uses no field at all. A physical field would be sampled by nothing, stripped by the linker, and the page's own guard would refuse to start — exactly how uLimb went, two sections down. So the repair is to attribute the one field correctly.

How much it mattered. A footprint is computed from where a lens actually is, so displacing the compositor moves which vertices fall inside one. blendWidthM is attribution rather than a changed number: rigs.ts takes the blend from the panel for both rigs, so the two agree today. It is now right by construction instead of by coincidence.
The geometry stays unprefixed, and that is a statement rather than an economy. Both rigs trace ONE hierarchy, because a misregistration is a disagreement about where the lenses are and not about what shape is standing in the room. uMeshShadowBias sits with it, being a property of the surface. buildDisplayUniforms now refuses two rigs on two surfaces — there is no uCBvhNodes to carry a second shape, so they would silently share whichever got packed and the reader would be shown a substitution dressed as a misregistration.
One question, one place
surfaceIntersect is each shader's Surface.intersect: sphere or mesh, returning (t, triangle, u, v) with the triangle negative where there are no faces. The sphere keeps raySphereIntersect with the same arguments in the same order, which is why this is a routing change rather than an arithmetic one, and why the bench did not move.
sampleSurface (harness) and shadeTwoRig (app) take that hit rather than a bare point, for the reason SurfaceLocation exists on the CPU: a mesh's normal and content coordinate belong to the face that was struck, and re-finding it from the point alone is a search a flat wall defeats completely. Four decisions now follow the face — the normal, the content coordinate, whether each projector reaches it, and the weight it gets — and every one of them was a sphere closed form until this phase.
A model takes no rotation offset and no polar mask: its UV is anchored by its own unwrap, and it has neither a pole nor a mount to occlude one. The same two exceptions render.ts and warp.ts already make.
The room's own occlusion follows: shadeFloor's ray asked whether the sphere was in the way and now asks the surface. A model that shadows the floor is exactly what a sphere could never do.
The app: the second intersection, and the picker
shadeTwoRig sends each pixel back out through the calibration the compositor believed and asks what should have been there. That second ray now hits the same model — a misregistration is a disagreement about where the lenses are, not about what shape is standing in the room.
pickMarker occludes against the model too. Its own note already said it must answer the question the shader would answer, and the shader stopped answering "a sphere" here; without it a viewer clicks through a building and selects a projector they cannot see. It asks the surface — the simulator's float64 traversal over the hierarchy these texels were packed from — rather than growing a third traversal to match float32 exactly. That difference is nanometres and a click is pixels; a different shape is the thing that changes an answer.
The traversal is written out twice, deliberately. That is the choice every other chunk in the app's shader already makes — the optics, the blend and the transfer curve are all there a second time. Two shaders each claiming to implement one model are worth more than one shader used twice: a transcription error shows up as a parity disagreement, and a shared bug shows up as nothing at all. What they share is pack.ts, which is a byte order rather than an answer.
A bug carried across from PR #6's last round
Both shaders computed the radiometric reference distance as uLimb[i].x - uRadius — a lens distance from the world origin minus a bound about the origin, which is one statement only for a body the origin sits inside. That is the same error the fifth review round found on the CPU. It is now uRefDistance[i], re-derived in harness/src/uniforms.ts from the model's centre and extent exactly as prepareProjector does, and read straight off the prepared projector in the app.
uLimb is now gone from the app's shader, not merely unused, and smoke:app is what found that: GLSL strips an unread uniform, and the page's own guard refuses to start, saying a declared uniform the linker did not expose is "a term of the model that has stopped being applied". The compositor keeps uCLimb — its blend is still the closed form on the angle from its own limb — and glsl.test.ts pins the asymmetry rather than leaving a hole in its rig-pairing rule.
The BVH stack-depth guard, which a review read as off by two
packMesh refuses a hierarchy at maxDepth >= 32 because the shader's traversal stack is a compile-time int stack[32]. A review read packages/sim's new Int32Array(bvh.maxDepth + 2) as the traversal needing maxDepth + 2 slots, which would make a 31-deep hierarchy overflow and render with holes.
Measured rather than argued. Peak stack occupancy is exactly maxDepth + 1: popping a node at depth d leaves at most one deferred sibling per ancestor level, and the deepest node that pushes anything is internal and therefore at maxDepth - 1. Checked over every binary tree shape up to ten leaves (6918 of them), over balanced trees to 20 levels, and over the hierarchies buildBvh produces for the fixtures — nothing exceeds maxDepth + 1, and a balanced tree reaches it exactly. The CPU's + 2 is one spare slot, not a requirement, and the threshold stands.
What was missing is that nothing held the three statements of this one number together — the shader's #define, the TypeScript constant, and the property of the loop. Any one could be edited alone, and since every push is guarded by sp < BVH_STACK the failure would be a silently dropped subtree: a hole in the model, no crash. Two tests now pin them, the bound asserted as an equality so a traversal that stopped pushing would fail it too.

The blend-attribution tests follow the same discipline. The one asserting the field moves with the rig checks the difference is sentinel-sized rather than within a tolerance, so a preset where the two rigs happened to agree fails the test instead of quietly making it vacuous; and the uniform test asserts bvhFieldAt actually fetches uCBvhField, because declaring the uniform while still fetching a physical one would pass a names-only check.
Every new assertion is mutation-checked.
What is NOT in this PR
The page still hands the display shader no model, and it should not until parity.ts says what its verdict means on one.
An earlier draft of this description named a constant LIT_BOUNDARY_FRACTION here. No such constant exists — the real one is BOUNDARY_PIXEL_ALLOWANCE in packages/harness/src/parity.ts, and correcting the name turned out to matter, because reading the actual gate showed the concern is real for a different reason than the one stated.
judge() passes a track only when both p999 <= tolerance and fractionOverTolerance <= BOUNDARY_PIXEL_ALLOWANCE (1%). Those two are not independent: if more than 0.1% of samples are over tolerance then the 99.9th-percentile sample is one of them, so p999 > tolerance necessarily. percentileOk therefore implies boundaryOk, the conjunction reduces to percentileOk alone, and the 1% allowance documented as tolerating geometric boundaries actually tolerates 0.101%. On a sphere the boundary population sits under that and it never showed.
A mesh does not. Measured on the branch that adds the harness Surface control (which stacks on this one), against SwiftShader: a tessellated sphere puts 0.72% of room samples and 0.87% of projector samples over tolerance — inside the 1% allowance, past what the percentile actually enforces — and the page reports DISAGREE. The disagreeing samples are isolated single pixels scattered through the interior at facet edges, not a region and not the silhouette, while the same shader text's JS mirror agrees with packages/sim to 3.8e-6 on the identical grid.
So the verdict logic needs re-deriving before the live view gets a model, and that is a decision about what the project's headline gate number means rather than a change to make in passing. It belongs with the commit that makes the switch, not here.

Copilot, on both binders, and correct in both.

`uploadMesh` skips work when the model it is asked for is the one already
uploaded -- which is what keeps a megabyte hierarchy off the per-frame path. The
bug is what the initial value of that record has to be. `null` is a legitimate
model: no model, the 1x1 placeholders. So starting the record at `null` made the
very first call -- which every page makes, with no model -- return immediately,
and the three textures were created and never given storage. A sampler bound to
an incomplete texture is undefined behaviour on some drivers rather than an
unused uniform, which is the exact thing `uploadMesh`'s own doc comment claimed
to be avoiding, two lines above the code that did not do it.

`undefined` now means "nothing has been put there yet" and `null` means "the
placeholders are". Three states rather than two, and the guard needs no special
case: `undefined === null` is false, so the initial value carries it.

**Nothing could have caught this but a reader.** `smoke:app` runs the real page
in a real browser and came up green: software rendering tolerates an incomplete
texture the shader never samples, and the shader never samples these -- uMeshMode
is 0 and bvhIntersect returns before it fetches. The failure is a driver's
prerogative, on hardware this container does not have.

Pinned over the source text in both test suites rather than by calling
`uploadMesh`, and that is a limitation rather than a preference: `web/gl.ts`
needs DOM types, the root tsconfig withholds them from `packages/sim` on purpose,
and a test that imported the binder would drag `WebGL2RenderingContext` into the
config that keeps the simulator free of the browser. Mutation-checked -- putting
`null` back fails both.

Copilot's second pass on this PR returned "Needs a closer look" rather than
findings -- driver-sensitive GPU paths, strong tests, review effort Lite. The
closer look found seven correctness problems, all in the gap between "954 tests
pass" and "the three renderers agree". Every one is verified against the
`packages/sim` counterpart the changed code declares it mirrors.

**Two places kept the very expression this PR was written to remove.**
`reference.ts`'s `shadeSurface` still computed the radiometric reference as
`p.limb[0] - u.radius` -- a lens distance from the world ORIGIN minus a bound
about the origin -- while the GLSL `shadeSurface` it mirrors had moved to
`uRefDistance[i]`. On a model standing away from the origin the two halves of
link (3) disagreed about brightness, and the harness parity number would have
called that a shader fault. `compareShading` cannot see it: it stops at
`sampleSurface` and never calls `shadeSurface`.

And `sim/src/render.ts`'s own `shadeFloor` was still on
`p.distanceM - rig.radiusM`. That is the site PR #6's fifth round missed while
claiming the class was closed, so the CPU's floor glow and the GPU's disagreed
on a translated model.

**A GL texture unit leaked, reintroducing the bug `bindContent` documents.**
`uploadMesh` bound units 1-3 and left unit 3 selected; `ensureReadTarget` and
`ensureVideoTarget` both call `bindTexture` with no `activeTexture`, which is
unit 0 when the invariant holds and was unit 3 after an upload. The read-back
target landed on `uBvhField`, and the identity guard meant the field texture was
never re-bound -- so the blend would sample the framebuffer's own colour
attachment for the life of the model. Binding and uploading were one function and
that was the mistake: the binding is per FRAME, the upload is per MODEL. Split,
in both binders, and the active unit is restored.

Pin the BVH stack-depth guard, which a review read as off by two
…
66d40e8
```
`packMesh` refuses a hierarchy at `maxDepth >= 32` because the shader's
traversal stack is a compile-time `int stack[32]`. A review read
`packages/sim`'s `new Int32Array(bvh.maxDepth + 2)` as the traversal NEEDING
`maxDepth + 2` slots, which would make a 31-deep hierarchy overflow and render
with holes, and asked for the threshold to be tightened.

Measured instead of argued, because the argument is subtle enough to go either
way twice. The traversal's peak stack occupancy is exactly `maxDepth + 1`:
popping a node at depth d leaves at most one deferred sibling per ancestor
level, so at most d entries sit below it, and the deepest node that pushes
anything is internal and therefore at `maxDepth - 1`. Checked over every binary
tree shape up to ten leaves (6918 of them), over balanced trees to 20 levels,
and over the hierarchies the real builder produces for the fixtures: nothing
exceeds `maxDepth + 1`, and a balanced tree reaches it exactly.

So the CPU's `+ 2` is an allocation with one spare slot, not a requirement, and
tightening the guard on that reading would cost a legitimate model its last
level of depth. The threshold stands.

What was actually missing is that nothing held the three statements of this one
number together: `#define BVH_STACK 32` in shader text, `BVH_STACK_DEPTH` in
TypeScript, and the property of the loop that relates them. Any one could be
edited alone and the failure would be a hole in a model rather than a crash,
because every push is guarded. Two tests now pin them:

  - the bound itself, asserted as an equality on balanced trees rather than an
    inequality, so a traversal that quietly stopped pushing would fail it;
  - the three-way agreement, including that every push but the root seed is
    guarded by `sp < BVH_STACK`.

`BVH_STACK_DEPTH` is exported for the second one, and carries the derivation in
its doc comment so the next reader does not have to redo it.

Mutation-checked, four ways: moving the threshold to 30, moving the shader's
`#define` to 64, dropping one `sp < BVH_STACK` guard, and making the model
traversal stop pushing the near child each fail a test, and only the intended
one.

```
buildDisplayUniforms already states the rule: "the blend and mask are the
COMPOSITOR's -- the weights belong to the calibration the content was generated
against, not to where the light physically landed", and it reads uRampShape,
uWidthDeg and uRampGamma off content.blend for exactly that reason.

On a mesh the ramp needs two more terms, and both came from whichever rig was
handed to packMesh -- the physical one. The shader then ramped on where the
light really goes while coverageAndWeights ramped on where the compositor
thought it went. Another instance of code stating a rule and then not doing it,
which is the same shape as the placeholder-texture finding earlier in this PR.

NOT a second field texture, which is where this was heading. There is exactly
one blend in the display shader and it is the compositor's: the compositor
computes the weights and bakes them into the pixels it sends, a projector emits
those pixels, and where they land is physics. rampWeight is called twice, both
inside contentWeight; bvhFieldAt is called once, feeding it. A physical field
would be sampled by nothing, stripped by the linker, and the page's own guard
would then refuse to start -- which is exactly how uLimb went two commits ago.
So the repair is to attribute the one field correctly, not to add a second.

  uBvhField        -> uCBvhField
  uMeshHasField    -> uCMeshHasField
  uMeshBlendWidthM -> uCMeshBlendWidthM
  DisplayMesh.field/fieldWidth/blendWidthM -> contentField/contentFieldWidth/
                                              contentBlendWidthM
  packMesh(rig) -> packMesh(content), the parameter named for the rig it needs

The geometry stays unprefixed and that is a statement rather than an economy:
both rigs trace ONE hierarchy, because a misregistration is a disagreement about
where the lenses are and not about what shape is standing in the room.
uMeshShadowBias sits with it, being a property of the surface. buildDisplayUniforms
now refuses two rigs on two surfaces -- there is no uCBvhNodes to carry a second
shape, so they would silently share whichever got packed and the reader would be
shown a substitution dressed as a misregistration.

How much this mattered: a footprint is computed from where a lens actually is, so
displacing the compositor moves which vertices fall inside a footprint. Measured
on the wall fixture under BOULDER_PRESET, the two rigs' fields differ by 8.5e5 --
footprint.ts's `unreachable` sentinel, not drift. Vertices inside one rig's
footprint are unreachable in the other's, so the shader was ramping smoothly
across faces where the compositor drew a hard seam. Both pictures look like a
blend.

blendWidthM is attribution rather than a changed number: rigs.ts takes the blend
from the panel for both rigs, so the two currently agree. It is now right by
construction instead of by coincidence.

Three tests, each mutation-checked -- restoring the physical fetch, disabling the
surface guard, and making the two rigs' fields identical each fail one, and only
the intended one:

  - the mesh blend uniforms are the compositor's, the geometry ones are neither,
    and bvhFieldAt actually fetches uCBvhField (declaring the uniform while still
    fetching a physical one would pass a names-only check);
  - the field really does move with the rig, asserted at sentinel size rather
    than at a tolerance, so a preset where the two rigs happened to agree fails
    the test instead of silently making it vacuous;
  - two rigs on two surfaces are refused, and a sphere pair stays free.
