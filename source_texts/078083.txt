Before: ground, trees and placed pieces were drawn as cubes, and the far view was blocky columns with box-shaped forests.
After: nothing in the natural world has a cube silhouette. Ground rolls smoothly, tree crowns and trunks are rounded, placed pieces get soft rounded surfaces, and the far view is smooth hills with round crowns and conifer spires. Building still snaps to the voxel grid. Lanterns and grass blades stay as they were.
How: mesh.rs now meshes every solid voxel with surface nets over a lightly blurred occupancy field. The blur is clamped so single voxels never disappear. Smooth normals are packed into the vertex (octahedral) and decoded in world.wgsl, and grass fades to dirt on steep slopes. far.rs builds a smooth height field per tile, with trees as surfaces of revolution. Meshing costs about 0.27 ms per chunk in the release bench, which fits the frame budget.
