ABSTRACT
Point clouds are an essential representation of 3D scenes and objects,
but their growing data volume poses significant challenges for storage and transmission, highlighting the need for efficient and practical
compression techniques. We propose a novel framework—Lossless
Dynamically Grouped Point Cloud Geometry Compression (LDGPCGC). First, the k Nearest Neighbor (kNN)-based Transformer
Occupancy Probability Estimator (KTOPE) jointly models spatial
dependencies and predicts voxel occupancy, enhancing representation capacity. Then, the Probability-based Dynamic 8-stage Lossless Coder (PDELC) further enhances compression efficiency by
employing a dynamic grouping strategy to achieve more accurate
voxel occupancy probability estimation, thereby reducing bit consumption. In the lossless compression setting, our method achieves
a 48.4% compression ratio (CR) improvement over the MPEG standard GPCC and a 7.7% gain compared with state-of-the-art learningbased approaches, demonstrating superior efficiency and spatial context modeling.
Index Terms— Point cloud, Geometry compression, Lossless
coding, Dynamic grouping
1. INTRODUCTION
Point clouds are a fundamental representation of 3D objects and are
widely used in applications such as Augmented Reality (AR) or Virtual Reality (VR), autonomous driving, and digital cultural heritage.
Their ability to realistically model 3D content has attracted increasing attention from both academia and industry [1]. However, the
rapidly growing volume of point cloud data poses significant challenges for storage and transmission, underscoring the need for efficient compression to reduce bitrate, optimize storage, and ease bandwidth requirements. In particular, lossless compression is crucial
for industrial and safety-critical applications, such as archiving highprecision 3D building information models in digital twin systems [2]
and maintaining accurate 3D maps for autonomous robots.
*Corresponding author
Accurate occupancy probability estimation plays a key role in
minimizing bit consumption in lossless point cloud compression.
While rule-based standards such as MPEG G-PCC [1, 3] offer practical solutions, their rigid design limits compression efficiency. Deep
learning-based methods [4, 5] have significantly outperformed traditional techniques, yet challenges remain in balancing efficiency,
flexibility, and complexity.
Voxel-based representations are widely adopted for geometry
compression due to their regular 3D grid structure, analogous to
pixels in 2D images. Early works like PCGCv1 [6] rely on uniform grids, while VoxelDNN [7] adopts autoregressive modeling for
occupancy prediction. MSVoxelDNN [8] extends this by introducing multiscale grouping for parallel encoding. PCGCv2 [9] reduces
overhead by replacing dense convolutions with sparse convolutions
[10], and SparsePCGC [11] further improves prediction accuracy
through multi-stage cross-scale context modeling combined with an
advanced grouping strategy. In addition, methods such as ConPCAC
[12] and PCGFormer [13] incorporate Transformer-based context
modeling on top of sparse convolutions, further enhancing compression performance.
However, most existing methods still rely on fixed grouping and
convolutional features, which struggle to capture voxel sparsity and
irregularity, especially in early stages. This limits context modeling
capacity and occupancy prediction accuracy, ultimately constraining
compression performance.
To address these challenges, we propose a lossless compression
approach that enhances spatial dependency modeling and improves
coding efficiency.

• We propose a unified and efficient lossless point cloud compression framework that explicitly tackles the challenge of

knowledge, this is the first framework that integrates kNNbased attention modeling with dynamic probability-driven
coding for lossless compression.

2. THE PROPOSED METHOD
As illustrated in Fig. 1, we propose a framework termed Lossless
Dynamically Grouped Point Cloud Geometry Compression (LDGPCGC). The framework follows the paradigm of multi-scale tensorbased point cloud compression, where decoded voxels from a lower
scale provide informative context for occupancy probability estima-
tion at the current scale, thereby enhancing the accuracy of probability prediction and ultimately improving compression efficiency.
At each scale, the framework performs dyadic voxel refinement
(2 × 2 × 2 ↑ ), where each parent Positively-Occupied Voxel (POV)
from the previous scale is subdivided into eight child Probable Positively Occupied Voxel (PPOV). This hierarchical refinement not
only enriches multi-scale feature representations but also enables the
framework to naturally adapt to different resolutions. At each scale,
the encoder compresses the occupancy state of every PPOV, while
the decoder reconstructs POV from the upsampled PPOV by interpreting the compressed syntax.
To ensure cross-scale consistency, both encoder and decoder
share a unified context model that consists of the Feature Aggregation Module (FAM), Voxel Upsampling Layer (VUL), the kNNbased Transformer Occupancy Probability Estimator (KTOPE), and
the Probability-based Dynamic 8-stage Lossless Coder (PDELC). In
this process, decoded POV from the preceding scale serve as conditional input to the context model, which estimates the occupancy
probability pP P OV for each PPOV in the upsampled tensor. These
probabilities are subsequently utilized for arithmetic encoding and
decoding.
SparsePCGC [11] adopts a uniform eight-stage processing strategy and achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in lossless geometry compression. However, its fixed spatial partitioning fails to
fully exploit the occupancy probability distribution of voxels, and its
convolutional operations rely on kernels with fixed receptive fields,
lacking adaptability. As a result, inter-group contextual information
remains limited, prediction errors increase, and coding costs rise.
To address these issues, we propose LDG-PCGC, which incorporates two key components: first, the KTOPE, which captures both
local and global contextual dependencies in sparse point clouds to
achieve precise occupancy probability estimation; and second, the
PDELC, which leverages probability-guided dynamic grouping to
strengthen context modeling and improve coding efficiency. By integrating these modules, LDG-PCGC effectively exploits cross-scale
dependencies, achieving efficient and accurate reconstruction while
ensuring lossless compression.

2.1. kNN-based Transformer Occupancy Probability Estimator
(KTOPE)
Convolution-based methods with fixed kernels have limited receptive fields and struggle to capture sparse and irregular point cloud
structures. To address this, we propose the kNN-based Transformer
Occupancy Probability Estimator (KTOPE), which integrates the
kNN-based Transformer Feature Aggregation Module (KTFAM)
and the Occupancy Probability Output Layer (OPOL). KTFAM
constructs local neighborhoods via kNN search and applies selfattention to aggregate both local and non-local dependencies (as
shown in Fig. 2), while OPOL employs sparse convolutions with
ReLU and a Sigmoid layer to predict voxel occupancy probabilities.

more accurate occupancy probability estimation for sparse and irregular structures. As illustrated in Fig. 3a, the detailed workflow of

We propose the Probability-based Dynamic 8-stage Lossless Coder
(PDELC), which leverages probability-driven dynamic grouping to
improve context modeling. By accurately estimating the occupancy
probabilities of Probable Positively Occupied Voxels (PPOVs),
PDELC reduces the bit consumption in arithmetic encoding. The
overall process of PDELC (as shown in Fig. 5) is as follows:
(1) Probable Voxel Generation: The Positively Occupied Voxels (POV) decoded from the previous scale i are first processed
through the pre-trained FAM, VUL, and KTOPE modules to generate the Probable Positively Occupied Voxels (PPOV) for the current
scale.
(2) Dynamic Grouping by Probability: The predicted occupancy probabilities of the PPOV are then used to dynamically
partition the voxels into multiple groups. In the subsequent stages,
these groups are processed sequentially, with higher-confidence voxels prioritized during encoding.
(3) Stage-wise Feature Aggregation:
In Stages 1–4, we
employ KTOPE to effectively capture voxel sparsity patterns and
achieve more accurate occupancy probability estimation. In Stages
5–8, as the voxel distribution becomes denser, we switch to the
Occupancy Probability Estimator (OPE), which does not involve the
kNN-Transformer but is enhanced with FAM (as shown in Fig. 3b),
to improve both coding and decoding efficiency. The occupancy
probability of a voxel is denoted as pP P OV , which guides the
arithmetic encoding (or decoding) of voxel occupancy states.

and the compression ratio (CR) gain relative to the standardized

In terms of runtime, LDG-PCGC achieves encoding and decoding times of 1.49s and 1.37s, respectively. While not the absolute
fastest, it remains in the top-performing tier of efficiency, striking
a favorable balance between speed and effectiveness and ultimately
delivering the best overall compression performance.
Finally, we conduct an ablation study (Table 2) to quantify the
contributions of each proposed module. Incorporating KTOPE alone
reduces the bitrate from 0.56 to 0.55 bpp, while adding PDELC
yields 0.53 bpp. Combining both modules achieves the best performance at 0.52 bpp, validating that KTOPE and PDELC are complementary in improving occupancy probability estimation and coding
efficiency.
3.1. Dataset
Two datasets are selected for training: the 8i Voxelized Full Bodies
(8iVFB) [16] and the Microsoft Voxelized Upper Bodies (MVUB).
Their fixed quantization precision establishes them as standard
benchmarks for fair lossless compression performance evaluation.

4. CONCLUSION
This paper presented LDG-PCGC, a unified and efficient lossless point cloud geometry compression framework. The proposed
method explicitly addresses voxel sparsity and irregularity by integrating the kNN-based Transformer Occupancy Probability Estimator (KTOPE) and the Probability-based Dynamic 8-stage Lossless
Coder (PDELC). KTOPE captures long-range voxel correlations to
improve occupancy probability estimation, while PDELC leverages
probability-driven dynamic grouping to enhance coding efficiency.
Experiments demonstrate that LDG-PCGC delivers state-of-theart compression performance while maintaining competitive runtime
efficiency. These results highlight the novelty and effectiveness of
probability-driven dynamic context modeling in lossless point cloud
compression.
In future work, we plan to further optimize the encoding and
decoding speed to improve computational efficiency, and extend the
framework to variable-rate and lossy compression scenarios, thereby
enhancing its flexibility and applicability in real-world 3D applications.
