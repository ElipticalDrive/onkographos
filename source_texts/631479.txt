Objects hidden behind a body are drawn anyway: a vessel on the far side of a planet, a moon behind its planet, scatters below the horizon. Only frustum and sub-pixel culling run (Culler). The depth test already discards their pixels, so the cost is vertex work and draw calls, which grows with scatters (#46) and vessels with many parts.
Bodies are spheres, so the test is exact and cheap on the CPU: an object whose bounding sphere lies entirely inside the cone a body's sphere hides from the camera is culled. #42 culls terrain tiles below the horizon; this covers every other object.
LightOcclusion already intersects rays from the camera with body spheres for lens effects; the two may share geometry.
Open questions
Occlusion by anything other than a body's sphere (vessels, terrain relief, non-spherical bodies from Non-spherical bodies #187): GPU occlusion culling against a hierarchical depth buffer, worth it only with GPU-driven rendering and once profiling shows the need.

None: engine work, independent of any pack.
