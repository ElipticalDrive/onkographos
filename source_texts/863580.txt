What
Meow2/Math/Packing.h provides the vertex packing the renderer (M5/M7) will use. Each format has a tested error bound:
half floats: toHalf / fromHalf, using the F16C instructions from [M3] SIMD: AVX2 baseline, CPU check at startup, batch helpers #106;
normalised integers: UNORM/SNORM 8 and 16 bit, decoded exactly like the GPU does;
octahedral normals: 4 bytes;
tangent frames as one quaternion: normal + tangent + handedness in 8 bytes;
positions quantised to 16 bits inside the mesh bounds: 6 bytes.
How it works
Halves use DirectXMath's XMConvertFloatToHalf / XMConvertHalfToFloat. With F16C (our AVX2 build), those are single instructions.
Normalised integers: lround(clamp(x) × 255 / 65535 / 127 / 32767) to pack, v / max to unpack. SNORM decodes with max(v / 127, −1), the D3D12/Vulkan rule.
Octahedral:
packing projects the normal onto the octahedron |x|+|y|+|z| = 1, folds the lower half (z < 0) into the corners of the square, and stores x, y as SNORM16;
unpacking reverses the fold and normalises.
QTangent:
the tangent is re-orthogonalised against the normal;
the rows (tangent, normal × tangent, normal) form a rotation matrix, which is converted to a quaternion with w ≥ 0;
w is kept at least one SNORM16 step above 0;
the whole quaternion is negated for handedness −1;
on unpacking, the sign of w is the handedness, and the axes are the rotated +X (tangent) and +Z (normal).
Positions: (p − min) / size as UNORM16 per axis. A zero-size axis (a flat floor) stores 0 and decodes to its single value.
Why it's done this way
Concepts
Why pack vertices:
A GPU often waits on memory, not arithmetic. A 48-byte vertex that becomes 20 bytes means 2.4× fewer bytes fetched per vertex and smaller vertex caches misses.
The hardware decodes R16G16_SNORM, R16G16B16A16_UNORM, R16_FLOAT etc. for free during the fetch, so packing costs nothing at draw time.
Normalised integers: an integer interpreted as a fraction of its maximum. UNORM covers 0..1, SNORM −1..1. 8-bit SNORM has −128 and −127, both meaning −1; that keeps 0 exactly representable and the range symmetric. We never write −128, and we decode it like the GPU.
Octahedral mapping: storing x, y and rebuilding z = √(1 − x² − y²) loses z's sign and is imprecise near the equator. Projecting the sphere onto an octahedron, then unfolding it flat, spreads the codes almost uniformly. With 2 × 16 bits the worst error is below 0.0001 rad; the test checks 10k random normals.
QTangent: the tangent frame (tangent, bitangent, normal) is a rotation plus a mirror flag. A rotation fits in a quaternion (4 numbers instead of 9), and since q ≡ −q, the sign is free to carry the mirror flag. The catch is that when w is exactly 0 the sign would vanish, so w is clamped to at least one step. Crytek introduced this for skinned meshes, where tangent frames must also be interpolated and rotated cheaply.
Position quantisation: inside a mesh's own bounds, 16 bits per axis give 65,536 steps. For a 2 m character that's 0.03 mm, far below a pixel. The bounds are applied back in the vertex shader or folded into the world matrix.
Decisions
Exact GPU decode rules, so CPU tests, CPU-side picking on packed meshes, and the shader all agree.
Never write SNORM −128: the GPU rule makes it equal to −127 anyway, and leaving it out keeps the code symmetric.
PackedTangentFrame and QuantizedPosition are plain arrays of integers: they're bytes for a vertex buffer, and the renderer defines the layouts.
The re-orthogonalisation in packTangentFrame: importers give tangents that aren't quite perpendicular, and a non-orthonormal basis isn't a rotation.
Alternatives rejected
Option
Why not
Spherical coordinates for normals
uneven precision (dense at the poles), trig to decode
Normal + tangent as two octahedral values + a sign
9 bytes, and the tangent isn't guaranteed perpendicular after decoding
10:10:10:2 normals
3 × 10 bits waste space on the third component; octahedral 2 × 16 is more precise in 32 bits
meshoptimizer's codecs now
a dependency (needs approval); worth it for whole-mesh compression later
