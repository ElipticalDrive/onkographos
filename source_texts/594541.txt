Problem
The CPU columnar voxel raycaster kept rendering as radial color slabs / a flat olive screen, no matter how I tuned camera height, scaleHeight, FOV, terrain density, or zNear. At 320×256 the near plane only covers a couple of heightmap cells, so each column stretches one cell color across many pixels — fundamental algorithmic mismatch for this resolution.
Solution
Replaced the whole part with a pure-shader heightfield raymarcher (the modern equivalent of Comanche-style voxel landscapes):
Per-pixel ridge + fBm noise terrain in the fragment shader.
Low-flying camera with a gentle S-curve and slow yaw.
Variable-step raymarch with linear refinement at the height crossing.
Stratified Christmas-night palette (valley → pine → grass → rock → snow) blended by height, lit by finite-difference normal diffuse shading.
Starfield, moon, halo, and distance fog blending into the sky.
Y mirror so the camera "up" matches the blit shader's flip.
Result
Snowy moonlit pine mountain range with depth and detail. Verified locally via Puppeteer screenshot.

The CPU columnar voxel raycaster was producing radial color slabs instead
of detailed terrain — at 320x256 internal resolution the near plane
covered only a couple of map cells, so each column stretched one cell
color across many screen pixels. Tuning the existing algorithm hit a
dead end.

Replaced it with a pure-shader heightfield raymarcher (the standard
modern equivalent of Comanche-style voxel landscapes):

- Per-pixel ridge+fBm noise terrain evaluated in the fragment shader.
- Ray origin/direction derived from a low-flying camera with a gentle
  S-curve and slow yaw.
- Variable-step raymarch with linear refinement at the height crossing.
- Stratified Christmas-night palette (valley, pine, grass, rock, snow)
  blended by height with finite-difference normals for diffuse shading.
- Starfield + moon + halo + distance fog into the sky color.
- Mirrors the blit shader's Y flip so "up" in the camera matches what
  the player sees.

Result: a snowy moonlit pine mountain range with proper depth and
detail rather than flat radial slabs.
