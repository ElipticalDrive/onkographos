In game development, terrain creation within engines such as Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) heavily relies on heightmaps—two-dimensional grayscale representations where pixel values correlate to 3D grid vertex elevations. To automate and enhance terrain generation based on incomplete user inputs, such as path-guided constraints or sparse sketches, this review evaluates various artificial intelligence image generation algorithms for their applicability to 3D terrain heightmap synthesis. We analyze generative models—including DRAW, Spatial LSTMs, autoregressive architectures (PixelRNN, PixelCNN, Gated PixelCNN, PixelCNN++, PixelSNAIL, Pixel Constrained CNNs), Conditional Neural Processes (CNPs), and variational autoencoders (VAE, VQ-VAE, VAEAC)—focusing specifically on their capacity to complete images from partial data, image fragments, or sparse pixel inputs. Among the evaluated architectures, CNPs, Pixel Constrained CNNs, and VAEAC exhibit the strongest capability for generating coherent outputs from arbitrary pixel hints and fragments, making them key candidates for future integration into procedural terrain generation pipelines.
