A small DICOM viewer in Rust. Point it at a folder, or a folder of folders, and it lists every image by patient, study and series. Each series, or each multi-frame file, can be scrubbed, played as a loop, saved as an MP4, measured with rulers, coloured, or turned into a 3D stack.
How it works
Scan (scan.rs): walks the tree in parallel and reads each header only up to the pixel data. The list fills in while the scan runs.
GPU (render.rs, image.wgsl): frames go to the GPU as raw values (R32Float, or RGBA8 for colour). A WGSL shader applies window/level, inversion and the colour map, filters bilinearly, and takes several samples per pixel when zoomed out. It runs on wgpu: Vulkan, Metal, DirectX 12 or OpenGL.
Loading (loader.rs): worker threads decode frames nearest first into an LRU cache capped at 2 GB, so a loaded series scrubs smoothly. Multi-frame files stay open between frames.
Decoding (decode.rs): uncompressed and RLE data are read directly, so one frame of a large multi-frame file costs one frame. JPEG and JPEG 2000 go through dicom-pixeldata. Frames also carry their pixel size and slice position.
Rulers: millimetre rulers along the bottom and right edges, from Pixel Spacing (including enhanced multi-frame groups), the ultrasound calibrated region, or Imager Pixel Spacing, which the ruler labels "at detector".
Colour maps (colormap.rs, common.wgsl): grey, hot iron and rainbow, applied after the window, on screen and in videos.
3D (volume.rs, render3d.rs, volume.wgsl): the 3D button builds a volume in the background (half floats, at most 512 px across and 256 slices). The current slice tilts back and the others fan out at their recorded spacing. Slices are drawn far to near into a 16-bit float offscreen image, either blended (dark is clear, bright is solid) or keeping the maximum (MIP), and a final pass puts the result on screen. Drag turns, Ctrl+drag sets the window, the wheel zooms.
MP4 export (export.rs, mp4.rs): "Save MP4…" or Ctrl+S writes the current series as H.264 on a background thread, with a progress bar and Cancel. Frames are drawn as the viewer draws them (window, level, inversion, smoothing, colour map), without zoom, pan, rulers or overlay text, so no patient details go into the file. Small images are scaled up to at least 512 px on the long side and large ones down to at most 2048. OpenH264 does the encoding, built from bundled source. mp4.rs writes the container itself, with the index before the data so playback can start before the file has loaded.
--check <folder>: decodes everything without a window and reports which files fail.
CI: formats, lints, tests and builds on Linux, Windows and macOS (universal binary), and uploads the binaries as artifacts.
Two dependency problems worked around
dicom-pixeldata 0.10 decodes 8-bit greyscale RLE one byte out of place: it writes from offset 1, not 0. RLE is now decoded here, and unit tests cover it.
The pure-Rust openjp2 port passes a null pointer to dealloc when it frees a code block, which Rust's debug UB check catches. The build uses openjpeg-sys instead, which compiles the C OpenJPEG with the cc crate and needs no cmake.
Testing
26 unit tests: decoding (signed 12-bit with rescale, MONOCHROME1, frame offsets, planar RGB, YBR, RLE at 8 and 16 bits and multi-frame, downsampling); grouping and sorting; export (output sizes, window and inversion, BT.709 colour, colour maps, letterboxing, MP4 box layout, start-code splitting, and an encode with the real encoder settings); colour maps; half-float conversion and slice shrinking for 3D; ruler lengths and labels.
Generated synthetic files with pydicom (CT, MR, CR as JPEG 2000, US cine raw and JPEG, XA as RLE, NM, palette, planar RGB, YBR) and compared every decoded frame with pydicom's pixels. All match exactly, apart from lossy JPEG, where the two decoders differ by at most 3 of 255.
Exported every multi-frame test series. ffprobe reads each as H.264 Constrained Baseline, BT.709 limited range, with the right frame count, rate and duration, and ffmpeg decodes them all without errors. Luma PSNR against the frames fed to the encoder: 47 dB for CT, 51 dB for XA, 52–54 dB for ultrasound, 41 dB for MR and 37 dB for NM, which is pure Poisson noise.
Ran the GUI under Xvfb with Mesa lavapipe (software Vulkan) and drove it with xdotool: browsing, scrubbing, window/level, zoom, pan, playback, MP4 export and Cancel. For this round, I made a head phantom (skull, brain, ventricles, spiral vessel, lesion) and a vessel phantom (spiral, ball and rod in air), with pixel spacing and slice positions:
Rulers read 2 cm on the CT, 1 cm on a calibrated ultrasound (0.20 mm pixels), and "1 cm at detector" on a radiograph with Imager Pixel Spacing.
The 3D button turns the slice into the stack smoothly, rotates on drag, and turns back to the 2D slice. Blend and MIP both work, in grey and in hot iron, and the colour maps work in 2D.
Release build, 500 CT slices of 512×512 plus a 300-frame 640×480 RGB cine: the scan takes about 0.01 s once the files are in the OS cache, and decoding every frame takes 0.26 s and 0.33 s. In the GUI the first slice shows within 0.7 s of launch. Exporting either series to MP4 takes about 3.6 s.
CI passes clippy, the unit tests and a release build on Linux, Windows and macOS, and the binaries are attached to the run. Nobody has yet opened the window on Windows or macOS.
