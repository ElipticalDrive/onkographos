3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training relies heavily on iterative densification. While efficient for view synthesis, this mechanism often disrupts scene geometry and extends convergence time. We propose Error-Decomposed and Attributed Refinement for 3D Gaussian Splatting(EARGS), a framework built on FixedBudget Optimization, to resolve these structural instabilities without inflating the primitive count. Distinct from Unbounded Adaptive Density Control, our approach utilizes back-propagation to decompose rendering errors at the pixel level, assigning specific contribution scores to individual Gaussians. This attribution process acts as a direct guide for geometric refinement, ensuring that long-axis splitting occurs only where structural deficiencies are strictly identified. By systematically pruning low-contribution primitives to reallocate capacity for complex boundary repairs, EARGS achieves geometric coherence and reconstruction fidelity superior to traditional densification-pruning loops.
