Wonderful work.
I have some questions related to the details of this work.
(1) If the densification is consistent with the original 3DGS, what is the function of the cross-view region and the rendered error for the Gaussian?
(2) When identifying ill-conditioned points, do you only consider two frames, or do you reconstruct the entire cross-view region to determine whether these points are not located in all cross-view regions? If you only consider two frames, the defined ill-conditioned points may belong to other cross-view regions.

Thank you for your interest in LPM. We maintain the densification operations as in the original 3DGS, including the split and clone processes. While the operations remain the same, the execution conditions differ. Rather than solely performing densification on points with a large view-averaged gradient, we also identify points driven by the error map and cross-view information that exhibit lower gradients.
For identifying ill-conditioned points, we currently consider only two views. Although these points may belong to other cross-view regions, they still contribute incorrect opacity values, which further influence the current view rendering. Thus, they can be treated as ill-conditioned.

We identify under-optimized points that do not utilize pixel-aware gradients. Our fundamental idea is that only relying on a large threshold, same to the original 3DGS's 0.0002, does not adequately capture all under-optimized points. However, lowering this threshold increases the number of points, subsequently reducing performance, as demonstrated in Table 3. Consequently, we believe that only the error zones require a lower threshold. We use the error map and multiview information to dynamically identify these zones in each iteration.
