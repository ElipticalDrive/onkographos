Summary
Explore more physically grounded lighting and chrome while preserving sharp photographic reflections and interactive animation. Lighting is evaluated on the projected 3D geometry, not via hypothetical 4D light transport.
Before this change
The old renderer used a custom artistic shader, not a physical metal/light model:
Lighting was effectively directional and camera-relative. The diagonal, top-down and quad presets supplied fixed directions compared with view-space normals. Although the shader named them “light positions,” it normalized each position directly instead of subtracting the shaded point's position. Every point on a face therefore received the same light direction; moving the camera changed the apparent orientation of the lights relative to the object.
“Light distance” was a global brightness control, not actual surface-to-light distance. It scaled lighting by 1 / (1 + 0.1 * lightDistance²). Increasing it did not produce the spatial changes in illumination expected from moving a nearby point light.
Highlights were hand-tuned Blinn–Phong terms. A fixed ambient contribution of 0.1, cosine diffuse shading and a power-32 specular highlight were combined with preset-specific weights. There were no cast shadows or mutual face reflections.
Chrome was a color mixture. It blended 80% cubemap color with 20% silver-gray, pushed that mixture toward the cubemap at grazing angles, then multiplied it by 0.7 * lighting + 0.3. The environment reflection direction itself was already computed in world space, but its brightness was modified by this artistic lighting multiplier rather than a physical metallic response.
Color arithmetic happened directly on encoded cubemap values. Reflections, lighting, grain and dirt were combined without explicit sRGB-to-linear conversion or tone mapping, then written out with dithering. This helped define the old look but was not linear-light shading.
The change is therefore more than adding stronger highlights: it replaces a camera-relative brightness treatment with world-space point lighting and a physical chrome material, while retaining deliberate artistic surface effects.
Lighting and materials
Decode sRGB cubemaps, shade in linear light, then apply ACES tone mapping and sRGB output.
Use world-fixed, inverse-square point lighting with correct two-sided surface normals.
Replace the artistic chrome mixture with a physical metal material; polished regions retain sharp source-cubemap detail while dirty regions use filtered reflections.
Add cached point-light shadows and correct planar self-shadow banding.
Restore position-based grain and keep dirt as a reflective film rather than an opaque coating.
Optional mutual reflections
Add a persisted mirror reflections setting, disabled by default and included in export/resume signatures.
Trace one additional face reflection, then terminate at the environment; this is bounded, not recursive full-scene transport.
Use explicit texture gradients to remove dotted reflection seams without blurring the photographs.
Interaction and performance
Reuse animated geometry buffers and a merged shadow caster instead of allocating geometry every frame.
Recompute shadows when the scene changes, not merely when the camera moves.
Clamp camera/target coordinates to [-3, 3]; allow a 0.01 minimum zoom and near plane.
Validation
GPU regression checks cover shader compilation, color conversion, lighting, polished detail, grain/dirt, shadows, and bounded face reflections.
Geometry, audio, settings persistence, and browser/export checks were exercised.
Reproduced and visually checked the reflection seam fix using an actual 1920×1080 export, rather than relying only on shader compilation.
Limits / review notes
Extra reflections can substantially increase GPU cost, so they remain opt-in. Secondary hits include surface tint/Fresnel/dirt and environment reflection, not full secondary lighting or vertex-sphere geometry.
Linear-light grain and the new materials change the appearance and the image-driven soundtrack; this is an artistic change, not an attempt at pixel/audio equivalence with the previous renderer.
The commits are incremental so individual rendering changes can be compared or cherry-picked.
Stacked on feat/dirty-mirrors. Test organization and faster day-to-day feedback are handled separately in #17, targeting this branch.
