Summary
This PR introduces diffcpm, a complete JAX implementation of the closest point method (CPM) for solving PDEs on implicit surfaces with full differentiability with respect to both PDE coefficients and surface geometry. The implementation enables PDE-constrained optimization directly on neural implicit surfaces without requiring meshing.
Key Changes
Core Infrastructure
grid.py: Banded Cartesian grid construction with fixed sparsity patterns and Ruuth-Merriman bandwidth estimation
interp.py: Differentiable Lagrange interpolation weights (extension matrix E) using nodal basis to avoid singularities at grid lines
operators.py: CPM operators combining finite-difference Laplacian (L) with interpolation, including stabilization schemes (diagsplit, lapsharp)
solve.py: Differentiable linear solves via jax.lax.custom_linear_solve that compute exact adjoints without unrolling iterations
Geometry and Implicit Surfaces
sdf.py: Closest point computation on level sets via Newton's method with implicit function theorem differentiation; includes SIREN neural network utilities
surfaces.py: Closed-form closest point maps for sphere/circle/torus and generic level-set solver for ellipsoids and learned SDFs
Inverse Problems
inverse.py: Observation operators, misfit functionals, regularizers, and finite-difference gradient verification utilities
Five complete examples demonstrating source recovery, diffusivity recovery, shape optimization, SDF error propagation, and spectral validation
Testing
Comprehensive test suite (tests/test_diffcpm.py) with 19 tests covering:
Operator correctness and stability
Interpolation accuracy and transpose properties
Geometry gradient verification
Adjoint correctness
Continuous-to-discrete convergence rates
Shape derivative behavior under grid alignment
Notable Implementation Details
Fixed sparsity pattern: The band structure and stencil pattern remain constant during optimization, enabling JAX tracing despite moving geometry. Only integer gather indices change, which carry no gradients.
Nodal Lagrange basis: Avoids barycentric form's division-by-zero singularities when closest points land exactly on grid lines (common for symmetric surfaces on symmetric grids), ensuring smooth derivatives everywhere.
Consistent base-point computation: Computes stencil base index and local coordinate from a single division to prevent rounding inconsistencies that could cause O(1) interpolation errors.
Adjoint-based gradients: Uses custom_linear_solve to compute exact adjoints of the discrete operator without unrolling, achieving memory-independent gradient computation.
Geometry diagnostics: Provides clearance() and violations() checks to detect when moving surfaces leave the band, with early warning before accuracy degrades.

Implements the CPM forward solve so that it is differentiable with respect
to PDE coefficients and to the geometry, including the weights of a neural
implicit surface, for PDE-constrained optimization on implicit surfaces
with no meshing step.

Structure:
  diffcpm/grid.py        banded Cartesian grids, Ruuth-Merriman bandwidth
  diffcpm/interp.py      differentiable Lagrange weights, extension matrix E
  diffcpm/operators.py   banded Laplacian, stabilized CPM operator
  diffcpm/solve.py       lax.custom_linear_solve: dense LU or matrix-free GMRES
  diffcpm/sdf.py         closest points on a level set by Newton + IFT; SIREN
  diffcpm/surfaces.py    circle/sphere/torus closed forms, ellipsoid implicitly
  diffcpm/inverse.py     observation operator, misfit, regularizers, FD checker

Conventions follow the MATLAB cp_matrices/ in this repository: ndgrid tensor
grids, C-order linear indices, the band radius of rm_bandwidth.m, the base
point convention of findGridInterpBasePt.m, and the diagonal-split
stabilization of lapsharp.m.

Gradients use the adjoint rather than unrolling: lax.custom_linear_solve is
given the forward matvec and a transpose solver, so reverse mode produces
A^T lam = dJ/du and dJ/dp = lam^T (db/dp - (dA/dp) u) with memory independent
of the Krylov iteration count. Geometry gradients flow through the
interpolation weights, which are polynomials in the closest point. Closest
points on a level set are obtained by Newton on the first-order conditions
and differentiated with lax.custom_root, i.e. by the implicit function
theorem at the converged root.

Two departures from the MATLAB interpolation code, both for differentiability:

- Lagrange weights use the nodal form prod_{m!=j}(s-m)/(j-m) instead of the
  barycentric form of LagrangeWeights1D.m. The barycentric form divides by
  x - x_j, which MATLAB special-cases with a branch returning binary weights;
  under autodiff that branch returns a constant and so a zero derivative
  exactly at grid lines, which closest points hit routinely on symmetric
  geometry. The nodal form is a polynomial, exact and smooth everywhere.

- The stencil base index and the local coordinate are computed from a single
  division and never re-derived from each other. Computing s as
  (x - (relpt + I*dx))/dx rounds the same real number twice, and when a
  closest point lands on a grid line the two roundings can straddle the
  integer, leaving I and s describing stencils one cell apart. That produced
  an 85% error in the solution on the unit circle at dx=0.1, visible only
  under jax.jit because it depends on how the compiler reassociates the
  arithmetic.

Verified in diffcpm/tests/test_diffcpm.py (17 tests): E is exact on
polynomials of degree <= p; apply_sparse_T is the exact transpose; the
forward solve is second order near the surface; custom_linear_solve
reproduces the hand-assembled adjoint to 1e-11; dense and GMRES agree on
value and gradient; dJ/dtheta and dJ/d(shape) match central differences for
a circle radius, ellipse axes, torus radii, a 3D sphere radius and SIREN
weights; diagsplit and lapsharp stabilizations coincide exactly while the
raw product L E is unstable.

Experiments in examples_diffcpm/ cover source recovery on a circle,
diffusivity recovery on a sphere, the convergence of the discrete shape
derivative against a closed form, how a learned SDF's geometric error
propagates into recovered parameters, and ellipse shape optimization with a
spectral check of the surface Laplacian.

Known limitations, including the moving-band issue, are documented in
diffcpm/README.md rather than left implicit.

Two findings from running the experiments, and the diagnostics they call for.

Band staleness has two failure modes, and the natural check catches the
wrong one. InterpPattern.violations asks whether the interpolation stencils
of the band points still land in the band; that is the LAST thing to fail.
The first is the outer edge of the band, where the Laplacian drops
out-of-band neighbours and imposes an artificial Dirichlet condition, coming
within the Ruuth-Merriman radius of the surface. On a band built for the unit
circle at dx=0.1, drifting to R=1.1 leaves violations at zero while the
solution near the surface is already contaminated.

BandedGrid.uncovered_near_surface and BandedGrid.clearance are the sufficient
check: they ask the question the band was built to answer -- is every grid
point within the Ruuth-Merriman radius of the CURRENT surface in the band? --
and report the largest fraction of that radius still covered. Measured
against required_bw (the RM radius for this p and stencil radius) rather than
bw, so slack added by extra_bw is spent on surface motion instead of on
inflating its own requirement.

What losing clearance costs is lopsided: the objective degrades by about a
factor of 2 while its shape derivative loses more than two orders of
magnitude. The gradient is far more sensitive to band staleness than the
solution, which is the wrong way round for an optimizer that moves the shape.
test_band_clearance_predicts_degradation pins down both the ordering of the
two failures and this disparity.

Second finding: the discrete shape derivative converges more slowly than the
objective. Against the closed form on a circle, J_h -> J at a clean second
order, but dJ_h/dR -> dJ/dR non-monotonically and at roughly first order on
average, so refining the grid can make the derivative error worse. Averaging
over sub-cell grid offsets recovers second order while the spread across
offsets is as large as the mean error, which identifies the cause as grid
alignment: the CPM discretization error depends on where the surface sits
relative to the grid lines, and differentiating in a shape direction
differentiates that dependence as well as the physics. The adjoint itself is
exact for the discrete objective (checked to 1e-11), so this is a statement
about the discretization and an argument for tapered or blended stencils.
ex3 is that experiment; the test now asserts what actually holds rather than
a rate that does not.

Also in this commit:

- solve: note that GMRES is ~6x faster than dense LU per value-and-gradient
  at ~1000 unknowns and agrees to every printed digit; dense stays the
  default because it cannot fail to converge.
- tests: 18 total, adding band clearance and splitting the 3D sphere into a
  GMRES forward check and a dense gradient check (the gradient check needs a
  step below the stencil-crossing scale, at which an iterative solve stopped
  at 1e-13 puts a floor of 1e-13/eps on the difference quotient).
- ex1: project the surface trace of u for the forward check rather than
  band values, which were contaminated by the band edge.
- ex2: sweep the sensor count rather than only the regularization weight;
  at 60 sensors and 9 unknowns the weight is inert, so the earlier table
  showed nothing and its narrative claimed a degree trend the numbers do
  not support.
- ex5: matrix-free GMRES, and clearance assertions at both ends of the
  optimization.

clearance is now the distance from the surface to the nearest out-of-band
grid point, in units of the Ruuth-Merriman radius, capped at 1. Previously it
searched a ladder of safety levels and so returned a coarse 0.1-quantised
answer, and evaluated the distance function once per level. That matters when
the distance function is a closest point solve through a neural network:
uncovered_near_surface now only evaluates it at out-of-band grid points, and
clearance evaluates it once rather than ten times. Values agree with the old
search (R=1.05 read 0.9, now 0.941; R=1.10 read 0.8, now 0.802).

adam puts its whole loop inside one lax.scan and jits it. Run step by step
from Python, a few thousand steps of a small SIREN spend nearly all their time
in dispatch: 4000 steps went from minutes to 25s. A callback still forces the
Python loop, since a callback cannot be traced.

interp hardening: the snap tolerance is now relative to |t|, since a fixed 1e-9
in cell units stops separating rounding noise from a genuine near-integer once
a grid has more than ~1e7 cells on a side. InterpPattern rejects an
interpolation degree above the one the band was sized for (the wider stencils
are not guaranteed to fit) and a grid too large for its int32 linear indices.

New test: the Newton/IFT closest point solver against the closed-form sphere
and torus maps, to ~1e-14, with first-order residuals at machine precision,
including on a level-set function with |grad f| != 1. 19 tests, all passing.

README gains a scope section mapping the three steps of the plan's first
experiment to what is actually here, including the two that are partial and
why: no bunny (the code path is verified on a SIREN-fitted circle; what a
bunny adds is scale, which needs CPM multigrid rather than dense LU), and no
spectral morphing (a closed curve's Laplace-Beltrami spectrum depends only on
its perimeter, so the 2D target is degenerate -- ex5 verifies the spectrum
against that formula instead and recovers ellipse axes, which is identifiable).

ex4 retuned from a measured sweep: five fits spanning a factor of ~17 in
geometric error, avoiding both ends, where the level set is not yet a closed
curve and where Adam starts to oscillate.

ex5 gains a noise-free control run, because the previous closing text
attributed the residual axis error to the piecewise-C^1 shape derivative
without evidence. Repeating the recovery on noise-free data separates
noise-limited from discretization-limited instead of asserting a cause.

The getting-started snippet is checked to run verbatim: install, path, and a
minimal end-to-end differentiable solve on a circle, with the two checks
(violations and clearance) a caller should make before trusting a result.

"Fixed sparsity pattern" was overstated. The *shape* of the pattern is fixed
-- every row has exactly (p+1)^dim interpolation entries and
1 + 2*dim*stenrad difference entries, set by the band and the stencil rather
than by the geometry -- but which columns those entries land in does move with
the surface. That movement is integer arithmetic on a gather, so it costs
nothing at trace time and carries no gradient, and nothing is reallocated,
re-factorized or re-banded. Worth stating precisely, since it is the property
that makes the whole thing traceable.

ex5's noise-free recovery returns the ellipse axes to 2.4e-14, and the text
read that as evidence the discretization bias is negligible. It is not
evidence of anything of the kind. The data are generated by the same discrete
forward model that is then inverted, so the discretization error cancels
identically and what the number measures is that the adjoint and the optimizer
agree with each other. Reporting it as accuracy would have been the most
misleading claim in the package, so it is now named as the inverse crime and
labelled a self-consistency check.

The measurement the old text was reaching for now exists. Control 2 generates
noise-free data on a grid twice as fine and inverts on the coarse one, which
breaks the cancellation:

  discretization bias at dx = 0.08     -4.9e-4 / +2.4e-4
  between-grid data mismatch            1.9e-4 rms
  noise-driven error, noisy run        +3.6e-4 / -2.3e-4

Same order, so at this noise level neither alone binds -- a more useful
statement than either number by itself, and one the earlier version could not
have made.

ex4 had the same defect in its narrative: its "exact" row is set by the
optimizer's stopping tolerance, not by discretization, for the same
cancellation reason. Its SIREN rows are unaffected and are the point of the
experiment, because those invert a genuinely different geometry from the one
that generated the data -- which is what makes the 1.03 log-log slope of
parameter error against geometric error meaningful.

Also: fix a leaked %.2f in ex5's output; ex1 prints per-mode errors in
exponential form, since the mode-1 error is far smaller than the others and was
displaying as +0.00000; correct the examples README runtimes from measurement
and note that XLA compilation is a visible share of the shorter runs.

Two new experiments, and the library work the first of them forced.

diffcpm/mesh.py reads an ASCII PLY and computes exact closest points on a
triangle mesh, so the Stanford bunny already in ../surfaces/tri/ can be driven
through the pipeline. Closest points use a KD-tree on triangle centroids for
candidates and an exact point-to-triangle kernel covering all seven Voronoi
regions. The tree is a heuristic, so a second pass makes it exact: any point
whose candidate set is not provably sufficient is re-queried over every centroid
within dbest + max_reach, a ball that provably contains any triangle that could
win. Validated against brute force over all 69,451 triangles, agreeing to
5.6e-17; the exact pass costs 0.7s per 4,000 points and changed no answers,
which makes k=24 provably right here rather than luckily right.

examples_diffcpm/ex7_bunny_diffusivity.py is the checkpoint: recover a
diffusivity field on the bunny from 120 sensors, at three resolutions, with data
generated on a finer grid. It runs mesh closest points and SIREN closest points
side by side, so discretization convergence and the cost of the learned surface
are measured separately. scripts/fit_bunny_siren.py fits and caches the network.

examples_diffcpm/ex6_topology_change.py is the experiment that distinguishes this
from differentiable mesh FEM: two circles, smoothly unioned, whose separation is
the shape parameter, so the topology changes at a known value (s = r + k log 2,
confirmed numerically). Part A sweeps through the merge and shows the solver's
residuals not noticing while the region's component count steps from 2 to 1.
Part B recovers the separation from data generated on the merged shape at a finer
grid, starting from a separated shape, so the optimizer has to cross the merge.

Findings that changed the code:

- The closest point Newton was not robust on a real learned SDF. Where a SIREN's
  gradient is small the Gauss-Newton step is unbounded, and on the bunny that put
  closest points thousands of units away with the residual stuck near 1. _newton
  now takes the best of a geometric ladder of step lengths and only accepts a
  step that reduces ||F||, which makes the residual non-increasing by
  construction, plus optional step clipping. Failures on the bunny fell from
  10.1% to 2.4% of band points, and what remains is a clean per-point failure the
  caller can detect. level_set_residuals reports residuals per point for exactly
  that, since a maximum cannot distinguish a few bad points from a bad solve.

- A band built from the first-order distance estimate |f|/|grad f| had a hole
  exactly at the seam of the smooth union, because the smoothing flattens the
  gradient there and the estimate overshoots. That gave 58 interpolation
  violations and zero clearance at the merge, i.e. the band failed precisely
  where the experiment happens. ex6 now builds its band from the exact distance
  to the un-smoothed union, which underestimates near the seam, and
  underestimating is the safe direction.

- adam takes an optional key and threads it, so a loss can draw minibatches.
  Full-batch training on 120k 3D samples is not affordable on CPU.

Two measurements worth recording, neither of them flattering:

- On the bunny, 2 to 4% of band points have a genuinely non-unique closest point
  at every resolution from dx = 0.12 to 0.04, and the fraction does not shrink
  with refinement. Real scanned geometry has feature sizes below any grid spacing
  worth running.

- I guessed those ambiguous points were the ones where Newton fails. They are
  not: the two rates nearly coincide, 2.4% against 2.2%, but the sets overlap by
  only about 11%. The coincidence is a coincidence.

Also revised: GMRES needs no preconditioning at checkpoint scale, reaching 1e-14
in about 2 seconds at 10k unknowns, so the earlier claim that multigrid was a
prerequisite for the bunny was too pessimistic. It remains one for the ~8e5
unknowns that full feature resolution would need.

The first ex7 run produced a usable mesh-geometry result and a worthless
learned-geometry one: field error 865% at dx=0.12 and 314% at dx=0.09, with J
around 1100x its noise floor. Three causes, all mine.

Each geometry now gets a band built from its own surface. Building the learned
surface's band from the mesh looks harmless and is not. A SIREN with 0.008 rms
signed-distance error still places its zero level set as much as 0.7 from the
mesh at a few points -- larger than a band half-width of 0.49 at dx=0.12 -- so
the learned surface pokes out of a mesh-built band. One interpolation violation
in that row is the evidence. The learned surface's band is built from |f|, which
is affordable over a whole grid where a Newton solve is not, and then checked
with violations and clearance on real closest points rather than trusted.

The data-generating grid moves from dx=0.05 to 0.035. At 0.05 it was only 1.4x
finer than the finest inversion grid, which is not enough separation for the
refinement study to be about anything other than the inverse crime again.

The fit gains an eikonal penalty on |grad f| - 1, and more steps. Plain distance
regression drives the rms down while leaving a tail, and the tail is what breaks
CPM: the band cares about the largest deviation, not the average. The same term
addresses the other failure mode, since the closest point Newton blows up exactly
where |grad f| collapses. The fit script now reports |grad f| percentiles and the
p99 and max of the geometric error, not just the median.

Long-running examples flush their output, so progress is visible instead of
appearing all at once at the end.

Results so far, mesh geometry, data at dx=0.05 (superseded by the 0.035 rerun):

  dx     band n   J       ||dc||   field err
  0.120   5552    1637    0.291    0.307
  0.090   9938     574    0.364    0.171
  0.070  16299     607    0.128    0.117

Worth reading both error columns. ||dc|| is non-monotone while the field error
falls steadily and roughly halves per refinement: the four coefficients are not
equally determined and trade against each other at nearly constant misfit.
Quoting ||dc|| alone would read as non-convergence, and quoting the field error
alone would hide the conditioning. J stays 10 to 27 times its noise floor of 60,
so model error rather than sensor noise is what the fit is up against, which fits
the 2 to 4% of band points whose closest point is genuinely non-unique.

Also: the SIREN's {f < 0} is a single connected component, so the 0.7 tail is not
a spurious blob. That was my second wrong guess about this fit in a row, after
the ambiguity-equals-Newton-failure one; recording both rather than only the
hypotheses that survived.

A new test exposed a bug the earlier fix had missed. Backtracking inside the
Newton solve only guarantees the residual does not increase from where it starts,
and on a learned SDF the *initial guess* is already gone: the projection step
-f grad f / |grad f|^2 has length |f| / |grad f|, which is unbounded exactly
where the gradient decays. On tanh(5(|y|-1)), whose zero set is the unit sphere,
the initial guess landed 1.4e9 away before Newton ran at all. The projection
steps are now clipped to max_step as well. Clipping only slows the approach to
the surface; it cannot move the converged root, which the first-order conditions
fix.

Four tests added, 23 total, all passing, and the 19 that predate the Newton
changes still pass:

- mesh closest points against brute force over every triangle, on an
  icosahedron, including query points inside the mesh and on its surface where
  the barycentric region logic is actually exercised;
- signed distance sign inside and out, with magnitude equal to the closest
  distance;
- the PLY reader on the repository's bunny, pinning the 1,113 orphaned vertices
  that made an earlier assertion fail;
- the flat-gradient case above, asserting both that the hardened path converges
  and that the naive path still fails, so the test keeps exercising the fix.

Measured contribution of the clipping on the bunny, same network and band, only
the solver settings changed: failures 2.67% -> 2.20%. It does not touch the
geometric error tail, which stays at 0.73 max against a 0.055 p95, so that tail
is a fit-quality problem rather than a solver one. The eikonal term is the lever
for it.

Checkpoint result with the learned surface, and it is a failure worth recording
precisely. At dx = 0.12, 0.09, 0.07 the recovered coefficients are nonsense --
||dc|| 2.72, 2.38, 2.45 against a true coefficient norm of 0.53, with the z
coefficient at 2.07 instead of 0.30 and y sign-flipped -- J sits about 1250 times
its noise floor, and refinement does not help. The mesh geometry on the same
problem converges, so this is the learned surface, not the solver or the
discretization.

The mechanism looks like this: the closest point solve fails on a few percent of
band points, and on a *different* few percent for each grid spacing, 1.24% on the
data grid against 1.76 to 3.10% on the inversion grids. So each grid carries a
slightly different corrupted geometry, which is grid-dependent model error that
does not shrink under refinement. That would explain both the huge misfit and the
flat convergence, and it contrasts sharply with the clean first-order propagation
measured on a circle in ex4. A learned surface at bunny scale is not a scaled-up
version of a learned circle.

scale, with ||dc|| around 2.4 to 2.7 and no improvement under refinement, and
proposed that grid-dependent closest point failures were the mechanism. With the
setup corrected -- each geometry's band built from its own surface, an eikonal

  dx      ||dc|| mesh   ||dc|| siren   field err mesh   field err siren
  0.120   0.390         0.313          0.324            0.187
  0.090   0.753         0.305          0.360            0.274
  0.070   0.311         0.231          0.196            0.200

The proposed mechanism is also wrong. The eikonal fit halved the closest point
failures, 2.20% to 1.10%, and that is not what changed the outcome; the band
built from the wrong geometry and the insufficiently fine data grid were. Both
were my errors, not findings about learned surfaces.

A second correction, in the other direction. The earlier mesh-geometry result
"field error halves per refinement, 0.31 to 0.17 to 0.12" was obtained with the
data grid only 1.4x finer than the finest inversion grid, so it was measuring
partly the inverse crime it was meant to avoid. With proper separation neither
geometry converges cleanly: field error is about 20% at best and non-monotone,
and J stays 9 to 73 times its noise floor. That earlier number should not be
quoted.

The most likely cause of the floor is the one quantity measured here that does
not shrink with refinement: 2 to 4% of band points lie beyond the medial axis at
every spacing from dx = 0.12 to 0.04, because the bunny has feature sizes below
any grid worth running. A fixed fraction of invalid closest point extensions
gives a fixed error floor. That is a measured reason to put multigrid first,
rather than the estimate I had before.

Recorded as a hypothesis, not a finding, since three mechanisms I proposed for

diffcpm/fem.py is a P1 surface FEM: cotangent stiffness, lumped mass,
matrix-free, and differentiable with respect to both the coefficients and the
vertex positions, driven by the same lax.custom_linear_solve adjoint as the CPM
layer so the comparison is between discretizations rather than solvers. It
handles triangles in 3D and segments in 2D, with barycentric observation as the
counterpart of the CPM Observer.

Validated before being used for anything: total area converges to 4 pi at second
order; the eigenvalues of M^-1 K come out 0, 2, 2, 2 and 5.966 five times against
the exact l(l+1) = 0, 2, 2, 2, 6...; solving (-Delta_s + 1) u = 3z on the unit
sphere converges at rate 1.71 to the exact u = z; and gradients with respect to
the reaction coefficient and with respect to vertex positions both match central
differences to about 1e-9. It differentiates through vertex motion correctly,
which matters -- the comparison must not be against a straw man.

The controlled smoothness test settles the mechanism. Same unit sphere, two
closest point maps, only smoothness differing:

  dx      analytic ||dc||   mesh ||dc||   analytic J/floor   mesh J/floor
  0.160   5.5e-3           2.3e-2        0.8                2.2

The analytic map sits at the noise floor at every resolution. The triangulated
map is 4 to 11 times worse and its misfit *rises* under refinement. A fixed
geometric offset cannot produce growth, so this is not the icosphere's 2.2e-3

and the degree-3 interpolation behind the CP extension loses its accuracy against
a map that is only piecewise smooth.

explanation committed earlier: CPM accuracy depends on the smoothness of the
closest point map, not only on its pointwise accuracy. A smooth neural SDF is a
better input to this method than an exact triangle mesh.

Chasing a spike in ex8's objective produced the sharpest result of this work, and
it is an obstruction rather than a tuning problem.

The symptom: near s = 0.6925 the CPM objective rose from about 1e6 to 3.6e8 across
three steps of 5e-4, and the exact adjoint gradient went to +5.3e12 then -2.7e11.
Two hypotheses were wrong and are recorded as such. It is not band overlap in the
gap between the circles: narrowing the band from half-width 0.330 to 0.205 left
max|u| identical to four digits. It is not the closest point solve: residuals are
2e-16 at every s and identical under a much tighter Newton.

The cause is a spurious eigenvalue. The true operator -Delta_s + 1 has smallest
eigenvalue exactly 1, the constants on each component. The discrete operator's
smallest eigenvalue fell 0.812, 0.246, 0.055 as s approached 0.6925, so a mode
below the true floor passed near zero, and max|u| grew 2.8, 5.6, 19.2 with it.

It is caused by band points that are valid for some shape in the family but not
for the one being solved. The band was built as the union over s in [0.50, 0.80],
and 51.7% of its points are farther from the current surface than the
Ruuth-Merriman radius, up to 0.40 against a radius of 0.18 -- deep inside the
discs, where a closest point extension means nothing. Rebuilding as the
intersection over the family removes the spurious mode completely: no stale
points, smallest eigenvalue 1.6 to 6.6, and max|u| smooth and monotone.

But the intersection band has clearance 0.000: it does not cover the surface, so
its answer is wrong for a different reason. That is the obstruction, in two
numbers. Over a family whose shapes move by more than about a band width, no
single fixed band is both sufficient (covers every shape's stencils) and valid
(holds no point outside any shape's reach). The union is sufficient and invalid,
the intersection valid and insufficient, and there is nothing in between to tune.

BandedGrid.stale_fraction is the missing check. clearance tests sufficiency only,
which is why a union band passes it at 1.000 while carrying 51.7% invalid points.
Verified: union 0.517 stale at clearance 1.000; intersection 0.000 stale at
clearance 0.000; neither passes both.

This gives the tapered-weight idea a measured justification and a test: taper to
zero the weights of points that are stale for the current shape, so the band stays
nominally the union and is effectively valid, then check that the spurious
eigenvalue below 1 does not appear.

Consequences for results already reported. ex6 part B's recovery to 1.9e-3 ran on
a union band with about half its points stale, and succeeded partly because L-BFGS
jumped from s = 0.750 to 0.426 in one step, over the bad region rather than
through it. ex8's gradient at s = 0.70 is exact for its discrete objective and that
objective is corrupted; the smallest eigenvalue is a healthy 1.0 at that s, but the
spike sits 0.0075 away. Neither result is retracted, and neither should be quoted
without this.
