Collision detection gives importance in physics-based simulations, especially when dealing with rapid particle interactions. Swept Axis Aligned Boundary Box (Swept-AABB) resolves only the first collision encountered within a simulation frame. This limitation can result in overlooked interactions and inaccurate particle positioning where multiple collisions occur in rapid succession. The proposed Trajectory-Based Continuous Collision Detection (T-CCD) approach overcomes this drawback by allowing particles to continue their motion and resolve all collisions within a single frame. A two-dimensional particle simulation was developed to implement the concept. It supports elastic collisions and dynamic time stepping and integrates spatial partitioning techniques, including axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) and voxel traversal, to enhance performance. Experimental evaluation across low (1500 units/sec), medium (3000 units/sec), and high (5000 units/sec) velocity regimes demonstrates that T-CCD achieves statistically significantly higher collision detection accuracy at medium velocity, which performs equivalently to Swept-AABB at low and high velocities. Computation time analysis showed that Swept-AABB maintains shorter runtimes and higher frame rates across all velocities. Conservation error analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in energy and momentum preservation between the algorithms, indicating stable physical fidelity. Statistical testing confirmed rejection of the null hypothesis for collision accuracy at medium velocity and found no significant effect for conservation error. These results suggest that T-CCD provides a robust framework for accurate multi-collision handling, with potential advantages in scenarios where collision accuracy is prioritized over computational efficiency.
