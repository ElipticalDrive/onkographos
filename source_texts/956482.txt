Summary
Adds support for see-through blocks: a new Glass block (cut out, like Minecraft's) and see-through Water (blended). Also replaces BlockDef.Opacity with a derived BlocksLight, and adds OpaqueModel for model blocks that fill their cell (the Fan).
Render layers
BlockDef.Layer decides how a full-cube block is drawn, and GreedyMesher builds one mesh per layer for each chunk:
Layer
Blocks
Drawn
Opaque
stone, dirt, …
fs_main, with the world, nearest chunk first
Cutout
glass
fs_cutout, after the opaque meshes. Texels under half alpha are discarded; the rest write depth like stone, so no sorting is needed. Back faces are culled. It's a separate entry point and draw so fs_main has no discard and keeps the early depth test.
Translucent
water
Alpha-blended in a new RenderTransparent stage after the sky and overlays. A depth pre-pass (fs_chunk_transparent_depth) lays down the nearest translucent face at each pixel, and the colour pass (fs_chunk_transparent) draws only that one. So every pixel blends exactly one translucent layer, whatever the draw order. Both sides are drawn, so the surface is seen from underneath too.
Transparent (any layer but Opaque) drives culling and light: a transparent block never hides a neighbour's face. Its own faces are hidden by an opaque cube, an opaque model (Fan), or another block of its own type.
Translucent face opacity is the texture's alpha × BlockDef.Alpha, packed into the spare byte of ChunkVertex.
The streamed world still meshes chunks without their neighbours, except for transparent faces, which are culled against their real neighbour across chunk borders. A chunk remeshes when the cell beside one of its border transparent blocks changes, or when that neighbour loads or unloads.
Blocks
Glass (new, BlockId.Glass = 17): the spritesheet's glass texture, Cutout. It collides and lets light through.
Water: Translucent at 60% opacity, and lets light through. It is now Passable: raycasts still hit it, but bodies sink through it. There's no swimming yet.
Both are on the hotbar.
Lighting and opaque models
Opacity was only ever read as >= 15. It's replaced by a derived BlocksLight: true for full cubes unless they're Transparent, and for OpaqueModel model blocks.
OpaqueModel (set on the Fan, whose housing fills its cell) also hides the faces of blocks against it, like an opaque cube does. Otherwise those faces sat on the model's sides and flickered.
A light-blocking cell holds no real light of its own (the light pass gives it flat ambient and full sun). So fs_model now lights a model block in such a cell from the brightest of its open neighbouring cells.
Physics
A grid owned here gets its body only once CollidersReady says every nearby terrain chunk with blocks has its collider.
A chunk whose blocks don't collide (only water, now that water is passable) builds an empty collider. That was never recorded, so every grid within 64 blocks of such a chunk waited forever: drawn, but with no body to stand on.
PhysicsBodySystem now records empty colliders, and CollidersReady counts them as done.
The same latent bug was already possible on main for a chunk containing only levers or wheels.
Known limitations
Glass's half-transparent streak pixels are cut to fully clear or fully solid. As in Minecraft, you see only the near side of a glass block.
Only the nearest translucent (water) layer shows. Glass doesn't count as a layer, so water is visible through glass.
Light isn't dimmed or tinted by water or glass: light is either fully blocked or fully passed.
Glass and water faces get flat rather than smooth lighting.
A transparent face against a model that only partly fills its cell (lever or wheel base) can still z-fight where the two touch.
Testing
dotnet build is clean, and all 181 tests pass:
TransparentBlockTests covers registration, render layers and the BlocksLight categories; per-layer meshing and culling; chunk-border culling and the remesh triggers; and alpha packing.
TerrainGatingTests.TerrainWhoseBlocksDontCollideCountsAsReady covers the physics fix; it fails without it.
The generated WGSL passes naga validation.
Run in-game under lavapipe (software Vulkan), using temporary, uncommitted debug hooks:
Rendering: a 6×6×6 box straddling a chunk corner in the streamed world, placed one block at a time. With all water, the mesher emitted no faces on any chunk border, and the depth pre-pass removed the shading seams that out-of-order blending had caused along the borders. With the box half glass, the glass draws as a cutout with the water visible through it.
Physics, on a real save with several ships: before the fix, four ships near placed water never got a body and the player fell through them. After it, every ship gets a body on load and the player stands on the 569-block ship.
