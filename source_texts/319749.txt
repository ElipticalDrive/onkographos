Abstract
Several effective acceleration techniques for volume rendering offer efficient means to skip over empty space, providing significant speedup without affecting image quality. The effectiveness of such an approach depends on its ability to accurately estimate the object boundary inside a volume with minimal computational overhead. We propose a novel boundary cell-based acceleration technique for ray casting which skips over empty space by accurately calculating the intersection distance for each ray. Very short distance estimation time is achieved by exploiting a projection template to calculate the parallel-projection values of each boundary cell and the coherency of adjacent cells. Since no hardware acceleration is used, the projection procedure can also be efficiently parallelized. Experimental results are provided to demonstrate the performance of our new algorithm.
Introduction
With the increasing complexity of phenomena recorded in volumetric datasets, a new challenge arises to provide the user with the means to explore these datasets. Near-real-time interactive direct-manipulation techniques are one approach to this problem, allowing the user to move the eye point to a desired location, interactively change the rendering parameters, and view the resulting image after a short time step[1].
Volume rendering turns out to be a more challenging candidate than surface rendering to realize such an interactive direct-manipulation. Since all voxels are contributing to the image, volume rendering displays much more interior phenomena of the volume dataset by using transparency, without losing small or poorly defined features. In addition, volume renderers support interactive data segmentation, which is of particular importance in providing insight into complex phenomena with unknown interior structures. However, volume rendering methods are time-consuming and often far from operating in real-time or interactive speed on existing graphics workstations[2].
In order to accelerate the volume rendering rate, many acceleration techniques have been proposed: some of them exploit image-space coherence by adaptively sampling in the image plane[3]; some exploit object-space coherence by reducing the sampling rate in the homogeneous area of the volume[4]; and some of the most effective techniques facilitate efficient means to rapidly traverse empty space, thus providing significant speedup without affecting image quality5, 6, 7, 8, 9.
When a volume consists of one object surrounded by empty space, an attractive method for skipping this empty space is using the techniques of bounding-boxes. First, surround the object with tightly fit boxes or other easy-to-intersect geometric primitives such as spheres. Then, calculate the intersection of the rays with the bounding object. Finally, start the actual volume traversal along each ray from the intersection point as opposed to starting from the volume boundary. The effectiveness of this approach depends on its ability to accurately estimate the intersection distance for each viable ray with minimal computational overhead.
The PARC (Polygon Assisted Ray Casting) algorithm[7]strives for a better fit by using pre-calculated subvolumes aligned with the primary axes to bound the object inside the volume. The subvolumes are generated by dividing each axis of the volume into 2l equal distance regions, where l is the subdivision level. For each particular view, the subvolumes are projected into a pair of front and back z-buffers to obtain closer bounds on the intervals where the ray integrals need to be calculated. Projection of the subvolumes can be performed quickly on graphics hardware, since shading of the subvolumes is unnecessary. This algorithm achieves speeds comparable with some of the fastest volume renderers. The only pre-processing done is a scan over the volume to determine which subvolumes contain the object boundary. However, this algorithm also suffers from some inherent problems which limit its efficiency. For example, the performance of the PARC algorithm rests on the subdivision level l chosen for each axis in object space. In order to reach an accurate object boundary estimation, a high level subdivision is required. Unfortunately, the projection time with a high level subdivision is prohibitively expensive even with hardware acceleration. Furthermore, when this algorithm is parallelized on multiprocessors for faster rendering speeds, the projection procedure has to be implemented in software, leading to an even worse estimation overhead[10].
In this paper, we propose a novel boundary cell-based acceleration technique to skip over empty space in the volume with an accurate estimation of the object boundary, which is equal to that of the highest level of subdivision in the PARC algorithm, with very short projection time. Since no hardware acceleration is used, the projection procedure can also be efficiently parallelized.
