Owner
A second all-hex mesher alongside buffer. Adds pub mod marching; in cut/mod.rs, and a small extension of the grid marching cubes (below).
Tessellation::marching_hex
```
tessellation.marching_hex(spacing, Marching { placement, finish }) -> Result<Mesh<3>, _>
```
Marching, Placement, Finish, Freedom are exported from geometry::mesh.
Clips every cell of a uniform lattice to the surface (marching-cubes style) and splits what is left about its midpoints — all-hexahedral, no hanging nodes, no optimizer, no element insertion.
Placement — where a boundary vertex sits on a straddling edge:
Midpoint (Dhondt / Protais): first-order boundary, no cell can degenerate
Crossing(guard) (Tong / Zhang): second-order, held guard off each edge end
Finish — what happens to the boundary after the split:
Cut — leave it at the clip (provably valid on all 256 sign masks)
Draw(share) — bisection projection onto the surface, keeping share of each hexahedron's as-cut scaled Jacobian; boundary only, no volume DOF. The default.
Fit(Freedom::{Whole, Shell}) — hand the split mesh straight to the Protais energy fit; the mesh deforms onto the surface, connectivity fixed, nothing added, no shell layer. Whole frees every node, Shell the boundary and one ring. Beats buffer on smooth/graded inputs (tighter boundary gap); inverts on sharp creases, where buffer stays the robust choice.
MarchingCubes::hexahedra — a sampled field
```
MarchingCubes { level, spacing, gradient, .. }.hexahedra(&volume, placement) -> Result<Mesh<3>, _>
```
Meshes the object where a scalar field on a regular grid (Voxels<f64>) reaches its level, with the same clip-and-split, on the same level / spacing / gradient that extract takes and in the same frame as its isosurface. No STL in between.
The samples are the lattice nodes; a crossing is the field interpolated along the edge, held off the ends by the same Crossing(guard).
spacing may differ along each axis. (The Tessellation route is still isotropic.)
Cut only: with no target surface there is nothing to draw onto or fit to. method, step, and degenerate do not apply, and an object reaching the edge of the grid is cut flat there.
Shared case topology, and Method::Separated
The clipping of a cell into polyhedra (clip, loops, pieces, cell, Vertex, CORNERS, FACES) moves out of cut/marching/polyhedron to grid::marching_cubes::separated, and the hexahedral pathway imports it from there. That lets the isosurface use it too:
Method::Separated cuts by the signs at the corners alone, every ambiguous face kept apart, and fans the cut polygons into triangles — so the surface is exactly the boundary of the hexahedra from the same samples. Winding and normals match Lewiner's. It takes every sample (step != 1 is refused).
Lewiner's switch is deliberately not reused: it decides ambiguous faces from the field values and can join them, which is unsound to subdivide (a pinched solid).
Method is a public enum, so this adds a variant — exhaustive matches downstream need an arm.
Supporting pieces
quality/metrics/hexahedron/bernstein — a provable positive-Jacobian certificate for a trilinear hex via its Bernstein coefficients (certifies, coefficients), stronger than corner-sampled scaled Jacobian. pub(crate).
cut/marching/{polyhedron, project, split} — the hex-side internals: which cells to visit, the boundary projection, the midpoint split.
Tessellation::lattice_shifted(spacing, shift) — a jitterable lattice, so marching_hex can retry when a lattice corner lands exactly on the surface.
Mesh::fit widened pub(super) → pub(crate) so Finish::Fit can drive it directly.
MarchingCubes::resolve — the level defaulting / range check, factored out of extract so hexahedra shares it.
Tests
All 256 sign masks split into positive, certified hexahedra; a sphere and a creased star mesh without inversions.
The separated surface is closed and consistently oriented on a sphere and on a field of noise, and keeps a diagonal pair of samples apart (mutation-checked against the ambiguity rule).
The hexahedra hold the volume of a sphere sampled at uneven spacing, conform on a field of noise, and have the separated surface's vertices on their boundary (spacing and interpolation mutation-checked).

Certify hexahedral Jacobians rather than sample them
…
05d4deb

```
The Jacobian determinant of a trilinear map is tri-quadratic, so it
has twenty seven coefficients in the Bernstein basis. Those basis
functions are non-negative, so coefficients that are all positive
prove the determinant is positive throughout the element, where the
corner values the existing metrics take prove only that it is positive
at the corners. A test fixture reaches elements where those corners
agree and the element is inverted regardless.

Sufficient rather than necessary: a negative coefficient is not proof
of an inversion.

Test-only until something consumes it.
```

```
Creased and spiky fixtures, and a count of how many cut cells are
3-regular, which is what decides whether they can be subdivided into
hexes.

Creases fail: a box off axis fails as badly as a stellated sphere, and
an axis aligned one only passes because its creases land on lattice
planes. The dual path fails on the same fixtures, so this is the cut
machinery rather than the lattice.

Of the cut cells fewer than half are 3-regular. Agglomeration accounts
for much of that and snapping for the rest, since a node pulled onto
the surface meets three cell edges there already. Turning both off,
along with the sliver collapse that costs nothing either way, reaches
94%, which is where a midpoint subdivision could take over.
```

Mesh with hexahedra alone by splitting clipped cells
…
8840aca
```
Dhondt's cut, as Protais describes it, and Tong and Zhang's marching
cubes differ only in where a vertex goes on an edge whose ends
straddle the surface, so the two are one pathway with that as a
policy. Midpoint keeps every cell as far from degenerate as it can be;
Crossing follows the surface and is held off the ends instead.

Every cell of the lattice is clipped to what lies inside the surface
and then split about the middles of its edges, faces and body, which
leaves one hexahedron per vertex and meets across cells because every
node is named by the vertices it lies among rather than by position.

The lattice is moved until no corner of it lies on the surface, since
a corner there has no sign, and a lattice on round numbers meets one
readily.

Smooth surfaces come out whole: a sphere gives 39928 hexahedra, none
inverted and every one certified, with no smoothing anywhere. Midpoint
holds a minimum scaled Jacobian of 0.4386 whatever the surface, which
is the combinatorial bound rather than a measurement of it. Creased
and spiky surfaces do not, and want local refinement.
```

Draw the boundary onto the surface without an optimizer

```
Each boundary node is moved toward its closest point on the surface as
far as leaves every hexahedron it belongs to certified and still
holding half the scaled Jacobian it was cut with. Nodes are answered
for one at a time against a check that proves rather than samples, so
nothing settles an energy and nothing waits on convergence.

The share kept is a dial rather than an outcome: the mesh comes out at
exactly that share of what it was cut with.

Bone at 64 divisions, average distance from the surface as a fraction
of a cell: 0.151 to 0.030 for midpoint, 0.033 to 0.005 for crossing.

Quality wants the scaled Jacobian rather than the Bernstein margin,
which a flattened cell holds onto while losing its shape; the
certificate is still what rules out an inversion between the corners.
```

```
The share of quality a cell must keep becomes a parameter beside the
placement, and the two arrive as a Marching with a default: crossings
a fifth of an edge off either end, seven tenths of the quality kept.

Swept against each other on a bone, the two are complements rather
than alternatives. At matched quality a crossing held a fifth off the
ends leaves the boundary four times closer than one held at the middle
and drawn harder, so placing the boundary well and drawing it on are
worth doing both. Quality is better spent through the guard, which
only moves the boundary, than through the share, which costs every
cell.
```

```
Enumerating all 256 sign configurations shows the cells that come out
inverted are the ones whose faces join across an ambiguity and the ones
whose inside corners fall into two pieces sharing a body point. Neither
is the doubly crossed edge the bail in loops watches for, which never
fires. Separating every ambiguous face is the only resolution both cells
sharing it can reach alone, and it leaves the face test with nothing to
decide, so the cast that answered it goes as well.
```

```
regular grid reaches its level, with hexahedra alone, on the same level,

crossing is the field interpolated along the edge (held off the ends by the
```
