Build an interactive, graphically rich explainer of the Navier-Stokes equations on a WebGPU fluid solver, in navier-stokes-explainer/.
Planned chapters:
The terms one at a time: advection, pressure projection, viscosity and forcing, each toggleable on a mouse-stirred field.
A Reynolds number slider running from laminar flow through vortex shedding to turbulence.
2D versus 3D: 2D is globally regular because it has no vortex stretching; colliding vortex rings show stretching in 3D.
A live Beale–Kato–Majda vorticity integral and energy spectrum, so the reader can watch the grid lose resolution.
The Córdoba/Martínez-Zoroa cascade construction and the September 2026 AI blowup results, with a careful account of what was claimed and what is still being verified.
Open question for chapter 5: whether the blowup result is forced or unforced. Sources disagree, so read the primary source before writing it.
Delegated to a sibling Claude Code session spawned from session_01U1S7j9Vts5pem2am2ggCQj. That session posts blockers and questions here.

Shipped in 518e4c7: navier-stokes-explainer/. The page is live at https://austegard.com/experiments/navier-stokes-explainer/web/ once the pages run for this commit finishes; there is also a private Artifact copy at https://claude.ai/artifact/PuZvwbZWNHKZUd3wFJqhCt.
All five chapters run on WGSL compute shaders:
The four terms. A 320 × 180 stable-fluids grid. Advection, pressure, viscosity and forcing each switch off from a button or by clicking the term in the equation, with dye, vorticity, speed and divergence views.
Reynolds number. A cylinder 22 cells across in a channel, with an Re slider from 1 to 1000. Headless, a von Kármán street forms at Re 158.
Stretching in 2D and 3D. A 2D vortex field whose max|ω| trace never rises above its start, next to 3D vortex-filament rings: all-pairs Biot–Savart, RK2, 8,000 smoke tracers, and head-on, tilted and leapfrog presets. Head-on rings reach 3.75× stretch. Each run stops at 4.5× stretch, where the markers stop resolving the core.
Beale–Kato–Majda. A toy plot of the integral with an α slider, plus forced 2D turbulence on a 128², 256² or 512² grid with a live max|ω| trace, the BKM average and an energy spectrum E(k) marked with the grid's N/2 limit.
Cascades and the 2026 results. A cartoon of a layered cascade with a T* timeline, and a draggable (λ, μ) plane that shows the 3D window λ^5/2 ≤ μ ≤ λ² against the 2D line. Below them: the Clay A–D table, a table of each group's claims, a timeline, the open verification questions and sources.
Forced vs unforced is resolved: the result is forced. OpenAI's 166-page manuscript states its theorem as Clay alternative (C), breakdown on ℝ³ with a smooth force, and derives (D) as a corollary. It does not touch the unforced (A) and (B). Quanta's own text calls a smooth forcing function the remaining hurdle, and arXiv:2609.17642 calls it "the OpenAI 2026 forced blow-up construction". openai.com returned 403 to every fetch, so the theorem and corollary numbers come from the lilting.ch explainer, not the manuscript. In Muninn's memory, c43844eb is superseded by edba5729 and anomaly 7ba0692a is retired.
Verification: node run/verify.mjs drives every chapter in headless Chromium on SwiftShader WebGPU and passes 12 of 12 checks (verify.json). One finding went into METHODS.md: a WebGPU canvas in headless Chromium also needs --use-vulkan=swiftshader. Without it the device is lost on the first frame and every readback returns zero.
Not checked: real-GPU frame rates (SwiftShader takes about 3 ms per dispatch, so its timings say nothing about real hardware), and how the page renders inside the Artifact viewer's frame.
