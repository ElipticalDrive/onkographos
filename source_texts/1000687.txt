What just happened?
Using diffuse-specular materials, the glossiness has no effect on reflection sharpness.
Reflections of projector lights (note: NOT the projected texture itself) have always been affected by glossiness, resulting in smoothed reflections and sometimes used to fake environment shine with projector light "environment boxes".
The classic crude environment sky gradient is not affected by the glossiness parameter, which might always have been an inconsistency to begin with, but considering glossiness is meant to behave like the inverse of roughness it doesn't seem correct that it has no effect on proper environment reflections now that they're available. The relationship between apparent surface smoothness and environment reflections is broken up by the environment shine always being fully sharp, which was not an issue when the environment shine was a blurred gradient in the past.
The glossiness does affect the non-environment reflections as expected.
What were you doing when it happened?
Testing legacy materials.
What were you expecting to happen instead?
Glossiness to have some effect for environment reflections.

JIRAUSER341268 commented at 2023-12-20T19:40:18Z
Hi Frionil,
The light model has now changed somewhat since the introduction of GLTF and this behaviour that you're seeing is expected.
"Glossiness" controls the surface roughness of the non-metallic contribution, and "environment" controls the metallic contribution, but there's no control for the roughness of the metallic contribution so it's always roughness 0.
Another way to interpret this is that Glossiness now also uses part of the environment map. And the environment map is now generated from reflection probes.
