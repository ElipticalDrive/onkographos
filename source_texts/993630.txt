Plasma's warm sprite previously cast blue light, while a faint Gauss tracer lit and reflected almost as strongly as Ion Rifle. Ordinary tracers had no reflections at all. Python RTX now describes each weapon as a finite source with independent illumination/reflection gains, reflection width, and support. The original projectile drawing, simulation, and classic renderer are unchanged.
Share source geometry and response controls across wet ground, creature shading, and bonus coatings. Reflection-only tracers retain glints without diffuse spill; artistic rims and thin-part creature transmission follow illumination gain and reach.
Give ballistic/Gauss tracers narrow reflections and an optical fade suited to their visible alpha; restrain Gauss radiance and spill. Match plasma flight/impact colors and core/tail sizes to its artwork. Add restrained Pulse/Shrinkifier sources, localized rocket exhaust, expanding detonations, and spatially grouped flame particles. Match muzzle suppression flags and warm flash artwork.
Select up to 16 sources independently for each response, upload their union (at most 32), and mask unselected channels without double counting. Rank finite segments by their nearest point to the view center. Reflection support no longer inherits the illumination pass's head bias.
Validation:
Full Python suite with native GPU terrain tests: 3,959 passed, 285 skipped; after integrating creature translucency from feat(render): add thin-part translucency to RTX creatures #810, all 761 renderer tests passed, including 120 focused source/material/creature/bonus tests. GPU controls verify that reflection-only points/tracers and sources beyond illumination reach produce zero transmission while retaining specular catches.
Native oracle: 285 passed. Name audit, native game-closure verification, match regressions, Ruff, type checks, docs checks, ast-grep scan/tests, and package build passed.
Native OpenGL before/after renders cover ordinary tracers, a six-shot Gauss fan, plasma, rocket exhaust, flames, blood, creatures, and pickups. Reproduced the reported Survival scenes at ticks 3420 and 7080. Captures use the SDR shoulder.
Selection and flame-cell transitions can still pop under load; temporal smoothing and chain-arc emitters remain follow-ups. Values are art-directed, not calibrated radiometry.
