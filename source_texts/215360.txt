Step 4 of the chitin horn: the SDF horn made into triangles on the CPU, a baked chain of levels of detail, a mesh hash that pins the bits, and sculpt's sweep with the scale any function of arc length.
What's in it
sdf/src/mesher.cone: surface nets on the CPU
surfaceNet(shape, box, cellSize, relax, sparse) SurfaceNet samples any Shape over a box (grown by one cell each way) in cubic cells.
Each crossed cell gets a vertex at the average of its edge crossings, moved relax times along the unit gradient by the distance (clamped to its cell).
Normals are the unit gradient, by central differences, not face normals.
Each grid edge the surface crosses gets one quad, counterclockwise from outside.
A manifold result. Naive surface nets gives a cell one vertex even where the surface passes through it twice, which pinches the mesh. Here a cell gets one vertex per piece of surface in it: the pieces marching cubes would draw there (Nielson's dual marching cubes). The pieces are worked out once for all 256 corner sign patterns, with each face's ambiguous case always separating the inside corners, so neighbouring cells agree.
Sparse sampling. Blocks of 4×4×4 cells whose centre distance exceeds half the block's diagonal (with 1% to spare) are not sampled. This is exact for a 1-Lipschitz field, and the tests confirm it is bit for bit the dense result.
toMesh() cuts each quad along its shorter diagonal. Also added: toPolyMesh(problems), netLevels(shape, box, finest, count, relax), meshHash(&Mesh) u32 and Horn.bounds().
meshHash folds the counts, then the positions' and normals' f32 bits, then the indices, through noise.pcg.
Why sdf and not mesh: the mesher needs Shape, and mesh must not depend on sdf (sdf already depends on mesh through sculpt). Design\mesh.md already put "a scalar-field grid and a polygonizer" in sdf. The output is mesh's Mesh and PolyMesh.
sdf/tests/meshing.cone (123 checks):
Hand-derived cases:
A lone inside sample: 8 vertices, 6 quads, Euler 2, vertex 0 at (−0.1, −0.1, −0.1).
Both ambiguous cases (inside samples diagonal on a face, and across a cell): 16 vertices and two separate closed nets. Naive nets would give 14 vertices and a pinch.
The unit sphere: closed. Relaxed vertices lie within 5e-4 of the surface and normals within 1e-4 of radial.
Sparse against dense: hashes equal, for the sphere and the horn.
The horn: closed, validate clean and Euler 2 at cells of 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.08 and 0.16, relaxed or not.
Pinned hashes.
sdf/examples/hornmesh.cone:
The sdf: signed distance fields -- primitives, smooth operators, fillets, repetition along a curve, the horn #212 horn (Horn.make(3.4, 0.34, 0.97, 1.7, 22, 0.12)) meshed at cells of 0.01, 0.02, 0.04 and 0.08 into render's LodChain. The level is chosen by projected size (500 / 250 / 120 px, hysteresis 0.1), and L forces a level.
Drawn in PbrMaterial.chitin(180, 520, 7) with the thin film, under Sky.dusk and the Environment, then bloomed and tone-mapped with AgX.
The camera, turn and chitin numbers are hornmarch's, so the frames line up.
Flags: --frames N, --screenshot FILE (plus -lod1, -lod2, -lod3 and -nofilm), --obj PREFIX. It also runs a pixel check and an offscreen timing at 1080p.
sculpt: sweepScaled(profile, path, twistAngle, caps, scale &fn(u f32) f32)
The profile at arc length s is scaled by scale(s / L). The fn-ref parameter is last and its parameter is named, as the grammar needs.
sweep now builds its linear scales and shares the ring code, so its output is unchanged. The test checks this bit for bit.
New test: a diamond swept along z in 16 pieces, scaled by 1 + 0.25 sin(8πu). Nine vertex positions were worked by hand: crests at 1.25, troughs at 0.75, and v still s/L.
Also changed: hornmarch's and parity's comments now say SDL3, not SDL2. The package map in CLAUDE.md and packages/sdf/README.md (with a new "Meshing" section) are updated.

Cone gaps
None new. Everything I hit is documented behaviour:
Temporaries can't be borrowed. read(&make()) fails with Error 1016, as reftypesafe.html and refarrayref.html say. The test holds the mesh in a local first (netHash).
&l[i] on a read-only List is refused (Error 1056, whose message says to write l[i]). This was already noted in sculpt.
by, each and local are reserved words, so locals were renamed.
fn-ref parameters: the one new parameter is last and named, as the grammar requires.
Unruled choices (for Jon)
The mesher lives in sdf (reason above), not mesh.
One vertex per piece of surface, which gives a manifold mesh, not naive one-per-cell. The ambiguous face always separates the inside corners, the same rule everywhere.
Sparse sampling: blocks of 4 cells, a 1% margin on the half-diagonal test.
The grid is the box grown by one cell per side, with cubic cells.
Inside means d < 0 (0 counts as outside).
Normals are central differences at h = cell/10 (6 evaluations), not sdf's tetrahedron. The tetrahedron's error is first order: 0.006 on the unit sphere, against 2e-5.
Relaxation is one step along the unit gradient by the distance, clamped to the cell. The example relaxes once.
Quads are cut along the shorter diagonal, the first diagonal on a tie.
meshHash is 32 bits, h = pcg(h ^ word). It includes the normals as well as positions and indices.
Levels: cells of 0.01 / 0.02 / 0.04 / 0.08, switched at 500 / 250 / 120 px, hysteresis 0.1. Each level is meshed from the field, not simplified from the finer one.
sweepScaled passes the normalized arc length u = s/L, not s itself. It takes a function reference, not a sampled curve (neither form carries parameters; the ribbed test hard-codes four ribs).
Horn.bounds() is the union of the cells' balls.
The example draws the mesh alone, framed like hornmarch rather than beside it in one window. hornmarch's full-screen trace doesn't share render's pass, so a side-by-side comparison is by screenshot. Blinn-Phong beside it is step 5's.
The horn test allows at most 4 quads facing against the gradient, and only where the horn's radius is under a cell (its tip). Measured: 1 to 3 at cells of 0.02 to 0.08. Everywhere else every quad faces outward.
The Drive .obj names are mesh-horn-lod*.obj.
Later: dual contouring (sharp edges are rounded here), Transvoxel seams, and threads for the level-0 bake (1–1.4 s on one thread). Level 0's grid bookkeeping is about 70 MB while it meshes.
