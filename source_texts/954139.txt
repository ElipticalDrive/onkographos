Issue body actions
The sky is simulated, with presets, a sun, a moon and seasons, but it has no clouds. An open-world game's sky reads as empty without them, and the target look has them on every quality level.
The change
3D clouds drawn into a sky map. Each frame the clouds are raymarched once, from the camera's position, for every direction above the horizon, into a small texture. The sky pass reads its pixel's direction from the map instead of raymarching, and the sky probe reads it too.
The cost is fixed whatever the screen resolution, and lowest on weak machines.
Nothing is carried between frames, so fast turns don't smear.
One map serves both eyes in VR.
Limits: edges are a little softer than the screen's pixels, and the camera and the world stay below the clouds.
Lit properly from the start. The clouds shade themselves toward the sun and the moon, include an approximation of light bouncing inside the cloud (what makes clouds bright and cottony rather than grey and smoky), and use a cloud phase function for the bright edge near the sun. The sun glows through thin cloud and disappears behind thick cloud. Distant clouds fade into the level's haze.
A varied sky. A weather map opens and closes stretches of sky, groups clouds, and mixes heaped cumulus with flat stratus, so one sky has several kinds of cloud in it.
Clouds that live. The wind carries the clouds, and they change shape as they go: they billow, grow and fade, at a rate the level sets.
A Clouds component on the sun's entity, beside the Skybox.
Presets: Fair, Scattered, Broken, Overcast.
A Custom mode that unlocks coverage, height, thickness, density and puffiness.
The change rate, the wind and a seed work with every preset. The seed gives another sky of the same kind.
A level with no Clouds pays nothing.
Shadows on the ground. Each frame, a small top-down map of how much cloud lies toward the sun. The lit pass reads it once per pixel, so cloud shadows sweep across the land with the wind, sunlight dims under a cloud, and the sun's shadows fade there. An Overcast sky has no hard shadows.
Ambient light follows the clouds: blue under a clear sky, soft and grey under overcast. Calculated on the CPU from the coverage, like the sky's ambient light already is, so it costs nothing per pixel.
A graphics setting. A clouds block in the player's settings: the sky map's resolution and the number of raymarch steps, set by the Low, Medium, High and Ultra tiers. Plus enabled, read only when the game sets playerSetsClouds in game.json, the way fog's is. Off draws no clouds and no cloud shadows; the ambient light still follows the coverage, so the level's lighting doesn't change.
Room for higher-quality settings. The cloud model is one shader include: a density function and the lighting, taking a ray and returning the light it gathers and how much of what lies behind shows through. The sky map is one way of choosing the rays, and everything that shows clouds samples them through one function. Per-pixel clouds (Clouds drawn for every screen pixel, as a higher graphics setting #718) and authored cloud shapes (Clouds can have authored 3D shapes #719) plug into these without changing the cloud model.
Not in this issue:
God rays through gaps in the clouds (God rays: visible beams of sunlight through gaps in clouds, trees and windows #720).
Mid and high cloud layers, such as altocumulus and cirrus (Mid and high cloud layers: altocumulus and cirrus #724).
Storms (Storm clouds: towering cumulonimbus with anvil tops #721), rain and snow (Rain shafts under rain clouds #722), and weather that changes over time (Weather that changes over time #723).
Done when
A level can have clouds, lit by the sun and the moon through the day, and the sky probe sees them.
Cloud shadows move across the ground, and the ambient light follows the coverage.
The player can lower the clouds' quality, and turn them off when the game allows it.
Clouds cost nothing when a level has none.
Agent notes
Rendering roadmap R14. Triage F14.
The HDR-image sky this issue once included is A level's sky can be an HDR image instead of the simulated one #717, not planned for now.
Sky probe: Global sky IBL probe #143.
Methods compared with the user on 2026-11-06: painted textures, 2D and 2.5D layers, billboards, mesh clouds, authored volumetrics, per-pixel raymarching, HDR time-lapse skies, film methods. The sky map was chosen as the base; per-pixel and authored shapes were filed as optional upgrades.
Ambient today: AmbientFromSky in IndirectLighting.hpp averages SkyRadiance samples on the CPU into a sky and a ground colour.
Estimate raised from 4 to 8 once shadows on the ground, ambient and the setting joined the scope.
A first try at mid and high layers looked like brush strokes; the user chose to split them out as Mid and high cloud layers: altocumulus and cirrus #724.
The model follows Nubis (Schneider, Guerrilla): a weather map baked each frame into a 512² texture covering 300 km around the camera, Perlin-Worley shape noise eroded by Worley detail noise, a cloud-type height profile, Jendersie and d'Eon's droplet phase function for single scattering, and Wrenninge's octaves for multiple scattering.
Tiers, measured on an RTX 3070 at 1080p, ship build, CloudCheck level, clouds pass only: Low 512/16 0.18 ms, Medium 1024/24 0.32 ms, High 2048/24 0.82 ms (the default), Ultra 4096/24 2.7 ms. Before optimising, High cost 2.06 ms and Ultra 4.0 ms.
Each ray takes its steps per thickness of the layer crossed, up to three times the steps straight up. With the same steps for every ray, rays along the horizon stepped about a kilometre at a time, and at Ultra this showed as terraced streaks.
