Adds the last four chapters of Part II of the collision detection book (16–19), with 8 new 3D figures.
Chapters
Triangles:
why 3D is made of triangles; normal, area and winding (with Unity's clockwise exception);
point in triangle, using the cross product to give each edge a side; the test also works for points above or below the triangle;
ray vs triangle (with a mention of Möller–Trumbore), closest point on a triangle, sphere vs triangle.
Polygons into triangles:
a flat polygon's normal as the sum of its fan's triangle normals, which is robust for concave shapes and equals Newell's method;
fans for convex polygons, and why they fail on concave ones;
ear clipping: what an ear is, the convex-corner and empty-ear checks, and the two ears theorem;
flattening to 2D instead, and holes via a bridge;
ray and sphere against a polygon or a whole mesh through its triangles, with a note on bounding volume hierarchies.
The separating axis test in 3D:
face normals plus edge × edge axes, and why the edge pairs are needed;
ConvexShape, turned boxes (OrientedBox, axesTurnedAroundY, boxToOriented), triangle vs box;
notes on other convex shapes and GJK.
All the functions in 3D: every Part II function in one file (collision3d.ts/.js/.py) with an at-a-glance complexity table, generated from the chapters' own code blocks.
Figures
Triangle: point, ray and sphere modes. It shows each edge's side test, the ray's hit point on the triangle's plane, and whether the closest point lies on the face, an edge or a corner.
Triangulation: corners drag within the polygon's plane. Fan triangles that leave the polygon turn red; ear clipping steps ear by ear with buttons, and inward corners are hollow.
Separating axis: turned boxes, or a box and a triangle, with sliders. It draws the first separating axis, both shadows on it and a piece of the plane between the shapes. The edge × edge figure starts from a pose found by search, where no face normal separates the boxes.
Scene gained corner controls and a settings row, and Block draws turned boxes too.
Other changes: the front page's "Still to come" line is removed, and the rays chapter now leads into triangles.
Tested
All 114 cases pass in TypeScript, JavaScript and Python. They cover every Part II function, including ear clipping on a notched polygon and a star, polygon normals, the two-sticks pose, and 45°-turned cubes at 2.3 (touching) and 2.5 (apart). The code under test is the same extraction the "All the functions in 3D" chapter is built from.
The extracted TypeScript passes tsc --strict.
Chrome interaction run: stepping through ear clipping (8 corners give 6 triangles), a concave fan getting flagged, the sliders changing the separating axis, the triangle-mode naming, P leaving a triangle, and no sideways scroll on phones for the four new chapters.
npm run check and ESLint are clean, with no KaTeX errors.
