Add optional device-array mesh extraction using Warp 1.19's dense MC, explicit-cell sparse MC, and Lipschitz callback MC. Reconstruction remains independent of meshing.
The default sparse path streams extraction-grid slabs and compacts a crossing-cell bitmask. It retains every crossing cell on the matched dense grid, without assuming an SPSR field is an SDF or that reconstruction support nodes are extraction cells. All sampling and mesh construction stay on device; required allocation/count synchronizations are documented. The callback backend requires an explicit proven Lipschitz bound.
Tracked callbacks and directly specified LevelFields support mesh-vertex gradients, including a tracked device isovalue. Float64 extraction and requests for full reconstruction gradients fail explicitly. This advances #77; #76 and the end-to-end reconstruction-to-mesh chain remain open.
Validation: 387 passing local tests across the full suite and the four restored-fixture checks, plus one expected upstream failure. All 31 public reconstruction gates pass unchanged. The full eagle has exactly matching dense/sparse crossing cells and corner values, equal raw counts, and matching oriented geometric triangles within float32 coordinate rounding. All 1,110 boundary edges lie on the extraction box, with no edge shared by more than two faces. Raw degeneracies, CPU Lewiner differences, field residuals, and all 256 MC cases are covered/documented.
Five isolated warm runs per backend measure 512³ sparse field-to-mesh at 95.6 ms versus 277.7 ms dense, with 309.6 ms for reconstruction plus sparse meshing. Sparse 1024³ takes 1.010 s combined. Peak pools are 2.344/2.392 GiB for sparse versus 7.568 GiB for dense512. A–B–B–A reconstruction-only repeats on sphere, torus, and eagle show no reproducible latency regression and byte-identical pool peaks. docs/meshing.md, the three docs/results/gpu-meshing-*.json reports, and reproducible benchmark/coverage tools record timing scopes and per-run evidence.
