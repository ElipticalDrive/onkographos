What this adds
Probe baking (runtime/lighting/lightBaker.ts)
The World Editor bakes a level's indirect light into a grid of light probes, a few probes per frame in the viewport.
At each probe, a 16 px cube camera sees the level's static geometry and lights. The sky it sees reproduces the scene's ambient, hemisphere and environment fill. Each view is reduced to first-order spherical harmonics.
The reduction runs on the GPU into a small strip that is read back once every 64 probes, instead of six synchronous reads per probe. That made the room test about 8.7× faster (16.5 s → 1.9 s) with the same results.
Bounce 1 lights geometry with the open sky. Each further bounce lights it with the previous bounce, so an enclosed room gets only what came in through its openings.
A front/back-face pass finds probes buried in geometry; they take their neighbours' light.
Shadow maps are fitted to the whole level and drawn once per step.
Static geometry means props, foliage and terrain that never move (no Mover, Rotator or non-fixed Rigid Body on them or their parents), plus instanced layers. Lights on moving objects are left out.
Shading (runtime/lighting/bakedLighting.ts)
Standard, physical, lambert, phong and toon materials read the volume through a shared shader chunk, in place of the uniform sky fill. The volume replaces ambient, hemisphere and the environment's diffuse light; specular reflections stay.
Sampling: hardware-trilinear 3D textures with a normal offset, fading out past the outer probes.
Each scene sets the shared uniforms to its own volume, or none, as it starts drawing. This means World Editor views, play, previews and the model viewer each draw correctly.
Characters and other moving objects take the light of wherever they stand.
Storage and builds
New validated project asset type application/vnd.auvra.lightvolume, with an AUVRALV1 header and 12 float32 per probe. The host checks its header, dimensions, size and finite values.
The asset is referenced from the level's new bakedLighting record (settings plus probes), so it goes into builds with an .alv file extension.
Light Bake Volume component
A box, drawn in the editor, that limits where probes go. Without one, probes cover all static geometry, so a large terrain spreads them thin.
With volumes, probes fill the smallest box around them at the chosen gap, while geometry outside still blocks and bounces light.
Adding, moving or resizing a volume marks the bake out of date.
Editor
The Lighting panel gains a Baked Lighting section: Gap, Bounces, Intensity, Bake (with progress and Cancel), and Clear.
It shows the probe count and spacing, and flags Out of date when static objects, their lights, the sky or the bake settings change.
Intensity changes apply without baking again.
Checked in the editor
Starter Game 3
The bake took about 23 s: 15,463 probes, with spacing widened to 2.56 to stay within the 16,384-probe limit. Before the GPU reduction, it took about 2 minutes for 6,480 probes.
It was saved and reloaded from the project asset with the same values, and drawn in the World Editor and in play.
A closed room with one doorway, baked offscreen in the editor:
Open ground facing up gets about 0.48 / 0.55 / 0.74, close to the unbaked sky fill.
Inside the room is about 4–5× darker.
Near the doorway is brighter than deeper in.
Surfaces facing the red wall get red bounce light (0.097 / 0.026 / 0.031).
320 buried probes were found and filled.
Light Bake Volume
A 20 × 8 × 20 volume in Starter Game 3 gave 605 probes at the requested 2.00 spacing, baked in about 5 s.
I then removed it and baked the whole level again.
No errors
No console errors while baking, after a reload, or in play mode.
Fix found along the way
The material preview canvas is now in the renderer's stable surface list (also pushed to Node-based material editor #68, where CI had failed on it).
Tests
Frontend:
bakedLighting (settings, asset round trip, grid, hole filling, which objects and lights the bake sees, signature, shader install, per-scene volume stack)
the scene, navigation, hydration and level persistence tests
Host: test_light_volume (asset validation, MIME sniffing, schema, build extension), plus the splat map, template build and material override tests.
Checks: tsc, tsc with the strict config, the diagnostics, renderer and frame checks, and the editor and player builds.

The Materials tab lists the project's material assets and edits each
one as a node graph: a palette of input, UV, texture, surface, math and
vector nodes, typed pins with conversions, click-to-remove wires, a
loop check, a settings panel for the selected node and a live preview
sphere. Edits save on their own.

then shaded by the graph wherever the model draws. Every canvas runs
one shared material clock, so Time nodes animate in each viewport, in
play and in builds.

it in the same project change, and deleting a texture clears it from
the graph nodes that sample it, so neither leaves a reference the
project would refuse.
