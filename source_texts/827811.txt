What and why
The first PR of the volumetric-look stack (separate from the erosion stack; based on #18 as asked). The water was a tinted sheet whose colour came from the simulation's depth alone, so a pool looked like a stream. Now it reads as a body of water: what lies behind it is seen through it, and the more water its light crosses, the more it takes the water's colour. The game renders deferred and gives the camera a depth texture (checked in game: DeferredShading, Depth, MotionVectors), so the shader knows how far behind the surface the bed is.
Thickness: from the scene's depth behind the surface, capped at the water's depth over the viewing angle, so a missing depth texture or sky behind a fall stays sane.
Absorption: red goes first and blue last (Beer–Lambert, per channel). The light crosses the water twice, the sun's way down to the bed and back up to the eye, because the game lit the bed as if it were dry. The water's own colour fills in what is lost. 15% of that colour shows even where the water is thin (_SurfaceTint), so a film still reads as water.
Refraction: the scene is grabbed once a frame (GrabPass), bent by the ripples, more through more water and less far off. It never bends onto something standing in front of the water. The grab uses its own coordinates (ComputeGrabScreenPos), since the grabbed image is upside down on some platforms.
Settings, applied live: WaterClarity (how far one sees into the water, 4.5 m) and WaterColour (RGBA hex, 081C24FF). WaterLook.Absorption is pure and unit-tested. flowingwater mat custom now sets vector properties as vectors (a colour would be gamma-converted).
Smoothness 0.92 (was 0.8): the sun glints off the ripples in sparkles instead of a pale beige sheen, which hid deep water from above.
Recorder: a prep scenario readies an editor test world: it generates the zones at given points and digs once there, so the editor may make new ground. The dig grid also had a bug that dug nothing at a small radius. A pools scenario fills each pool of a test world from a spring sized to its volume, then films it from the side and from above in every look. The look-variant parsing is shared with look.
Where it shows
Server simulation
Client (drawing, swimming, current)
Docs only
How it was tested
4 new unit tests (clarity gives the mean absorption, red > green > blue, the default matches clear fresh water, clearer absorbs less and zero clarity stays finite); 80 in all pass. Bundles built for Windows and Linux with Unity 6000.0.75f1; Unity preview rendered.
In game (real game hidden, local server), on a test world made with the World Editor: trees cleared and four pools dug (3 m wide and 1 m deep, 5 m/2 m, 8 m/4 m, 12 m/7.5 m), each filled by a spring, then filmed from the side and from above:
Default, murky (clarity 1.5 m) and clear (12 m) looks.
Smoothness 0.8, 0.92 and 0.96.
Shallow rims stay clear and show their banks. The deep ponds turn blue-green and then dark in the middle, and murky and clear differ as expected. No exceptions in the game or server logs.
