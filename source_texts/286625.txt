Two commonly used meshfree approximations are first presented in this chapter: the moving least squares (MLS) and the reproducing kernel (RK). They are compactly supported and can achieve arbitrary orders of continuity and completeness. No element connectivity is needed, and MLS and RK can be constructed entirely based on a scattered set of points. A unique property of these approximations is that they rely only on the global Cartesian coordination without needing to map to the local parametric coordinate as in the conventional finite element shape function construction. As a result, MLS and RK are naturally conforming, allowing arbitrary smoothness without the burden of enforcing continuity along the element boundaries. This property also makes adaptive refinement procedures in a meshfree discretization relatively effortless, which is in stark contrast to the finite element where the imposition of element boundary compatibility in adaptive mesh refinement is required. This chapter also discusses the mathematical properties of these approximations and their convergence in solving partial differential equation. Various efficient ways of computing the derivatives of MLS and RK functions are introduced, such as diffuse, synchronized, and implicit gradients, and their relation to the generalized finite difference method and state-based peridynamics with correspondence are discussed.
