Abstract
Traditional 3D reconstruction methods are difficult to reconstruct accurately because of the lack of sufficient visual information when dealing with areas with no texture or low texture. In this paper, a 3D shape reconstruction method based on neural field (NF) is proposed. This method uses plane cross-section data as input, aiming at solving the problem of 3D reconstruction in textureless areas and improving the accuracy and robustness of reconstruction. By combining the powerful representation ability of NF with plane cross-section data, this study can effectively capture finer geometric details and global consistency. Our method includes three main steps: preprocessing plane section data, constructing NF model and optimizing it. In the preprocessing stage, the high-resolution camera is used to shoot the target object from multiple angles, extract the plane cross-section information, and carry out data cleaning, cross-section correction, feature extraction and data standardization. In the stage of NF model construction, a deep neural network is constructed to learn to map points in 3D space to corresponding color values and density values, which implicitly represents the whole 3D scene. In the optimization stage, the plane constraint is introduced to optimize the geometric shape by combining Manhattan world hypothesis and implicit neural representation. The experimental results show that this method is superior to traditional methods on multiple data sets, especially in the reconstruction of texture-free areas and complex scenes, showing higher accuracy and robustness. This study provides a new solution for 3D reconstruction technology, especially when dealing with texture-free areas and complex scenes, and provides more accurate and realistic 3D models for augmented reality, virtual reality and other applications.
