Affine.Simplex.exists_pos_ray_mem_face
Affine.Simplex.exists_ray_exit (lines 45–97) supplies the same exact nonnegative closed interval in every simplex dimension, and additionally the exact interior interval. At its positive endpoint, closed membership and failure of interior membership give an opposite face by Mathlib's existing Simplex.closedInterior_sdiff_interior. Main's triangle proof performs precisely this boundary extraction at lines 118–125; the boundary identity itself applies to any positive-dimensional simplex, as required here.
Affine.Simplex.exists_pos_ray_mem_unique_face
Affine.Triangle.exists_ray_exit_edge (lines 109–159) supplies the unique edge at a positive parameter under the same positive-ray vertex avoidance, additionally proving edge-interior membership and excluding every other edge along the whole nonnegative ray. Combine it with Affine.Simplex.exists_ray_exit for the exact closed interval. Their exit parameters agree: edge membership forces the edge parameter to be at most the interval endpoint; a strict inequality would put it in the simplex interior, disjoint from the edge. Thus the unique-face result and interval hold at the same parameter, exactly as in #294.
The point-pair and velocity formulations have the same scope: take q = v +ᵥ p. The interior hypothesis puts p in the affine span; the vector-span hypothesis puts v in its direction, hence q is in that affine span. v ≠ 0 gives q ≠ p, and AffineMap.lineMap_apply with vadd_vsub identifies lineMap p q a with a • v +ᵥ p. Vertex avoidance consequently translates without strengthening. Both versions use arbitrary affine torsors over the same ordered-field assumptions, without topology or ambient dimension assumptions. Main's general theorem also covers more simplex dimensions. The remaining differences are theorem names, this equivalent ray parameterization, and duplicated proof implementations; no additional mathematical result remains.
