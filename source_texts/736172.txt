Stacked on #40 (merge train position 20/32).
Summary
Closes SIZE-12 (checklist §2, P2): heavily downscaled images aliased.
Image textures had one level and a bilinear sampler. When an image was drawn much smaller than its source, each screen pixel sampled a single 2×2 neighbourhood, and fine detail either vanished or turned into moiré. The checklist case is a 1500×900 grid pattern drawn at c=30: its vertical lines were gone and its horizontal lines came out as irregular stripes.
Now a texture drawn downscaled gets the mip levels that draw samples, and the sampler is plain trilinear.
Images drawn at 1:1 or larger never get mips. They cost nothing extra, and they render pixel-identically to before (magick compare -metric AE = 0 on the 1:1 and 2× images below, and the 1:1 crop matches the source PNG byte for byte).
The work is bounded per frame (see design notes), so a thumbnail grid can't stall a frame.
Review round 1 (see the reply on the PR for the item-by-item list):
only the needed levels are built;
small textures are built on the CPU;
mip work is capped per frame and spread over later frames;
odd sizes use 3-tap weights;
queued work is dropped with its slot;
a missing encode call is fail-safe and debug-asserted;
a −0.5 LOD bias was tried and then removed in round 2 (below).
The flat tint at c=30 (divergence, acknowledged by the PM)
kitty's grid at c=30 is itself aliasing.
150 grid lines across 1500 px, drawn into about 270 px, gives a true pitch of 1.8 px, which can't be displayed.
kitty's visible lines are at x = 6, 15, 24, 33, 42, 60, 69, …: a 9 px pitch, i.e. every 5th source line, skipping one where the phase slips.
A correct prefilter turns a sub-2-px grid into an even tint, which is what toastty draws. This is recorded in the checklist.
At c=60 (4 px pitch) toastty shows an even grid in both directions.
Design notes
Levels per draw. levels = min(full, floor(log2(ratio)) + 2), where ratio is the larger of source px / drawn px on each axis. Trilinear reads floor(lod) and the next level.
A ½-scale video frame needs 3 levels (2 passes, previously 9); a 128 px thumbnail drawn at 20 px needs 4.
A later, smaller draw adds levels by rebuilding the texture in place, with the same texture_index.
The byte budget counts the levels actually allocated.
Where levels are built.
Textures up to 256² texels build on the CPU (image/mip.rs::cpu_levels) and are uploaded with write_texture, so thumbnails need no render passes. The CPU path uses premultiplied linear-light floats, and the first level is reduced straight from the bytes. A 128² image to 4 levels takes about 0.13 ms at load average 67.
Larger textures use one GPU pass per level (shaders/mip.wgsl), queued and then encoded into the frame's own command encoder by encode_pending_mips.
Both paths use the same filter, and a test reads back the GPU levels and checks them against the CPU ones.
Per-frame cap (MipLimits): 16 GPU passes, 256K CPU texels, and 4 ms of wall-clock CPU time. At least one image always progresses each frame.
An image over the cap draws from the levels it already has (level 0 at worst) and reports ResolveStats::deferred.
The renderer then keeps frames coming: through next_redraw_deadline (16 ms), past the idle skip gate, as full redraws, until nothing is deferred.
A level is bound only once it's filled. The bind group's view covers 0..levels_ready.
CPU levels become ready immediately. GPU levels become ready in encode_pending_mips.
So if that call were dropped, images would only lose quality, never sample empty levels. render() also debug_asserts that no unencoded GPU work is pending, and a test locks that assertion.
Queued GPU work is dropped when its slot is released or replaced, so skipped frames (Outdated, Timeout, Occluded) can't pile up work or keep dead textures alive.
Filter.
It is alpha-weighted, because kitty images are straight alpha.
It runs in linear light: Rgba8UnormSrgb on the GPU, lookup tables on the CPU.
Even axes use a 2×2 box. Odd axes use the 3-tap footprint (n−i, n, i+1)/(2n+1) over 2i … 2i+2, which preserves each source texel's weight. The reviewer's 1023-wide case now keeps its coverage at every level (unit test).
No LOD bias (review round 2).
A −0.5 bias sharpened thin lines but brought aliasing back at moderate downscales. On the reviewer's zone plate, the residual that should be flat rose from 20.9 to 41.2 at 1.5× and from 17.5 to 27.9 at 2×; Lanczos gives 23.3 and 14.8.
Plain trilinear stays slightly soft: a 1 px diagonal widens to about 3 px at c=30. That trade-off is recorded in the checklist. A bias of at most −0.25 would only be considered after re-measuring.

Review of #34 (SIZE-12 mipmaps): Changes requested
The mip generation is correct and the visual result is a clear improvement. 1:1 stays byte-exact. The shader validates on every backend. The blocking problem is cost: building a chain takes one render pass per level, and with many downscaled images, or downscaled re-sends, that is far more expensive than the PR's timing section says. Details and numbers are below.
Issues
1. should-fix (perf): the first draw of many downscaled images stalls for hundreds of ms to seconds.

File-manager previews (yazi, ranger), timg --grid and kitten icat galleries all hit this path.
Suggested fix (any of these, ideally the first two):
Build only the levels the draw needs: levels = min(full_mip_count, floor(log2(max(src_w/box_w, src_h/box_h))) + 2). Rebuild in place when the image is later drawn smaller; the existing upgrade path already handles that, with a mip_levels < needed test in place of == 1. A 128→20 px thumbnail then needs 4 levels (3 passes) instead of 8 (7 passes). A ½-scale 640×360 video frame needs 3 levels (2 passes) instead of 10 (9 passes).
Cap the mip passes per frame, e.g. about 64. Images over the cap draw single-level this frame and upgrade on a later one, so no single frame is unbounded.
For small textures (say ≤ 256²), box-filter on the CPU and write_texture every level. That needs no render passes and no RENDER_ATTACHMENT usage.

3. nit (mip correctness, low impact): odd sizes drop the last row or column, which shifts content toward the top-left at heavy downscale.
crates/toastty-render/shaders/mip.wgsl:28-33.
The level-n texel i averages source [i·2ⁿ, (i+1)·2ⁿ), but UV maps level n across the full width. So every odd step loses up to one texel of that level at the right and bottom edges.
Measured live: a 1023×1023 image whose right 25% is red. At c=1 the red coverage in linear light is 1.63 px against an ideal 2.50 (35% lost). At c=2 it is 4.13 against 5.00. The same image at 1024 wide is exact: 2.50 and 5.00.
Typical images are only slightly affected: for 1500×900 at c=30 the loss is under 0.3%. It is still a systematic bias of up to about 1 output px on images like 1023 or 511 wide.
Fix: when the source dimension is odd, use the standard 3-tap footprint for that axis. With n = dst_dim, the weights are (n-i)/(2n+1), n/(2n+1) and (i+1)/(2n+1) over source texels 2i, 2i+1 and 2i+2. Alpha-weight them as now.
4. nit (robustness): pending_mips can grow while frames are skipped, and it keeps released textures alive.
pipeline.rs:67 and :667, and lib.rs:3049.
resolve_kitty_textures runs before get_current_texture(), but encode_pending_mips runs only after a successful acquire.
On Outdated, Timeout or Occluded, a re-send at a downscale still pushes a full-chain texture clone that the byte budget doesn't count. If that repeats over a run of skipped frames (for example a hidden Hyprland workspace with FIFO acquire timeouts), the list grows. The next good frame then encodes passes for textures that were long since replaced.
Fix: drop the pending entry when its slot is released or replaced. One way is to store (texture_index, Texture) and retain in release_texture and in the in-place rebuild. Another is to check at encode time that self.slots[idx] still holds that texture.
5. nit (test gap): nothing locks the call at lib.rs:3049.
Every new GPU test calls encode_pending_mips itself through render_offscreen. If the call in render_term were dropped or moved after the image passes, all tests would still pass.
At runtime, though, downscaled images would sample wgpu's lazily zero-initialised levels, and would fade or vanish whenever they are drawn below 1:1.
Fix: a renderer-level GPU test: render_term with one downscaled placement, then assert that the image region isn't background.
6. nit (quality): trilinear overblurs thin features.
At c=30 and c=60 the 1-px diagonal of the pattern becomes a grey band about 3 px wide (see the zooms below). This is the softness the PR acknowledges.
If it matters later, textureSampleBias(.., -0.5) on instances flagged as downscaled, or anisotropic filtering, would sharpen it. It doesn't block this PR.
