In an era increasingly dominated by immersive digital experiences and stream-able content, the ability to reconstruct and visualize 3D scenes from two-dimensional images has become increasingly popular and necessary. Although humans are generally able to perceive depth, spatial relationships and view-dependent effects from limited visual observation, enabling computers to achieve similar understanding and photorealistic rendering capabilities like the human eye represents one of the most challenging problems in the field of Computer Vision. The advancement and iterative improvement of deep learning methodologies have led to approaches like NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) which are able to generate high quality novel view synthesis from limited input images. This project evaluates the performance of NeRF and recent voxel-based approaches, specifically analyzing PlenOctrees and Plenoxels to determine their strengths and limitations in practical scenarios. We propose a method that extends Plenoxels by integrating monocular depth estimation for geometry-informed sparse grid initialization with per-voxel adaptive learning rates. This approach achieves statistically significant reduction in floater artifacts while maintaining comparable image quality metrics, offering a practical and extensible pathway for improving neural-free radiance field methods.Bachelor's degre
