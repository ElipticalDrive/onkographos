Deep-learning based porosities segmentation applied to twin robotic CT images
Julie Escoda1, Marius Costin1, Victor Bussy1
Université Paris-Saclay, CEA, List, Palaiseau, France, e-mail: julie.escoda@cea.fr

Abstract
This work stands in the framework of twin robotic Computed Tomography. Such type of acquisition allows the possibility of
new viewing angles included in the set of reconstructed projections, compared to classical circular acquisition. However, due
to the size and shape of the measured objects as well as the kinematic constraints of the robots, not all viewing angles can be
accessed. For many applications, generating sufficient information for a mathematically correct and reliable reconstruction is
therefore not possible. Reconstruction with classical algorithms then leads to artifacts corresponding to Sparse-View
acquisition (streak artifacts) and limited angle acquisition. The aim of this work is to evaluate the benefit of a Deep-Learning
based algorithm for 3D porosities segmentation on CT data acquired with a twin robotic CT system, compared to classical
image segmentation. Classical SART algorithm is chosen for the reconstruction. In order to use supervised approaches for the
segmentation task, we build a database using CIVA software. The database is used to train a 3D UNet based algorithm for
segmentation.
Keywords: twin robotic CT, segmentation, Deep-Learning, simulation, sparse-view, limited angle CT.
1 Introduction
Twin robotic Computed Tomography (CT) systems represent an alternative to classical CT systems where the X-ray source and Xray detector are mounted on six-axis robots [1]. As such, they allow flexible movement of source and detector and the access
to viewing angles which are not possible with traditional circular scanning trajectories. Twin robotic CT systems are therefore able
to reach better CT image quality for a given number of projections. In order to generate enough information for a 3D
reconstruction of any area of an object, X-rays must pass through that area from different viewing angles. However, due to the
size and shape of the measured objects as well as the kinematic constraints of the robots, not all viewing angles can be accessed.
For many applications, generating sufficient information for a mathematically correct and reliable reconstruction is therefore not
possible. Reconstruction with classical algorithms then leads to artifacts corresponding to Sparse-View acquisition (streak
artifacts) and limited angle acquisition.
The aim of this work is to evaluate the benefit of a Deep-Learning based algorithm for porosities segmentation on CT data
acquired with a twin robotic CT system, compared to classical image segmentation. More specifically, we consider the
acquisition of a toroidal propeller. Classical SART [9] algorithm is chosen for the reconstruction; indeed, it allows the
reconstruction of data on non-circular trajectories. Furthermore, we chose to focus in this study on the evaluation of the
segmentation task, independentely to the use of more efficient reconstruction algorithm [4-6].
In order to use supervised approaches for the segmentation task, we build a database using CIVA software [7]. It allows us to
simulate the tomography acquisition process, including the X-Ray source, detector, object materials and shapes, as well as
possible flaws. We simulate tomography images for the propeller, and with varying porosities (in shape, size and position). In
order to make the database more realistic, we include variations on the propeller orientation. The database is used to train a 3D
UNet [8] based algorithm for segmentation.
In Section 2, we will present the simulated data that we will use for the method training and evaluation. Then the algorithm will
be described in Section 3 and finally results will be given in Section 4.
2 Data
We consider in this paper the acquisition of CT scans for a three-blades aluminum propeller (see Fig. 1). The diameter of the
propeller is 72mm. It contains porosities of ellipsoidal shapes which are randomly located inside seven distinct areas. The
three semi-axis of each ellipsoid follows a uniform distribution between 0.2mm and 0.5mm.

The dataset is simulated using CIVA software [7] developed by CEA List. The datasetcontains 15 scans. For each scan, the
orientation of the propeller, as well as position and ellipsoids sizes, are randomly generated. Cone-beam acquisition is
simulated, with a source-axe distance of 250mm and an axe-detector distance of 250mm. The detector is of size 1024*1024
pixels, and the pixel size is of 0.2mm. Three acquisition set-ups are considered: sparse-view circular set-up, limited angle
view circular set-up and spherical set-up. Spherical set-up describes here an acquisition where the source and the detector
are located at a fixed distance of the object, so describing a path on a sphere. Moreover, during spherical acquisition, the
source direction remains perpendicular to the dectector plane and pointing to the dectector center.
The sparse-view set-up is the following (see Fig. 2): the propeller is set on a stand so that it has an angle of 20° with respect
to the acquisition plane. 30 projections are acquired between 0 and 348°. The reconstruction is performed on a grid of size
686*296*730 voxels and resolution 0.1mm.
Figure 2: Description of the simulated sparse-view acquisition set-up (on the left) and one slice of the reconstructed 3D image (on the right).
The limited angle view set-up is the following (see Fig. 3): the propeller is set on a stand so that it has an angle of 20° with
respect to the horizontal plane. 60 projections are acquired between 0 and 118°. The reconstruction is performed on a grid
of size 679*296*723 voxels and resolution 0.1mm.

For each of these three set-ups, 5 tomography acquisitions are simulated and reconstructed. For the reconstruction, SART
algorithm is used with 5 iterations and no regularisation. Indeed, SART method allows the reconstruction on the spherical setup. Moreover, we chose in this work to use a classical method for reconstruction and focus on the porosities segmentation.
For each 3D reconstructed image, a ground truth image for the segmentation is generated with the knowledge of porosities shape
and position, combined with the information about the reconstructed zone and its discretisation.
This set of 15 images is separated into three datasets: train dataset, validation dataset and test dataset. The train dataset
consists of 3 images of each set-up (sparse-view, limited angle view and spherical set-up), while validation and test datasets
each incorporate 1 image of each set-up.
3 Method
3.1 Network description
For our segmentation task, we use a UNet [8] based architecture trained with the data presented in Section 2.

More precisely, we use a 4 levels UNet architecture with skip connections. Each level consists in a 3D double convolution with
a kernel size of 2 with batch normalization and SILU activation. The UNet step is followed by a residual layer made of two 3D
convolutions with kernel of size 3.
Segmentation networks operate as a classification on pixels (voxels in the case of 3D). In our case, given the cumulated volume of
porosity relatively to the total volume of the image, the two classes involved (porosity/non-porosity) have very different
occurrences and the database is unbalanced. In order to take into account this unbalanced database, we use a focal loss for the
training step.
3.2 Training step
For the training step, we use random patches extracted from the simulated images from the training database. Patches are of size
(64, 32, 64) in order to have large patches enough to get relevant information through a 4 levels UNet. The second dimension is
reduced in order to take into account the limited second dimension in some of the 3D data (data from the spherical setup).
Patches are extracted at random positions including part of the propeller. Higher probability of selection is set for patches
including porosities, in order to limit the imbalance between porositie and non-porositie classes, while also including training
patches without porosities.
Last, training step is performed on patches using AdamW optimizer and a learning rate of 0.001.

5 Conclusion
In this work, we presented a supervised method for 3D porosity segmentation, and applied it to an image of a three-blades
propeller acquired in the framework of twin robotic Computed Tomography. This work will be completed in a future work by
an extensive quantitative evaluation, including a comparison to other methods. Application to real data will also be
performed. Finally, in the context of robotic acquisition, the choice of acquired projections is crucial, ans we will compare
the result we obtain in this paper to the one obtained when using projections determined with dedicated methods (see [2,
3]).
