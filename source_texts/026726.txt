Abstract
Introduction
Although 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) supports real-time
rendering, training relies on offline geometry, long-horizon
densification, and memory-intensive differentiation. We cast
post-capture static-scene training as resource-constrained
finite-horizon optimization, where terminal quality depends
jointly on the initial representation and reference-consistent
gradient execution. To the best of our knowledge, PocketGS
is the first publicly documented pipeline to complete geometry construction, Gaussian initialization, and 3DGS optimization on a commodity smartphone. Its I ◦G warm start combines refined poses, a filtered dense prior, and surface-aligned
anisotropic Gaussians. Its T operator replays compositing
interactions and scatters gradients to canonical parameters,
matching a numerical reference at the reported precision. Controlled 500-step experiments on LLFF, Mip-NeRF 360, and
MobileScan show high-quality reconstruction at the device
horizon. Across MobileScan, every scene remains below 3 GB
peak memory and post-capture processing averages 255.2 s,
enabling an on-device capture-to-render workflow. Separate
30k experiments characterize native-schedule behavior.
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al. 2023) combines high-fidelity scene modeling with real-time rendering for mixed reality, digital twins, and robotics. Its explicit, rasterization-friendly primitives replace implicit neural fields (Mildenhall et al. 2021) and motivate complete 3D reconstruction on commodity mobile devices. Ondevice processing benefits privacy-sensitive capture, poorconnectivity field work, and interactive AR requiring immediate feedback without uploading images or poses.
Despite efficient inference, 3DGS training assumes a workstation regime: offline SfM (Schönberger and Frahm 2016)
supplies its input, long-horizon densification discovers scene
structure, and differentiable rendering retains substantial intermediate state. Mobile time, memory, and thermal limits
constrain both the number and cost of updates. Shortening
the desktop schedule leaves weak geometry and initialization
insufficiently corrected, while transferring its backward pass
causes excessive memory traffic.
Recent work accelerates optimization (Mallick et al. 2024;
Chen et al. 2025; Hanson et al. 2025), supports mobile ren-
dering and pruning (Lin, Feng, and Zhu 2025; Du et al.
2026; Sanim et al. 2025), or addresses large-scale deployment (Liu et al. 2025), but still relies on offline reconstruction
or workstation-class training. To the best of our knowledge,
PocketGS is the first publicly documented 3DGS pipeline
to complete geometry construction, initialization, and optimization entirely on a commodity smartphone. We study
post-capture reconstruction of static scenes rather than online
SLAM or cross-device deployment.
This raises a central question: how should the initial
representation and gradient execution be co-designed so
that 3DGS reaches high-quality reconstruction within
a device-determined number of updates and bounded
memory? We view this as resource-constrained finitehorizon optimization. Under an update horizon K, the objective is reconstruction quality at the deployed iterate ΘK ,
subject to end-to-end time and peak-memory constraints,
rather than only long-horizon performance.
This perspective identifies representation formation and
reference-consistent gradient execution as coupled optimization variables: scene structure must be introduced before
the short horizon, while each update must remain affordable without approximating its gradient. For the former, mobile captures provide noisy poses and sparse geometry from
on-device VIO (Qin, Li, and Shen 2018); standard 3DGS
seeds isotropic Gaussians and relies on prolonged densification to recover structure (Kerbl et al. 2023). With small K,
the starting state must instead provide reliable spatial support and encode local surface geometry before optimization.
For the latter, differentiable splatting requires intermediate
compositing states, although mobile GPUs expose only final
blended outputs. Readback, full-cache maintenance, or recomputation can make each update prohibitively expensive.
PocketGS addresses these variables through three coupled operators. The geometry operator G refines poses and
constructs a compact dense prior, reducing reliance on inloop densification. The initialization operator I maps local
surface statistics to surface-aligned anisotropic Gaussians,
reducing scale and rotation correction during early updates.
Together, I ◦G provides the geometry-informed warm start
Θ0 . The training operator T unrolls differentiable compositing, replays valid interactions, and maps their gradients to
canonical parameters. It preserves reference gradients at the
reported precision while reducing mobile memory traffic.
This joint design targets ΘK under one shared device budget rather than optimizing preprocessing, initialization, and
training independently.
Our main contributions are:
• A geometry-informed warm-start design for finitehorizon 3DGS optimization: GPU-native pose/point refinement and single-reference MVS construct a compact
dense prior, while surface-aligned covariance seeding
moves structure formation before the iteration-limited optimization stage.
• A hardware-aligned differentiable splatting operator that
replays valid-interaction state and resets only valid-entry
counters, reducing mobile memory traffic while matching
the numerical reference at the reported precision.
• To the best of our knowledge, PocketGS is the first publicly documented end-to-end 3DGS training pipeline to
run fully on a commodity smartphone under strict runtime
and memory constraints, enabling on-device capture-torender workflows.
Experiments on LLFF, Mip-NeRF 360, and MobileScan
show that PocketGS produces high-quality reconstructions
under its intended 500-step training budget. Every evaluated MobileScan scene remains below 3 GB peak memory,
a representative post-capture run takes approximately four
minutes, and T matches the numerical reference at the reported precision. Native-schedule workstation experiments
separately characterize long-horizon behavior.
Related Work
Novel View Synthesis and Gaussian Splatting
Novel view synthesis (Tewari et al. 2022; Gao et al. 2022)
progressed from neural radiance fields (Mildenhall et al.
2021; Barron et al. 2021, 2022) and explicit grids or
hashes (Fridovich-Keil et al. 2022; Sun, Sun, and Chen 2022;
Müller et al. 2022; Barron et al. 2023; Guo, Wang, and Chen
2025) to real-time 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al. 2023;
Fang and Wang 2024a,b). Subsequent work improves SLAM
integration, geometry, and optimization (Peng et al. 2024;
Yan et al. 2024; Wenzhi et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024; Chen
et al. 2024; Höllein et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2025; Fang and
Wang 2025), but generally retains offline SfM (Triggs et al.
1999; Schönberger and Frahm 2016) and assumes poses and
an initial point set. PocketGS instead places pose refinement
and dense-prior construction inside on-device training.
Efficient and Mobile Gaussian Splatting
Efficient 3DGS reduces training or memory through pruning
and adaptive optimization (Mallick et al. 2024; Lan et al.
2025). EDGS and Turbo-GS study densification, initialization, and resolution trade-offs (Kotovenko, Grebenkova, and
Ommer 2025; Lu et al. 2024); RAIN-GS handles inaccurate
clouds (Jung et al. 2024), and Mini-Splatting2 accelerates
workstation training (Fang and Wang 2024b). These accelerators retain workstation execution or offline inputs. MobileGS targets mobile rendering kernels (Du et al. 2026), with
extensions to larger deployments and mobile perception (Liu
et al. 2025; Liu, Shi, and Yang 2025). VIO/MVS scanning
stacks (Picard et al. 2023; Qin, Li, and Shen 2018; Yao et al.
2018; Kim et al. 2022; Feigl et al. 2020; Mur-Artal and
Tardós 2017) and mobile neural-rendering systems (Chen
et al. 2023; Rojas et al. 2023) provide geometry or inference, but do not jointly connect mobile geometry recovery to
trainable 3DGS primitives and device-side backpropagation.
PocketGS couples prior construction, Gaussian initialization,
and backpropagation within one phone resource envelope.

Prior-Conditioned Gaussian Parameterization
Standard 3DGS initializes isotropic Gaussians from interpoint distances, leaving local surface scale and rotation to
be recovered during optimization. Under a short K, these
corrections consume available updates. I instead maps P to
a surface-aligned anisotropic Θ0 , providing spatial support,
scale, and rotation before optimization and supporting rapid
early convergence within the fixed horizon.

misaligning optimizer states. We therefore decouple the
sorted view (rendering/backprop) from the canonical layout (parameters and optimizer moments). A GPU sort yields
a permutation π mapping sorted index i to canonical index
π(i); kernels operate on the sorted view while gradients are
scattered back as

memory. PocketGS thus co-designs Θ0 and the K referenceconsistent updates yielding the deployed iterate ΘK .
Experiments
Platform and budget. PocketGS is implemented in Swift
on Apple Metal and runs entirely on an iPhone 15 (Apple
A16, 6 GB unified memory). We evaluate two complementary regimes. The controlled 500-step study matches image
sets, held-out splits, resolution, metrics, and optimization
steps while retaining each system’s stated preprocessing and
initialization; densification and pruning are disabled to evaluate terminal quality at the finite horizon K = 500. For PocketGS, this lies within its intended five-minute post-capture
compute budget, measured from loaded inputs to the trained
model. The 30k workstation study retains every optimizer’s
native density-control and scheduling mechanisms to characterize long-horizon behavior. The former reports geometry, initialization, and optimization time; the latter reports
training-script wall-clock time and excludes geometry and
initialization-data generation. Full protocol details are provided in the supplement. Unless noted otherwise, quantitative
results report means over five runs.
Baselines. Geometry baselines feed COLMAP SfM
(3DGS-SFM-WK) or dense MVS (3DGS-MVS-WK) into
vanilla 3DGS (Schönberger and Frahm 2016). Training
baselines use the same SfM prior with Mobile-GS (Du
et al. 2026), DashGaussian (Chen et al. 2025), Taming3DGS (Mallick et al. 2024), FastGS (Ren et al. 2025),
and Speedy-Splat (Hanson et al. 2025). COLMAP uses
two RTX 3090 GPUs; PocketGS instead co-designs its prior
and initialization. Runtime reports per-system latency on
the stated hardware; we do not use cross-platform time for
speedup rankings.
Datasets and metrics. We evaluate on Mip-NeRF
360 (Barron et al. 2022), LLFF (Mildenhall et al. 2019),
and our phone-captured MobileScan. We uniformly downsample LLFF by 32× and Mip-NeRF 360 by 16×; the latter
uses 100 frames per scene. MobileScan contains 16 varied
scenes, each with 50 smartphone keyframes and ARKit intrinsics, poses, and sparse structure; every eighth frame is
held out. Image sets, resolutions, held-out splits, metrics,
and step counts are matched across methods; geometry and
pose sources follow each stated preprocessing pipeline, and
public splits follow 3DGS (Kerbl et al. 2023). Public benchmarks provide the primary cross-method evidence; MobileScan tests the complete capture pipeline, device resources,
and ablations under realistic pose noise. For the controlled
results, we report PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, Gaussian count, and

Conclusion
We present PocketGS, an end-to-end 3DGS training framework for static-scene reconstruction on a smartphone. Its
geometry-informed warm start I ◦ G seeds surface-aligned
Gaussians from a filtered dense prior, while T provides
bandwidth-efficient, reference-consistent mobile gradient execution. Public benchmarks support the controlled 500step comparison; MobileScan evaluates the phone-capture
pipeline. On the tested smartphone, PocketGS averages four
minutes post-capture, remains below 3 GB peak memory,
and supports real-time rendering. Limitations include one
mobile SoC, static scenes, and mobile-resolution inputs.
