As reported by a user of the volume rendering component:
When attempting to move within a volume, it suddenly disappears. This
appears to be related to the proximity of the user/camera. When I move
away from the volume, it reappears. I call this a bug because there is
no way for me to be within the volume and look around to see the
relationship of any structures within that volume.

The current volume rendering algorithm does not support to locate the camera inside the volume. The volume is rendered in the outside surface of a cube, when the camera goes inside the volume it disappears because both the front-faces and the back-faces of the cube are needed to render the volume.
This is a feature not a bug, an alternative rendering algorithm will be needed to achieve such result. A way to see into the volume is modifying the opacity with a transfer function.

Well, I think that obtaining a volume rendering output when the camera is inside the volume could be compatible with the current approach, but there are multiple facts to consider:
Any improvement will imply changes on the current GLSL shaders and there will be conditional branching (a bunch of annoying if-else's in the GLSL code, which kills the performance). Thus, a performance loss will be expected, specially when looking inside the volume is not the main use case.
As the GLSL rayscasting shaders is generated dynamically when the X3D scene is loaded, this feature could be conditionally enabled with a new attribute on the VolumeData node, like solid=false or enableInsideLook=true, so a modification/addition to the X3D node should be discussed. Also, compatibility with the current styles and in which cases makes sense to enable it should be studied.
@mlimper The volume rendering technique is achieved by casting a ray from the camera viewpoint towards a box and transforming the ray position into 3D texture coordinates (given as a 2D Atlas).
The initial approach is described in:
Volume Ray Casting in WebGL
The current approach is a refined version which uses a single-pass instead of a two-passes strategy. The 3D coordinates of the cube are transformed into 3D texture coordinates and the camera location is used to calculate the ray direction.
@npolys About the clipping planes for volume rendering, I guess they should be implemented as shader clipping planes which will require further modification of the shaders. The clipping plane information should be made somehow available in the shader.
In theory, adding clipping planes should be possible in the current implementation. Again there may be performance implications to consider since the internal raycasting method has to be modified to support clipping planes.
From the X3D standard point of view, I am not aware on the relationship between the VolumeData node and the clipping plane entity. I guess, a new definition (let's say, VolumeClipPlaneNodes) might be necessary in the X3D standard.

I have hacked a fix which allows to inspect the volume from inside. As expected, a conditional branch is required in the volume rendering GLSL shader, reducing slightly the performance.
As the patch should be optional somehow, I propose to add an attribute to the X3DVolumeData node allowViewpointInside=true. VolumeData could be another place, but this is totally open to discussion (the name too, of course).
The default behaviour of this flag would be true. Therefore the volumes could be inspected from the inside by default. If this feature is not required, it could be deactivated to gain some performance.
Would this proposed attribute be accepted?

allowViewpointInside should be available for all the X3DVolumeData nodes, thus inherited in the VolumeData, SegmentedVolumeData and IsoSurfaceVolumeData. The RadarVolumeStyle is an addition out of the X3D spec that differs from the base algorithms to consider the depth and it is not totally integrated yet within the other nodes.
