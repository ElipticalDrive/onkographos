Show vortex structures in the 3D view as a Q-criterion isosurface. Listed in the 3D spec's Out of scope section, as a follow-up that needs its own spec.
Notes from the 3D research report:
Classic WebGPU marching cubes (mark, scan, compact, emit) takes 40.8–54.4 ms on an M2 Max for 256³ (Will Usher). Scaled to our 2M cells, that's roughly 5–7 ms, too much to spend every frame.
FluidX3D skips compaction: each cell runs marching cubes on its 2×2×2 Q stencil and emits its triangles directly. Tiling the velocities in local memory cut the loads per cell from 32 to 2.6.
The WebGPU route: compute Q into a scalar buffer once, emit triangles with atomicAdd into a buffer drawn with drawIndirect, and bind it read-only in the vertex stage (read-write storage is invisible to vertex shaders).
Its render cost competes with the solver for bandwidth, so the steps/s floor (≥ 480 at defaults on the M5) still has to hold.
