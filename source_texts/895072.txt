The third visualizer scene, after the particle field and the fluid. One full-screen fragment pass marching a Mandelbox signed distance field, fitted into the scene select and the post stack #58 and #57 built, with no change to how the renderer survives a popout remount.
What changed
scenes/Raymarch.ts with shaders/raymarch.common.wgsl, raymarch.march.wgsl and raymarch.upscale.wgsl. Each round of the fold pushes the point back into the unit box, inflates it out of an inner ball or inverts it inside a shell, scales it and adds the point it started from, while a running derivative turns the folded length back into a distance. The fold is about ten units across at its corners, which would put any sensible camera inside it, so the march divides through a constant and works on a shape a few units wide.
The scene holds no simulation state, unlike the other two. A frame comes from the 128-byte uniform and nothing else, so a remount loses nothing and a resize rebuilds nothing.
Shading is one light. The normal is four samples on a tetrahedron rather than six on the axes. The shadow is a second march of at most 18 steps that keeps the nearest the field came to the ray, so the edge is soft for one sample, and a face turned away from the light skips it, which is half the surface on a convex fold. Ambient occlusion is the step count the ray already took. So is the halo around a miss, which is the only thing a miss draws and the only thing the bloom has to work with out there.
Audio mapping in scenes/raymarch.params.ts: bass opens the fold scale, treble sets how many times the fold runs (6 to 12), beatPulse pulls the camera in but never past the shell, since inside the fold the distance estimate is no use and the frame turns to noise, and energy drives the light and the halo. Colour is a cosine ramp read at the surface's distance from the middle plus its step count, drifting with time and bass.
Both caps live in that same pure module: a step cap and a distance cap. The step cap is chosen in the stage's top bar (64 or 112) and remembered as musimo.visualizer-raymarch-steps. On a software rasteriser it is halved and the march runs at half the canvas each side, then one bilinear tap a pixel stretches it back; that upsample pass is the only texture the scene ever allocates.
cameraBasis in gpu/math.ts, the camera as three axes rather than a matrix, because a ray is built from them directly. It borrows another axis for the one case where the cross product is undefined, a camera looking straight along its own up vector.
The scene select, the size control beside it, the popout provider's state and the storage key follow the pattern Add a 2D fluid scene to the Now Playing visualizer #58 added. Nothing new was invented for the switch.
Unit tests for the parameter mapping and the camera basis, a structural check in e2e/visualizer.spec.ts, and docs/visualizer.md, docs/player.md, docs/now-playing-popout.md and the changelog.
Checked by hand, Mac (Apple M5 Pro, macOS 26.6), headed Chromium 153, real library on pancakes over Tailscale
Four tracks, each searched for, started from the tracks list and opened with in-app navigation so the analyser survived: Donkey Rhubarb, an Above & Beyond track, CamelPhat's Easier in the Sub Focus remix and Andy C's Back & Forth. The artist on the footer was checked before anything was read, because the saved queue comes back from the backend a moment after the page loads and a click that lands early gets overwritten by whatever played last. Frame time is data-frame-ms after ten and a half seconds, the post stack on throughout, with 250k particles as the control. The display runs at 120 Hz, so 8.3 ms means the GPU is not the limit.

Docked, the march is at the cap like everything else. At 4K it is not, and I would rather say so than bury it: 15 to 20 ms is 50 to 65 frames a second, which is what a fill-bound pass shading eight million pixels through a fractal costs on this machine. The two caps do not separate. Walking the same rays outside the browser, the average ray uses about half the cap at 64 and under a third at 112, and the share that leaves the march unfinished falls from six per cent to about one; that share is the soft fringe around the silhouette, not the bulk of the work. The cap is a ceiling on the worst case rather than the price of the frame, which is why it is still worth having on a slower machine even though it barely moves the number here.
Everything else in that run:
Twelve scene switches around all three scenes while a track played: one requestAdapter and one requestDevice for the whole run, one configure and no unconfigure, no uncapturederror and no console errors. data-scene and data-detail followed the select every time and the frame time held at 8.3 to 8.4 ms docked.
Six popout round trips with the march drawing: one adapter and one device, the number of configured canvas contexts always exactly one ahead of the number unconfigured, no uncaptured errors and no console errors. Both canvases carried raymarch and 64-step raymarch with advancing frame times, the docked stage came back at 320 by 320 each time, and playback was never interrupted.
Six switches between the two step caps: data-detail followed each one, and the choice survived a reload.
The half-resolution path. No machine here has a software rasteriser, so it was forced on by hand for one run: the march drew into the smaller texture, the upsample pass stretched it back with no console errors, and the frame was visibly softer, which is the whole point of it.
Screenshots at 3840 by 2160. The fold reads as a folded box with its faces cut away into galleries and filigree, lit from one side with the crevices dark and a coloured halo around the silhouette. Donkey Rhubarb keeps it at six or seven folds with broad plain faces and the camera sitting back. CamelPhat and Andy C drive it to twelve, the faces break into filigree down to a pixel, and the camera is pulled in on almost every onset, so the shape breathes at the beat. Nothing clipped to a flat white mass.
The gate passes: 66 unit tests, lint, format:check, build and test:e2e:types. The Playwright suite itself was not run, because its global setup needs the Docker CI stack and this Mac has no Docker; the spec change is structural and type checks, and the browser work above was done against the live library instead.

```
A third scene behind the Scene interface: one full-screen fragment pass
marching a Mandelbox distance field. It holds no simulation state at all,
so a frame is drawn from the uniform and nothing else, and a remount or a
resize costs it nothing.

Bass sets the fold scale, treble sets how many times the fold runs, the
beat pulls the camera in and energy drives the light. The occlusion is
free, read off the steps the ray already took, since a ray that scraped
its way in was in a crevice. The shadow is a second short march that a
face turned away from the light skips, and a miss draws only the halo that
same step count gives, which is what the bloom picks up.

The march is bounded by a step cap and a distance cap, both in
raymarch.params.ts. A march has no natural cost: a passage that folds the
field tighter makes every ray creep, and without the caps one bar could
cost ten times the frame budget of the one before. The cap is chosen in
the stage's top bar, 64 or 112, and remembered as
musimo.visualizer-raymarch-steps. A software rasteriser gets half of it
and marches at half the canvas, then the frame is stretched back.

Docked it sits at the 120 Hz cap like the other two scenes. At 3840 by
2160 it costs 15 to 20 ms on this Mac, which is what a fill-bound pass
shading eight million pixels through a fractal costs, so full screen runs
at 50 to 65 frames a second. The two caps did not separate in the
measurements, because the frame cost follows how much of the screen the
fold covers rather than a ceiling on rays that have already stopped.
```
