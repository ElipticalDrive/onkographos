What
Snapshots without copying. A snapshot is just a sequence number. While one is open, an overwrite or delete keeps the old version in a small chain on that key's index entry. Releasing the last snapshot frees the chains. kiban instead copies the whole memtable on every snapshot, and its shared handle does that copy while holding the write lock.
Atomic batches. write(ops) writes a single batch record with one checksum over every operation. Recovery applies all of it or none of it. Operations inside a batch use their own record kinds (4 and 5). That way a file scan never mistakes one for a top-level record, and a torn batch at the end still reads as a torn tail rather than mid-file damage.
Compare-and-swap. putIf(key, value, .absent) or .{ .seq = n }, plus seqOf(key). This stops two agents from overwriting each other's memory. kiban has nothing like it.
Compaction expands batch records and pauses while any snapshot is open.
Shared gains write, putIf, seqOf, snapshot/release/getAt, scan (optionally at a snapshot) and latest.

Summary
Adds sequence-based snapshots, atomic batch records, compare-and-swap, and matching methods on Shared.
Snapshots keep earlier values readable while writes continue.
Grouped writes stay together when the log reopens after a crash.
Conditional writes only replace the version a caller expects.
