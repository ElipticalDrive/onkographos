Abstract
In this paper, we aim to learn a semantic radiance field from multiple scenes that is accurate, efficient, and generalizable. Existing methods such as Semantic-NeRF simultaneously learn color and semantic labels from a typical NeRF structure with multiple heads. However, semantic prediction requires a larger receptive field and higher-level information than color prediction. Semantic-NeRF therefore heavily relies on positional encoding to overfit the semantic labels and has to train one specific model for each scene. To address this, we first propose Semantic Ray (S-Ray) to fully exploit semantic information along the ray direction from its multi-view reprojections, and design a Cross-Reprojection Attention module with consecutive intra-view radial and cross-view sparse attentions. While S-Ray achieves encouraging performance with carefully extracted ray features from multiple views, it still queries each ray independently, leading to inconsistent semantic label predictions. Therefore, we further propose Semantic-Frustum (S-Frustum) by introducing frustum-based rendering. Since extracting rich semantic features across frustum rays, points along each ray, and source views is computationally expensive, we design a unified semantic rendering framework by reformulating the problem as attention computation over an \(M^3\) Scene Tensor. By fully exploiting the sparsity and local redundancy of the tensor, we efficiently learn frustum features for accurate and consistent semantic modeling. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization ability of S-Ray, and also show that S-Frustum further boosts the performance on both scene and semantic rendering tasks.
