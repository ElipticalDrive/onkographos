The first real indirect light: an equirectangular HDR environment lights the scene in place of its ambient colour (Scene::setEnvironment, an empty path going back to the ambient colour).
HDR: the std has no .hdr reader, so HdrImage decodes Radiance files (flat or run-length encoded scanlines). The RGBE texels are uploaded as they are stored and decoded by the shader.
Vulkan wrappers: ImageCube (6 layers and their mips, allocImageCube), TextureCube (sampled as a cube through all its mips, each mip written through a TextureView of its faces), and a max-lod sampler option, because colour samplers only read the first mip before.
Precompute (EnvironmentLighting), recorded once in the transfer pass when the environment changes, never per frame: the image projected onto a cube (twice a quarter of its width, 64 to 1024) and its mips averaged, an irradiance cube (32²), a prefiltered cube (128², 6 mips, one roughness per mip, 512 GGX samples with filtered importance sampling), and the split-sum BRDF table (R16G16_SFLOAT, 256², made once at start).
Compose: the diffuse light is the irradiance times the diffuse colour, and the specular light is the prefiltered mip of the roughness times the table's reflectance. The AO shades the diffuse term directly and the specular term through Lagarde's specular occlusion. Where the depth is at the far plane, the environment is the background (Scene::skybox). Scene::environmentIntensity scales all of it.
The table is an extended storage format, so on devices without shaderStorageImageExtendedFormats the scene keeps its ambient colour, with a warning (the same rule as the AO).
Demo: --environment, --environment-intensity, --no-lights; E cycles through the two CC0 Poly Haven HDRIs added to res/environments (1k, with a README giving their source and licence).
Checked on screen under X11 with the validation layers on (no errors): with the outdoor environment, the smooth gold sphere mirrors the hills and the sky and the reflections blur as roughness grows; with --no-lights and the studio environment, the scene is lit by the environment alone. Without an environment the scene looks as before. Tests cover the HDR decoding and the sizes and parameters of the lighting.
Not checked: switching environments at runtime with E (no input injection here). Sky pixels have no motion vectors, so TAA may ghost the sky when the camera turns.
