Abstract. High-fidelity 3D head avatars have reached near-photorealistic
quality. While recent methods enable text-driven manipulation, they
struggle to provide fine-grained localized control, often entangling features or lacking geometric consistency. Modifying geometry through natural language currently requires slow per-prompt optimization or compromises identity and rigging. We present ManifoldSplat, the first end-toend framework for language-guided semantic shape editing of animatable
3D Gaussian Splatting avatars reconstructed from monocular videos. By
performing edits within the structured FLAME manifold rather than directly optimizing an unstructured Gaussian cloud, we strictly preserve
identity and animation. We introduce DeltaRegion, a per-region disentangled Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) delivering feedforward shape deltas, alongside a refining stage to recover view-consistent
details. ManifoldSplat reconstructs and edits an avatar in ∼ 90 seconds on
a consumer GPU, rendering at ∼ 800 FPS. Extensive evaluations demonstrate our approach sets a new state-of-the-art in localized prompt alignment, geometric coherence, and identity preservation. Project page and
code: https://a-canela.github.io/manifoldsplat/

Introduction
Photorealistic animatable 3D head avatars are a core building block of telepresence, virtual production, and assistive technologies. By combining a parametric face model such as FLAME [19] with 3D Gaussian Splatting [14], recent
methods [31,41,34,42,18] can reconstruct a personal, drivable head from a short
monocular video in minutes and render it in real time. Once reconstructed, however, these avatars are frozen identities: there is no built-in way for a user to
manipulate specific facial attributes through text and obtain, in seconds, an
animatable avatar of the same person with the requested change (Fig. 1).
Closing this gap remains challenging. Text-to-3D-face generators [40,1,11]
synthesize meshes from scratch, failing to preserve personal identity. Distillationbased editors [2,24] require lengthy per-prompt optimization, focus strictly on
appearance, and often break multi-view consistency. Meanwhile, generic 3D
scene editors [4,39] demand multi-view captures. Crucially, no existing method
achieves localized geometric editing of a personal monocular avatar while preserving its animation rigging and sub-minute latency.
Our key observation is that FLAME’s identity subspace is already a structured manifold of plausible head geometry, but it lacks explicit correspondence
with individual facial attributes, making isolated edits inherently difficult. Learning a text-conditioned generator inside this manifold - rather than in the Gaussian or NeRF parameter space - yields edits that are by construction physically
plausible, identity-preserving, and trivially compatible with downstream animation. The remaining challenges are then to localize an edit to the region the user
names without entangling unrelated attributes, and to make the Gaussian avatar
robust to the resulting geometric change without a full per-edit optimization.
We propose ManifoldSplat, a two-stage pipeline on a mesh-bound 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar: a text-guided shape-editing stage produces a localized
identity delta on the FLAME manifold, followed by a targeted rendering stage
that adapts the splats so the re-parameterized identity renders cleanly (Sec. 3).
In our evaluations, ManifoldSplat outperforms GaussianAvatar-Editor [24]
and InstructPix2Pix [2] on prompt alignment, identity preservation and visual
cleanliness while being one to two orders of magnitude faster; at the shape level, it
significantly outperforms DESCRIBE3D [40] and Semantify [11] in edit locality.
Contributions.
– ManifoldSplat: an end-to-end pipeline achieving state-of-the-art languageguided semantic shape editing of animatable 3D Gaussian head avatars reconstructed from monocular video, with targeted rendering enhancements

Related Work
ManifoldSplat sits at the intersection of multiple lines of research: parametric 3D
face models, animatable head avatars with neural and Gaussian representations,
and text-driven 3D generation and editing.
2.1
Parametric Face Models.
Linear morphable face models [7,19] and the encoder-based identity predictors
built on them [47] derive their shape spaces via global dimensionality reduction,
so their parameters are highly entangled and lack explicit correspondence with
localized semantic attributes. While mapping linguistic descriptions to global
shape spaces has been explored for parametric body models [35], achieving localized, disentangled control over specific facial attributes on the FLAME manifold
remains an open challenge. DeltaRegion addresses this gap by operating entirely
inside the FLAME identity shape space, giving us a strong inductive bias for
plausibility while enforcing localized, semantic control, and keeping every edit
trivially compatible with downstream FLAME-rigged assets.
2.2
Head-Avatar Reconstruction.
Early monocular head avatars relied on neural radiance fields [27,9,48,45]. Driven
by its real-time efficiency and high visual fidelity, 3D Gaussian Splatting [14]
shifted the dominant paradigm toward explicit, animatable Gaussian representations rigged on a parametric mesh [31,34,41,42,18]; a parallel branch tackles
cross-identity, feed-forward avatar synthesis from one or a few images [5,13,17].
We build on the Gaussian-blendshape formulation of [18] because its identityshape-driven parameterization makes shape edits both fast and well defined; our
rendering-side enhancements (Sec. 3.4) are nonetheless orthogonal and could be
integrated into other mesh-anchored Gaussian avatars.
2.3
Text-Driven 3D Face Generation.
A growing body of work generates 3D faces or full heads from a text prompt,
either via Score Distillation Sampling [29] on top of generic text-to-3D backbones [21,36] or with face-specific pipelines [40,44,1,46,20,23]; CLIP-space stylization methods [26,38] explore a related formulation. All of these target generation rather than editing of an identity reconstructed from a user-supplied
video, so comparing ManifoldSplat against them would conflate two problem
statements. To still isolate the shape-editing aspect of our pipeline, we benchmark DeltaRegion against DESCRIBE3D [40] and Semantify [11] as the closest
practical shape-level counterparts, noting that both synthesize from a neutral
mesh without conditioning on a source identity.

2.4
Editing 3D Avatars and Scenes.
Text-driven scene editing was popularized by InstructPix2Pix [2] and its 3D
lifts Instruct-NeRF2NeRF and Instruct-GS2GS [12,37], followed by multi-viewconsistent editors [4,39,3]. Head-avatar-specific methods include GaussianAvatarEditor [24] (per-prompt InstructPix2Pix iteration on an animatable 3DGS head),
FATE [43] (textural edits), AvatarStudio [25] (dynamic-actor editing via 2D diffusion priors), and ShapeFusion [30] (localized shape edits via a 3D diffusion
model conditioned on sparse handles). Most multi-view scene editors require
COLMAP-style calibration or assume a static scene, both violated by monocular talking-head video. The only published methods that meet our constraints
(animatable, monocularly reconstructed Gaussian head, free-form text instruction) are GaussianAvatar-Editor [24] and an InstructPix2Pix-based editor of the
trained avatar, which we use as our two avatar-level baselines.

3.1
Background - Preprocessing and Mesh-bound Backbone
Preprocessing. We adopt RGBAvatar [18] as our reconstruction backbone and
inherit its preprocessing stack: MICA [47] for identity initialization, metricaltracker [47] for per-frame FLAME pose and expression, and Robust Video Matting [22] for foreground extraction at 512×512.
FLAME [19] parameterizes a head mesh as M (β, ψ, θ) with identity β ∈ R300 ,
expression ψ, and pose θ in separate linear subspaces, so editing β alone changes
who the subject is without altering how they move.

a set of 3D Gaussians whose canonical centers are anchored on the FLAME

3.2
Synthetic Dataset
Training DeltaRegion requires a large pool of triplets (β 0 , t, β 1 ), each pairing a
source identity β 0 and an edit prompt t with the target identity β 1 = β 0 + ∆β
realizing the prompted edit, densely covering both the identity manifold and
the space of region-localized semantic edits. No public corpus pairs free-form
prompts with identity-preserving FLAME shape deltas, and hand-annotating
target meshes per subject does not scale. Our key design choice is to specify each
edit once as a sparse set of 3D vertex displacements on the canonical FLAME
mesh, rather than as a precomputed shape-coefficient target. Since all FLAME
identities share the same topology, the same prototype can be projected onto
any source identity β 0 by solving for the ∆β whose induced vertex motion best
matches the prescribed displacements on that subject’s deformed mesh, turning
a small library of prototypes into a combinatorially large supervised set without
per-subject mesh annotation.
Scalable Generation. We author ∼130 fine-grained prototypes spanning the six
face regions (face, nose, lips, ears, neck, eyes); each is a JSON descriptor that
picks a small set of FLAME vertex indices in the targeted region, assigns each

3.3
Stage 1 - Shape Editing
Problem. Given a prompt t and a source identity β 0 , we want β 1 = β 0 + ∆β
such that (i) the edit matches t semantically, (ii) it is local - it should not spill
into facial regions the prompt does not mention - and (iii) the model exposes
a continuous knob for edit strength while remaining deterministic enough to
be reproducible. We cast this as a conditional generative model mapping the
(prompt, source-shape) pair to the edited FLAME shape.
Architecture. DeltaRegion (Fig. 2, bottom) is a conditional VAE that fuses a
frozen Flan-T5 [6,33] text encoding with a small MLP embedding of β 0 via crossattention, then samples a structured latent z ∈ RKd split into K=6 contiguous
blocks of dimension d, one per face region. A small gate network predicts a
softmax distribution g ∈ ∆K−1 over regions from the prompt embedding alone,
and each block is multiplied by its gate value before being decoded alongside
the source shape embedding into the shape delta ∆β ∈ R300 . This gate explicitly
enforces disentangled editing: any latent dimension belonging to a non-selected
region is multiplied by a near-zero value before reaching the decoder, so its
contribution to ∆β vanishes by construction. Full layer sizes, the cross-attention
block and the prior/posterior parameterization are listed in the supplementary
material.

3.4
Stage 2 - Gaussian Refining
Once the shape has been edited by DeltaRegion, plugging the new identity β 1
into an off-the-shelf mesh-bound avatar reveals multiple failure modes: Gaussians
that were comfortable on the original surface now hover above or sink below the
edited mesh (floaters), high-frequency color transitions become jagged because
the local Gaussian neighborhood has shifted, and persistent appearance cues
that the avatar had quietly memorised on the original geometry - a mole, a stray
eyebrow hair, the rim of an iris - now sit in the wrong place. We address each with
a targeted change to the training pipeline. Naive local geometric or colorimetric
smoothing could not play this role: these artifacts are systematic misalignments
between splats and the edited surface rather than zero-mean noise, so local
averaging would leave the misplaced cues in place while irreversibly blurring the
high-frequency detail (skin pores, moles, iris rims) that photorealism depends
on; our objectives instead re-align that detail with the new geometry. These
changes do not alter the avatar’s representation or renderer interface; they only
affect how Gaussians are parameterized, activated, and re-optimized.
Temporally, this stage consists of two sequential optimizations. First, the
base avatar is reconstructed once per subject with the surface-consistency regularizer (a) and the smooth color activation (b) enabled: Lsurf is added to the
backbone’s photometric objective, and all terms are minimized jointly. Then, after DeltaRegion delivers β 1 , the short warped-refining pass (c) restarts from this
base checkpoint (shared across all edits of the same subject) and simultaneously
minimizes a single combined objective - comprising the photometric loss on the
warped pseudo-ground-truths, Lsurf , and the anchor term defined in Equation 3.

Experiments
In the absence of existing baselines that perform localized geometric editing on
monocular animatable avatars, we decouple our evaluation into three complementary tasks: (i) the full avatar editing task, comparing the complete ManifoldSplat pipeline against GaussianAvatar-Editor [24] and an InstructPix2Pixbased [2] editor of the trained avatar; (ii) the shape-only generation task in
which DeltaRegion is compared qualitatively against DESCRIBE3D [40] and
Semantify [11] on a neutral FLAME identity; and (iii) an ablation study isolating the 3DGS enhancements (Surface Consistency, Smooth-Color tanh, Warped
Refining).
4.1
Experimental Setup
For avatar editing evaluation, we use the publicly-available, pre-processed subset of NeRSemble [16] released by GaussianAvatar-Editor [24]. Each subject’s
front camera is held out for evaluation, with 100-300 test frames per (subject,
edit) pair. We evaluate 18 natural-language edits per subject covering all six attribute regions. The evaluation prompts are not verbatim copies of the training
tuples: they introduce small linguistic variations in wording and qualifiers over
the canonical fragments, which the frozen Flan-T5 encoder maps robustly to the
intended edit. We note, however, that robustness to strongly out-of-distribution
phrasings is not the focus of this work, whose emphasis lies on the geometric
locality and avatar consistency of the edits. All experiments were run on a single
NVIDIA RTX 5090. DeltaRegion is trained for 100 epochs, taking ∼ 40 h. At test
time, per-subject avatar reconstruction runs for one minute with our enhancements enabled, while the optional warped refining stage requires an additional
30 seconds. To evaluate our method, we report three complementary metrics:
CLIP-D [32,10] for prompt alignment, ArcFace [8] for identity preservation, and
Anisotropic 2D Total Variation (T V2D ) as a no-reference image-quality proxy.
Detailed metric definitions are provided in the supplementary material.

4.4
Limitations
An inherent challenge is the geometric discrepancy between the parametric
FLAME model and the 3D Gaussian representation. While surface-consistency
regularization improves this correspondence, perfect topological alignment remains difficult, and residual misalignments can amplify rendering artifacts under
extreme shape deformations.
Furthermore, the edit space is bounded by our finite library of predefined
deformation prototypes; the model cannot generalize to completely novel structural changes outside this training distribution. Supporting genuinely unseen
deformations still requires manual annotation, making automated, data-driven
prototype extraction an interesting future direction. Finally, our edits are strictly
limited to the FLAME surface geometry; external regions like hair, alongside
appearance attributes (e.g., skin or eye color), are not addressed in this work.
5
Conclusions
We introduced ManifoldSplat, the first end-to-end pipeline for language-guided
semantic shape editing of animatable 3D Gaussian head avatars reconstructed
from monocular video. By decoupling text-driven geometric manipulation via
DeltaRegion from the underlying splat-based rendering, we achieve localized,
identity-preserving edits without compromising animation fidelity. Furthermore,
our targeted rendering regularizations ensure that the avatar remains visually
coherent under structural deformations, reducing each edit to a brief refining
of the pre-trained avatar instead of a costly full re-optimization. Ultimately,
ManifoldSplat unfreezes the geometric identity of monocular avatars and lays
the groundwork for fully conversational, real-time avatar sculpting. This bridges
a critical gap in virtual telepresence and dynamic media, bringing us a step closer
to highly responsive, language-driven 3D digital humans.

A.1
DeltaRegion architecture
Text and shape encoders. As illustrated in the bottom panel of Fig. 2 in the
main manuscript, the prompt is encoded by a frozen Flan-T5-Large [6,33] encoder, yielding 1024-D token features T ∈ RL×1024 (we cap L=128 tokens) and
a masked-mean sentence embedding tg ∈ R1024 . The source shape β 0 ∈ R300 is
projected by a small MLP (hidden sizes [512, 512], LayerNorm, dropout 0.15) to
a single 512-D token s ∈ R512 .
Cross-attention fusion. A multi-head attention block (d=512, 8 heads, attention
dropout 0.1) treats s as the query and the linearly projected text tokens (R1024 →
R512 ) as keys/values, preserving the T5 padding mask. A residual connection
and LayerNorm yield a fused embedding h ∈ R512 carrying shape-conditioned
semantic context.
Gate network. Conditioning the gate solely on the global text embedding tg
forces the network to localize the edit strictly from language. A 1-hidden-layer
MLP (hidden size 512, dropout 0.15) maps a separate R1024 → R512 projection of
tg to K=6 logits and applies a softmax, producing g ∈ ∆K−1 over the FLAME
regions {face, nose, lips, ears, neck, eyes}.

0.15) parameterize diagonal Gaussian distributions over a structured latent z ∈

Training data
We synthesize triplets (β 0 , t, β 1 ) from 300 MICA-fitted LYHM [7] identities. For
each of the six FLAME regions we author ∼130 fine-grained edit templates specified as small sets of canonical vertex indices paired with 3D displacement vectors
(nose: short/long, narrow/wide, deviated, bridge/tip variants, etc.; analogous
for the other regions). At each scale s ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5} and for every (subject,

shape basis with an outside-region penalty (λout =102 ) that keeps the solution
from leaking into unrelated areas. Each (β 0 , edit, scale) tuple is paired with five
template-paraphrased prompts whose qualifiers (“very subtly”, “slightly”, . . . ,
“drastically”) reflect the scale, yielding ∼500k supervised triplets. Data generation takes ∼70 hours on a single RTX 5090, and DeltaRegion training takes
∼40 hours on the same GPU. 15% of the triplets is held out for validation.

B.3
Extended qualitative results
Figure 8 extends the qualitative results of the main paper (Fig. 5) to six subjects
covering a broad range of ethnicities, skin tones, ages, and hairstyles, with three
prompts per subject sampled across the eyes, lips, face, neck, nose and ears regions. Because the underlying FLAME delta is itself local (Fig. 7), the warpedrefining stage transports the avatar’s appearance along the perturbed geometry instead of re-synthesizing unedited regions: “close-set eyes” alters the interocular distance without touching the lips or hairline, and “wide lips” stretches
the mouth without modifying the nasal base. The effect persists under intraregion variation - three distinct prompts on the same subject (e.g. row 3: big
forehead, small forehead, wide jaw ) yield clearly different face shapes while preserving eyebrow shape, skin texture and eye color, which is what drives the high
ArcFace scores in Table 4. Consistent with our per-region analysis (Sec. B.1),
edits to the ears naturally appear as subtle silhouette changes from a frontal
view, also confirming that the central identity features remain completely disentangled. Since every edit acts on the mesh-bound 3D Gaussian avatar rather
than on a single 2D image, the rendered portraits keep their view-dependent appearance and can be driven by the tracker’s expression/pose stream at >800 fps;
the normal-coded FLAME insets visualize the underlying geometry that any
downstream animation will inherit.
To demonstrate robustness beyond frontal views and controlled environments, Fig. 9 evaluates ManifoldSplat on an in-the-wild monocular video lacking a studio setup or controlled lighting. Despite this domain shift from the
NeRSemble [16] dataset, the avatar maintains a coherent silhouette and viewdependent shading in near-profile poses. These non-frontal viewpoints are crucial
to clearly visualize shape changes in nasal attributes (“aquiline nose”, “protruded
tip nose”, and “recessed nose bridge”) that are largely unnoticeable in purely
frontal views. In all cases, the deformations are sharply confined to the targeted
region, preserving the surrounding identity and face structure, thereby confirming our method’s edit locality and multi-view consistency on unconstrained data.
