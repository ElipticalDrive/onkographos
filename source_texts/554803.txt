Abstract. High-fidelity 3D head avatars have reached near-photorealistic quality. While recent methods enable text-driven manipulation, they struggle to provide fine-grained localized control, often entangling features or lacking geometric consistency. Modifying geometry through natural language currently requires slow per-prompt optimization or compromises identity and rigging. We present ManifoldSplat, the first end-toend framework for language-guided semantic shape editing of animatable 3D Gaussian Splatting avatars reconstructed from monocular videos. By performing edits within the structured FLAME manifold rather than directly optimizing an unstructured Gaussian cloud, we strictly preserve identity and animation. We introduce DeltaRegion, a per-region disentangled Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) delivering feedforward shape deltas, alongside a refining stage to recover view-consistent details. ManifoldSplat reconstructs and edits an avatar in ∼ 90 seconds on a consumer GPU, rendering at ∼ 800 FPS. Extensive evaluations demonstrate our approach sets a new state-of-the-art in localized prompt alignment, geometric coherence, and identity preservation. Project page and code:

## 1 Introduction

Photorealistic animatable 3D head avatars are a core building block of telepresence, virtual production, and assistive technologies. By combining a parametric face model such as FLAME [19] with 3D Gaussian Splatting [14], recent methods [31,41,34,42,18] can reconstruct a personal, drivable head from a short monocular video in minutes and render it in real time. Once reconstructed, however, these avatars are frozen identities: there is no built-in way for a user to manipulate specific facial attributes through text and obtain, in seconds, an animatable avatar of the same person with the requested change (Fig. 1\).

Closing this gap remains challenging. Text-to-3D-face generators [40,1,11] synthesize meshes from scratch, failing to preserve personal identity. Distillationbased editors [2,24] require lengthy per-prompt optimization, focus strictly on appearance, and often break multi-view consistency. Meanwhile, generic 3D scene editors [4,39] demand multi-view captures. Crucially, no existing method achieves localized geometric editing of a personal monocular avatar while preserving its animation rigging and sub-minute latency.

Our key observation is that FLAME's identity subspace is already a structured manifold of plausible head geometry, but it lacks explicit correspondence with individual facial attributes, making isolated edits inherently difficult. Learning a text-conditioned generator inside this manifold - rather than in the Gaussian or NeRF parameter space - yields edits that are by construction physically plausible, identity-preserving, and trivially compatible with downstream animation. The remaining challenges are then to localize an edit to the region the user names without entangling unrelated attributes, and to make the Gaussian avatar robust to the resulting geometric change without a full per-edit optimization.

We propose ManifoldSplat, a two-stage pipeline on a mesh-bound 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar: a text-guided shape-editing stage produces a localized identity delta on the FLAME manifold, followed by a targeted rendering stage that adapts the splats so the re-parameterized identity renders cleanly (Sec. 3\).

In our evaluations, ManifoldSplat outperforms GaussianAvatar-Editor [24] and InstructPix2Pix [2] on prompt alignment, identity preservation and visual cleanliness while being one to two orders of magnitude faster; at the shape level, it significantly outperforms DESCRIBE3D [40] and Semantify [11] in edit locality.

## 2 Related Work

ManifoldSplat sits at the intersection of multiple lines of research: parametric 3D face models, animatable head avatars with neural and Gaussian representations, and text-driven 3D generation and editing.

### 2.1 Parametric Face Models.

Linear morphable face models [7,19] and the encoder-based identity predictors built on them [47] derive their shape spaces via global dimensionality reduction, so their parameters are highly entangled and lack explicit correspondence with localized semantic attributes. While mapping linguistic descriptions to global shape spaces has been explored for parametric body models [35], achieving localized, disentangled control over specific facial attributes on the FLAME manifold remains an open challenge. DeltaRegion addresses this gap by operating entirely inside the FLAME identity shape space, giving us a strong inductive bias for plausibility while enforcing localized, semantic control, and keeping every edit trivially compatible with downstream FLAME-rigged assets.

### 2.2 Head-Avatar Reconstruction.

Early monocular head avatars relied on neural radiance fields [27,9,48,45]. Driven by its real-time efficiency and high visual fidelity, 3D Gaussian Splatting [14] shifted the dominant paradigm toward explicit, animatable Gaussian representations rigged on a parametric mesh [31,34,41,42,18]; a parallel branch tackles cross-identity, feed-forward avatar synthesis from one or a few images [5,13,17]. We build on the Gaussian-blendshape formulation of [18] because its identityshape-driven parameterization makes shape edits both fast and well defined; our rendering-side enhancements (Sec. 3.4\) are nonetheless orthogonal and could be integrated into other mesh-anchored Gaussian avatars.

#### 2.3 Text-Driven 3D Face Generation.

A growing body of work generates 3D faces or full heads from a text prompt, either via Score Distillation Sampling [29] on top of generic text-to-3D backbones [21,36] or with face-specific pipelines [40,44,1,46,20,23]; CLIP-space stylization methods [26,38] explore a related formulation. All of these target generation rather than editing of an identity reconstructed from a user-supplied video, so comparing ManifoldSplat against them would conflate two problem statements. To still isolate the shape-editing aspect of our pipeline, we benchmark DeltaRegion against DESCRIBE3D [40] and Semantify [11] as the closest practical shape-level counterparts, noting that both synthesize from a neutral mesh without conditioning on a source identity.

#### 3.1 Background - Preprocessing and Mesh-bound Backbone

Preprocessing. We adopt RGBAvatar [18] as our reconstruction backbone and inherit its preprocessing stack: MICA [47] for identity initialization, metricaltracker [47] for per-frame FLAME pose and expression, and Robust Video Matting [22] for foreground extraction at 512×512.

FLAME [19] parameterizes a head mesh as M(β, ψ, θ) with identity β∈R 300 , expression ψ, and pose θ in separate linear subspaces, so editing β alone changes who the subject is without altering how they move.

Mesh-bound Gaussian-blendshape avatars represent a personal head as a set of 3D Gaussians whose canonical centers are anchored on the FLAME mesh and deformed by a small set of B learned linear blendshapes driven by the tracker's expression vector. Each Gaussian gi stores a rest position x (0) i expressed in the tangent-bitangent-normal (TBN) frame of its anchor triangle, a set of per-blendshape offsets {x (b) i } B b=1 in the same local frame, plus rotation, scale, opacity and spherical-harmonic coefficients (DC band in our setting). Trianglelocal parameterization is key: any deformation of the underlying mesh - including a change of identity β - is automatically propagated to the Gaussians through the triangle TBN, bypassing full re-optimization.

#### 3.2 Synthetic Dataset

Training DeltaRegion requires a large pool of triplets (β0 , t, β1 ), each pairing a source identity β0 and an edit prompt t with the target identity β1 = β0 + ∆β realizing the prompted edit, densely covering both the identity manifold and the space of region-localized semantic edits. No public corpus pairs free-form prompts with identity-preserving FLAME shape deltas, and hand-annotating target meshes per subject does not scale. Our key design choice is to specify each edit once as a sparse set of 3D vertex displacements on the canonical FLAME mesh, rather than as a precomputed shape-coefficient target. Since all FLAME identities share the same topology, the same prototype can be projected onto any source identity β0 by solving for the ∆β whose induced vertex motion best matches the prescribed displacements on that subject's deformed mesh, turning a small library of prototypes into a combinatorially large supervised set without per-subject mesh annotation.

Scalable Generation. We author ∼130 fine-grained prototypes spanning the six face regions (face, nose, lips, ears, neck, eyes); each is a JSON descriptor that picks a small set of FLAME vertex indices in the targeted region, assigns each a 3D displacement vector, and provides a canonical prompt fragment (e.g. "wide nose bridge"). Source identities come from MICA-fitted LYHM [7] subjects; for every (subject, edit, scale) tuple we recover ∆β by minimizing the squared distance between the deformed mesh and the displaced anchor vertices under an outside-region penalty that prevents leakage into unrelated areas, with displacements linearly rescaled per tuple to vary edit magnitude. Each tuple is then paired with several prompt variations, generated by injecting scale-dependent qualifiers into templates built around the canonical fragment and paraphrasing via Flan-T5 [6,33] for linguistic diversity. This single pass yields ∼500k supervised triplets (details in supplementary).

#### 3.3 Stage 1 - Shape Editing

Problem. Given a prompt t and a source identity β0 , we want β1 = β0 + ∆β such that (i) the edit matches t semantically, (ii) it is local - it should not spill into facial regions the prompt does not mention - and (iii) the model exposes a continuous knob for edit strength while remaining deterministic enough to be reproducible. We cast this as a conditional generative model mapping the (prompt, source-shape) pair to the edited FLAME shape.

Architecture. DeltaRegion (Fig. 2, bottom) is a conditional VAE that fuses a frozen Flan-T5 [6,33] text encoding with a small MLP embedding of β0 via crossattention, then samples a structured latent z ∈ R Kd split into K=6 contiguous blocks of dimension d, one per face region. A small gate network predicts a softmax distribution g∈ ∆K−1 over regions from the prompt embedding alone, and each block is multiplied by its gate value before being decoded alongside the source shape embedding into the shape delta ∆β∈R 300. This gate explicitly enforces disentangled editing: any latent dimension belonging to a non-selected region is multiplied by a near-zero value before reaching the decoder, so its contribution to ∆β vanishes by construction. Full layer sizes, the cross-attention block and the prior/posterior parameterization are listed in the supplementary material.

Training. We train DeltaRegion on the corpus of Sec. 3.2 under the combined objective

L = λ in v L in v + λ out v L out v + λβ Lβ + λg Lgate + λKL L fb KL, (1)

Here a ∗ ∈ {1, . . . , K} denotes the ground-truth region label of each training triplet and Va∗ the set of FLAME vertices belonging to that region. The loss combines two region-masked vertex reconstruction terms: L in v , which optimizes the vertices inside the ground-truth region mask Va∗ toward the target shape, and L out v , which anchors the vertices outside the mask to the unedited source shape. Additionally, the objective includes a shape-parameter ℓ2 penalty Lβ, a crossentropy loss Lgate for the predicted region probabilities against a ∗ , and a free-bits KL divergence regularization L fb KL [15] with linear warm-up. The λ factors are scalar weights balancing the terms; we use λ in v =λ out v =5×105 (compensating the metric scale of vertex coordinates), λβ=10, λg=10 and λKL=0.05. The joint supervision of the masked vertex and gate terms is what yields the per-region disentanglement observed at inference (Fig. 4\); full term definitions are detailed in the supplementary material.

Inference. At test time we drop the posterior and decode the mean µp of the learned conditional prior pθ(z | t, β0 ) = N (µp ,σ 2 p ), rescaled by a prior-scale factor s, z = s µp , which compensates for the natural attenuation of µp relative to training-time posterior samples; we set s=1.2 empirically on a held-out validation split. The decoder then yields ∆β in one feed-forward pass, and the mapping is deterministic per prompt. Edit strength is exposed through two complementary continuous knobs: linguistically, the scale-dependent qualifiers in the prompt ("slightly", . . . , "drastically"), which the model learns to associate with displacement magnitude from the scale-varied training tuples; and numerically, the prior-scale factor s itself, which can be varied continuously at test time to smoothly attenuate or exaggerate the decoded delta.

#### 3.4 Stage 2 - Gaussian Refining

Once the shape has been edited by DeltaRegion, plugging the new identity β1 into an off-the-shelf mesh-bound avatar reveals multiple failure modes: Gaussians that were comfortable on the original surface now hover above or sink below the edited mesh (floaters), high-frequency color transitions become jagged because the local Gaussian neighborhood has shifted, and persistent appearance cues that the avatar had quietly memorised on the original geometry - a mole, a stray eyebrow hair, the rim of an iris - now sit in the wrong place. We address each with a targeted change to the training pipeline. Naive local geometric or colorimetric smoothing could not play this role: these artifacts are systematic misalignments between splats and the edited surface rather than zero-mean noise, so local averaging would leave the misplaced cues in place while irreversibly blurring the high-frequency detail (skin pores, moles, iris rims) that photorealism depends on; our objectives instead re-align that detail with the new geometry. These changes do not alter the avatar's representation or renderer interface; they only affect how Gaussians are parameterized, activated, and re-optimized.

Temporally, this stage consists of two sequential optimizations. First, the base avatar is reconstructed once per subject with the surface-consistency regularizer (a) and the smooth color activation (b) enabled: Lsurf is added to the backbone's photometric objective, and all terms are minimized jointly. Then, after DeltaRegion delivers β1 , the short warped-refining pass (c) restarts from this base checkpoint (shared across all edits of the same subject) and simultaneously minimizes a single combined objective - comprising the photometric loss on the warped pseudo-ground-truths, Lsurf, and the anchor term defined in Equation 3.

(a) Surface-consistency regularization. Because each Gaussian's rest position x (0) i and its per-blendshape offsets {x (b) i } are expressed in the TBN frame of the anchor triangle, an L2 penalty on these vectors is exactly a penalty on deviation from the mesh surface:

Lsurf = λx X i ∥x (0) i 2 2 + λ∆xX i,b ∥x (b) i 2 2 . (2)

Penalizing both terms, with λ factors as weights, pulls splats onto the canonical surface and keeps their per-blendshape motion coherent with the mesh, ensuring Gaussians follow when the mesh is later re-parameterized by a shape edit.

(b) Smooth color activation. The backbone maps spherical-harmonic coefficients h to colors through a sigmoid-like activation that saturates abruptly and produces sharp transitions where neighboring Gaussians disagree - an effect exacerbated when an edit perturbs the local Gaussian neighborhood. We replace it inside the CUDA rasterization kernel by c = 1 2 (tanh(v) + 1), where v is the unbounded color value obtained by evaluating h from the current view direction. The mapping has the same [0, 1] range but a milder slope around zero and a symmetric saturation profile, yielding smoother color fields under shape edits at zero rendering cost.

(c) Warped refining. Even with (a) and (b), simply swapping β0 → β1 cannot recover the view-dependent detail that the trained avatar had implicitly encoded against the unedited geometry. We therefore add a short warped-refining stage that exploits the shared FLAME topology to compute, in closed form, a perpixel resampling map Wt from each training-frame render ˜It of the unedited mesh to the rasterization of the edited mesh: the source pixel is found via triangle-andbarycentric reprojection. The view-consistent pseudo-ground-truth ˆIt = Wt( ˜It) follows from a single texture-space reprojection. Because the warp is derived from the shared mesh topology rather than from image-space optical flow, it is insensitive to texture and free of the bracketing failures of optical-flow estimators on low-texture regions like cheeks or forehead.

We then re-optimize the avatar on { ˆIt} with the edited FLAME shape, the color channels frozen (so the warped appearance serves as a strict chromatic reference), the surface-consistency regularizer of Eq. 2 still active, and an anchor term that prevents the non-frozen parameters Φ (positions, opacities, scales, rotations and the position/rotation blendshape bases) from drifting away from the warm start Φ (0):

Lanchor = X ϕ∈Φ ∥ϕ − ϕ (0)∥ 2 2 . (3)

Freezing color channels is what turns refining into a geometric correction: positions, scales and rotations realign with the new surface while the warped image dictates where pre-existing appearance cues should sit, which is what removes the ghosting artifacts of bare re-parameterization. Coupled with the anchor loss, this stage produces a tightly local edit that preserves the unedited avatar's overall identity and rendering quality everywhere outside the region the prompt targets.

## 4 Experiments

In the absence of existing baselines that perform localized geometric editing on monocular animatable avatars, we decouple our evaluation into three complementary tasks: (i) the full avatar editing task, comparing the complete ManifoldSplat pipeline against GaussianAvatar-Editor [24] and an InstructPix2Pixbased [2] editor of the trained avatar; (ii) the shape-only generation task in which DeltaRegion is compared qualitatively against DESCRIBE3D [40] and Semantify [11] on a neutral FLAME identity; and (iii) an ablation study isolating the 3DGS enhancements (Surface Consistency, Smooth-Color tanh, Warped Refining).

#### 4.1 Experimental Setup

For avatar editing evaluation, we use the publicly-available, pre-processed subset of NeRSemble [16] released by GaussianAvatar-Editor [24]. Each subject's front camera is held out for evaluation, with 100-300 test frames per (subject, edit) pair. We evaluate 18 natural-language edits per subject covering all six attribute regions. The evaluation prompts are not verbatim copies of the training tuples: they introduce small linguistic variations in wording and qualifiers over the canonical fragments, which the frozen Flan-T5 encoder maps robustly to the intended edit. We note, however, that robustness to strongly out-of-distribution phrasings is not the focus of this work, whose emphasis lies on the geometric locality and avatar consistency of the edits. All experiments were run on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090. DeltaRegion is trained for 100 epochs, taking ∼ 40 h. At test time, per-subject avatar reconstruction runs for one minute with our enhancements enabled, while the optional warped refining stage requires an additional 30 seconds. To evaluate our method, we report three complementary metrics: CLIP-D [32,10] for prompt alignment, ArcFace [8] for identity preservation, and Anisotropic 2D Total Variation (T V2D) as a no-reference image-quality proxy. Detailed metric definitions are provided in the supplementary material.

#### 4.4 Limitations

An inherent challenge is the geometric discrepancy between the parametric FLAME model and the 3D Gaussian representation. While surface-consistency regularization improves this correspondence, perfect topological alignment remains difficult, and residual misalignments can amplify rendering artifacts under extreme shape deformations.

Furthermore, the edit space is bounded by our finite library of predefined deformation prototypes; the model cannot generalize to completely novel structural changes outside this training distribution. Supporting genuinely unseen deformations still requires manual annotation, making automated, data-driven prototype extraction an interesting future direction. Finally, our edits are strictly limited to the FLAME surface geometry; external regions like hair, alongside appearance attributes (e.g., skin or eye color), are not addressed in this work.

## 5 Conclusions

We introduced ManifoldSplat, the first end-to-end pipeline for language-guided semantic shape editing of animatable 3D Gaussian head avatars reconstructed from monocular video. By decoupling text-driven geometric manipulation via DeltaRegion from the underlying splat-based rendering, we achieve localized, identity-preserving edits without compromising animation fidelity. Furthermore, our targeted rendering regularizations ensure that the avatar remains visually coherent under structural deformations, reducing each edit to a brief refining of the pre-trained avatar instead of a costly full re-optimization. Ultimately, ManifoldSplat unfreezes the geometric identity of monocular avatars and lays the groundwork for fully conversational, real-time avatar sculpting. This bridges a critical gap in virtual telepresence and dynamic media, bringing us a step closer to highly responsive, language-driven 3D digital humans.

## A Implementation Details

We detail the architecture of the DeltaRegion network, the generation process for our synthetic paired dataset, and the hyper-parameters governing the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization.

#### A.1 DeltaRegion architecture

Text and shape encoders. As illustrated in the bottom panel of Fig. 2 in the main manuscript, the prompt is encoded by a frozen Flan-T5-Large [6,33] encoder, yielding 1024-D token features T ∈ R L×1024 (we cap L=128 tokens) and a masked-mean sentence embedding tg ∈ R 1024. The source shape β0 ∈ R 300 is projected by a small MLP (hidden sizes [512, 512], LayerNorm, dropout 0.15) to a single 512-D token s∈R 512 .

Cross-attention fusion. A multi-head attention block (d=512, 8 heads, attention dropout 0.1) treats s as the query and the linearly projected text tokens (R 1024→ 512) as keys/values, preserving the T5 padding mask. A residual connection and LayerNorm yield a fused embedding h ∈ R 512 carrying shape-conditioned semantic context.

Gate network. Conditioning the gate solely on the global text embedding tg forces the network to localize the edit strictly from language. A 1-hidden-layer MLP (hidden size 512, dropout 0.15) maps a separate R 1024→R 512 projection of tg to K=6 logits and applies a softmax, producing g∈∆K−1 over the FLAME regions {face, nose, lips, ears, neck, eyes}.

Prior and posterior. Two MLP heads (hidden sizes [768, 512], LayerNorm, dropout 0.15) parameterize diagonal Gaussian distributions over a structured latent z∈ Kd with K=6 and d=8 (so dim z=48): the prior pθ(z | t, β0 ) = N (µp ,σ 2 p ) is regressed from h, while the training-only posterior qϕ(z|t, β0 , ∆β) = N (µq ,σ 2 q ) takes [h; E∆(∆β)] as input, where E∆ is a [512, 512] MLP encoder of the groundtruth shape delta. z is sliced into K contiguous d-dimensional blocks, one per attribute.

Decoder. The latent is gated per region, z˜k = gk zk, flattened, and concatenated with the source shape embedding s (passed through dropout p=0.3 to discourage the decoder from shortcutting through source identity and bypassing z). A 4 layer MLP D (hidden sizes [768, 512, 512], dropout 0.15) then produces the shape delta:

∆β = D gk zk K k=1 s ∈ R 300 . (4)

The predicted edited shape is then naturally obtained as β pred 1 = β0+∆β, which is subsequently used to compute the geometric training objectives.

Inference. At test time we drop the posterior and use the prior mean rescaled by a prior-scale factor, z = s µp with s=1.2, which compensates for the natural attenuation of µp relative to a sample from qϕ during training. The mapping is deterministic per prompt; varying s continuously modulates edit strength.

Optimization. DeltaRegion is trained with AdamW (learning rate 10−4 , weight decay 0.01, gradient-norm clipping at 1.0) for 100 epochs at batch size 256 on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090.

#### A.2 Training data

We synthesize triplets (β0 , t, β1 ) from 300 MICA-fitted LYHM [7] identities. For each of the six FLAME regions we author ∼130 fine-grained edit templates specified as small sets of canonical vertex indices paired with 3D displacement vectors (nose: short/long, narrow/wide, deviated, bridge/tip variants, etc.; analogous for the other regions). At each scale s ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5} and for every (subject, edit template) pair we recover the corresponding ∆β ∗ by an iterative projection (Adam, 1500 steps, lr 10−2 , ℓ2 regularization weight 10−6 ) onto the FLAME shape basis with an outside-region penalty (λout=102 ) that keeps the solution from leaking into unrelated areas. Each (β0 , edit,scale) tuple is paired with five template-paraphrased prompts whose qualifiers ("very subtly", "slightly", . . . , "drastically") reflect the scale, yielding ∼500k supervised triplets. Data generation takes ∼70 hours on a single RTX 5090, and DeltaRegion training takes ∼40 hours on the same GPU. 15% of the triplets is held out for validation.

#### A.3 Full training objective

The full objective combines vertex-space supervision, parameter supervision, an ELBO term, and the gate cross-entropy:

L = λ in v ∥V (β pred 1 )−V (β1 2 Va∗ + λ out v ∥V (β pred 1 )−V (β0 2 V \Va∗ + λβ ∥β pred 1 −β1∥ 2 + λg CE(g, a∗ ) + λkl w(t) X Kd i=1 max FB, KL q (i) ϕ ∥ p (i) θ , (5)

where a ∗ is the ground-truth region, Va∗ are its vertices, V (·) denotes the FLAME forward at zero expression/pose, w(t) is a linear KL warm-up schedule, and FB is a per-dimension free-bits floor that prevents posterior collapse without distorting the prior. The two vertex terms decompose the supervision into the region where the edit should occur (toward the target mesh) and its complement, anchored to the source. We use λ in v =λ out v =5×105 , λβ=10, λg=10, λkl=0.05, FB=0.03, with w(t) ramping linearly from 0 to 1 over the first 10 epochs.

#### A.4 Warped refining: per-pixel resampling map

Let M0(β0 ) and M1(β1 ) be the unedited and edited meshes; for each training camera and frame t we (i) render the base avatar with M0 to obtain ˜It and (ii) rasterize M1 to get, at every pixel p that the edited mesh covers, the originating triangle f(p) and its barycentric coordinates λ(p)∈ ∆2 . The exact source pixel to sample from is then π P k λk(p) V (0) f(p),k , the reprojection through the same camera π of the point with the same triangle/barycentric on the unedited mesh V (0). This is a per-pixel resampling map Wt, and the view-consistent pseudoground-truth ˆIt = Wt( ˜It) is obtained by a single remap (pixels outside the edited mesh fall back to identity sampling). Because the warp is derived from the shared mesh topology rather than from image-space optical flow, it is insensitive to texture, exact under the rasterization of M1, and free of the bracketing failures that optical-flow estimators exhibit in low-texture regions such as cheeks or forehead.

The anchor term in the refining stage,

Lanchor = 1 |Φ| X ϕ∈Φ ∥ϕ − ϕ (0)∥ 2 2 , (6)

prevents the non-frozen parameters Φ (positions, opacities, scales, rotations and the position/rotation blendshape bases) from drifting away from the warm start Φ (0); we use λanchor=10−2 and freeze the spherical-harmonic DC color channels throughout refining.

#### A.5 3DGS backbone and refining hyper-parameters

Both the base reconstruction and the refine pass share the same RGBAvatar [18] backbone with B=20 identity blendshapes layered on top of the 129 FLAME bases, 256-resolution texture, isotropic scale initialization 0.8×10−3 and opacity initialization 0.5. We train for 50,000 iterations at batch size 10 with the Adam learning rates of the original RGBAvatar schedule (position=8×10−4 , exponentially decayed to 8×10−6 over 30,000 steps; opacity=0.25; scale=2.5×10−2 ; rotation=5×10−3 ; SH features 1.25×10−2 ). Blendshape branches are activated after 3,000 warm-up iterations. The surface-consistency regularizer of the main paper (Eq. 2) corresponds to λx=3 and λ∆x=1 in our notation. Warped refining is initialized from the base-reconstruction checkpoint of the same subject (which is trained once per identity and shared across all of its edits) and reuses the same configuration with the SH-DC color channels frozen, the anchor loss enabled, and the training images replaced by the warped pseudo-ground-truths { ˆIt}.

#### A.6 Evaluation metrics

We report three complementary metrics that jointly probe prompt alignment, identity preservation, and rendering quality. Let I src n and I edit n denote the source frame and our edited render for frame n, t src and t edit the source and edit prompts, and N the number of evaluation frames.

CLIP directional similarity (CLIP-D) [32,10]. Building upon the text-driven semantic manipulation established by StyleCLIP [28], we evaluate semantic alignment using the Directional CLIP Similarity metric introduced by Gal et al. in StyleGAN-NADA [10]. Rather than the absolute image-text similarity (which is dominated by identity-agnostic content), CLIP-D measures whether the visual change between source and edit aligns with the textual change between source and edit prompts in the CLIP ViT-B/16 feature space:

∆T = ET (t edit) − ET (t src), ∆In = EI (I edit n ) − EI (I src n ), (7)

CLIP-D = 1 N X N n=1 ⟨∆In, ∆T⟩ ∥∆In∥ ∥∆T∥ , (8)

with EI , ET the CLIP image and text encoders. Reporting dirs  keeps the aggregated score positive and prevents sign cancellation across frames whose camera or expression briefly flips the projection's sign while the average direction remains aligned with the prompt. Higher is better.

ArcFace identity similarity [8]. For each frame, we detect a centered face and use the same five-point landmarks to align both I src n and I edit n to a canonical 112×112 crop, extract ℓ2-normalized 512-D ArcFace embeddings f src n and f edit n , and report the average cosine similarity:

ArcFace = 1 N X N n=1 ⟨f src n ,f edit n ⟩ ∥f src n ∥ ∥f edit n ∥ . (9)

This isolates identity drift from texture and pose differences and is the strictsense identity-preservation metric we report. Higher is better.

Anisotropic 2D Total Variation (T V2D). We use T V2D as a no-reference imagequality proxy that penalizes high-frequency spatial artifacts - floaters, jagged color seams, stair-stepped edges along the silhouette - characteristic of splatbased renderings with misaligned Gaussians. For an RGB frame I ∈[0, 1]H×W×3 ,

TV2D(I) = 1 |Ωy| X (i,j,c)∈Ωy |Ii+1,j,c−Ii,j,c| + 1 |Ωx| X (i,j,c)∈Ωx |Ii,j+1,c−Ii,j,c|, (10)

where Ωy and Ωx are the sets of valid vertical and horizontal pixel pairs across all channels, and the average is finally taken over the N evaluation frames. Normalizing by pixel count makes the score scale-comparable across resolutions; lower is better.

The locality is moreover semantic rather than rigidly bounded by the authored vertex masks: the "nose bridge shift left" edit tapers smoothly along the nasal ridge instead of clipping to the canonical nose mask, indicating that the prior pθ has internalized the correlation structure of the FLAME manifold rather than mechanically reproducing the authored edit template.

### B.3 Extended qualitative results

Figure 8 extends the qualitative results of the main paper (Fig. 5) to six subjects covering a broad range of ethnicities, skin tones, ages, and hairstyles, with three prompts per subject sampled across the eyes, lips, face, neck, nose and ears regions. Because the underlying FLAME delta is itself local (Fig. 7\), the warpedrefining stage transports the avatar's appearance along the perturbed geometry instead of re-synthesizing unedited regions: "close-set eyes" alters the interocular distance without touching the lips or hairline, and "wide lips" stretches the mouth without modifying the nasal base. The effect persists under intraregion variation - three distinct prompts on the same subject (e.g. row 3: big forehead, small forehead, wide jaw) yield clearly different face shapes while preserving eyebrow shape, skin texture and eye color, which is what drives the high ArcFace scores in Table 4. Consistent with our per-region analysis (Sec. B.1\), edits to the ears naturally appear as subtle silhouette changes from a frontal view, also confirming that the central identity features remain completely disentangled. Since every edit acts on the mesh-bound 3D Gaussian avatar rather than on a single 2D image, the rendered portraits keep their view-dependent appearance and can be driven by the tracker's expression/pose stream at >800 fps; the normal-coded FLAME insets visualize the underlying geometry that any downstream animation will inherit.

To demonstrate robustness beyond frontal views and controlled environments, Fig. 9 evaluates ManifoldSplat on an in-the-wild monocular video lacking a studio setup or controlled lighting. Despite this domain shift from the NeRSemble [16] dataset, the avatar maintains a coherent silhouette and viewdependent shading in near-profile poses. These non-frontal viewpoints are crucial to clearly visualize shape changes in nasal attributes ("aquiline nose", "protruded tip nose", and "recessed nose bridge") that are largely unnoticeable in purely frontal views. In all cases, the deformations are sharply confined to the targeted region, preserving the surrounding identity and face structure, thereby confirming our method's edit locality and multi-view consistency on unconstrained data.
