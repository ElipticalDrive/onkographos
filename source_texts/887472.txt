Part of #10932 (assessment). Scene: verse --meteor-showcase (docs/verse/meteor-showcase-handoff.md). Measure with meteor_showcase_capture --live and attach before and after impact frames from the same moment. Ideas only from the Unreal docs; no Unreal code (docs/research/unreal/AGENTS.md).
Add temporal anti-aliasing to the physical path: sub-pixel projection jitter (Halton), reprojection with motion from the previous camera and per-object motion for moving chunks, neighborhood clamping in YCoCg, and a sharpening pass. It removes edge shimmer on timber frames, roofs, and thin debris, and steadies bloom and smoke. Start on desktop High, then Medium; keep a switch to turn it off. It lays the groundwork for rendering below native resolution with upscaling.
Acceptance: a camera pan and orbit over the houses show no edge crawl in a side-by-side capture; no smearing on fast debris beyond one frame; frame-time cost under 1 ms at 1080p on High.

High-tier TAA landed in d9144ec (on main). Leaving this open for the Medium tier, which the issue names and which has no depth prepass to reproject through, and for per-object motion vectors.
What it does: Halton(2,3) 8-phase jitter on the drawn camera only (culling and shadow cascades keep the steady camera so cached cascades stay cached); resolve rebuilds the current frame at pixel centers from the jittered 3x3 neighborhood, reprojects via the depth prepass + last camera with Catmull-Rom history reads, YCoCg neighborhood clamp tightened to 1.5 sigma, inverse-luminance blend that trusts fast-moving pixels' current frame; bounded sharpen writes back before bloom. VERSE_TAA=0 turns it off.
Evidence (meteor_showcase_capture --orbit --live --settle-light, VERSE_QUALITY=high, licensed kit, this Mac): edge crawl = mean abs second temporal difference of luminance at the steepest-gradient tenth of pixels.
0.03 rad/s orbit: off 2.21 mean / 32.2 p99 -> on 1.12 / 12.7
0.1 rad/s orbit: off 6.23 / 112 -> on 3.27 / 49.9
Cost: GPU wait 4.66 vs 4.67 ms p50 at the same GPU clock (well under 1 ms at 1080p); frame p50 6.76 vs 6.94 ms.
Impact frames show no smear on fast debris. Side-by-side: docs/verse/captures/meteor-showcase/taa-orbit-side-by-side.mp4 and taa-orbit-zoom.jpg (committed proxies, off left).
Crawl is halved, not gone; details in docs/verse/meteor-showcase-handoff.md.

Done in 1f16fad (on main), on top of d9144ec.
Changes
Silhouettes: each pixel reprojects with the nearest depth of its 3x3 neighborhood, so an edge moves with what stands in front. Before this, a pixel on the roof's edge against the sky reprojected with the roof one frame and the sky the next, and that was most of the remaining crawl.
History rejection: the resolve writes a depth history (clip w, R32F). Where the history at a pixel's last place holds another surface (a chunk that moved on its own, or what it uncovered), the pixel trusts the current frame in a tight box. This handles moving chunks in place of per-object motion vectors.
Still pixels clamp to a 2.5-sigma box instead of 1.5, so the jitter's shifting neighborhood doesn't pull history back and forth.
--orbit --live no longer casts, so the houses stand for the whole orbit.

Medium tier: it stays on 4x MSAA, documented in taa.rs and the handoff. Medium is the tier for phones and the web and has no depth prepass. Adding one would draw every caster again (about 1,500 draws here) and add two full-size histories to read and write each frame, which are the costs a tile-based GPU feels most. Left: the crawl is reduced, not zero. Turning the sharpen off would cut it further (0.74 / 5.1) but softens the image. True per-object motion vectors (debris drawn with TAA rather than rejected) would need previous instance transforms in the prepass.
