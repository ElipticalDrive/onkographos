Abstract
Conventional laser-transmission display holograms have a pixel structure so complex as to be impractical for computational simulation or real-time electronic display. By reducing the information content to better match the limited appetite of the human visual system, we have made possible the computation and display of moving holographic images for the first time. However, these simulations must be further simplified and approximated in order to reach truly interactive speeds. Here we present the background of the information reduction concepts, and show how they lead to simplifications and approximations that are well suited to the structure of highly-parallelized computations. Recent progress in the implementation of interactive holographic imaging is also described.
