The million-point nested-query experiment exposes a large error hidden by one refinement step. Using the same 256 coherent finite-cube200 draws, sphere-surface collision probabilities agree at 256 and 512 points, then change by up to 21.875 percentage points at 1,024. Further changes are 3.516 pp at 2,048 and 0.391 pp at 4,096. These are surface-probe proxies, not continuous solid collision tests.
The declared simultaneous paired-disagreement bound still fails 2 pp at 2,048 → 4,096 (one affected draw out of 256; 2.770 pp upper bound). Rays have 8 affected draws at 256 → 511 positions; no later changes through 4,081 positions. Zero changes give a 1.963 pp upper bound on a finite-grid difference, not continuous-geometry accuracy. Absolute Monte Carlo accuracy and full-kernel/scalar-band accuracy remain separate open requirements.
Work:
Preserve the five-level diagnostic and all failures; do not declare convergence from one unchanged refinement.
Develop deterministic well-spaced nested sphere probes and multiple independent geometry seeds, measuring coverage gaps rather than point count alone.
Explore adaptive sphere/ray subdivision near sign changes and near-zero values, with a sound exclusion criterion where possible. Same-sign endpoints alone cannot exclude a crossing.
Distinguish surface intersection, solid overlap and containment; align the intended collision event with the downstream application.
Use held-out draws and declared statistical families for new adaptive rules; current 256 draws are development data.
Compare geometry and action errors at equal numerical and Monte Carlo accuracy; include adaptive-query overhead and a strong packed, plan-reusing direct NUFFT reference.
Keep continuous-error claims separate from finite-grid difference bounds.
Done when collision/ray resolution has a justified stopping rule and retained failure cases, with equal-quality timing through event reduction. Parent #3; also #10, #12, #23.
Report: reproduction/results/query_refinement/README.md (being published with this experiment).
