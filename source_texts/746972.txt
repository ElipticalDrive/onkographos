This study investigates the application of Conditional Neural Processes (CNPs)—an architecture combining Gaussian Processes with deep neural networks—for procedural terrain heightmap generation. Evaluated against the MNIST benchmark and a custom dataset of grayscale heightmaps (HMs) across various resolutions (28x28, 128x128, and 256x256), the CNP model successfully learned simple low-resolution shapes but failed to generate sharp, detailed features, producing heavily blurred outputs even after scaling model dimensionality. Furthermore, high-resolution tests (512x512) encountered VRAM limitations on standard consumer hardware, highlighting significant computational overhead. The findings indicate that while CNPs are unsuitable as a standalone primary generator for detailed terrain, they could potentially serve as a preliminary phase for establishing macro-level global landforms, to be refined by subsequent super-resolution techniques.
