A sixth ShaderKind, so a density field is authored with nodes instead of hand-written WGSL — fog, smoke, dust, a gas that lights.
Why it is cheap, which is the whole argument
The five kinds today — Surface, Unlit, PostProcess, Transparent, TransparentUnlit — all evaluate per pixel. A volumetric one evaluates per froxel, and the grid in kooch_lighting is 4096 cells against ~921,600 pixels at 1280x720: a factor of 225.
That is the difference between a ray-march per pixel, which does not fit in 13.9 ms, and writing a density field once into a 3D texture that every pixel then only reads.
What the grid already gives it
🔴 cluster_common.wgsl already reserves the per-cell list types:
```
// 🔴 Ranges for probes, volumes and decals are reserved now — five words
//    per cell — or adding them is a rewrite.
const CLUSTER_TYPE_VOLUME: u32 = 3u;
```
So a volume can join a cell's lists without redesigning the grid, and page_mark_froxels.wgsl is the precedent that the grid is already read by something other than shading.
A volume injected into the cell lists is lit by the clustering that already runs, rather than shading itself against every light in the scene.
Scope
ShaderKind::Volumetric, named by a // kind: volumetric line like the other five.
A node-graph entry point over a position in the volume rather than a surface: something shaped like fn volume(VolumeInput) -> VolumeOutput returning density, scattering albedo and emission.
Dispatched over the froxel grid, writing the 3D texture the shading pass samples.
The graph's preview has to show a slice or a ray-marched thumbnail — a density field renders to nothing as a flat swatch.
Dependencies
#977 is the real one: volumetric light and fog on the froxel grid. That issue builds the pass, the 3D texture and the temporal reprojection; this one makes the field authorable instead of hard-coded. Doing this first would be a graph kind with nowhere to run.
Related: #1178 (shader kinds in the graph — the epic this is a member of), #1235 (volumetric particles in the froxel grid), #249 / #731 (clouds, which are a different scale — see below).
Clouds are the same grid, and that is already decided
An earlier draft of this issue said clouds could not live in the froxel grid because ClusterSettings::far is 200 m. That was wrong, and #731 had settled it on 2026-08-26: the range is a parameter, not a limit of the technique.
The owner had already shipped clouds as volumetric fog in another project — the froxel volume as the cloud medium rather than a separate cloud technique — and it performed. Godot's volumetric_fog_length reaches 1024 m and volumetric_fog_detail_spread biases the Z distribution, which is what lets one volume cover both the fog near the camera and the layer above it.
So the grid serves four consumers, not three: clustering (#780), aerial perspective (#976), fog (#977) and clouds (#731). Sizing it is the whole design and it is decided once — which raises the stakes on this issue rather than lowering them, because a Volumetric kind authors the field all four read.
Measured, on the device (#731, 2026-08-13): the per-pixel cloud raymarch cost 39.83 ms of the sky scope against 1.22 ms with clouds off — 97 % of the pass, nearly three whole 13.9 ms frames. The froxel volume is what replaces it. The remaining blocker there is quarter-resolution temporal reprojection, which needs motion vectors that do not exist yet (#481 / #732).
