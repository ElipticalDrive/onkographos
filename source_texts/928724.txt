Requested by Jeff
Before: the scattering was a loose set of BlinkScript nodes built by a script, lit only by hand-typed sun knobs. The HDRI was used only as a background for the preview comp.
After: one EzAtmosScatter node (Nodes > EzAtmosScatter) with an hdri input and an optional sun Axis input. It finds the sun in the HDRI and takes its colour, brightness and sky light from the image. It has plain-named knobs and a view output menu (Scatter Preview Comp by default, then Scatter, Sky (Rayleigh), Haze (Mie), Sky Light (Ambient) and Transmittance). Every pass is also written to its own atmos_* layer.
How:
blink/EzAtmosLight.blink (new) scans a 512x256 copy of the HDRI. It finds the brightest spot above the horizon, treats a 6° cone around it as the sun (centroid direction and irradiance), and averages the rest of the sky. It also flags a clipped sun, meaning a peak under 20x the average sky.
blink/AtmosScatter.blink reads that through a second input:
Sun direction: from the HDRI, the Axis's world position, or the manual knob.
Unclipped sun: its irradiance is divided by the transmittance towards the sun, giving top-of-atmosphere light.
Clipped sun: it is sized from the sky with the model's clear-sky ratio, and sun boost scales that.
Sky light: a new skyAverage * (1 - transmittance) ambient term.
python/build_gizmo.py builds the group from the kernels and writes gizmos/EzAtmosScatter.gizmo. init.py and menu.py register it. It replaces python/build_test_setup.py.
Loading the kernel through kernelSourceFile fixes the old script's headless failure, where setting kernelSource from Python never rebuilt the parameter knobs.
Tested in Nuke 15.1v3 (headless, NukeX):
Gizmo: it loads, all six view outputs and five layers render, and Axis mode with and without an Axis connected behaves as expected (it falls back to Manual when no Axis is plugged in). The "copy detected sun to manual" button works.
Synthetic HDRI with a known sun (az −150°, el 38°): it detects (−0.392, 0.616, 0.683) against the true (−0.394, 0.616, 0.682), and measures sun irradiance 23 against about 21.5 expected.
Gemini JPEG panorama with a clipped sun: it detects (−0.18, 0.69, 0.71) and the clipped path gives a sky close to the unclipped one.
Not covered: reference/atmos_reference.py still mirrors only the scattering core (no HDRI light or sky light), and no NumPy run was possible here.
Speed-up (second commit):
The optical depth towards the sun is read from a 256x64 table (blink/EzAtmosTransmittanceLUT.blink, Bruneton's parameterisation) instead of being marched at every view sample. The Transmittance and Sky Light passes skip the sun entirely.
New viewer resolution (GUI, default Half) and render resolution (terminal and farm, default Full) knobs compute the passes at Full, Half or Quarter size. $gui decides which knob applies. The sky and ground halves are scaled up separately so the horizon stays sharp.
Checked against the previous build on the Gemini HDRI: the mean difference is 0.1–0.4%, the max is 2%, and the horizon edge stays a one-row step at all three resolutions.
Warm render times at 3044x1522 on CPU: 8.3 s before, then 3.1 s at Full, 0.95 s at Half and 0.36 s at Quarter. On the GPU (RTX 3070) both builds take about 0.2 s, since the GPU was already fast enough.
Terminal Nuke only uses the GPU with --gpu, and every new process spends about 7 s (GPU) or 30 s (CPU) compiling the kernels. Both points are noted in the README for farm renders.
Depth haze (replaces the earlier "fill below horizon"): feedback said that Extend Horizon and Mirror Sky weren't physical, so they're gone. In their place:
A Haze Color view and an atmos_haze_color layer: the colour infinitely deep haze settles to in each direction, computed as (sun · Tsun · scatter(μ) + sky · scatter) / extinction at camera height. It has no ground and no horizon line, so a plate can be hazed with any depth pass using plate·T + hazeColor·(1−T).
A read-only haze density (1/m) knob giving the extinction at camera height, for computing T = exp(−density·depth) in a comp.
Haze height, moved to the main panel, and a Haze Preview view. The preview hazes the preview comp to haze distance, using the exact optical depth for haze that thins exponentially with height (flat-ground approximation).
A passes switch (Atmosphere / Depth Haze). In Depth Haze mode, Scatter, Sky, Haze, Sky Light and Transmittance are computed for the haze around the camera out to haze distance, so the layers composite exactly as before. I checked that the layers sum to Scatter and that the comp equals hdri·T + scatter.
Tested on the Gemini HDRI: Haze Preview at 0 km equals the preview comp, the gizmo shows no node errors, and I rendered a distance series at 1, 3 and 10 km.
