Adds a DICOM Volume example that downloads a permissively licensed head MRI at runtime and renders it three ways with a fragment-shader raymarch, MPR (a movable cross-section plane), MIP (maximum intensity projection), and DVR (direct volume rendering with a transfer function). Window/level, the transfer function, colormap, and the view mode are live shader uniforms, so there is no per-frame CPU work on the volume. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, and a button toggles a black or white backdrop.
Flutter GPU has no 3D textures, so the scalar volume is stored in a 2D slice atlas (Z slices tiled into a grid) that the shader samples with manual trilinear filtering across tiles. The data is packed as r32Float (single-channel 32-bit float), which holds any 12 to 16 bit DICOM value exactly. flutter_scene's internal GPU shim did not mirror r32Float yet, so this adds it to the analyzer stub and maps it to WebGL2 R32F/RED/FLOAT in the web backend. That is the only change to the published package, and it mirrors a format already present in flutter_gpu on the Flutter master channel.
The volume is oriented from the DICOM patient coordinate system so that Superior is up and Anterior faces the camera. Because patient space is right-handed, the placement uses the same odd-parity flip the glTF importer uses (scale(1,1,-1)), so the anatomy renders un-mirrored. An orientation compass in the top-right labels the world axes R/L, A/P, S/I from the same mapping, so the anatomical orientation is verifiable at a glance.
A minimal DICOM reader parses uncompressed little-endian files (explicit and implicit VR) and reads only the tags the renderer needs. Encapsulated and compressed transfer syntaxes are left as a TODO(dicom). Parsing and atlas packing run on a background isolate; the GPU upload stays on the raster thread. Byte-level tests cover the reader and the volume assembly (tag parsing, slice ordering by ImagePositionPatient, and MONOCHROME1 inversion).
The reference dataset is the datalad example-dicom-structural set, a de-faced T1 head MRI released into the public domain under the PDDL, fetched once and cached under the app support directory, with a CC-BY-SA head MRI on Zenodo as a fallback.
Verified on macOS/Metal. The single-texture atlas for the reference volume (274x384x384) is close to the 8192 texel limit, so very large volumes are not yet supported; a split-atlas or a downsample-to-fit is a follow-up, along with the cross-backend smoke matrix and quality passes (gradient shading, empty-space skipping, half-resolution DVR).
