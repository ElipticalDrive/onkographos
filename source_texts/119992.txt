1.2 Shape
Preserving
Interpolation
A shape preserving interpolant is a function which passes through some given data and
retains the intrinsic shape implied by the given data.
_) Four papers related to this topic, which were noted in last year's report, were published in
1989, namely [Foley and Ely' '89], [Foley, Goodman & Unsworth "89], .[Foley and Nielson
"89] and [Nielson and Foley "89].
Researchers:
Foley, Gregory, Hamann, Lane, Neuser
• Research on curve and surface interpolation with interactive shape control parameters was
published in [Foley and Neuser "89]. This method uses piecewise quintic polynomials to
represent a function which optimizes a norm on the first, second and third derivatives.
I
presented this research in January at the Sixth Texas Symposium on Approximation Theory.
• Hamann and I have worked on an interpolation method that opt_zes
a measure of the
function's second derivative. See B. Hamann's section for figures of this technique. A draft
of the paper has been written.
• John Gregory, of Brunei University, visited our research group in April. John and I
developed a convexity preserving curve interpolation method based on geometric principles,
and D. Lane implemented the technique. This effective approach was presented in November
at R.P.I., at which time we were surprised to learn that someone else had developed the same
method in the past year, but they had not yet published it. We did obtain additional theoretical
results and we are investigating how to generalize this method to surfaces.
