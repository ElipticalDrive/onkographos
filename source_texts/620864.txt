Change
Preparation can now say what an object's default camera looks at, with no browser. tools/objects/default-view.mts builds the default scene matrix the way the runtime does (rotateX(scene pitch) · rotateY(yaw)) and hands it to the runtime's own worldCameraFromPresentation, which gives the pose the app reports through captureWorldCamera. From that pose: the sub-camera point on the body, and the screen angle of any direction.
The generic lane uses it as a rule: a surface-observation lens names the body point its frame looks at, and the default camera must look within 25 degrees of it. Two of Betelgeuse's default views today pointed at the far side and were only found with screenshots; this refuses them at preparation time with the numbers in the message.
Sources and processing
No sources change. The check reads the prepared camera plan, the object's world frame and the body-fixed matrix from src/platform/solar-geometry.mts.
Evidence and limits
Proves the change:
tests/objects/unit/betelgeuse/default-view.test.mts: the sub-camera point is 18 degrees from the lens's sub-observer point (longitude -13.7, latitude -11.8), the pole sits 40.1 degrees from screen-right, celestial north is up. Every number agrees with what Chrome measured through the app's world camera and the polar-cap leaf on 2026-09-16 (sub-camera -12.1, -10.5 at pitch 0; pole 40.06 degrees).
The check refuses the far-side default that shipped earlier today (yaw 270): "166.0 degrees from the lens's sub-observer point; the limit is 25".
Betelgeuse prepares unchanged under the rule; tests/objects/unit/betelgeuse 9 pass; pnpm typecheck clean.
Measured and left open: the same math shows celestial east 90 degrees clockwise of north on screen at the default view, the sky's mirror. The test pins it as measured so a renderer fix flips it deliberately; the Betelgeuse README records it.
