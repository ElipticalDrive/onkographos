What
MeshDeviation's map only sampled the vertices. Between them, the only certified number was the global Hausdorff bound. ComputeTriangleBounds adds an interval per triangle of Mesh:
TriangleDeviationLower / TriangleDeviationUpper: the interval contains the largest (unsigned) distance from points of the triangle to the reference.
No point of the triangle is farther than the upper bound.
The lower bound is attained at TriangleWorstPoint and is at least the largest corner value.
TriangleBoundsUnconverged: triangles that ran out of their MaxTriangleSubdivisions budget (default 1000). They keep a wider, still valid interval.
Cap by the Hausdorff bound: with ComputeCertifiedBound, the upper bounds are capped by HausdorffUpperBound. A triangle that the cap brings within the tolerance counts as converged.
TriangleUpperStatistics: statistics of the upper bounds, weighted by area. FractionWithin(tol) is a certified lower bound of the area fraction within tol.
TriangleDeviationBounds.MaxDistance runs the branch and bound of MeshHausdorffDistance (Sacht & Jacobson 2024, cascading upper bounds) on a single triangle:
Determinism: each triangle is refined sequentially and writes only its own slots. The parallel loop is deterministic.
Far from the origin (Analysis: meshes far from the origin, LO-RANSAC measured, benchmark results #102): it runs on the local copies (with the reference's offset), and the default tolerance is 1e-4 of the bounding box diagonal, not of the coordinates.
Reuse: the corner distances and closest triangles come from the vertex pass.
The max-heap of MeshHausdorffDistance is now shared (DeviationMaxHeap<T>). No existing public member changed.
One side effect of computing the Hausdorff interval before the triangle bounds: when Compute() returns false because the Hausdorff step fails (cancelled, or a non-finite vertex), the triangle outputs stay null. The return value is the same as before.
Tests (MeshDeviationTests, 7 new)
Hole: a reference plane with a square hole and one triangle over it.
Every vertex has deviation 0, and the triangle interval contains the exact 1.
With 0 subdivisions the interval is wider but valid and reported as unconverged, and it is capped by the Hausdorff bound.
Cap converges: a corner at the hole center, with 0 subdivisions. The cap brings the interval within the tolerance, so the triangle counts as converged.
Shifted spheres: a coarse sphere against a finer, larger, shifted one.
A 12×12 barycentric sampling stays below every upper bound.
Each interval is at most the tolerance wide.
The intervals overlap the certified Hausdorff interval.
Determinism and invariance: a run on a new tree gives bitwise the same result. Meters (×0.001) and geo coordinates (offset 5e6) give the same results, worst point included.
Gaps and a given tree: gaps in the mesh's IDs, and a tree given by the caller on far coordinates. The width stays ≤ 1e-4 of the diagonal there.
MaxDistance inputs: a wrong tree, non-finite corners, and a triangle that lies on the reference.
Mutations (filter MeshDeviationTests; each caught by at least one test):
no subdivision loop;
upper = lower;
pruning too early;
worst point not tracked;
worst point without the offset;
no local copy far from the origin;
tolerance from the coordinates (survived at first; caught after 2afac5f);
unconverged triangles not counted;
no cap by the Hausdorff bound;
the cap does not update the convergence (caught after f3b8011).
One mutation is not tested: Math.Max(lo, HausdorffUpperBound) → HausdorffUpperBound. The Math.Max only guards against rounding, since the Hausdorff bound is never below a distance that is actually attained.
Benchmark (AnalysisBench.cs, BENCH_ANALYSIS=deviation, 100 of the 400 meshes: awk 'NR%4==1', medians)
Two cases per mesh, each timed for the vertex values alone and with the triangle bounds (tolerance 1e-4 × diagonal):
red: the copy reduced to half against the input. Its triangles are big compared with the input's details.
pert: the input against a copy whose vertices are moved along their normals by up to 2e-3 × diagonal, in a fixed pattern per vertex. This case is the same in every transform.

Bounds against the vertex values (none):
red:
The largest upper bound is 2.0× the largest vertex value.
On 9.6 % of the area the interior is certainly (lower bound) farther than its corners plus the tolerance.
The area certified within 1e-3 × diagonal is 99.86 %.
pert:
The largest upper bound is 1.025× the largest vertex value.
On 43.6 % of the area the interior is certainly farther than its corners.
The area certified within 1e-3 × diagonal is 20 %.
Invariants: in all three transforms there are 0 NaN, 0 lower bounds below a corner value and 0 inverted intervals. The red intervals never lie apart from the Hausdorff interval.
Invariance:
pert: the largest lower and upper bounds differ by ≤ 1e-4 × diagonal between none, meters and geo, that is, within the tolerance.
red: it differs by up to 0.07. That is the Reducer: its collapse order depends on rounding, so the reference differs per transform (the reason analyze_analysis.py leaves out dev_reduced_*).
Found with it: on 16–20 of the 100 meshes, the largest upper bound in red was above the certified Hausdorff bound, from unconverged triangles. Hence the cap (34ee488).
The numbers above are from the run before the cap.
The benchmark computes the triangle bounds and the Hausdorff interval in separate instances, so it does not measure the cap. The cap is covered by the unit tests.
With both outputs, the run time is the sum of the two.
Limits:
An interval of width 1e-4 × diagonal on every triangle costs about as much as the global Hausdorff interval, or more: the global bound may drop every triangle below its lower bound, the map may not.
Triangles over plateaus of the distance (many reference triangles at nearly the same distance) can run out of the budget. They then keep a valid but wider interval. That is about 0.5 % of the triangles in red.
