Transform::rotation is declared glm::quat rotation{}; (engine/include/transform.hpp). Without GLM_FORCE_CTOR_INIT, which the engine does not define, glm's quaternion constructor is defaulted. So {} value-initialises it to all zeros (w = x = y = z = 0), not to the identity (w = 1).
Where it shows:
Every saved scene has "rotation": {"w": 0.0, "x": 0.0, "y": 0.0, "z": 0.0} on any entity whose rotation was never set (see editor/src/scene/*.json).
glm::toMat4 of the zero quaternion happens to give the identity matrix, so Transform::Update and drawing come out right. That is why nothing has looked wrong.
Anything that uses the quaternion as a rotation gets nonsense: rotation * v is the zero vector, glm::slerp and glm::normalize divide by zero, and glm::eulerAngles is meaningless. Code reading a default transform's facing has to go through a matrix to avoid it, as the editor's camera controller does (glm::mat3_cast).
What it needs:
Default the field to the identity, glm::quat rotation{1.f, .0f, .0f, .0f};, as Camera's and Light's rotations were before place cameras and lights by their entity's transform #33.
Read a saved zero quaternion as the identity, so the scenes already saved come back right. Optionally, rewrite the editor's scenes.
Check other components with a defaulted glm::quat for the same problem.
