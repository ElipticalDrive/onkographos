Abstract
3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) has gained popularity in inverse rendering due to its superiority in efficiency and versatility. However, existing methods are usually affected by primitives detached from the scene surface,i.e., Gaussian floaters, which lead to an unsmooth surface. To address this issue, we propose a glocal Gaussian splatting-based framework (G2S2-IR) for inverse rendering, which introduces global and local surface awareness to guide a reasonable distribution of Gaussian ellipsoids and therefore achieves high-quality physically based rendering results. Specifically, pixel-aligned global geometry initialization is first proposed to initialize the Gaussian ellipsoids surrounding the coherent surface. The seemingly well-initialized Gaussian ellipsoids cannot eliminate the floaters due to the under-constrained optimization. Therefore, the local surface aware manipulation is designed to compensate for the overfitting to multi-view RGB images. Further, we introduce a differentiable splatting technique with a geometry-guided density control to optimize the unknown illumination and the material-aware Gaussians. Extensive experiments on five public benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in geometry reconstruction and novel view synthesis, as well as its competitive performance in inverse rendering, including material decomposition and relighting.
