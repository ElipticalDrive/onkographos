The distance rings rendered as a smooth shell: lod_tile_mesh sampled one height per grid
vertex and joined the corners, so distant mountains were clean triangles with gradient-shaded
slopes and no step anywhere. Each coarse cell is now one flat top at its own quantised height,
each difference to a neighbour is a vertical wall spanning the full step, and every face carries
its own normal and colour, so a top and the wall under it break hard.
Based on main at 8367740 (#77 and #78 already merged). The diff is the tile mesh builder, its
tests, and one doc-comment line in the sample.
What changed
Flat tops. One column sample per coarse cell centre, over a (32+2)^2 grid (one cell of
halo each side). The halo is what makes a tile border decidable: the height across the border is
the neighbouring tile's own cell centre, so both tiles see the same pair of numbers.
Walls. A face between two drawn cells belongs to the higher cell, so exactly one of the
two tiles emits it. Tiles meet with neither a crack nor a doubled wall; the tests check both, on
x borders and on z borders (the pair that catches a row inversion).
Quantisation. Heights are floored to a per-level step - never raised above the sampled
ground, so a ring cannot occlude the finer mesh inside it. LOD_STEP_M sits next to
LANDSCAPE_RINGS with the derivation in the doc comment: a step must subtend 2 px at the ring's
outer half-extent (65 degrees over a 1440 px viewport = 1.576 mm per metre of distance),
rounded to the nearest power of two metres and never below the world's 1 m voxel. That gives
1, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 m for the 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 m rings; a test reproduces the table from the
ring set with the same integer arithmetic the comment describes.
Greedy merge. Tops merge into maximal rectangles of equal (height, top colour), walls into
runs of equal (top, foot, colour). A flat 1024-cell tile is one quad; a wall run is one quad
however many cells it spans.
Flat shading. Four vertices per face, one normal and one colour each; top normal +Y, wall
normals exactly ±x/±z. Winding is checked against the normal per quad (the main pass culls
back faces), and the axis normals also make the shader's indirect_diffuse take its axis path
instead of returning zero for a non-axis normal.
Wall colour is the sub-surface material - soil under grass, stone under snow - for a step
inside sub_depth, bare stone below that. This replaces the old always-STONE skirt colour and
is what gives a wall a real break against the top above it. It is the same material
material_in_column reports, without the per-voxel ore/rock hash; stated simplification.
Skirts are gone, not kept. A face against a cell this level does not draw (hole, bound, clip
edge) is a wall that reaches FILTER_WALL_CELLS (= 2, the old skirt depth) cells below its own
top, and further down when the neighbour across it stands lower. So the free-edge guarantee is
the same depth as before, expressed as one merged run per step instead of per-cell quads.
cell_drawn answers "always yes" for halo cells. A halo cell is a neighbouring tile's cell, and
the sample normalises a clip to the tile before caching a filter, so letting the clip decide a
halo cell would make two filters with equal geometry produce different meshes - the existing
normalizing_a_filter_cannot_change_the_mesh test caught exactly that.
Heights, quantisation, wall spans and positions are integer arithmetic; only the vertex colours and
the i32 -> f32 position conversion are float, as before.

I am not claiming that one as a pass. Two effects, both stated rather than tuned away:
Fog, not shading, limits the far band. Aerial perspective (FOG_DENSITY 0.0006) leaves 30% of
a surface's own colour at 2 km, 9% at 4 km and 3% at 8 km; a top-vs-wall lighting break is ~33
levels before fog and under the metric's 8 levels past ~4 km. The 4-8 km mountains are hazy in the
before image too. FOG_DENSITY is the sample's knob and the lead's call; this change does not
touch it.
K2 falls on the big crop, from 17.4 to 2.6: flat shading removes the per-triangle gradient
that was inflating the distinct-colour count there, and the band is mostly smooth sky and sea. The
metric's K2 is therefore the one number that a flat-shaded mesh moves the other way on a
fog-dominated crop - on the terrain crop it still passes (19.8).
A per-window scan of the after frame (256 px windows of the y 330-370 band) gives K1 0.028 and
0.035 in the two leftmost windows, where the land is 1-2 km away, 0.020 in the third, and 0.007 in
the rightmost, where the same land is 4-8 km away and fog-washed. The same scan on the before frame
is 0.007, 0.010, 0.010, 0.005.

After: the same hills are stacked plateaus - flat tops with grey stone/soil faces under them,

```
`lod_tile_mesh` sampled one height per grid vertex and joined the corners, so
every ring rendered as a smooth heightfield: distant mountains were clean
triangles with gradient-shaded slopes and no step anywhere. Each coarse cell is
now one flat top at its own quantised height, every difference to a neighbour is
a vertical wall spanning the full step, and every face carries its own normal
and colour, so a top and the wall under it break hard.

- Heights come from one sample at the cell centre, over a (32+2)^2 grid, so a
  tile border is decided from the neighbour's own cell across it. A face between
  two drawn cells belongs to the higher one, so two tiles agree on the wall
  between them without drawing it twice: no crack and no doubled wall.
- Heights are floored to a per-level step derived from what subtends 2 px at
  that ring's outer distance - 1 m at the 2 m ring, 16 m at the 64 m ring.
  `LOD_STEP_M` sits next to `LANDSCAPE_RINGS` with the derivation and a test
  that reproduces it from the ring set.
- Coplanar tops are greedy-merged into rectangles and equal walls into runs:
  a flat 1024-cell tile is one quad, a wall run is one quad however many cells
  it spans.
- A wall shows the cell's sub-surface material - soil under grass, stone under
  snow - which is what makes it read as a face rather than a darker copy of the
  top above it.
- The boundary skirts are gone. A face against a cell this level does not draw
  (hole, bound, clip edge) is a wall that reaches `FILTER_WALL_CELLS` cells
  below its own top, and further down when the neighbour across it is lower.

Heights, quantisation, wall spans and positions are integer; only the vertex
colours and the i32 to f32 position conversion are float, as before.

Measured at the sample's spawn camera over the 384-tile plan (host, llvmpipe,
debug build, A/B on one machine): tile geometry 57240 KiB to 31145 KiB, 697792
to 379674 triangles, per-tile generation 0.73 ms to 0.83 ms. The tile digest
moves with the builder: 0xa5ee_08d9_33ba_1fc4 to 0x63ec_1387_d1e7_09da (the
digest now mixes the whole normal, not just its y channel).

Tests: flat tops at their own quantised height per cell, one wall spanning the
step across both x and z tile borders, walls on a hole edge, per-face shading
and winding, the step table against its derivation, the merge rules on synthetic
grids, a flat sea tile costing one quad, and the ring plan geometry budget
against the pre-voxel numbers.
```
