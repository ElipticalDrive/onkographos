Question
How do existing projects represent a spherical voxel Planet, and how do they render it from the ground up to orbit?
Cover:
Grid choices: cube-sphere projection, local gravity-aligned grids, or a plain Cartesian grid with radial gravity, and the trade-offs of each (distortion, seams, "up" direction, building on a curved surface)
Large-world precision: double precision versus floating origin versus camera-relative rendering
LOD and streaming schemes that keep a huge render distance and support a continuous Zoom to orbit (clipmaps, octree LOD, impostors at distance)
Prior art: Astroneer, Space Engineers, Seed of Andromeda, Outer Wilds, No Man's Sky, Avorion, and voxel-planet devlogs
Include rough feasibility numbers relating Planet radius to Voxel size: the data needed for full-detail editing near players versus generated-on-demand far away.

Resolved. Findings: https://github.com/theHimanshuShekhar/bhayanakvoxel/blob/research/spherical-planets/docs/research/spherical-planets.md
Grid: one plain Cartesian Voxel grid centred on the Planet, with radial gravity (same approach as Space Engineers and Astroneer). It has no seams and keeps every Voxel a true cube, which suits path tracing. Use a cube-sphere only for 2D generator inputs and the orbit-level heightfield.
Precision: int32 Voxel coordinates are enough for every option in scope (at most 27 bits for 1000 km at 2 cm). Use doubles for entities and physics, and send the GPU float32 values relative to the camera (RTE/RTC). Plain float32 Planet coordinates break above roughly 10–100 km.
LOD and storage: a camera-centred clipmap of 2× levels over sparse bricks. Store only an Edit delta on top of a deterministic generator and regenerate everything else. The rendered working set is bounded by screen pixels, not by Planet radius.
Numbers: radius mostly sets gameplay and adds about one LOD level per doubling. Voxel size is the expensive setting: 125,000 Voxels per m³ at 2 cm against 1,000 at 10 cm. Suggested starting point: 10 cm Voxels and R ≈ 4–16 km, both kept configurable.
