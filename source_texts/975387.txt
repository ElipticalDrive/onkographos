What is asked
A viewer renders the real scroll volumes from a reproducible pose, streams only what the camera needs, paints ink predictions honestly, and compares traced surfaces with published ones.
Why it matters: Moving through the scan answers questions that flat slices answer badly, and streaming a terabyte-scale volume is a data-movement problem first.
In ring 0: LplVesuvius is a separate hosted project; it reuses LplPlugin's voxel and zarr modules but does not run in ring 0.
What is true today
Done by 2026-08-31: the scroll adapter and lpl-scrollwalk, the volume streamer, the lpl-scrollfly window, the ink overlay, segment loading and surface comparison, and per-level calibration. The repository was rewritten on 2026-09-28; apps/scrollfly, apps/scrollwalk and the scroll streamer and segment code are still there (checked 2026-10-02).
What is missing
The disk cache of coarse levels, corridor prefetch, real ink maps with their provenance, the measurement scripts in the tree, and three viewer ideas.
How we will know it is done
Flying at walking speed along the corridor never stalls on a load, and every painted ink map shows its source and score.
