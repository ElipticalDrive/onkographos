Epic recently published details regarding their approach in https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf. Advancements over our current Bruneton model include:
Precompute low resolution data for the current viewpoint each(?) frame to simplify fragment shader workload. Improves performance at high resolutions; good for VR! Falls back to raymarching from space, might be able to fix that with better parameterization.
Volume texture for aerial perspective. Avoids subtle horizon artifacts, but requires making assumptions about the scene. May or may not be worthwhile. I tried this before based on Bodare's work but had trouble with severe depth aliasing, but it might be worth revisiting, especially given working example code from Unreal.
Approximate infinite-order scattering and improved numerical stability.
Fast precompute, allowing responsive dynamic atmosphere parameters.
According to the paper, the Unreal approach can afford to recompute every LUT each frame while being only ~60% slower; choosing to instead cache results should produce a much faster solution.
