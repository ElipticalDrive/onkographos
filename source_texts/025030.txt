Isosurface extraction is a standard method for visualizing scalar volume data that can be used to render a specific\nmaterial boundaries inherent in multi-material data sets. Multiple transparent isosurfaces can thus be used to\nvisualize multiple material boundaries, but still fail to capture any data in between the boundary layers. We\ndescribe how isosurfaces can be “enriched” with surrounding material information. By visualizing surrounding\nmaterial, both material boundary information and gradient - or change in density - information of the scalar\nfield are represented. Visualizing multiple transparent material-enriched isosurfaces leads to a fairly effective\nvolumetric impression. Thus, our approach approximates results obtained from direct volume rendering.\nThe visualization of multiple transparent isosurfaces requires a back-to-front rendering of the typically triangulated\nsurface components. The order of the surfaces’ triangles is imposed by the location of the convex cells they are\nextracted from, which supports fast rendering of multiple isosurface.
