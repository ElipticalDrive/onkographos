Issue body actions
A triangle mesh of any shape EBGeometry can evaluate (a tape, a fused composition, a mesh SDF, a user shape), extracted by dual contouring over an adaptive octree. Three uses, in order of how much they ask of it: rendering, surface meshing, and as a structure that bounds the exact surface, which turns a sphere-traced shape into a ray-traced one.
Why
Every shape without a ray form is marched today: a ray steps through the distance field until it reaches the surface. Smooth unions and blends, mollifications, Perlin noise, and meshes inside smooth operations in a tape all work this way. Marching is slow where it matters most: near grazing surfaces, in thin gaps, and on a GPU, where neighboring rays need very different numbers of steps (#206, #289, #255). A triangle mesh of the shape can be ray traced, rasterized and exported, and its octree tells where the surface can and cannot be.
The mesher
Octree, culled by the distance bound. A cell is empty when the value at its center exceeds its half-diagonal. For a shape of Exact or Bound quality, the true distance is at least the value's magnitude, so the cull is safe. A NotADistance shape gets no cull and needs a dense grid. Cells near the surface are refined to a target size, or by an error criterion.
Hermite data. On each cell edge whose ends have opposite signs, find the crossing (by bisection, or a few steps along the edge) and the normal there. Normals come from the gradient by finite differences, or in closed form once Closed-form gradients (closest point) for the mesh SDFs, and gradients through the tape #204 lands.
One vertex per surface cell, one quad per crossed edge.
First milestone, surface nets: the vertex is the mean of the cell's edge crossings. Simple, and the mesh is closed, but sharp edges come out rounded.
Dual contouring: the vertex minimizes the cell's least-squares fit to the tangent planes, clamped to the cell. That puts the vertex on the corner or edge where the planes meet, so the box edges and drilled holes of a CSG part stay sharp.
Surface ids. Each triangle carries the surface it lies on: closestSurface() at its center (Tape: surfaces and labels, for boundary conditions (Label(expr, name), tape.surfaces()) #256), so labels, per-member ids and parts carry over to every use below.
Known limits.
Features thinner than a cell can be missed (no sign change at the cell's corners).
Plain dual contouring can make non-manifold vertices where two sheets meet in one cell; a manifold variant fixes that at more complexity.
Meshing time grows with the number of surface cells, so a geometry that changes must be meshed again.
Use 1: rendering
A raster mode in the viewer: rasterize the mesh while the camera moves, and ray trace the exact frame once it rests. Rasterizing is fast exactly where ray tracing is slowest today: marched scenes and meshes in tapes. Section planes become clip planes, x-ray becomes blending, and coloring by surface comes for free from the per-triangle ids. The window is SDL2 already, so an OpenGL context is the only new dependency. This is the "tessellated preview" of #289, made concrete. The default ray-traced render path must not get slower from any of it.
Use 2: surface meshing
Export to STL, OBJ or PLY, with the surface ids as face groups or physical groups (#288 is the import side). Uses:
feeding other tools: finite-element and volume meshers, 3D printing, CAD viewers;
a CSG or smooth part turned into a TriMeshSDF, whose closed-form ray form then replaces marching;
a geometry check: the mesh's distance from the shape, measured as |signedDistance| at sampled mesh points, bounds the deviation.
Use 3: a dual structure that bounds the exact surface
The octree's surface cells (the cells the cull did not reject) contain every point of the surface of an Exact or Bound shape. A BVH over those cells, with the mesh inside each cell as a first guess, is then a conservative cover of the exact surface:
Rays, rendering included.
A ray that meets no surface cell misses the shape: that is empty-space skipping with a guarantee (GPU sphere tracing of CSG tapes without tiny steps (Lipschitz pruning, optional BVH empty-space skipping) #206).
A ray that meets one intersects the cell's triangles for a first guess, then refines the exact crossing within that cell in a few steps. That replaces hundreds of march steps across the scene, and turns a sphere-traced shape into, in effect, a ray-traced one.
Shadow and occlusion rays only need the any-crossing test, which the cover answers mostly without refinement (Viewer: interactive speed on marched scenes (exact smooth unions in tapes, one-pass crossings, a tessellated preview) #289).
Fast intersection tests.
Segment, box and particle tests against the shape (intersects(), particle-wall collisions in RayParticles, contacts in Simulations/: a folder for complete EBGeometry programs (particle settling and collisions, neighbour merges) #249) first ask the cover: outside every surface cell, there is no crossing.
Only a query that reaches a surface cell evaluates the exact shape.
As a shape wrapper. It could be a wrapper, say Covered<S>, holding the shape, its octree cover and its mesh, with a ray form, so that any code tracing rays gets it without change.
Plan, one pull request each
The octree, its cull and the Hermite data, with surface nets on the host. Mesh output with surface ids, and export.
Dual-contouring vertices (sharp features), an accuracy report, and a device build.
The cover as a ray form (the wrapper above), tested against marching for the same shapes, with performance cases on marched scenes.
The viewer's raster mode (Viewer: interactive speed on marched scenes (exact smooth unions in tapes, one-pass crossings, a tessellated preview) #289).
Open questions
How fine to refine: a fixed cell size, an error tolerance against the shape, or curvature.
Whether the mesher's octree should be the same structure as per-leaf tape shortening (Tape: shortened per octree leaf, as a Tape option (Lipschitz pruning, MPR-style; GPU tape plan B) #253), which also subdivides space by the distance bound.
How much the cover costs in memory on large scenes, and whether a cell's mesh is stored or rebuilt on demand.
Whether a manifold variant is needed for export to volume meshers.
