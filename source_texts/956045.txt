Your idea
Feature Overview
Implement a lightweight, math-driven biomes layer that estimates local soil temperature using existing terrain variables (Latitude, Elevation, and Surface Normal/Aspect). The procedural generation engine will use this calculated "Virtual Soil Temperature" to organically dictate tree line elevation, foliage density, and asset transitions without requiring any external data files.
Technical Concept: The Estimation Formula
Instead of querying a map, the engine calculates a Virtual Soil Temperature ($T_{soil}$) for any given point on the terrain using three runtime inputs:
$$T_{soil} = T_{base} - (Lat \times Cost_{lat}) - (Elev \times LapseRate) + (Aspect \times SunExposure)$$
Base Temperature ($T_{base}$): The theoretical sea-level temperature at the equator.
Latitude Penalty ($Lat$): Drops the temperature the further a chunk is generated from the world’s equator.
Lapse Rate ($LapseRate$): Approximates the real-world adiabatic cooling rate (e.g., dropping $\sim 6.5^\circ\text{C}$ per 1000m of elevation).
Aspect Modifier ($Aspect$): Uses the terrain's surface normal to calculate sun exposure. Slopes facing the sun get a temperature bonus; shadowed slopes (facing away from the sun) receive a penalty.
Trees will organically thin out and stop spawning whenever $T_{soil}$ drops between $6.5^\circ\text{C}$ and $6.0^\circ\text{C}$.

2. Microclimate Simulation via Terrain Normals
Sunny vs. Shadowed Slopes: The generator will calculate the dot product between the terrain's surface normal and the world's primary light vector.
The Result: On a single mountain peak, the sunny side will automatically feature a higher, lusher treeline, while the shadowed side's treeline will plunge down much lower into the valley, creating a highly realistic, organic boundary.
