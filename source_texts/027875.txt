Abstract
Publisher Summary
The ubiquitous nature of modeling clouds makes them an important modeling and animation task. Modeling clouds is a very difficult task because of their complex and amorphous structure. This chapter discusses the visual and physical characteristics of clouds and the important rendering issues for clouds. Clouds are made of visible ice crystals and water droplets suspended in air, depending on the altitude of the cloud and, hence, the air temperature. Cloud shape varies based on the process that forces the air to rise or bubble up, the height at which the clouds form, and various other conditions. An overview of recent approaches to interactive cloud modeling is presented. The chapter describes the volumetric procedural approach for cloud modeling and animation that allows easy, natural specification and animation of the clouds. This technique combines the flexibility of volumetric procedural modeling with the smooth blending and ease of control of primitive-based implicit functions to create a powerful new modeling technique. This technique also demonstrates the advantages of primitive-based implicit functions for modeling semitransparent volumetric objects.
