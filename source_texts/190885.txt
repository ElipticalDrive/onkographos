Abstract. Digital holography generates large volumes of data that require
efficient storage and processing, where quantization plays a crucial role.
Conventional uniform quantization often fails to adapt to the non-uniform
statistical distribution of holographic data, resulting in degraded reconstruction
quality. To address this challenge, histogram-based quantization methods have
been developed, allowing adaptive allocation of quantization levels according to
the actual data distribution. This article explores in detail the methods of
quantization of digital holograms based on histogram analysis, with particular
emphasis on a high-quality non-iterative algorithm. The algorithm avoids the
computational complexity of iterative approaches by directly exploiting
cumulative histogram analysis to assign quantization levels. Consequently, it
ensures both efficiency and high fidelity in hologram reconstruction.
Keywords: Digital holography; quantization; histogram analysis; noniterative algorithm; adaptive quantization; holographic reconstruction; image
fidelity; optical information processing.
Introduction. Digital holography, which has become one of the most
promising techniques in modern optics and information technology, relies on the
efficient storage, transmission, and processing of holographic data. Since
holograms often contain large amounts of information, compression and
quantization play a decisive role in practical applications. In particular, the
quantization stage significantly affects the accuracy of reconstruction and,
consequently, the quality of the final image. Traditional quantization methods,
although widely used, do not always provide optimal results, especially when
working with holograms that contain highly non-uniform distributions of
intensity values. Therefore, the development of advanced quantization methods
based on histogram analysis has attracted considerable attention.

First of all, it is essential to clarify the fundamental principles underlying
quantization of digital holograms. Quantization can be defined as the mapping of
continuous or high-resolution values into a finite set of discrete levels. In
holography, the data is represented as complex amplitudes or phase distributions.
Therefore, quantization directly influences how accurately these distributions
can be reconstructed [5].
On the one hand, uniform quantization, which divides the intensity or phase
range into equal intervals, is simple and computationally efficient. On the other
hand, it often fails to adapt to the actual distribution of values within a hologram.
As a result, high-density regions of the histogram may be poorly represented,
leading to visible distortions in the reconstructed image. This is where histogram
analysis becomes particularly relevant, because it provides insight into the
statistical properties of the holographic data.
When analyzing hologram histograms, one can observe that the distribution
of pixel intensities or phase values is usually non-uniform. Certain ranges contain
dense clusters of values, while other ranges remain sparse. Accordingly,
histogram-based methods adjust the quantization levels to the actual distribution
of data, thereby reducing quantization errors [1].
Furthermore, histogram equalization has been widely applied in image
processing, and its adaptation to holography allows for a more balanced
allocation of quantization intervals. In practice, this means that more quantization
levels are assigned to dense regions of the histogram, while fewer levels are
assigned to sparse regions. Thus, the overall fidelity of the holographic
reconstruction is improved without increasing the total number of quantization
levels.
While iterative optimization methods have been proposed in order to refine
quantization levels based on histogram feedback, they often require significant
computational resources. As a result, their application to large-scale holograms
or real-time processing is limited. In contrast, non-iterative algorithms provide a
direct solution.
The proposed non-iterative algorithm consists of several key steps. Firstly,
the histogram of the hologram data is constructed. Secondly, cumulative
distribution analysis is performed in order to determine optimal partitioning
points. Thirdly, quantization levels are assigned directly based on this
partitioning, without any need for iterative adjustment. Finally, the quantized
hologram is generated and can be reconstructed with minimal distortion [4].
In addition, because this method avoids the repeated recalculations inherent
in iterative approaches, it is not only faster but also less prone to convergence
problems. More importantly, experimental results reported in recent studies
demonstrate that non-iterative histogram-based quantization maintains high
peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) in
reconstructed holograms, thus achieving both efficiency and quality.

In comparison with traditional uniform quantization, the histogram-driven
non-iterative approach offers several advantages. Firstly, it provides adaptive
quantization, which leads to a better representation of holographic information.
Secondly, it achieves a high quality of reconstruction while reducing
computational cost. Thirdly, it is particularly well-suited for hardware
implementations, since non-iterative procedures are easier to integrate into
embedded systems.
Moreover, these algorithms are applicable not only in digital holographic
microscopy and three-dimensional imaging, but also in optical data storage,
holographic displays, and telecommunication systems where both efficiency and
fidelity are essential. Therefore, this method contributes significantly to bridging
the gap between theoretical research and practical applications in digital
holography.
Conclusion. To sum up, the quantization of digital holograms plays a crucial
role in ensuring high-quality reconstruction and efficient storage. Although
uniform methods remain simple, they fail to adapt to the non-uniform nature of
holographic histograms. Consequently, histogram-based methods have emerged
as superior alternatives. Among them, non-iterative algorithms stand out because
they combine high quality with computational efficiency. By directly exploiting
histogram analysis, they provide adaptive quantization that enhances both
accuracy and performance. Hence, this approach is likely to become a cornerstone
of future holographic technologies, supporting innovations in fields ranging from
biomedical imaging to immersive visual communication.
