A step-by-step, interactive series that rebuilds a Wolfenstein-3D-style raycaster in plain JavaScript. It runs directly in the browser from index.html, with no libraries and no build step.
What's included
Part 1: the maths (top-down 2D diagrams)
Vectors and coordinates: dot product, projection, perp. The 1992 box covers 16.16 fixed point, with a calculator that shows how the fraction bits encode values.
Angles and rotation: cos/sin, atan2, rotation matrix (MathML). The 1992 box covers sine tables and Doom's binary angles.
The camera: direction + plane, one ray per column, camera matrix M = [p d] and its inverse.
Ray marching: why fixed steps are inaccurate and slow.
DDA: exact grid traversal, which you can step through iteration by iteration.
Part 2: from 2D to "3D" (320×200 software framebuffer)
6. Perspective projection: similar triangles, first rendered frame.
7. Fisheye: depth vs. Euclidean distance (with an explanation of why depth is the right divisor), and why DDA's t is already depth.
8. Movement and collision: dt, strafing, box collision, sliding along walls.
9. Textured walls: procedural embossed bricks, texture coordinates, column inspector.
10. Floor and ceiling: row casting via the camera matrix run forwards.
Each step has a "How it was done in 1992" box. From step 5 on, each step carries its own copy of raycaster.js, so diffing two steps shows exactly what changed.
Plans: PLAN.md describes Part 3 (sprites, doors, shooting, HUD) and Part 4 (how Doom differs: sectors, BSP, visplanes).
Testing
All 11 pages load in headless Chromium with no console errors. The game demos were driven with the keyboard, and the rendered views and diagrams were checked by screenshot.
