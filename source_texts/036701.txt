Why
World lighting needs a shared allocation and presentation system: headlights, combat flashes, industrial fixtures and atmospheric sources must compete within a known budget and produce genuine surface illumination. This extends the existing lighting PR into NOCTURNE, while retaining the existing Look system and Three.js forward renderer.
Draft implementation review. Runtime integration and focused WebGL checks pass. Whole-game visual acceptance and target-hardware performance acceptance remain open for the concrete reasons below.
What this changes
Unified sources and director: stable logical handles, authored sockets and transforms, power/damage/envelope state, priority classes, camera/frustum/projected-influence selection, bounded cadence and temporal hysteresis. Normal source churn does not change physical light counts.
Shared direct-light ownership: migrate headlights, VFX, weapon events, overhead engine effects, Bolus lanterns and Marrow searchlights into the same runtime. Keep compatibility lighting for isolated scenes.
Independent local shadows: one reusable spotlight map, quality-scaled resolution/cadence, caster invalidation, cached stationary depth and safe private admission. Player-critical lights receive strong priority; occlusion-required sources fail closed without a shadow slot.
Indirect and reflections: bounded normal-dependent diffuse fields and roughness-dependent local radiance lobes, plus optional per-root externally authored PMREM reflection bindings. These analytic fields are not baked GI or captures of scene geometry.
Actual atmospheric scattering: depth-clipped participating media, low-resolution ray marching, extinction, temporal reprojection/history rejection and depth-aware reconstruction. Clear vacuum skips the effect. The same runtime-owned pass works through bloom and the AO-enabled SpaceRenderGraph, before tone mapping.
HDR and illustrated materials: retain genuinely evaluated local RGB after attenuation/shadows instead of flattening it under the pigment ceiling; keep broad Look shaping, calibrated inverse-square contribution, constrained authored exposure and genuine HDR bloom.
Content and authoring: 18 named profiles, 21 fixture descriptors and five compositions, including distinct industrial, derelict, combat, alien and vacuum arrangements. Integrate actual station/factory/gate classifications, ship drives/navigation, reactor states, luminous ore and fauna emission. Strict GLB/JSON metadata checks and reproducible previews support future assets.
Settings and lifecycle: existing Video/profile/preset controls, same-frame combat updates, floating-origin invalidation, world-boundary cleanup, context recovery and idempotent disposal. Presentation reads world state without writing lighting state into the simulation/save.

Validation
125 actual test cases pass across 16 focused suites, with subprocess-error/empty-output rejection and stable before/after source hashes.
2,467 source modules parse cleanly. Reachability and generated module-preload checks pass; the latest mainline preload-target regression also passes all three cases.
The real isolated Settings screen passed seven recorded control transitions with no browser/resource errors.
Chromium WebGL fixtures use the vendored Three revision 184, production lighting/shader/compositor modules, real comparison-depth spotlight shadows and actual context loss/restoration.
The content fixture uses the default 144 WU / 60° tilt / 50° FOV chase pose. With bloom and atmosphere disabled, direct lighting changes 120,201 industrial; 73,047 derelict; 4,540 combat; and 23,109 alien non-emissive surface pixels. Bright Arcade/sun comparison also passes.
Both atmosphere routes preserve their program census across enabled/disabled/vacuum states: 6/6/6 for bloom and 9/9/9 for graph/AO. Atmosphere adds one draw. Vacuum and disabled output match exactly below the diagnostic label.
The industrial/derelict/alien geometry is explicitly labeled diagnostic authoring rigs. Combat uses the real release Kestrel GLB, verified against its Git blob. These are not screenshots of accepted full-game routes.

Open acceptance items
Upstream startup blocker: the public route stops before its first Play button because audioSystem.js imports absent src/audio/trailerScore.js and src/audio/recipeCaptions.js. Static inspection also finds absent src/ui/station/stationLife.js. These are missing Git-tree files at the observed upstream revisions, not sparse-checkout omissions. Evidence and defect D250 are retained. No replacement stubs were used.
After those modules are restored, inspect actual station/mining/derelict/combat/dense gameplay and transitions; measure whole-game frame distributions, calls/programs, memory/allocations and component GPU costs on integrated and discrete hardware.
Complete remaining individual asset/location passes, including instanced-only mineral emitters and legacy welding/arc producers. Unshadowed local-light leakage and the one-map shadow allocation need in-world judgment. No dynamic GI or existing captured-reflection asset coverage is claimed.
The existing draft status is intentional. This update does not approve a merge or deployment.
