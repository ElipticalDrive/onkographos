Summary
Replaces the all-procedural WebAudio synth in src/sound.js with a hybrid mixer that mixes CC0 audio samples for one-shot SFX with a fully procedural day/night-aware ambient layer for nature sounds. Total bundle cost: ~220 KB for all 22 SFX samples, zero bytes for ambient (it's all generated from one shared 8s noise buffer).
One-shot SFX → real samples

Behaviour kept procedural where samples don't fit (cozy 2-tone food blip, dash whoosh, generic bomb boom, bell, death, arrow, abilities) — these read better as synth than any short sample I could find.
Procedural ambient layers
A single 8-second seeded shaped-noise buffer is decoded once and looped forever through 4 filtered taps that feed the new ambBus:
Wind — low-passed brown noise, cutoff slowly LFO-driven 250→900 Hz so gusts feel like real moving air. Always on, slightly louder during the day.
Pondside lap — narrow bandpass (~380 Hz) on the same noise, gain modulated by the player's distance to the nearest water cell (probed via world.isWaterAt in a 12 m ring). Silent away from ponds, swells in as you walk to a shore.
Forest hum — bandpass (~1.5 kHz) gated by the world's tree-density noise, so dense-forest chunks audibly differ from open meadow.
Campfire crackle — high-passed bed + a recursive scheduler that fires tiny pulse-shaped crack bursts at random intervals. Audible only within ~14 m of origin (where world.js plants the spawn-ring fire).
Plus two sparse-event schedulers for the day/night chorus:
Day-time bird chirps — random selection of three 2-note triangle sweeps, probability gated on dayWeight².
Night-time crickets — 3–6 quick sine pulses at ~4 kHz, probability gated on (1−dayWeight)².
All layer gains are smoothed with a ~1.2 s time constant in Sound.updateAmbient(dt, ctx), called once per RAF from Game._loop. Walking past a pond or crossing dawn is a gradual fade, not a click. The whole ambient stack costs one decode + one BufferSourceNode per layer for the lifetime of the page — no graph rebuild, just a handful of setTargetAtTime calls per second.
Optimisations
Lazy loading — _loadBuffer only fetches+decodes a sample on its first triggered play. The first call emits a synthesised tone/noise placeholder so the action still has feedback while the .ogg is in flight; every subsequent call uses the decoded AudioBuffer. resume() prefetches the four most-common combat samples on the user's first input gesture.
Voice cap + rate limit per category (e.g. hitFlesh cap 5, min 30 ms gap). Prevents a fireball AoE landing on 8 enemies from smearing into a single muddy roar.
Per-shot pitch detune (~±200 cents on AudioBufferSourceNode.detune) + random variant pick → no two consecutive plays sound identical without baking N more samples.
Single AudioContext, three buses (master → {sfxBus, ambBus} → destination). Volume sliders just write gain.value; no graph rebuild on slider drag.
_activeVoices counter decremented on onended so the cap stays accurate without a full per-id voice list.
UI / settings
A new "Окружение" slider appears in the pause-menu Audio tab next to the existing Master / SFX sliders, persisting through settings.values.audio.ambientVolume (default 60%). The existing master / SFX / muted toggles already work because the ambient bus is just another child of master.
Asset licensing
All 22 .ogg files are sourced from two CC0 (public-domain) Kenney audio packs and documented in public/sounds/LICENSE.txt:
RPG Audio — sword/cloth swings, creaks, coins
Impact Sounds — punches, plate hits, glass/wood breaks
README.md updated with full source attribution, the file mapping, and a short "how to add new sounds" guide.
Review & Testing Checklist for Human
Pull, run npm run dev, start the game and confirm:
First sword swing plays a synth placeholder (decode-in-flight) and subsequent swings play the real swing_blade* / swing_cloth* samples
Hitting enemies plays hit_flesh*, killing them plays enemy_die*
Smashing a clay pot plays a sharp glassy crash; smashing a wooden crate plays a wood-splinter crack (different from each other and from the bomber explosion)
Picking up gold plays coin*
Pause menu → Audio → "Окружение" slider audibly cross-fades the wind/water/forest/fire layers without affecting combat SFX
Walk close to a pond and confirm a soft lapping layer fades in within a few metres of the shore, then fades back out
Stand near origin and confirm a quiet campfire crackle bed with intermittent cracks
Wait until in-game night (or scrub world.dayTime) — bird chirps fade out and cricket trills fade in
Confirm muting the master volume silences both the new ambient bed and one-shot SFX
Verify the bundle size hasn't ballooned: du -sh public/sounds/ should report ~220 KB total
Notes
Backwards compatible: every old method on Sound (tone, noise, swing, hit, enemyHit, enemyDie, pickupGold, pickupFood, hurt, dash, buy, bell, death, arrow, bomb) still exists with the same signature, so abilities / enemies / pickups didn't need changes. The only call-site update is in Game._onBreakableDestroyed, which now picks potBreak vs woodBreak based on the breakable's kind instead of always firing bomb.
World.dayWeight is now exposed on the instance so audio can read the same phase the renderer uses for sky/ambient lighting — no recomputation.
This branch deliberately stays scoped to the audio system to avoid conflicting with the building/farming work happening in a parallel session.
