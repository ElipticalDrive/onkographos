Close up, the best step now costs about what the high step does, because both march at half resolution there. The cheaper steps already marched at a half or a quarter, so they can't go coarser in these views. Far away, every step keeps its resolution.
How it works
The rule. The finest noise texel is 1/(64 × noise_frequency × 2.7) m, 2.6 cm by default. A resolution is allowed while that texel still spans one raymarched pixel at the far side of the farthest volumetric smoke on screen. That gives limits of about 23 m for ⅔, 17 m for ½ and 8.5 m for ¼, at 1080 rows and Bevy's default 45° field of view. The step's resolution is the finest the march uses.
Hysteresis. A coarser resolution is taken once the smoke is within 0.8 of its limit, and kept until the smoke passes the limit itself. In the demo's budget walk at the best step (24 m → 3 m → 24 m), the march went to ⅔ at 12.9 m and to ½ at 8.2 m on the way in, and back to ⅔ at 12.0 m and to full at 17.8 m on the way out: four changes in the whole walk.
Where. quality::march_resolution is a pure function, unit-tested for the limits, the hysteresis band and never going finer than the step. The quality controller calls it per camera with the step it picks, and keeps the result in SmokeQuality::volumetric_resolution, which the renderer marches at.
Why the box's far side. It's cautious: inside a dense cloud, rays end long before it. Close up, a quarter would keep the noise as well; the scratch build forced to ¼ looked the same in stills. Getting there safely would need the march to measure how far its rays get, so it is left on the roadmap.
API (0.2.0's window):
VolumetricQuality::resolution_by_distance (on at every step) and SmokeQuality::volumetric_resolution are new fields.
SmokeResolution derives Default (Full).
SmokeCamera has MINIMUM, LOWER, LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH and ULTRA. LOW (0.2) and MEDIUM (0.4) were the dial's "lower" and "low" steps; they now select the steps they are named after.
The budget controller's ratios. Close up, the volumetric steps cost 0.59 / 0.59 / 0.79 / 0.85 / 0.94 / 1.0 of the best one now, instead of 0.09 / 0.10 / 0.18 / 0.22 / 0.38 / 1.0 (which predated 0.2.0 GPU 3: one single-smoke march per smoke (volumetric close up −27 to −32 %, same picture) #30). The faceted ones are 0.25 / 0.26 / 0.33 / 0.36 / 0.49 / 1.0.
The demo. The HUD says when the march is coarser than the step, and the budget script's CSV has a march_divisor column. The showcase's low-quality shot uses LOWER, the step it used before. The demo's starting dial uses LOW.
The picture
Lit pictures (tier sweep, every step and view, against 0.2.0 GPU 2: the composite draws into the resolved image when nothing transparent follows (mid-range −38 %, close −13 %, same picture) #32): at most 19 levels out of 255, more than 8 levels on at most 0.005 % of a frame, more than 2 on at most 0.33 %. Filled: 19 levels, 0.48 % over 2. Capped: 37 levels and 1.2 % over 2. That's the review's predicted effect: the band along the edges the rule blocks widens close up.
Fairness (tools/fairness.py --volumetric, agreement runs at every step against 0.2.0 GPU 2: the composite draws into the resolved image when nothing transparent follows (mid-range −38 %, close −13 %, same picture) #32's best step, grown and halfway through the fade): every step stays within the bound. The worst view is still the cheapest step's (0.15 % and 0.26 % of a view's smoke pixels letting 0.3 more of the scene through; the bound is 1 %). The best step's close view lets more than a tenth more through on 0.02 % of the smoke's pixels, and 0.3 more on none.
Picture against the rule (tools/agreement.py, Capped and Filled, grown and halfway through the fade): no pixel the rule blocks lets more than 1 % of the scene through, in any view. The edge pixels close up even dropped (Capped grown: 893 → 363).
Measured first
A scratch build of #32's branch tried the rule without hysteresis, and with forced ½ and ¼, before this PR. Those batches caught heavy background load (LM Studio's GPU bursts), so only their close-up figures are reliable: the rule took close up at the best step from 3.23 to 1.59 ms, forced ¼ to 1.04. Requiring two raymarched pixels per noise texel instead of one changed nothing in the sweep's views, so it was dropped. The fairness and picture checks above were run on this branch's own build.

Faster volumetric march: one single-smoke dispatch per smoke

Where no ray can meet two volumetric smokes' boxes (their screen
rectangles, grown by three smoke-buffer texels and two pixels, don't
touch), each smoke gets a march dispatch of its own over its own
rectangle, with a kernel compiled for one smoke (SINGLE_SMOKE). It keeps
none of the per-ray interval arrays and reads its slot from the view
uniforms (params.z), so the smoke's data is the same for the whole
dispatch. Smokes that no point light reaches use a variant without the
point-light code (NO_POINT_LIGHTS). Every ray takes the march's one-smoke
path either way, so the picture is the same up to rounding (one level out
of 255 in a few dozen pixels a frame). Where smokes overlap on screen, and
until the single-smoke kernels have compiled, the general kernel draws as
before: its text is unchanged but for the @if lines, and forced on, it
draws main's exact pixels at main's cost.

The single-smoke kernels compile on first use (SpecializedComputePipelines),
for the noise mode and MSAA each camera uses: two pipelines on a camera's
first frame, at most twelve in all.

On an Apple M4 at 1920x1080, close up at the best step, a grown cloud
takes 3.67 instead of 5.15 ms (-29 %), halfway through the fade 2.45
instead of 3.36 (-27 %); with NoiseWithinRule::Capped -29 to -30 %, with
Filled -27 to -32 %.

The light list read each PointLight's ViewVisibility in build_frame,
which ran unordered between Bevy's reset_view_visibility (in
VisibilitySystems::VisibilityPropagate) and check_visibility_cpu_culling
(in CheckVisibility). In between, every flag is cleared, so the list was
always empty and no muzzle flash or explosion ever lit volumetric or
faceted smoke. The lights are now gathered by a system of their own,
after CheckVisibility. Bevy marks a point light visible where its range
reaches into a camera's view, so a light that can't light anything on
screen still doesn't take one of the eight places. Crystal smoke, which
Bevy lights itself, was never affected.

A plugin test runs Bevy's visibility systems in a headless app (no
window, no GPU), and fails on the old schedule. The demo's tier sweep
gains SMOKE_DEMO_SWEEP_LIGHTS=<n>: n steady muzzle flashes in the smoke,
to measure what flashes cost.

With MSAA, the smoke's overlay passes (the volumetric composite and the
faceted trace or composite) drew into the multisampled colour target, so
the transparent pass that follows could continue from it. Each such pass
loads, stores and resolves the whole multisampled target, whatever the
smoke's size: about 0.2 ms a frame at 1080p on an Apple M4. Bevy runs
its transparent pass only when something is queued in it, so when a
camera's Transparent3d phase is empty, the overlays now draw straight
into the resolved image with single-sample pipelines. Otherwise, and
until those pipelines have compiled, they draw as before. The blend is
linear, so the picture is the same up to rounding.

At full resolution (the best dial step), the volumetric composite takes
each pixel's own smoke texel and reads no depth: the depth-aware weights
of its neighbours were all but zero there.
