Describe the feature
Add two domain-specific metrics widely used in emission tomography:
the Contrast to Noise Ratio (CNR) which measures the contrast in reconstructions defined by:
$CNR = \frac{\mu_{roi} - \mu_{bg}}{\sigma_{bg}}$ where $\mu$ is the mean and $\sigma$ the standard deviation, and "bg" stands for background.
see a python implementation here: https://3dct.github.io/vIQA/nr_metrics/generated/viqa.nr_metrics.cnr.contrast_to_noise_ratio.html#viqa.nr_metrics.cnr.contrast_to_noise_ratio
The metric should expect a reconstruction, and masks for background and regions of interest.
the Contrast-Recovery (CR) which measures how much of the contrast we recover, and which is defined as:
$CR = \frac{\frac{\mu_{roi}}{\mu_{bg}} - 1}{\frac{a_{roi}}{a_{bg}} - 1}*100$
where the numerator is the contrast in the reconstruction, and the denominator the contrast in the ground-truch, with $a$ standing for the ground-truth activity in ROIs and background.
The metric should expect a reconstruction, a ground-truth and masks for background and ROIs.
Motivation
These metrics are very standard for the evaluation of emission tomography reconstructions and users will need them to evaluate their methods.

That torchmetrics issue seems to talk about using ignore_index. While that can be useful, I think here we also want something different; where/if/what to mask can be different for each sample in the dataset / batch.
So it also makes sense to just have a metric accept a mask arg in forward which, taking batches of shape B,1,H,W as example, can be either B,1,H,W, or 1, H,W.
So mask should be given in the dataset's getitem. Which currently is not supported by the dataset output deepinv enforces?
I do think allowing for mask specifically to be part of the tuple a dataset is allowed produce would lead to further complexification (especially as throughout time more such special dataset outputs get added). Maybe allowing an additional dict to be given by getitem which can have a 'mask' key.
Overall, I think long-term having a dataset output a dict will be more sustainable, precisely for such features (and removing some complexity from the trainer), but that would be quite a large change impacting users.
