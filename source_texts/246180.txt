Was a "decide the level" stub; the levels are answered below. Depends on #1115 (particle pools, simulation dispatch, indirect draw) and reads #1235 for the volumetric side.
Decided
Level 4, simulated fluid particles, as its own solver on the GPU, with a surface rebuilt in screen space. Levels 1–3 (a water plane with waves, reflections and underwater fog) are a different feature and stay out of this issue: an ocean is a surface shader, not a solver.
Simulation
Position Based Fluids (Macklin & Müller 2013) over epic(render): GPU particle system — simulated, culled and drawn without the CPU counting them #1115's SoA pools: positions, velocities and densities as parallel arrays, particles as u32.
Neighbours from a hashed uniform grid, rebuilt every frame in compute — counting sort, no pointer chasing.
Fixed iteration count per frame, so a splash cannot cost more than still water.
Forces come from the same places a particle's do: kooch_gravity's sources (a fluid on a small planet falls towards its centre), drag, wind (feat(render,physics): one wind field — analytic base plus painted flowmaps and flow fields, same on CPU and GPU #978).
Collision against the depth buffer first, as epic(render): GPU particle system — simulated, culled and drawn without the CPU counting them #1115 decides, and against the analytic shapes the scene already has.
🔴 No crate to adopt. Dimforge's wgsparkl (WebGPU MPM/SPH) and wgrapier3d are abandoned — the same finding that put GPU physics at Nexus. Their design is worth reading; the code is not adoptable.
Rendering
Screen-space fluid rendering (van der Laan et al. 2009): particle depth, bilateral smoothing, normals rebuilt from the smoothed depth, then thickness for absorption, refraction and foam. A marching-cubes mesh is the alternative and is rejected: it rebuilds geometry every frame for a surface that is already only ever seen from one side.
Spray and mist ride the volumetric path (feat(render): volumetric particles in the froxel grid #1235) instead of the surface: they have no surface.
Budget
13.9 ms whole-frame on the OneXFly. The solver scales with particle count, the smoothing with resolution, and they are measured separately. 🔴 Spike first: a fixed 50k-particle dam break, timed on the OneXFly, before any API is designed. If the solver alone eats the budget, the count is the knob and the design does not change.
Acceptance
A dam break of 50k particles that pools, flows and splashes, entirely on the GPU, with CPU time independent of the count.
The surface shows refraction and foam, and holds together while the camera moves — no boiling from frame to frame.
The same fluid on a small planet's far side falls towards its centre.
Measured on the OneXFly, with the particle count, the solver's iterations and the smoothing's radius as the knobs.
