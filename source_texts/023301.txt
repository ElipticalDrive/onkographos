To explore the influence of initial guess or estimate (uniform as “ones” and “zeros” vs. filtered back projection [FBP] image) as an input image for maximum likelihood expectation–maximization (MLEM) tomographic reconstruction algorithm and provide the curves of error or convergence for each of these three initial estimates.
Methods:
Two phantoms, created as digital images, were utilized: one was a simple noiseless object and the other was a more complicated, noise-degraded object of the section of lower thorax in a matrix of 256 × 256 pixels. Both underwent radon transform or forward projection process and the corresponding sinograms were generated. For filtering during tomographic image reconstruction, ramp and Butterworth filters, as high-pass and low-pass ones, were applied to images. The second phantom (lower thorax) was radon-transformed and the resulting sinogram was degraded by noise. As initial guess or estimate images, in addition to FBP tomographic image, two uniform images, one with all pixels having a value of 1 (“ones”) and the other with all having zero (“zeros”), were created. The three initial estimates (FBP, ones, and zeros) were reconstructed with iterative MLEM tomographic reconstruction (with 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 iterations). The difference between the object and the updated slice was calculated at the end of each iteration (as error matrix), and the mean squared error (MSE) was computed and plotted separately or in conjunction with the MSE curves of other initial estimates. All computations were implemented in MATLAB software.
Results:
The results of ones and zeros seemed strikingly similar. The curves of uniform ones and uniform zeros were so close to each other that overlap near-perfectly. However, in the FBP slice as an initial estimate, the resulting tomographic slice was similar with a much higher extent to the object even after 1 or 2 iterations. The pattern of convergence for all three curves was roughly similar. The normalized MSE decreased sharply up to 5 iterations and then, after 10 iterations, the curves reached a plateau until 32 iterations. For the phantom of the lower thorax section with its noise-degraded sinogram, similar to the pattern observed for simple disk-shaped phantom, the curves (normalized MSE) fell sharply up to 10 iterations and then rapidly converged thereafter until 64 iterations.
Conclusion:
Similar results are observed when choosing different initial guesses or estimates (uniform vs. FBP) as the starting point, based on the error calculation using MSE. The algorithm converges almost similarly for all initial estimates. Therefore, selecting a uniform initial guess image can be an appropriate choice and may be preferred over an FBP image. Reducing the processing time can be a valid reason for this choice.

The current choice for image reconstruction in nuclear medicine imaging is iterative tomographic reconstruction. Maximum likelihood expectation–maximization (MLEM) is one of the basic algorithms of iterative techniques, in that repeated tasks of forward and backward projections are done (i.e., iterations) to improve the estimated image step-by-step. At each step, the sinogram of the estimated image is generated and then compared to the measured sinogram created from the real phantom or object, which shows the original spatial distribution of activity. The error, which refers to the potential discrepancy between the measured and estimated sinograms, is computed, and an appropriate correction factor is used, followed by the necessary updates being implemented. This process is repeated until a satisfactory result (as per a predetermined error) is achieved, or for a predetermined number of iterations.[123] There are several factors that control and regulate the workflow of this procedure among which are the selection of initial estimate or guess as the starting point for the iterative algorithm. The impact of the initial estimated image or initial guess has long been a subject of debate. A uniform initial guess (an image with all pixels being either one or zero, as “ones” or “zeros,” respectively) is used by some. To achieve better results, some others prefer an initial image that is reconstructed by filtered back projection (FBP) before the iterative MLEM. This issue has not received much attention yet. Do the results differ between using a uniform image or an FBP image as an initial estimate? Furthermore, the convergence of successive iterations and the number of iterations necessary for a satisfactory result are unknown. The convergence rate of the algorithm and the speed of computations are closely connected to these factors.[456] To evaluate the performance of the algorithm, the mean squared error (MSE) is calculated as the error index, which represents the discrepancy between the prior image and the newly updated image. To illustrate the convergence of the algorithm, the curve is plotted after aggregating the errors calculated in each step. The result also gives insight into the approximate number of iterations needed. The present study aims to assess the influence of initial guess or estimate (uniform as “ones” and “zeros” vs. FBP image) as an input image for the MLEM tomographic reconstruction algorithm and provide the curves of error (or convergence plot) for each of these three initial estimates. The simulations are conducted on a noiseless simple object as well as a noise-corrupted, more complicated object to take the effect of noise into account on the error curves or algorithm convergence as well as final results.
M<sc>ETHODS</sc>Digital phantoms
Two phantoms, created as digital images, were utilized: one was a simple noiseless object and the other was a more complicated, noise-degraded object of the section of lower thorax. In a matrix of 256 × 256 pixels, a simple round-shaped object was created. The radius of the disk was assumed to be 64 pixels and the pixels within were assigned a value of unit (or 1). To prevent sharp changes in activity distribution that are typical of nuclear medicine images, blurring was applied to the images. The second, also created in a matrix of 256 × 256 pixels, was utilized as an emission map of the human lower thorax, containing the objects of the left ventricle and liver.
Filters and filtered back projection reconstruction
Images were filtered during tomographic image reconstruction through the implementation and application of a one-dimensional (1D) ramp filter and a 2D Butterworth filter, respectively, high-pass and low-pass filters. The ramp filter was windowed by the Hann function as well. Filtering was applied in the frequency domain following the Fourier transformation of images using the fast Fourier transform (1D-FFT and 2D-FFT) algorithm. Butterworth filter (with a cut-off frequency of 0.2 of Nyquist frequency and an order of 3) was employed to smooth the noise-degraded sinograms before tomographic reconstruction. The Butterworth and Hann-windowed ramp filters are visualized in Figure 1. The radon transform or forward projection process was applied to both digital phantoms. By this method, the corresponding sinograms were generated. A 360° arc and an angular spacing of 1° were selected for the acquisition procedure. As a result, 360 1D projections were acquired. The noiseless sinogram of the simple phantom (disk-shaped object with blurred borders) was filtered by a Hann-windowed ramp filter and was entered the process of FBP tomographic reconstruction to obtain the corresponding tomographic slice. The second phantom (a section of lower thorax) was radon-transformed and the resulting sinogram was degraded by noise. Then, the procedure of FBP was performed.

Iterative maximum likelihood expectation–maximization reconstruction
As initial guess or estimate images, in addition to FBP tomographic image, two uniform images, one with all pixels having a value of 1 (“ones”) and the other with all having zero (“zeros”), were created. The three initial estimates (FBP, ones, and zeros) were reconstructed with iterative MLEM tomographic reconstruction. For this purpose, the procedure was performed with 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 iterations. During the iterative MLEM reconstruction algorithm, the initial estimate underwent radon transform to generate corresponding sinograms. Then, this sinogram (estimated sinogram) was compared to the initial sinogram generated from the object (measured sinogram). Hence, the ratio of the measured sinogram to the estimated sinogram was calculated and was then back-projected to create a correction slice. The latter was multiplied pixel-by-pixel to the initial guess or estimate to achieve a newly updated tomographic slice. Finally, the slices were normalized. D<sc>ISCUSSION</sc>
MLEM iterative reconstruction is currently the most frequently used computed tomography algorithm in nuclear medicine imaging. Compared to the FBP method, it is computationally more efficient and produces fewer artifacts and less image degradation. The principal concept in this algorithm is assuming an estimate and then correcting the initial estimate step-by-step. Selecting a greater number of iterations has no additional benefits because doing so increases the computational load and may produce more noise and artifacts. Therefore, one should decide when to stop or terminate the algorithm to achieve the optimal result. Some instructions about the optimal number of iterations have been put forth.[1234789] The input image, often known as initial guess or estimate, is one issue that could potentially have an impact on the final result of the MLEM algorithm. In most circumstances, in commercially available software frameworks for image processing and analysis, one has the liberty to select a uniform image or a more accurate estimation from prior FBP reconstruction. Although the latter might have some benefits, it lengthens the processing time. Therefore, the aim of the present study was to investigate the impact of choosing the initial guess image on the final result as well as on the convergence rate of the algorithm. According to the results of this study, there is no overall difference in selecting the initial guess or estimate (uniform vs. FBP) as the starting point. The algorithm converges almost at the same rate for all initial estimates. The error, generally, decreases sharply up to 5 iterations. In case of using FBP image, even 2 or 3 iterations may suffice. Worth noting that after 10 iterations, the difference among the three initial estimates is too small between consecutive iterations to warrant performing more iterations. Taken together, the pattern of all curves is roughly similar. A similar pattern was observed when using a more complicated object with noise consideration during sinogram generation. As a result, by incorporating noise into the model, the pattern of convergence of the algorithm does not differ considerably. Therefore, selecting a uniform initial guess image can be an appropriate choice and may be preferred over an FBP image. Furthermore, uniform “ones” seems better than uniform “zeros” since the algorithm may not work in this condition unless it is developed in a way that maintains the flexibility to accept different types of initial guess image. This issue comes from the way the correction is applied as we used a ratio of measured to estimated sinograms and some modifications were implemented. In some other algorithms, a subtraction of measured and estimated sinograms is employed for error correction, and thus, it differs slightly from the former one. The present study focused on the algorithm of MLEM, in particular, and the impact of different initial estimates on the results. We developed a deterministic model for the forward projection process or sinogram generation; thus, we did not include radiation attenuation and scatter into the model. However, we incorporated noise in our simulation to demonstrate its impact on the performance of algorithm and the final results. The physical factors of scatter, radiation, etc., are considered to play a key role during the formation of sinograms.[12] Then, using this sinogram as the measured one is fed to the MLEM algorithm. Therefore, we decided to simplify the model and place our particular focus on the MLEM iterative technique.
As the most widely used algorithm in tomographic image reconstruction at the present time, the MLEM algorithm was chosen for the current investigation among a number of different iterative approaches. As such extrapolating the results to other iterative techniques may be not possible. Several version of this method have been developed thus far with the aim of enhancing its performance, accuracy and quality of resulting image as well as expediting the computation time or convergence rate.[13141516] It is important to consider potential discrepancies between the modified versions of MLEM algorithm with the basic form, as demonstrated in the present study.
C<sc>ONCLUSION</sc>
Similar results are observed when choosing different initial guesses or estimates (uniform vs. FBP) as the starting point, based on the error calculation using MSE. The algorithm converges almost similarly for all initial estimates. Therefore, selecting a uniform initial guess image can be an appropriate choice and may be preferred over an FBP image. Reducing the processing time can be a valid reason for this choice.
