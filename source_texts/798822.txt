SQ-CBF: Signed Distance Functions for Reliable Superquadric-Based Safety Filtering
Contributions

We identify that both the implicit function value and its gradient can be ill-conditioned, which can make the underlying optimization infeasible.

Abstract
Ensuring safe robot operation in cluttered and dynamic environments remains a fundamental challenge. While control barrier functions (CBFs) provide an effective framework for real-time safety filtering, their performance critically depends on the underlying geometric representation, which is often simplified, leading to either overly conservative behavior or insufficient collision coverage. Superquadrics (SQs) offer an expressive way to model complex shapes using a few primitives and are increasingly used for robot safety. To integrate this representation into CBF, most existing approaches directly use their implicit functions as barrier candidates. However, we identify a critical but overlooked issue in this practice: the gradients of the implicit SQ function can become severely ill-conditioned, potentially rendering the optimization infeasible and undermining reliable safety filtering. To address this issue, we formulate an SQ-based safety filter that uses signed distance functions (SDFs) as barrier candidates. Since analytical SDFs are unavailable for general SQs, we compute the distance using the efficient Gilbert–Johnson–Keerthi algorithm and derive its gradient in closed form. Extensive simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate consistent collision-free manipulation in cluttered scenes, showing robustness to challenging geometries, sensing noise, and dynamic disturbances, while improving task efficiency in teleoperation tasks. These results highlight a pathway toward safety filters that remain precise and reliable under the geometric complexity of real-world environments.

Superquadrics are expressive, but their implicit function is a poor barrier candidate.
The superquadric allow us to represent a wide family of shapes, from spheres to rounded boxes, with a minimal set of parameters. Prior works often use its implicit function directly as a control barrier function, which is not a distance metirc and could be severely ill-conditioned: for box-like shapes and large separations, its value and its gradient grow by several orders of magnitude, and the CBF-QP becomes ill-conditioned or infeasible. In the same setup, the signed distance always remains bounded and well-conditioned.

The SQ-CBF safety filter
Robot and obstacles are both represented by compact sets of superquadrics. The signed distance between every robot–obstacle pair is chosen as the barrier function, densely discretized as polytopics and computed with GJK/EPA, and its gradient is derived in closed form in terms of the superquadric poses and witness points.
Robot Obstacles GJK/EPA returns the signed distance d for every pair, the closed-form gradient gives ḋ, and the CBF-QP minimally modifies the uncertified command from teleoperation or a planner, intervening only when a collision is imminent.

Superquadric collision model
15 strictly convex superquadrics cover 97.1% of the Franka FR3 with substantially less over-approximation than cylinders and boxes.
2
Signed distance as barrier function
We choose h = d − εδ. Between strictly convex bodies the SDF is continuously differentiable wherever they are positively separated, so it's a valid CBF on the safe set. Each superquadric is densely sampled into a convex polytope, and GJK/EPA evaluates the distance in real time.
3
Closed-form gradient
Positive homogeneity of the support function eliminates all Hessian terms, such that the SDF Jacobian expression requires only the 6D pose of superquadrics and the witness points.
4
Robust margin
The polytope discretisation error is bounded and incorporated into the CBF margin. In this way we ensure that satisfying the approximate constraint implies the exact one. At 200×200 samples the additional margin is ~0.84 mm.
