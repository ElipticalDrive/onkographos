Abstract
Ray tracing can show shadows and the effects of reflection and refraction. For this reason it is superior to other methods of rendering. It also enables us to make pictures directly from CSG models in a very general way which avoids the complication of finding intersections between primitive objects.
For many applications, we do not need the realism of the ray tracer, but we still value its generality. By using a sparse grid of rays and by following edges in the picture plane, we can extract most of the information from the model at greatly reduced cost.
