Abstract
We propose a physically-based, multispectral simulation to render underwater scenarios in real time, which also takes into
account the RGB response curve of arbitrary sensors. Underwater illumination is mostly governed by multiple scattering,
where light is scattered a number of times between particles before reaching the sensor. This phenomenon is therefore very low
frequency and can be modeled as a function of depth and wavelength. Our approximation to multiple scattering is based on
the measurable coefficient of diffuse downwelling attenuation. We combine this approximation with the standard froxel lighting
technique for single scattering, and show examples simulating underwater appearance under different scattering, absorption
and downwelling coefficients of the Jerlov water types.
1. Introduction
Spectral rendering, where more than the three RGB wavelength
bands are considered, is especially well suited for underwater scenarios. The more common atmospheric image formation model is
not appropriate to simulate the appearance of the ocean, since the
absorption coefficient (often more prevalent than scattering) shows
great wavelength dependency [AT18].
We present a spectral approximation to low frequency underwater illumination suitable for real-time rendering, capable of reproducing a large variety of physically-based scenarios, such as the
different Jerlov water types [SM15], or different depths. We also
combine this illumination with the standard froxel lighting technique for high frequency single scattering.
Participating media Illumination in participating media such as
water is more complex than surfaces, as every point along the ray
contributes towards the illumination reaching the eye [GJJD09]. On
non-emissive media, there are only two coefficients that model this
behaviour. The scattering coefficient, σs (m−1 ) is the differential
probability of light being scattered per unit length travelled along
the medium, while the absorption coefficient σa (m−1 ) represents
the probability of absorption. These are combined into extinction
σt = σa + σs , which fully accounts for radiance reduction events.

(RTE), introduced by Chandrasekhar in 1950 [Cha50], describes
this interaction between light and media in a differential manner. In computer graphics, it is common to formulate the RTE in
its integral form, also known as the volume rendering equation
(VRE) [FWKH17]. This expression accounts for the final radiance

2. In-scattering approximation
In this work, as illustrated in Figure 1, we propose to separate
in-scattering into two components: multiple scattering (which occurs when light is scattered several times in the medium before being in-scattered) and single scattering (light from the surface is directly in-scattered). This allows us to combine an industry standard approximation for single scattering, froxel lighting [Vos14,Hil15,Kov20], with a novel multiple scattering approximation derived from oceanography.

Single scattering To add realism, LSS (Figure 1) can be approximated separately and summed to the other radiance. In this work,
we have integrated our LMS approximation into the High Definition
Rendering Pipieline (HDRP) [Lag18], in the Unity Engine. This allows us to utilize HDRP’s implementation of froxel lighting.
Our proposed method achieves a real-time frame rate rendering a
wide variety of spectral underwater scenes. We have integrated our
method into the Unity engine HDRP to combine it with some of
its physically-based rendering features, and provide an interactive
demo application.
There is a trade-off between speed and physical accuracy in the
number of wavelength bands. The proposed approximation of the
complex phenomenon of multiple scattering is limited by modeling
downwelling irradiance as a function of the distance to the surface.
We assume homogeneous coefficients and an isotropic phase function.
Tackling these limitations would be a promising research direction moving forward. As our model has been formally derived
from radiative transfer theory and oceanography, it could represent
a sound foundation for further development.
