Partitioning for Parallel Computing
◦
Partition data (mesh, matrix) into k parts to be processed
on k processors (cores, GPUs)
◦
Wish to balance the load (work) and minimize
communication
◦
Typically, the partitioning itself must be done in parallel
◦
Problem: Most partitioning software today only runs on
CPU not GPU
◦
This talk: Progress towards performance portable
partitioning software
◦
Focus on Trilinos and the Zoltan2 package

Randomized Eigensolvers
§
The most expensive phase (90-95%) in spectral partitioning is the
eigensolver
§
Fairly low accuracy is sufficient to obtain good partitioning
§
Key idea: We can use a randomized eigensolver instead of LOBPCG
§
Randomized methods often get low-accuracy solutions very fast
§
We follow the approach in Halko, Martinsson, Tropp (2009)
§
Variation of subspace iteration (8-16 iterations is usually enough)
We have explored this approach in a prototype
§
Empirical results show 10-80X speedup
§
§
§
Little change in quality for skewed-degree graphs, but worse for meshes
HPC implementation in Trilinos/Sphynx is in progress

Jet: New Multilevel Partitioner
• Spectral methods are well suited for GPU but this may not be
the best algorithm
• Multilevel algorithms (Chaco, Metis, Scotch, KaHip, etc) are
generally better but hard to parallelize
• Previous work (ParMetis, MtMetis, ParHip, MtKaHip) is limited
to CPU only
• We are currently developing Jet, a Kokkos-based partitioner
for both CPU and GPU

Conclusions
• Until now: Lack of portable partitioners for both
CPU and GPU
• Trilinos/Zoltan2 offers several options:
• MJ, Sphynx, Jet (soon)
• Performance-portable thanks to Kokkos
• Usual trade-off between run-time and quality
• These are primarily intended for Trilinos users, but
can be used by any application
• Jet currently limited to single node but
distributed memory extension (MPI) is planned
