Two additions, both building on outputs this pipeline already produces.
1. Bone surface area
generate_meshes.py already builds femur_mesh.vtk, tibia_mesh.vtk and patella_mesh.vtk. Surface area is one property on the mesh; return it in the step's metrics dict and it flows to the website's results file and analyses row with no orchestrator change.
It is also a shape measure that survives inputs with no cartilage — useful for a bones-only model, where thickness is unavailable.
⚠️ Decide and record which mesh it is measured on. resample_surface(clusters=...) resamples to a fixed count (20000 femur/tibia, 10000 patella) and area is sensitive to that. Raw marching-cubes area and post-pyacvd area are different numbers. In the A/B against the monolith the tibia meshes differed in area by 0.1 mm² purely from a different clustering, so this is not a hypothetical.
2. NSM-correspondence regions for mean thickness
Regional thickness today comes from mskt.image.cartilage_processing.get_knee_segmentation_with_femur_subregions() — a rule-based split: weight-bearing region by percent distance, anterior boundary from a logistic-regression fit, medial/lateral by centroid. Every region is derived from that subject's own geometry.
So the regions do not correspond across subjects. med_wb_fem_cart_mm_mean is a slightly different patch of anatomy in each knee, which limits what a cohort comparison of it can mean.
run_nsm.py already produces a reconstruction in correspondence — same vertex index, same anatomical location, across every subject fitted. Regions of interest defined once on the canonical NSM mesh can be transferred to every subject, giving thickness means that are anatomically comparable.
This does not replace the pymskt subregions. Those stay: they are what ~296 archived jobs used, and they work without NSM.
Constraints worth knowing before starting:
NSM fits the femur only today. nsm_bones loops over bones but the mesh paths inside the loop are hardcoded to femur_mesh_NSM_orig.vtk (run_nsm.py:376-390), so more than one bone would refit the femur. Fixing that loop is a prerequisite for tibial/patellar ROIs, on top of needing NSM models for those bones.
Thickness lives on the bone mesh as a per-vertex thickness (mm) array, not on the cartilage mesh. The ROI transfer therefore has to land on the bone mesh's vertices.
Whether this is a new step or an extension of run_nsm is open. A separate step keeps NSM's 600 s-per-fit timeout away from a cheap geometric operation.
Related: gattia/kneepipeline_segmentaton_website (registry entry and database columns).
