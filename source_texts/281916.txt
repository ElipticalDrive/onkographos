Summary
Component 5 of the meshing plan: an octree hexahedral core, as library code in aquaflux/meshing/. Not yet wired into CadMesher or the case file; a later PR does that.
Octree (octree.py): root cubes of the target size on a lattice covering the region's box, refined by criteria (NearSurface(inside, size) splits cubes a surface passes through), then balanced() so any two leaves sharing a face or part of an edge are at most one level apart.
OctreeCore(size, clearance, refinements).mesh(inside, lower, upper) -> HexCore (hex_core.py). The region is any inside test on points, so an analytic body, a solids body or later the CAD model all work. A leaf is kept when the cube grown by clearance is inside, tested on sample points.
No hanging nodes, resolved by templates. A cube with a smaller cube's corner at the middle of one of its edges is cut into pieces meeting at its centre. Each face becomes one of three things:
one quad, when none of its edges is halved;
four quads through the face centre, when the cube across is split;
a fan of triangles from the face centre, when edges are halved by cubes sharing only the edge.
A quad and the centre make a pyramid, a triangle and the centre a tetrahedron. Every face is decided from the whole tree rather than the kept set, so any subset of cubes is conformal where its cubes meet. The dual turns these pyramids and tets into polyhedra, so the solver's mesh still has none.
Pinches: cubes touching only along an edge or at a corner would make the boundary non-manifold. Each round drops at most one cube per pinched point (the most exposed one), until none is left.
Caps: every boundary quad gets an outward pyramid, so surface is a closed triangle surface. It is wound into the core, ready to be the hole in NetgenProcess.fill.
element_mesh (elements.py) also takes hexahedra and pyramids, numbered after tetrahedra and prisms, so its output for existing inputs is unchanged (tests/unit/test_meshing_elements.py untouched).
Design choices and their reasons are in the meshing rules file; measurements are in the meshing log.
Tests
test_meshing_octree.py:
The balance is checked by brute force over every pair of leaves.
Cubes the sphere passes through reach the target size.
The same refinement without balancing has neighbours three levels apart.
The root lattice covers the box.
test_meshing_hex_core.py:
A box keeps exactly its uniform grid of cubes, with 88 caps.
Refined core:
its volume equals the cubes';
its boundary area equals the cube union's, counted on the finest grid;
no mesh point sits at the middle of any cell edge.
The capped surface is closed, has one fan of faces per point, and encloses minus (cubes + caps).
Two boxes touching along an edge are parted only on that edge.
Hexahedra and pyramids through element_mesh, and the order cells are numbered in (tetrahedra, prisms, hexahedra, pyramids).
test_meshing_netgen.py: core + caps + Netgen fill inside a box wall.
The dual is closed and has volume 1.
Each point surrounded by 8 hexahedra becomes a cube of their size with 6 quad faces.
Mutation pass: 18 of 19 mutations turned a test red. The survivor, dropping leaf_at's level check, cannot change the result: levels are visited coarsest first, so a wrong early match is always overwritten. The check stays so the lookup doesn't depend on that order.
Local runs: the meshing tests plus the layering, vocabulary, scope, skip-census, validation-API, CAD-mesh case and OpenFOAM-writer tests plus the mesh-orientation tests (212 passed, Netgen included; rebased onto Project the dual's boundary onto the drawing #776–Grow prism layers from the wall, sized for the dual #778); ruff check and format; tools/build_docs.sh clean. The full fast gate was not run locally; CI runs it.
