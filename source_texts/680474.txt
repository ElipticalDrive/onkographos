Abstract
The benefits usually attributed to skip connections are optimization-theoretic: a smoother
loss landscape and better gradient propagation. We ask a representational question instead:
given a residual block x 7→ x + MLP(x), does a residual-free MLP of the same width
compute the same function? The answer is no, unconditionally and at every depth, for every
activation used in current frontier language models (ReLU2 , ReGLU, SwiGLU, GeGLU).
For ungated ReLU and GELU absorption is possible, but only on a set of weights of measure
zero. The two families are therefore generically disjoint: removing a skip connection and
retraining cannot recover exactly the same function at equal width.
Keywords: Residual networks, skip connections, ridge functions, deep learning
1 Introduction
Skip connections were the breakthrough that enabled systematic training of deep networks
(He et al., 2016a,b). Every frontier architecture uses them or a variant: the Transformer
(Vaswani et al., 2017), Vision Transformers (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), ConvNeXt (Liu
et al., 2022), LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023; Dubey et al., 2024), DeepSeek (DeepSeek-AI,
2024), and Gemma (Gemma Team, 2024) wrap their sub-layers in residual branches x 7→
x + f (x), while hyper-connections (HC) (Zhu et al., 2024) and manifold-constrained hyperconnections (mHC) (Xie et al., 2025) replace the identity with learnable mixing matrices.
The prevailing account is optimization-theoretic: skips smooth the landscape (Li et al.,
2018) and help gradients propagate (He et al., 2016b; Xiong et al., 2020).
We ask whether skip connections change what an MLP can represent. Fix MLP2 (x) =
Wdown σ(Wup x). When does there exist MLP1 (x) = Vdown σ(Vup x) of the same width with
x + MLP2 (x) = MLP1 (x)

If such absorption is possible, the skip is representationally redundant at that width. If
not, it grants access to a different region of function space. The converse question is equally
natural: rearranging MLP1 (x) = x+MLP2 (x) as (−Id )x+MLP1 (x) = MLP2 (x) shows both
directions are instances of the same problem, and our results settle both (Appendix B.4).
Relation to universal approximation. Lin and Jegelka (2018) showed that a very
deep ResNet with a single neuron per hidden layer approximates any Lebesgue-integrable
function on Rd in L1 , in sharp contrast to fully connected networks, which are not universal
approximators at width at most d (Lu et al., 2017; Hanin and Sellke, 2017). That result

concerns the bottleneck regime, where width is the binding constraint. Once each hidden
layer has at least d + 1 neurons, plain networks are already universal as depth grows, so the
skip adds no approximation power there. Our setting is that regime in practice (N = 4d; we
assume only N ≥ d), and our question is orthogonal: not whether a target is approximable,
but whether one fixed function is computed exactly without the skip at the same width. Two
blocks can approximate each other to any tolerance while remaining distinct as functions.
Contributions. For homogeneous activations of degree k ̸= 1, absorption is unconditionally impossible (Proposition 1), which covers ReLU2 and ReGLU. The same holds for gated
activations whose gate is differentiable at the origin and vanishes there (Proposition 2),
covering SwiGLU and GeGLU. Both extend to arbitrary depth (Corollary 3). For ungated
ReLU and GELU we give a single if-and-only-if characterization valid for generic weights
(Theorem 4) and deduce that the two families are generically disjoint (Corollary 5), while
d extra hidden units generically suffice (Proposition 8). Proofs are in the appendices.

3 Homogeneous and gated activations: unconditional impossibility

4 Ungated ReLU and GELU: an exact characterization
Ungated activations behave differently, because the odd part z/2 in (2) contributes a linear
term that a sign flip can turn against the skip. Write wi for the i-th row of Wup .
Theorem 4 (Identity absorption, ReLU and GELU) Let d ≥ 2, N ≥ d, and σ ∈
{ReLU, GELU}. Suppose Wup has pairwise non-collinear, non-zero rows and Wdown has
non-zero columns. Then there exist Vup ∈ RN ×d and Vdown ∈ Rd×N with

Where the condition comes from. Both proofs show any absorbing pair satisfies
Vdown Vup = Wdown C Wup with C = diag(±1), so, up to positive row scaling for ReLU,
Vup is Wup with some rows sign-flipped. Flipping row i leaves the even part untouched,
since E(−wi⊤ x) = E(wi⊤ x) and |−wi⊤ x| = |wi⊤ x|, while the odd part changes by −wi⊤ x.
Collecting flipped rows in S := {i : ci = −1}, their contribution is −Wdown [:, S]Wup [S, :]x,
which must equal +x. The factor 2 from the flip cancels the 1/2 in the odd part, which is
why (3) has −Id rather than a multiple of it; comparing ranks then forces |S| ≥ d. Note
that sign flips are not a symmetry here: they change the function, which is exactly why
they can cancel a skip. The genuine gauge group of a generic GELU MLP is the hidden-unit
permutations (Wang and Wang, 2025).
Corollary 5 (Generic disjointness) Let d ≥ 2, N ≥ d, and σ ∈ {ReLU, GELU}. For
Lebesgue-almost every (Wup , Wdown ) ∈ Θ there exist no Vup , Vdown with Wdown σ(Wup x) +
x = Vdown σ(Vup x) for all x.
Condition (3) imposes d2 polynomial constraints, and there are only finitely many subsets S, so the absorbing locus is a finite union of proper subvarieties of Θ and has measure
zero. The condition constrains W alone, so the statement is the strong one: for almost
every W , absorption fails for every V , not merely for almost every pair.
5 Discussion
The role of width. For ReLU and GELU, condition (3) stays measure zero even at
N ≫ d: width adds candidate subsets S without relaxing the d2 equations any single
subset must satisfy. Extra residual-free width does help: as σ(z) − σ(−z) = z, splitting d
neurons into opposed pairs adds an arbitrary linear map, so width N +d generically absorbs
the skip and, by the counting argument of Grillo and Montúfar (2026, Lemma B.3), N +d−1
does not (Proposition 8). The results of Section 3 hold for unequal widths too. Huang et al.
(2018) read a residual network as a sum of weak learners, one per block, which is available
only because each block has the form x + MLP(x). Our result says that, at equal width,
this additive form is a property of the function rather than of how it is written.
Gauge symmetry without normalization. Wang and Wang (2025) prove the TransQ (ℓ)
former gauge group factors over layers, GModel = ℓ GBlock , using a non-equivariance lemma
for LayerNorm: for generic gain and bias, LN(M x) = M LN(x) forces M = I. Normalization is removable in practice: Baroni et al. (2025) fine-tune it out of GPT-2 up to XL at
about +0.03 cross-entropy for GPT-2 XL, and Graef (2024) simplifies skipless transform
