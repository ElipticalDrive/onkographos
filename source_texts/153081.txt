Abstract Wayfinding studies recently often commenced in virtual environments due to their ability to provide a controlled system and environment. Researchers aimed to establish wayfinding simulation systems able to elicit natural participant responses so that the findings can increase their transferability to field settings and operational navigation contexts. Such validity is particularly important for specific wayfinding studies, such as transitional spaces, where cognitive responses at decision points rely on clear environmental cues. However, virtual reality (VR) studies still face challenges in achieving high perceptual realism. Additionally, empirical studies using photorealistic image-based virtual environments are still limited. Recently, Gaussian splatting (GS) surfaced as a technique that is capable of producing photorealistic models through a relatively efficient reconstruction method. Various studies have explored its feasibility through case studies; however, it has not been evaluated through wayfinding simulations. Therefore, this paper reports a pilot feasibility study (n=6), assessing whether a GS-based VR environment provides adequate usability/comfort, realism, presence, and wayfinding support to facilitate future wayfinding studies in transitional spaces. The research initiated with a VR experiment, in which participants completed a questionnaire after experiencing the GS environment via a VR head-mounted display (HMD). The result indicated that GS has preliminary support for the feasibility of the wayfinding study. Nonetheless, some limitations can be improved through controlled simulation movements, enhanced image acquisition, and viewpoint coverage.
