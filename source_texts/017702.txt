In direct volume rendering, the transport of light is modelled by equations originating from physics. These equations describe the behaviour of light in certain media. In most cases, a simplified equation is used, which is generally called low albedo approximation (Krueger 1991). It describes the behaviour of light in a volume which is composed of particles that receive light from surrounding light sources and reflect this light to the observer according to the material properties of the particles. The (simplified) intensity I(x,s) at position x integrated along the line x+ ts, t0 ≤ t ≤ tl, in this model is given by I(x,s) = Z tl t0 f (x+ ts)e− R t t0 α(x+us)dudt. (1) The point of entrance into the volume of particles is t0 and the point of exit is tl. The function α, commonly called opacity, is related to the density of the particles. An even more simplified model sets α = 0, which means that all particles have equal contribution regardless of their position in space. Using this model, the integral reduces to simple line integration: I(x,s) = Z tl t0 f (x+ ts)dt. (2) Since there is no opacity term, the result of this will be an X-ray like image.
