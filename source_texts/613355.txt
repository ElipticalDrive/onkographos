Streamed gsplats (lod-meta.json octrees) now choose their LOD by walking a node hierarchy from the top, instead of evaluating every leaf on each LOD update. The cost follows the size of the rendered cut, not the size of the octree, which matters most for large scenes with tens of thousands of leaves.
Changes:
At load, the leaves-only lod-meta.json is reshaped in memory into a hierarchy where each node holds one LOD level and is replaced by its children, one level finer. A level moves up to the highest node whose subtree holds at most 4K splats in that level and all coarser ones, and whose leaves' ranges form one contiguous range of one file. No data is changed or reloaded, and the file format is unchanged.
The splat budget is fit with a priority queue of refinements, seeded with the top nodes and stopping at the first refinement that does not fit. Distances are measured only for the nodes the walk reaches.
While the cut streams in, only loaded data renders: a loaded coarser node stands in for children that are still loading, or the finer loaded data stays until the new nodes load. No frame shows a region twice or not at all.
The per-leaf LOD path, its LOD tables and the scale histogram are removed.
GSPLAT_DEBUG_NODE_AABBS draws the nodes the cut renders, colored by LOD, and in gray the nodes it is waiting for.
The hierarchy is the same shape LCC2-style formats store natively, so those can later load into it directly.
Examples:
gaussian-splatting/lod-streaming: every LOD preset range ends on 20, so it reaches each scene's coarsest level; a speed hash param scales the camera speed; the LOD Box Shrink slider is removed.
Performance:
Poland x64 (26,571 leaves, 31,329 nodes after the reshape): the LOD update during a camera flight drops from 10.2 ms average (92 ms max) to about 1 ms median in a debug build. An update visits about 2,500 nodes and renders about 1,200 at a 4M budget.
The selected levels match the per-leaf selection for 86-96% of drawn splats at a 4M budget across the tested scenes, and differ by one level elsewhere.

[P2] Preserve gap filling when converting sparse LOD chains
Emitting an empty node for every zero/missing count changes the supported sparse-chain behavior, not just the hierarchy representation. For example, a single leaf with counts [100, 0, 20, 10], base distance 5, multiplier 3, and camera distance 10 selects the empty level-1 node in limit mode and draws nothing even with all real files resident; the base revision draws the 100-splat level 0. Likewise [0, 0, 20, 10] disappears at close range instead of using level 2. These cases were explicitly handled and tested by the removed GSplatLodTable. Please retain the distinction between an interior/finest-end gap (use available data) and levels beyond the last real coarse level (intentionally empty), including when combining leaves into parents.

Keeping this as is, by design. A level renders as the data holds it: every splat a leaf has at a level is drawn when that level is selected, and a leaf with no splats at a level draws nothing there. Levels are trained separately, so a region's distant splats can sit in neighbouring leaves at one level and not at another - filling the gap with the finer level would draw those splats on top of the neighbours'.
Filling it is also expensive: the fill is a range in another level's file, which stops the parents from taking over that level and every finer one. On Poland x64 (3,631 leaves with such a gap) the tree grows from 31K to 138K nodes and a LOD update from ~0.15 ms to ~14 ms. Documented in buildLodTree in 2ce2268.

[P2] Retain loaded children while their siblings are still streaming
When a coarse parent stays drawn until both finer children are available, the child that finishes first is neither drawn/referenced nor requested by this loop. pollFileResource() schedules that zero-ref file for cooldown, and nothing here cancels or protects it. I reproduced a two-child refinement where A loads while B remains pending: A has refcount 0, expires after cooldownTicks, and the next cut requests A again. The base revision retains A with refcount 1. With a slow sibling or a large set of files, this wastes downloads and can repeatedly prevent the children from being resident together. Please retain files needed for the pending replacement cut until the transition completes or is abandoned, without rendering the children on top of the parent.

[P2] Refresh distances for the finer nodes drawn as fallback
These descendants were not visited by the budget walk, so drawing them here leaves nodeInfos[n].worldDistanceSq at an earlier camera position (or its initial zero). That field is also used by GSplatWorld.applyWorkBufferUpdates() to decide when SH colors need refreshing. In a reproduction, LOD 0 was measured at distance 1000; after moving to distance 1 and reducing the budget to select an unloaded coarse node, the retained LOD-0 fallback still recorded distance squared 1,000,000. A subsequent 0.1-unit movement with colorUpdateAngle = 1 triggered no color update, whereas the base revision updated it. Measure the fallback paths/rendered nodes with the current camera terms as well, so the traversal optimization does not leave stale SH thresholds.

[P2] Retain intermediate fallback nodes while their siblings load
The underfill check still excludes data needed for staged refinement. With a loaded L2 root, two L1 children A/B, a requested L0 cut, and lodUnderfillLimit = 0, let A finish while B remains pending and both L0 descendants are absent. The root stays drawn because B is not coverable, but this condition rejects loaded A (1 - 0 > 0) and recurses into its unloaded L0 child. A therefore keeps refcount 0, expires after cooldownTicks, and is requested again. I reproduced that sequence; loading both A/B together immediately afterward renders both at L1, confirming A was useful fallback data despite being outside the underfill limit. Please retain the loaded fallback coverage the pending transition can actually use, including intermediate nodes outside the limit, and add a three-level staggered-loading regression test. Otherwise slow sibling loads can still cause repeated downloads and delay refinement, so the previous retention finding is only partially fixed.
