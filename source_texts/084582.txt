Android SplatNode (sceneview/src/main/java/io/github/sceneview/node/SplatNode.kt, materials/splat.mat): hardware-instanced camera-facing quads. Per-splat data sits in two RGBA16F textures, fetched in the vertex shader by instance id. Premultiplied-alpha blend, depthWrite:false / depthCulling:true (opaque geometry occludes splats). Batches of at most 65535 instances per draw. CPU painter's sort on a background thread, because Filament's Android Java API exposes no compute/SSBO (verified through 1.72.1, see the header comment in splat.mat). Isotropic billboards: the max-axis scale is used and rotations are carried but never read.
Web SplatNode (sceneview-web/.../nodes/SplatNode.kt, materials/splat_web.mat): the same design, compiled by a second matc against the filamentWeb pin (material v52), shipped as an embedded base64 blob. Floats replace ints because Filament.js embind has no setIntParameter. Browser gate: samples/web-demo/tests/splat-bundle.spec.ts.
SplatNode also works in AR as it stands: ARSceneScope extends SceneScope, so the composable is available inside ARSceneView { }. The demo just never uses it that way.
Demo splat-preview (samples/android-demo/.../demos/SplatPreviewDemo.kt): Niantic's MIT raccoon capture, cropped to 233,808 splats / 3.1 MB. It has a splat-count slider plus a file-size and decode-time readout. Known visible defect: hazy and over-bright, the P1 isotropic artefact (issue feat(sceneview): SplatNode — 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering (Android Filament custom material; iOS RealityKit native; web follows) #2646 comment of 2026-09-16, capture docs/ux/captures/android-3620-splat-preview-2026-09-16/).
iOS: nothing. grep -ric splat SceneViewSwift/Sources = 0, and parity-manifest.yml lists splat-preview as android-only.
Still open in feat(sceneview): SplatNode — 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering (Android Filament custom material; iOS RealityKit native; web follows) #2646: anisotropic projection, iOS SplatNode on GaussianSplatComponent, KHR_gaussian_splatting, SH > 0, the AR-portal demo, and streaming/LOD beyond about 500k splats.

A. Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
What it is in 2026
A scene is represented as millions of anisotropic 3D gaussians (position, rotation, 3-axis scale, opacity, spherical-harmonic colour), rasterised as sorted, alpha-blended screen-space ellipses. Radiance-field captures made this way are the de facto output of Scaniverse, Polycam, Luma, KIRI and every INRIA-style trainer. In 2026 it became a standard:
glTF KHR_gaussian_splatting: RC in Feb 2026, and the extension README now reads "Complete, Ratified by the Khronos Group". Splats are a POINTS primitive with KHR_gaussian_splatting:ROTATION/SCALE/OPACITY/SH_DEGREE_0_COEF_0 attributes (SH up to degree 3), and non-supporting renderers fall back to a point cloud. Compression is layered in separate extensions (an SPZ-based one exists). Backed by NVIDIA, Google, Adobe, Cesium and others.
OpenUSD UsdVolParticleField / UsdVolParticleField3DGaussianSplat, in OpenUSD 26.03 (March 2026), co-authored by Apple, NVIDIA, Adobe and Pixar.
Apple RealityKit 27 (WWDC26 session 279): GaussianSplatResource + GaussianSplatComponent on iOS, iPadOS, macOS, Catalyst and visionOS, Apple7 GPU family and later. It is format-agnostic: the app supplies position/scale/rotation/opacity/SH buffers as LowLevelBuffers (float or half). There is an internal splat-count cap, and the initializer throws above it.
SPZ (Niantic, MIT): about 10× smaller than PLY. SPZ 4 (May 2026) replaces gzip with six parallel ZSTD streams and lifts the 10M-point cap, and the Scaniverse conversion tool now emits v4 (reported). SceneView reads only v2/v3.
Web renderers (all permissive): Spark / Spark 2.0 (World Labs, MIT, three.js, WebGL2, LoD streaming; reads PLY/SPZ/SPLAT/KSPLAT/SOG), PlayCanvas engine + SuperSplat (MIT, SOG format, WebGPU), mkkellogg GaussianSplats3D (MIT), gsplat.js (MIT), Babylon.js (Apache-2.0).
Mobile/native: MetalSplatter (Swift/Metal, MIT); NVIDIA vk_gaussian_splatting (Apache-2.0); MCGS (Vulkan compute, runs on Android); Mobile-GS (ICLR 2026, 116 fps on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3; mobile code not released). Filament itself has no splat support (google/filament discussion #8033).
Single image → splat: Apple SHARP (apple/ml-sharp, Dec 2025), under the Apple ML research licence. The model licence needs checking before any shipped use.
Feasibility per platform
Platform
Verdict
Notes
Android (Filament)
Shipping, needs a quality pass.
Anisotropic projection is a vertex-shader extension of the proven material: add a rotation texture (or pack a quaternion and scales into a third RGBA16F texture), compute Σ = R·S·Sᵀ·Rᵀ, project it with the view Jacobian to a 2×2 covariance, eigen-decompose to major/minor axes, and place the quad corners on those axes. getViewFromWorldMatrix() and getClipFromViewMatrix() are available in Filament material vertex code. No compute is needed. Sorting stays on the CPU. That is fine up to about 300–500k splats; beyond that, LOD/pruning is needed.
iOS (RealityKit 27)
Thin port, gated on OS 27.
@available(iOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27, *) SplatNode fed from the same KMP SplatCloud (via the sceneview-core XCFramework bridge, or a small Swift port of the PLY/SPZ parsers) into LowLevelBuffers → GaussianSplatResource. RealityKit owns sorting and SH. On iOS 18–26: either stay unsupported (honest) or fall back to MetalSplatter (MIT) via LowLevelMesh/Metal, which costs L. Recommended: OS 27 only.
Web (Filament.js)
Shipping, the same quality pass applies.
Port the anisotropic math to splat_web.mat (float params). Do not mount Spark/PlayCanvas next to Filament.js: that means two WebGL contexts, no shared depth, and splats could not be occluded by glTF content. Spark is still the right reference for LoD/streaming and for SPZ v4/SOG decoding.
Demo concept: "Your scan, in your room" (then "Step into a world")
Open a scan (exists): orbit a real phone capture, with the splat-count slider and a cost readout. After the anisotropic pass it looks sharp instead of hazy.
New AR screen: the user taps a floor or table and the raccoon family appears there at real scale, occluded by nothing. Walking around it reveals a photoreal capture sitting in a live camera feed. This is the moment people screenshot. It uses ARSceneView + the existing placement stack + SplatNode.
"Open with" / share target for .spz / .ply splats: export from Scaniverse, tap Share, choose SceneView, and the scan is in AR. The Android demo is already an "Open with" target for .ply meshes, and SplatParser.parse auto-detects.
Later, an AR portal: a doorway on the floor through which you step into a Marble world. This bridges A and C.
Effort and risks
Anisotropic material (Android + web, two matc pins): S–M. iOS SplatNode on RealityKit 27: M. AR placement screen: S. SPZ v4 (ZSTD) decoding in KMP: M (needs a pure-Kotlin ZSTD decoder, or a platform codec: zstd-jni (BSD) on Android, fzstd (MIT) on the web). SH > 0 and streaming LOD: L, and later.
Performance on mid-range phones: fill rate and overdraw dominate. Anisotropic ellipses reduce overdraw compared with max-axis discs, so this is also a performance win. The CPU sort is O(n log n) per re-sort, plus about 16 B/splat of texture re-upload (24 B with a third texture); at 233k splats that is about 5.6 MB per re-sort. Mitigations: the existing motion threshold, the splatCount LOD hook, opacity pruning at load.
Asset size: SPZ runs about 13 B/splat (3.1 MB for 233k); a 1–2M-splat Marble world is 15–30 MB. Bundle one small asset and fetch the rest on demand.
Format churn: SPZ v4 files already fail with a clear error. Fix this before inviting "open your own Scaniverse export".
Licences: SPZ (MIT); the raccoon sample (Niantic, MIT, already credited). User captures belong to the user.
API sketch
The current Android API requires the caller to wire onFrame → cameraPositionProvider. An AI will forget that, and the only symptom is popping, with no error. The web port already sorts against the live camera automatically. Proposal: make the scene's camera the default sort source, and add a path loader that mirrors rememberModelInstance.
```
// Android: proposed (additive; today's signature keeps working)
SceneView(modifier = Modifier.fillMaxSize()) {
    rememberSplatCloud("splats/scan.spz")?.let { cloud ->   // null while decoding, off main thread
        SplatNode(splatCloud = cloud)                       // sorts against the scene camera by default
    }
}

// AR: same node, real scale
ARSceneView(modifier = Modifier.fillMaxSize(), planeRenderer = true) {
    anchor?.let { AnchorNode(anchor = it) {
        rememberSplatCloud("splats/scan.spz")?.let { SplatNode(splatCloud = it) }
    } }
}
```
```
// iOS 27+: proposed, mirrors ModelNode.load
SceneView {
    if let scan { scan }
}
.task { scan = try? await SplatNode.load("scan.spz") }   // PLY / SPZ, format sniffed from bytes
```

B. OpenUSD
What it is in 2026
AOUSD Core Specification 1.0 was published on 2025-12-17 (the first formally ratified core spec), and 1.1 (animation, scaling, conformance) is in progress for 2026.
OpenUSD 26.03 added UsdVolParticleField (splats in USD).
Apple, WWDC26 session 285: USDKit, a first-party Swift framework to open, edit, compose and export USD stages, integrated with RealityKit and Spatial Preview; exportPackage with mesh compression (reported up to 90%) and AVIF textures. macOS Preview gained 3D editing.
Runtime libraries: Pixar OpenUSD (Modified Apache 2.0; heavy C++, not practical in an APK). TinyUSDZ (lighttransport, Apache-2.0/MIT, dependency-free C++14, USDA/USDC/USDZ, security-focused, JS/WASM binding on npm). three.js USDLoader (MIT; USDA + USDC + USDZ, subset). Needle @needle-tools/usd (OpenUSD wasm + three.js Hydra delegate, PolyForm Noncommercial, so it is not usable for us). Autodesk's USD-for-web wasm work (2022).
Android / Filament: no USD support. Google's own 3D stack (Filament, Android XR, Scene Viewer) is glTF-first.
Feasibility per platform
Platform
Verdict
iOS
Already loads .usdz/.usda/.usdc/.usd through RealityKit (ModelFormat.swift). New on OS 27: USDKit authoring/export, and ParticleField splats would render through the same GaussianSplatComponent path as A. Low effort, fold it into A's iOS work.
Android
Needs a reader. The realistic path is TinyUSDZ via JNI → in-memory glTF → Filament, the same pattern as 3MF (sceneview-core converts 3MF → GLB). Covers UsdGeomMesh + UsdPreviewSurface + textures; skinning and MaterialX are out. Adds a native .so of roughly 1–3 MB per ABI. M–L.
Web
TinyUSDZ wasm → glTF → Filament.js, or three.js USDLoader logic ported. M.
Demo concept: "An iPhone scan, opened on Android"
Object Capture, Scaniverse and Polycam export .usdz. On Android today, "Open with SceneView" does not list .usdz. The demo: AirDrop, share or download a .usdz from an iPhone, open it on Android and place it in AR. Cross-platform honesty is the wow here, not the rendering. A second beat on iOS 27: open a USD file containing a ParticleField splat next to a mesh, the first scene mixing both in one file.
Effort and risks
Effort M–L (JNI build for four ABIs, fuzzing untrusted input, conversion fidelity, materials).
Risks: the subset problem (USD composition, variants and payloads give "it opened but looks wrong"); native-code attack surface on untrusted files, where TinyUSDZ's security focus helps; APK size; low visible wow for the effort.
Licences: TinyUSDZ Apache-2.0/MIT, fine. Avoid Needle (non-commercial) and a full OpenUSD build (size).
API sketch
This needs no new API, which is what makes it AI-proof: the format is sniffed from the bytes, the way 3MF already works.
```
rememberModelInstance(modelLoader, "scan.usdz")?.let { ModelNode(modelInstance = it, scaleToUnits = 1f) }
```
```
let model = try await ModelNode.load("scan.usdz")   // unchanged
```
```
await viewer.loadModel("https://example.com/scan.usdz"); // proposed: sniffed, converted in-memory
```
C. World models / AI 3D generation
What it is in 2026
There are two different things under this name:
Interactive world models, which generate video frames conditioned on actions. Genie 3 (DeepMind, Aug 2025) runs at 720p/24 fps with minutes of consistency. Project Genie reached Google AI Ultra subscribers in the US on 2026-01-29, and there is no public API. The output is pixels, not 3D assets, so there is nothing for a 3D SDK to render. Not actionable for SceneView.
3D world/asset generators that output real 3D:
World Labs Marble (+ World API, Jan 2026): text/image/video → explorable world exported as Gaussian splats .spz/.ply (about 500k or 2M tiers) plus a collider GLB (100–200k triangles) and optional high-quality meshes. Reported at about $1.20 per world (Marble 1.1). The output is directly consumable by SplatNode, with SPZ version caveats (see A).
Object generators (→ GLB): Tripo (API; already in sceneview-mcp), Meshy, Rodin (commercial APIs). Open weights: Microsoft TRELLIS / TRELLIS.2 (MIT), Tencent Hunyuan3D 2.x (community licence, excludes the EU/UK/South Korea), Meta SAM 3D Objects (reported to permit commercial use). Tencent HunyuanWorld covers world generation with open weights under a community licence.
Single image → splat: Apple SHARP (see A).
Feasibility per platform
Rendering is already solved: splats go to SplatNode (A) and GLBs to ModelNode (Android/web) or USDZ conversion (iOS). The hard part is not the renderer, it is the service:
Keys and billing are an invariant. A shipped demo app cannot embed a World Labs or Tripo key. Live generation needs a proxy (e.g. the existing Cloudflare worker pattern) with per-user rate limits and a hard spend cap. This is a decision for Thomas.
Latency: worlds take minutes and Tripo up to about 100 s. The UX has to be "generate, get notified, open", not a spinner.
iOS: Marble GLB needs USDZ conversion for meshes; splats go through the A iOS path (OS 27).
Demo concept: "Describe a place, then step into it"
The user types "a sunlit Japanese tea house" (or picks from 4 pre-generated worlds) and a portal opens on the floor in AR. Walking through it puts them inside a photoreal Marble world, rendered by SplatNode, with the collider GLB (hidden) used for hit-tests so objects can be dropped on its floor. The companion beat is "describe an object" (Tripo), which drops a generated GLB into the same world. Phase 1 ships only pre-generated worlds bundled or hosted on sceneview.dev, so there is zero runtime cost, zero keys, and the full wow.
Effort and risks
S for pre-generated worlds, once A (anisotropic + SPZ v4) lands. M–L for live generation (proxy, quotas, moderation of prompts, abuse).
Risks: Marble ToS on redistributing generated worlds inside an app (must be checked before bundling); asset size (2M splats is about 30 MB, so use the 500k tier on phones); 500k+ splats on a mid-range phone with a CPU sort needs A's LOD/pruning; content moderation for free-text prompts; vendor lock-in (keep the pipeline format-based: SPZ/PLY/GLB in, nothing Marble-specific in the SDK).
Licences: TRELLIS (MIT) is the only permissive open-weight option to self-host; Hunyuan3D's regional exclusion rules it out for a global app.
API sketch
The SDK stays format-based. Generation lives in the MCP and in app code, never in the rendering API:
```
// Pre-generated world, hosted
rememberSplatCloud("https://sceneview.dev/worlds/tea-house.spz")?.let { SplatNode(splatCloud = it) }
rememberModelInstance(modelLoader, "https://sceneview.dev/worlds/tea-house-collider.glb")?.let {
    ModelNode(modelInstance = it, apply = { isVisible = false })   // hit-test only
}
```
MCP side (exists for objects, proposed for worlds): generate_3d_model({ prompt }) → GLB; proposed generate_world({ prompt }) → { splatUrl, colliderUrl } (BYOK WORLDLABS_API_KEY, same shape as the Tripo tool).
D. Volumetric video / 4D
What it is in 2026
Time-varying 3D captures, now dominated by 4D Gaussian splatting:
Representations: per-frame keyframes plus motion deltas (a codec-style scheme; Gracia); continuous per-gaussian trajectories with temporal opacity (4DV.ai, SpacetimeGaussians-style polynomial motion). Research on streaming and compression (4DGCPro, 4D-MoDe, PD-4DGS, SwinGS) is very active.
Products: Gracia (streamable 4DGS apps on Quest and Vision Pro, a WebXR stream in March 2026, a free App Store viewer); a 4-minute DNE × Gracia performance (April 2026); an 11.5-minute 4DGS film premiered at SIGGRAPH 2026 (4DV.ai/Dell).
Bandwidth: Gracia reports about 80 Mbit/s for about 120k splats per frame.
No open interchange standard yet (neither KHR nor USD covers time-varying splats in a ratified form). Open research datasets (N3DV, Technicolor, CMU Panoptic) are mostly non-commercial.
Feasibility per platform
Platform
Verdict
Android
Per-frame re-upload plus a per-frame CPU sort of 120k splats at 30 fps (about 3 MB/frame, 90 MB/s of texture uploads) is not viable on mid-range and marginal on flagships. Viable variant: a motion-in-shader 4DGS: static per-splat attributes plus per-splat motion coefficients (linear/polynomial trajectory + temporal opacity window) in extra textures, evaluated in the vertex shader from a time uniform. Sorting stays occasional (motions are small). This is an extension of splat.mat, the same technique as A.
iOS 27
GaussianSplatComponent buffers are referenced, not copied, and "can be animated by rewriting content" (reported), so a keyframe player is plausible with RealityKit doing the sorting.
Web
Same shader approach as Android; Gracia proves browser playback is possible.
Demo concept: "A performer on your table"
A 5–10 s looping volumetric clip (a dancer, a pet, a musician) placed in AR on a table, with scrub, pause and "infinite slow motion" by walking around a frozen moment. Where this lands, it outclasses everything else here, because nobody expects a phone SDK to do it.
Effort and risks
L. It needs A's anisotropic material first, a 4D asset pipeline (converter from a published 4DGS format into our motion textures), a playback clock that interacts correctly with FrameRatePolicy (a playing clip must hold Continuous(); see llms.txt "Render on demand"), and asset sourcing.
Risks: content licensing (open datasets are mostly NC, and commercial captures belong to Gracia/4DV); size (seconds, not minutes, and even a 10 s clip at 50k splats is tens of MB); mid-range performance; no standard to target, so we would be picking a winner too early.
API sketch
```
val clip = rememberSplatSequence("volumetric/dancer.4dspz")        // proposed; null while loading
val player = rememberSplatPlayer(clip, loop = true)                 // exposes isPlaying, time, seek()
SceneView(frameRatePolicy = if (player.isPlaying) FrameRatePolicy.Continuous() else FrameRatePolicy.OnDemand()) {
    clip?.let { SplatSequenceNode(sequence = it, time = player.time) }
}
```
```
SceneView { if let clip { clip } }
    .task { clip = try? await SplatSequenceNode.load("dancer.4dspz", loop: true) }   // iOS 27+
```
5. Ranking and reasoning
A: Gaussian splatting. About 70% built. The remaining work is exactly what makes the demo visibly better (anisotropic), unlocks iOS cheaply (RealityKit 27 native), and is what every other direction reuses. The standards arrived this year (KHR ratified, USD ParticleField, SPZ 4), so it is "hype" with staying power.
C: AI world generation. The best story for an "AI-first SDK", and nearly free once A lands, because Marble outputs SPZ/PLY and the MCP already generates GLBs. Start with pre-generated worlds so no key or billing question blocks the demo. Genie-style video world models are out: no API, and no 3D output.
D: Volumetric/4D. Highest wow, but L effort, no standard, and licensing traps. Do it later as a shader extension of A (motion-in-vertex-shader), not as a streaming engine.
B: OpenUSD. iOS already covers it. On Android and the web it is real format work (TinyUSDZ → glTF) with little visible wow. Do it as a format-coverage item (.usdz "Open with" on Android) when the formats track ([formats] One entry point for every format: ModelFormat sniffing, supportedFormats, unit, typed error, and the llms.txt section that sells it #3489/[formats] sceneview-web loadModel, the /open page and the view_3d_model widget accept 3MF, STL, OBJ and PLY #3491) resumes, and fold the USD ParticleField part into A's iOS work.
6. Minimal first prototype for #1 (Android)
Goal: prove on one platform that SceneView renders a real phone capture at a quality worth putting in a store screenshot, and puts it in the user's room.
Scope (one PR per step, both on Android):
Anisotropic splat.mat
SplatBuffers: add a third RGBA16F texture, splatRotation (quaternion xyzw). splatPositionScale.a stays for compatibility; the 3-axis scale goes into a fourth texture, or scale xyz plus position are repacked across three textures. Unit-test the packing like the existing SplatBuffersTest.
Vertex shader: Σ₃ = R·S²·Rᵀ → view space → J (perspective Jacobian from getClipFromViewMatrix() focal terms) → Σ₂ (+0.3 px² low-pass as in the reference 3DGS) → eigenvalues λ₁, λ₂ and eigenvectors → corners = centre ± 3·√λ·v (clamped). The fragment shader evaluates exp(-½ dᵀ Σ₂⁻¹ d) from the interpolated quad coordinates.
Recompile splat.filamat with the filament pin (tools/GenerateFilamat.sh --check). The web port (splat_web.mat, filamentWeb pin) follows in a second PR.
Acceptance: before/after captures of the raccoon scan on the Pixel_7a AVD (same pose, light + dark), with the haze gone and edges crisp. The existing DemoRenderingScreenshotTest baseline is updated deliberately. Frame time is equal or better (less overdraw).
"Your scan, in your room" AR screen
A new demo card (or a mode of splat-preview) using ARSceneView + the existing placement stack + SplatNode at authored scale (captures are metric). Its cameraPositionProvider comes from the AR camera.
Acceptance: a capture of the scan standing on a detected plane (the AR emulator scene), and a walk-around sequence that shows no popping.
