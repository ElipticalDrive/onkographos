Closes #1. In HQ, an emitter was found only by a bounced path that happened to hit it. Small emitters lit almost nothing (a candle room was near black at hundreds of spp), while their own faces blew out. The research behind the change is in docs/research/emitters.md, each line marked cited or inferred.
What changed
NEE with MIS. A light quad is picked by power, then a texel within it by its share of the quad's power. Each pick is weighted against BSDF sampling by the power heuristic (Veach, PBRT). Kernel, WGSL and CUDA share one layout and one random stream, with two new buffers: the light list and an emitter table. --no-light-sampling renders the reference without it.
The look and the light are separate, as Blender's camera-ray visibility and Unreal's EmissiveBoost separate them:
camera and specular rays see texel × level/15 × --emitter-glow (default 1.5);
diffuse surfaces receive texel² × light scale × --emitter-intensity (default 1).
Each block casts power 600 × (L/15)² whatever its shape. This rule is our own: no source calibrates a path tracer to the game's 15-level falloff, and the research doc says so. Power ∝ L² makes reach proportional to level under inverse-square falloff, so a torch and a glowstone block light a room about equally, as in the game.
Falloff is 1/max(d², 1), from Chunky, applied to both strategies so MIS stays consistent.
Measured
Candle room, 640×360:
NEE at 256 spp against NEE at 4096: RMS 5.51/255.
Without light sampling at 256 spp against the 4096 reference: 61.87.
At 4096 spp, linear means with and without light sampling agree within 0.27%, so the estimator is unbiased.
emitter_calibration: a torch on a white floor, HQ against quick at light levels 12 down to 3. Mean error 0.76 levels, worst 2.11, within bounds of 1.5 and 3. Half power and double power both fail the test.
Daylight showcase at 1024 spp:
RMS 4.07/255 against a seed-noise floor of 1.30; the difference sits in tiles that hold emitters.
Render time 13.3 s → 24.7 s, because the shadow ray to emitters is traced on every bounce.
Parity:
Metal: gpu_matches_cpu_in_hq_mode and determinism pass.
RTX (Vulkan via dzn): 0.001% / 0.021%; the known environmental crash at thread exit follows the checks.
CUDA: the PTX builds; the parity test skipped because WSL exposes no CUDA device.
Open, recorded in the research doc
Walls facing a lamp read brighter than quick mode, because the game ignores a surface's angle to the light. On a plain floor, 4 candles read about 1.8 levels darker than the game.
Dark-coloured emitters (red candles) cast oversaturated light, because power is normalised by luminance.
No spatial light structure exists for many emitters.

… by their level

The scene builds a light list: one entry per instance of a quad of a
light-emitting block, faces against an occluding neighbour and buried bricks
left out, picked by binary search on a power-proportional CDF. Each diffuse
vertex samples one point on one light and weighs it against cosine BSDF
sampling with the power heuristic; a BSDF ray that finds an emitter takes the
complementary weight from the quad's stored pick probability. The kernel,
the WGSL mirror and the CUDA entry point take the light list as a new buffer.

An emitter's look and its light are separate: the camera (or a specular chain
from it) sees texel x level/15 x the glow scale (misc_f.x, --emitter-glow,
default 1.5); diffuse surfaces receive texel^2 x the quad's light scale x
misc_f.w (--emitter-intensity, default 1). A block's light scale makes its
total power LEVEL_15_POWER x (level/15)^2 whatever its shape (calibration
constant still to be measured).

Kernel test: on a lamp-lit floor at 16384 spp the frame mean with light
sampling is within 4% of BSDF sampling alone (0.40% apart at 65536 spp:
0.00923 vs 0.00927) and one sample's variance falls from 0.0154 to 0.0027.

…clamp, and calibrated

Light sampling now picks a texel cell of the chosen quad by its share of the
quad's emitted power (an emitter table per light quad: pick probability and
cumulative cell shares), so a torch's flame texels take the samples its dark
stick used to. A hit records its (s, t) so BSDF hits evaluate the same
density for MIS. The table is a new kernel buffer (WGSL binding 7, two more
CUDA arguments).

Emitted light reaching a surface closer than one block is scaled by d^2:
falloff 1/max(d^2, 1), as Chunky's emitter sampling does, applied to light
sampling and BSDF hits alike so MIS stays consistent. Without it, the floor
touching the torch made a hot spot whose indirect light speckled every wall.

LEVEL_15_POWER = 600: emitter_calibration's best fit at the old value of 64
was a multiplier of 9.19 (CPU, 256 spp, macOS). At 600: mean |level error|
0.76, worst 2.11 over game levels 12..3; at half the power 2.34, at double
3.19. The test now also asserts that half and double fail its bounds.
