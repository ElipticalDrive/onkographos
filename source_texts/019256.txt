The lung and pulmonary nodules visualization is crucial in preoperative planning. However, it is very difficult to volume rendering the nodules without occlusion due to the nodules are nested within the lung. Although the semi-transparent visualization can solve the occlusion problem, it lost texture of pulmonary tongue on the surface of the lung in volume rendering. To conquer this problem, we provide a new computational frame to cut lung tissue around the nodules by selective volume clipping. In this framework, an arbitrary-shaped closed curve centered on the nodule is first drawn in volume rendering. For facilitating the decision on whether the voxels should be retained, voxels through coordinate transformation are all projected onto screen frame. Then the voxels in the closed curve except for the voxels of pulmonary nodule are all cut off. Thus the occlusion problem can be solved in clinical visualization. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
