ABSTRACT
The lung and pulmonary nodules visualization is crucial in
preoperative planning. However, it is very difficult to volume
rendering the nodules without occlusion due to the nodules are
nested within the lung. Although the semi-transparent
visualization can solve the occlusion problem, it lost texture of
pulmonary tongue on the surface of the lung in volume rendering.
To conquer this problem, we provide a new computational frame
to cut lung tissue around the nodules by selective volume clipping.
In this framework, an arbitrary-shaped closed curve centered on
the nodule is first drawn in volume rendering. For facilitating the
decision on whether the voxels should be retained, voxels through
coordinate transformation are all projected onto screen frame.
Then the voxels in the closed curve except for the voxels of
pulmonary nodule are all cut off. Thus the occlusion problem can
be solved in clinical visualization. Experimental results
demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
Volume clipping is typically used in visualizing medical data,
because it conveniently clips away selected parts of volume data
and keep the desired reference information. So, volume clipping
can play a crucial role in exploring the interior details of volume
data. Currently, volume clipping methods also are widely studied
in [6]-[11]. For example, Weiskopf et al. proposed clipping
methods that are capable of using complex geometries for volume
clipping [6]. Kai et al. proposed volume cutting method based on
hardware-accelerated [7]. Chen et al. proposed a GPU-based point
radiation technique to remove and carve the occluding materials
and isolate and extract different regions of interest [8]. Zhang et al.
proposed a novel hybrid cutting method by combining nonprogressive cutting with progressive cutting [9]. In additional,
Turkiyyah et al. proposed a mesh cutting mesh during real-time
physical simulation, but it cannot be applied in volume cutting
[10]. However, these volume clipping methods generally used
common clipping geometry that can be structured by implicit
functions to clip medical images.

1. INTRODUCTION
Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death
worldwide. In resection of lung cancer, it is very important to
visually observe the shapes and relative positions of lung and
pulmonary nodules in preoperative planning. Volume rendering is
important visualization technique for exploring and visualizing
structures in 3D images. By observing volume rendering results,
users can discover some structures of interest and have intuitive
In this paper, we use a flexible and timesaving framework to
cutting and visualizing lungs and pulmonary nodules
simultaneously to solve the occlusion problem. In this framework,
the physician can intuitively observe where the pulmonary
nodules are located in the lung, while keep the texture of
pulmonary tongue on the surface of the lung. Here, we
interactively cut the lung tissue around the nodules using closed
curve with arbitrary shape [11]. But in our method, we first
extract lungs of interest to exclude unwanted structures, and then
actively retain pulmonary nodules in the cutting process.
Experimental results also demonstrate the effectiveness of the
proposed method.

2.1 Volume Render Lungs and Pulmonary
Nodules
In order to visualize lungs and pulmonary nodules, it is necessary
to first segment them in the chest CT images. The lung
segmentation with 2D feature space is a novel approach in
medical image segmentation. The 2D feature space can be defined
as a 2D histogram which describes and counts the attribute
distribution of the gray and gradient value in the chest CT image.
In the 2D feature space, the horizontal and vertical coordinate
represent the gray scale and magnitude of gradient, respectively.

2.2 Binary Mask Image Generation
A closed curve is usually drawn on screen to clip away or keep
some regions of volume data. According to the coordinates of
pixels on the closed curve, which voxels of volume data inside the
curve and which ones outside would be decided. Here, in order to
obtain the closed curve, we first draw some discontinuous points
onto the region of interest of the screen in Figure 4(a), then fit
them by B-Spline to generate the closed curve in Figure 4(b).
Now, we only need to select region R1 to obtain the voxel set of
the lungs, as shown in Figures 1(a). When selecting a region U
(this paper assumes U = R1, representing the selected gray and
gradient interval) from the 2D feature space of a chest CT image
f x, y, z for the lungs, a set of voxels (denoted byΦU , Eq.1) is
determined from x, y, z . Although ΦU also contains some
uninteresting structures, they are not connected spatially, as
shown in Figures 1(b). Based on this property, we can easily
extract the lungs from ΦU by the following tracing algorithm:

3.2 Discussion
In resection of lung cancer, it is very important to visually observe
the shapes and relative positions of lungs and pulmonary nodules
in surgery planning. Now our method is able to accomplish this
purpose manly because it has two merits in cutting process.
(2)
The selective volume clipping process can be seen in Figures 5.
We first draw some discontinuous points centered on the nodule
in volume rendering, and then these points are fitted to generate a
closed curve. Finally, we can retain or cut away some voxels by
the binary mask image. It is important that the algorithm can
determine whether the voxels projected inside the closed curve
belong to lungs or pulmonary nodules. Therefore, our method is
able to selectively cut the voxels projected inside the closed curve
to retain the nodules.
The one is that the 2D feature space and the tracing algorithm are
first used to extract the lung in the chest CT images. This is a
flexible and timesaving strategy.
The other is that our method can assign different voxels with
different classes when the lung and pulmonary nodules are
separated. Thus the algorithm can selectively cut the voxels
projected inside the closed curve and retain pulmonary nodules.
