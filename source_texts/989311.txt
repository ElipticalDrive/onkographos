Goal:
Implement a bounded procedural-campaign framework so worlds, districts, incidents, factions, sites, staff candidates, anomalies, artifacts, rumors, and prehistory are generated from seeded authored rules rather than loose randomness, creating replayable variation that remains mechanically legible and historically persistent.
Scope:
support seeded campaign generation so a world can be reproduced, shared, debugged, balanced, and compared by seed rather than only by ad hoc save state
support human-authored rule tables and constraint packets for domains, biomes, factions, entities, interactions, materials, incidents, and site types instead of pure random generation
support incident generation producing meaningful operational situations from compact seeded rules rather than requiring fully authored scenario chains
support procedural incident seed composition combining actor, location, triggering action, motive, and condition columns into reusable operational prompts with suspicious incongruity and downstream links to map nodes, witnesses, follow-up mission cards, rumors, corpses, pursuit, institutions, and hidden-place hooks
support localized incident scale guardrails so contracts stay at cabin, camp, hospital, block, or comparable bounded scales rather than defaulting to metropolis or invasion scale
support seeded ruined-district region persistence where factions, incidents, salvage, rumors, and district control remain replayable campaign substrate
support urban rumor feeds that announce threats, faction movement, scenario hooks, and false leads without collapsing into one truth stream
support modular scenario, faction, hazard, and district packs as bounded generator inputs rather than one opaque result table
support authored content providing bounded seeds and rules rather than requiring every campaign path to follow a fixed narrative sequence
support incidents, timers, travel pressure, symbolic clues, stewardship duties, unresolved objectives, and faction activity colliding into emergent structure
support layered generation from macro to micro, such as world parameters to regions to districts to sites to factions to anomalies to incidents to evidence to reports
support perceived uniqueness safeguards that prevent procedural sameness by making generated campaigns differ in domain profile, behavior patterns, locations, witnesses, consequences, archive traces, and downstream faction reactions rather than only shuffled labels
support fairness and survivability constraints so generated content produces pressure and uncertainty without defaulting into unwinnable or incoherent noise when the player is reasonably prepared
support hybrid procedural-plus-handcrafted structure where authored anchors such as patrons, unique artifacts, sealed strata, recurring arcs, and capstone incidents coexist with generated variation
support campaign parameter sliders or setup knobs for anomaly frequency, secrecy difficulty, faction hostility, urban density, resource scarcity, public trust, supernatural intensity, or comparable top-level tuning inputs
support generated pre-campaign history such as old incidents, faction rivalries, abandoned sites, missing records, unresolved deaths, relic provenance, and public rumor strata that shape the starting world
support persistence and memory so generated events leave archive entries, staff trauma, faction stance shifts, material scars, rumor drift, and future hooks rather than vanishing after resolution
support rule-driven emergence where domains, factions, materials, sites, staff, and timing systems interact to produce believable cases instead of one isolated random-case generator
distinguish this seeded content framework from any one generator slice such as naming, one-shot assembly, exploration decks, or incident packets alone
connect the layer to world-turn systems, faction activity, campaign setup, archives, incident generation, recruitment variance, and authored content templates where relevant
Constraints:
deterministic only
no assumption that randomness alone creates meaningful variety
no full everything-generator with no authorial guardrails
countdown progression remains with dedicated countdown owners rather than expanding here
prefer compact rule tables, seeds, generation passes, and persistence hooks over sprawling uncontrolled combinatorics
keep seed inputs, generation decisions, authored-rule influence, and persistence outcomes legible enough for debugging and balancing
Acceptance criteria:
at least one campaign can be recreated from the same seed with matching high-level world structure and content setup
at least one generated campaign differs meaningfully from another in more than superficial names or counts alone
at least one authored rule table or constraint packet materially changes generated outputs in a deterministic way
at least one procedural incident seed composes actor, location, trigger, motive, and condition into an operational prompt
at least one generated prehistory element affects starting world conditions or later case interpretation
at least one generated event leaves persistent records or scars that influence future play
at least one setup parameter changes campaign pressure or composition in a predictable, player-facing way
targeted tests or validation examples cover deterministic seed replay, layered generation, authored-rule influence, uniqueness safeguards, and persistence of generated history
Source reconciliation — environment-compatible generated assignments
Generated operational assignments must consume authored location/environment constraints before publication so terrain-, route-, or region-specific templates are not surfaced in incompatible operational geography.
Location/environment compatibility is an eligibility constraint inside the existing seeded-generation contract, not a second generic predicate framework.
Rejection must be deterministic and inspectable from the same seed plus authored region/environment state.
Additional acceptance coverage:
at least one terrain- or environment-tagged assignment is generated only in a compatible region/location class;
the same authored template is rejected in an incompatible region without being renamed or silently retargeted;
targeted validation proves deterministic compatibility filtering without introducing a parallel mission-generation system.
Stochastic identity migration — 2026-10-02
Seeded procedural-generation decisions must derive from the canonical shared stochastic-identity/key-derivation contract when they are semantic stochastic decisions, rather than depend on unrelated global stream draw count. Existing generated objects, IDs, historical records, and resolved generation outcomes remain authoritative and are never regenerated during migration. New spawn/generation decisions after cutover use stable semantic identity plus selector version. Existing keyed generators should adopt the shared kernel where doing so preserves their authored semantics; migration must not collapse deterministic no-RNG generation into random selection.
Shared-universe release eligibility input
Seeded procedural generation must respect the campaign's bound shared-universe release whenever authored inputs derive from promoted shared-universe canon.
Release-scoped eligibility is applied before candidate selection for shared-universe-derived factions, anomalies, historical premises, organizations, locations, artifacts, rules, or related authored inputs.
The same campaign seed plus the same bound release and authoritative configuration must resolve the same eligible shared-universe input set.
A later external canon release must not retroactively regenerate an existing campaign.
Existing generated objects, incidents, archives, and consequences remain authoritative once created unless an explicit adoption/migration path says otherwise.
This issue consumes the release-scoped resolver and must not implement a second canon catalog.
Source reconciliation — Randy Random incident selection and pacing
Source: https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Randy_Random
Approved bounded incident-generation policy:
evaluate authoritative domain eligibility before any weighted incident-family selection;
allow authored relative weights only among currently eligible incident families, with stochastic choice routed through Canonical stochastic identity and deterministic replay contract #4041's canonical scoped stochastic identity and selector-version contract;
keep long-horizon incident frequency/intensity distinct from short-horizon clustering or volatility so similar average pressure can still produce different temporal distributions;
permit bounded maximum-silence / anti-starvation priority only over eligible incident families; the mechanism must not fabricate an incident that violates start conditions, world law, compatibility, or domain ownership;
do not require one universal post-incident cooldown: independent producers may create clustered incidents where their authoritative rules permit it, while chronology/overlap remains owned by Incident chronology and overlap engine #827;
keep route-, district-, site-, civic-, faction-, and other domain-specific weighting inputs with their existing subsystem owners rather than centralizing all weights here;
keep eligibility-check cadence distinct from actual event-generation cadence where the owning time/start-condition systems require that distinction;
preserve deterministic, inspectable selection and causal authored constraints; do not introduce a universal storyteller/director subsystem.
Additional acceptance coverage:
a deterministic fixture can select among at least two eligible incident families using authored relative weights and reproduce the same result from the same scoped stochastic identity and selector version;
a fixture demonstrates that an ineligible incident cannot be selected even when it has a higher configured weight;
a pacing fixture demonstrates that long-run incident intensity can remain comparable while short-run clustering/quiet intervals differ under authored configuration;
a bounded anti-starvation example, if configured, elevates only an eligible incident family and never bypasses start-condition or compatibility rules.
Completion rule:
Keep this parent open until seeded/procedural campaign generation, authored-rule influence, incident generation/selection, persistence of generated history, configuration influence, and downstream generation consumers collectively satisfy the full parent boundary. Completion of one selector, opposition-composition, or pacing slice is not sufficient to close the parent.
Phase 4 source reconciliation — RimWorld World Generation (C891, C893, C922)
Approved fold-in from the RimWorld World Generation pattern extraction. This extends the existing seeded-generation boundary only; it does not create parallel contamination, faction, access, or route systems.
Implementation requirements:
distinguish generated contamination coverage/prevalence from runtime contamination source, propagation, exposure, and severity state; generation may choose where contaminated conditions initially exist, while runtime contamination behavior remains with its canonical hazard/contamination owners;
support bounded generated organization-composition constraints, including eligible organization/faction presence or count where campaign setup explicitly exposes those controls, while canonical organization identity and institutional behavior remain with existing faction/institution owners;
generated mandatory campaign content must have at least one deterministic legal capability/access path under the resolved campaign configuration; intentionally blocked, dangerous, inaccessible, or capability-gated optional locations may remain valid when they do not softlock required progression;
consume canonical access, route, obstacle, organization, and contamination outputs rather than duplicating their state inside procedural generation.
Additional acceptance coverage:
at least one generated campaign distinguishes contamination placement/coverage from later runtime contamination severity or spread;
at least one organization-composition constraint changes eligible generated content without creating a second faction record model;
at least one required generated objective is rejected or rerouted when no legal capability/access path exists, while an optional inaccessible location may remain generated;
targeted validation proves these checks are deterministic and preserve canonical subsystem ownership.
Traceability: RimWorld Wiki — World generation; candidates C891, C893, C922.
Phase 4 source reconciliation — RimWorld Biomes residual pass (C30, C35)
Approved fold-in from the residual RimWorld Biomes extraction.
C30 — environment-gated procedural generation
Environment-conditioned generation may use deterministic include, exclude, require, and conditional-exception rules when deciding whether a generated feature, site element, assignment, or other authored candidate is eligible in the resolved environment.
environment-feature definitions remain authoritative under Environment feature table registry #1629 and related content registries;
generator content-pack activation/gating remains authoritative under Generator content-pack registry #1533;
Contextual generator substitution resolver #1529 applies only when an incompatible generated result should be deterministically substituted rather than rejected;
this issue owns the procedural eligibility decision, not canonical environment state or feature identity;
landmark/site/exception conditions may re-enable or replace otherwise excluded generated content only through explicit authored rules with inspectable provenance;
equivalent seed + authoritative environment/context + authored rules must produce the same eligibility result.
Additional acceptance coverage:
at least one environment rule excludes an otherwise valid generated candidate;
at least one explicit conditional exception restores or replaces a normally excluded candidate without bypassing provenance or deterministic replay;
targeted validation distinguishes rejection from substitution and preserves canonical ownership of environment/content state.
C35 — context-filtered weighted-event normalization
No new scope is added. Existing eligibility-before-weighting rules already require weighted selection to operate only over currently eligible candidates, so filtered-out entries do not become failed rolls or reduce event incidence implicitly.
Traceability: RimWorld Biomes residual candidates C30 and C35.
Ambient case-generation stochastic isolation and eligibility — C431–C435/C438/C442/C443 (2026-11-06)
Ambient case generation keeps its domain semantics here while consuming #4041 for intentionally stochastic decisions.
Treat faction_offer, faction_pressure, and world_activity generation as distinct semantic decision domains so adding/removing a draw in one path does not silently re-key another path.
For world_activity, district choice and time-band choice are separate generation subdecisions. They consume authoritative district/time schedule state from SPE-109 without moving schedule semantics into this generator.
Speculative candidate/spawn-plan evaluation must not consume stochastic state that changes a different plan which later becomes authoritative. Discarded plans cannot perturb the committed plan's semantic stochastic decisions.
Candidate pools used by weighted selectors require canonical authored or stable semantic ordering; object insertion/iteration order is not authoritative identity. Selector-policy/version provenance is explicit wherever stochastic weighting is used.
Eligibility is resolved before weighting/selection. Exhausting a preferred pool does not make an otherwise ineligible template eligible. The generator must select an explicitly authored valid fallback or return an explicit no-selection/unsupported result.
Equal-priority spawn-plan arbitration uses a declared deterministic authored order or stable semantic tie-break. Incidental insertion/object order is not simulation law. A stochastic tie-break is permitted only when the owning generation policy explicitly declares that decision stochastic, in which case the Canonical stochastic identity and deterministic replay contract #4041 identity/version contract applies.
These rules add no parallel district scheduler, pressure selector, or contradiction subsystem and require no new child under this parent.
