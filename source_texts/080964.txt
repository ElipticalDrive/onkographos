Abstract:
Voxelization is a standard representation technique for arbitrary geometries on Cartesian space. It is typically used as a pre-processor for any solver in computational fluid dynamics (CFD) problems. Voxelization typically is a computationally intensive and can consume significant amount of time on complex geometries on grids of high resolutions. In this paper, we propose a novel GPU acceleration method for both surface and solid voxelization aimed at CFD solvers dependent on the popular Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM). Towards that, we design new mechanisms for data parallelism that can allow efficient SIMT execution on modern GPUs. In contrast to prior works, which typically extract parallelism in the space of the surface geometry, our method works at a finer grain where we extract parallelism in the space of the Cartesian fluid mesh. This leads to higher degree of parallelism which provides significant performance benefits. Our method is able to compute surface voxelization for a benchmark geometry (Stanford Bunny) in 16.8 ms at a grid size of 10243• Our method for solid voxelization on the same geometry takes 1546.3 ms. Our implementation is able to extract around 7.14 TFLOPS single precision (around 50% of peak) performance on a NVIDIA V100 GPU.

I. Introduction
Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations are widely popular for the design and synthesis in a myriad of fields such as aerospace engineering, machine design, heat and mass transfer, electronics and more. Inherently, CFD techniques rely on the discretization of governing equations that describe the physical properties of different entities within a fluid domain. On the basis of discretization technique, the method can be broadly classified as finite difference (FD) and finite volume (FV) [1]–[3]. In former, the fluid property is solved at a single point/node (for uniformity, we mention nodes as “point” in the rest of the paper), unlike in the latter where property is determined over a control volume (bounded by points) assuming to be concentrated in its geometric centre [1]. Lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) is a finite difference based approach that utilizes a Cartesian grid where each of the points typically represent a packet or group of fluid particles residing in it. The points can also represent a physical mass inside the fluid, a slip-wall, and also the velocity boundaries. Significant progress have been made in the development of new numerical frameworks and methodologies that successfully addresses problems associated with phase change, moving boundaries, fluid-structure interaction, and turbulence using LBM [4], [5]. LBM possesses remarkable advantage over the conventional CFD methods as far the scope of parallelization, scalability and computational performance is concerned [6], [7]. In this work, we shall be particularly focusing on moving geometries where a given solid body is moving through another medium where LBM has shown significant applicability [8]-[10].
