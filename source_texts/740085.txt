The morphology Overview viewer now builds a closed surface mesh from the SWC itself, in a pool of Web Workers, and draws it with three.js. The traced skeleton shows at once, each fibre as wide as the mesh will draw it, and the mesh takes over once it is built: about half a second for a cortical cell. It is a port of the local-morph-meshing proof of concept, with the circuit viewer's chrome around it.
Replaces the server-generated GLB
This replaces #1955 and openbraininstitute/morphoviewer#54, which showed the cell_surface_mesh GLB from the server-side mesh generator. With the mesh built in the browser:
Every morphology with an SWC gets a mesh, not only those with a generated GLB asset, and there is no second download.
The surface is closed and consistently oriented. The hollow look caused by the generator's inverted winding (the known issue in Switch a morphology between its mesh and skeleton in one viewer #1955) is gone.
Neurite types can be left out: hiding one rebuilds the mesh without it.
Mesh and skeleton share one scene, so there is no switching between two views.
#1955 and morphoviewer#54 stay open until this lands, and can be closed then.
Changes
Meshing (morpho-viewer/engine/): tubes are swept where a neurite runs alone, and voxel patches are meshed around branch points, the soma and contacts, joined by collars. The voxel work runs in WebGPU where the browser has it and on the CPU otherwise, and the build falls back to voxels throughout if the tubes fail. Workers start when first needed: a build uses at least 4, and one more per 2 mm of the cable it meshes, up to 12. They talk over Comlink, and a new build or load cancels the running one. The build parameters are fixed in constants.ts.
Viewer: 14 looks (Studio by default), ambient occlusion, bumps, type tint, wireframe, the traced or processed skeleton, spin and a min. width. It opens in an orthographic view with the scale bar, with a perspective view to switch to.
Skeletons: the traced skeleton is the file as written, to check the mesh against: a neurite joins the soma at the soma point the file links it to, which on a soma of several points can lie at its edge. The processed skeleton is what the mesh is built from, the necks from the soma's centre included.
Bumps: an organic roughness of the surface, drawn by the shader and on by default. The looks on three's own materials shade it per pixel, so it shows on the soma's wide triangles and along the tubes, and in a far view it fades out instead of shimmering. The three custom-shader looks and the ambient-occlusion pass keep it per vertex.
Colours: by section, or by path distance to the soma, which the workers measure. Neurite colours are kept per background, and an eye per type rebuilds the mesh without it.
Chrome, as the circuit viewer's:
fullscreen, settings (the look is its first row), and a Debug menu behind the morphology-debug flag, which is listed in local, preview and staging. It holds the statistics, the build controls (the POC's build parameters, a GPU switch and the bump sliders; a change rebuilds the mesh) and export;
the Colour by pill and the neurite key;
an axes gizmo in the bottom-right corner, showing the cell's X, Y and Z as the camera sees them. A click on a tip turns the view to look from that axis, or from the opposite one when it already does;
a "?" help card on every control.
The viewer is as tall as the circuit viewer.
Export: GLB, Draco GLB and STL, written in a worker that loads on first use.
SWC parser: lines ended by a bare carriage return are read, and a repeated sample id or a loop of parents is rejected, saying where; both used to drop or misplace samples silently.
Detail page: morphologies no longer have a Mesh viewer tab, which showed the same viewer as the Overview. Old /mesh-viewer links to a morphology redirect to the Overview. EM cell meshes keep theirs.
Shared with the circuit viewer: its settings popover shell moves to color-by/chrome-menu.tsx, and the panel styles to contrast.ts. The circuit viewer's DOM stays the same.
Dependencies:
three and @types/three go from ^0.159 to ^0.186;
new: @gltf-transform/core and /extensions, draco3dgltf and meshoptimizer;
next.config.ts aliases fs to an empty module in the browser, for Draco's glue.
The only other three.js code, services/bluenaas-single-cell, has no mounted renderer: the single-neuron workflows draw the neuron with morphoviewer. Only createBubblesInstanced still builds three.js objects that nothing renders, and they build under 0.186.
Deleted: src/components/MorphoViewer/, src/state/morpho-viewer/ and the orphaned CSS modules under morpho-viewer/.
Docs
morpho-viewer/README.md is the overview, with diagrams of what runs where and of a build. morpho-viewer/engine/README.md is the in-depth reference for the meshing.
Testing
Vitest: new suites under src/__tests__/cell-morphology/:
the engine: closed-surface checks on a sample cell, the worker pool, path distances and the exports;
the React side, with the engine faked.
The full suite passes (2,973 tests).
Lint and build: Biome is clean on the changed files. next build passed before the axes gizmo commits; the files they change type-check.
In the browser (Chromium):
cells: ch150801A1, C261296A-P1 (a soma of stacked points), an Allen Pvalb interneuron, a MICrONS EM cell, MouseLight AA0187 and a synthesized morphology;
all 14 looks, with and without the bumps, perspective, the dark background, distance colours, hiding types and the three exports;
the axes gizmo: the tips' turns and flips, a drag during a turn, keyboard focus on a light look, and the neurite key in the shortest viewer, where it now scrolls above the gizmo;
after the review fixes: ch150801A1 on the GPU and the CPU, with tubes and with voxels throughout (all closed), the workers per build (7, 6 with the axon hidden, 12 for the projection neuron 191812_00037), Cajal with AO, the Toon outline in the wireframe, the /mesh-viewer redirect, and the single-neuron simulate and synaptome build pages.
Not checked yet: fullscreen and double-click fullscreen, Firefox and Safari, a file without a soma (unit tests only), and opening the exports in Blender.

Draw the processed skeleton as the mesh builds it, necks included
…
ab72b3c
```
In an SWC file, each arbor's first point names one soma point as its
parent. On a soma of several points (an outline, or MorphIO's stack of
cross-sections) that is usually the first one, at the edge, so the
skeleton's lines met outside the mesh's soma, which is built around the
average of the soma points.

- The Processed skeleton now comes from `meshedSections` (mesher.ts): an
  arbor with a neck (`somaNecks`) starts at the soma's centre and goes on
  from the neck's target, as the mesh builds it. Arbors without a neck
  keep their traced start, as in the mesh. Every type is included, since
  the viewer hides types in the skeleton itself.
- The Traced skeleton, and the stand-in drawn from it while the mesh
  builds, stay as the file is written, to check the mesh against.
- The Skeleton help card and the README say what each one shows.
- Tests: valid, too-near and far arbors through both planners, a plan
  that meshes only the soma, a cut arbor, and a point untangling added
  ahead of a first sample.

Shade the bumps per pixel in every look on three's own materials
…
13545b1
```
Only the EM segmentation look shaded the bumps per pixel. The others
tilted each vertex's normal and let the triangles interpolate, which
drew the soma's wide triangles as crumpled facets and left the long
strips of a swept tube smooth: Studio with bumps looked nothing like EM.

- `withDisplacement` now shades the bumps per fragment in any shader
  with three's `normal_fragment_begin`: every look but Fluorescence,
  Cajal and Depth-coded, whose custom shaders keep the tilt per vertex.
  The vertex normal stays untilted there, so three's specular
  anti-aliasing reads the smooth normal as without bumps. Glossy's
  clearcoat takes the bumped normal too.
- An octave fades out as its noise cells shrink from four pixels across
  to two, so a far view does not shimmer; the fragment code runs only
  while the bumps are on.
- The declarations go in front of the shader, which the gold leaf's
  flakes now share instead of their own noise and position varying.
- The ambient-occlusion pass keeps its normals tilted per vertex
  (`perFragment: false`): it rebuilds the surface from depth, which has
  only the per-vertex displacement.
- The toon outline is pushed out just before it is projected, after the
  displacement whichever hook runs first.
- Tests: which looks shade per fragment, the clearcoat, the occlusion
  pass's normals, the gold leaf's flakes after the hash, and no shader
  declaring a function, uniform or varying twice.

The pool started every worker, up to 12, as the viewer mounted, each
