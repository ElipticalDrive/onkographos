Right now the worldgen just calculates a terrain height and a dirt depth value per XY coordinate using multiple octaves of perlin noise.
Do we want to use simplex instead of perlin noise?
Gradient noise alone is too boring. Better terrain features can be achieved by mapping the output from the gradient noise through a spline (crate we could use for this)
Overhangs and such can be achieved by sampling 3D noise as a "density". Density biased such that it's generally higher the further down you are. But I don't know how this is generally done, i.e. what function is used for this.
We need to split terrain generation into several steps. We watched a YouTube video where they described their system, in which each step in chunk generation can use the previous step of neighbor chunks to resolve dependencies, while still making the whole algorithm parallelizable.
Then of course we want to populate the world with more than just stone, dirt, grass and air:
Water, sand (beaches)
Vegetation: Flowers and trees
Caves (voronoi noise)
Ores (what noise to use?)
Biomes: Modulate the whole worldgen by a voronoi noise.
