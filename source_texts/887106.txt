Claim
I'm claiming this. It is item 11 of #96.
Problem
Scene shading (render-scene/src/shader/lighting.wgsl, frame.rs) has three limits:
The ambient term is fixed to a hemisphere approximation.
The roughness remapping, visibility term and ambient-occlusion convention are fixed.
There is no way to select an SH9 diffuse environment.
render-lighting already has spherical-harmonic and reflectance kernels, and render-scene already has prefiltered environment maps and a BRDF LUT preparation step. Even so, a consumer can't express the common combination: SH9 irradiance for diffuse, a prefiltered radiance map with a split-sum BRDF LUT for specular, and a declared visibility and roughness convention. The consumer also needs this combination applied the same way to terrain, meshes, cards and water.
Scope
Diffuse environment. Add a scene lighting descriptor that selects the diffuse environment: the existing hemisphere, or SH9 irradiance with typed, validated coefficients. Document the basis, normalization and orientation in the engine's Z-up frame.
Specular conventions. Add a specular convention selection covering the roughness remap, the visibility term (for example height-correlated Smith versus Schlick-GGX), and how material AO and specular occlusion combine. The existing behaviour stays the default with unchanged bytes.
Shared expression. All scene shading paths use one lighting expression: terrain, meshes, cards and water. SSR replacement composition also uses it. No path gets a separate copy.
Reuse. Use the existing environment prefilter, BRDF LUT and SH kernels. Do not duplicate them.
Tests
SH9: projection and evaluation vectors in render-lighting, checked against an analytic environment (constant, linear gradient, single lobe). Orientation tests confirm +Z is up.
Specular: visibility and roughness-remap reference values at sample points. Energy checks for the specular term with the LUT.
Pass consistency: terrain, meshes, cards and water produce the same lighting for identical material and normal inputs.
Defaults: byte-identical to current output.
GPU asserts: at least 0.1 away from sRGB rounding boundaries.
Refs #96.
