Abstract
With the rise of the metaverse and augmented reality industry, there is a growing demand for three-dimensional (3D) models, especially 3D cartoon avatars. However, conventional methods of creating 3D avatars must be done by professional computer graphics designers and require significant effort. The artificial intelligence (AI)-based 3D Gaussian splatting method has been introduced and applied in 3D avatar reconstruction from videos but is limited to real-world videos. In this paper, we introduce a novel AI-driven method named Cartoon Gaussian Splatting (ToonSplat) that reconstructs a 3D avatar from animated cartoon videos. Reconstructing 3D avatars from animated cartoons is extremely difficult and remains challenging due to the inconsistency of the geometry and texture of characters in each video frame. Moreover, the characters' body shapes and facial expressions in the animated cartoons are exaggerated and often do not follow real-world physics. To handle these problems, we leverage surface normals in a 3D Gaussian representation to accurately capture the geometry and propose learnable skinning weights. Additionally, we propose frequency transform loss to enhance the sharpness of the 3D avatar and adopt style normalization layers to reduce the inconsistency of texture colors and patterns in each frame of animated cartoons. We demonstrate the effectiveness of ToonSplat with multiple datasets and significantly outperforms other state-of-the-art methods.
Keywords: Three-dimensional Avatar Reconstruction, Gaussian Splatting, Pixel-wise Segmentation, Aritificial Intelligence, Metaverse
