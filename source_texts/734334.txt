Idea
Extend the DrewsAnalyticRendering air hierarchy from whole-region all-air flags to conservative analytic bounds on terrain occupancy. For each large region, store one or a few inexpensive quadratic upper envelopes. Along a camera ray, solve the associated quadratic inequalities to find intervals where a voxel hit is possible, and jump over the rest. This is acceleration only: keep the same 6.25 cm voxel geometry, no LOD or changes to the source terrain.
Small research prototype
Build a certified parent bound over a group of existing 8 m quadratic patches; compare a single bound on the composite field with bounds for each of the five existing branches (only if branch correspondence makes those bounds meaningful).
Derive valid interval exclusions along r(t)=o+td; clip them to parent boxes, select the nearest candidate interval, and descend only where a hit remains possible.
Include a voxel-center margin and conservative numerical padding: continuous ray-point exclusion alone is not sufficient for exact stepped voxel hits.
Keep current grid DDA, all-air hierarchy and CPU reference unchanged as baselines.
What to learn
Do these tighter analytic bounds skip mixed (nonempty) regions that the current all-air hierarchy cannot? Measure bound tightness, candidate patch visits, quadratic tests, hierarchy memory/build cost and warmed GPU timing for close, long-range and grazing rays. Check occlusion, tangent rays, inside-solid cases and patch seams.
This is an experiment, not a guaranteed speedup or traversal-free first-hit algorithm. If the bound is too loose or expensive, document the failure.
Related: #16 (existing conservative patch hierarchy), #19 (longer-term direct first-hit research), #13 (profiling). Code: src/analytic/traversal.rs, src/analytic/raycast.wgsl.
