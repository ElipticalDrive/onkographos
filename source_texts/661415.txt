Part of #68, part 3. Changes rendering on purpose: it removes double-counted light, and the regression baseline is regenerated for CornellBoxMesh.
Changes
integrator/PT.cpp: next-event estimation (DirectIllumination) is skipped at a vertex whose BSDF is delta (perfectly specular): else { ... } → else if (!bsdf->IsDelta()) { ... }.
Why it matters: double counting
At a specular vertex, the old code still called DirectIllumination:
Light sampling contributes 0, because a specular BSDF's Evaluate is 0.
BSDF sampling (the second half of DirectIllumination) samples the mirror or refraction direction with a finite pdf. If that direction hits the light, it adds the emission with MIS weight PowerHeuristic(1, pdf, 1, lightPdf), which is less than 1 but not 0.
The path then continues in that same specular direction, and because deltaBoundEvent is set, it adds the light's emission again at full weight.
So light reached through a specular bounce was counted more than once. The biggest effect is light through the glass bunny onto the floor.

The mean drop (0.05–0.06) is about five times the noise in the mean (0.01). The heatmap shows a slight, even darkening, strongest in the caustic under the bunny. Scenes without specular materials (CornellBoxMeshWithSlope, EnvironmentMapScene) are byte-identical.
Also slightly faster: one fewer shadow ray and BSDF sample per specular vertex (mesh/BVH at 1024 spp: 19.8 s → 17.8 s).
Note: BSDF::IsDelta() is true if any lobe is specular. No current material mixes specular and non-specular lobes. If one is added, both this check and the existing deltaBoundEvent logic need a per-lobe decision.

Next-event estimation can't contribute at a perfectly specular surface:
the BSDF is zero for any sampled light direction. Light reached through
a specular bounce is already counted when the BSDF-sampled path hits the
emitter (deltaBoundEvent). Skipping it saves a shadow ray per specular
vertex and stops consuming random numbers there.
