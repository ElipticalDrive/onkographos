Understanding complex 3D magnetic vector fields is a notorious challenge in science education, primarily due to the heavy intrinsic cognitive load required for spatial mental modeling. Traditional 2D representations and low-density 3D Web simulators often fail to convey the dynamic topologies of superimposed fields. In this paper, we present a novel, high-performance WebGL-based simulation framework utilizing GPU hardware instancing to render a dense array of over 4,000 dual-encoded physical glyphs in real-time. We propose a "pose-volume" dual-mapping mechanism where glyph orientation represents field direction and non-linear volume scaling reflects field magnitude based on Stevens’ Power Law. To evaluate the system’s pedagogical efficacy, an empirical study (N = 95) was conducted. The results revealed that non-STEM participants achieved a 62.2% accuracy rate in complex tensor topology diagnostics. A binary logistic regression analysis indicated that post-task ratings of intuitive visual mapping (p = 0.007) and interaction fluidity (p = 0.039) were positively associated with task success. Notably, the observed correlation between formal STEM background and performance was attenuated in the presence of these perceptual and interaction variables (p = 0.249). Furthermore, Pearson correlations established a significant negative correlation between real-time 6-DOF interaction fluidity and task frustration (r = −0.31, p < 0.01), a pattern consistent with sensorimotor coupling and cognitive offloading. This study suggests that modern Web-based hardware-accelerated environments may serve as effective cognitive scaffolds for complex physics education.
