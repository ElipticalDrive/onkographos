Abstract
Neural implicit surfaces and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) bring complementary strengths to 3D reconstruction, with implicit
surfaces yielding coherent geometry and consistent topology while 3DGS captures high-frequency appearance details. Recent
hybrid methods couple the two representations primarily through depth and normal consistency losses, which is insufficient
to resolve the geometric ambiguities inherent to volume rendering. We propose GSNSR, a neural implicit surface framework
that leverages 3DGS in two complementary ways to guide SDF optimization, refining global object contours and recovering
fine geometric and appearance details. For contour refinement, we introduce a confidence-aware free-space loss that weights
pseudo-depth by per-pixel reliability to suppress spurious density in unoccupied regions. For detail recovery, we forgo 3DGS
depth as a feature anchor and instead use the ray-surface intersection predicted by the SDF network itself. At this anchor, a
decoupled aggregator separately consolidates geometric and appearance attributes from neighboring Gaussians via distancebased weighting, providing the SDF and color networks with non-interfering guidance. Extensive experiments on the DTU
and NeRF Synthetic datasets demonstrate that GSNSR achieves highly competitive reconstruction accuracy while significantly
suppressing artifacts in challenging scenes.

1. Introduction
Recovering high-fidelity 3D structures from 2D images is a fundamental challenge in computer vision, with broad applications in
AR/VR, robotics, and cultural heritage preservation. Among existing approaches, multi-view 3D surface reconstruction has attracted
significant attention for its accessibility, requiring only calibrated
images rather than specialized scanning hardware. Recent advances
in neural implicit representations, such as signed distance functions (SDFs) optimized via volume rendering, have demonstrated
remarkable ability to reconstruct smooth, topologically consistent
surfaces. However, these methods still struggle with geometric ambiguities in complex or sparsely observed regions, motivating the
need for stronger geometric priors.
A pioneering work, NeRF [MST∗ 20], harnesses differentiable
volume rendering to achieve novel view synthesis. To enable more
precise geometric recovery, NeuS [WLL∗ 21] integrates the Signed
Distance Function (SDF) into this framework, achieving highfidelity surface reconstruction by ensuring geometric consistency
during the rendering process. Nevertheless, NeuS suffers from slow
convergence due to inefficient position encoding, often extending
training times to several hours. Recently, 3DGS [KKLD23] has
emerged as a compelling alternative that represents scenes through
a collection of anisotropic Gaussians, enabling significantly faster
rendering via GPU-accelerated rasterization. However, 3DGS is inherently designed for novel view synthesis rather than surface reconstruction, and its discrete nature makes it ill-suited for precise
surface reconstruction.
Implicit SDFs excel at surface topology while 3DGS captures
high-frequency appearance details. This complementary relationship motivates a hybrid paradigm that couples 3DGS with implicit
SDF representations. Representative works [LSH∗ 24, YLX∗ 24]
demonstrate that jointly optimizing these two representations can
accelerate convergence and improve reconstruction fidelity compared to SDF-only baselines. These methods commonly adopt rendered depth and normal maps from 3DGS as geometric constraints,
supervising the SDF network to align with the Gaussian-derived
geometry. However, such coupling operates primarily at the lossfunction level, leaving the fundamental ambiguity of volume rendering unresolved. As a result, these methods remain vulnerable to
the quality of 3DGS geometry. If the Gaussians fail to converge to
accurate surfaces, the erroneous depth and normal cues propagate
into SDF optimization, potentially misleading rather than benefiting reconstruction.
Building upon this motivation, we propose GSNSR, a hybrid
neural surface reconstruction framework that leverages 3DGS as
a geometric and photometric prior to drive SDF optimization. Our
central observation is that volume rendering provides no explicit
supervision over unoccupied space, so the network readily converges to spurious density and floaters, especially in sparsely observed regions. We introduce a confidence-aware free-space loss
that turns the 3DGS-rendered pseudo depth into an explicit freespace prior, enforcing that rays accumulate no opacity before reaching the surface localized by the pseudo depth. This directly attacks the volume-rendering ambiguity that loss-level depth and normal consistency leave unresolved. Crucially, because 3DGS depth
is not unconditionally reliable, we design a per-pixel confidence
score that combines local depth support, local stability, and viewdependent geometric reliability, and convert it into per-pixel loss
weights. This imposes stricter free-space constraints where the
pseudo depth is trustworthy while relaxing them in uncertain regions, so the constraint suppresses artifacts while largely preventing unreliable depth from injecting erroneous geometry.
To recover fine geometric and appearance detail, we further fuse
3DGS features into the SDF network at the architectural level. The
key difficulty here is where and how the discrete Gaussian features are injected into the continuous field. Prior fusion methods
anchor the injection at the 3DGS-rendered depth, so any error in
that depth directly displaces the anchor and corrupts the local geometry. Instead, we localize the anchor from the SDF field itself
via the ray-surface intersection implied by a sign change along the
ray; this severs the dependence on unreliable Gaussian depth and
lets the anchor sharpen together with the geometry network during
training. At this anchor, rather than hard-replacing the nearest sample’s feature, we adopt a distance-based fusion strategy that aggregates neighboring Gaussians and redistributes the result to the two
samples bracketing the intersection by their distance to it, supplying spatially grounded guidance. To deliver geometry and appearance cues without interference, the aggregated features are routed
through separate geometry and color pathways.
In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
• We introduce a confidence-aware free-space loss that converts 3DGS-rendered pseudo depth into an explicit, perpixel reliability-weighted free-space prior, directly suppressing
floaters and spurious density.
• We propose an SDF-guided fusion scheme that derives the
feature-injection anchor from the ray-surface intersection, and
redistributes the aggregated Gaussian features to the samples by
distance, yielding faithful guidance while preserving representational continuity. Geometry and appearance attributes are routed
through separate pathways to avoid cross-interference.
• Evaluations on the DTU and NeRF Synthetic datasets demonstrate that GSNSR achieves competitive surface reconstruction accuracy while effectively reducing artifacts in challenging
scenes.
2. Related Work
2.1. 3D neural surface reconstruction
NeRF [MST∗ 20] pioneered differentiable volume rendering for
neural implicit representations, enabling high-quality novel view
synthesis from multi-view images. Subsequent works have extended this paradigm in complementary directions. Mip-NeRF
[BMT∗ 21] mitigates multi-scale aliasing artifacts through integrated positional encoding over conical frustums, while InstantNGP [MESK22] introduces multi-resolution hash encoding to dramatically accelerate training convergence. Despite these advances
in rendering fidelity and efficiency, NeRF-based methods model
scene geometry through a volumetric density field that lacks explicit surface correspondence; meshes extracted via density thresholding are therefore typically noisy and topologically ill-defined,
motivating subsequent research into surface-aware neural rendering.

Existing surface reconstruction approaches can be mainly categorized into volume rendering methods based on Neural Implicit Fields and explicit representation methods based on 3DGS
[WYZ∗ 24, CW24]. Combining volume rendering with Signed Distance Fields (SDF) has proven effective for acquiring high-fidelity
geometric models in implicit reconstruction. NeuS [WLL∗ 21] formulates surface rendering via an unbiased SDF-based volume density, while VolSDF [YGKL21] models volume density as a transformed SDF, establishing a principled connection between the two
representations and enabling accurate surface localization through
tighter control over the density-to-geometry mapping. However,
NeuS often faces slow convergence challenges stemming from positional encoding. Several follow-up works have sought to address
this limitation from different angles. NeuS2 [WHH∗ 23] replaces
positional encoding with multi-resolution hash grids, achieving
significantly faster convergence while maintaining reconstruction
quality comparable to the original NeuS. Instant-NSR [ZJY∗ 22] integrates hash grids with SDF, specifically tailoring the framework
for human performance reconstruction. Although utilizing occupancy grids facilitates efficient sampling and pruning, optimization
is prone to local minima, particularly in regions with occlusion or
uneven lighting. Beyond convergence speed, reconstruction accuracy also demands further improvement. Neuralangelo [LME∗ 23]
leverages multi-resolution hash encoding alongside numerical gradients for higher-order smoothness regularization, enabling the recovery of large-scale scenes with fine-grained surface details.
3DGS [KKLD23] has gained attention for its impressive novelview synthesis quality and real-time rendering speed. As a discrete
explicit representation, it encodes scene geometry through a set of
anisotropic Gaussians, enabling GPU-accelerated rasterization that
is orders of magnitude faster than NeRF-based methods. This efficiency makes 3DGS an attractive foundation for surface reconstruction, as it can produce a reasonable geometric scaffold within
minutes rather than hours. To extract explicit surfaces from Gaussian primitives, several works have proposed geometry regularization strategies. SuGaR [GL24] introduces a regularization term that
encourages Gaussians to align with object surfaces, enabling mesh
extraction via Poisson reconstruction. 2DGS [HYC∗ 24] replaces
volumetric 3D Gaussians with flat 2D disks, which are inherently
better suited for surface fitting and allow more accurate normal estimation. GOF [YSG24] models scene geometry as the level set
of a Gaussian opacity field, enabling direct isosurface extraction
without post-processing. Despite these advances, 3DGS-based surface reconstruction faces inherent limitations. The discrete nature
of Gaussian primitives makes it difficult to enforce global topological consistency, and directly extracted geometries are often prone to
noise, holes, and fragmented surfaces in textureless or sparsely observed regions. Furthermore, since 3DGS optimization is primarily
driven by photometric loss, geometric accuracy is not guaranteed.
Gaussians may adopt arbitrary shapes and orientations that reproduce appearance faithfully while deviating significantly from the
true surface. These limitations motivate the integration of 3DGS
with implicit representations that offer stronger geometric inductive biases.

2.2. Hybrid representation neural surface reconstruction
Recent hybrid approaches typically employ joint optimization that
combines the complementary strengths of 3D Gaussian Splatting
(3DGS) and Signed Distance Function (SDF)-based representations. Specifically, 3DGS provides efficient, view-dependent appearance modeling through explicit, rasterization-friendly Gaussian primitives, enabling real-time rendering with high visual fidelity. Meanwhile, the SDF-based neural representation serves as a
geometrically rigorous implicit surface prior, enforcing smooth and
topologically consistent surface reconstruction [ZLH24, CLWL25,
XLC∗ 25]. GSDF [YLX∗ 24] designs a dual-branch system with
synchronized optimization, where depth maps rendered by the
GS branch narrow and guide the ray-sampling range of the SDF
branch, while SDF-predicted geometry provides spatial guidance
to regulate the density of 3D Gaussian primitives. This joint supervision enables mutual reinforcement between the two tasks. However, this dual-branch coupling also introduces a fundamental vulnerability. Since 3DGS is inherently an appearance-first rendering framework, its optimizer tends to generate floaters at viewdependent boundaries and high-frequency texture regions to minimize photometric loss. These floaters bear no correspondence to the
true object surface yet effectively compensate for rendering residuals. Consequently, the depth maps produced via alpha-blending
represent a weighted expectation over all Gaussians along each
ray, rather than a faithful measurement of surface depth. When
such geometrically unreliable depth estimates are fed back as supervisory signals into the SDF branch, the resulting gradients can
systematically bias the zero-level set, giving rise to spurious surfaces or localized geometric collapse. An inherent conflict arises
between the photometric constraints of the 3DGS branch and the
geometric regularization of the SDF branch. This creates a tradeoff where favoring 3DGS supervision yields noisy surfaces, while
strict SDF constraints lead to over-smoothed details. One line of
research addresses this by imposing stronger geometric regularization on Gaussian primitives to improve their surface alignment,
while another explores more principled fusion strategies that go beyond depth supervision, seeking to extract and transfer richer geometric and photometric information latent in the Gaussian representation to the implicit network.
In fact, a well-pretrained 3DGS model inherently encodes rich
spatial occupancy and appearance distributions, making it a robust
prior for implicit surface reconstruction. SplatSDF [LSD∗ 24] extends Neuralangelo by aggregating the geometric and appearance
attributes of Gaussian primitives into high-dimensional feature representations. Rather than applying post consistency constraints,
SplatSDF pursues an architecture-level integration of 3DGS and
SDF-based NeRF. Specifically, a 3DGS Aggregator extracts and
consolidates multiple Gaussian attributes into a compact feature
embedding. The depth map rendered by the Gaussian Splatting
branch is then used to localize an anchor point along each ray, at
which the aggregated 3DGS feature is fused with the SDF feature.
This design allows the geometric information encoded in the Gaussian representation to directly inform and guide the SDF network
during surface querying. However, SplatSDF still exhibits several
limitations that leave room for improvement. Although 3DGS naturally supports a decoupled representation of geometric and appearance attributes, SplatSDF employs a monolithic aggregator

may become sensitive to inaccuracies in the 3DGS depth estimate.

4. Method
We propose GSNSR, which treats a pretrained 3DGS model as a
geometric and photometric prior and uses it in two complementary ways to guide SDF optimization within the Instant-NSR-PL
[Guo22] framework. The first targets the overall object contour.
Because the volume-rendering objective places no constraint on
unoccupied space, the SDF network tends to accumulate spurious
density in sparsely observed regions and thereby distort the object
contour. To counter this, we introduce a confidence-aware freespace loss (Sec. 4.1 and 4.2) that suppresses such density using the
3DGS-rendered pseudo-depth as a soft prior, weighted per pixel by
a reliability score so that the penalty is enforced only where the
depth can be trusted. At this stage the depth need only indicate, in
coarse terms, that the space in front of the surface is empty, so its
inaccuracy is tolerable.
The second use targets fine geometric and appearance detail, and
fuses 3DGS features into the SDF network at the architectural level,
following the principle of SplatSDF [LSD∗ 24] rather than coupling
the two representations through loss terms alone. GSNSR is not a
simple decoupled variant of SplatSDF, and we return to each of
the differences below in detail in Sec. 4.3, 4.4 and 4.5. For aggregator design, where SplatSDF encodes all Gaussian attributes
jointly in a single module, GSNSR adopts a Decoupled Aggregator
that processes geometric and appearance attributes through separate modules. This design follows the way 3DGS itself separates
these two groups of attributes. (Sec.4.3) For anchor localization,
SplatSDF places the anchor at the surface position rendered from
3DGS depth, so any error in that depth directly displaces the anchor
and injects features at the wrong locations. Anchoring demands a
precise position that the noisy 3DGS depth cannot supply. GSNSR
therefore derives the anchor from the ray-surface intersection implied by a sign change of the SDF along the ray, which severs the
dependence on Gaussian-rendered depth and lets the anchor grow
more accurate as the geometry network sharpens during training.
(Sec.4.4) For the fusion stage, SplatSDF overwrites the hash encoding of the sample nearest the anchor with the aggregated Gaussian
features. GSNSR instead supplies these features through a dedicated auxiliary channel, assigning zero-valued features to samples
far from the surface and weighting the samples that bracket the intersection by their distance to the anchor, so the sample closer to
the intersection receives a more faithful description of the local geometry than a single hard replacement at the nearest point would
provide. (Sec.4.5) The full framework is illustrated in Fig. 2.

5. Experiment
5.1. Experimental settings
Datasets. Following prior work [LSD∗ 24], we conduct experiments on 12 selected scenes from the DTU dataset [JDV∗ 14]. Each
scene comprises 49 to 64 images captured by a robot-mounted
monocular RGB camera. The dataset features a diverse range of
materials and complex geometries. Each scene is evaluated using
the foreground masks as provided in 2DGS [HYC∗ 24]. We additionally evaluate on the NeRF Synthetic dataset [MST∗ 21]. Each
scene provides 100 training images and 200 test images at a resolution of 800 × 800 pixels, rendered from upper-hemisphere viewpoints with a white background.
Implementation Details. We adopt a coarse-to-fine multiresolution hash encoding [MESK22, LME∗ 23] with 16 levels,
whose grid resolutions grow geometrically from 25 to 211 and
whose per-level hash table is capped at 222 entries. Each level
uses 2 features per hash entry, which we found sufficient for our
object-centric scenes while keeping training cost low. For the proposed Decoupled Aggregators, the output dimensions of geometric and color features are set to 32 and 64, respectively. Following
SplatSDF [LSD∗ 24], we set the number of neighboring Gaussians
K = 4 for feature aggregation. A coarse-to-fine optimization strategy is adopted to improve convergence stability. To ensure stable
ray-surface intersection during volume rendering, the aggregators
are activated after 10k iterations, by which point the SDF network
has established a reliable initial geometry and intersection points
can be reliably located. For the pseudo-depth confidence map, the
neighborhood support threshold is set to rthresh = 0.85 in Eq.4. The
consistency weight and relative standard deviation threshold are set
to γ = 0.25 and τσ = 0.03 in Eq.5. The depth gradient penalty is set
to λg = 1.0 in Eq.6. The loss weights follow those of the baseline,
with λ1 = 0.1, λ2 = 0.1, λ3 = 0.0005 and λ4 = 0.3. The safety
margin ϵ is adaptively configured based on the depth scale of the
scene: it is set to 20% of the bounding sphere radius for the DTU
dataset to account for geometric noise, and 10% for the NeRF Synthetic dataset owing to its more accurate pseudo-depth priors. All
experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.
The model is trained for 300k iterations, requiring approximately 8
hours per scene.

Quantitative Evaluation. We select Instant-NSR-PL [Guo22], a
PyTorch-Lightning implementation that provides object-level DTU
results, as our primary baseline for its remarkable efficiency. It
is distinct from the original Instant-NSR [ZJY∗ 22], which targets human-performance capture and does not evaluate on DTU.
To demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, we conduct a comprehensive comparison against several state-of-the-art methods,
including SDF-based methods (Instant-NSR-PL [Guo22], NeuS
[WLL∗ 21], NeuS2 [WHH∗ 23], Geo-NeuS [FXOT22], Neuralangelo [LME∗ 23], SplatSDF [LSD∗ 24]) and 3DGS-based methods
(SuGaR [GL24], GaussianSurfels [DXX∗ 24], 2DGS [HYC∗ 24],
MVS-GS [KPL25]). The quantitative results of Instant-NSR-PL
are directly cited from NeuS2. The experimental results in Table
1 demonstrate that our method achieves highly competitive performance across the majority of scenarios, with substantial gains
observed in scan63, scan83 and scan110. Our approach effectively
captures intricate object details while maintaining overall geometric integrity.
Table 2 reports a quantitative comparison of PSNR and training time across methods on the DTU dataset. Our method attains
the highest PSNR on scans 24, 37, 40, 55, 63 and 114 and ranks
second overall, with a mean PSNR of 32.19 dB, indicating competitive rendering quality. Although Neuralangelo achieves the best
mean PSNR (33.41 dB), it requires roughly 14 hours of training; by
contrast, our method reaches comparable quality in only 8 hours,
reducing training time by 33% relative to SplatSDF. Overall, these
results indicate that our method offers a favorable trade-off between
rendering fidelity and training efficiency, outperforming most baselines under comparable or shorter training budgets.

5.4. Limitation and Future Works
Although we introduce a confidence-aware mechanism to evaluate the reliability of Gaussian depth, our framework consumes this
depth as a soft prior and does not correct it. The confidence weighting down-weights unreliable depth wherever its failure is detectable
from local support, local stability, or viewing geometry, yet it can
only attenuate the influence of an erroneous prior. Where the Gaussian depth is severely wrong, the residual free-space constraint still
leaves a slight geometric bias that weighting alone cannot fully re© 2026 The Author(s).

move. The method therefore also struggles on scenes with excessive noise, where low-confidence depth priors may insufficiently
suppress geometric artifacts. Our current framework builds upon
vanilla 3DGS, whose geometric accuracy is inherently limited.
As illustrated in Fig. 7, Gaussians reconstructed on DTU scenes
exhibit a notable proportion of floaters detached from the actual
surface, whereas those on NeRF Synthetic scenes conform more
tightly to the object geometry. This discrepancy directly affects the
reliability of the pseudo-depth priors used in our free-space loss,
and partially explains the relatively weaker artifact suppression observed on DTU. We believe that integrating more geometrically
faithful Gaussian representations would substantially benefit our
pipeline.
Our method exhibits suboptimal reconstruction quality on largescale, unbounded scenes such as those in the Tanks and Temples
dataset. We attribute this primarily to the limited representational
capacity of our current network architecture, which was originally
designed and optimized for object-centric, bounded scenes. In such
settings, the MLP-based SDF network can adequately cover the
scene’s geometric complexity within a compact volume. However,
unbounded outdoor scenes introduce significantly greater spatial
extent, depth range ambiguity, and viewpoint diversity, which collectively exceed the expressive capacity of the current network.
The fixed network size struggles to simultaneously capture both
fine-grained local surface details and the global geometric layout
of large scenes, leading to incomplete or inaccurate reconstructions
in distant regions. We hope that future work will explore scene contraction strategies, spatial partitioning, or hierarchical network designs to scale the model’s representational capacity to unbounded
environments, enabling more accurate SDF learning across the full
scene extent.
