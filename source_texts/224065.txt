Fourth of the aquaflux.radiation series. Each source's contribution is now attenuated by the
water it crosses. UniformAbsorption is τ = a·r; VoxelAbsorption carries a coefficient that
varies in space and integrates it along every segment by walking the grid.
Walking the grid is not ray marching
Marching takes fixed steps and holds the coefficient constant across each one, which
systematically overestimates the surviving fraction — Jensen's inequality — with a bias that
shrinks only as the step does. Walking the real cell boundaries finds every crossing, so the only
error left is in the field's own representation, and it removes a convergence parameter:
there is no step size to choose. The step count is fixed at nx + ny + nz + 1, computed on the
host from the grid's shape, so the loop traces and carries no dependence on where any ray goes.
Three details make it exact rather than careful
Each cost a debugging round.
Cut on the sample planes, not the cell faces. They're half a cell apart. The interpolated
field is a separate cubic between neighbouring samples, so a piece straddling a sample plane
straddles a kink and no quadrature rule rescues it. Measured on a linear field with a closed-form
answer: 0.43% wrong on faces, exact on sample planes. That's the worst size for an error
to be — large enough to matter, small enough to read as discretization.
Generate cuts only where samples exist, at both ends. Beyond the outermost sample the field is
clamped and has no further kinks, so cutting on the infinite lattice spends the fixed budget
outside the grid — and a segment reaching well past it exhausts the budget and silently loses
its tail, which reads as weaker absorption rather than as a bug. The near end matters too: a
segment starting far outside meets its first real plane a long way in.
Simpson per piece, which is exact. A trilinear field along a straight line is a cubic in the
path parameter, and Simpson integrates cubics exactly — so the optical depth is the exact integral
of the interpolated field, not a quadrature of it. Verified to 1e-12 against fine quadrature
of the same interpolant, over segments inside the grid, straddling it, far outside it,
axis-aligned, reversed, and lying within one cell.
Choices that follow from wanting gradients
The field is interpolated, never nearest-cell: nearest is piecewise constant in position, so
its derivative is zero almost everywhere and undefined on the faces — a staircase in a path this
module promises gradients through. Outside the samples the value is clamped, so a segment
straying past the edge attenuates like the water at the edge rather than like vacuum. There's no
clamp on the optical depth — in double precision exp(-τ) reaches zero where zero is right, and
a clamp would only flatten dG/da across a region.
Gradients are checked against finite differences both cell-by-cell in a graded field and in the
segment endpoints.
The slab case, and which exponential integral is which
Attenuation is taken along the facet centroid's path — the field's standard per-segment treatment,
and another thing the refinement criterion is for. On the Beer–Lambert slab a uniform disc is
2% wrong near the axis and no extra radius fixes it; refining against the probes brings it
under 2e-3. So that test refines first, which also demonstrates PR #440's criterion end to end.
It pins the pair that gets swapped: G = 2M·E₂(κx) and E = 2M·E₃(κx), not exp(-κx), which
is the collimated result. Against collimated, the fluence-rate ratios are 0.80 at κx = 0.1 and
0.28 at 2; the irradiance ratios at the same depths are 0.920 and 0.445 — a factor of one and a
half at depth, with both numbers looking plausible. Swept to κx = 2 deliberately: at small depth
exp(-t) ≈ 1 − t and the integrals sit close to it.
Testing
28 new tests in test_radiation_absorption.py plus 13 at the gather level; 199 in the package.
Six mutations, all red: cutting on cell faces, midpoint instead of Simpson, cutting past the last
sample plane, not skipping to the first, nearest-cell sampling, and a gather that ignores the
medium.
A cross-check worth noting: a graded field whose values happen to be equal must reproduce the
closed-form strategy exactly, so the cheap path is the reference for the expensive one, and any
dropped or double-counted piece shows up against a formula.

WALKING THE GRID IS NOT RAY MARCHING. Marching takes fixed steps and holds the
coefficient constant across each one, which systematically overestimates the surviving
fraction -- Jensen's inequality -- with a bias that shrinks only as the step does. Walking
the real cell boundaries finds every crossing, so the only error left is in the field's
own representation, and it removes a convergence parameter: there is no step size to
choose. The step count is fixed at `nx + ny + nz + 1` and computed on the host from the
grid's shape alone, so the loop is traceable and carries no dependence on where any
particular ray goes.

Three details make the traversal exact rather than merely careful, and each cost a round
to find:

CUT ON THE SAMPLE PLANES, NOT THE CELL FACES. They sit half a cell apart. The interpolated
field is a separate cubic between neighbouring samples, so a piece that straddles a sample
plane straddles a kink and no quadrature rule rescues it. Measured on a linear field with
a closed-form answer: 0.43% wrong cutting on faces, exact cutting on sample planes. That
is the worst size for an error to be -- large enough to matter, small enough to read as
discretization.

GENERATE CUTS ONLY WHERE SAMPLES EXIST, AT BOTH ENDS. Beyond the outermost sample the
field is clamped and has no further kinks, so cutting on the infinite lattice spends the
fixed budget outside the grid; a segment reaching well past it then exhausts the budget
and silently loses its tail, which reads as weaker absorption rather than as a bug. The
near end matters too: a segment starting far outside meets its first real plane a long
way in.

SIMPSON PER PIECE, WHICH IS EXACT. A trilinear field restricted to a straight line is a
cubic in the path parameter, and Simpson integrates cubics exactly, so the optical depth
is the exact integral of the interpolated field rather than a quadrature of it. Verified
to 1e-12 against fine quadrature of the same interpolant, over segments inside the grid,
straddling it, far outside it, axis-aligned, reversed, and lying within one cell.

The field is interpolated and never sampled at the nearest cell: nearest is piecewise
constant in position, so its derivative is zero almost everywhere and undefined on the
faces between, which is a staircase in a path this module promises gradients through.
