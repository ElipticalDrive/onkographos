raycast_ellipsoid computes the quadratic discriminant in f32 after mapping the ray into the splat's unit-sphere frame. When the ray origin is far from the splat relative to its size (|o| >> 1, e.g. small splats seen from a few meters away), b^2 - ac suffers catastrophic cancellation. Real hits get rejected, or come back at the wrong distance: in the included test, a 5 mm splat at ~1.4 km is hit about 40 cm short of its surface.
This replaces it with the Lagrange identity a - |o × d|^2, which equals b^2 - ac for a unit sphere and does not cancel. Everything stays in f32: the change is limited to the two discriminant lines plus the test. The signature and the degenerate/flat-splat branches are unchanged.
In a random sweep (8M rays, distance/scale ratio 1e2–1e7), the old discriminant starts returning wrong hits around a ratio of ~3e2; with the Lagrange identity this moves to ~2e5. Evaluating it in f64 gives no measurable gain (same threshold, error rates within ~1%), because the remaining error comes from the f32 center subtraction and rotation.
Found while building a faster picker (see #468). In our tests the fix doubled the share of picks landing within 5 cm of the true surface (12% → 24%) on a multi-million-splat scan.
Related: #75, #468
