Screen-space GI 2/4: the trace and the composite. It builds on #1998's GI targets.
What changes
ScreenSpaceGlobalIllumination, a camera component with the same conventions as ScreenSpaceReflections: absent means off, present means on.
Fields: enabled, intensity, rays (4), steps (24), stride (0.2), thickness.
It is reflected and registered, and reaches the surface through WgpuSurface::set_ssgi from a new exclusive feed_ssgi system. That's because the render system is at Bevy's 16-parameter ceiling, and render_frame's argument list is at its practical limit too.
Trace (ssgi pass, before SSR):
Each pixel with a surface traces N cosine-weighted rays about its normal, rotated per pixel and per frame. Each ray marches through the depth buffer exactly as the reflection ray does.
A ray that goes behind what's drawn, within the thickness, has hit it, and brings back that surface's light from this frame's lit HDR scene.
The pass writes ΣL / N and the share of rays that hit, both times the intensity.
It is ΣL / N, not π·ΣL / N, because the shading keeps irradiance as E/π: the flat ambient lights a surface ambient × albedo, and the sky and probes store E/π. With the π, a floor that saw only floor brightened by π.
⚠️ No self-hits. On a surface seen at a slant, a ray just after its start is a fraction of a pixel above that surface. The depth it is compared with is the pixel centre's, which is nearer than the ray, so it reads as behind its own surface, and a flat floor lit itself with its own colour. A ray leaves the plane it starts in at once, so a "hit" within half a step of that plane is rejected and the ray marches on.
Composite (ssgi composite pass): colour + reflectance·traced − share·indirect, added to the HDR scene (a signed add, which the float target holds).
Only the share of directions that found something is replaced; missed directions keep the probe, lightmap or sky light. That's Unity HDRP's SSGI rule.
So a surface beside something darker than the sky darkens.
Tests (pixels_ssgi.rs, 5, plus 1 in the plugin)
The fixture is a coloured floor (0.8, 0.5, 0.3) under a flat ambient of 0.3, and a wall standing on it to the right.
A red wall bleeds onto the floor beside it: red rises, green and blue barely move, and more beside the wall than away from it.
A black wall darkens the floor it shades: replacement, which a trace that only added could never do. The darkening is bounded by the wall's share of the hemisphere, which a ray counted twice would exceed.
A wall as bright as the ambient changes nothing: a wall glowing 0.3 and reflecting nothing moves the floor beside it by under a tenth of what the same rays take when the wall is black, per channel. This holds the units, the per-channel reflectance and the share to one another.
With nothing to find: a floor alone is byte for byte its frame without SSGI, across the whole interior. Only a handful of edge pixels differ, where the depth jumps to the background. A disabled component is no component.
Intensity: at 0.5, the black wall takes half of what it takes at 1.
Plugin: the camera's component reaches the surface, and goes when the component does.
All also pass with the test device forced to DX12.
Mutations: 9/9 killed, each applied alone to the committed tree. Two survived a first round, and the tests were strengthened:
green and blue swapped in the reflectance: under an absolute tolerance, too few rays hit for it to show, so the bound is now relative to the black wall's;
intensity ignored: every test used 1.
The π bug and the self-hits were both found by the tests while writing them, not by mutation.
Not yet
PR 3: temporal accumulation and a spatial denoise. At 4 rays a frame the result is noisy.
PR 4: hi-Z and half resolution, the previous frame's HDR for more than one bounce (TAA's history is LDR), the editor/MCP toggle, and release costs.
