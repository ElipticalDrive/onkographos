Index boxes of every structure in the in-memory trees
An R-tree, a quad-tree or a k-d tree partitions its boxes on the axes they
carry and compares them on those axes, so a tree holds boxes of one
structure: the same axes and, where they carry a value or a spatial axis, the
same span type, SRID and geodetic flag, bbox_same_structure states it. A point
cloud box has the structure of the spatiotemporal box it begins with, its SRID
the one of its schema. An index keeps the boxes of each structure in a tree of
their own, chained from the one its constructor returns, and every entry point
walks them: an insertion goes to the tree of the structure of its box, a load
builds one tree per structure, a join pairs the trees of the two indexes, a
nearest-neighbour cursor orders the entries of every tree in one heap, and the
counts, the size, the height and the release cover every tree. A
space-partitioning tree keeps a box of its structure as received, since a
point cloud tree holds its boxes projected.
A search answers as its operation applied to every stored box does, as a scan
of the boxes would. Whether the operation raises an error on a pair of boxes
depends on their structures alone, and index_op_is_valid tests it without
raising: it is the conjunction of the non-raising halves of the validators the
operation calls, tbox_tbox_is_valid, stbox_stbox_is_valid,
tpcbox_tpcbox_is_valid, span_type_is_same, srid_is_same, geodetic_is_same and
dimension_is_common, each ensure_* reporting its error when its half fails.
Where the test fails for a tree, the search applies the operation to a box
stored in it through index_op_apply, which raises the error a scan raises, and
answers -1; elsewhere the tree is searched on the query projected onto the
axes the tree carries by index_query_project, the node predicates reading
those axes. index_strategy_is_valid reads the operation of a strategy of a
PostgreSQL index for its operator classes. An operation the box type does not
order is refused by ensure_index_op: a span orders its single axis to the left
and to the right, whether it holds values or times, a temporal box orders its
value to the left and to the right and its time before and after, and a
spatiotemporal or point cloud box orders also its other spatial axes. A
temporal insertion answers the insertion it makes.
A nearest-neighbour cursor answers the distances a scan computes, in their
order. Whether the distance raises on a pair of boxes is tested by
index_distance_is_valid from the same halves, with has_X and
same_spatial_dimensionality: a span of another type, a temporal box without
a value span or of another value type, a spatiotemporal box without space or
of another dimensionality. Where it fails for a tree, the opening applies the
distance to a box stored in it through index_distance_apply, which raises the
error of a scan, and answers NULL.
Witness: over 240 boxes cycling through every set of axes of the temporal box
(3), the spatiotemporal box (5) and the point cloud box (3), every search
operation asked with four queries of every set of axes, the index answered
otherwise than the scan on 2332 of the 2340 cells of the nine trees, seven of
which aborted on an assertion, and the nearest-neighbour cursors on 44 of 132
arms, the 12 of the temporal box R-tree aborting on an assertion; every cell
and every arm now answers as the scan.
Measured: meos/test/index_structure_test.c asserts the 2340 cells, and checks
index_op_is_valid against the operation itself on every ordered pair of boxes
of different structures, value span types, SRIDs, geodetic flags and schemas
included: 325 temporal box pairs, 1701 spatiotemporal box pairs and 425 point
cloud box pairs answer as the operation. Its nearest-neighbour arms open a
cursor of each tree with queries of every set of axes, 12 per tree for the
temporal box and 20 for the spatiotemporal box, and compare the distances
with those of every stored box sorted, or the error with the error; they also
check index_distance_is_valid against the distance on 25 and 81 pairs. The
programs of the in-memory trees pass unchanged and the library builds with no
warning. No timing was taken:
the test is read once per tree and search, and the projection once per search.
Why: a tree compares its boxes on the axes they carry, so a box of another
structure inserted in it was compared on axes it lacks, which aborted on an
assertion or answered otherwise than the scan. Testing whether the operation
raises from the halves of its own validators states each condition once, so
the index cannot drift from the operator it answers for.
Index boxes of every structure in the GiST and SP-GiST operator classes
A column of temporal, spatiotemporal or point cloud boxes holds boxes of
several structures, as bbox_same_structure states them, and the operator
classes keep the boxes of each structure apart as the in-memory trees do. A
GiST key over boxes of several structures carries MEOS_FLAG_PARTS: the union
sets it, the penalty prefers a key of the structure of the box, the split
separates the structures first, the consistent methods descend such a key for
every query and the distance methods visit it first. An SP-GiST inner tuple
whose prefix carries the flag sends the boxes of its structure to its first
node and the others to its second without adding a level, and a box reaching
an inner tuple of another structure splits it. A point cloud box is indexed as
the spatiotemporal box it begins with.
No consistent method raises an error. Where index_strategy_is_valid states
that the operator raises on the boxes under a key or a centroid, the entry is
a candidate with the recheck set and the operator applied to the row raises
the error a sequential scan raises; elsewhere an SP-GiST query is compared on
the axes its tree carries, projected by index_query_project. Where
index_distance_is_valid states that the distance raises on a key, a centroid
or a node, the distance method answers minus infinity, so the entries under it
are visited first, and a leaf the executor rechecks answers the same, the
distance applied to its row raising the error of the scan. The exact leaf of a
GiST index over a column of boxes is the value, which an index-only scan
returns without a recheck and PostgreSQL refuses to recheck there, so its
distance method applies the distance to it through index_distance_apply,
which raises the error of the scan: the one place an index raises, on a stored
value and never on a key. The design note
doc/contributing/index_design_notes.md states the contract and the one
mechanism the MEOS trees and the operator classes share.
Witness: in the probes of the index lane, a column cycling through boxes of
every set of axes, through value spans of integers and floats with and
without time, or through planar boxes of SRIDs 3857 and 5676, a geodetic box
and a box with time only, crashed the server at CREATE INDEX for the
temporal box GiST, quad-tree and k-d tree and the spatiotemporal k-d tree, and
the spatiotemporal quad-tree answered && with a query with X and T on 44 rows
where the scan answers 564. Ordered by |=| with the search fixed, the
temporal box quad-tree and k-d tree crashed the server, and the
spatiotemporal quad-tree and k-d tree answered 25 distances 0 on 3 queries
where the scan raises. Every index now answers as the scan.
Measured: index_structures() of 044_temporal_indexes_tbl,
074_tpoint_indexes_tbl and 439_tpc_gist_tbl asks every operator, |=|
included, with a query of every structure, 14 x 5, 22 x 7 and 22 x 4 answers
per operator class, and each of the nine operator classes reads the index and
differs from the scan on none. Where the boxes of several structures raise
different errors the error is the one of the first box a plan meets, which no
plan orders, so two errors agree. The probes of the index lane answer as the
scan for every column of temporal and spatiotemporal boxes, searched and
ordered by |=|. Over two point cloud schemas sharing an SRID the scan raises
the schema error, the quad-tree and k-d tree answer 0 on 6 cells of @> and ~=,
and ordered by |=| the index raises another error than the scan on 11
queries: an index of point cloud boxes is the index of their spatiotemporal
boxes. The extension builds with no warning.
Why: an index answering otherwise than its operator answers a query
differently because the index exists. Raising in the recheck makes the error
the operator's own on a row the query reads, as the consistent functions of
PostgreSQL and PostGIS never raise; only an index-only scan, which has no
recheck of a distance, meets the error on the stored value itself.
