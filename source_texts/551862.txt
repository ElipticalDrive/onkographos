Before: a sieved dataset could be read and rendered, but nothing measured its internal voids.
After: vs-porosity part.vsieve --out dir reports every pore (Lunker) and every zone of loosened microstructure (Gefügeauflockerung) with position, bounds and void volume, plus part volume and total porosity. It writes porosity.json, projection images along x, y and z (grey thickness, pores red, zones yellow) and porosity.vdb with grids pores and zones for Blender or Houdini.
On an 830 MB synthetic scan (1025 × 775 × 525 voxels, 6 lunkers, 3 loosened zones, noise 500, 10 % cupping) every lunker lands within 2 % of its ground-truth volume, every zone within 2 %, and total porosity within 1 %. Analysis takes 14 s on 4 cores. Sound parts with cupping produce no findings.
How: the analysis streams level 0 brick by brick and keeps only sparse state.
Pores are 26-connected components of below-threshold voxels that are not connected to outside air. Their volume is a partial-volume void sum over the component plus a one-voxel shell.
Zones are 8³ blocks whose mean lies clearly below the material level at the same depth under the surface. That depth-based reference is what keeps cupping from showing up as porosity.
Method and limitations are in ADR 0006. The known limitations are zones right at the surface, small false zones under strong ring artefacts, and a slight part-volume bias with very steep cupping.
A minimal PNG writer (stored deflate) avoids a new dependency.
Tests: tests/test_porosity.cpp scores sound parts, lunkers (including one large enough to leave completely empty OpenVDB leaves), loosened zones under cupping, and the output files against SyntheticScan ground truth. The CLI smoke tests run vs-synth → vs-sieve → vs-porosity. Debug and ASan pass 49/49, and tools/lint.sh is clean.
