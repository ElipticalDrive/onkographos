Goal
Reconstruct the Sweet–Ott–Yorke four-mirrored-spheres photograph (D. Sweet, E. Ott, J. A. Yorke, Topology in chaotic scattering, Nature 399, 315, 1999) as a ray-traced 3D fractal in FF, and expose its Wada exit basins as a zoomable fractal.
Physics
Four mutually tangent mirror spheres at the vertices of a tetrahedron. The gaps between each triple of balls form 4 openings (one per tetrahedron face). A camera ray entering through one opening reflects specularly until it exits through one of the 4 openings. Pixel colour = what lies beyond that exit (in the photo, the opening behind the viewer shows the dark photographer). The basin boundaries have the Wada property: every boundary point borders all basins.
This is analytic ray–sphere tracing (closed-form quadratic intersection + reflect), not DE sphere-marching. Cheap, exact, deep zoom in double.

Independent invariant (validation)
A ray launched in the plane through three sphere centres stays in that plane (every hit normal lies in it), so it reduces exactly to the 2D 3-disk billiard. In-plane outcomes (exit gate, bounce count) must match ChaoticBilliardCalculator ThreeDisk with tangent disks of equal radius. Second invariant: time-reversal — reversing an exiting ray's final direction from its exit point retraces the bounce sequence.
