Abstract:
This paper addresses the computational scalability challenges in automating inspection tasks across large-scale spatial domains, where a fundamental trade-off exists between modeling fidelity for obstacle avoidance and planning tractability. While motivated by the autonomous inspection of offshore wind farms using Autonomous Surface Vessels (ASVs), the proposed hierarchical framework offers a generalizable solution for navigating vast environments with sparse obstacles. Existing planners often suffer from prohibitive computational costs when processing environments with millions of grid cells. To overcome this, we present a novel multi-stage framework integrating hierarchical environment modeling with optimized search techniques. The core contributions are threefold: (1) A layered refinement grid strategy that unlike generic hierarchical structures, optimizes memory efficiency for extreme scale disparities to reduce the total grid count by 99.9%; (2) A hexagonal grid compression method that further decreases search nodes by over 95%, thereby mitigating directional bias and preserving topological clustering fidelity; and (3) A bidirectional shortcut-embedded A algorithm that executes efficient local detour planning within constrained corridors. Experimental validation in a large-scale real-world scenario (54 turbines) demonstrates the framework’s efficacy. The proposed method achieves hierarchically-refined path inspection paths in 1.21 seconds, significantly outperforming a baseline approach (5.97 seconds). This work provides a scalable, computationally efficient solution applicable to broad classes of automated inspection and logistics systems. I. Introduction
The global offshore wind power sector has experienced substantial growth in recent decades [1]. Data from the International Renewable Energy Agency indicate that the cumulative installed capacity increased from 11.735 GW in 2015 to 79.434 GW by 2024 [2], with individual wind farms now typically comprising dozens to hundreds of turbines. Concurrently, many early-commissioned offshore wind turbines are nearing their designed service lifespan, generally estimated at 20 to 25 years [3]. Notable examples include the world’s first commercial offshore wind farm, Denmark’s Vindeby Wind Farm, commissioned in 1991 and decommissioned after over 25 years of service, as well as China’s first demonstration project, installed in 2007, which has now entered its later operational stages. This progressive aging of early installations underscores the growing need for reliable inspection technologies to ensure structural integrity and long-term operational safety.
