What it does
The 3D view used to draw each slice as a flat quad, far to near. It now casts a ray through a 3D texture for every pixel and composites the samples front to back. The volume looks solid from any angle, and no gaps show edge-on.
One 3D texture. The volume is R16Float with trilinear filtering. It is sized to the GPU's 3D texture limit, which main.rs now requests from the adapter (usually 2048 a side). Full detail keeps every slice up to that limit. Upload is one layer at a time, inside the same error scopes as before.
Blend. Opacity per step comes from the window as before, now per unit of path length. Density means how opaque the volume's whole thickness would be, seen face on, at the top of the window. Rays exit early at 99% opacity.
MIP. The brightest sample along each ray. Inversion and the colour map are applied in the composite pass, as before.
Shading (new Shade toggle, on by default). Light comes from the viewer, with normals from the volume's gradient.
The gradient is taken two voxels each way. With one voxel each way, normals jump between voxels and smooth surfaces show rings. I found this on a test sphere and compared four variants before choosing.
Flat regions stay unlit, so noise doesn't sparkle.
Sampling.
Each pixel starts its ray at a fixed random offset, so the sample layers don't show as bands.
While the view moves: one sample per voxel along the fastest axis, at half resolution. Once it rests: two samples per voxel at full resolution.
A still frame is capped at about two million rays. That keeps a frame on a slow GPU well inside Windows' two-second limit.
A view that hasn't changed is not cast again.
The turn into 3D keeps its feel. The volume thickens out of the current slice as it turns. That slice glows and fades as the volume fades in, so the view starts exactly at the 2D image.
The default opacity rises from 8 to 30, so bone and contrast-filled vessels look solid. 8 was tuned for the slice stack.
Files
src/volume.wgsl: ray casting, lighting and the composite pass.
src/render3d.rs: 3D texture upload, the ray pass with caching, and Scene → VolumeUniforms. The view geometry and rotation moved here from app.rs. Split into new/prepare/draw so tests can drive it.
src/app.rs: uses Scene. Half resolution while moving, Shade toggle, new defaults and hints.
src/volume.rs: depth and side limited by the 3D texture limit; Volume::from_values for tests.
src/main.rs: requests max_texture_dimension_3d.
README.md, docs/3d.png: new 3D section, picture and limits.
Testing
cargo fmt --check, cargo clippy --all-targets -D warnings and cargo test (42 tests) pass.
New GPU test render3d::rays_find_the_cube renders a bright cube in a 32³ volume:
face on;
edge on, where the old slice stack would show nothing;
tilted with shading;
as MIP;
at the start of the turn, where only the current slice shows (a slice through the cube shows it, one beside it doesn't).
It skips when no GPU is present; locally it ran on lavapipe.
I checked that trilinear filtering of the R16Float 3D texture works: a 4-voxel ramp renders as a smooth gradient.
naga translates the shader to HLSL, MSL, SPIR-V and GLSL ES 3.0.
Checked by eye under Xvfb, on smooth head and vessel phantoms with 1 mm voxels and on the older thick-slice phantoms:
bone and soft-tissue windows;
opacity 8, 30 and 80;
hot iron;
the turn in and out.
Known limit, in the README: with thick slices, smooth surfaces show faint steps where the slices were.
