Clip submerged geometry out of the reflection pass
At the default shore viewpoint, mirroring the camera places it below the terrain: its reflected height is approximately -5.68 while the ground is at 3.93. This pass draws the entire lake basin with depth testing and no water-plane clipping, so rays toward reflected trees and mountains hit the underside of the near shoreline first. That underwater terrain consequently occludes the intended scenery in the reflection texture. Clip fragments below the water plane during the mirror pass, or use an equivalent oblique clipping plane.

Add hysteresis to the reflection performance fallback
On hardware that renders below 22 FPS with reflections but above 22 FPS without them, this condition repeatedly switches the reflection pass off and back on at each FPS update. The water alternates between scene reflections and a flat fallback color roughly every second, and performance never settles into the cheaper mode. Retain the fallback decision or use separate disable/re-enable thresholds with a cooldown rather than immediately re-enabling based on FPS measured without the pass.

Keep the automatic resolution-reduction threshold reachable
On slow hardware, automatic resolution reduction can no longer activate: dt is capped at 0.07 seconds before being accumulated into drawSec, so drawCount/drawSec is always at least approximately 14.29 and the rounded state.fps can never satisfy the new <14 condition. For example, actual rendering at either 5 or 10 FPS reports 14 FPS and never reduces resolution, whereas the previous <16 threshold triggered the fallback. Measure FPS using unclamped wall-clock time or retain a threshold reachable with the existing estimator.
