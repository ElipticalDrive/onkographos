I am looking for works in the volume-rendering / radiative-transport literature that treats the direction  $\hat{\omega}(x)$  as an  optimizable function  rather than a known input function. That is, one seeks  $\hat{\omega}^\star\in S^2$  is the optimizer of a scalar objective built from the rendered radiance. 
 For concreteness, consider the standard volume-rendering integral (written informally) 
 $$
L(x,\hat\omega)
=\int_0^{d_0} \exp\!\Big(-\int_0^t \sigma(x_s)\,ds\Big)\,f(x_t,\hat\omega,L)\,dt
\;+\;\exp\!\Big(-\int_0^{d_0}\sigma(x_s)\,ds\Big)\,g(x_d,\hat\omega,L).
$$ 
 One such definition of an objective  $J(\hat\omega)$  built from  $L(x,\hat\omega)$  can be  $J(\hat\omega) = \mathbb{E}_{x \in D}\left[L(x,\hat\omega)\right]$ , and the problem aims to solve the variational problem
 $\hat\omega^\star=\arg\min_{\hat\omega\in S^2} J(\hat\omega)$ . 
 I do find similarities of this to the stochastic control problem, however in my application I am more interested in it from the rendering perspective (i.e., with monte carlo methods, path tracing, etc), instead of the viscosity solutions perspective. 
 Are there established references, formulations, or algorithms that exists in volume rendering literature that somehow resembles this? Short pointers or keywords to follow up on is most useful!
