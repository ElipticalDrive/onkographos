doc 35 § What is deliberately not built, second row:
| Volumetric underwater — god rays, participating media | It is
19's volumetric fog with a different phase
function, and volumetric fog is ⬜. When it lands, underwater is a preset for it rather than a
second implementation |
Volumetric fog landed. Core/Vixen.Rendering.PostFx/VolumetricFogRenderer.cs is built with
shadowed in-scatter, a per-froxel punctual light loop and temporal reprojection, and
ScreenSpaceEffectAssets.cs gives it a !VolumetricFog node kind a document can write. So the
deferral's stated condition — "when it lands" — has been met, and the row is now a description of
available work rather than a refusal.
⚠ The row's own framing is what makes this worth doing rather than large: "underwater is a
preset for it rather than a second implementation." Water already composes a post-process volume
for the underwater grade (D9), and AppGraphics wires
Volumes.Shapes ??= Water so a body's shape is the volume's containment test. What is missing is the
medium — a phase function biased forward, a density and an extinction that come from the zone's
water rather than from an authored fog volume, and the shafts that fall out of the existing shadowed
in-scatter.
Two things to decide, both small and both worth deciding rather than discovering:
Where the numbers come from. A fog volume is authored; water's absorption and scattering are
already on the body's profile, and having two places to set "how murky is this lake" is the failure
doc 32 avoided for the lens. The zone should feed the fog, not sit beside it.
⚠ What happens at the waterline. D9 already names the waterline as "the
hard part" and budgets it separately from the volume; a participating medium that starts abruptly
at a plane the camera is straddling is that same problem with a second symptom.
Not in scope, and the row is right to keep them out: caustics (a photon gather is a research
project) and wet surfaces.
Source: docs/plan/35-water.md.
