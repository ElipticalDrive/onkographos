Contributor
Lets UTempoLidar see dust, smoke and fog, with the rule that whatever attenuates the camera's view attenuates the beam, and no per-effect setup: no tagging of emitters, no CPU particle model. Medium echoes come back labeled like surface returns.
Approach
A scene-depth lidar sees through everything the fog and translucency passes draw, because nothing in those passes writes depth, and the final pixel color is a line integral that cannot be inverted into a range. So the lidar rebuilds the transmittance profile along each beam from the same data those passes render from, evaluated at many ranges instead of at the one range of the opaque surface. Per tile, inside the lidar's own render:
Profile pass (compute): the exponential height fog (closed form, mirrored from the engine's fog math), the volumetric fog froxel grid (its alpha is the eye-to-froxel transmittance the engine already integrated, so this covers Volume-domain materials and injected local fog volumes), and analytically composed local fog volumes, discretized into log-spaced range bins in three 3D textures: optical depth, optical depth weighted by albedo (the fog component's), and the bin's largest single contribution over its contributor's label.
Translucency pass (mesh pass with two plugin-owned material shader types, compiled for every translucent surface material): re-rasterizes the view's translucent batches with no color output, evaluates each fragment's real opacity (soft-particle fade and all) and atomically adds -ln(1 - opacity) to the profile, the same weighted by an albedo estimate from the material's color, and the primitive's label (the custom depth stencil value the labeler assigned it, read off the proxy, not rendered). Sprites are read as the balls they stand for: the optical depth is spread along the ray's chord through a sphere of the particle's radius, densest at the center, so puffs are round rather than flat discs facing the sensor. Additive materials block nothing and are not compiled for the pass.
Resolve pass (compute): the sensor model. The surface return is attenuated by the two-way transmittance and dropped below MinDetectableIntensity. The medium's backscatter is integrated per bin, each bin's return integrated through its own depth (so a dense cloud returns from its front face rather than vanishing), weighted by the bin's albedo, the two-way transmittance and the lidar's existing range falloff, into one medium echo whose range is drawn from that distribution, whose reflectivity is its return-weighted albedo, and whose label is that of whatever contributed most to the bin it landed in. Medium inside MinDistance attenuates but returns nothing.
The results ride a second staging texture under the atlas copy's fence, written at each tile's atlas slot and cleared to "no media" ahead of every capture. The decode picks Strongest, First, Last or Dual returns per beam.
Because the engine's volumetric fog history blend assumes a render every scene tick, a sub-rate sensor's grid converged N times slower and moving dust trailed its emitter. The lidar and the camera now rescale r.VolumetricFog.HistoryWeight to its Nth power around their own render, the same correction the motion vector rewarp applies to velocities.
No engine changes. Engine-private access stays in TempoMultiViewCapture.cpp; the shaders module links Renderer for the mesh pass.
Labels
Media are labeled from the same label table as everything else, with no custom depth rendering involved:
Rasterized translucency carries its primitive's label. A Niagara component is labeled like any other component: by its ComponentTags, the system it plays, the meshes it renders, or its owning actor. The label table gains a NiagaraSystemTypes column so an effect can be labeled by what it is, exhaust or dust, on every actor that plays it; it is the only handle on sprite and ribbon emitters and outranks the meshes a system's mesh renderers instance. The table validator and the get_semantic_classes RPC know the column.
Fog is labeled through its actors. The height fog and the volumetric fog grid carry the label of the height fog actor whose fog the renderer composes (an ActorTypes row for ExponentialHeightFog); each local fog volume carries its own actor's label, matched to the instances the view composes by center and evaluated per instance through each bin, so a steam volume reads differently from the ambient fog. The labeler exposes the value it assigns an actor for callers labeling something that renders no custom depth.
API
Lidar.proto gains LidarReturnMode and, on LidarScanSegment, return_mode plus second_distances_m, second_intensities, second_labels and second_reflectivities (populated in Dual mode; same layouts as their top-level counterparts). labels now also covers medium echoes. Segments from a lidar without media simulation are byte-identical to before. Labels.proto reports niagara_system_types per semantic class. The Rust SensorPlayground draws second returns beside first returns.
Settings
On the lidar: bSimulateParticipatingMedia (on by default; off, no extra pass runs and the output is unchanged), bMediaIncludesTranslucency, MediaExtinctionScale, MediaBackscatter (the backscatter of a white medium; each medium's return is that times its albedo), MinDetectableIntensity, ReturnMode (Strongest by default), and, advanced, bStochasticMediaReturns and MediaRangeBins. Documented in docs/plugins/tempo-sensors.md. Since media are on by default, a level with height fog now reports slightly lower far-range intensities than before.
Cost
Well under 2x the no-color lidar render in a dusty scene: the engine's fog passes (new in no-color mode, already paid in color mode), two small compute passes, one opacity-only rasterization of translucent primitives, 8 more readback bytes per pixel, and a transient bins x 12 bytes per pixel profile. Translucent materials compile two more shaders once.
Verification
All Tempo.Sensors automation tests pass, including new tests for the return-mode selection and the results packing. Tested in-editor against fog and sprite dust on 5.8; the 5.7 engine paths used were checked against 5.7 source.

```
depth. The color of a pixel is a line integral over everything translucent along its ray, so it
cannot be inverted into a range either. Instead, rebuild the transmittance profile along each

volumetric fog froxel grid (whose alpha is the eye-to-froxel transmittance the engine already
integrated, including Volume-domain primitives and, when so configured, local fog volumes) and
local fog volumes composed analytically. Whatever the camera sees as fog, the beam sees.

Two compute passes per tile, run by a view extension gathered only into the lidar's own render:
one discretizes the profile into log-spaced range bins of optical depth (a 3D texture other
passes can add to), one resolves it with a sensor model. The surface return is attenuated by the
two-way transmittance and dropped below a detectable intensity; the medium's backscatter is
integrated per bin, weighted by the two-way transmittance and the sensor's range falloff, into
one medium echo whose range is drawn from that distribution. The results are read back through a
second staging texture under the atlas copy's fence, and the decode chooses what each beam
reports: strongest, first, last, or two returns.
```

Rasterize translucent primitives into the lidar's media profile
…
7578c6a
```
Most dust and smoke effects are not fog at all: they are Niagara sprites and ribbons, mesh
particles and translucent meshes, whose opacity the camera's translucency pass evaluates and
whose depth it throws away. Nothing volumetric holds them, so the profile built from the fog
sources never sees them.

Draw them a second time. Two material shader types, registered by the shaders module so they
compile into every translucent surface material's shader map, rasterize the view's translucent
batches (dynamic and static, gathered as the renderer gathers them) depth-tested against the
opaque scene with no color target. The pixel shader evaluates the real material's opacity, soft
particle fade and all, and adds -ln(1 - opacity) to the profile bin at the fragment's range
with an atomic. Additive materials add light and block none, so they are not compiled for the
pass; modulate materials block what they darken; Substrate materials take the translucent
shadow pass's coverage path.

The mesh pass is built with the public FMeshPassProcessor and DrawDynamicMeshPass API, so the
shaders module now links Renderer. bMediaIncludesTranslucency on the lidar turns the pass off.
```

Rescale the volumetric fog history for sub-rate sensor renders
…
f2c4f27
```
The engine blends each render's volumetric fog grid with the previous render's by a fixed weight,
0.9 by default, which assumes a render every scene tick: the history decays over about ten
ticks. A sensor rendering every N ticks blends once per N ticks, so its grid takes N times longer
in scene time to converge, and dust moving with its emitter trails it by up to seconds. The lidar
now reads that grid, and the camera's fog color always did.

Raise the weight to the Nth power around each sensor's own render, N being the ticks since its
tiles last rendered, the factor the motion vector rewarp already computes. One blend then has
the decay of N per-tick blends and the grid converges per tick of scene time at any rate. The
variable is render-thread safe, so the change and its restore are applied in order with the
render commands between them, and only that render sees it; its set-by priority is kept so
scalability settings still own it.
```

Read each sprite as a ball when adding it to the lidar's media profile
…
3f76b16
```
A camera-facing sprite has one range: every fragment of the quad lies on the plane through the
particle, so adding its optical depth at the fragment's range made each puff a flat disc facing
the sensor and a cloud of puffs a wall, with the roundness the camera shows coming only from the
texture's shading. A billboard shows the same picture from every direction, which only a shape
symmetric about the particle can do, and a radial falloff is the projection of a fuzzy ball; the
engine's own spherical particle normal and opacity read a sprite as a sphere of half its smaller
size around the particle.

Read it the same way. The pass asks the sprite vertex factory for the particle's center and
radius, finds where the fragment's ray passes closest to the center, takes the chord through
that sphere, and spreads the fragment's optical depth over the bins the chord crosses with a
density that falls off with the square of the distance from the center, whose share per bin is
a closed-form cubic. The total is unchanged, so the transmittance the camera implies is kept;
only where it sits along the ray changes. Fragments whose ray misses the sphere, the quad's
corners and the ends of a stretched sprite, fall back to the plane, as do meshes and ribbons.
```

```
different shape from every other field on the segment, and its name said one return when it held
```

Integrate each bin's medium return through its own depth
…
3c7dcdf
```
The per-bin return weight attenuated the bin's own return by its whole
optical depth, which is right for a thin bin but collapses for a dense
one: a sprite with a near-opaque centre, or a few overlapping sprites in
one bin, blocked the surface behind and returned nothing itself, so the
beam reported no echo at all. The weight is now the exact backscatter
integral through a bin of uniform density, (1 - e^-2tau) / 2, which
saturates at one half so a dense cloud still returns from its front
face, and the range drawn within a bin follows the same distribution.
```

Write each tile's media results at its atlas slot
…
67b3729
```
The renderer quantizes every view's rect in the scene textures up to an
8-pixel boundary, so a tile whose atlas slot does not start on one is
rasterized a few pixels to the right of the slot its output lands in.
The resolve wrote the results at the rasterized rect: the last tile's
then overran the results texture and was skipped, leaving its slot as
uninitialized memory that decoded as a fixed cloud of medium echoes, and
the tiles between were offset by a few columns from the atlas. The
results are now written at the view's unscaled rect, which is the slot,
while the scene textures are still read at the rasterized rect.
```
