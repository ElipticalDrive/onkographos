Summary
Lets frozen INR configurations run at large volume sizes on 40 GB A100s without changing batch size or optimization.
Tomography.reconstruct(grad_accum_steps=k) (default 1 = the unchanged single-pass path)
Each batch's ray samples are evaluated in k chunks under no_grad; the full-batch loss (consistency + every soft term) is built on the detached densities, so batch-coupled terms are exact: S3IM, the distortion eps normaliser, ragged box-mode sample means, and the TV subsampling and RNG stream all see the whole batch exactly as with k = 1. Ray-independent terms (plane TV, l21, pose TV) are counted once.
The loss backward gives the direct parameter/pose gradients and the density (and volume-TV tap) gradient. Each chunk is then re-evaluated with a graph and backpropagated with its slice of that gradient, and the accumulated coordinate gradient is pushed through the pose graph.
One clip, optimizer step, scheduler step and pose-gradient sync per batch. Under DDP the loss backward and all chunks but the last run inside no_sync(). Grad-step counting, snapshots, max_grad_steps, snapshot_steps, epoch losses and overhead windows are per optimizer step, as before.
Cost: one extra no-grad forward per step, about 1.3x step time (measured below).
Holdout validation is also evaluated in k chunks when k > 1.
ObjectINR.create_volume(chunk_size=None)
Grid coordinates are built per chunk on the device, so the full N**3 x 3 grid is never materialised on the host (about 24 GB at n=1024).
Chunks are min(5 * N**2, VOLUME_CHUNK_SIZE = 2**19) points. Below the cap (N <= 323) this is the previous chunking, and the output is bitwise identical to the old meshgrid path. Above it, results differ only by BLAS float rounding (about 1e-6).
