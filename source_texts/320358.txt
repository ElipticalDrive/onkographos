Optimal Sampling and Noise Mitigation for Vocal Fold Kinematics in High-Speed Videoendoscopy
Harikrishnan Unnikrishnan,
Rita Patel,
Kevin D. Donohue
Objectives: High-Speed Videoendoscopy glottal area waveforms conflate lateral fold excursion with longitudinal opening, precluding direct estimation of velocity, acceleration, and left-right asymmetry. Displacement waveforms preserve these quantities but are susceptible to spatial quantization and temporal sampling errors. We characterize these errors, evaluate reduction via Kalman smoothing and temporal interpolation, and determine optimal frame rate bounds relative to pixel excursion, speed quotient (SQ), and open quotient (OQ). Methods: Sampling theory was used to derive analytical frame-rate bounds required for kinematic feature fidelity. Kalman smoothing was compared with FFT-based denoising, paired with sinc or spline interpolation. Interpolation was evaluated on 4000 fps recordings (N=55) downsampled and reconstructed. A 16000 fps dataset (N=12) served as reference for noise reduction across 2000-8000 fps downsampled waveforms evaluated on peak displacement, velocity, OQ, and SQ. Results: Sinc interpolation yielded lower median mean absolute error than spline (0.29 vs 0.52 pixels), with 85% of recordings (47/55) versus 45% (25/55) below 0.5 pixels. For kinematic feature estimation (N=12), Kalman-sinc outperformed FFT-sinc in 75% of feature-by-frame-rate pairings. Conclusion: Computing kinematic features without artifacts requires joint consideration of temporal and spatial resolution. Kalman-sinc preprocessing at 8000 fps is recommended for velocity and SQ measures. Peak lateral displacement can be measured at 2000 fps, while OQ requires 4000 fps for typical spatial resolutions (9-14 pixels maximum excursion). Pixel excursion should be reported alongside kinematic measures to contextualize data reliability.

CalibCause: A Quality-Gated, Interpretable and Calibrated Deep Learning Framework for Diabetic Retinopathy Grading
Sohaib Sulman,
Muhammad Atif Saeed,
Akhtar Jamil,
Alaa Ali Hameed,
Roland Aydin
Most deep learning systems for diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading are evaluated on a single data source, with limited evidence of cross-site generalisation, uncertainty calibration, and interpretability. We propose CalibCause, a quality-gated mixture-of-experts framework that combines per-expert visual explanations, label-free image-quality conditioning, knowledge distillation, and calibrated confidence with deferral. We evaluated the framework using 51,310 fundus images from EyePACS, DDR, and APTOS 2019, with IDRiD and DeepDRiD for external testing. Evaluation included pooled testing, leave-one-environment-out experiments, paired bootstrap confidence intervals, and local grade-threshold adaptation. On the pooled test split, the label-free distilled student achieved quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) of 0.863 and accuracy of 0.865 with 27.8 million parameters, 52% fewer than its teacher. Refitting grade thresholds using 200 labelled images increased held-out EyePACS QWK from 0.533 to 0.621 for the label-free model. After vector scaling, expected calibration error ranged from 0.025 to 0.036 across CalibCause variants, and deferring the least confident 20% of images increased student accuracy from 0.865 to 0.923 on the remaining images. These findings demonstrate that quality-aware grading, interpretable expert routing, model compression, and calibrated deferral can be integrated without sacrificing cross-site performance relative to a plain baseline, although reliable deployment still requires local validation and adaptation.

Huseyin Umut Isik,
Ege Mert Balcik,
Duygu Atasoy,
Asutosh Sahu,
Seyda Ertekin,
Sila Kurugol
Airway segmentation from chest CT enables branch-level measurement of airway size and growth, but peripheral bronchi remain hard to segment, particularly in children, whose airways are smaller and for whom few annotated scans exist. Different models recover complementary branches of the airway tree. Their union improves overall tree coverage but also introduces disconnected non-airway objects. We propose anchored fusion (A-Fuse), which takes the voxel-wise union of TfeNet, a retrained MEDPSeg and a reimplemented AirMorph and retains the connected component with greatest overlap with the AirMorph mask. A-Fuse is deployed as an automated web application and we release all code, trained weights and an open-source ITK-SNAP extension for 3D tubular segmentation editing.

DiffUltra: Task-Guided Pathology Synthesis for Large-Scale Real-World Lung Ultrasound Data
Gary Y. Li,
Yu-Cheng Chou,
Li Chen,
Mohsen Zahiri,
Priyam Patel,
Balasundar Raju,
Jochen Kruecker,
Alvin Chen
Despite their scale, large medical imaging datasets may underrepresent clinically relevant pathological presentations. Synthetic data can expand coverage, but findings must remain compatible with surrounding anatomy. This is challenging in ultrasound, where findings resemble surrounding tissue and must be positioned relative to anatomical landmarks. We propose DiffUltra, a task-guided latent diffusion pipeline that models pathological structure and spatial context, and screens synthetic images for downstream utility. DiffUltra conditions generation on structural masks decomposing findings into anechoic, hypoechoic, and hyperechoic subregions. Two placement strategies insert findings into pathology-absent backgrounds or replace existing findings with structurally matched pathology from other patients. A filter trained exclusively on real data retains images whose predictions agree spatially with target boxes. For pleural effusion, frame-level average precision improved by 4.5 percentage points on average and by 6.1 points using only 10% of real data (p < 0.01 for both). Synthetic insertion also yielded larger average precision gains than an existing diffusion-based lesion-insertion method at every scale. At full scale, targeted synthesis improved AUROC for both pathologies by 1.1-3.9 points in an underrepresented anatomical zone and on a held-out acquisition system. These results suggest that anatomically constrained, task-guided synthesis can help address coverage gaps in ultrasound training data.

TheseusSyn: Unified Medical Image Synthesis via Context-Conditioned Masked Autoregression
Shibo Li,
Venet Osmani
High-fidelity medical image synthesis is promising for data-efficient learning and privacy-preserving model development. However, most existing approaches rely on symbolic or label-based conditioning, which constrains generative diversity and limits case-specific contextual fidelity. Inspired by the Ship of Theseus paradox, we propose TheseusSyn, a novel context-conditioned synthesis paradigm that generates images through iterative content replacement. Our framework employs a multi-codebook discrete tokenizer to convert medical images into sequences of token indices, extracted with a global representation token that serves as a contextual constraint. A masked autoregressive generative model then iteratively masks and predicts tokens conditioned on both local neighborhoods and the global context. By progressively replacing image content until the entire token sequence is renewed, TheseusSyn produces synthetic samples that preserve anatomical coherence and semantic consistency while introducing controlled diversity. We evaluate the proposed framework on multiple datasets from MedMNIST by constructing synthetic datasets with varying masking strategies and training ResNet-18 classifiers exclusively on either real or synthetic data. Experimental results demonstrate that classifiers trained on TheseusSyn-generated images achieve competitive performance on official test sets, indicating that the synthesized data preserve diagnostically relevant features and semantic consistency. Systematic analysis of masking strategies reveals their impact on structural fidelity and downstream classification accuracy. These results suggest that context-conditioned masked autoregression provides an effective and generalizable solution for label-free medical image synthesis, offering a scalable alternative to conventional data augmentation and class-conditioned generative models in medical imaging applications.

Report-Derived Labels, an Image Teacher and the Gap to Expert Labels in Knee MRI: An Analysis of One Entry to the RSNA 2026 Knee Abnormality Detection Challenge
Soheil Kooklan
In the RSNA 2026 Knee Abnormality Detection challenge, 58 of 4,407 training studies have expert labels for 12 findings, and the rest have only a radiology report. We compare training targets built from a language-model reading of the reports and from a public image model against two references: the expert labels and the 39,248 study-finding cells that the reports state as present or absent. The report labels and the out-of-fold predictions of the image model reach similar agreement with the expert labels (macro AUC 0.892 and 0.896) but fail on different findings. The report is better for ligament tears and patellofemoral osteoarthritis, which it never calls present when the experts do not; the image model is better for effusion, Baker's cyst, contusion, and synovitis, where the reports call the finding absent, leave it out, or call it present more often than the experts. Their equal mixture reaches 0.927 (gain over the report labels 0.035, 95% interval 0.019 to 0.052), also after removing the studies whose knee side was handled inconsistently. We did not observe a model-level gain from this better target: a 2.5D ResNet50 trained on the report labels alone scored 0.877 on the private leaderboard, after the deadline, against 0.872 for the same model trained on the mixture, and the out-of-fold advantage of the mixture is confounded by a change of code and by possible leakage through the image model's folds. A scanner prevalence prior raised agreement with the reports but not with the experts, so a report-derived reference can reward reporting habits. A rank correction for co-occurring findings gave small out-of-fold gains and no visible leaderboard effect. Our entry, a forked public ensemble with that correction, was displayed at rank 582 of 6,299 on the private leaderboard (0.943, a score shared by 287 teams). All results are exploratory; none of the models is meant for clinical use.

Cross-Device Transfer of a Hospital-Trained Fetal Head Circumference Model to a Low-Cost Handheld Ultrasound Probe
Patrick Gao,
Lara Hart,
Ken Sullivan
Automated fetal head circumference (HC) measurement reaches near-expert accuracy on the HC18 benchmark, but published models are trained and evaluated on hospital-grade equipment. We quantify what happens when such a model is carried to a low-cost handheld probe. A lightweight segmentation network (CSM, 0.13M parameters) was trained on HC18 across five independent runs and applied without modification to images from a $1,350 handheld probe (SonoQue C3), acquired at the same visit as the reference hospital scan in 18 patients. In-domain accuracy was 2.34 +/- 0.43 mm; on handheld images the same model gave 16.12 mm against the hospital record, a 6.9-fold degradation. Input preprocessing, a choice rarely reported, accounted for about a third of that gap: direct resizing gave 49.45 mm against 16.12 mm for an aspect-preserving pipeline. A leave-one-out data-efficiency curve showed that fine-tuning on two images significantly worsened accuracy, and that from four images upward error fell by 0.75 mm per doubling of the local dataset (p = 0.0045), although no training-set size up to 27 images significantly improved on the unmodified model. Much of the residual gap belongs to the reference rather than the model: scored against a manual tracing of the same image the model was already within 10.48 mm, and its disagreement with sonographer calipers on the same frame was a near-constant offset, negative in 10 of 10 cases. A single calibration constant estimated leave-one-out reduced that disagreement from 8.12 mm to 3.49 mm, reaching the intraobserver reproducibility reported for experienced sonographers, but did not improve agreement with the hospital record. Cross-device evaluations should therefore report the input preprocessing used and characterise the precision of their reference standard.

Anton Plietzsch,
Johannes Schiffer

Mohammadreza Barzegaran,
Hamid Jafarkhani

In practice, however, the communication network, transmission delays, and finite-rate communication directly affect the disagreement among agents. On the other hand, agreement among agents does not ensure accuracy, particularly when sensors have different measurement uncertainties. We develop a sampled consensus-plus-innovations framework under realistic wireless network constraints that jointly accounts for inter-agent disagreement and estimation inaccuracy. We derive a computable upper bound on the worst-case steady-state estimation error that captures consensus disagreement due to the communication graph, quantization error, and transmission delay, together with estimation inaccuracy, which accounts for measurement uncertainty against the true state. The proposed method has been evaluated on various test cases, and the results show that it reduces the estimation error while maintaining fewer links and allocating moderate link rates in a short time.

Paul Jürß,
Marija Boberg,

Fynn Foerger
Various applications require accurate 3-D characterization of magnetic fields with high spatial and temporal resolution. Building on our previously introduced spherical magnetic field camera employing 86 three-axis Hall sensors arranged on a spherical surface, we present a novel magnetic-field reconstruction approach based on the magnetic scalar potential and its spherical harmonic expansion. Dynamic Persuasion: Managing Reputation under Censored Feedback
Ahmet E. Arcaklioglu,
Ceyhun Eksin
We study a long-run sender who repeatedly communicates about a state to a sequence of myopic receivers and whose credibility is a public score updated from past interactions. Receivers face a lemon loss and an opportunity loss, and their behavior depends on these only through a single index, the tolerance. Because the score records only detected misrepresentations, it rests above the sender's true honesty rate; we characterize this credibility premium in closed form. In a fluid limit in which the score adjusts slowly, no policy earns more than operating at a single credibility level, and the sender's gain from managing credibility equals the value of the premium: against receivers who correct for imperfect detection, that gain vanishes. At a fixed adjustment speed, every policy is bounded by the fluid value plus a term linear in the speed. We then study three policy classes, linear, (s,S), and threshold, obtain the optimal parameters of the linear and threshold classes in closed form, show how receiver losses shape them, and show that, under an approximation, the optimal (s,S) band collapses to a threshold. Simulations of the dynamics agree with the closed forms and indicate where the single-level conclusion stops: bands pay when switching modes is costly, and cycles pay when demand is seasonal.

Nathaniel Mailhot,
Mohammad Pirani,
Davide Spinello
Communication links influence how strongly a malicious input at one sensor affects a consensus network. We study how a defender can reduce this influence by selecting one additional link after observing the currently attacked sensor. The attacked sensor changes over time, and vulnerability is measured by the trace of a finite-horizon controllability Gramian. A prescribed local epsilon-greedy attacker response rule defines a discounted Markov decision process for the defender. Exact dynamic programming (DP) provides an optimal policy using the full Gramian payoff matrix and attacker transition model. We then introduce approximate dynamic programming (ADP) that learns from sampled attacker transitions and empirical Gramian costs estimated from measured impulse responses. In a two-dimensional intruder tracking example, both policies reduce network-wide root-mean-square tracking error spread by approximately 25% relative to an unmitigated attack.

Sunny Kumar,
Madhavi Parimi,
Shashank Varma,
Sushama Wagh,
Alexsandar Stanković,
Navdeep Singh
When a multi-parameter model is fitted to measured data, only a few parameter combinations are often identifiable, as Fisher Information Matrix eigenvalues span several orders of magnitude. Sensitivity-based tools, including the Fisher Information Matrix and MBAM, reveal this structure analytically, while manifold learning can recover it from sampled trajectories. For an infinite-bus synchronous generator, both approaches show that inertia H and damping D are among the least identifiable parameters on a late post-disturbance window, t in [3,5] s, where six of the eleven parameters suffice to describe the response. We explain this result from the geometry of the dynamics rather than from that of the parameter-to-data map. The rotor swing is a fast fibre that has collapsed onto a two-dimensional normally attracting invariant manifold (NAIM) of the transient fluxes before the window opens. The zeroth-order NAIM is exactly independent of $H$, $D$, $T_{d0}''$ and $T_{q0}''$, four of the five MBAM limits. We prove, under stated smoothness and rank conditions, that each of the four limits is a boundary of the model manifold at a distance equal, to first order in the parameter, to its current profiled speed. The same analysis predicts how identifiability changes with the experiment: neither a larger initial speed deviation nor a load change restores H or D in the late window, whereas the early window [0, 2]s makes H the best-determined parameter. Evaluated in a physical metric fixed by synchrophasor accuracy limits, the late window identifies five parameters rather than six and the early window nine. The spiked-covariance structure of the linearised trajectory map explains why the principal components of whitened, sampled trajectories reproduce the prior-scaled Fisher spectrum, and regression on those components attains the Fisher-predicted recovery error in 113 of 114 parameter scenario pairs.

AV-QTSS: Class-Query Target Sound Spatialization in Multi-Source 360$^\circ$ Videos
Qingyu Luo,
Peng Zhang,
Davide Berghi,
Wenwu Wang,
Philip J. B. Jackson
Spatial audio is central to immersive 360$^\circ$ media, yet existing audio-visual spatialization renders the scene as a whole, leaving no control over an individual source. We introduce Audio-Visual Query-conditioned Target Sound Spatialization (AV-QTSS): given a monaural multi-source mixture, a 360$^\circ$ video and a class query from the 13-class DCASE sound-event ontology, it generates the first-order Ambisonics (FOA) signal of the queried source alone. The omnidirectional mixture contains no explicit spatial cues, so direction has to be inferred from the video, where several objects may sound at once. We propose a query-conditioned dense binding localizer that predicts a spherical probability map over the whole scene, coupled to the renderer so both are optimized jointly. Experiments with one to four concurrent sources show improved localization and spatial accuracy relative to established methods and ablated variants, and the localizer transfers to real 360$^\circ$ recordings after adaptation.

Covariance Learning for Direction-of-Arrival Estimation with Sparse Linear Arrays
Majdoddin Esfandiari,
Esa Ollila,
Geert Leus,
Daniel P. Palomar
Covariance learning (CL) estimates source powers and sensor-noise variance by minimizing the Type-II (marginal) negative log-likelihood of the array measurements. We develop both grid-based and gridless CL direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimators applicable to arbitrary sparse linear array (SLA) geometries. The grid-based DOA estimator minimizes the Type-II objective on an angular grid via successive convex approximation (SCA) and estimates the noise variance by a scalar bisection search. The gridless DOA estimator then refines each grid-based DOA over the continuum using a Newton method. Simulations on a six-sensor nested array, including an underdetermined case with nine sources, show that the proposed DOA estimators outperform state-of-the-art methods, despite jointly estimating the sensor noise variance.

Readiness-Constrained Pre-Islanding Dispatch for Military Microgrids
Aidan Looney,
Annie Weathers,
Nicholas Judson
Military installation energy readiness depends on having sufficient resources to sustain mission-critical loads throughout a prolonged utility outage. This paper develops a method to help operators manage energy resources during normal grid-connected operation while preserving islanded readiness. A robust viability kernel identifies combinations of battery energy, fuel, and load-shifting capability sufficient to sustain critical load under uncertain demand and photovoltaic generation. A probability bound translates stochastic generator start failures and recovery into a deterministic generator count selected to meet a specified availability requirement at islanding. Case studies for a fourteen-day islanding requirement at 0.999 islanding-entry availability show that advance warning of an outage can be used to prepare resources to meet a higher readiness requirement before islanding begins. The case studies further demonstrate that economic-only operation and the evaluated fixed battery reserve policy can fail to sustain critical load for the required duration, whereas enforcing the resource-dependent requirement preserves readiness at a modest increase in modeled grid-connected operating cost. These results demonstrate how active resource management can preserve long-duration energy readiness. The framework also helps commanders understand how readiness changes with operating conditions, even when the available resources remain the same.

In this paradigm, a base station that transmits and receives on the same time-frequency resources can sense continuously with its own downlink waveform while serving uplink traffic. The article concludes discussing emerging use cases and open challenges.

Learning Maximal Regions of Attraction on Compact Sets using Taylor Lyapunov Networks
Matthieu Barreau,
Nicola Bastianello
We consider the problem of learning maximal Lyapunov functions to compute the maximal region of attraction of nonlinear dynamical systems using neural networks. Two obstacles limit existing neural-network approaches: 1) finite-width approximations can fail to preserve the strict Lyapunov inequalities near the equilibrium, where they become arbitrarily weak, and 2) training is necessarily performed on a bounded computational domain for which the classical Lyapunov and Zubov theorems do not directly apply. To address these issues, first we derive Lyapunov and Zubov theorems directly on a prescribed compact computational domain, characterizing the maximal region of attraction within that domain as the solution of a boundary-value PDE. Second, we introduce the Taylor Lyapunov network, an architecture that prescribes the quadratic leading-order term via the system's linearization and represents the higher-order remainder through a structured cubic factorization, guaranteeing that any network in this class is a valid local Lyapunov function by construction. Finally, we prove that Taylor Lyapunov networks are universal approximators of maximal Lyapunov functions on compact domains, and combine these results into an unsupervised, physics-informed training procedure. Numerical experiments on systems of varying dimensions and stability types show that the proposed architecture is efficient at estimating the maximum region of attraction.

Existing automatic evaluation methods often collapse these aspects into coarse scores and are evaluated primarily on synthesized speech. Aamna Al Shehhi,
Ahsan H. Khandoker,

Understanding the structure of complex, nonstationary, high-dimensional time-evolving signals is a central challenge in scientific data analysis. In many domains, such as speech and biomedical signal processing, the ability to learn disentangled and interpretable representations is critical for uncovering latent generative mechanisms. Traditional approaches to unsupervised representation learning, including variational autoencoders (VAEs), often struggle to capture the temporal and spectral diversity inherent in such data. Here we introduce variational decomposition autoencoding (VDA), a framework that incorporates a strong structural bias toward signal decomposition into variational representation learning. VDA is instantiated through variational decomposition autoencoders (DecVAEs), i.e., encoder-only neural networks that perform latent autoencoding at the level of distributions to learn multiple latent subspaces aligned with time-frequency characteristics. We evaluate DecVAEs on simulated data and three publicly available scientific datasets, spanning speech recognition, dysarthric speech evaluation, and emotional speech classification. DecVAEs improve on VAE-based methods in disentanglement quality and robustness, and their latent subspaces become factor-specific when the generative factors have distinct spectral signatures. These findings position decomposition-aware architectures as potential tools for extracting structured representations from speech, and potentially from other dynamic signals with factors exhibiting distinct spectral signatures, with applications in clinical diagnostics, human-computer interaction, and adaptive neurotechnologies.

Francesca Lanzillotta,

In many learning tasks, certain requirements on the processing of individual data samples can arguably be formalized, in an intelligible way, as strict constraints of the underlying optimization problem, rather than by means of arbitrary penalties that often require heavy trial-and-error tuning. In this paper we show that, in these scenarios, learning can be carried out exploiting a sequential penalty method that allows to explicitly deal with constraints. For the proposed algorithm we show that, under classical assumptions and in the considered learning scenario, penalty subproblems can provably be solved in finite time with suitable guarantees of approximate stationarity in expectation; we also show that, when the latter condition is guaranteed for the subproblems, the overall sequential scheme possesses almost sure convergence properties to KKT-stationary points of (general) constrained problems. Finite-Sample FDR Control for Greedy Aggregation over Networks
Mehrdad Pournaderi
Distributed multiple testing asks $N$ sites, each holding p-values for its own hypotheses, to control the false discovery rate (FDR) of the discoveries made across the whole network while communicating only a small number of bits. Greedy interval aggregation (Pournaderi and Xiang, IEEE TSIPN, 2023) meets the communication budget but controls FDR only asymptotically, and its FDR can exceed the target at finite sample sizes; the same interval counts both select the rejection regions and calibrate the stopping rule, which biases the selected interval densities upward. We propose \emph{budgeted BONuS-GA}: each site mixes synthetic uniform p-values into its data, the center ranks candidate p-value intervals across sites using the pooled counts, and each site's false discoveries are estimated from its own synthetic counts under a per-site share of the level. The procedure needs no knowledge of the null proportions, uses every p-value for both selection and inference, and controls $FDR\le\alpha$ at every sample size for any fixed assignment of hypotheses to sites, assuming only that the null p-values are independent uniforms, independent of the non-null p-values. It keeps the original $O(\sqrt m\log m)$ communication, $m$ being the total number of p-values, when $N=O(\sqrt m)$. We also give a sample-splitting alternative, cross-fit greedy aggregation, with finite-sample control under a random-site model, and we quantify the selection bias behind the original procedure's failure. In a monitoring-network simulation the budgeted procedure retains most of the power of its heuristic counterpart at moderate-to-large site sizes.
