## Overview

A desktop application that reads a folder of DICOM slices, assembles them into a single 3D volume, and renders it interactively with GPU ray casting.

The interesting problem here isn't drawing one image, it's that a CT series is **volumetric scalar data**, not a picture. 142 slices of 512×512 is 37.2 million voxels, and every one of them has to map to a colour and an opacity before you see anything at all. Get that mapping wrong and you render either a solid grey block or nothing. So the real work is the **transfer function**: deciding which intensity ranges are bone, which are soft tissue, and which should be invisible, then letting the user swap between those interpretations live without reloading a 571 MB dataset.

![Volume rendering](screenshots/01_volume_rendering.png)

*A 142-slice head CT loaded as one volume, 512×512×142, 37,224,448 voxels, rendered on the GPU. The status bar reports the loaded dimensions and the active render mode.*

## Tissue presets

Each preset swaps the opacity and colour transfer functions, so different anatomy comes forward from the **same** voxel data. Nothing is reloaded, only the intensity-to-appearance mapping changes.
