3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can achieve efficient novel view synthesis but relies on point cloud initialization using Structure-from-Motion (SfM) or Multi-View Stereo (MVS). Due to motion blur, de-focus, or poor illumination, the reconstructed clouds are often sparse and noisy, leading to limited coverage and unstable convergence. In light of these observations, we propose the EDN-Gaussian method, which enhances visibility via joint covariance–opacity adjustment. It employs edge-directed adaptive densification with anisotropic narrowing and growth-rate control, and introduces a split regularizer to constrain the newborn Gaussians by their parent Gaussians.Vast experiments on the GS-Blur and Deblur-NeRF datasets demonstrate significant improvements in geometric accuracy and rendering quality with degraded inputs.
