Background
Planes and heightfields are environment geometry: static, never colliding
with each other, and not a good fit for the per-body bounding-box broad
phase:
Plane: its bound is infinite, so in the AABB matrix it would
"overlap" every body and add a dead row/column to every test.
Heightfield: finite but large. One AABB around the whole terrain
filters almost nothing; a body's AABB has to be mapped to the grid
cells beneath it.
So neither goes in the self-collision row set. Each is tested against
every body by its own pass.
Proposal
Plane
Environment data: a small fixed list of planes (normal n, offset d)
from scene config, not per-body rows.
A per-body LinearTraversal pass: for each body and each plane, do a
quick reject with the world AABB (n·c − d > |n|·h), then compute exact
depth from the body's support function in −n (from the convex-shapes
issue; until then, from the swept box directly).
Output per (body, plane) contact: depth, normal (= n), point.
Heightfield
Environment data: grid dimensions, cell size, origin, heights
(read-only, shared).
Per body: world AABB → covered cell range (clamped), then test each
cell's two triangles (or a column approximation to start) against the
body's convex shape.
The cell count per body varies at runtime. Start with a fixed cap per
body plus an overflow flag, matching the capacity policy in the
pair-list issue.
Not required first
Neither needs a two-sided archetype × archetype traversal: both are a
per-body pass reading shared environment data. If that traversal lands
later (vehicle vs environment archetypes), these can move onto it.
Acceptance
Plane: a body resting on or penetrating a plane reports the correct
depth/normal; group/mask filtering applies (environment gets its own
group bits).
Heightfield: matches a brute-force test over all cells; a flat
heightfield matches the equivalent plane.
Cost scales with bodies × (planes + covered cells), not with the
terrain size.
