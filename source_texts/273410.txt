The problem
Dragging up or down in the twin viewer stopped dead at the top and bottom of the object. Horizontal spin went all the way round, but the underside of a model was unreachable.
Nothing in our code narrowed it — there is no minPolarAngle, maxPolarAngle or camera.up set anywhere in dashboard/src. OrbitControls clamps the polar angle to [0, π] about a fixed up vector. It is a turntable, which is exactly what it is designed to be. Raising maxPolarAngle past π does not help: with the up vector pinned the camera flips at the pole instead of rolling through it.
So the limit is the control type, and the control type is what this changes.
The change
The twin viewer now uses drei's ArcballControls — already present in the installed drei 9.122 over three-stdlib 2.36.1, so no new dependency. An arcball has no pole: it tumbles in any direction and comes round underneath.
Three things were not drop-in:
Pivot. The arcball keeps its pivot in internal gizmo state, so fitToModel's target.set(...) would have moved nothing. setTarget is the real API.
Roll on reset. Free tumbling leaves camera.up wherever the drag ended and lookAt reads it, so fitToModel restores it first. Without that, Reset View lands in the right place at an arbitrary roll — which reads as a broken reset.
The arcball's own gizmos. It draws circles and axis lines over the scene; this viewer already has a view cube, so they are hidden once at mount. That holds: activateGizmos (called on every interaction) only changes their opacity, and makeGizmos swaps the group's children without touching its visibility.
Untouched on purpose:
FORGE-250's drag-to-pose suppression flips enabled, which an arcball carries too.
The view cube keeps working — GizmoHelper tests 'minPolarAngle' in controls and 'getTarget' in controls, matches neither, and falls through to defaultControls.target, which the arcball exposes. It also skips the orbit-only camera.up reset, which is the behaviour being removed.
FacePickerViewer, UrdfPreviewPanel and StructureView keep OrbitControls. Face picking reads better with a stable up, and the URDF path is the one place that already does its own Z-up→Y-up correction.
Trade-off
Arcball rotation is grab-point dependent: near the centre it tumbles, near the edge it rolls. That is the standard CAD free-orbit feel and the only way past the pole, but it is a genuinely different feel from a turntable. If it turns out to be disliked in use, the honest fallback is OrbitControls and the vertical limit — there is no third option that gives both.

npm run build and npm run lint clean (3 pre-existing warnings, none in the changed file).

Still needs eyes-on in a browser, which jsdom cannot cover since drei mocks the controls away: tumble past vertical, click view-cube faces, Reset View after tumbling, and a robot joint drag.

…RGE-612) (#1128)

Reverts the arcball of FORGE-610 (#1121) and brings back the ground grid
FORGE-34 hid, because together they are what the viewer was missing.

FORGE-610 read the complaint as "rotation stops at the pole" and swapped
OrbitControls for an arcball to remove the stop. That was the smaller
half. The viewer had no visible ground at all -- the grid has been
commented out since MET-623 -- so a model floated in a void with no
reference for the motion, and an orbit read as the object wobbling
rather than the view moving around something standing on a floor. The
arcball made that worse: free tumbling abandons the fixed up vector, and
that vector is what makes a workplane read as a plane instead of a sheet
pitching about underneath the model.

So: a turntable. Nothing is clamped beyond what a turntable implies --
the azimuth is unbounded, and the polar range is the full [0, pi], from
directly overhead to directly underneath, so every face of a model is
reachable. The underside is reached by continuing down past the horizon
rather than by rolling over the top, and the model is never left rolled.
Both angle props are OrbitControls' own defaults, written out because
they are the point of the change rather than an accident of it.

The grid is two GridHelper layers, minor cells and major sections, in
the colours the old one used. They are real line segments with a
LineBasicMaterial, not drei Grid's procedural lines anti-aliased with
screen-space derivatives -- that shader is what shimmered during an
orbit and forced the hiding, and it cannot happen to geometry. Size and
spacing still scale with the model, so the grid sits under whatever is
loaded rather than at a fixed spot the model may be nowhere near
(MET-620).

Two judgement calls worth naming. The robot-description path computes no
modelBounds, so it gets no grid: a floor from a guess would cut through
the robot, where the invisible shadow catcher can guess safely because a
wrong height there costs only a misplaced shadow. And the division
counts are capped -- near-constant for any model that sizes the grid
from its radius, with the cap biting only on a very small one, where the
20-unit floor would otherwise ask for thousands of lines.

Four mutation checks, each caught by the intended test: clamping the
sweep to pi/2, removing the grid, swapping back to the arcball, and
dropping the no-bounds guard. Resolves the grid half of FORGE-34.
