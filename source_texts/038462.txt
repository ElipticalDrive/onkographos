Web-based large-scale terrain visualization is often limited by traditional graphics APIs in computation and data transfer. This paper presents an efficient parsing and rendering method using WebGPU, covering data organization, compute pipeline scheduling, and rendering optimization. A block-based quadtree index is designed for GPU batch reading, with elevation and texture data rearranged by spatial proximity. Compute shaders handle frustum culling, continuous LOD calculation, and dynamic vertex generation, offloading geometry processing from CPU to GPU. Multi-level index buffers and instanced drawing reduce draw calls. Experiments on SRTM30m and NASADEM datasets show that, against a WebGL 2.0 baseline, our method maintains 58–62 FPS at 10 million triangles, cuts initial loading time by ∼62%, and reduces memory usage by ∼41%. This work offers a feasible path for high-performance web-based geospatial visualization.
