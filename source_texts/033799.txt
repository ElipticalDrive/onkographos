Neural 3D scene representation methods have enabled significant advances in photorealistic reconstruction and novel view synthesis, but often at the cost of high computational and energy demands. In particular, Neural Radiance Field (NeRF)–based approaches rely on dense volumetric sampling and repeated neural network inference, resulting in long training times and substantial power consumption. Such characteristics limit their scalability and suitability for sustainability-driven applications that require frequent updates, long-term deployment, or operation under energy constraints. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently emerged as an efficient alternative that replaces implicit neural fields with explicit Gaussian primitives and rasterization-style rendering, achieving real-time performance with significantly reduced computational overhead. However, this efficiency comes with reduced robustness under sparse-view supervision, where limited input data leads to geometric instability and degraded reconstruction quality. To address these limitations, recent extensions such as Few-Shot Gaussian Splatting (FSGS) and Co-Regularized Gaussian Splatting (CoR-GS) introduce principled strategies for initialization, densification, pruning, and supervision that improve stability without reintroducing computationally expensive neural rendering. This paper presents a concise review and unified discussion of Gaussian-based sparseview reconstruction methods from the perspective of energyefficient and sustainable AI. By interpreting recent advances through a quality–energy trade-off lens, we highlight how explicit geometric representations enable scalable 3D perception while aligning with clean energy and sustainability goals. The analysis underscores the role of energy-aware design in advancing responsible and deployable AI systems, in direct alignment with the objectives of IEEE BHTC 2026.
