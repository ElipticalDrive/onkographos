Abstract
The thickness of a part is one of the fundamental parameters in machine design, requiring a thickness definition that is suitable for complex shapes and can be visualized in an easy-to-understand manner. Conventional methods define thickness using the maximum inscribed sphere, which causes the corners of the part to be judged as thin. In this study, we proposed a method in which the thickness of a point inside a part is defined by the width of the maximum inscribed cube encompassing the point. Using this method, the thickness of a part can be defined more appropriately than with conventional methods. Software was developed to convert the part geometry into a voxel model and calculate the thickness value of the center point of each voxel. Furthermore, a volume rendering technique was developed to properly display the voxel model with the obtained thickness values. Software was implemented to visualize the thickness distribution inside a polyhedral model of a machine part, and its effectiveness was verified by applying it to several parts.
