The Marching Cubes algorithm is a popular high-resolution isosurface extraction method used in volume data visualization. However, it is relatively computationally intensive making real-time operation on normal workstations a difficult goal when applied to large datasets. One solution is to transform the serial algorithm into a vector-parallel algorithm designed to exploit the potential computing power supplied by a supercomputer. In this paper, we present an implementation of the Marching Cubes that considers the inherent parallelism in the algorithm as well as the specific characteristics of the pipelined CPU of a vector-parallel supercomputer (Cray C90). In our approach, we vectorize two time-consuming operations in the Marching Cubes. The first operation is the interpolation of the intersection points between the isosurface and the cube edges.
