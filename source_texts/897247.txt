Fourth step of the renderer milestone: HDR rendering and post-processing.
Frame order
Shadows, then a depth prepass, then GTAO, then shading into an RGBA16F target (opaque, sky, blended), then TAA (optional), then bloom, then tonemapping into the caller's target.
What's in it
Depth prepass: uses the same vertex shader as shading, so shading tests depth without writing it and has no overdraw.
GTAO:
Normals are reconstructed from depth.
Horizons are sampled with bilinear depth. Snapping samples to pixel centres made grazing planes darken themselves.
A depth-aware blur clamps the result after averaging.
It darkens indirect diffuse light, and indirect specular light through specular occlusion.
Bloom: a 13-tap downsample with Karis average, then a tent upsample. The result is normalized and mixed in, so it doesn't add energy; the white-furnace test still passes.
TAA (off by default):
Halton jitter applies only to the shading projection.
History is reprojected with depth and the previous camera.
Variance clipping in YCoCg.
Final pass: exposure, ACES tonemapping and sRGB encoding.
API: PostProcessSettings, setPostProcessSettings(), resetTemporalHistory(), temporalJitter(), jitterProjection().
Viewer: --taa option.
ADR 0016, English and Turkish docs.
Verification
MSVC Debug/Release and clang-cl Debug build with zero warnings; all tests pass, benchmarks included. GPU tests ran with zero validation errors.
New GPU tests:
AO darkens the ground next to a sphere's contact but leaves open ground unchanged (with AO off, both match).
Bloom spreads light outside an emissive sphere, and stops when turned off.
TAA creates partially covered edge pixels while flat interiors stay unchanged.
Unit tests: the jitter sequence (range, period, mean) and the exact pixel shift of jitterProjection.
Existing tests:
drawCalls now includes the prepass, so expectations changed from 1 to 2.
Two tests that check exact black clear pixels turn bloom off, since bloom correctly spreads a little light there.
Debugging GTAO: I dumped a pixel column to find false occlusion on far grazing ground. Bilinear depth sampling fixed it.
I checked the viewer's screenshot visually: contact darkening, subtle bloom, no artifacts.
Known limitations (also in ADR 0016)
TAA uses camera motion only; per-object motion vectors are planned in ROADMAP M3e. That's why it is off by default.
Exposure is still manual; there is no auto exposure yet.

The scene renderer now shades into an internal HDR target and finishes
each frame with post-processing.

- Depth prepass (same vertex shader as shading, so shading tests depth
  without writing it and has no overdraw), alpha-masked variant.
- GTAO from the prepass depth: 2 slices x 4 steps per side, normals from
  depth, horizons sampled with bilinear depth (pixel-centre snapping made
  grazing planes self-occlude), depth-aware blur that clamps afterwards.
  Applied to indirect diffuse and, via specular occlusion, indirect
  specular light; not to blended surfaces.
- Energy-preserving bloom: 13-tap downsample with Karis average, tent
  upsample summing all levels, normalized and lerped in (the white
  furnace test still passes).
- Optional TAA: Halton jitter on the shading projection only, closest-
  depth reprojection with the previous camera (homogeneous, sky
  included), YCoCg variance clipping, reversible-tonemap blending.
- Final pass: exposure, ACES, sRGB encoding into the caller's target.
- PostProcessSettings / setPostProcessSettings() / resetTemporalHistory(),
  temporalJitter() and jitterProjection(); gltf_viewer --taa.
- Unit tests for jitter, GPU tests for creases, bloom spread and TAA edge
  smoothing; ADR 0016, EN/TR docs.
