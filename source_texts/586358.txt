Summary
Motion blur simulates the image smearing that occurs when a camera shutter is open while a subject or the camera itself is moving. In games it can improve perceived smoothness at lower frame rates and strengthen the sense of speed. It is a commonly requested toggle because player preference varies significantly — many players disable it entirely.
Behavior
Camera (screen-space) motion blur is the standard real-time approach:
The difference between the current and previous frame's view-projection matrix is used to compute a per-pixel velocity vector
Each pixel's color is sampled along its velocity vector and averaged over a configurable number of samples
The result is composited onto the scene
Tunable parameter:
Strength — scales the velocity vectors, controlling the maximum blur length
Runtime Requirement
Motion blur must be togglable on/off at runtime from a Graphics settings menu without restart. Strength must also be runtime-adjustable. Given that this effect is divisive, the default should be off.
Acceptance Criteria
Motion blur can be enabled and disabled at runtime with no restart
Blur strength is player-tunable at runtime
Fast camera rotation produces visible directional smearing when enabled
When disabled, no performance cost is incurred and output is identical to the unblurred frame
Default state is off
Notes
Screen-space camera motion blur requires storing the previous frame's view-projection matrix. Per-object motion blur (which also requires a velocity buffer render target) is out of scope for the initial implementation.
