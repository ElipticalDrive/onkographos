Sprites, particles, sphere effects, projectile beams and the sky can now be drawn in world space through the real view and projection matrices, behind graphics.world_space_billboards (default off). BaseRenderer builds a WorldBillboard next to each legacy billboard at the same index, so both go through the same stable sort, and the legacy list stays the picking input. Quads face the view with its roll taken out and are drawn through glbillbshader with depth test on and depth writes off. They keep the old blend modes, palette lookup and sprite pack variants, with fog by eye distance. The new glskyshader does the sky plane intersection per pixel, outdoors on a full view quad and indoors on the sky faces. External world targets always take the world space path, so in VR it's on regardless of the flag. The combined culling frustum isn't cheap here, because the head can turn away from the party camera that prepare culls with. It stays a follow-up.
Verified with check_style and the shader lint for GLSL 410 core and 320 es. Run_UnitTest passed 446 and Run_GameTest_Headless_Parallel passed 415, both with the MM7 data and with OE_BUILD_VR off and on. A throwaway screenshot harness covered 42 scenes: Emerald Island day, night, foggy and with fog off, looking up and down, lightning bolts and fireballs outdoors and in Castle Navan, and Celeste's sky faces. With the flag off all 42 are byte-identical to master. With it on, the differences are:
Sprite edges differ by a pixel here and there, from real 3D quads against the old integer-centred screen quads and the 2n depth mapping.
Fireball spheres are now hidden where they pass through walls and floors, where the old path drew them flat at the depth of their nearest vertex.
The sky's horizon fade now follows the ray elevation rather than screen rows. Looking up shows a deeper blue, and looking down shows less cloud above the horizon. The cloud pattern is a little different away from the centre of the view, and the same goes for Celeste's indoor sky. Fog, the foggy-weather sky and the horizon cut with fog off match.
Lightning bolts, particles and decorations look the same.
On the PICO runtime, picked per process through XR_RUNTIME_JSON with the registry untouched, issue_427b logs 42 world billboards for each eye. Eye images dumped by a throwaway build show sprites in both eyes against the level geometry. The only offset between the eyes is the PICO's ±5° display cant, under which each eye turns the quads to its own view. The in-headset check is still to do.

Sprites, particles, sphere effects, projectile beams and the sky were
projected to the screen on the CPU from the party camera and drawn through
an orthographic matrix, which can't work for the two eyes of a headset.
With graphics.world_space_billboards set they're drawn through the view and
projection matrices instead. External world targets always get the world
space path, so the VR eyes use it whatever the flag says. The flag defaults
to off, and with it off the stock path draws exactly what it drew before.

BaseRenderer builds a WorldBillboard next to each RenderBillboardD3D, at the
same index, in TransformBillboard, MakeParticleBillboardAndPush and
BillboardSphereSpellFX. Sharing the index, the world list goes through the
same stable sort of pSortedBillboardRenderListD3D, and the legacy list stays
the picking input. A sprite is a quad anchored at the bottom centre of its
world position, sized by its frame scale and mirrored by its flag, with the
tint and opacity TransformBillboard computes. RenderBillboard now keeps the
sprite scale for this, since screenspace_projection_factor has the view
depth folded in. A particle keeps the rotation and the 12 unit offset of its
screen space quad, in world units. A sphere effect keeps its whole world
space triangle and the GPU clips it. Quads face the current view with the
roll taken out, so sprites stay upright in the world when a headset tilts.
They're drawn back to front through glbillbshader with depth test on and
depth writes off, with the same blend modes, palette lookup and sprite pack
variants as before, and fogged by their distance from the eye. A projectile
beam becomes a quad between its world end points, 20 units on each side of
its axis as before, turned across the line of sight.

The sky gets a shader of its own. Outdoors it's drawn on a quad covering
the view, indoors on the sky faces themselves, and for each pixel the shader
intersects the view ray with the sky plane like DrawOutdoorSky and
DrawIndoorSky do on the CPU, with the same plane heights, tilt, horizon
offset, animation, tint and fog. The fade into the horizon colour now goes
by the elevation of the ray, between the elevations the legacy fade spans on
a level view, so it stays put in the world when the view pitches.

The forced perspective fog setup moves into forcedPerspectiveFog for the
sky shader to share. The VR log line for each eye now says how many world
billboards it drew.
```
