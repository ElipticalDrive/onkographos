Just wanted to point out that the unsigned 64-bit integer 0xAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAB passes this test, but it's also the modular multiplicative inverse of 3 when the modulus is 2 to the 64. That's a particularly thorny constant for a lot of tests on SplitMix-style generators, because every third step is equivalent to a step up by just 1. The test I've been using to find gamma values uses four population counts, and either takes the worst one (furthest from half of the bits 0, half of the bits 1) or sums all counts' absolute differences from 32 set bits. The counts are of the gamma, its Gray code (gamma ^ gamma >> 1), the modular multiplicative inverse of the gamma (which always exists because the gamma is always odd, and the modulus is a power of two), and the Gray code of the modular multiplicative inverse of the gamma. You can find quite a few gammas this way that work, even without finding duplicates, with the right way of going from an index to a gamma. Here's my code: https://github.com/tommyettinger/juniper/blob/main/src/main/java/com/github/tommyettinger/random/EnhancedRandom.java#L1697-L1729 The first 268435456 odd inputs (this also tolerates given only even inputs) won't have duplicates found even if the threshold is the strictest, 1. I haven't looked into the average or maximum number of iterations this takes to find a gamma that matches threshold 1, though. It never was an issue for me.

I was really thinking more about parameterizing PCGs than SplitMix in my write-up. I was mostly thinking of SplitMix as a hash function testing stepping stone. But anyway: one thing that could be interesting for the SplitMix is the target case would be to reject any value that are too small or too large. Like say top two bits are either 00 or 11. The idea being of sticking to reasonable candidates for a low discrepancy sequence (which a common recommendation is something like limiting to a normalized value on [0.3, 0.7])
