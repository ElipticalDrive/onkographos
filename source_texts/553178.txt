additive blending. A dense specimen therefore accumulated radiance without
limit: the front of the volume never occluded the back, and a bright channel
washed the others out instead of sitting in front of them.

volumetric compositing would reduce to storage order without adding a depth cue.

What I checked and found clean
Attr plumbing on all five entry points. absorption rides **attrs into write_gsplats in every variant; io/_compiler/gsplat_assembly.py:432-443 only stamps the 1.0 default when the key is absent, so explicit values survive, and io/_compiler/node_common.py:284-287 validates through validate_absorption (accepted range [0, ∞), so κ=5 is fine). Nothing is silently swallowed, including demo_gsplats_3d_kidney_multichannel_toggles.py:484 where fill_sigma / extend_to_all / dim_order are named params.
The wrapper cases, which were my main worry. For lod_group=True (core/group/gsplats_pipeline/lod_dispatch.py:85-106) and for the graft path demo_gsplats_recipes_tribolium.py:509 takes on the nested tiles/overview/adaptive files (gsplats_pipeline/from_io.py:133-145), absorption lands on the wrapper only — the child_<i> / part leaves get the stamped 1.0. That still resolves correctly: data/attrs-composer.ts:69 composes absorption multiplicatively root→leaf with identity 1.0, blending_mode is nearest-setter-wins, and rendering/node-factory/create-gsplats-node.ts:37 reads the composed attrs. So no child falls back to additive or κ=1, and the layer panel's κ slider stays consistent because the wrapper is also the layer=True node.
κ=5 with opacity 1.0 doesn't degenerate. w(a) = −ln(1−a) and ALPHA_CLAMP apply to the per-splat colour alpha and are gated on 4-column colours (rendering/gsplat-geometry.ts:515); these demos pass RGB or a colormap, so that branch is inert. S(τ) takes its series branch below τ<1e-3, so nothing divides by zero at either end.
Nothing in the test suites pins demo blending. demo-validation.spec.ts covers packages/luxar/examples/*.py, all-examples-smoke-test.spec.ts covers datasets/examples/*, the blending/κ suites build synthetic fixtures, and no Playwright snapshot is derived from these demos. So no baseline breaks — though it also means nothing would catch a silent revert.
Hover picking is unaffected. volumetric is deliberately excluded from surface-pick depth (rendering/picking/picking-system.ts:665-688) and keeps additive-style brightest-wins picking, so the tooltip behaviour in ct_totalsegmentator and celegans_tracking is exactly what it was.
Scope of the sweep. No bioimaging gsplat demo was missed; the interop demos correctly stay on normal (alpha-over is the right call for photogrammetric imports).
One non-issue worth stating so it doesn't get "fixed" later: absorption=1.0 is already the stamped default (typing_utils/constants.py:39), so 19 of the 20 new kwargs are no-ops and only the capsid's 5.0 changes any pixel. I'd keep them anyway — being explicit at the call site is worth more than the saved line.
Findings
1. demo_gsplats_3d_milky_way_dust.py:326, demo_gsplats_3d_tng_cosmic_web.py:345 — minor. The PR description's "Not changed" section says the two astronomy gsplat demos "already use volumetric compositing." They don't — both are still blending_mode="additive". The demo that's already volumetric is the Gaia points demo (κ=1.3), not these. So the stated rationale for excluding them doesn't hold, and dense dust / cosmic-web fields are exactly the case where unbounded additive blows out. 2. demo_gsplats_2d_cmu1_pathology.py:509-517, demo_gsplats_2d_codex_pancreas.py:548-559 — minor. These two are strictly 2D (Dimension("x"/"y", display=True) and control_type="ortho" at :434-442 and :476-484), so every splat sits on one view-depth plane and the sort kernel takes its degenerate branch — !(zMax > zMin) ⇒ identity ordering (wasm/typescript/depth-sort.ts:73-82, Rust twin wasm/rust/src/depth_sort.rs:128-140). Volumetric therefore composites in fit/storage order with no depth meaning, and the 12 coplanar CODEX channel nodes tie in the cross-node renderOrder pass too, so channel occlusion order is arbitrary-but-fixed and toggling one layer changes the look of the others. For a 2D fit the additive sum is the reconstruction, so there's fidelity to lose and no depth cue to gain. I'd leave the 2D pair on additive.
3. demo_gsplats_lod_tribolium.py:327, demo_gsplats_lod_embryo_line.py:304, demo_gsplats_recipes_tribolium.py:509 — minor. BLENDABLE_MODES = {additive, luminous} in scene/lod-fade.ts:49 gates both LOD anti-popping mechanisms — the coverage cross-fade (isBlendableSubtree) and the streaming 1/e(k) energy compensation (:142). volumetric is excluded, and docs/guides/specs/VOLUMETRIC_BLENDING_SPEC.md:566-580 calls both out as open follow-ups ("a volumetric leaf streaming a partial ladder gets NO compensation — the brightening pop the mechanism removes returns for volumetric"). So the three demos whose subject is LOD now do hard level swaps and get the brightening pop back while a ladder streams. lod_tribolium colour-codes its levels deliberately so a hard swap is arguably fine there, but recipes_tribolium builds ladders for every recipe and lod_embryo_line places 100 laddered embryos. Worth an actual look at those three before landing, or keeping them additive until the fade predicate is split.
4. Tuning breadth — minor. τ = κ·opacity·intensity (rendering/materials/gsplat/shader-glsl.ts:521), and 18 of the 20 demos keep their additive-era dimming (scale_intensity(0.1), or 0.03 in the two tribolium demos). Still describes changed demos as additive: cells3d "additive blending", cryoem_virus "additive HDR rendering", ct_totalsegmentator "additive HDR rendering". The in-code comments were updated but the demo README wasn't; :670 (milky_way_dust) stays correct since that demo is unchanged.

* feat(demos): adopt the preferred viewer appearance for four gsplat demos

Bakes in the layer/HDR settings that were found to work best when driving
these scenes interactively, so a fresh `luxar demo run` opens on the good

- milky_way_dust: volumetric blending at absorption 0.3 (near dust softly

* feat: make ACES the recommended tone mapping and stop warning about it

ACES is the right choice for almost every scene: its filmic highlight
rolloff is what keeps bright structure from clipping flat. The tree was
built around the opposite assumption — 17 demos pinned Neutral, and the
compiler scolded anyone who did not.

The LUT tone-mapping warning now fires ONLY when the author set no
tone_mapping at all. Before, its predicate was `!= "Neutral"`, so it also
fired on an explicit "ACES" — nagging about a deliberate decision while
its own text spoke of "the viewer's *default*", which is only what you get
by saying nothing. Setting any value, ACES included, now silences it. Five
parametrized cases cover the explicit-choice path (it had none, and
explicit ACES demonstrably warned before this change).

Sixteen demos move from Neutral to an explicit ACES, and the comments and
README lines that justified Neutral are rewritten to match. Verified on
the LOD Tribolium demo, whose per-level debug colours (green -> amber ->
red) are the case most at risk from a hue shift: they stay clearly
distinguishable, and the render is near-identical to Neutral.

Two deliberate exceptions, both flagged for review rather than assumed:

- demo_gsplats_3d_tribolium_embryo keeps Neutral. It was tuned to Neutral
  explicitly and that choice is the whole point of its exposure=1.97
  pairing; overriding it here would discard a verified setting.
- demo_flywire_connectome and demo_gsplats_2d_cmu1_pathology carry notes
  recording WHY they used to pin Neutral (luminous glow blowout; faithful
  H&E stain colour), so the reasoning survives if either needs reverting.

Docs follow: CLAUDE.md, the HDR guide, and the ViewerConfig.tone_mapping
docstring now recommend ACES and set it explicitly, keeping Neutral for
the narrower case where a LUT carries an exact scientific colour encoding.

Also records the first real-data verdict on `normal` blending in
GSPLAT_DEPTH_SORTING_SPEC.md, which listed it as an open product risk to
be settled by evaluation: positive on the Tribolium light-sheet volume,
with the caveats that it is one dataset at one viewpoint and that the
dominant mechanism was the peak-vs-ray-integral projection rather than the
coverage-alpha behaviour the risk reasoned about.
