What to build
The brick pool grows when an edit needs more capacity, and never exceeds the configured memory budget.
Sizing. Initial capacity is the required bricks plus 25% headroom. Growth is by 1.5× through a full rebuild candidate.
Budget check. Before a replacement pool is allocated, the complete predicted peak (old capacity, new capacity, and staging) is checked against the configured budget. Exceeding it produces a defined, reported convergence failure, replacing the exhaustion failure from the incremental-convergence ticket when growth is possible.
Bounded rebuilds. Retained rebuild candidates are bounded, so superseded work cannot accumulate allocations.
Observations. Growth observations are exposed: trigger revision, old and new capacity, predicted and actual peak, and rebuild time.
Acceptance criteria
CPU tests on the convergence owner cover growth planning, the predicted-peak calculation, and deterministic rejection when growth would exceed the budget.
CPU tests show that superseded growth candidates release their allocations and that retained rebuild candidates stay within the bound.
A GPU adapter test performs an actual pool-growth installation, and its observations afterwards match the oracle.
Growth observations are available to tests and measurement, separately from ordinary edit observations.
