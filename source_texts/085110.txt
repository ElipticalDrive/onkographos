Question
Hello all, I am currently working on a PoC for a game idea I have.
Basically I am going to have a voxel terrain that utilizes the dual contouring algorithm, but I am having a really, really hard time understanding how to apply it to a voxel world.
What I keep hearing is that for every edge that changes signs(intersects the isosurface) you create a vertex at the position that minimizes the QEF. But the only problems I can't seem to get my head around are these: Where do I get the isosurface to begin with? Isn't the point of the meshing algo to create the surface based on the voxels?

I designed my own algorithm from scratch. Here's a blog post I wrote about it forever ago: https://deftware.blogspot.com/2014/06/voxel-polygonization-and-game-engines.html
Basically it just creates lone vertices between solid/empty voxels at the center of their shared voxel face. Then it brute-force logically deduces how to connect up all the vertices in 3 passes to create all the edges of the isomesh. Then lastly it generates triangles from loops of 3-verts/3-edges. Could likely be sped up further.
The result was a mesh that has a bunch of 45 degree angles and whatnot, which is what I was going for, but I also managed to use signed distance functions to generate a distance field and perturb vertex positions of a generated isomesh to smooth our the geometry. Good times!

I found these 3 pages really helpful:
This one explains what dual contouring is https://www.boristhebrave.com/2018/04/15/dual-contouring-tutorial/
This one explains signed distance fields: https://www.alanzucconi.com/2016/07/01/signed-distance-functions/#part3
And, since I'm using Unity, this github repo was really helpful: https://github.com/elemel/qef-csharp
which is a C# port of https://github.com/nickgildea/qef
Unfortunately I didn't get the QEF to give me vertices that actually lie within the voxel. But it is really helpful to have a working implementation to try it for yourself.
