The scan renders upside down in follow mode, uprights the instant a finger touches it, and turns over again on the double-tap back to following.
That is one bug, not three. OrbitCamera::view() has two branches:
```
if (following_) return glm::inverse(device_pose_);                      // carries the device's roll
return glm::lookAt(target_ + direction()*distance_, target_, kWorldUp);  // imposes world +Y
```
The turntable drops roll by construction, so the drag is not correcting the roll — it is discarding it. 7ad32c2 said exactly this and then took the drag's testimony anyway, one level up.
The zero is in the wrong place
The viewport correction's zero sat at raw 0 (interface landscape-left); it belongs at raw 2 (landscape-right). Two independent readings say so, and neither is new to this file:
ARCamera.transform's prose — +X "always points along the long axis of the device, from the front-facing camera toward the Home button". The Home-button end is to the right exactly in UIDeviceOrientationLandscapeLeft, which is the interface's LandscapeRight (UIApplication.h states that inversion outright).
The sensor's own image, with no reference to the prose — a back-camera portrait frame is EXIF orientation 6, whose 0th column is the visual top, so image +u runs down the screen in portrait. The camera's +X is +u, since cv_from_gl_camera negates the second and third basis columns and leaves the first alone.

Note this is a moved zero, not a flipped sign. A sign flip is 180° in portrait and a no-op in landscape, so it would upright portrait and leave both landscapes over. This is 180° in all four — which is why the symptom was orientation-independent.
Device-verified
Measured on an iPad Pro 11-inch (M5), 2026-08-10 — landscape-right and portrait both upright, with orient read off the console at each to confirm the renderer held the value under test. This mapping has carried some form of "not device-verified" since it was first written; it no longer does.
Two orientations, because one cannot do it, and which two is not arbitrary:
Landscape-right pins the zero. It is the orientation the constant names, so the turn applied there is 0 — upright is the statement that the sensor basis and the viewport genuinely coincide. The previous build turned 180° here and was upside down: the same observation inverted.
Portrait pins the step, and is the only orientation that can. The turn is ±90° × (raw − 2), which at both landscapes lands on 0° or 180° — each its own negation — so the landscapes look identical whichever way the step runs, and a sign error there is invisible by construction.
That is the mirror image of the trap that produced the original bug, where portrait alone could not separate a wrong zero from a wrong sign. It needed one of each. Landscape-left follows: two points determine the line, and it is the same expression evaluated from a zero and a step that were both measured. raw 3 stays untestable — Info.plist does not declare it.
Also confirmed on device: the orientation value survives a rotation (portrait (1) with a 1668 x 2420 drawable), both camera modes exercised, 6748 frames fused / ~98k verts / 4% jetsam, no render or ARKit failure.
One caveat, stated rather than assumed away: this was measured on an iPad, and the sighting that sent the mapping the wrong way originally may have been taken on an iPhone. ARCamera.transform documents its basis without reference to the device and both readings behind the constant are device-independent, so there is no reason to expect a difference — but nobody has looked.
The trap in getting there
if (quarter_turns) tested the enum value, which coincided with a zero angle only while the zero sat at raw 0. Changing the formula alone would have made landscape-left silently skip the largest turn of the four. It now tests the angle.
Single source of truth
A zero and a sign restated in two places is how this went wrong twice, in two directions. So:
kSensorBasisOrientation is now the only place in the app that turns an orientation into an angle, and carries the derivation and the measurement on it.
VolumetricRenderer.h documents the enum as which way the interface is facing and defers, rather than restating the rule; the call site points at the constant.
The arithmetic subtracts two raw values and reads the difference as quarter turns, so that premise is pinned by static_assert rather than assumed. Verified by reordering the enum: the build fails naming the enumerator that moved.
Swift's read-out label switched from a string array indexed by rawValue — a second encoding of the same ordering, which would have silently relabelled the one line whose job is to say what the renderer holds.

iOS arm64 (Debug), no new warnings, pre-commit clean.
