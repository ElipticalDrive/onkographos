Scope
Target branch: frontier-voxel-engine
Status: Proposed terrain-generation options for discussion/experimentation, not a mandate to replace the currently planned generator or begin large implementation immediately.
Related: FV05 — geological macroshape and ridged-FBM terrain, FV06 — erosion and drainage, FV09 — multiresolution source and error bounds, frontier architecture.
Goal
Generate beautiful, geologically plausible valleys and mountain ranges without the ubiquitous needles, uniformly jagged slopes, or featureless/noisy mountain blobs that naive ridged FBM often produces.
The canonical surface must be a single deterministic world function, independent of render LOD and voxel discretization (6.25 cm canonical cells). Broad landscape geometry, important ridges and the river network must survive all automatic distant-LOD representations. No artist-made LOD files or quadratic fitting.
Why FBM often looks spiky
For octaves of wavelength lambda_i and amplitude a_i, slope contribution scales roughly like a_i/lambda_i. With lacunarity 2 and gain 0.5, small-scale octaves can contribute approximately as much slope as large-scale ones. Using sharply peaked ridged transforms everywhere exaggerates this.
Investigate lower octave gain (start 0.35–0.45), fewer strong ridged octaves, a gentle ridge transform (e.g. 1 - abs(noise) before aggressive exponents), landscape-dependent roughness, and amplitude filtering by scale.
Proposed minimal recipe — first prototype
Macro geography first. Use very low-frequency, smooth coherent noise/province or tectonic masks to place mountain belts, broad valleys, plateaus and plains. The terrain should look good with all high-frequency layers disabled.
Gentle coordinate domain warp on kilometer scales, e.g. two independent low-frequency noise channels providing ~50–150 m of horizontal displacement at ~3–6 km wavelength. Avoid excessive warping that creates tangled terrain or destroys coherent river features.
Broad smooth FBM for rolling geography (~1.5–4 km wavelength, 50–150 m height influence; ~3 octaves).
Mountain mask from a separate low-frequency (3–8 km) coherent field. Smoothly transition from plains through foothills to mountains. Try mountain_weight = smoothstep(a,b,mask_noise)^2.
Masked ridged FBM, present primarily in mountain regions (initial wavelength ~0.8–2 km, ~150–400 m height influence where fully active, ~3 octaves, gain ~0.38). Avoid uniformly sharp peaks throughout plains and valleys.
Foothill and local relief FBM (~150–500 m wavelength, ~5–25 m amplitude); smaller conditioned surface detail (~10–80 m, ~0.5–4 m) only where geology/slope warrants it.
Drainage/erosion shaping on a coarse regional source, with realistic branching valleys and downhill channels. Keep river/catchment topology coherent across tile borders and independent of LOD. Do not require costly per-native-voxel hydraulic erosion.
Material/geology conditioning for exposed rock, sediment, snow, soil, scree and slope; do not rely on geometry noise to supply all visual realism.
These values are starting ranges, not optimized constants or claims of physical accuracy. Keep them configurable and evaluate multiple seeds/regions.
Illustrative heightfield composition
```
p = (x,z) + low_frequency_domain_warp(x,z)
macro = smooth_fbm(p; wavelength≈2500m, octaves=3, gain≈0.42)
mask = smoothstep(-0.15, 0.45, mountain_region_fbm(p; wavelength≈5000m))^2
ridges = ridged_fbm(p; wavelength≈1400m, octaves=3, gain≈0.38)
foothills = smooth_fbm(p; wavelength≈300m, octaves=3, gain≈0.40)
H = H_base + 90m*macro + 320m*mask*ridges + 12m*foothills
H_final = geology_and_drainage_correction(H) + filtered_local_detail
```
The example is a shape sketch, not a recommended exact formula for hydrology or non-heightfield features. The authoritative world still uses a 3D density/voxel source for caves, arches, undercuts, buildings and edits; this issue only addresses broad surface relief.
Evaluation plan (cheap, falsifiable)
Start with a small deterministic gallery (mountain belt, broad river valley, mixed foothills, plains, rocky cliffs), plus a held-out seed.
Compare A: baseline current terrain, B: masked FBM alone, C: B + domain warp, D: C + regional erosion/drainage. Don't add multiple complex systems until a simpler one is demonstrably insufficient.
Capture ground-level valley views, high viewpoints, 2+ mile distant ridges, and continuous flight toward mountain silhouettes, with fog/haze disabled.
Quantify excessive slope/curvature/spike distributions, drainage coherence, cross-region seams, runtime/source evaluations, and visual silhouette preservation against canonical fine terrain.
Use identical canonical input across LODs; filter omitted bands by projected footprint. Do not independently change the mountain function by LOD. For large ridges, accompany the renderer with conservative geometric error bounds/feature anchors (see FV09).
Preserve editable canonical voxel behavior and existing versioning: adjusting terrain recipe likely changes the baseline world and needs an explicit world-generator version migration/compatibility decision, not silent save changes.
Prefer direct CPU evaluation initially; evaluate SIMD/FastNoise2 or GPU generation after results establish that noise evaluation is a bottleneck. No costly training or full-world baking prerequisite.
Open questions / later options
Compare simple masked ridged FBM against ridge-network- or tectonic-uplift-driven macroshape.
For more realism, investigate stream-power/fluvial erosion, sediment/deposition, and slope-aligned procedural patterns. Keep erosion cached/coarse, not run separately for every LOD patch.
Consider octave energy normalization and slope-constrained detail to prevent coarse-to-fine roughness spikes.
Separate surface displacement from realistic lighting/texturing/vegetation; some apparent 'spikiness' is material/shadow aliasing rather than height geometry.

Legacy reference policy
R / selective P only by review: Inspect the analytic branch for FBM shaping and the old main branch for geology, hydrology, seeding, and existing tests. Small deterministic noise or river algorithms may be ported only after source-version parity, performance, and seam tests. Do not port quadratic fitting, hard-boundary constraints, old CPU coarse-mesh renderer, LOD scheduler, or any representation that makes the landscape depend on render resolution. Work under frontier/ only.
Expected output: a documented short comparison, configurable recommended FBM recipe, deterministic reference gallery and tests; integrate into FV05/FV06/FV09 only after choosing a verified option.
First focused pass — ffc960c
Implemented independently under frontier: selectable legacy (source v1, exact parity retained), regional-unwarped, and regional (source v2) recipes. CLI defaults to regional; the user also selected voxel-filter on by default. Regional source combines smooth macro relief, a squared smoothstep mountain province mask, gently rounded masked ridges, conditioned foothills/detail and restrained 100m warp. No erosion/drainage/biome system or arbitrary 3D generation added. --start-xz X,Z supports inspecting different physical regions.
Three recipes × seeds7/19/83 ×2km/8km CPU galleries plus actual ground/aerial/held-out GPU captures inspected. Seed7 8km slopes p50/p95 fell from49.9°/69.2° to5.2°/20.2°, with ~298m relief. Regional source costs19 noise calls versus11 legacy; roughly2× source-query cost. Clean Radeon760M1080p MSAA4 filter-on15m/s15s runs: p95/p99 legacy7.94/10.81ms versus regional5.58/8.19ms; native GPU payload535MB versus135MB. Cold CPU preparation285ms versus682ms. These are different scenes and yaw directions, not a matched renderer speedup claim.
89 tests pass, one benchmark ignored. Visual assessment: much calmer hills/plains and regional variation; still rounded/plain, basic materials, no connected drainage. Broader geometry/appearance goals remain open. Historical benchmark scripts explicitly retain the legacy recipe.
