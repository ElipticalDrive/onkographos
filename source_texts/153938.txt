TMUshare
TMUshare digital repository is an open access repository for scholarship and various research and educational outputs produced at Toronto Metropolitan University. TMUshare offers faculty, students, staff, and researchers a free and secure home to share their scholarship openly and in a free to read format. By depositing in TMUshare, researchers can increase the discoverability of their work, and comply with funder and publisher requirements.
TMUshare is also home to TMU’s version of record student digital theses and dissertations.

Sanctuary and Resistance 2026: Adapting to Escalating Enforcement, Expanding Infrastructure, and Cross-Sector Solidarity
(2026) Tortorici, Tom; Dreher, Nick; Sharma, Kanushka
Show more
Between February and May 2026, the Soli*City Research Network and the Zolberg Institute on Migration and Mobility brought together people committed to protecting migrants and refugees in the United States by convening the second iteration of the Sanctuary and Resistance series. Building on the foundational discussions of 2025, four online roundtables brought together over 100 municipal leaders, legal advocates, scholars, grassroots organizers, and clinical law professors from across the United States and beyond.
The 2026 conversations documented a critical transition in the sanctuary landscape: a shift from anticipating federal administrative change to actively adapting to high-intensity enforcement operations and massive infrastructure investment. Panelists detailed unprecedented federal funding appropriations directed toward immigration detention, aggressive tactical deployments in sanctuary jurisdictions, and the severe operational strain placed on legal service providers and community organizations. At the same time, the sessions highlighted remarkable tactical evolution. Participants shared how municipalities have passed innovative ordinances to limit enforcement abuses and how university research centers and legal clinics are adjusting their frameworks for university-community partnerships to preserve community dignity amid systemic instability.
This report is for the people on the front lines of this work: local officials, community organizations, legal advocates, clinicians, and researchers committed to the lawful protection of immigrants and defense of human rights. The report serves two purposes: to archive these proceedings, and to offer a strategic resource that local actors can use right now while informing broader conversations about enforcement, detention, and sanctuary.

“Lost in Transcription?”: A case study of using AI and humans-in-the loop to create captions and transcripts for a library symposium
(International Federation of Library Associations and Institutions (IFLA), 2026-06-06) Lu, Eva; Panangaden, Sonya; Weiler, Mark; Clark, Michael
Show more
In many regions, academic libraries are increasingly expected to generate recorded content as part of their services and operations, such as hosting public or academic events. These recordings require quality captions and transcripts to address the needs of users with disabilities as required by human rights legislation.
Unfortunately, auto-generated captions cannot be relied upon to provide error-free outputs. This paper presents a case study of how we used AI and involved 35
volunteers from across North America to create captions and transcripts for recordings of a 3-day virtual accessibility symposium. This initiative was put in place
to ensure all attendees had equitable access to the recordings. Our experience asserts the necessity of including humans in digital environments that integrate AI
workflows.

Dreaming the West: how migration imaginaries are constructed and transformed among middle-class Iranians
(Taylor and Francis, 2026) Hormozinejad, Sara
Show more
This article examines how migration imaginaries are constructed prior to migration and transformed through the lived experiences of settlement. It conceptualises imagination not as a static or purely individual act but as a dynamic, socially embedded process that shapes aspirations before departure and undergoes transformation in response to lived realities abroad. Drawing on in-depth, semi-structured interviews with 30 middle-class Iranian immigrants and return migrants holding Iranian and Canadian citizenship, the study explores two interlinked dynamics. First, it investigates how a homogeneous, idealised imaginary of the West emerges before migration, captured by the Persian notion of Kharej, and shaped by Western media, the moral economy of departure, and the context of emigration under restrictive mobility regimes. Second, it analyzes how this migration imaginary unravelsduring settlement in Canada, particularly through the devaluation of cultural capital, experiences of racial discrimination, and exposure to the broader political economy of migration. The article introduces the concept of collective fantasy to theorise imagination not simply as hopeful or liberatory but also as hegemonic, idealising, and at times distorting. In doing so, it reveals how migration imaginaries both shape and complicate mobility aspirations, and how middle-class privilege becomes unstable across borders

Wavefront-adapted phase coordinates for closed-form broadband focused-wave fields
(2026-10-26) Sharma, Shivani; Xu, Yuan
Show more
Closed-form descriptions exist for special focused fields, but general finite-aperture broadband fields commonly retain diffraction integrals and coherent frequency synthesis. We introduce nonlinear effective phase
coordinates that retain the exact Euclidean dependence on the observation point while expanding only in the local aperture coordinate. The coordinates are the exact zeroth- and first-order aperture-phase coefficients; for an unsteered source they reduce to z2 = R0 − d and x2 = R0 sin β, revealing equal-range and direction-cosine coordinates. The mapping preserves the aperture Fourier-transform form and yields a close broadband field/point-spread-function representation in a mixed spatial–spatial-frequency domain, avoiding frequency-by-frequency numerical aperture integration. Applied to synthetic-aperture ultrasound, the formulation improves agreement with Field II simulations in both image and spatial-frequency domains. We use focused ultrasound as a demonstration; we outline the extension of the same phase-coordinate strategy to other prescribed wavefronts when the total phase is locally affine in the aperture coordinate.

Understanding COVID-19 Vaccine Hesitancy in Latinx, MENA, and South Asian Communities in Toronto: A Scoping Review
(2023) Zinaic, Rade; Wong, Josephine Pui-Hing
Show more
COVID-19 vaccination is the foundation of Canada’s COVID-19 response. Yet decent initial willingness and uptake revealed serious structural barriers which left many unvaccinated to suffer dire outcomes and overwhelmed the public healthcare system. Our scoping review narrows the broader Canadian context to the Greater Toronto Area (GTA) to explore the effects of pandemic policy on ethnoracial communities. Thus, we inquired into the primary reasons for vaccine
hesitancy in the South Asian, Latinx, and Middle Eastern and North African (MENA) communities. We applied the methods pioneered by Arksey and O’Malley along with Levac, Colquhoun, and O’Brien. A total 3685 articles were retrieved from PubMed, OVID, JSTOR, ERIC. Vetting for research relevance as well as de- duplication left us with 42 relevant sources for data extraction. Our results revealed that there are four intersecting and symbiotic factors for COVID-19 vaccine hesitancy amongst the South Asian, Latinx, and Middle Eastern and North African (MENA) diaspora in Toronto: (a) access; (b) mistrust; (c) racism; and (d) misinformation. We concluded that these intersecting causes of vaccine hesitancy can uphold access barriers while normalizing misinformation in the service of suspicion, thereby doubly stigmatizing racialized peoples by race and vaccination status in a way that further marginalizes them.
