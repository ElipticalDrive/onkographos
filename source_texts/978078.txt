Image inpainting and demosaicing via total
variation and Markov random field-based
modeling
Marko Panić, Dušan Jakovetić, Vladimir Crnojević, and Aleksandra Pižurica
Abstract–The problem of image reconstruction from incomplete data can be formulated as a linear inverse problem
and is usually approached using optimization theory tools.
Total variation (TV) regularization has been widely applied
in this framework, due to its effectiveness in capturing spatial
information and availability of elegant, fast algorithms. In this
paper we show that significant improvements can be gained
by extending this approach with a Markov Random Field
(MRF) model for image gradient magnitudes. We propose a
novel method that builds upon the Chambolle’s fast projected
algorithm designed for solving TV minimization problem. In
the Chambolle’s algorithm, we incorporate a MRF model
which selects only a subset of image gradients to be effectively
included in the algorithm iterations. The proposed algorithm
is especially effective when a large portion of image data is
missing. We also apply the proposed method to demosacking
where algorithm shows less sensitivity to the initial choice of
the tuning parameter and also for its wide range of values
outperformes the method without the MRF model.
Keywords inpainting, MRF, TV regularization

inpainting and demosaicking that builds upon the Chambolle’s method [8] and incorporates the MRF model in the
method. The proposed method results in better agorithm
performance for high percentage of missing pixels (more
the 70%) and low Signal to Noise ratio (SNR) and comparable performance in other cases. While past work [9]
also incorporated MRF-like models, for a different imaging
problem, the approach presented here is distinct from past
work. Namely, we incorporate the MRF-based inference
directly in the iterative procedure of the Chambolle’s
algorithm. When compared with [8], the proposed method
shows better reconstruction for high percantage of missing
pixels and low SNRs, as well as a better robustness
to the tuning parameter choices in demosaicing. This
results in smaller number-of iterations for the algorithm
to converge. We implemented the statistical inference in
graphical process unit (GPU) which reduces computation
time and enables processing of high resolutional images.

In the following, we present a novel method that builds

m presents intensity changes in both directions x and y
of image x resulting in an approximately sparse representation of it. Since the edges and contours in m are
described in the form of a cluster of pixels along some
important structure in the image we assume structured
sparsity on m. Knowing the structured sparsity support
