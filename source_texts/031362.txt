Recent advances in neural-based rendering, particularly radiance field methods, have significantly enhanced the synthesis of photorealistic 3D scenes from novel viewpoints. Point-based 3D Gaussian splatting (3D-GS) [1] achieves ray-traced quality at interactive frame rates while avoiding the heavy network inference and dense sampling required by NeRF [2]. However, when deployed on resource-constrained edge platforms, 3D-GS rasterization exposes three critical bottlenecks as shown in Fig. 1. First, α-blending of hundreds of thousands of Gaussians across $16 \times 16$ screen-space tiles consumes over 70% of total compute time. Second, conventional AABB-based tile assignment misallocates more than 40% of Gaussians, inflating the key-value-pair(KVP) set and increasing both sorting and blending overhead. Third, TileBased Rendering(TBR) incurs highly irregular External Memory Access(EMA) patterns, which drive off-chip traffic to only one-third the efficiency of sequential accesses.
