Shadows are a big offender usually, and caves too if you added lots of them. I suppose big textures might have an impact too, but probably less? Depends on how many of them are being rendered, see my answer about materials. There is no occlusion culling, and it's not a simple thing to implement + Godot kinda makes it harder because it lacks an API to implement custom culling (while it does have an occlusion culling system, it is not tuned for dynamic environments such as chunked voxel terrain). Increasing mesh chunk size to 32 usually gives a boost, but unfortunately many of the relatively simple optimizations I know about (and have tested in a non-Godot project) are impractical to do in Godot (no SSBO in shaders, shadow cascade staggering, index buffer re-use, indirect drawing, to name a few).

my chunk size is 32, but why is there such a limit?
Because beyond 32 you hit another diminishing return, chunks start to get too noticeable when they load in and out, not granular enough when using LOD and frustrum culling, and especially rebuilding them takes much longer (8 times longer). While they might be fine for completely static terrain, I haven't planned to have such a high size, knowing also that voxel chunk size is decoupled from mesh size, and is not configurable so just removing the clamp wouldn't be enough, you'd have to change both in C++ along with suff that might depend on it.
