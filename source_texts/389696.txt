Summary
NativeBackend::raycast used to return no hits, so LiDAR, ground checks, and any other ray-based sensor saw nothing on the native backend. This PR implements it with a new raycast module.
What a ray hits: every non-sensor collider at its current pose. That covers fixed and kinematic colliders, and also links of moving assemblies, which are posed by forward kinematics of the current state.
Per shape:
Spheres, capsules, boxes, planes: treated as solids, with planes as half-spaces. A ray that starts inside one hits it at distance 0 with a zero normal, the same way the Rapier backend reports solid hits.
Height fields: hit on the same bilinear surface the contacts use (height_field_surface). The ray is clipped to the field's bounds and cut at the grid lines. Within each cell the height difference along the ray is quadratic, so it is solved exactly.
Triangle meshes: Möller–Trumbore against every triangle. Meshes are surfaces, hit from either side.
Compounds: the nearest hit over their parts.
Convex hulls: not hit. This is documented as a limitation.
Results:
Hits are sorted by distance, with ties broken by entity index, as the PhysicsBackend contract requires.
Normals face the ray.
A ray with a zero direction, a non-finite origin, or a negative maximum distance returns no hits.
Capability: the backend now advertises RaycastBatch and passes that conformance vector. The external conformance test runs it automatically, because it checks every capability the backend advertises.

Testing
rne_physics_native now has 21 tests, all passing. New:
Unit tests, solid shapes: spheres, capsules (cap and side), rotated boxes, and planes.
Exact distances and normals.
A ray starting inside a solid gives a zero-distance hit.
Misses, and a box just beyond max_distance_m, give no hit.
Unit tests, surfaces:
A sloped height field, hit by a vertical ray and by a level ray, to 1e-12.
A triangle, hit from both sides.
A compound, which gives the nearest of its parts.
Integration test:
Before the first step, a downward ray hits a free box at its spawn height and then the ground.
After the box settles, the ray hits the box's top face at its resting pose.
A sideways ray hits the box's −x face and misses the ground.
Short rays and zero-direction rays return nothing.
raycast_batch matches single raycast calls.
External conformance: passes all five advertised capabilities, now including RaycastBatch.
