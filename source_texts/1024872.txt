Question
How must the compute-ray Render Path consume the established three-command Voxel Edit Outcome burst and converge from its Visible Voxel Scene Revision to its newest Required Voxel Scene Revision? Resolve full versus incremental traversal-data preparation, bounded pending state, cooperative supersession, stale gates, atomic image/data visibility, behavior while inactive or switching, contextual failure and explicit retry, presentation lifecycle, resource retirement, and shutdown—without assuming the raster path's Raster Region implementation is the reusable abstraction.

Resolution
Decision
Use full, immutable, copy-on-replace compute-ray candidates as the milestone baseline. Every accepted newer changed Voxel Edit Outcome prepares one complete revision-attributed compute installation from its pinned Voxel Scene View: volume headers, flattened 32-bit voxel/material words, the complete Render-Path-local material table, and—if the gated experiment survives—the complete occupancy directory. The worker reads fixed-capacity rectangular Voxel Regions into the final candidate and checks cancellation between those private preparation blocks. A preparation block is neither a Raster Region nor a new semantic partition.
Cold construction, adjacent edits, discontinuities or resyncs, and explicit retry all use the same full path. The canonical Large scene may therefore rebuild and upload the complete 32 MiB dense payload. Do not patch installed GPU data in place, clone-and-patch a partial candidate, or incrementally maintain even the optional occupancy directory in this milestone. Localized or incremental compute preparation remains a later optimization justified only by GPU evidence.
Admission and bounded state
Consume Voxel Edit Outcomes in publication order. Unchanged and non-newer outcomes do not mutate convergence state. A changed outcome must match the Voxel Scene identity, its view must match the change-set successor, and its revision must be strictly newer than Required. Because the complete pinned view is authoritative, a discontinuous but otherwise valid newer submission becomes a full rebuild rather than requiring retained edit history. Each accepted outcome and retry receives a monotonically newer private generation as well as its Voxel Scene Revision.
Retain at most:
one complete installed bundle at the Visible Voxel Scene Revision;
one active CPU preparation, running or ready, with at most one full candidate allocation;
one replaceable newest pending target containing only its pinned view and stamps;
one complete uploaded hidden GPU candidate;
one current paused-failure target, occupying the target slot rather than adding another queue;
bounded typed events with compacted counters; and
explicitly owned, bounded retirement or cleanup debt until its completion token makes destruction safe.
A newer requirement signals the active worker, replaces the pending target, clears any superseded pause, and may prepare while an older uploaded candidate remains hidden. It never starts a second full preparation worker before the old worker reaches a cancellation boundary and is collected. If lifecycle suspension prevents boundary progress, CPU work may finish and remain ready, but upload stops when the single hidden slot is occupied.
Supersession and stale gates
Cancellation limits wasted work; it never establishes correctness. Check the scene identity, revision, and generation when accepting worker completion, immediately before GPU allocation or upload, after upload completion, and immediately before frame-boundary installation. The installation gate additionally checks the expected Visible installation identity and current configured path state. A same-revision older generation is stale, including an obsolete retry. Late completion or failure from a superseded generation remains observable but cannot pause or replace the newest target.
The fixed burst must prove exactly this sequence:
Revision 2 is held after one bounded preparation block, then cooperatively stopped after Revision 3 becomes Required.
Revision 3 becomes the sole complete uploaded hidden candidate.
Revision 4 becomes Required before the installation gate, making Revision 3 stale.
The next safe boundary rejects and retires Revision 3.
Revision 4 alone installs; Visible moves directly from 1 to 4, so 2 and 3 never produce a presented frame.
Atomic data and image visibility
A hidden candidate owns the entire immutable compute scene-data bundle. At advance-frame-boundary, after the preceding frame fence completes and before image acquisition, perform every fallible validation and bookkeeping step first, then make one infallible designation swap and advance Visible exactly once. The former installed bundle moves to safe retirement at that boundary.
The offscreen storage image is camera- and presentation-dependent output, not part of the revision candidate. Each recorded compute frame overwrites the complete image from exactly one installed bundle, attributes the observation to the installed Voxel Scene Revision, latest applied Camera State Revision, and Presentation Configuration identity, applies an explicit compute-write-to-graphics-sample barrier, and composites that image in the same submission. Do not retain a pre-rendered hidden image: camera changes and resize would immediately stale it and it would add another large candidate resource. The last old frame remains usable until the swap; the first new frame is wholly generated from the new bundle. A record or submit failure produces no mixed frame and follows the Presenting Render Path lifecycle policy; it does not roll convergence back.
Runtime switching roles
There is no persistent inactive compute path. A Replacement Render Path cold-builds the switch's fixed, already-converged Voxel Scene View and remains Preparing until its complete bundle is installed, the latest Camera State and current Presentation Configuration are acknowledged, and it reports Recordable. Edit admission is closed throughout switching, so a replacement receives no edit outcomes and never presents before handoff.
A Presenting compute path accepts edits only while switching is idle. A switch away is accepted only when Required equals Visible and no preparation, pause, hidden candidate, or cleanup debt remains; compute continues presenting that stable installation until handoff. A Retiring compute path receives no further state and never records again—only release and shutdown proceed.
Failure and explicit retry
Classify compute convergence failures as Preparation, Upload, or Installation. Preserve transition and generation identity, path kind and role, Voxel Scene identity, failed revision, current Required and Visible revisions, phase, Voxel Volume or bounded-work context when useful, Presentation Configuration identity when relevant, resource disposition, and the source. Never report a Raster Region identity.
If a failure still matches the current required generation, keep the prior Visible installation recordable, clean or retain ownership of every partial and hidden resource, retain the pinned required view, pause automatic convergence, and emit the typed failure. Explicit retry advances the generation and repeats the same full preparation for the retained revision. A strictly newer valid outcome supersedes the pause without retry. Late older failures are diagnostics only. Invalid identities or revision stamps are typed rejections with no state mutation rather than asynchronous retryable failures.
Arrange installation so no fallible operation remains after the designation swap. A current pre-swap installation failure leaves Visible unchanged, retires the failed candidate safely, and pauses. After a successful swap there is no rollback. A replacement without a usable prior installation reports its contextual failure through the switching module, which cleans it up and requires a fresh switch request as already decided.
Presentation lifecycle and retirement
Scene-data bundles are target-independent and survive presentation recreation on the same device. At a fence-safe release, detach and destroy only target-bound output and composite resources: the offscreen storage image, view and memory; composite target objects; and descriptor bindings that reference them. Preserve the installed scene bundle, active or ready CPU preparation, newest pending or paused target, and any completed target-independent hidden scene bundle. Invalidate the old Presentation Configuration acknowledgement.
Configure creates the complete new output/composite set, rebinds installed and hidden data, and acknowledges the new Presentation Configuration only after complete success. No installation, recording, or switch handoff occurs until the new identity and latest camera revision are acknowledged. Zero-size suspension retains logical state and the latest camera and may let an already-running CPU preparation reach one bounded ready state, but it performs no boundary advancement, installation, recording, or upload beyond the single-hidden-candidate bound. Restoration resumes automatically; it is not a convergence retry.
Cancelled CPU allocations are dropped only after their worker completes. Uploaded stale or failed candidates remain owned until their upload-completion token proves destruction safe. The preceding-frame fence makes the former Visible bundle safe to retire at installation rather than one extra frame later. Presentation resources use their own frame-completion and detachment gate. Cleanup debt remains attributed and observable and prevents the state from exceeding its resource bound. Evidence must balance created, installed, hidden, retired, and peak bytes and objects.
Shutdown
After the Render Backend's device-idle gate, shutdown is idempotent and continues through every cleanup step: signal cancellation, release deterministic barriers, join the worker, discard pending, paused, and ready CPU state, complete or account for uploads, destroy hidden, stale, retirement, installed scene-data, output, descriptor, pipeline, and composite resources, and clear retained views. Aggregate all role, phase, revision, source, resource, and worker errors instead of short-circuiting or silently discarding any result. Drop is a logging fallback only.
The milestone must prove normal close during active CPU preparation and with a complete hidden uploaded candidate. Both cases end with zero compute-owned resources, zero workers, and zero Vulkan validation warnings or errors.
Rejected alternatives and consequences
Incremental or in-place voxel updates introduce partial visibility, synchronization, rollback, and candidate-lifetime rules before evidence shows they are needed.
Raster Regions remain raster-specific; only the proven newest-required policy and lifecycle ordering are reused.
An unbounded change history is unnecessary because each newest pinned view is complete.
Concurrent full-preparation workers violate the intended memory and ownership bound.
Cancellation cannot replace generation and revision gates.
A hidden pre-rendered output image conflicts with live camera and presentation changes.
Automatic retry can create an allocation and failure loop; retry stays explicit and newer work supersedes naturally.
Destroying target-independent scene data on every presentation recreation needlessly couples convergence to the swapchain lifecycle.
This decision gives Define the semantic ray oracle and cross-path evidence contract exact revision, camera, and presentation attribution; the bounded preparation-block barrier; the three convergence failure phases; explicit retry; lifecycle-held-candidate and retirement accounting; and the two shutdown cases. It introduces no new Wayfinder ticket or fog. The provisional post-compute roadmap remains unchanged.
