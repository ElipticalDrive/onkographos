Abstract
Image transmission from space borne equipment is a challenging task owing to its restrictions on bandwidth and memory requirement. Many of the state of art existing image compression techniques cannot be applied for remote sensing color images because of its constraints on memory, bandwidth, energy, and processing capabilities. To resolve the ineffectiveness of the existing methods, this paper introduces an efficient near lossless image compression algorithm named Dissemination of Spatial Correlation (DSC). The proposed method achieves compression by performing the dimensionality reduction process through the transformation of three-dimensional RGB values to one dimensional grayscale image values with the computation of its proportion to reconstruct the original image. Furthermore, the obtained grayscale image with its proportion, i.e., DSC transformed values are then encoded using Burrows Wheeler Transform (BWT). Experimental results on different benchmark remote sensing images demonstrate that the presented model outperformed the classical and recent approaches in terms of compression performance, reconstructed image quality and bandwidth utilization. Particularly, the proposed DSC method exhibits superior results over the state of art methods with an average space saving of 56.68% and Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 56.51. Moreover, the obtained results clearly showed that the proposed method is suitable for resource constrained remote sensing image processing systems.
