The game
A Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion garden at /science/garden. The child touches or drags the bed and living Turing patterns grow from the stroke - spots, stripes, labyrinths - evolving on their own after the touch. Do first, see the effect, then the name arrives. No questions, no quiz, tactile only.
Verified by observation, not narration (headless Chrome over CDP, real pointer events, audio analyser tap)
Mount gate: 720 free-running frames with zero interaction: bed empty (lit 0.0000), no naming cards in the DOM, no AudioContext exists.
Alive: one drag seeds lit 0.063 and it grows unaided to 0.337 over 4s.
Calm Mode: identical stroke, half the sim rate (0.177 vs 0.355, ratio 0.49 = the documented 8-vs-16 steps per frame). Calm slows; it never freezes or hides.
prefers-reduced-motion: sim steps only on touch - lit is bit-identical at 1.5s/4.5s/9.5s between touches, moves again on the next touch.
Audio: off by default and genuinely silent (RMS 0.000000 with the context live); an explicit press to 80 produces RMS 0.028 / peak 0.041. The AudioContext is built on the child's first explicit gesture, which is the bed, the control stone or the sound slider, not the bed alone. All three are deliberate acts and none of them makes a sound on its own.
Tests: 103 pass / 0 fail (sim aliveness sweep, discovery reducer, guided-naming). tsc --noEmit clean.

Fractal Grower: 16ad49d, and the fix round 7b73a2d
The game
A seed on the ground at /science/fractal. Drag up from it and a stem comes out; keep dragging and the stem splits, and each piece does exactly what the stem did. Drag sideways across the structure and the branching angle opens - a tight pine spreads into an oak, at every size at once, because the angle under the child's finger is the angle used at every split all the way down. Drag up and down and children get longer against their parents until everything overlaps and it is a coral. Four seed buttons hand the same recursion a different split: tree, fern, lightning, river. Do first, see the effect, then the name arrives; four sentences, once each, after the child has already made the thing each one describes.
One 2D canvas, one self-stopping render loop, one pure sim. No 3D dependency: the structure is genuinely three-dimensional and is put on screen with a weak perspective divide, and the camera is placed from the structure's own measured depth.
The measured claims
Every sentence the activity says to a child is a claim about fractal-grower.ts, so each one is a measurement rather than a comment.
"The small branches copy the big ones." The turn off the parent is recovered from the geometry at every generation and asserted equal to the angle the child is holding, within 0.1 rad. Length shrinks by exactly the ratio parameter at every generation to nine decimal places; thickness by exactly the taper.
"You moved one thing and the whole shape changed." Twenty-sample sweep of the angle: width 0.668 to 3.083 at the peak (4.61x) then easing back to 2.815, height 3.379 to 2.222 falling at every single step. The easing back is real and is asserted rather than claimed away.
"One rule made all four." The recursion is handed a rule that exists nowhere in the product and grows it, so no seed can have code of its own.
Growing does not reshuffle what is already there. Grown to two heights, every already-grown segment is identical, because the wobble is hashed per node rather than counted in traversal order.
Colour. Every colour every seed can produce is outside hues 270-350, and so is every RGB blend between any two of them: the arcs are measured on the circle and asserted narrower than 180 degrees with no banned hue in them.
Depth. What a child can grow runs from a fern 0.016 trunk lengths deep to a tree at 7.85 front to back, a half-depth of 3.93. That five-hundred-to-one spread is the argument for placing the camera from the structure; the perspective it produces is invariant, near-to-far scale ratio exactly 2 and every projected k inside 0.6..1.8.
Ceiling. Each seed is a full tree of its own arity, so its worst case is exactly its geometric sum: fern 121, river 121, lightning 255, tree 511. SEGMENT_CAP is 4096, eight times the worst honest case, asserted against a measured sweep.
296 unit tests pass, 0 fail. tsc --noEmit clean.
Three defects the observed pass found, fixed in 16ad49d
The naming card sat over the band a drag has to start in. With a card up, a full-length grow drag moved the structure not at all: every event went to the card, so the child was told "the stem split into two branches" and then could not grow it further until they found the close button. Then, with the card alone made transparent, the drag was caught by its wrapper instead. Only buttons in there take touches now.
The frame was fitted to the structure at full growth, so a child's first split came out 176 pixels tall on a 1943 pixel canvas - nine percent of the screen, a speck above the seed, on an activity whose whole point is do-then-see. The frame now runs ahead of the child rather than all the way ahead.
The tip mark was sized off segment length, so on a bare stem it was a 90 pixel blob sitting exactly on the split being named; and the trunk rolled its frame, throwing both first children into the depth axis so the child's first split projected as a bump on a stem. Capped, and the trunk now splits in the picture plane.

Wave 4: Sound Drawing (ca5e6af)
The pitch-and-octave row of the issue #225 catalogue, which was the last row
still unbuilt that a pair of hands can manipulate rather than watch. A
harmonograph: two pendulums hang over a sheet of paper in a lit room, a pen
rests on the sheet, and sliding a bob up its string makes that pendulum swing
faster. What the pen leaves behind is the shape of how the two swings compare,
and nothing else. The same comparison is a musical interval, so with the sound
on the two pendulums are the two notes of it and the shape on the paper and the
chord in the room are one fact arriving through two senses.
The child is told none of that. They are given two strings.

files: src/lib/harmonograph.ts (pure, no DOM), src/lib/harmonograph-discovery.ts
(pure reducer), src/components/Harmonograph.tsx (one hand-rolled canvas, no
three.js, SVG for the four card thumbnails), src/app/science/harmonograph/page.tsx,
plus the two suites. safeHue/hueIsAllowed are imported from
pattern-garden.ts, never copied.

A dark bed the child paints seed into. Two substances spread and react under
the Gray-Scott rule, and out of those four terms come spots, stripes and
labyrinths that look like the coats of animals. Turing, 1952, under a finger.
The control is a map of the rule that is itself a live garden grown under the
rules it offers, so there are no numbers and no dead ground on it: the suite
grows real beds across the whole control and fails if any corner is blank or
flooded. Four naming lines, decided by a pure reducer rather than inline in the
canvas, so "nothing is named before the child acts" and "nothing is named
twice" are mechanical rather than hoped for.

Driven through the Chrome DevTools Protocol against a real page with real
pointer input, sampling the bed canvas rather than trusting the code to be
telling the truth. litFraction is the fraction of sampled pixels carrying
growth.

  Mount gate. 720 frames ran freely with nobody touching anything: bed stayed
  at 0.0000, zero naming lines in the DOM, and no AudioContext ever built.
  Nothing is named for a pattern the child did not put there.

  Alive. One drag: 0.0630 seeded, then 0.1494 at 2s, 0.3370 at 4s, 0.3668 at
  8s. The bed carries the pattern outward on its own after the finger leaves.

  Calm Mode. Same stroke, same wall clock, 0.3551 with it off against 0.1767
