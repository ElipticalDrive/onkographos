What
Environment::fog (color, density, heightFalloff, baseHeight): exponential height fog. The density is density * e^(-heightFalloff * (y - baseHeight)); the optical depth between the camera and each surface is the closed-form integral of that density along the view ray, so the fog thickens in low ground, and the result is right whether the camera is inside the layer or above it (a plain distance fog would not give that). With heightFalloff = 0 it is a uniform fog (Beer-Lambert).
Applied to every surface: lit, unlit, alpha-tested, transparent (before blending). The background (clear color / sky) is not fogged: give color the horizon's color.
One formula, sw::fogVisibility, mirrored line for line in the mesh fragment shader. Numerically safe: exponents are clamped, and the x -> 0 limit uses a series.
Off by default (density = 0): no change for existing code.
Is it "volumetric"?
It is a volume of fog with varying density, integrated exactly along each ray, but not light scattering: no light shafts / god rays, no shadowed in-scattering. That would need ray marching the shadow map per pixel, which is costly on the software backend. Say if you want it as a follow-up.
Checked locally
kronk3d_tests: 92/92 (6 new). The closed form is compared with a 40 000-step numerical integration on five rays (inside the layer, from above going down, camera below the base, steep, and almost level to exercise the series branch); also Beer's law, no overflow far below the base, lit / blended surfaces fogged and the background not, fog off by default.
kronk3d_gl_tests: 7/7. gl_fog_matches_the_software_backend (lit floor and sphere, unlit sphere, glass sphere; height fog and uniform fog): mean difference 0.07 / 255, 0.005% of pixels above 8 (silhouette pixels only), on the AMD Radeon 610M.
Not run: Windows (CI), NVIDIA.
