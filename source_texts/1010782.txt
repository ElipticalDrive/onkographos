There is no random number source. Games, simulations, tests that want varied input and hash-flooding protection all need one.
Ask
Core gets a small, fast, seedable generator with no allocation: a PCG or xoshiro variant behind a Rng struct. Rng.seeded(uint64) gives reproducible streams for tests.
Methods: next_u64, next_u32, range<I: Integer>(lo, hi) (unbiased, by rejection), float in [0, 1), and chance(p).
On slices (#300): shuffle(&var T[]) (Fisher–Yates) and choose(&T[]): &T?.
std seeds one from the operating system's entropy (getrandom / /dev/urandom): Rng.from_os().
Not cryptographic, and the book says so plainly. A cryptographic source is a separate, later question.
