The haze now draws an estimate of the light it scatters more than once, fitted to a Monte Carlo of each body's haze.
multiple.py follows lines of sight backward from the camera through every order of scattering in a fitted haze. Its first order matches the model's single scattering within 0.3%, which checks it.
The estimate, in scattering.py and haze.glsl alike: a beam, sunlight scattered forward again on its way to the camera (two and three scatterings, as a Poisson count in the optical depth along its path, with the lobes convolved, their asymmetries to the n-th power), and a glow of light scattered many times, the same in every direction, fading through the terminator. It follows the Monte Carlo within about 10% rms.
Neptune's and Titan's fits run in three rounds, the haze fitted with the estimate that a Monte Carlo of the last round's haze gives, and share the estimate's step and printout in multiple.py.
Neptune's haze is thin: its fit moves by a few per cent, and in the game it looks the same.
Titan's fit comes out with about a fifth more extinction and darker haze, albedos of 0.74, 0.67 and 0.69 against 1.00, 0.85 and 0.92. Its misfit to Cassini's frames hardly changes, by about 1%; half of it is the haze differing with latitude. In the game the sunlit ring is closer to Cassini's in colour and shape, and the ring at right angles to the sun is a quarter to a third darker than Cassini's.
The README describes the Monte Carlo, the estimate, the rounds and what changed, with Tomasko and others (2008) for the haze's albedo at Huygens's descent.
The version stays at 0.11 for now.
