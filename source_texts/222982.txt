What a user cannot get. The six-face closed-box plane wave (add_tfsf_source(closed_box=True)) is the only plane-wave source that illuminates a finite body (the default slab source makes y and z periodic). With it, a user can compute the monostatic and bistatic RCS of a finite body by a plain forward()/run(), but:
No gradient. forward() with closed_box=True refuses every external JAX transformation — jax.grad, value_and_grad, jvp, vmap, jit, checkpoint, scan — at entry (rfx/api/_execute.py _refuse_transformed_extended_tfsf, origin/main 23a6c27). So the gradient of any scattered-field observable of a finite body (RCS, NTFF data, flux) with respect to materials or a design density does not exist in rfx: the user receives a refusal, not a number.
No oblique incidence. closed_box=True raises for angle_deg != 0. The oblique engines do not cover a finite 3-D body: method="bloch" is periodic and narrowband at f0; method="methodB" (1-D auxiliary grid along k̂, real fields) uses a four-edge box with z thin-periodic (2.5-D, z-invariant), Ez only. So the RCS of a finite body under oblique illumination is not available, broadband or not.
Reproduction (plain forward runs; the gradient is refused). origin/main 23a6c27, Python 3.11, jax/jaxlib 0.10.2, CPU, seconds:
```
import jax, jax.numpy as jnp
import rfx
from rfx import Simulation
dx = 2e-3
sim = Simulation(freq_max=12e9, domain=(0.06,)*3, dx=dx, boundary='cpml', cpml_layers=8)
sim.add_tfsf_source(f0=10e9, bandwidth=0.5, polarization='ez', direction='+x', margin=10, closed_box=True)
sim.add_ntff_box((0.006,)*3, (0.054,)*3, freqs=[10e9])
lo, hi = (0.026, 0.026, 0.026), (0.034, 0.034, 0.034)   # dielectric block, eps scaled by a design scalar
def obs(a, n=40):
    r = sim.forward(n_steps=n, checkpoint=False, design_box=(lo, hi),
                    design_eps_override=a * jnp.ones((5, 5, 5)))
    return jnp.real(jnp.sum(r.ntff_data.x_lo))
print('rfx', rfx.__version__ if hasattr(rfx, '__version__') else '?', 'jax', jax.__version__)
print('plain forward:', float(obs(2.0)))
try:
    print('grad:', float(jax.grad(obs)(2.0)))
except Exception as e:
    print('grad:', type(e).__name__, str(e)[:300])
```
Output:
```
plain forward: 1.4958798360154707e-13
grad: NotImplementedError CustomWaveform and closed_box TFSF cannot be called under external JAX transformations (grad/value_and_grad/JVP/vmap/jit/checkpoint/scan): transformed scattered-field traces exceed the 9-ULP cross-trace contract. ...
```
For (2): the same scene with angle_deg=30 and closed_box=True raises NotImplementedError("CustomWaveform and closed_box require normal incidence (angle_deg=0)") (rfx/api/__init__.py, add_tfsf_source).
The question for (1): which quantity exceeds which bar, and why. The refusal's own reason is that the traced per-step scattered-field values exceed the 9-ULP cross-trace bar. The cross-trace rule has two bars: 9 float32 ULP at the array's peak for a per-step quantity, and 1e-4 of its peak for a quantity summed over the record (a DFT accumulator, NTFF data, an objective, a gradient). A user-facing RCS and its gradient are summed quantities. So the measurement that decides whether this is a fix or a research question: traced against plain on the summed RCS / NTFF data (judged at 1e-4), and the gradient against central finite differences and a closed form (e.g. a dielectric sphere against Mie) — if the summed quantities meet 1e-4, the refusal is broader than the rule it cites.
Related. #1422 (the oblique source cannot launch p-polarization) is the same source family but a different cause. Capability introduced in PR #1336 (ledger entry "Finite six-face normal TFSF admits real custom waveforms": "external JAX transformations … refuse").
Witness of what it changes. Both items block gradient-based design of any finite scatterer under plane-wave illumination (coatings, metal patterns, shapes), and any oblique RCS of a finite body. No silently wrong value is involved; both are refusals.

Why oblique incidence is needed.
Synthetic case: a 0.30 m PEC plate at 3 GHz, lit by a short dipole on its normal at distance R; the reference is an independent solver.
Replacing the dipole's incident field by one plane wave puts the echo off by 14° at R = 2a²/λ (1.8 m) and by 27° at 0.9 m.
Within about 2a²/λ, 5–25 plane waves are needed to stay inside 1 dB / 10°.
With the source 45° off the normal, 5 plane waves are needed out to 3.6 m.
The extra waves arrive off-normal, so a normal-incidence closed box cannot supply them.
Body: a finite 3-D body inside a six-face closed box. So far a grid-aligned PEC plate about 3λ × 3λ (node-thin sheet), and spheres. The body is not rotated on the grid.
Angles: any direction on the illuminated side.
In the cases above, the central direction is 0–45° off the body normal.
The bundle spreads about ±35° around the central direction at the shortest range.
So directions up to about 80° from a box face normal; a stated upper limit would be enough for us to plan around.
Both azimuth planes are needed, because a bundle is a cone, not one plane of incidence.
Polarizations: two orthogonal linear polarizations per direction (any pair, stated in the output). Weights are applied afterwards.
Band:
One frequency (a single DFT bin) is enough today.
A broadband pulse is welcome if the incident field stays accurate at the bin read.
Observable:
The complex tangential E and H on a closed surface around the body at the DFT bin, as export_ntff_surface gives now.
Normalised by the incident field at a stated phase origin (the box centre) for an arbitrary k̂, so that responses to different directions can be summed with complex weights.
Not only the far field: the user evaluates the scattered field at finite distance from these surface currents.
Several plane waves at once:
Not needed; one run per (direction, polarization) is what we use, because the same per-wave responses are reused with different weights for every source position.
Up to about 49 directions × 2 polarizations per body, so per-run cost matters more than simultaneity.
Gradient: not needed now. Later, possibly with respect to body geometry; we will say so here before relying on it.

Item 2 design: one closed-box plane wave for every direction, from an exact discrete plane wave. Posted before any code; comments from the two users welcome, especially on §8.
Base: origin/main 43769ab (PR 1675 merged).
Facts are marked with how they were checked; estimates are marked "estimate".
1. What is asked (two users, #1662 comments of 2026-10-12)
A finite 3-D body in a six-face closed box, not rotated on the grid. User A: a ~5 λ flat plate with a dielectric
backing, 1.25 mm cells and finer; user B: a ~3 λ node-thin PEC plate and spheres.
Directions: A 0–60° off the body normal in ~10° steps, one plane first; B any direction up to ~80° off a face normal,
both azimuth planes (a cone of directions).
Both linear polarizations (A: s and p; B: any orthogonal pair, stated).
Band: A 8–12 GHz from one pulse; B one DFT bin, a pulse welcome.
Observables: A NTFF far field (monostatic and bistatic), normalised by the incident amplitude; B the complex tangential
E and H on a closed surface (export_ntff_surface), normalised by the complex incident field at a stated phase origin
so per-direction responses can be summed with complex weights. One run per (direction, polarization); B up to 49 × 2
runs per body, so cost per run matters.
Gradient: A yes (item 1 at oblique incidence); B not now.
2. Why the existing engines do not extend (leader read the code)
closed_box=True refuses angle_deg != 0 (rfx/sources/tfsf.py:303). Its corrections are the sparse form of the
mask commutator M·curl(F_inc) − curl(M·F_inc) for one direction and one polarization (_apply_closed_box_e/_h,
rfx/sources/tfsf.py:837-891), fed by a 1-D line along x.
method="bloch" (rfx/sources/tfsf_2d.py) fixes k_y at f0: periodic and narrowband by construction (its docstring:
"broadband oblique would need a 1D-aux-along-k̂ method"; ledger trap "Oblique Bloch TFSF at fixed k_y always excites
the fan's cutoff line").
method="methodB" (rfx/sources/tfsf_oblique_open.py) is a 1-D line along k̂ whose cell is chosen so its phase
velocity matches the 3-D grid's at f0, read by linear interpolation, on a four-edge 2.5-D box (z periodic, Ez only).
Extending it to 3-D keeps two errors that grow over a 40 % band and a 5 λ box: the dispersion match holds at f0 only,
and the interpolation is approximate. Estimate: a phase-velocity mismatch of ~1e-4–1e-3 across 5 λ leaves −30 to −50 dB
of incident field in the scattered-field region off f0 — above a small body's backscatter. Not measured; it is the
reason to choose an exact method, not a claim.
3. Method: the exact discrete plane wave (Tan & Potter, IEEE TAP 58(8), 2641–2648, 2010)
Choose an integer triple m = (m_x, m_y, m_z) with gcd 1. A field that depends on the Yee lattice only through the
projected index p = m_x·i + m_y·j + m_z·k (i, j, k including each component's half-integer offset) stays of that form
under the 3-D Yee update in vacuum, because the update commutes with every lattice shift s with m·s = 0. So the 3-D update
restricted to such fields IS a 1-D update over p:
ex(p) += (dt/ε0)·[ (hz(p + m_y/2) − hz(p − m_y/2))/Δy − (hy(p + m_z/2) − hy(p − m_z/2))/Δz ]
and its five siblings. Each component lives on one residue class of p mod 1, spacing 1 in p (physical spacing Δ/|m| on a
cubic grid). Consequences:
The incident field it gives at every 3-D node is an exact solution of the 3-D discrete equations, for every frequency,
every component and both polarizations — not a dispersion match at one frequency. The TF/SF cancellation is exact up
to float arithmetic (Tan's thesis reports −300 dB, the double-precision floor; rfx runs float32).
The 1-D update is a restriction of the 3-D one, so it is stable whenever the 3-D grid is.
The realized direction is k̂ ∝ (m_x/Δx, m_y/Δy, m_z/Δz): a requested direction is realized by the nearest such triple.
A table of nearest integer triples (119 directions θ 0–80° × φ 0–90°): with components ≤ 100 the realized
direction is within 0.10° of the request for all 119 (median 0.013°), |m| ≤ 173; ≤ 40 gives ≤ 0.36° (median 0.06°).
User A's set in one plane: θ = 10/20/30/40/50/60° → m = (3,0,17), (4,0,11), (15,0,26), (21,0,25), (25,0,21), (26,0,15),
within 0.03°. The source reports the realized m, k̂ and polarization vectors; requested-vs-realized tolerance is a
parameter (default 0.1°).
Normal incidence is m = (±1, 0, 0): the same 1-D line rfx uses today. One engine serves every direction; the current
normal closed-box implementation is replaced, not kept beside it.
4. The pieces
1-D line. Six component arrays over p, no absorber: the line is long enough that no reflection from either end reaches the
box within the record (total length ≈ |m|·(c·T)/Δ plus the box's projection, Σ|m_u|·N_u; estimate for user A's scene at |m| ≈ 30 and
T ≈ 2,300 cells of travel: ~1.4e5 nodes per component, ~3 MB). This removes the auxiliary-absorber reflection, whose
reading above ~80° is an instrument reading (ledger, Oblique TF/SF auxiliary absorber at 80–82°: the reading does not behave like a PML reflection (15 cells 60 dB below the continuum limit; doubling 200→400 cells reads 6 dB worse) #1371). Advanced inside the scan, under stop_gradient (the
incident field does not depend on the design), as the 1-D line is today.
The length needs a bound on the record: until_decay is admitted with the closed box today (only a CustomWaveform is
refused, rfx/api/_execute.py:4134-4138), so the line is sized from the run's maximum step count and the oblique box
is refused when no bound exists — otherwise an end reflection re-enters the box at full amplitude, silently.
The line kernels repeat the 3-D update's own association of terms (rfx/core/yee.py), so line and grid round alike
as far as the compiler allows.
One compiled program per box and record length serves every direction and polarization: m, the index tables and the
line (padded to the longest in a set) are data of fixed shape, not trace-time constants — user B's 98 runs, and a vmap
over directions, then compile once.
Launch. A soft source on a plane p_src before the box. Each E component sits on its own residue class, so each is
added at its own nearest p with the analytic waveform delayed by its projected offset; the argument is formed as
(step − d_c)·dt/τ with d_c in steps, because t is float32 (rfx/stepping/uniform.py:587). Estimate: launched
cross-polarization ≤ −80 dB (the mismatch is mostly a longitudinal residue at the source plane, zero after a zero-mean
pulse). The pulse comes from the waveform class (The truncated differentiated-Gaussian drive starts with a jump and leaves a nonzero integral, and it is implemented as several inline copies #1668: the inline truncated pulse leaves a static charge). The
launched wave is not the waveform (monostatic RCS is not translation-invariant: 2.194 dB spread from moving the target inside a FIXED box with byte-identical rasterization #820): the incident field is always read from the line itself (item 4).
Box corrections. The mask commutator on the six faces with all incident components, gathered from the line by
static int32 index tables (one entry per face node; no interpolation weights). Edges and corners follow from the
commutator; the current normal code is its one-direction special case.
Incident reference. The spectrum of the launched incident E (three complex components per bin) at a stated phase
origin (the box centre), from the line's DFT at each component's nearest node shifted to the origin by the exact
discrete phase exp(−i k̃(ω)·δ), k̃ from the 3-D dispersion relation along k̂. Its time stamps are the ones the NTFF
accumulators use for E and for H (rfx/ntff_surface.py): today's reference stamps E at n·dt unless reference_index
is passed (rfx/sources/tfsf.py:754), which is harmless for |σ| but is a 2π·f·dt phase (≈10° at 12 GHz) in user B's
complex weights. Replaces measure_normal_incident_spectrum for all directions.
Declaration. add_tfsf_source(closed_box=True, ...) gains a direction (θ, φ about a declared normal axis, or a
vector) and a polarization ('s'/'p' with respect to the plane holding k̂ and that axis, or a vector); the realized
values are readable after declaration. At θ = 0 the s/p basis is degenerate: φ then names the E direction. Same
refusals as today (each checked at declaration, not only in prose): graded mesh, multi-device, subgridding, ADI, 2-D,
order 4; vacuum on the box shell and its stencils (the one vacuum rule, every entry); CPML on all faces.
Observables. Far field through the ONE far-field transform and directivity quadrature (G7, lead order), with
monostatic = direction −k̂, bistatic over a declared set, co/cross components in θ̂/φ̂ of each scattering direction,
normalised by item 4. export_ntff_surface divides by the incident component at the phase origin and records the
origin, realized k̂ and polarization in its output. The surface must lie in the scattered-field region: the existing
rule already refuses an NTFF box that does not enclose the closed box on all three axes
(rfx/boundaries/tfsf.py:245, planes from rfx/sources/tfsf.py:124); user A's margins (CPML + gap + box) are
budgeted in V8.
Gradient. forward() under transforms as item 1 (PR 1675), with oblique rows added to
tests/contracts/test_tfsf_closed_box_transforms.py.
5. Checks declared before any code (the predeclaration file will hold the bars and is not edited after)
V0 the corrections alone, no propagation: build the dense incident on the grid from the index tables and compare the
sparse corrections with M·curl(F) − curl(M·F), as test_six_face_corrections_equal_mask_curl_commutator does for the
normal box (tests/unit/sources/test_tfsf_closed_box.py:46-70). Catches an index, sign or half-step error directly.
V1 exactness: empty closed box; directions 0°, 10–60° by 10° in one plane, 30° in the other, a cone direction
(θ 45°, φ 30°) and 80°; s and p. The field in the scattered-field region over the whole record, per step, relative to
the incident peak: ≤ −100 dB on the small always-on scene. A wrong index, sign or half step reads −20 to −60 dB
(estimate). Obliquely the grid sums two nonzero differences where the normal case sums one, so line and grid can round
differently and the residue grows with the record (ledger cross-trace entry: one last bit times the accumulated field).
On user-scale records V1, V2 and V6 are therefore judged as growth over three record lengths against that declared law,
not against a fixed dB.
V2 total field equals incident inside the empty box at sampled nodes, per step, within 9 float32 ULP of the incident
peak.
V3 normal incidence continuity: m = (1,0,0) against the current closed box, with the launch at the old distance from
the box (source 3 cells inside the old line's absorber, AUX_SRC_OFFSET_1D, rfx/sources/tfsf.py:65,436).
Bit-identical until the old absorber's reflection can return (the window is computed and stated before the run), or, if the general commutator rounds differently, within the cross-trace bars over
three record lengths with that cause shown; after that, the difference is the old absorber's reflection, measured and
stated.
V4 sphere against Mie at oblique incidence: dielectric sphere eps_r 4, a = 6 mm; incidence 0°, 30°, 60°, s and p;
bistatic σ in the plane of incidence at 9 bins 8–12 GHz; meshes 1.5 / 1.0 / 0.75 mm. A sphere is rotation-invariant, so
σ against the angle between k̂ and the scattering direction must collapse across incidences. Expected: the gap to Mie
of the same size and order as the normal-incidence trend (backscatter mean 1.30 / 1.07 / 0.88 dB) and falling with dx;
the spread between incidences at one mesh is the staircased sphere's anisotropy.
V5 reciprocity on a coated plate: σ for (k̂_i → k̂_s, polarization q → r) against (−k̂_s → −k̂_i, r → q). The Yee
scheme is reciprocal; the residual is the far-field quadrature. Bar set in the predeclaration file.

Realized direction within 0.1°, reported exactly: yes, acceptable. We build the plane-wave sum after the runs, and the weights can be computed for the realized directions. From absorber, item 2 (oblique closed box): a design finding, caught before any merge.
What was found. The 1-D line of the exact discrete plane wave carries more than the intended wave. A field f(m·r) sampled on the integer lattice also contains every 3-D plane wave with k′Δ ≡ q·m (mod 2π per axis). At larger q these wrap onto small k′ in other directions: real, well-resolved plane waves at another angle.
The box corrections cancel them outside the box exactly, so the scattered-field leakage stays at −124 to −134 dB on every tested direction.
Inside the box, though, they are extra incident field from the wrong direction.
A single-node soft source excites them. Measured on the cone direction (45°, 30°): −30 dB cross-polarized and longitudinal content, which depends on distance and record length.
Why it matters for the users here. The lowest frequency where such a wave propagates scales as 1/dx.
At 1 mm it is 9–10 GHz for 30°, 50° and 60° and user A's set, and 7.4 GHz for 40°.
At user A's 1.25 mm it falls to about 7–8 GHz, inside 8–12 GHz.
Change. The launch becomes a forward-travelling soft source with a Gaussian profile along the line. Its width, 5/q₁ nodes, puts the aliased branches more than 100 dB down; the in-band shaping is at most 1.4 dB and is absorbed by the incident-spectrum normalisation. Normal incidence keeps today's source.
New check: launched purity of at most −60 dB cross-polarized and longitudinal on every direction, at two distances and two record lengths.
Design: rfx-archive rfx/records/20261013-oblique-closedbox/ (DESIGN.md Appendix A; it will be updated there with the next records commit).
