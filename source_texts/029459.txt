Summary
Background
#228 made firstHit()/intersects() work on any shape, but only the closed-form shapes and the mesh SDFs (#236) were traced exactly. Every composition (fused nodes, BVHUnion, Tape) was sphere traced, at tens of signed-distance evaluations per ray. Marching also reports a ray that passes within its tolerance of a surface as a hit, so a near miss can come back as a crossing up to the width of a feature too early. That is where the two host/device DIFFER rays on #228's and #236's receipts came from.
This is the second follow-up of #228 (roadmap #232, "Span ray tracing"). The renderer (tier 4) waits for it. The design was settled in an interview (decisions D27–D35):
a side table for the Tape;
unions, intersections, complements and affine maps traced exactly, everything else marched in its box;
mesh crossings collected into spans;
span lists of 8 with a horizon and a restart;
unions asking only for first crossings;
a public spans() on RayForm;
a forced-march switch;
one PR;
signature detection instead of the available flag.
A scratch measurement first checked that exact tracing pays off. On a scene of 50 spheres and boxes, it ran 10–16× faster than marching on the host and 10–75× on the GPU. In that measurement marching was wrong on 0.15–0.3% of rays, and the exact answer matched a dense sampling of the SDF on every one of them.
Solution
Spans. A composition is traced from where the ray is inside each operand: its spans. Union, intersection and complement combine span lists as sets of points; a difference is an intersection with a complement. An affine map traces its operand along the mapped ray and maps the distances and normals back.
RaySpansT<T> holds the list: up to 8 spans, each a pair of SpanEndT. An end is a distance, the leaf it lies on, and whether it is an exact crossing, a marched one, or an Open end. It carries no normal: 12 bytes in float, 16 in double.
The normal and primitive are found once per ray, at the crossing the trace ends at. The composition follows the leaf id down to the leaf, mapping the ray through the affine maps above it, and calls the leaf's own firstHit() over a small window. The normal is mapped back by the transposed matrices and turned to face against the ray where it enters and along it where it leaves, which covers any complements in between.
Leaf ids: a fused node numbers its leaves depth first (LeafCount/FirstLeaf traits), BVHUnion by primitive, and the Tape by the leaf's ray clause, which it finds again by following its ray program, into the BVH-union operand whose clauses hold it. That operand is found in O(log n): a bisection over where the ray programs begin, then up a parent entry per sub-program (8 bytes each); the union's ray clause holds its operands' sub-program range, so a sibling union's leaf is skipped. A scan over the operands took 9.8 µs per ray on a union of 4,096 balls, the bisection 0.71 µs.
A union inserts its operand's spans into the list in place, with no scratch list and no copy back.
The list also carries a horizon: how far along the ray it is complete. A list that overflows drops the far spans and lowers its horizon. If the first crossing is not found before the horizon, the trace restarts from there. So the answer is never wrong, only slower for a ray through more than 8 pieces.
An operand under unions only needs its first crossing (its list stops there, with its horizon), unless the ray starts inside it. An operand of an intersection or a complement gives all its spans. Each union operand is traced only up to the union's current horizon.
The span maths (EBGeometry_RaySpans.hpp) is shared by the fused nodes, BVHUnion and the Tape, as CSGOps is for values.
Ray forms.
RayForm<S> may now define spans(shape, ray, tMin, tMax, options, firstOnly, spans). Without it, a composition finds an operand's spans by calling its firstHit() from one crossing to the next.
The closed-form shapes give their one span. The sphere, the capsule's caps and the cylinders now solve their quadratic without cancellation (discriminant r² − |oc × d|², nearer root c / q): before, in float, a small ball or thin cylinder far from the ray's start was hit or missed by rounding within about 1e-3 of its radius, which made 74 of 262,144 crowd rays differ between host and device. The mesh SDFs collect the nearest 17 crossings in one BVH walk and pair them by the side each face is crossed from; TriangleAoSoA::rayCrossings() gives every lane's crossing for this.
Fused:: union, intersection, difference, complement and affine maps get ray forms (EBGeometry_CSGRayForms.hpp). The other fused operations have none and are marched.
BVHUnion walks its BVH along the ray, nearest box first, pruning boxes beyond the union's horizon.
The available flag is gone: a ray form is found by its firstHit() signature (HasRayFirstHit; also HasRaySpans, HasRayIntersects). Each detector is one place to swap for a concept under C++20. Public hasRayForm<S, T> and hasRaySpans<S, T> expose them, so a user can static_assert that a ray form is found.
Tape.
Beside the clauses, the compiler writes a ray program for the tape and each BVH-union operand. It covers the expression's unions, intersections, complements and affine maps over primitives with ray forms, in clause order, and runs on a stack of span lists (Tape::RayStackSize = 4).
A subtree with anything else (a blend, offset, elongation, repetition, blur, mollify, or a primitive without a ray form), or one that would need more lists, is marched as a whole over its own clause range.
The ray programs are stored after the evaluator's clauses and programs, in the same pool arrays. evaluateBody() is untouched and never reads them.
getNumPrograms() and getNumClauses() still count only the evaluator's.
A hit names the leaf crossed, or for a marched subtree the deciding leaf (closestPrimitive()).
RayOptions::m_march marches any shape even where it has a ray form: a reference to compare with, and a way round a ray form.
Tests (TestRay):
span lists against the sets of points they cover;
traced normals against the normalised gradient of each composition's own value at the hit, through affine maps and BVH unions (away from edges);
fused compositions and tapes (BVH union, difference, complement, rotation and scaling, deep copy and view) against their values sampled along rays from outside and inside;
a row of 20 balls with a slab cut out (overflow and restart) against crossings worked on the axis, and cut by a half-space so that the first crossing lies exactly where a full list stops;
a composition whose operand never decides ends Unresolved after its 64 passes;
two sibling BVH unions in one tape, one rotated and translated, name each leaf and its normal;
rays past a small far ball and a thin far cylinder at 1 ± 3e-4 radii are hit or missed by their exact distance;
hasRayForm/hasRaySpans, including a mistyped ray form;
the three mesh SDFs, a user shape with only firstHit() and a marched offset inside differences, and a mesh in a tape;
the composition test now expects exact crossings, and the marched ones on request;
[gpu]: a fused part, two tapes and a mesh in a fused difference against the host.
All pass in both precisions, under CUDA on the device, and under every preset.
Probe Performance/RayCSG: 25 balls and 25 boxes less 10 balls, fused and through a tape, traced and marched; each traced case notes marched/traced. crowd-tape: 4,096 small balls as one BVH union in a tape, traced, which times the leaf lookup. It is a new folder rather than new cases in Ray/, following the probe-inlining lesson. Docs:
Rays.rst: a "Compositions" section.
ImplemRays.rst: the ray forms table, the user RayForm without available, a "Spans" section, and m_march.
ImplemCSG.rst: the tape's ray program.
PerformanceTesting.rst, Performance/README.md, TestingLocally.rst and README.md updated to match.
Side-effects
Breaking (dev crunch mode): RayForm<S>::available is removed. A user ray form is detected by its firstHit() signature; one with a wrong signature is now marched instead of failing to compile. static_assert(hasRayForm<MyShape<T>, T>) turns that back into a compile error (ImplemRays.rst shows it); an old available member is ignored.
firstHit()/intersects() on fused compositions, BVHUnion and tapes now return exact crossings (m_exact true where every leaf has a ray form) where they used to march. Answers move by up to the march tolerance, and near misses that marching counted as hits are now misses. The Ray/csg-* checksums change accordingly.
The Tape's pool grows by the ray programs: about one 16-byte clause per expression node, plus one 8-byte range per sub-program. On the probe's tapes that is +12–66%; the small tapes grow most.
Tape::Layout gains m_numPrograms and m_rayPrograms (two uint32_t). Tape is the friend of RayForm<Tape>.
Host inlining: the extra compile-side code in EBGeometry_TapeImplem.hpp made GCC keep double's whole primitive dispatch out of evaluateBody<0>, costing up to +28% on the double Tape cases. The fix is a fixed host split of the dispatch: Tape::dispatchRest(), marked with the new EBGEOMETRY_HOST_NOINLINE, takes the types from index 6 on, which is where dev's float build already split. With it, the probe's float symbols match dev byte for byte, and double inlines one shape more than dev.
The value kernels still use 168 registers (1- and 16-evaluation float).
The device frame of a traced Tape is a few kilobytes per call level: span lists in local memory.
Why span ends carry no normal. The first CUDA receipt showed the classic CSG solid, fused in float, 3–6× slower than dev marched it. Nsight Compute showed the kernel stalled on local-memory instructions: 65% waiting on L1TEX loads, 41% on a full LG queue. The cost was the number of span words moved, not the lists' size: capacity 8, 4 and 2 timed the same. Dropping the normals from the ends and uniting in place took that case from 1.29 to 0.58 ms per 262,144 rays (marched: about 0.4 ms), and the 60-part scene from 8.95 to 3.38 ms in float.
New macro EBGEOMETRY_HOST_NOINLINE (EBGeometry_GPU.hpp).
Alternative solutions
Plain sphere tracing, improved (box skipping). Simpler, but it stays 10–75× slower on the scenes measured, and only right to within its tolerance.
Op::RayMark in the clause list (as [DO NOT MERGE] Ray tape: closed-form ray forms, the tape's ray program, a generic renderer (3/3) #216). One structure, but a new case in evaluateBody(), the register-sensitive path (Tape: evaluator performance swings 10-20% on unrelated code changes (GPU registers, GCC inlining of the primitive dispatch) #226).
Dropping spans past the capacity (as [DO NOT MERGE] Ray tape: closed-form ray forms, the tape's ray program, a generic renderer (3/3) #216). Simpler, but it can give a wrong first crossing under an intersection or complement.
An event merge with no span lists (step through the operands' crossings, stop at the first change of the combined state). No capacity, but it re-queries operands and needs a per-level cursor stack in the tape's walk.
Ray clauses in pool arrays of their own. Clearer, but each array's padding dominates on small tapes; they now share the evaluator's arrays.
Normals from the gradient of the composition's value at the hit, instead of from the leaf crossed. Simpler, but the normals become approximate (about 1e-3 in float), and blurred at edges.
Follow-ups noted:
On the GPU, a small fused solid in float still traces slower than marching, which ran it in registers: 1.5–3× on the receipt's classic CSG solid.
The fused path on the host is about 3× slower than a hand-written exact loop on a union of 50: general list handling per operand.
A Tape built only for value queries still stores its ray programs (+12–66% pool bytes on the receipt). A switch to leave them out would spare value-only users (AMReX, Chombo); raised in review round 1.
The host dispatch split (Tape::DispatchSplit, which fixed double signedDistance()) also puts primitives past the sixth type out of line in closestPrimitive(): about 6% slower on a tape of fused parts (accepted on the receipt). Tune the split.
Two death tests (Analytic shapes: invalid constructor arguments…, Fused: rejects invalid arguments…) failed once each locally under ctest -j8 with builds running, and passed on rerun.
All of these are worth a look after the renderer.
Performance
Both receipts are red, with the reasons accepted on them (Performance/receipt.md), recorded on the merge with dev 8f43bf5 (after #234 and #238), with #238's defaults (2 rounds of 3). Another session ran a one-core simulation during the recording.
Host (beea848 against dev 8f43bf5): 18 major, all for pool bytes; 1 minor; 3 faster.
The Tape's signedDistance() (Tape/*) runs within noise. Its pool grows +14–80%: the ray programs and the parent entries.
ClosestPrimitive/parts is about 6% slower (+5.8% float, +19.3% double with a wide interval; +2–8% on the three earlier receipts): the host dispatch split puts the fused parts, user primitives past the sixth type, out of line. Accepted, with a follow-up.
Minor: Ray/mesh-polygons/intersects/float +4.3% [+2.3%, +6.3%]; not seen on the earlier receipts, possibly the simulation.
Ray/csg-tape traces 47–48% faster, Ray/csg-fused 8% (float) and 30% (double); their checksums change.
New RayCSG cases: traced is 6.8–7.5× faster than marched.
Accepted: "Ray programs stored beside the Tape's clauses (about 16 bytes per expression node, and 8 per sub-program) grow its pool; compositions are now traced exactly instead of marched, which changes the CSG ray checksums. closestPrimitive() on a tape whose primitives sit past the host dispatch split (fused parts, meshes, user types) is about 6% slower; a follow-up tunes the split."
CUDA (beea848 against dev 8f43bf5): 2 major, 6 faster, 6 differ.
Ray/csg-fused float is MAJOR: +214% (morton), +82% (random). Dev marched this 5-part solid in registers.
Everything else traced is faster: Ray/csg-tape 21–66%, Ray/csg-fused double 72–78%. On the new scene, traced is 3.5–4.3× faster than marched (fused, float), 30× (fused, double) and 20–23× (Tape).
DIFFER: one ray per case, float only: the marched scene's grazing ray, and one crowd ray (74 before the sphere fix).
Accepted: "On the device, a small fused solid in float traces slower than dev marched it in registers (the span lists live in local memory); the Tape and larger scenes trace faster. A few float rays graze a surface tangentially; host and device round them differently. Checksums change because compositions are now traced exactly."
The register checks still hold: the Tape's value kernels use 168 registers (1- and 16-evaluation float kernels), as on dev.
Device compile time: the CI CUDA target set builds in about 12 minutes on two cores (dev: 5); review round 1 fixed a blow-up past CI's limit.

…y from their operands' spans

Compositions are traced from where the ray is inside each operand (RaySpansT, up to 8 spans with a
horizon; a trace restarts from the horizon when its first crossing lies beyond it). Union, intersection
and complement combine span lists, affine maps trace the mapped ray; unions ask only for first
crossings. RayForm may define spans(); the closed forms give their span, the mesh SDFs pair their
nearest crossings. Fused union/intersection/difference/complement/affine and BVHUnion get ray forms;
the Tape gets a ray program beside its clauses, stored in the same arrays, which the evaluator never
reads; subtrees with other operations are marched in their boxes. RayOptions::m_march marches any
shape. RayForm::available is replaced by signature detection.

The Tape's primitive dispatch is split at a fixed point on the host (Tape::dispatchRest,
EBGEOMETRY_HOST_NOINLINE), so GCC inlines the common shapes whatever else the file holds.

…e leaf crossed

On a device the span lists live in local memory, and the profile showed the cost is the number of
local-memory operations, not the lists' size. A span end is now a distance, the leaf it lies on and its
kind (12 bytes in float, was 24). The normal and primitive are found once per ray: the composition follows
the leaf down (fused: depth-first leaf ids; BVHUnion: primitive index; Tape: the leaf's clause, found again
by following its ray program into BVH union operands), maps the ray through the affine maps above it,
calls the leaf's firstHit() over a small window, maps the normal back, and turns it to face the way the ray
crosses. A union now inserts its operand's spans in place, with no scratch list or copy.

GPU, 262,144 rays, fused: the 5-part solid in float 1.29 -> 0.58 ms, the 60-part scene 8.95 -> 3.38 ms
(float) and 15.3 -> 8.87 ms (double). The traced normals are checked against the gradient of each
composition's value in TestRay.

Round 3 review of #237: the Tape ray-program compiler and evaluator
I read the ray code in EBGeometry_TapeImplem.hpp: compileProgram, emit, raySlots, traced, flatten, rayEvaluate<D>, rayLeaf/dispatchRayLeaf, rayUnion<D> and rayLeafHit<D>. I also read traceSpans() for how it uses them. I did not build or run anything, and I did not read the ray declarations in EBGeometry_Tape.hpp line by line. I found no correctness bug. There is one cost problem and a few robustness and test gaps.
What I checked and found sound
Span-list stack bound.
raySlots() and the level + raySlots > RayStackSize test in emit() agree with how rayEvaluate uses lists[]. The first operand sits at level L, the others at L+1, and slots = max(first, 1 + other).
A marched subtree needs 1 slot. A parent that passed the check can never leave a child with no room.
A BVH union counts as 1 slot because its operands run in their own rayEvaluate frames.
Complement and affine nodes keep the level, matching RayEmit{true, level}.
all/first-only flags.
Compile-time flags (RayEmit::m_all) are set to true for intersection and complement subtrees.
At run time firstOnly = !a_all && flag == 0. Operand programs of a BVH union are compiled with the same m_all, so passing a_all through rayUnion stays consistent with the union clause's own flag.
flatten. The layout is clauses, then marched ranges, then ray programs. Marched clauses get m_a += numPrograms, which matches the marched ranges' position in m_flatPrograms. getNumPrograms() is unchanged.
Call depth. calls() is recorded in buildUnion for every operand, so rayUnion<D> has D > 0 wherever a UnionBVH ray clause exists. A BVH union inside a marched range gets its depth from the same bookkeeping.
Affine frames. The value PushAffine is emitted before the ray PushAffine, so a marched range sees the mapped point. Subtree marches in the unit-direction mapped frame, and mapBack rescales and clamps. rayLeafHit maps t0 through mapRay and the normal back through the transposed matrices. hit.m_point comes from the original ray in traceSpans, so closestPrimitive(hit.m_point) for MarchedLeaf is evaluated in the right frame.
Findings
rayLeafHit descent into a BVH union is O(operands) per hit (TapeImplem.hpp:2058-2076).
consider() loops over every unbounded and bounded operand of the union and reads each one's m_begin, to find the program with the greatest m_begin <= a_clause.
The trace itself prunes through the BVH, so a union of thousands of parts pays that linear scan once per ray hit. The probe's 50-part scene hides this. At around 5k parts it could be a visible fraction of a traced ray.
Two ways to avoid it. At compile time, store the operand programs in a table sorted by m_begin and binary-search it. Or put the operand's program id in the leaf id.
Whatever you choose, please add a RayCSG case with a few thousand parts, so a regression shows up on the receipt.
Several UnionBVH clauses in one ray program pick the wrong operand first (TapeImplem.hpp:2049-2081).
Take Intersection(UnionBVH A, UnionBVH B) with the hit in B. The first UnionBVH clause met is A's. Every A operand begins before a_clause, so A's last operand is chosen and rayLeafHit<D-1> runs over it for nothing.
The answer is still right. No clause in that operand equals a_clause, the returned hit is not a hit, and the outer loop goes on to B. But it is wasted work, and it walks the operand's own nested unions as well.
Choosing only an operand whose program range, with its nested programs, contains a_clause would remove it. The next program's m_begin can serve as the end bound. This falls away if finding 1 is solved with a table that records ends.
I did not find a test with two sibling BVH unions in one program, or with a BVH union inside an affine map inside a union operand. That would pin both this and the frame mapping.
A failed descent is silent.
traceSpans() takes leaf.m_primitive and leaf.m_normal from a_resolve without checking leaf.hit(). If the leaf cannot be found again, which would be a compiler/evaluator disagreement, the caller gets Hit with a zero normal and a garbage primitive.
After the loop in rayLeafHit, adding EBGEOMETRY_EXPECT(hit.hit()) would catch that in debug builds. The leaf branch forces m_status = Hit, so a real match always satisfies it.
The same applies to dispatchRayLeaf/dispatchLeafHit, which already have EXPECT(false) on the fall-through.
Frame size (informational).
Each rayEvaluate frame holds lists[4] plus scratch, and rayUnion adds operand. That is six lists per call level, and it is multiplied by MaxCallDepth on the device. The PR body already notes this.
If a future operation needs more depth, the scratch use in Intersection and Complement is the cheapest to share with operand. They are never live at the same time in one frame, but operand lives in rayUnion, one level up.
Minor, emit() comment. The comment above the leaf number (TapeImplem.hpp:718-719) says the number is "in the clause's otherwise unused m_b", but the ray clause also stores it there (pushRay(..., index, m_nextLeaf, ...)). Saying so would make it clearer that the ray Leaf clause's m_b is the closestPrimitive leaf number and not the clause id used for nameLeaf(pc, ...).
Verdict
No blocking issue in the ray-program compiler or evaluator. I would address finding 1 with a probe case before the renderer depends on large unions, add a test for finding 2, and add the EXPECT from finding 3. The .rst audit is still pending now that the PR is out of draft.

Round 4 review of #237: the bisected BVH-union operand lookup
I read rayLeafHit<D>, the parent entries in flatten(), how finalize() and the deep copy carry them, compileProgram (m_current and m_parents), and the UnionBVH ray clause emission (first..end). I did not build or run anything, so this review is from reading only. I found no correctness bug.

Layout. flatten() writes the compiled programs, then the marched ranges, then the ray programs (m_rayPrograms + i), then one parent entry per sub-program (m_rayPrograms + numPrograms + i, parent in m_begin). rayLeafHit reads exactly those offsets.

Bisection.
The ray programs' m_begin values do not decrease with the program index, because flatten lays them out in index order.
Empty ray programs (null a_ray, from marched subtrees) have begin == end, equal to the next program's begin. The last program with begin <= a_clause is then never an empty one: a following non-empty program shares its begin, and a trailing empty one begins at the total clause count, which is greater than any clause id.
lo starts at 0 and begin(0) == 0, so a clause before every other begin still resolves.
Parent walk.
compileProgram records m_current as the parent and restores it on exit, so an operand's parent is the program that contains the union.
The walk stops at the direct child of a_program. The range test [c.m_b, c.m_c) then also confirms the child belongs to this union and not to a sibling.

A leaf that sits in a_program itself ends the walk at operand == a_program, which is skipped.
A nested union inside an operand is reached by the recursive call, with D - 1.
