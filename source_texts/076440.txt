Volume Rendering
We spent the whole hour considering merits of volume rendering.
Frequently used in sci viz.
Not many Visit customers using volume rendering. Possible reason is because primarily doing 2D work, not 3D work.
Why are people unaware of 3D rendering? Maybe because they have never ventured out to it. Some of the stuff is unintuitive. Transfer functions are a big investment of time+effort. New techniques are hard to learn and know how to use for quant. Volume rendering can be very helpful for exploratory analysis, but may not currently be performant enough.
Volume rendering is less of a quantitative analysis tool and more of a communicative tool (e.g. Making beautiful movies).
How can we get one of our customers to start using volume rendering?
Need to know more about what they are looking for.
Most customers are looking to validate against experiment data (1D and 2D).
How useful is volume rendering for quant analysis outside of the lab?
Seems like it's much more useful for communication + monitoring.
Image-space vs. Object-space Design of Visualization tools
Object-space design: data is read from disk, operations are performed to extract out objects such as material boundaries, iso-surfaces, stream-lines, external surfaces, glyph clouds and these objects are rendered to provide the image
Image-space design...everything is effectively a density field of some kind and rays are traced through it from the eye-point via xfer-functions, surface properties are synthesized with secondary rays (to light sources, reflected, refracted, etc.).
All the work of the object-space approach is codified into the transfer functions in the image-space approach. The image-space approach suggests simpiler software engineering because everything boils down to casting rays through stuff. This approach even nicely directly numerically supports non-linear (e.g. high order) elements.
It maybe possible to fuse many of the kinds of operations we think of in the object-space (revolve, transform, iso-surface) into a single complex transfer function in the image-space approach.
Using image-space approach (shooting rays) to answer questions like: How much mass does this object have?
There are ways to establish some correlation of the samples of the different rays to query for these metrics (volume, surface area, etc.).
Image-space approach is very attractive because everything is handled the same way--rays through a density function.
Do our (LLNL/ASC) customers use it very much? No. They mostly do surfaces such as filled boundary or iso-contours or 2D (slices) and 1D plots. While VR has merit for possible exploration/discovery scenarios, it is also slow and hard to make interactive (>1 fps) for large scale datasets. Performance could be addressed by resampling onto ortho-grids and using many GPUs and then avoiding high zoom-in.
VR is hard to use to get great images. Transfer functions hard to define. Worse, don't know if you don't have a good transfer function until after you've spent the price to render an image. Is it possible to provide LLNL/ASC customers with a menu of VR transfer functions that work to produce decent images for the kinds of materials that tend to comprise the problems they simulate?
Is VR a good modality for analysis? Maybe not but it does work for communication and producing some eye-catching images which is important to stakeholders, sponsors, etc.
