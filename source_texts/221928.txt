Before: vizz could only draw a particle as light (the additive glow) or as a lit disc (the surface mode). A 3D Gaussian Splatting capture loaded as a plain point cloud, which threw away everything that makes it look like the thing captured.
After: /particles/splat (0–1, the opacity) draws every particle as a soft, oriented ellipse of colour, sorted and blended from far to near, so near splats cover far ones. Drop the .ply a splat trainer or phone capture app exports and each splat keeps its own size, rotation, opacity and colour, so the capture renders as itself and still turns, twists and morphs like any cloud. Any other cloud is drawn as round splats the size of the particles.
How:
Reading. The binary PLY reader picks up f_dc_*, opacity, scale_* and rot_* when all of them are present, and decodes them (exp, sigmoid, normalised quaternion, DC colour). normalize_with_splats scales the sizes with the positions.
Storage. Splats go into two new half-float banks laid out like the slot texels. A capture smaller than the slot marks its repeats so they are not drawn twice.
GPU passes. A compute pass evaluates each particle with the dots' own body(). It carries the ellipsoid through the shape's turn and scale, then the projection's Jacobian (EWA splatting, Zwicker et al. 2001), and writes a depth key. A bitonic sort (Batcher 1968) orders the splats with one dispatch per step, using dynamic uniform offsets: 190 steps at the 500k particle ceiling. A quad per splat is then drawn with premultiplied "over" blending.
Sampling. While splats are up, each particle takes its own texel spread evenly over the capture, and clouds stop flowing through their points. Without this, a random pick misses about a third of the splats and doubles others.
Mode precedence. A solid, ink or the liquid still need the surface pass, so they win over splats. The plain surface switch gives way to splats.
Colour. Only the zeroth spherical-harmonic band is used, so a capture's colours don't change with the view, and the palette doesn't tint capture colours.
Sources. Kerbl et al. SIGGRAPH 2023, Zwicker et al. 2001 and Batcher 1968 are cited in the code, README, docs and the clouds page.
Tests:
reads_a_gaussian_splatting_capture checks the decoding, and that a plain PLY has no splats.
the_sort_steps_sort runs the sort network on the CPU.
splats_cover_from_the_front_whatever_the_file_order is a GPU test. With the sort dispatches removed it fails.
splats_keep_their_shape_and_turn is a GPU test that checks a long splat's footprint is wide, and that a quarter turn makes it tall.
Workspace tests and clippy pass on 1.98.1. The clouds and docs pages were checked at 1280 and 390 px.
Not covered: no real-world capture was tried. The plates use a synthetic checkered torus written in the trainer's format, and the sort's cost on a real GPU at high counts was not measured.
