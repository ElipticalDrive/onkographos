arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03279
Title: 3DGSI-Assessor: A Large-Scale Dataset and An LMM-based Method for 3D Gaussian Splatting Image Quality Assessment
Authors: Yuke Xing, Jiarui Wang, William Gordon, Zhu Li, Guangtao Zhai, Yiling Xu
Published: 2026-08-04
Summary
The paper addresses perceptual quality assessment of images rendered from compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models, arguing that conventional IQA metrics miss 3DGS-specific artifacts (floating Gaussians, surface scattering) and, critically, cannot separate geometry from color degradation even though modern 3DGS compressors tune geometric and color attributes via independent parameters. It contributes 3DGS-IEval-15K+, a subjective dataset of 15,200 rendered images from 760 trained 3DGS models (10 real scenes × 6 compression algorithms × systematically designed distortion levels × 20 viewpoints), annotated with 45,600 MOSs across overall, geometry, and color dimensions from ~684K raw ratings. It then proposes 3DGSI-Assessor, a full-reference LMM metric (InternVL2.5-8B) that concatenates global InternViT semantic features with two frozen, separately pretrained ResNet50 "local dimension-specific" encoders for geometry and color, trained in two LoRA stages (text-level instruction tuning, then three MLP regression heads). It reports SRCC 0.9630/0.9489/0.9601 on overall/geometry/color, beating 30+ baselines, and leads all three external NVS benchmarks zero-shot (Table VI).
Proposed idea
Description: The core mechanical novelty is twofold. On the data side, the distortion design is not a generic compression sweep: for the four multi-parameter methods (LightGS, C3dGS, Compact-3DGS, CompGS) the authors identify which key parameters govern color versus geometry (Table II — e.g. vq ratio/codebook size for LightGS color, prune percents for its geometry) and cross 4 color levels × 4 geometry levels into 16 distortion levels, while HAC and Scaffold, being anchor-based with a single dominant parameter, get 6 levels (Table III). This factorial design is what makes decoupled geometry/color MOS labels meaningful rather than merely a second opinion on the same degradation. On the model side, rather than fine-tuning an LMM directly on scores, the geometry and color branches are pretrained separately against geometry-MOS and color-MOS with L1, then their regression heads are discarded and the encoders frozen — explicitly to "prevent optimization conflicts during LMM fine-tuning" (Sec. IV-A). Multi-scale features are taken from ResNet50 conv2_10/conv3_12/conv4_18 plus GAP of the final layer. Prediction is all-in-one: hidden states at the three quality-level token positions are concatenated into h_fusion and fed to three independent 5-layer MLP heads, so one forward pass (~0.11 s, ~18 GB) yields all three dimensions.
Novelty vs. prior art: Against 3DGS-VBench [13] (same first author, 2025), which is the largest prior 3DGS quality benchmark at 660 compressed video sequences over 6 algorithms, this work switches the unit of annotation from video to per-view image and adds the geometry/color split; the paper's justification (Sec. I, limitation 3) — that reconstruction quality varies strongly with viewpoint so averaging along a camera trajectory destroys the attribution signal — is a legitimate methodological argument, not just a scale increment. Against the authors' own 3DGS-IEval-15K ACMMM'25 conference version [16], the delta is explicitly stated (Sec. I, contribution 1): same 15,200 images, annotation scheme extended from one dimension to three, "tripling the annotation volume to 45,600 MOSs." This is an honest disclosure, but it also means the image corpus, scene selection, viewpoint-selection strategy (Fig. 4), and distortion-level design are not new here — the genuinely new dataset contribution reduces to the two extra annotation dimensions. Against NVS-SQA [12] and NeRF-NQA [37], Sec. II-B gives an unusually careful and convincing exclusion argument rather than a dismissal: NVS-SQA is calibrated for within-scene relative ranking and so is inapplicable to cross-scene absolute scoring; NeRF-NQA needs a densely sampled view sequence with poses plus a COLMAP point cloud, produces one score per scene, and is scoped to front-facing NeRF. Against Q-Align [55], which also teaches LMMs to score via discrete text-defined levels, Stage 1 here is essentially the Q-Align recipe; the increment is the dimension-specific frozen local encoders and the shared-fusion/independent-heads structure.
Clarity — 4/5
The dataset construction is documented at a level that would genuinely permit replication: Table II and Table III give the actual parameter values per compression level per algorithm, Sec. III-B gives closed-form definitions of all four testing-viewpoint criteria (Eqs. 2–5) with the weights (all w = 0.25), K = min(10,|T|), and β = 0.3 for the position/direction feature balance, and Sec. III-D gives the Z-score normalisation and rescaling explicitly (Eqs. 6–8). Sec. II-B's treatment of why NVS-SQA and NeRF-NQA are excluded is a model of how to handle non-comparable prior art. Three concrete clarity defects: (i) Table I's row for 3DGS-IEval-15K+ leaves the "Year" and "Syn" columns as "-", which reads as evasion of the synthetic-scene gap that Sec. VI later admits; (ii) Fig. 6 is severely overloaded — three sub-panels, two training stages, four loss arrows, and the Eq. 11 level-binning formula are crammed into one figure, and the text-extracted caption ordering suggests the panel labels (a)/(b)/(c) do not follow reading order; (iii) a typo in the key annotation-count sentence, "15 valid annotators per iamge" (Sec. III-D). Ablation Table VII is also hard to read: the column headers for rows (5)–(7) mark which LoRA is disabled only implicitly, and the reader must reverse-engineer which configuration each row corresponds to.
Experiments — 4/5
Benchmarks: In-domain 3DGS-IEval-15K+ (Table V) plus three external zero-shot benchmarks (Table VI): ENeRF-QA [11], NeRF-VSQA [17], GSC-QA [18]. The external set is well chosen — it spans both NVS representations (NeRF and 3DGS) and both synthetic and real scenes, so it tests transfer across representation, not just across scene.
Baselines: Unusually thorough: 9 handcrafted (PSNR through DISTS), 13 zero-shot LMMs (LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B/7B, DeepSeekVL, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5/3, Gemini1.5-pro, CogAgent, Q-Align), and 8 deep IQA models. Crucially for a dataset paper, the baselines are retrained on this dataset where applicable: Sec. V-A states the deep learning models follow their original protocols on "the identical 4:1 training and testing split used for our model," and are trained independently per dimension. Two LMMs (Qwen2.5-VL 7B, Llama3.2-Vision 11B) are fine-tuned with "the same LLM fine-tuning approach in our model," and — this is the strongest fairness move in the paper — trained and inferred dimension-wise separately, i.e. three specialist models versus the authors' one generalist. That is a harder baseline than the authors needed to build, and it removes the most obvious "SOTA by unfair comparison" objection. The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL* reaches 0.9023/0.8127/0.8804 SRCC, so the margin is over a genuinely strong, matched-protocol competitor. One gap: the InternVL2.5-8B backbone is evaluated only zero-shot (0.6678 overall SRCC); a fine-tuned plain InternVL2.5-8B without the LDS-FEM branches would isolate backbone choice from architecture, and ablation row (3) is the nearest proxy but still includes the two-stage recipe.
Ablations: Table VII sweeps 8 configurations over ViT-only, LDS-FEM-only, +LLM, ±Stage-1 quality-level tuning, and ±LoRA on ViT/LLM. The LLM fusion is the dominant factor (row (2) → (3): overall SRCC 0.9232 → 0.9562). But the paper's own headline component, the pretrained LDS-FEM, is worth only +0.6% geometry and +0.5% color SRCC by the authors' own reporting in Sec. V-D — comparing row (3) 0.9368 geometry to row (8) 0.9489, that is a 1.3% relative gain, i.e. the "hierarchical visual encoding" that motivates the whole architecture contributes marginally next to simply putting an LMM on top of good features. There is no ablation on the frozen-versus-jointly-trained choice for LDS-FEM despite "preventing optimization conflicts" being the stated rationale, no ablation on the three λ weights in Eq. 13, and no ablation on the concatenated-h_fusion design versus per-dimension hidden states.
Statistical rigour: Better than typical for this literature but with real holes. Positives: 5-fold cross-validation with results averaged, an explicit within-scene 4:1 split with no image in both sets, Z-score-based MOS with per-subject normalisation, 15 annotators per image on an ITU-T P.910 11-point impairment scale, dual-stimulus side-by-side presentation, a calibrated 27-inch AOC Q2790PQ under controlled illumination, and 8 balanced subsets to control fatigue. Table IV reports inter-dimensional correlations and uses them as a substantive argument: geometry↔color SRCC 0.6505 is the lowest pairing, supporting the claim that the two dimensions are semi-independent and worth separating. Negatives, and they matter for a subjective study: no outlier/subject-rejection procedure is described at all — no ITU-R BT.500 screening, no kurtosis-based rejection, no correlation-with-MOS subject exclusion. The phrase "15 valid annotators per image" implies some screening happened but it is never specified, so the reader cannot tell how many raters were recruited or discarded. No inter-rater reliability is reported — no ICC, no Krippendorff's alpha, no SOS-hypothesis analysis, no MOS confidence intervals. For a dataset whose entire selling point is 45,600 MOSs across three dimensions, the absence of any reliability statistic for the two new dimensions is the paper's most serious omission: geometry and color quality are harder, more abstract judgments than overall quality, and the reader has no evidence that naive raters could make them consistently. Also absent: viewing distance, subject demographics/count, training/anchor session details, and any significance test on the Table V/VI margins.
Results: The in-domain margins are real and largest exactly where the paper claims they should be: geometry SRCC 0.9489 versus 0.8600 for the best baseline (STAIRIQA), the +10.3% relative improvement cited in Sec. V-B; color 0.9601 versus 0.9269 (HYPERIQA), +3.6%. Note that the geometry dimension is where every baseline is weakest (all deep baselines sit in 0.81–0.86 while scoring 0.90–0.93 on color), so the headline gain is concentrated in the hardest, most saturated-headroom dimension — a favourable framing, though also the honest one. The zero-shot table supports a nice negative finding stated in Sec. V-C: HYPERIQA is near-best in-domain (0.9406) but only mid-pack externally, DBCNN is runner-up on GSC-QA but drops to 0.6598 on ENeRF-QA, MANIQA leads on ENeRF-QA/NeRF-VSQA yet is weak on GSC-QA — so in-domain fit does not predict transfer, and 3DGSI-Assessor leading all nine external columns is meaningful. Two caveats: the GSC-QA margin is thin (0.7769 vs DBCNN 0.7697, on a 120-sample dataset — well within noise, and no CI is given), and the external comparison includes only deep IQA baselines, omitting the fine-tuned LMM baselines that were the strongest competitors in Table V. Sec. V-A's claim that the method "has been adopted as a baseline in the IEEE Standards Association project 3366.5" is an impact assertion with no citation or document number.
Contribution — 4/5
Primary: The dimension-decoupled subjective dataset. The intellectual move worth keeping is the observation that in modern 3DGS compressors geometric attributes (position, covariance, opacity) and color attributes (SH coefficients) are governed by separate compression parameters requiring separate tuning, which makes a single scalar quality score structurally inadequate as a feedback signal — you cannot tell it which knob to turn. The 4×4 factorial parameter design in Table II is the operationalisation of that insight, and it is what distinguishes this from a scale-up.
Significance: Concretely useful rather than transformative. The stated downstream application — using dimension-specific predictions as a perceptual reward to guide separate tuning of geometry versus color compression parameters in rate-distortion optimisation (Sec. I and Sec. VI) — is a genuine gap, since PSNR/SSIM currently serve that role in [5]–[9] and Table V shows PSNR at 0.6031 geometry SRCC. That said, the paper only proposes this use; it never demonstrates that substituting 3DGSI-Assessor into a 3DGS training or RD loop improves anything, so the central motivating claim for the metric's value remains unvalidated. The model side is competent engineering assembled from existing parts (InternVL2.5 + ResNet50 multi-scale features + LoRA + Q-Align-style level tuning) rather than a new idea, and its own ablation shows the novel component contributes ~1% relative.
Limitations acknowledged: Sec. VI is short and covers only two items: no synthetic scenes (with a mildly defensive framing that real scenes "constitute the dominant application scenario"), and the untested reward-signal application. Elsewhere the authors are more forthcoming than the limitations section: Sec. III-E honestly explains the color-MOS right skew as an artifact of HAC/Scaffold predominantly affecting geometry, and concedes the train-versus-novel-view quality gap "remains modest due to dense training view configurations used in our project" and would be far larger under sparse views — an admission that the challenging-viewpoint selection strategy of Fig. 4 achieves less separation than intended. Not acknowledged anywhere: the absent subject screening and reliability statistics, the reuse of the conference version's image corpus, the full-reference-only design (which limits deployment, since a ground-truth reference view is exactly what is unavailable at a genuinely novel viewpoint — the paper never notes this tension), or the 10-scene ceiling on scene diversity given a within-scene split that lets the model see every scene during training.
Relation to field — 4/5
Related-work coverage is strong and correctly organised. Table I is a proper positioning instrument, not decoration: it tabulates 9 prior NVS QA datasets by year, scene counts, NeRF/3DGS model counts, render mode, whether distortion levels were systematically designed, annotation type, and sample counts, making the four claimed gaps (scale, distortion diversity, IQA-versus-VQA, single-dimension annotation) checkable rather than asserted. The transition narrative from NeRF-era benchmarks (NeRF-QA 48 samples, NeRF-VSQA 88, FFV 220, ENeRF-QA 440) to 3DGS-era ones (GSC-QA 120, GS-QA 64, NVS-QA 65, 3DGS-VBench 660) is accurate and the claim to be largest is verifiable from the table. The exclusion of NVS-SQA and NeRF-NQA (Sec. II-B) is argued on methodological grounds — assessment paradigm, input requirements, and original scoping — rather than waved away, which is more scrupulous than most papers manage. Two positioning weaknesses. First, the self-relationship is under-emphasised: 3DGS-VBench [13] and 3DGS-IEval-15K [16] are both this group's own work and together supply the algorithm selection and the image corpus, so a reader skimming Table I would overestimate how much of the pipeline is new here. Second, the broader-impact framing is entirely internal to the compression-metric community; there is no discussion of how a learned LMM-based perceptual metric might be gamed when used as a training reward (the standard failure mode when a differentiable perceptual metric becomes an optimisation target), which is a live concern given the paper's own proposed use in RD optimisation. The IEEE SA 3366.5 baseline adoption, if substantiated, is meaningful standards-track relevance.
Overall — 4/5
Strengths:
The geometry/color decoupling is grounded in an actual property of the compressors being evaluated, not invented for novelty: Table II identifies the specific per-algorithm parameters governing each attribute class and crosses them factorially, and Table IV's geometry↔color SRCC of 0.6505 (the lowest of the three pairings) empirically supports treating them as separable.
Baseline fairness is handled better than the norm for dataset papers: deep IQA baselines are retrained on the identical 4:1 split, and two LMM baselines are fine-tuned with the authors' own recipe and trained dimension-wise separately, deliberately constructing a stronger three-specialist competitor than the single all-in-one model. Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL* at 0.9023 overall SRCC is a credible opponent, not a straw man.
The zero-shot transfer analysis in Sec. V-C makes a real argument rather than just reporting wins: it shows no baseline transfers consistently (HYPERIQA 0.9406 in-domain but mid-pack externally; DBCNN 0.7697 on GSC-QA but 0.6598 on ENeRF-QA), which is what makes the method's uniform first-place across NeRF and 3DGS, synthetic and real, hard to explain by scene memorisation.
Viewpoint selection is principled and fully specified (Eqs. 2–5), splitting 10 k-means-representative training views from 10 maximally-divergent testing views by distance, sparsity, convex-hull extrapolation, and directional novelty — a defensible protocol for probing view-dependent 3DGS degradation.
Weaknesses:
The subjective study, which is the load-bearing contribution, omits both outlier/subject rejection and any inter-rater reliability statistic. Sec. III-D says "15 valid annotators per iamge" without stating how validity was determined, how many raters were recruited or excluded, or reporting ICC/Krippendorff's alpha/SOS analysis/MOS confidence intervals. For the two new geometry and color dimensions — abstract judgments naive raters may not make consistently — there is no evidence of annotation reliability at all, and no significance test anywhere on the Table V/VI margins.
The dataset delta over the authors' own ACMMM'25 3DGS-IEval-15K [16] is only the annotation dimensions: the same 15,200 images, scenes, viewpoint strategy, and distortion design are reused. This is disclosed (Sec. I, contribution 1) but the abstract, Table I, and conclusion all present 3DGS-IEval-15K+ as a wholesale new dataset, which overstates the increment.
The architectural contribution is weak by the paper's own ablation: Sec. V-D reports the pretrained LDS-FEM — the component that motivates the "hierarchical visual encoding" framing — as worth +0.6% geometry and +0.5% color SRCC, while the generic LLM-fusion step (row (2)→(3)) is worth ~3.3 points. There is also no ablation on freezing versus jointly training LDS-FEM despite "preventing optimization conflicts" being the stated justification, nor on the λ weights of Eq. 13.
The central motivating application is never tested. Sec. I and Sec. VI both argue the metric's value is as a dimension-specific reward for re-tuning geometry versus color compression parameters at a storage budget, but no experiment substitutes 3DGSI-Assessor into a 3DGS training or rate-distortion loop. Relatedly, the full-reference design requires a ground-truth view — precisely what is unavailable at a truly novel viewpoint — and this tension goes unremarked.
Generalisation evidence has a ceiling: 10 scenes, all real, with a within-scene 4:1 split so every scene is seen in training. No cross-scene held-out-scene protocol is reported, which is the split that would actually test scene-independent absolute scoring — the very capability Sec. II-B uses to distinguish this work from NVS-SQA.
Questions for authors:
What subject screening was applied, and what does "15 valid annotators" mean operationally — how many were recruited and how many rejected, by what criterion? Please report inter-rater reliability (ICC or Krippendorff's alpha) separately for overall, geometry, and color, plus MOS confidence intervals. Did raters agree less on geometry and color than on overall quality?
What is performance under a cross-scene (leave-scenes-out) split rather than the within-scene 4:1 split of Sec. V-A? Given that Sec. II-B rejects NVS-SQA precisely for being within-scene-relative rather than cross-scene-absolute, this seems necessary to substantiate the paper's own positioning.
Does substituting 3DGSI-Assessor's dimension-specific scores for PSNR/SSIM in the rate-distortion loop of any of [5]–[9] actually improve perceptual quality at matched bitrate? Sec. VI proposes this as the metric's purpose; a single demonstration would convert the paper's main motivating argument from claim to evidence.
Why is the fine-tuned InternVL2.5-8B backbone (without LDS-FEM) missing from Table V, and why are the fine-tuned LMM baselines absent from the zero-shot Table VI? Both omissions favour the proposed method.
Are the Table VI margins on GSC-QA (0.7769 vs DBCNN 0.7697, n=120) statistically distinguishable? Please provide confidence intervals or a significance test.
Please cite or document the IEEE SA 3366.5 baseline adoption claimed in Sec. V-A.
This is a well-executed, carefully positioned contribution to NVS quality assessment whose central insight — that geometry and color compression parameters are tuned independently in modern 3DGS pipelines, so a scalar quality score cannot provide actionable feedback — is correct and operationalised with real care in the Table II factorial design. The baseline protocol is notably fairer than the field's norm, with matched retraining and a deliberately stronger dimension-wise LMM competitor, and the zero-shot transfer analysis makes a substantive argument rather than merely tallying wins. It is held back by the subjective study's silence on outlier rejection and inter-rater reliability, which is a serious gap in exactly the contribution the paper leads with; by a dataset delta over the authors' own prior conference version that is narrower than the framing suggests; and by an architecture whose distinctive component earns ~1% relative gain by the authors' own ablation. The motivating downstream application is proposed but never demonstrated.
Recommendation: accept
Overall 4, Contribution 4, and Relation-to-field 4 satisfy the accept rule mechanically, and the substance supports it: the dimension-decoupled dataset is a genuine and reusable resource, the baseline comparison is fair in the specific ways dataset papers usually are not (matched 4:1 splits, retrained deep baselines, dimension-wise fine-tuned LMM competitors), and the geometry SRCC gain from 0.8600 to 0.9489 is large and lands in the hardest dimension. Acceptance should be conditional on the authors adding subject-screening detail and inter-rater reliability statistics for all three dimensions, and on toning down the framing so the increment over their own ACMMM'25 3DGS-IEval-15K [16] — three annotation dimensions on a reused image corpus — is as clear in the abstract as it is in Sec. I. The architectural novelty is thin and should not be oversold, but the dataset alone justifies publication.
Wiki fit (for curator agent)
Pages to create/update: Honestly, this is a poor fit for this wiki and I would recommend against creating a reading page. This is pure CV metrology — a compression-quality benchmark and a regression head on an LMM. It has nothing to say about machine vision as a political or aesthetic regime, surveillance, or critical theory of technology; there is no Steyerl/Paglen-adjacent surface here. The one thread with any purchase is oblique: the paper is a clean case study in quantifying human perception into a training reward — 684K human ratings compressed into 45,600 scalars, then distilled into a model proposed (Sec. VI) as a "perceptual reward" that replaces human judgment inside an optimisation loop. If the curator wants anything, the minimal move is a short paragraph appended to an existing concept page on perceptual-metric-as-reward or the operationalisation of human vision into machine-legible scores, citing Sec. III-D (the Z-score MOS pipeline, Eqs. 6–8) and Sec. VI (the reward proposal) as a concrete instance. Do not create pages/readings/3dgsi-assessor.md; do not create entity pages for 3DGS-IEval-15K+ or the six compression algorithms — they will be permanent orphans in this taxonomy.
Cross-paper: Gap, not conflict — the wiki has no existing cluster on quality-metric engineering, and one 3DGS IQA paper does not start one. If a pattern of "human perception rendered as optimisation target" papers accumulates, that would be the concept page to open; a single instance is not enough.
