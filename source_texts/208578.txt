Summary
render_with_gradients() returned the configured display image but differentiated a different quantity. Its forward result had already been min-max normalised and inverted in NumPy, while grad_output went straight to the Slang backward kernel for raw Beer-Lambert intensity. Under the defaults, that makes the pose gradient point uphill.
This patch applies the exact vector-Jacobian product of the display transform before uploading grad_output. render(), render_with_gradients(), SlangDiffDRRFunction and TorchSlangDiffDRR retain their existing processed-image outputs. A keyword-only raw=True path exposes raw intensity and raw-intensity gradients explicitly for callers that need them.
The boundary repair
For a raw image $r$, let
$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \min_i r_i, \\
b &= \max_i r_i, \\
d &= b-a+\varepsilon, \\
z_i &= \frac{r_i-a}{d}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Inversion first maps the upstream gradient $g$ to $h=-g$. Without inversion, $h=g$. Let $m_a$ and $m_b$ be unit-mass masks over the pixels tied at the minimum and maximum respectively. The exact normalisation VJP is
$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial r} = \frac{h}{d} + m_a\left(-\frac{\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}h}{d} + \frac{h^{\mathsf T}(r-a\mathbf{1})}{d^2} \right) - m_b\frac{h^{\mathsf T}(r-a\mathbf{1})}{d^2}
$$
This includes the direct $h/d$ term and the derivatives through both extrema. Sharing the extremal terms through $m_a$ and $m_b$ matches torch.amin and torch.amax rather than choosing an arbitrary first pixel.
Moving the display transform into TorchSlangDiffDRR would fix the module path, but SlangDiffDRRFunction is exported and documented as returning the processed image, while the standalone render_with_gradients() API would remain wrong. Repairing the VJP at the shared renderer boundary fixes both paths without changing their public outputs.
