Conclusions
Limited convergence of a locally-varying BLB law may be
achieved with grid refinement
Error increases rapidly around dp /∆x ≈ 0.1
Concentration spectrum diverges due to resolution of point
particles, requires cutoff at early wavenumber
Connection between spectrum of concentration field and
convergence of BLB law is hypothesized, requires further
analysis
Develop more accurate techniques for evaluating transmission
without requiring excessive grid refinement (closure,
extrapolation, etc.)

Discrete versus continuous
Problem 1: A cloud has number density of particles n, each with
cross section σ, and length L. Compute the transmission.
Problem 2: The set of all individual particle positions ~xi is provided
to you, along with a cross section σ. Compute the transmission.
What changed?
One assumes in reading problem 1 that the particles are randomly
distributed. In problem 2, more information is given, and a
disparity between the discrete and continuum views develops.

What about a stochastic model?
Levermore-Pomraning assumes that the individual components
have well-defined transport coefficients Σai and Σsi .
Particles are governed by Mie scattering, solution of Maxwell’s
equations. Ballistic model of transport on the particle scale is
invalid. Requires upscaling to use a ballistic model (e.g. apply
volume averaging or Wigner transform to wave equation).
Analogy: neutron scattering is defined by interaction of neutron
with nucleus, compute microscopic cross-section σ. Upscaling is
used to define macroscopic transport coefficient Σ by assuming
independent targets and random distribution, Σ = σn.
