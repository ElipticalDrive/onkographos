`imageSmoothingQuality = "high"` picks a cubic and nothing said which
one. Replacing `CubicResampler::mitchell()` with `catmull_rom()` at
`src/node/filter.rs:592` left the entire suite green -- including the
three tests around that property, which pin *sharper when magnifying,
mipmapped when minifying* and are satisfied by either kernel. The same
was true of the other two sites, and one of those is a promise made to
callers in writing: `lib/index.d.ts` documents the `"cubic"` sampling
mode as "Mitchell-Netravali bicubic" by name.

Both halves are here because they fail differently. The unit tests
assert the coefficients, so they name the kernel and say what went wrong
when it moves. The rendering test asserts that a magnified hard step
does not ring, so it goes through `drawImage`, the real sampling path
and the rasteriser -- which is where a change that keeps the constants
and loses the effect would show up.

The threshold is measured, not derived. Mitchell (B=C=1/3) is
approximating and CatmullRom (B=0, C=1/2) is interpolating, so they
separate on how far each overshoots a hard step's endpoints. On a
32-to-224 step magnified 16x, both engines:

    step   Mitchell   CatmullRom
    128    5 levels   9-10
    192    7 levels   14

Ten sits between 7 and 14 with room either side, and the separation does
not depend on the engine -- CPU and GPU agree to a level on every row.
What it pins is a property rather than an identity: another approximating
cubic would pass. That is the cheap observable half of the roughness
measurement AGENTS.md records for this choice, and it is the half that
catches the swap.

The coefficients are written out as `1.0 / 3.0` beside a comment naming
Mitchell and Netravali's 1988 paper rather than compared against
`CubicResampler::mitchell()`. Comparing that helper against itself would
assert nothing: it would pass whatever the helper returned, which is the
failure mode the whole issue is about.

`parse_sampling` in `src/node/image_filter.rs` needed a
`FunctionContext` to reach its mapping, so the mapping is now a free
function and the context handling stays where it was. That is the only
non-test change in this commit and it moves no behaviour.

Three guards come with them, one per site, asserting that the *non*-cubic
modes do not acquire a cubic. A cubic sets `use_cubic` and Skia then
ignores the mipmap chain, so a mode that gained one would silently lose
mipmapping -- the exact failure the scale-aware mapping exists to avoid.
Those three stay green under the swap, deliberately: they are about the
other direction.

`SamplingOptions::from(CubicResampler::mitchell())` in each file -- the
three identity tests and the rendering test fail, and the three guards
pass.
