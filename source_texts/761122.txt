Cross-Device Progressive Streaming of 3D Gaussian Splatting Reconstructions for Browser-Based Interactive Visualization of Glazed Ceramic Products
Authors
Bing Luo, Liyao Xiao
Pages
643 - 652
DOI
10.3233/ATDE260851
Category
Research Article
Series
Advances in Transdisciplinary Engineering
Ebook
Volume 96: Image Processing, Electronics and Computers
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a state-of-the-art representation for real-time novel-view synthesis, but its storage footprint and Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) rendering requirements remain prohibitive for browser-based interactive visualization on heterogeneous client devices. A typical scene-scale 3DGS reconstruction occupies on the order of 250 megabytes (MB) and requires sustained millions of primitive evaluations per frame, both of which exceed the working budgets of mobile and tablet clients. This paper presents an end-to-end engineering framework that addresses these constraints through three coordinated mechanisms: (i) an offline importance-driven hierarchical pruning that derives a three-tier Level-of-Detail (LOD) representation from a vanilla 3DGS .ply file; (ii) a progressive streaming protocol that delivers the coarsest tier first to minimise time-to-first-paint and refines the representation through subsequent tiers; and (iii) an adaptive client-side rendering controller that selects the active tier per frame based on the device’s measured frame-budget headroom. The framework is implemented as a drop-in extension to existing WebGL 3DGS viewers and validated on a real reconstruction of a glazed ceramic product (1.1 million Gaussians, 262 MB). Across desktop, tablet and smartphone test devices, the proposed framework reduces time-to-first-paint by 84% while maintaining a stable 30 frames per second (FPS) interactive frame rate, at a Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 25.83 dB at the lowest tier and 30.94 dB at the highest tier.
