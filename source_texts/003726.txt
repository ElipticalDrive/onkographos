ABSTRACT
Volume rendering (VR) is amongst the most valuable techniques
in medical visualizations, or computer-aid diagnosis and other
areas that are related. This study discusses and review the
varying methods and techniques used to deliver a volume
rendering technique.

INTRODUCTION
Because volume rendering is very important, numerous
researchers have developed several volume rendering techniques.
Each has its own pros and cons. Three different types of volume
rendering techniques have been described in the next section,
namely:
1. Direct Volume Rendering (DVR) technique. It
includes Shear Warp, Image Splatting, Texture Slicing,
Cell Projection, and Raycasting,
2. Indirect Volume Rendering (IVR) technique. It
includes marching cubes, and Tetrahedral techniques.
3. Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) technique.

2.1.2 Image Splatting
Image splatting is a very well-known method. It was first
discovered by Lee Westover in 1989 [4]. In this method, voxels
are rendered by overlapping functions such as Gaussian kernel.
In Image Splatting, the volume is a field of 3D interpolation
kernel. Each grid voxel has its own kernel, the images produce
2D footprints on the screen, and each 2D footprint is a result of a
kernel (see Figure 2). This method is also fast as it neglects the
empty volume spaces, and only stores volumetric points from
the dataset. However, being doing so, this implementation
results in aliasing, blurring, and colour bleeding. [5-7]

The goal of the indirect volume rendering techniques or surface
rendering (SR) techniques, or iso-surfacing is to create a surface
with constant density from a 3D dataset. This will decrease the
computation complexity. Different methods were developed for
this technique, such as: contour tracing, marching cubes [12],
and marching tetrahedra [13]. In the next section, the steps of
the marching cubes algorithm will be discussed in more details.

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we reviewed several techniques of volume
rendering. We showed the benefit and drawbacks of each one.
The importance of this paper is to give the audience and readers
an information not only on one technique but several others. By
reviewing such content, researchers could review some of the
old and the new ones and furthermore try to improve them, by
creating a better new accelerated version of a technique.
4
FUTURE WORKS
This study provides a robust foundation for future work in
volume rendering technologies. One extent of our future work is
to combine the new developments in machine learning with
volume rendering. The goal is to develop software that would
render medical imaging data, which recognize real anatomical
structure, transforming grayscale data to RGB, and detecting
abnormalities. Moreover, our intent is to investigate more
volume rendering techniques and compare them with the abovementioned ones using benchmarks and statistics.
