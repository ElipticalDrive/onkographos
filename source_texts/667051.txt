Hypothesis
The current masked MSE averages over all valid nodes in a batch, so larger cruise meshes contribute more gradient terms than smaller raceCar meshes even though the sampler balances samples by domain. Computing volume and surface losses per sample over that sample's valid nodes, then averaging sample losses, will reduce mesh-size weighting bias and improve equal-weight surface-pressure generalization.

No launch-scoped numeric baseline or W&B run is available at assignment time. Compare against the exact-code anchor W&B run once posted; do not invent baseline numbers or use out-of-scope historical runs. The primary target is lower val_avg/mae_surf_p, confirmed by test_avg/mae_surf_p and the four split diagnostics.
Scope and exact change
Modify only train.py; do not edit data/, split manifests, dependencies, model architecture, sampler, optimizer, learning rate, schedule, or feature normalization. Keep current normalized squared-error objectives and surf_weight=10. Change only reduction semantics: for each batch sample, compute the masked volume mean over that sample's valid nodes and the masked surface mean over that sample's valid surface nodes, then average those per-sample losses across samples with valid denominators before combining them. Preserve current equal channel weighting and ensure padding and samples without valid surface nodes are excluded safely rather than producing NaN. Keep validation/test metrics and checkpoint selection unchanged.
