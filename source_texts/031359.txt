A Low-Cost 3D Reconstruction System Based on
COLMAP and 3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering
Kaiyuan Lu
College of Chemistry and Chemical Engineering, Shanghai Jiaotong University, Dongchuan

Abstract. 3D reconstruction technologies are increasingly applied in the
digitization of cultural heritage. However, traditional photogrammetry methods
often involve high costs, while deep learning approaches such as Neural
Radiance Fields (NeRF) require intensive computation, making it difficult to
balance reconstruction accuracy with efficiency. This study proposes a low-cost
3D reconstruction system based on COLMAP and 3D Gaussian Splatting
(3DGS) rendering. Using a custom-built dataset of cultural artifacts, the system
first applies COLMAP for sparse point cloud reconstruction and camera pose
estimation, followed by 3DGS rendering. The resulting models are compatible
with platforms such as Unity for downstream applications. As indicated by
quantitative evaluation, the system achieves an average PSNR of 22.06 and SSIM
of 0.80, with rendering completed in under 40 minutes, demonstrating practical
performance in balancing visual fidelity and computational efficiency. Compared
with traditional methods, the system offers a feasible and cost-effective solution
for museum digital exhibitions and scientific instrument preservation.
Keywords: 3D Gaussian Splatting, COLMAP, 3D Reconstruction, Cultural
Heritage Digitization, Computer Vision

Introduction
With the rapid development of digital technologies, 3D reconstruction has become
increasingly significant across various fields [1]. In autonomous driving, it is used for
obstacle detection and path planning; in digital education, it enables realistic simulation
of experimental equipment and historical sites; in digital healthcare, it aids in organ
modeling and surgical navigation; and in digital cultural tourism, it supports remote
visits, digital archiving, and heritage restoration, thereby promoting the digitized
preservation and dissemination of cultural heritage. Through virtual representation,
cultural heritage can overcome the limitations of physical space and avoid irreversible
damage caused by environmental degradation or human interference [2, 3].
At present, the demand for high-precision and high-efficiency 3D reconstruction is
growing rapidly. However, traditional methods such as photogrammetry often rely on
expensive imaging equipment and manual texture mapping, and are mostly used for
static model construction [4]. Traditional 3D reconstruction techniques, represented by

structured light, laser scanning, and photogrammetry, offer high accuracy in certain
static scenes. Yet, they typically require costly hardware and complex post-processing
workflows, making it difficult to meet the needs of low-cost and rapid modeling applications [5, 6].
To address these challenges, open-source tools such as COLMAP have gained popularity. COLMAP is a photogrammetry tool implementing structure-from-motion
(SfM) and multi-view stereo (MVS) algorithms to estimate camera positions and reconstruct 3D geometry from 2D images. It has been widely applied in fields such as
education and cultural heritage digitization. Nevertheless, these methods still face limitations in terms of model fidelity and real-time rendering, which restrict their applicability in highly interactive and lightweight scenarios.
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), a neural network-based technique, can achieve high
rendering quality. However, its training is time-consuming (often exceeding 30 hours),
requires powerful computational resources, and needs multi-view images as input.
Moreover, it presents limitations in preserving fine details and enabling real-time interaction [7]. In response, recent research efforts have attempted to combine COLMAP’s
sparse reconstruction with NeRF-based neural rendering to improve geometric accuracy and visual realism. For instance, Instant-NGP, proposed by Tancik et al., significantly accelerates NeRF training through multi-resolution hash encoding, alleviating
some of the computational burden [8]. However, these approaches still rely on extensive training and high-end GPUs during rendering, limiting their deployment in resource-constrained settings.
In contrast, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), an emerging rendering technique, combines efficient volumetric rendering with data compression. It improves model detail
while significantly optimizing rendering speed, thereby enabling dynamic visualization
and real-time interaction [9, 10].
This study adopts a low-cost and efficient 3D reconstruction pipeline by integrating
COLMAP with 3DGS rendering. It enables the rapid and high-precision construction
of 3D models using only regular image data and simple hardware setups. Furthermore,
it explores the application of this technique in education and cultural dissemination,
offering an accessible and effective 3D modeling solution for a broader user base.

2.1
Multi-view Data Acquisition
This study selected three artifacts of different materials from the Shanghai Museum —
pottery, porcelain, bronze, jade and stone. Using a mobile phone camera (12MP), images were captured using a surround-photography approach, with additional detail shots
focusing on key regions such as material texture, base, and ornamentation. Each set
consists of 100 raw images, with overexposed and underexposed frames removed to
form a preprocessed image set compatible with COLMAP input requirements.
2.2
Sparse Point Cloud Reconstruction
Upon completing data acquisition, COLMAP was employed for sparse point cloud reconstruction. This process begins with image feature extraction and matching to identify corresponding keypoints across images, followed by the estimation of camera camera poses to construct an initial 3D structure of the target object. The spatial relationships between images are established through matched feature points, and additional
image views are incrementally incorporated—starting from a small subset of images
and progressively adding new ones while simultaneously updating the existing
model—constituting the incremental reconstruction process. At the same time, bundle
adjustment is employed to further optimize both camera parameters and 3D point positions by minimizing the reprojection error of image features across different views.
These steps together form the Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline.
The sparse point cloud generated by COLMAP serves as foundational data for subsequent fine reconstruction and rendering processes. It provides high-precision 3D coordinate data necessary for model synthesis and visualization using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).
2.3
3DGS-Based Visual Rendering
Following the sparse reconstruction, the next step involves using 3D Gaussian Splatting
(3DGS) for detailed 3D model synthesis and rendering. This study utilized a cloud

server equipped with an RTX 4090 GPU to ensure sufficient computational resources

Environment Setup. A computing environment compatible with the 3DGS algorithm
was configured on the cloud server, with necessary dependencies such as CUDA,
PyTorch, and OpenGL correctly installed.
Data Import and Preprocessing. The sparse point cloud and images from COLMAP
were organized into a standardized dataset format and uploaded to the server.
Rendering Computation. The 3DGS algorithm generates a series of 3D Gaussian
points with varying colors, shapes, and spatial positions based on the reference images
and point cloud. These points are rendered volumetrically using the Gaussian Splatting
principle. Through iterative optimization, the positions, colors, and transparency of the
Gaussians are refined to approximate realistic lighting effects and surface features.
Output and Optimization. Rendered results are exported in ply format and imported
into Unity via the UnityGaussianSplatting plugin. Post-processing includes removing
artifacts ("ghost clouds") and trimming models to enhance final quality.
3
Experimental Analysis
3.1
Quantitative Evaluation
To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the 3DGS-rendered models, several
common image quality assessment metrics were employed. These indicators measure
the similarity, detail preservation, and perceived quality between rendered and real images. Table 1 shows the evaluation metrics for each model.
Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). SSIM measures structural similarity between
two images, accounting for luminance, contrast, and structure. Ranging from 0 to 1,
higher values indicate better structural preservation. Comparing SSIM scores between
real and rendered images helps assess how well 3DGS preserves structure and details.
Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR). PSNR is a classic metric used to evaluate image
fidelity, based on Mean Squared Error (MSE). Higher PSNR values indicate lower distortion and better visual quality.
Training Time. Training time reflects the computational efficiency of the 3DGS
model. It is influenced by hardware configuration (here, RTX 4090 GPU) and dataset
size. Shorter training times indicate higher efficiency and practical applicability.

Analysis and Discussion
Through high-precision sparse point cloud reconstruction with COLMAP and rendering with 3DGS, this study generated 3D reconstruction models applicable to educational and intangible cultural heritage scenarios (as shown in Figure 2). Based on the
evaluation metrics (Table 1), the model is able to preserve a relatively complete structure and reconstruct most of the detailed features.

Conclusion
This study presents a cost-effective 3D reconstruction system integrating COLMAP’s
sparse point cloud estimation with 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering. As indicated by
quantitative evaluation, the system achieves reasonable image fidelity (average SSIM
0.80, PSNR 22.06) and efficient processing times (under 40 minutes), enabling practical deployment in cultural heritage digitization.
Despite current limitations in data acquisition, computational cost, and hardware
adaptability, future integration of deep learning, real-time interaction, and cross-platform optimization will enhance system usability and generalizability, paving new paths
for virtual reality applications in cultural preservation and education.
