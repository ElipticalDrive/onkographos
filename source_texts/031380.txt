Abstract
Infrared imaging offers capabilities beyond visible-light sensing, including all-day operability and direct access to thermal radiation characteristics of observed targets. However, existing neural rendering frameworks such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) primarily model photometric appearance and lack explicit thermophysical interpretability for infrared image formation. To address this limitation, we propose Thermal-Physics-Informed 3D Gaussian Splatting (TPI-GS), a novel framework that embeds infrared radiative transfer modeling into the 3DGS representation. In TPI-GS, each Gaussian primitive is reinterpreted as a thermophysical radiative element augmented with learnable emissivity, temperature, and thermal texture parameters. Based on Planck’s law and radiative formulation, infrared image formation is decomposed into physically meaningful components, while atmospheric attenuation and sensor response are explicitly modeled. This design transforms 3DGS from a purely photometric renderer into a structured infrared scene representation capable of jointly modeling geometry and thermal characteristics. By optimizing geometric structure together with thermophysical fields under multi-view supervision, TPI-GS enables cross-view infrared rendering with improved radiometric coherence and structural consistency. Auxiliary frequency-domain supervision via Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) is further introduced to mitigate the inherent smoothing effect of Gaussian splatting. Experimental results demonstrate that TPI-GS achieves superior infrared rendering quality while maintaining real-time efficiency. By integrating thermophysical radiation modeling with differentiable Gaussian rendering, TPI-GS establishes a principled bridge between classical infrared simulation theory and modern neural point-based scene representation.

Introduction
As a special type of remote sensing technique, infrared or thermal imaging captures the temperature variations of objects and provides a unique capability for all-day imaging. Infrared imaging fundamentally differs from visible-light imaging in that image formation is governed by thermophysical radiation processes. The observed intensity depends on emissivity, temperature distribution, atmospheric transmission, and sensor response, making physical consistency crucial for reliable infrared scene modeling.
Recent advances in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [1] and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [2] have significantly accelerated research in Novel View Synthesis (NVS), enabling high-quality scene reconstruction and real-time image generation. These methods have also been extended to thermal field, facilitating practical applications such as infrared image generation [3] and temperature monitoring [4]. However, most existing approaches [5], [6] treat infrared images as grayscale counterparts of RGB images, adopting identical rendering pipelines without explicitly modeling the underlying thermal radiation mechanism.
Such simplifications limit physical interpretability and controllability, preventing the recovery of meaningful thermophysical quantities such as emissivity and temperature. As a result, existing infrared NVS methods remain largely appearance-driven rather than physics-informed.
To bridge this gap, this paper proposes Thermal-Physics-Informed 3D Gaussian Splatting (TPI-GS), a novel framework that reformulates 3D Gaussian Splatting from a purely photometric renderer into a thermophysically scene representation model. Instead of treating infrared images as grayscale appearance signals, TPI-GS embeds the infrared radiative transfer process directly into the Gaussian representation.
In TPI-GS, each 3D Gaussian primitive is reinterpreted as a thermal radiative element parameterized by emissivity, temperature, and thermal texture, in addition to geometric parameters. Spectral radiant exitance is computed via Planck’s law, and infrared image formation is modeled as a radiance accumulation process along the rendering path. Atmospheric attenuation and sensor response are further incorporated to maintain physical consistency.
As illustrated in Fig. 1, the radiation process fundamentally differs from the original 3DGS, which relies on view-dependent color blending via spherical harmonics. By replacing appearance-based blending with physics-driven radiance integration, TPI-GS jointly reconstructs geometric structure and thermal radiation fields in a differentiable and data-adaptive manner, achieving both physical interpretability and efficient rendering.
In addition, infrared images often exhibit blurred visual effects, low resolution, and poor contrast. Effectively reconstructing infrared scenes using 3DGS, which achieving information complementarity in multi-modal images remains a significant challenge. As a powerful signal processing and analysis tool, Wavelet Transform (WT) has been extensively used in image super-resolution [7], extract features [8], and object detection [9] in recent years. Frequency domain information has been demonstrated to play a crucial role in enhancing image generation tasks. Based on these findings, this paper explores whether WT can improve the quality of NVS based on 3DGS.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
•
We propose TPI-GS, a thermal-physics-informed extension of 3D Gaussian Splatting that embeds an infrared radiative transfer formulation into the 3DGS framework, enabling physically interpretable infrared scene representation and novel view synthesis.
•
We introduce a thermophysical parameterization of Gaussian primitives, where each Gaussian is augmented with learnable emissivity, temperature, and thermal texture attributes. By decomposing infrared image formation into physically meaningful components derived from radiative transfer physics, TPI-GS bridges classical infrared simulation theory and neural point-based rendering.
•
We design auxiliary modules, including a material prior assignment strategy for emissivity initialization and a DWT-based frequency supervision mechanism, that enhance reconstruction stability and high-frequency texture fidelity.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related work on infrared image generation and physics-based 3D Gaussian splatting. Section 3 provides preliminary knowledge on 3DGS and the infrared thermal radiation. Section 4 presents the proposed method in detail, including the overview, thermal radiation process, thermophysical model of Gaussian primitive, discrete wavelet transform, and the design of loss functions. Section 5 describes the experimental setup, reports results on multi-modal dataset, infrared-only dataset, ablation studies, and the computation cost. Section 6 is the discussion. Finally, Section 7 concludes the paper.

Infrared image generation
All objects with temperatures above absolute zero emit energy in the form of electromagnetic waves, a phenomenon known as thermal radiation. Through Planck’s law, thermal imaging converts the intensity of electromagnetic waves radiated from the surface of an object into thermal information, which is then used to create images. Infrared images are widely used in object detection, nondestructive testing [10], evaluating internal forces [11], and other fields [12], [13], [14].

Physics-based Gaussian splatting with uncertainty estimation for dynamic thermal field measurement
2027, Infrared Physics and Technology
Show abstract
Industrial failures are often preceded by thermal anomalies, yet conventional infrared cameras provide only two-dimensional projections that conflate depth, emissivity and temperature. Existing neural rendering methods do not model radiation physics and rarely provide calibrated uncertainty. Here we present Thermal-4DGS, a physics-based framework for dynamic thermal field measurement that addresses four bottlenecks: depth ambiguity, emissivity–temperature coupling, missing uncertainty, and thermal evolution that occurs without geometric motion. Each Gaussian primitive carries a canonical temperature 𝑇0𝑖, a time-dependent residual 𝛥⁢𝑇𝑖⁡(𝑡), surface emissivity 𝜖𝑖 and temperature variance 𝜎2𝑇,𝑖. The instantaneous temperature is 𝑇𝑖⁡(𝑡)=𝑇0𝑖+𝛥⁢𝑇𝑖⁡(𝑡), which allows stationary objects to cool or heat without deformation. Thermal emission is rendered in the radiance domain, and a three-level hierarchical deformation field (64→128→256 units) separates global motion, regional deformation and local thermal variation. Radar-based ranging provides a geometric constraint near occlusions, and heteroscedastic variance propagates analytically through alpha blending to yield per-pixel confidence. We report purpose-built industrial datasets and adapted public benchmarks separately, because only the former provides independent temperature references suitable for calibrated three-dimensional evaluation. On three industrial datasets (Tire, Engine and Battery) the method achieved 0.95 ± 0.07 °C mean absolute error (range 0.88 to 1.05 °C) against contact sensors. On three adapted public benchmarks (LLVIP, M3FD and DroneVehicle) it achieved 1.48 to 2.42 °C against calibrated two-dimensional camera readings. The cross-dataset average (1.43 ± 0.09 °C) is reported only as a summary across heterogeneous regimes and not as a single measurement-grade result. Ablation showed independent contributions from emissivity modeling, uncertainty calibration and hierarchical deformation. The system delivered calibrated uncertainty (expected calibration error = 0.042) at 32 fps.
Thermal target reconstruction and novel view synthesis from sparse UAV observations using 3D Gaussian splatting
2027, Expert Systems with Applications
Show abstract
In real-world unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based maritime observation scenarios, infrared data acquisition is often constrained by limited flight time, sensor capability, flight trajectory, and environmental conditions. As a result, only sparse thermal observations may be available for non-cooperative or weakly cooperative maritime targets, posing a fundamental challenge to data-driven reconstruction and rendering methods. To address this problem, we propose SparseIR-GS, a 3D Gaussian Splatting-based framework for sparse-view thermal scene reconstruction and novel view synthesis from UAV-captured infrared images. Specifically, we introduce an uncertainty-aware optimization strategy to constrain dual radiance fields, a thermal target-guided densification mechanism to encourage Gaussian allocation in thermally salient target regions, and a view-depth-guided pruning scheme to suppress geometrically inconsistent floating artifacts. To support realistic evaluation, we construct the Sanya IR dataset, a UAV-based multi-view thermal benchmark of maritime targets, comprising 7414 infrared images of five ship categories captured under diverse viewpoints and temporal conditions. Extensive experiments on both the Sanya IR dataset and the public TI-NSD dataset demonstrate that SparseIR-GS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods under sparse-view settings. In particular, our method achieves the best PSNR under 6-view and 12-view configurations, while producing more coherent target structures and improved geometric consistency from limited infrared observations. SparseIR-GS provides a practical and efficient solution for data-efficient infrared scene understanding, offering potential for UAV-based maritime situational awareness, non-cooperative target modeling, and sparse-data infrared view generation. Sanya IR dataset will be released at https://github.com/zhaoyunpeng57/SparseIR-GS.
