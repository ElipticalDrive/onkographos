Interiors, cave undersides and dense groves currently get the same flat hemisphere term as an open meadow, so they read as unlit rather than as indirectly lit — the pain called out in docs/SUGAR_CAVES_SHIP.md. This adds the one missing term: a soft coloured bounce, with no second render pass, no G-buffer and no ray budget.
Full doc: docs/IRRADIANCE_PROBES.md.
The one approach
Irradiance probes, per the brief's preferred option. Probes beat screen-space here on the cases that actually motivated the work: SSGI cannot light what is off-screen, and the cave undersides and grove interiors that need this most are exactly what leaves the frame. A probe volume is stable under camera motion, costs four texture fetches, and is a plain Data3DTexture — which the GLSL node backend handles as well as WGSL.
No RSM, no SSGI, no second technique stacked on top.
Why it is candy-first
The bake is deliberately not a physically-correct gather, because a correct one converges toward grey. Four pastel sources are summed:
Sky openness — hemisphere sky, gated by terrain-derived sky visibility. Zero under a roof.
Ground bounce — sun off the pale-mint terrain albedo (0x98fb98, matching createGroundMaterial()), falling off with height. This is what tints the underside of every cap and canopy.
Bounce donors — every point light, spot light and decorative fill already in src/rendering/lights.ts, treated as a coloured surface that bleeds. No new authoring: a grove of luminous flowers registers fills today, so it becomes its own bounce for free.
Interior fill — an icy cyan where sky visibility is zero, so sugar-cave interiors are dim, never black.
Then the pastel guard runs: magnitude is preserved (it carries the energy) and only chroma is re-saturated, with the same per-channel correction applied to the directional bands so the bleed keeps the hue of the flat term. Real bounce converges toward grey; this converges toward tint.
Albedo is never touched. The term is added to emissiveNode multiplied by the material's own colour, so it tints the surface instead of washing over it — pastels stay pastel.
Shape
```
gridX × gridY × gridZ probes, cellSize apart, centred on the camera
    ↓ snapped to whole cells (the volume cannot swim)
4 × RGBA8 Data3DTexture — SH L1: L0, L1x, L1y, L1z  (tex0.a = sky visibility)
    ↓ hardware trilinear
irradiance(n) = max(L0 + dot(L1, n) · directionality, 0)
```
The bake is amortised: probesPerFrame probes per frame, walked nearest-to-camera first, cycling forever so the day/night tint follows along with no explicit invalidation.

Known v1 limitations (documented)
Occlusion is heightfield-only — a probe reads "interior" under terrain, not under a mesh canopy. Groves get their bounce from registered fills instead; a donor/occluder proxy registry is the natural v2.
A volume shift restarts the bake, so the term settles over a few frames rather than instantly. Low-frequency and subtly weighted, so it reads as a settle, not a pop.
Unified materials only — terrain, batched foliage shaders and the sky do not sample the volume yet.
Single bounce, diffuse only, by design.
