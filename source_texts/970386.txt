Summary
A scattering atmosphere around a planet that renders as the sky from the ground and as a limb around the planet mesh from orbit, and that drives fog colour and ambient light from the same model. A camera descending continuously from space to the surface sees one consistent atmosphere with no hand-blended handover. Comparable: Unreal's Sky Atmosphere, Unity HDRP's Physically Based Sky.
What the engine has today (the gap)
ProceduralSky is a zenith, horizon and ground gradient with a sun disc, live per frame, with no planet, altitude or scattering model. Its sky evaluation depends on view direction only, with no observer position. SkyBody has a direction and an angular size but no position, so it cannot be approached. SkyBodyVeil and StarField are separate. DistanceFog with FogColorSource.Sky uses flat-world height falloff. So the ground sky, the orbital limb and the fog are separately tuned approximations, and nothing draws a shell around a real planet mesh.
Requested capability
Must-have: a planet atmosphere component: planet centre entity and radius, atmosphere height, scattering parameters with an artist-facing mapping including a tint or colour-bias control, and the sun from the scene's DirectionalLight.
Must-have: ground and space views from one model. Inside, it takes over ProceduralSky's role (sky draw, FogColorSource.Sky, per-frame SH ambient, ray-traced misses). Outside, it draws the shell depth-tested against the planet, with no parallax error on approach.
Must-have: aerial perspective and ambient light from the same model at the observer's altitude.
Must-have: evaluation relative to the planet centre, with the observer's offset computed in double precision on the CPU, so shading does not depend on the frame origin.
Welcome stretch: SkyBody and StarField veiled by the same atmosphere, and several atmospheres in view. One per scene is enough for a first version.
Out of scope - what stays game-side
Clouds, weather and precipitation.
Planet terrain, LOD and streaming.
Ocean rendering.
Physically exact spectral rendering; plausible and tunable is the bar.
Continuous specular probe updates; the game calls Rebake at the ends of a descent.
Acceptance criteria
From the surface at noon the sky is blue overhead and paler at the horizon; at sunset it reddens near the sun, with only the sun direction changed.
From high orbit the planet mesh shows a thin coloured limb on the lit side, fading to black, with stars visible above it.
A camera descending from about two planet radii above a small planet (a radius of hundreds of kilometres) to the surface in 10 s produces a continuous image, with fog and ambient consistent with the sky at every altitude.
Distant terrain fades toward the atmosphere's colour along the view ray.
All parameters are live per frame without a pipeline rebuild.
Scenes without the component are unchanged.
Priority and consumer
Low. A game project whose camera moves continuously from space to a planet's surface. That descent is not needed soon.
Interim
The game blends ProceduralSky, SkyBodyVeil, StarField.Visibility and DistanceFog by camera altitude, with a SkyBody stand-in and a textured limb shell mesh. This costs hand-tuned curves, a visible handover to the real mesh, and a flat-world fog model.
