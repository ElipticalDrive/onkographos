Image-space aliasing is caused by this binary categorization. The cause for this is due to the high frequencies generated by binaryclassified surfaces' abrupt transitions. Instead, we use a nonbinary surface classification approach that converts filtered results into opacity factors. As a result, the smoothness of the band-limited scene in object space transfers to image space, removing the harsh surface transitions. With the information supplied by the surface classifier, detecting a ray-object intersection is straightforward. To assess the filtered value at each sample point along the ray, we apply a trilinear interpolation among the surrounding voxels. The classifier then assigns the appropriate opacity factor. Compositing the shaded samples along the pixel ray in a front-to-back order yields a pixel colour, which is terminated when the accumulated opacity approaches opaque or the ray departs the dataset.
