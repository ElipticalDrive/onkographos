ABSTRACT
Via virtual reality (VR) and in 3D immersive virtual environments
(IVEs), viewers can observe their surroundings in any direction.
This unrestricted view challenges storytelling, as directors cannot
control the viewer’s perspective. To overcome this, we propose to
develop a system that enhances narrative focus by rendering only
high-quality story-critical elements within an IVE. This approach
ensures that viewers are guided to the intended view, maintaining
the director’s vision while preserving the immersive experience of
VR.

ARTIST STATEMENT
In traditional screen-based media, visual narrative is a crucial storytelling component, achieved through a sequence of various shots
[Kress and Van Leeuwen 2020]. These shots are framed in different
ways to influence the viewer’s perception. For instance, a wide
shot often appears at the beginning of a sequence to establish the
setting, gradually drawing closer to the action to increase narrative
intensity as the characters come into focus. Directors commonly
use storyboards to convey their vision for the visual narrative. A
storyboard, similar to a comic strip, consists of sequential panels
that depict shot sizes and framing. This visual tool helps identify
areas needing additional coverage and includes textual descriptions
of the action occurring in each shot, as illustrated in Figure 1.
We propose a novel method for enhancing storytelling in virtual
reality (VR) productions through automatic camera selection based
on a director’s storyboard. Volumetric video (VV), which captures
three-dimensional spaces and characters in high detail, offers unique
opportunities for immersive storytelling [Young et al. 2023a]. However, it also presents challenges in directing the viewer’s attention
within six degrees of freedom (6 DoF) in immersive virtual environments (IVE). Our proposed system leverages a semantic understanding of characters and events within a scene [Lavee et al. 2009]
to identify critical moments and create event boundaries around

Storytelling in VR remains an experimental field. In 360-degree
video, the viewer experiences an omnidirectional format. Techniques in this medium often constrain the viewing area to guide
the viewer’s focus, using methods such as blurring, darkening, or
lowering the quality of less important sections [Dowling et al. 2018].
We propose applying these blocking techniques to virtual productions with 6 DoF. Directors can use storyboards to indicate their
preferred or directed views. Based on the textural description, a
semantic understanding of the characters within the virtual scene
can be created, and volumetric characters can be rendered in high
quality for these selected views. This approach encourages viewers to orient themselves toward the directed view, ensuring they
experience the story as intended while retaining the freedom to
explore the environment. This approach will potentially ensure
the narrative is conveyed as the director envisioned, maintaining
viewer engagement and enhancing the storytelling experience.

TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION
Directing in immersive media is both more comprehensive for
viewers and more complex than traditional video production. This
complexity arises from the lack of established data for exploring,
innovating, developing, and validating new algorithms for highdimensional media content. It necessitates a robust understanding
of both artistic and technical aspects. Understanding the semantic actions within volumetric content is crucial for segmenting
long-form volumetric videos into smaller, manageable chunks for
storyboarding. Various modalities can be leveraged to visually summarize a video, including human actions, scene changes or cut shots,
and shifts in view direction. These summarization techniques are
essential for creating effective storyboards, and volumetric video
summarization represents a new area requiring extensive research.
For instance, as shown in Figure 2, a character performing a dance
move has action boundaries marked in blue based on the trajectory
of movements and limb positions. These boundaries differ significantly from adjacent actions and are identified under a specific
threshold. In the context of storyboarding, these boundaries can
be adjusted by adding or discarding segments. This automated storyboarding process enables artists to perform various tasks using
video chunks, such as editing, sound design, and color grading.

FUTURE WORK
The current proposed system demonstrates a novel approach to
directing immersive media by utilizing semantic understanding to
guide viewers’ focus within volumetric video. However, several
areas for future development can enhance the functionality and
effectiveness of this approach. In future work, we aim to expand
the textual descriptions to include camera movements such as pan
and tilt, and to incorporate tracking, where the camera dynamically
moves within the scene. This will enable more sophisticated and
fluid camera actions, providing a richer storytelling experience.
