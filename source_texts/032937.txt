Abstract. Recent work has positioned 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
as the leading framework for real-time photorealistic scene rendering.
However, semantic 3DGS scene representations remain a challenging
problem, limiting downstream tasks such as object-level editing and scene
understanding for applications in robotics, augmented/virtual reality,
and digital twins. Existing approaches typically rely on latent semantic embeddings or post-reconstruction semantic assignment and refinement. In this work, we introduce S33DGS (Seed-GS), a semantically
initialized 3D Gaussian Splatting approach that assigns discrete semantic identities during Gaussian initialization. Our key observation is that
Structure-from-Motion (SfM) covisibility tracks provide geometrically
consistent multi-view correspondences that can serve as reliable semantic
priors. We further perform optimization with joint photo-semantic supervision through unified differentiable rasterization. Unlike embeddingbased approaches, S33DGS maintains intrinsic semantic Gaussian identities throughout training, producing structurally coherent semantic Gaussian representations without semantic classifiers or post-training label
assignments. Experiments on 3DGS benchmark scenes demonstrate improved Gaussian semantic 3D consistency compared to Gaussian Grouping, competitive semantic rendering performance to both Gaussian Grouping and FlashSplat, and robust object-level editing capabilities while
preserving reconstruction fidelity.

However, constructing semantically coherent Gaussian 3D segmentation remains an open challenge. Existing semantic 3DGS approaches generally follow one of three paradigms. Embedding-based methods
[15,2,9] jointly optimize latent semantic features alongside scene reconstruction,
and subsequently recover semantic identities through follow-on decoding or classification. Other approaches [13] lift 2D segmentation masks onto pretrained
3DGS scenes to obtain explicit object-level 3D segmentation. More recent methods [17,5] further refine Gaussian structures through additional optimization or
decomposition stages to improve semantic boundaries and object separation.
These approaches either represent semantics implicitly through latent feature
spaces, resembling implicit NeRF representations [10], or treat semantic decomposition as a decoupled post-reconstruction process, despite its fundamental tie
to scene geometry.
In this work, we formulate semantic 3DGS as a joint photo-semantic reconstruction problem grounded in SfM geometry [12]. Our key observation is
that the SfM provides covisibility tracks associating sparse 3D points with their
multi-view 2D projections. Given consistent 2D segmentation masks, this can be
extended to associate the 3D points with their persistent 2D semantic identities.
We propose S33DGS, a semantically initialized 3DGS method that injects
explicit semantic identities during Gaussian initialization. Specifically, we propagate object identities from multi-view consistent 2D segmentation masks through
SfM covisibility tracks and resolve the ambiguities using a semantic consensus
voting strategy. The Gaussian representation is initialized using the SfM 3D
points, enabling each Gaussian to inherit a persistent discrete semantic identity
prior to optimization. Semantic identity is therefore treated as an intrinsic attribute of the Gaussian representation, extending the explicit 3DGS paradigm
to semantic scene structure. Figure 1 provides an overview of the pipeline.
Building upon this semantically seeded initialization, we further perform joint
optimization under unified joint photo-semantic supervision. Semantic probabilities are rendered using a similar α′ -blending formulation to photometric rendering, allowing semantic and photometric supervision to jointly constrain Gaussian
parameters throughout optimization. During densification, newly created Gaussians inherit their parents’ identities, preserving semantic consistency without

datasets demonstrate improved semantic 3D coherence, while preserving reconstruction fidelity.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
– We introduce S33DGS, a semantically initialized 3DGS approach that assigns persistent semantic identities during Gaussian initialization using SfM
covisibility tracks.
– We propose a geometry-grounded semantic seeding strategy that propagates
object identities from multi-view consistent 2D segmentation masks to sparse
SfM points through semantic consensus voting.

Related Works
Since its introduction, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [6] has received considerable attention from both academia and industry, leading to a substantial body of
follow-up work extending 3DGS representations beyond photorealistic rendering
toward semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting. Gaussian Grouping [15] extends the
original 3DGS with a learnable semantic identity encoding. This vector is optimized during training using an extended differentiable rasterizer supervised by
multi-view consistent 2D segmentation masks. The method further introduces
3D spatial regularization to encourage local 3D semantic consistency between
neighboring Gaussians. GAGA [9] introduces a 3D-aware memory bank that
allows the use of spatial information to address multi-view object identity inconsistency in multi-view 2D segmentation masks to improve semantic coherence
across views. SAGA [2] augments 3DGS with semantic affinity features, allowing promptable 3D segmentation using 2D prompts. This is achieved through
the distillation of SAM [8] 2D segmentation into multi-granularity features to
improve segmentation consistency across varying object scales.
Unlike embedding-based approaches, FlashSplat [13] formulates semantic
3DGS segmentation as a closed form Integer Linear Programming optimization
problem on pretrained 3DGS scenes, where Gaussian object labels are recovered
directly from their rendering contribution to the segmented objects. Similarly,

SAGD [5] proposes a projection-based semantic label assignment with multiview label voting to improve Gaussian decomposition around object boundaries.
More recently, COB-GS [17] presents boundary-adaptive Gaussian splitting of
semantically ambiguous boundary Gaussians, leveraging 2D semantic mask supervision to enhance object-edge reconstruction.
The existing semantic 3DGS approaches generally either encode semantic information implicitly within optimized latent feature spaces or establish semantic
decomposition independently from the geometric reconstruction process itself.
In contrast, our method directly grounds semantic identities in the multi-view
geometric correspondence structure provided by Structure-from-Motion (SfM)
covisibility tracks during Gaussian initialization. By propagating semantic identity as an intrinsic attribute of the Gaussians instead of relying on optimized
embeddings or post-reconstruction semantic lifting.

photo-semantic supervision for semantic 3DGS scenes.

4
Experiments
In this section, we evaluate S33DGS through quantitative and qualitative experiments, scene editing demonstrations, and ablation studies on benchmark
semantic 3DGS datasets.
4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets We evaluate S33DGS on multiple semantic 3DGS benchmark scenes,
covering indoors, outdoors, real and synthetic data. Specifically, we use the bear
scene from Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [4], the figurines, ramen, and teatime scenes
from LERF [7], the kitchen and counter scenes from Mip-NeRF360 [1], as well
as a subset of Replica scenes [14]. We use 2D segmentation masks provided by
Gaussian Grouping [15] and generated using SAM [8] and DEVA [3] tracking
pipelines for all scenes, except Replica, where we use the already provided segmentation masks.
Evaluation Protocol Following standard practice in novel-view synthesis evaluation, we reserve 1 image out of every 8 views as a held-out test image during
training. All reported quantitative 2D metrics are computed on these unseen
test views after optimization.
Baselines We compare S33DGS against recent semantic 3DGS approaches including Gaussian Grouping [15] and Flashsplat [13]. To ensure fair comparison, all baseline results are reproduced using the same inputs and under the
same training and evaluation protocol.
Implementation Details Our S33DGS implementation is built upon the original 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, with a modified differentiable Gaussian
rasterizer supporting semantic identity rendering alongside photometric rendering. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU with 40GB
VRAM. We use the original image resolutions provided by each dataset without
resizing and report results at the standard 7k and 30k optimization iterations.
All methods are trained using the default optimization hyperparameters.
Evaluation Metrics For photometric reconstruction quality, we report L1,
PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics. For semantic 2D evaluation, we report IoU,
Boundary IoU (BIoU), and Accuracy. For S33DGS, semantic 2D metrics are computed by taking the arg max over the rendered semantic probability distributions
and comparing the resulting semantic predictions against the ground-truth 2D
segmentation masks. Additionally, we introduce two metrics to quantify local semantic consistency in 3D, the kNN Label Agreement (KLA), which measures the
average fraction of neighboring Gaussians sharing the same semantic identity.

4.2
Results
Tables 1 and 2 report the quantitative results of 2D semantic segmentation
rendering and photometric reconstruction. We report the semantic metrics on
all test views of all scenes with full scene 2D segmentation rendering.
Overall, Gaussian Grouping attains the highest scores in terms of rendered
semantic segmentation metrics. This behavior can largely be attributed to its
embedding-based formulation, where Gaussian semantic embeddings are optimized and α′ -blended during rendering before being decoded by a learned scenespecific semantic classifier. Since the classifier is trained directly on rendered
feature mixtures, the method naturally produces highly optimized 2D semantic
renderings even in regions where the underlying Gaussian-level semantic decomposition may remain structurally ambiguous.
In contrast, S33DGS directly associates each Gaussian primitive with a persistent explicit semantic identity established during initialization through SfM
covisibility-guided semantic consensus. Consequently, semantic supervision directly constrains explicit Gaussian semantic identities rather than latent rendered feature representations. Although this formulation yields lower rendered
semantic metrics compared to Gaussian Grouping, it produces substantially
more coherent Gaussian-level semantic decompositions as explained further.
Importantly, S33DGS preserves the photometric reconstruction quality of the
original 3D Gaussian Splatting formulation despite jointly optimizing semantic
supervision throughout the entire training process. As shown in Table 2, our
method achieves reconstruction metrics nearly identical to original 3DGS and
Gaussian Grouping across all evaluation metrics, demonstrating that explicit
semantic supervision does not compromise rendering fidelity.

4.3
Scene Editing
The explicit semantic identities maintained by S33DGS enable direct object-level
scene manipulation, while other approaches require additional semantic classifiers or post-processing stages. Figure 4 demonstrates object removal results
obtained by directly selecting and removing Gaussians associated with specific
semantic identities. Because semantic identities are explicitly attached to Gaussian primitives rather than inferred from latent semantic embeddings, object
selection remains structurally coherent at the Gaussian level. This allows direct manipulation of semantically segmented scene components while preserving
the photorealistic rendering quality of the remaining scene structure. Additionally, although Gaussian Grouping employs 3D regularization during optimization
whereas our method does not, our method achieves superior results without requiring any post-processing.

4.4
Ablation studies
We further evaluate the individual contributions of semantic initialization and
joint photo-semantic supervision through two ablation experiments: (i) S33DGS
without semantic supervision, where we initialize the scene using semantic seeding but do not add semantic loss to the optimization, and (ii) S33DGS with
random semantic identity initialization.
S33DGS without semantic supervision. In this variant, Gaussians are initialized using the proposed SfM covisibility-guided semantic seeding strategy,
but the semantic loss term is removed during optimization. Consequently, semantic identities remain fixed throughout training while the Gaussian representation is optimized solely using photometric supervision. This experiment
demonstrates that geometry-grounded semantic initialization alone already produces meaningful semantic Gaussian decompositions. However, without semantic
supervision, semantic boundaries gradually become less consistent during adaptive density control and geometric refinement, particularly near object contours
and partially occluded regions. This behavior highlights the importance of joint
photo-semantic supervision for maintaining semantic consistency throughout the
optimization process.

Limitations
Despite its advantages, S33DGS remains strongly dependent on the quality and
multi-view consistency of the provided 2D semantic masks. Inconsistent segmentation masks, tracking failures, or missing semantic annotations directly propagate through the SfM-guided semantic initialization process and may lead to incorrect Gaussian semantic assignments. Additionally, small objects with limited
sparse SfM support may not receive sufficiently reliable semantic initialization
due to the absence of stable multi-view correspondence tracks. This limitation
is particularly noticeable in complex real-world scenes where small or partially
occluded objects are poorly represented within the sparse SfM reconstruction.
Finally, unlike embedding-based approaches capable of learning smooth semantic interpolation in rendered feature space, S33DGS relies on explicit discrete
semantic identities. While this formulation improves Gaussian-level semantic
coherence and interpretability, it may reduce flexibility in handling ambiguous
semantic transitions or noisy semantic supervision.
5
Conclusion
We presented S33DGS, a semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that grounds
semantic structure directly in the Structure-from-Motion initialization process.
By leveraging SfM covisibility tracks and multi-view semantic consensus, S33DGS
assigns persistent explicit semantic identities to Gaussian primitives prior to optimization, enabling geometry-grounded semantic scene reconstruction.
Experimental results demonstrate that our method preserves the photorealistic rendering quality of original 3DGS while producing more coherent 3D
Gaussian-level semantic decompositions and enabling direct object-level scene
manipulation.
