Replace the Access Chit with numbered keycards
…
cc1b1e8
```
The Access Chit was an item that did nothing. Its catalogue copy promised
"Passive. Opens keyed doors" and nothing in the engine ever read it, while
doors have always locked on a numeric `key` that `DoorStats` documents as a
keycard id. The gap was already on record as gotcha #5 in MAP_AUTHORING.

Three things said this feature was designed and then left unfinished.
`Door`'s own class doc: "only a terminal hack (or, later, a keycard) opens
it." The map already authors a key item — main1's chest carries "Key1",
which the loot reader dropped because no such item existed. And the art:
`access_chit.png` is cut from a source called `keycard icon.aseprite`, whose
build script notes that its other four `clearance_level` frames were "drawn
and waiting for a mechanic" because nothing carried a clearance to pick them
by. This is that mechanic.

`Keycard N` opens a door whose `key` is N, permanently, with no special
cases. Keycards are the one open-ended item family — a map may lock a door
on any clearance, so the matching card has to exist without anyone having
declared it. That is why they are a function rather than a constant, cannot
join KNOWN_ITEMS, are read by pattern in `resolveItemName`, and are answered
for dynamically by ItemCatalog and ItemIcons rather than by an entry each.

The trap was `Door.isManual`, which has two callers. `guardOperableDoorAt`
reads it to decide what a patrol may work for itself, so teaching it about
inventory — the obvious refactor — would have handed every guard on the
level whatever Rowan is carrying. The player's path moves to a new
`opensWith` instead; the rule lives headless in `doorOpensWith` so it can be
tested without a Phaser scene. The denial light follows it too: a door that
reads LOCKED at a man holding its own card is telling him something untrue.

Nothing new is placed, which is a deliberate open decision rather than an
oversight. Every locked door on the map is clearance 2, none has a terminal
within the unlock radius, and the only key the map authors is main1's
Keycard 1 — so they stay shut. `DoorKeys.test.ts` pins that state so it
changes on purpose. CHEST_DEFAULTS loses its third slot rather than gaining
a keycard: a numbered credential has to match a door somebody authored, and
a default table cannot know which.
```
