Replaces #149 (the browser biome lab). This is a Godot tool to build one biome from the ground's octaves in a node graph, raymarched live beside it.
What it is
Run it with tools/biome_mixer.sh. It is a GDScript scene (debug/biome_mixer/) plus one generated shader. It needs no build and doesn't use VoxelTerrain.
Notes: the ground's octaves, k = 3 .. lods + 4. Each note's seed and lattice offset is the engine's make_octave(SEED, k_top - k, k), ported to GDScript and pinned on both sides: --check, and a new case in test_exact_noise.cpp.
Nodes:
Shape: plain, billow, ridged, square or coeffs, with the engine's smoothed |n|.
Multifractal: the engine's chain, wired from coarse to fine notes.
Warp field: three seeded fBms, plugged into any note with its own distance.
Mix: volume × λ/8 capped at 3.3 km, with mute, solo and centre.
Add, Multiply, Constant.
Height curve: a draggable remap R(h), the engine's h + height_m · curve(ground).
Terrace.
Carve: preview only, for trying caves.
Output, with flat and steep colours.
Import: any world.json biome becomes a graph. Its multipliers, shapes, multifractal chain, warp fields, ground_stretch, its region's ground curves, terrace and colours carry over. Climate-field height terms are left out with a warning.
Saves go to climate/biomes/<name>.biome.json, and the session autosaves.
The shader
Structure becomes code; every parameter becomes a uniform. Dragging a slider sets uniforms only, and edits are batched into one recompile per frame.
Compile time: every note and warp octave is a row of one uniform table read in one loop, and the march, bisection, normal and shadow share a single density() call. Inlined per note and per call site, the driver took 31.8 s to compile the mountain. It now takes 0.3 to 0.4 s.
The march:
Notes fade out under two pixels; a Shape fades around its mean, so a faded note moves no height.
The march starts under the ground's bound.
It steps by the density over a slope estimate, with independent notes added in quadrature; overshoots are bisected.
Redraws only on change (low-processor mode, UPDATE_ONCE), at 1/3 resolution while dragging.

Checks

the octave pins match;
the plain template, an every-kind graph and all 15 world.json biomes compile;
each survives a save and reload with the same code and the same uniforms at float32.
tools/unit_test.sh "*make_octave*": 2 cases, 28/28 assertions.
Windowed shots of the mountain, as above.
/simplify (4 agents) and /code-review ran; their findings are fixed, apart from these, left out on purpose:
Engine constants (SMOOTH_ABS_E, NOISE_*_MEAN, DATUM_M, the amplitude cap) are retyped in GDScript and not pinned; only the octave seeds are. Pinning the rest needs a binding.
The octave table isn't packed into vector arrays.
Per-kind UI branches aren't turned into descriptors.
Limits and next
Not bit-exact: the noise is the engine's hash and gradients in float math relative to the preview's origin. The notes land where the engine's are.
Next:
export a graph to a table biome;
an "Apply" that loads it into the real terrain;
engine work for scaled warps and carving;
several biomes and their blend.
Size: ~2,600 lines, of which ~340 are shader text and ~230 node-kind data. That is over the ~1,000-line split rule. It is kept whole because it is one new standalone tool, and it can be split into core + UI if you prefer.

The user's feedback on the first run: the view is slow, the budget of a biome and what the Mix does are unclear, the
right-click menu opens in the wrong place, and there is no way to erase a note.

- View: two layers, 1/3 of the resolution on every change, then the chosen one in strips fitted to ~25 ms a frame
  (any edit cancels them): no frame over 38 ms on the mountain, against 150-200 ms for the whole view, which froze
  the window. The march steps twice the slope estimate's safe step (200 -> 150 ms for the whole view, no holes on
  the imported biomes; the horizon no longer runs out of steps). The shader reads its pixel from FRAGCOORD.
- Budget over the view: noise reads per point (notes, warp octaves; plain ground's for scale), the relief bound, the
  row that moves the most, the time to sharpen.
- Mix rows: named by their note's wavelength, with their swing in metres and their share of the Mix's, the formula
  and total under them.
- x on every node (or Delete) removes it and the Mix row it played; x on a Mix row removes the row and the nodes only
  it used (Kinds.cut_nodes / cut_row, checked by --check).
- The right-click menu opens at the cursor (an embedded popup's position is the window's, not the screen's); the
  graph is fitted a frame after it is built; Open no longer errors before the first save; --set=view.KEY=V.

The user's feedback on the second run: the height curve's in/out graph looks wrong (bound it by the theoretical
input), a note's and a warp's settings are unclear when every biome should play the same notes, and the warp costs

- A Note is the ground's note at its wavelength, nothing else: the variant (another seed) is gone.
- The Warp field node becomes Climate warp: no settings, the climate's wx, wy, wz (the model's climate_warp,
  world.json's by default, an import's from its table, a file from before migrated from its node), one table slot
  however many nodes, as the engine reads its climate once per point (12 reads at 4 octaves).
- Note warp, the cheap warp: x, y, z are Notes read earlier in the table (F * N of their rows), so notes the biome
  plays cost no read (warned when a note moves by a finer one: the engine reads coarse to fine). Preview only, said in
  the status line: the engine's warps are climate fields.
- Height curve: its bound and slope, and its editor's in axis, span its input's bound (compile's curve_in: the
  mountain's +-7.2 km) instead of the output's (+-8.4 km, printed on both axes); out spans what R makes of it, points
  beyond the input are counted, the axes hold still under a drag.
- The bisection ends outside the ground: from the inside end, deeper at the doubled step, the shadow ray started
  underground and drew rings of acne on smooth slopes.
- Budget line: notes moved by the climate warp and by notes; --check covers the Note warp and prints each curve's
  input bound against its points.
