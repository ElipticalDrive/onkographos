Port reusable MIT-licensed Fire Pro rendering techniques into Verse’s Rust/wgpu fire path. Preserve attribution to upstream revision c284f0b13ed2234752087b8dce24f0cb38854147. Add temperature-based spectral color, bounded turbulent volume rendering and optical emission/absorption for existing fire particles. Keep low-tier fallback and existing particle budgets. Capture matching before/after PNGs through the real renderer, validate shaders and affected Rust code, and push the implementation and evidence. This ports rendering techniques; it does not import Three.js, its editor, or a sparse fluid solver.

Implemented and pushed in 551e79e.
Ported the MIT-licensed spectral blackbody integration and adapted bounded emission/absorption ray marching from dgreenheck/threejs-fire-pro at c284f0b13ed2234752087b8dce24f0cb38854147. Shared WGSL now serves the physical particle renderer and the battle renderer's fire effects. Medium/high use 8/16 samples; low retains the original effect. Fixed turbulence and spectral textures total 36 KiB.
Captured and inspected matching real GPU frames at two times through both renderers before pushing. Camera, particles, exposure, lights, and grading match within each pair. No image editing. Pictures and capture receipts: https://github.com/OpenAgentsInc/openagents/tree/main/bench/verse/2026-10-06/fire-pro
Checks: cargo fmt -p verse-pbr; cargo test -p verse-pbr --lib -- --test-threads=1 (86 passed, 7 ignored), including native/GLES WGSL validation. Both renderer capture paths ran successfully on Metal.
Scope: procedural density inside particle proxies, not the sparse fluid solver. Dense overlapping flames still produce broad bright cores. Existing smoke and scene lights remain in use; multiplayer performance is not established by these captures.
