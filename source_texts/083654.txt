FPZIP and ZFP

Nice 🥳 Have you tried using SZ? https://github.com/szcompressor/SZ
When compressing the data with them I get the type bytes as output. To get the compression factor is it ok if I just use numpy.nbytes method to get the number of bytes from the data and len(compressed_bytes) to get the number of bytes as well and divide them? If not, how do I compute this value?
That is fine. I assume that compressed_bytes has the bytes format - then the length should return you the proper size.
Regarding nomenclature: before/after = compression factor, after / before = compression ratio
And the last question, if I want to know how much data im losing by compressing with those methods, is it ok to compute the MSE as well?
You can compute the MSE for all of them. Our approach and the one from ZFP, FPZIP and SZ. ZFP is the lossy successor of FPZIP.
Therefore I think it will outperform FPZIP quite often, but lets test this.
Additionally you have to test if the maximum error in the dataset is within the acceptable error bounds given to the algorithms ZFP, FPZIP and SZ. They have to guarantee that.
Comparing of our approach and them based on the compression ratio/factor can be done if we feed our maximum error as input to them as limit for the acceptable error. Then, we can compute the ratio/factor and compare. But they have to stay within the error bound.
Also consider that the compression ratio/factor calculated for our model is not the final one. The whole encoding step is missing. But, the reconstruction errors are final. Therefore, a comparison of them is fine.
