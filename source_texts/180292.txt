Abstract:
The shear-warp algorithm is considered as one of the most fastest algorithms in volume rendering. However, it achieves such speed at the price of reduced image quality. During the shear-warp resampling process, the sampling distance varies as the viewing orientation varies, and at some viewing angles, the more larger distance would result in the image-aliasing phenomenon in the resulting image and affect the rendering quality of image. To weaken the aliasing phenomenon effectly, this paper present a new interpolation method based on multiquadric method which is one of the radial basis functions interpolation. In this method, we construct a curve from four original and adjacent sampling points along the prime view orientation, and then a new sampling point is obtained in this curve and used to construct new slices, which are added into original slices. Finally, a new 3D data field can be got. Based on this new data field, shear-warp rendering could significantly improve the image quality and obviously reduce the aliasing phenomenon.

I. Introduction
The scientific computing visualization is a technology that translates the large scale data from science computing process and result into screen image and graphics using computer graphics and image processing. Rendering is the most important part during the processes of the scientific computing visualization, and it includes surface rendering and volume rendering. Surface rendering algorithm has a fast speed, but it can not reach enough accuracy in object's details. Volume rendering is a flexible technique for visualizing scalar fields with widespread applicability in medical imaging and scientific visualization. It renders the three-dimensional data field to final two-dimensional image directly instead of constructing the geometric describing of object's surfaces in surface rendering. There are two categories in volume rendering: image-order algorithms and object-order algorithms. The image-order algorithms, such as raycasting [1], make use of the template to speed up the sampling process and use the Early Ray Termination (ERT) technique to improve the compositing speed. The object-order algorithms, such as footprint [2], use the spatial data structure to skip the transparent voxels and the template of filter kernel to speed up the compositing. However, both of the raycasting and footprint methods have disadvantages of large amount of calculation and a long time for computing. So, it is hard to achieve the requirement of interactive applications that using the two methods above. The shear-warp algorithm was invented by Lacroute and Levoy [3], and combines the advantages of image-order and object-order algorithms. This method is based on a factorization of the viewing matrix into a 3D shear parallel to the slices of the volume data, a projection to form a distorted intermediate image, and a 2D warp to produce the final image. However, some shortcomings still exist in this algorithm. Since the interpolation only occurs within one slice at a time, more accurate tri-linear interpolation reduces to less accurate bilinear interpolation. The ray sampling distance varies between 1 and , depending on the view orientation. This lead to aliasing and staircasting effects at some viewing angles, especially near 45°.
