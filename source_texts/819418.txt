Split from #241 (operations; the shapes are in #298). Step 2 of the merge order in #232.
Background
Examples/Twist writes a twist as a user type, because the library has none. A twist is not the only gap: revolved and extruded profiles, periodic and polar arrays, deformations and fixed-radius blends are standard in SDF modeling and asked for by users of embedded-boundary codes. What exists today:
Operations: Translate, Rotate, uniform Scale, Reflect (all Affine), Elongate, FiniteRepetition, Complement, Offset, Annular, Blur, Mollify, Union/Intersection/Difference and their smooth forms (two blend kinds), the BVH unions.
Proposal
Each item lists the value quality it would have (an exact distance, or a bound, which keeps sphere tracing and BVH pruning safe).

Divide by sqrt(1 + (rate r)^2), r the box's largest distance from the axis (as Examples/Twist).

Evaluate at abs(p_i): the object and its mirror image, one evaluation.
C. Profiles: extrude and revolve
2D primitives (circle, rectangle, rounded rectangle, regular polygon, a general polygon from points, an arc, a 2D capsule) and two operations that make 3D shapes of them:
Operation
Value
Notes
Extrude(profile, height)
exact
Prisms, airfoil sections, channels.
Revolve(profile, offset)
exact
Nozzles, bottles, any axisymmetric part.
This is the largest item (a 2D shape concept beside isShape) and the one that unlocks the most geometry.
F. Blend operators
Operation
Notes
Chamfer and round (column) union/intersection/difference
hg_sdf's fOpUnionChamfer, fOpUnionRound, ...: a bevel or a fillet of fixed radius rather than a blend.
Xor(a, b)
Symmetric difference.
Mix(a, b, t)
Linear interpolation between two shapes (morphing).
What each needs (from CLAUDE.md)
A new operation: an ExprDetail::Kind, its box and distance-quality rule in the builder, its arithmetic in CSGOps, clauses in TapeDetail::Op and a case in Tape::evaluateBody, and a Fused:: operation and function; optionally span arithmetic, a RayOp and a fused ray form.
Unit tests (with a [gpu] case), InstantiateAll.cpp, Sphinx docs (concepts and implementation pages), Tape and Fused performance cases for new operations, and an example where one teaches something new (the Examples/Twist user type becomes the library call).
Suggested order
A and B together (twist, bend, taper, non-uniform scale; infinite, polar and mirror repetition): operations only, no new concepts.
C, profiles with extrude and revolve.
F, blend operators.
A, B, C and F touch the Tape evaluator and the expression builder, so they go one at a time.
To decide
The list, its names, and the order.
Whether 2D profiles get their own concept (isProfile) or are 3D shapes with an ignored axis.

Rounding and fillets belong here (maintainer, 2026-11-05). Shrinking then expanding one field cancels: (f + r) - r = f, so rounding needs either the shrunk shape built with its own exact distance, or an operation that makes the fillet itself:
Round and chamfer union, intersection and difference (hg_sdf's fOpUnionRound, fOpUnionChamfer and siblings, section F): a fillet or bevel of a fixed radius exactly along the edge where two shapes meet, concave or convex, needing each operand's distance only within that radius. Unlike SmoothUnion, the radius is a true fillet radius.
A Rounded(shape, r) helper for primitives: Offset of the shape shrunk by r, built as the same kind of shape (box, prism, cylinder, cone, tube, ...), which rounds every convex edge and corner exactly. The Shapes example does this by hand for a prism (Nine analytic shapes: ellipsoid, capped and round cones, polygon prism, tube, capped torus, cut spheres, wedge #300).
Not planned: opening or closing of a general composition, which would need the exact distance of the shrunk solid (a sampled field, CSG: more primitives (ellipsoid, frustum, prisms, Platonic solids, lattices, sampled fields, ...) #298 G).
