Abstract:
Accurate and efficient solar irradiance modeling in urban environments remains a significant challenge due to the complex spatial interactions among surfaces. This is especially important for renewable energy harvesting, such as photovoltaic (PV) systems. This paper presents a novel method for estimating solar irradiance over 3D voxelized laser scanned data using a Sparse Voxel Octree (SVO) structure, implemented using General Purpose Computing on Graphics Processing Units (GPGPU). The method extends conventional state-of-the-art by incorporating not only direct and diffuse irradiance, but also second-order reflective irradiance components. A spherical ray-casting approach, leveraging the SVO, is proposed to simulate visibility and irradiance transfer within urban environments. Performance and accuracy were evaluated by estimating the solar potential over high-resolution open LiDAR datasets from Vaihingen and New York City, revealing that the method is computationally feasible and robust across varying urban densities.

I. Introduction
In recent years, the volume of geospatial data derived from heterogeneous data streams has increased immensely. Especially due to the widespread adoption of LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) technology, which enables high-resolution 3D laser scanning and produces massive point cloud datasets [1]. The energy sector, in particular, has benefited from this adoption. Applications such as solar potential estimation [2]–[4], thermal performance modeling of buildings [5], and urban heat island (UHI) analysis [6] have seen notable improvements in accuracy and scalability due to detailed 3D data, especially in complex urban environments [7].
