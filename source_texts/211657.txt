Identifiable Joint-Embedding World Models

Abstract
LeJEPA learns representations by combining predictive alignment with isotropic Gaussian regularization, but the rotational symmetry of the Gaussian target leaves learned coordinates identifiable only
up to orthogonal transformations. We introduce LeJEPA-FGP, a structured extension that replaces
the pointwise isotropic Gaussian embedding law with a factorized Gaussian-process law over indexed
representations. Each latent channel follows its own GP covariance kernel across an index such as time,
space, or pose, while remaining marginally standard Gaussian at each point. We propose FGP-SIGReg,
a sketched characteristic-function regularizer for matching factorized GP embedding laws. We prove
that FGP-SIGReg consistently matches finite-dimensional factorized GP distributions. Under GP-OU
positive-pair transitions, alignment with exact FGP law matching forces the representation into the
first Wiener chaos, yielding linear recovery of the latent GP path. Under shared index-local encoders,
distinct covariance kernels reduce the residual ambiguity from orthogonal rotations to signed permutations, while repeated kernels yield block-orthogonal ambiguity. Synthetic GP experiments validate
signed-permutation recovery under distinct kernels and block recovery under kernel collision.
1
Introduction
Self-supervised representation learning has made substantial progress by training neural networks to
predict, align, or compare representations across views of the same underlying input. Contrastive and
non-contrastive joint-embedding methods such as BYOL, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, VICReg, and DINO
have shown that useful representations can be learned without explicit labels, provided that the training
objective avoids collapse and encourages nontrivial embedding structure (Grill et al., 2020; Chen and
He, 2021; Zbontar et al., 2021; Bardes et al., 2022; Caron et al., 2021). Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) push this principle toward world modeling by predicting latent representations
rather than reconstructing observations, thereby encouraging abstractions that discard irrelevant pixel-level
detail while preserving predictable structure (LeCun, 2022; Assran et al., 2023). This perspective is closely
aligned with the broader world-modeling view of learning compact internal representations that support
prediction, planning, and generalization (Ha and Schmidhuber, 2018).
A recent line of work has made this idea more explicit in the LeJEPA framework. LeJEPA combines
a joint-embedding predictive loss with Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), which
constrains the learned embeddings to match an isotropic Gaussian target distribution (Balestriero and
LeCun, 2025). This formulation is appealing because it replaces several common self-supervised heuristics,
such as stop-gradient, teacher–student asymmetry, or carefully tuned covariance regularizers, by a direct
distribution-matching principle. Even more importantly, recent theory shows that alignment together
with Gaussian regularization can recover the latent variables of a Gaussian world up to an orthogonal
transformation, thereby giving a mathematical account of when LeJEPA learns a world model (Klindt
et al., 2026). However, the same isotropic Gaussian law that enables this clean analysis is also invariant
under arbitrary orthogonal rotations. Thus, pointwise Gaussian regularization can recover a linearly useful
latent space, but it does not by itself select a factor-aligned coordinate system.
LeJEPA-FGP: Factorized Gaussian Process Regularization for Identifiable Joint-Embedding World Models
This paper asks whether the Gaussian target in LeJEPA can be made more structured without losing
the spectral mechanism that makes the theory work. Our answer is to replace the pointwise isotropic
Gaussian target by a factorized Gaussian process (FGP) law over indexed representations. The index
may represent time, spatial position, camera pose, rotation angle, sensor location, or any other coordinate
along which the world has structured variation. Instead of requiring only that each embedding vector be
marginally distributed as N (0, Id ), LeJEPA-FGP requires each latent channel to follow its own Gaussian
process covariance kernel across the index. The target remains Gaussian, but it is no longer rotationally
symmetric across channels when the kernels are distinct.
This construction connects LeJEPA to a long line of probabilistic latent-variable models that use
Gaussian process priors to capture smoothness and structured dependencies. Gaussian processes provide a
classical nonparametric prior over functions and are fully determined by their finite-dimensional Gaussian
marginals (Rasmussen and Williams, 2006). In deep generative modeling, GP priors have been incorporated
into variational autoencoders to model latent dynamics, temporal smoothness, missing-data structure,
and spatio-temporal correlations (Kingma and Welling, 2014; Casale et al., 2018; Fortuin et al., 2020;
Jazbec et al., 2020; Bing et al., 2021; Ashman et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2022). LeJEPA-FGP differs from
these GP-VAE approaches in its learning principle: it is a deterministic joint-embedding method and
performs distribution matching in representation space, rather than variational posterior inference through
a generative decoder.
The proposed factorized-GP target is also related to identifiability ideas in nonlinear ICA. Classical
nonlinear ICA is not identifiable without additional structure, but identifiability can be recovered by
exploiting auxiliary variables, temporal dependencies, nonstationarity, or structured priors (Hyvärinen
et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020). Recent spatial nonlinear ICA theory shows that, in the Gaussianprocess limit, independent GP components are identifiable precisely when their covariance kernels are
distinct (Hälvä et al., 2024). This observation matches the central symmetry of LeJEPA-FGP: if two
latent channels have the same covariance kernel, rotations within that equal-kernel block preserve the
target law; if their kernels are distinct, the rotational ambiguity is reduced to signed permutation.
We make three main contributions. First, we introduce FGP-SIGReg, a structured extension of
SIGReg that matches random one-dimensional projections after normalizing them by their target GP
variances. This gives a direct distribution-matching regularizer for factorized GP embedding laws. Second,
we prove that, under GP-OU positive-pair transitions, alignment plus exact FGP law matching forces the
learned representation into the first Wiener chaos, so the representation is linear in the latent GP path.
Third, under shared index-local encoders, we prove that distinct covariance kernels reduce the residual
ambiguity from arbitrary orthogonal rotations to signed permutations, while repeated kernels yield the
corresponding block-orthogonal ambiguity. Experiments on synthetic GP signals verify these theoretical
predictions: LeJEPA-FGP achieves stable source recovery under distinct kernels, while kernel collisions
produce the predicted block recovery structure.

Methodology
To move beyond pointwise representations, we consider latent variables that possess structure along an
auxiliary index (e.g., time, space, camera pose, or sensor location). Concretely, we model the world as d
independent latent processes, each equipped with its own covariance kernel. This leads to a factorized
Gaussian process (FGP) target that generalizes the isotropic Gaussian used in standard LeJEPA.

3
Experiments
We evaluate LeJEPA-FGP on controlled synthetic signals generated from latent Gaussian-process factors.
The experiments test the two qualitative predictions of the theory. First, distinct covariance kernels should
yield source recovery up to signed permutation. Second, repeated kernels should yield recovery of the
corresponding latent subspace rather than a unique componentwise alignment. All reported quantitative
results are averaged over three independent runs.
Metrics. We evaluate source recovery using the coefficient of determination R2 (h → z), the maximum
correlation coefficient (MCC) after matching learned channels to true sources, the signed-permutation
mean squared error, and a cross-kernel error measuring residual cross-channel covariance. Higher values
are better for R2 and MCC, while lower values are better for signed-permutation MSE and cross-kernel
error. For the kernel-collision experiment, we also track the within-block energy of the repeated-kernel
block; this quantity equals one when the learned representation recovers the full repeated-kernel subspace.
Recovery under distinct kernels. Figure 1a shows that LeJEPA-FGP rapidly converges to near-perfect
MCC, while pointwise LeJEPA converges to a lower and more variable channel alignment. The final
quantitative results are summarized in Table 1. With distinct kernels, LeJEPA-FGP achieves an MCC of
0.9962 ± 0.0002 and a signed-permutation MSE of 0.0076 ± 0.0003, whereas pointwise LeJEPA achieves
an MCC of 0.9273 ± 0.0324 and a signed-permutation MSE of 0.1453 ± 0.0647. Thus, both methods
learn informative representations, but the factorized-GP target substantially improves channel-level factor
recovery.
Kernel-collision block recovery. The kernel-collision setting tests the boundary case predicted by the
theory. When two latent factors share the same covariance kernel, LeJEPA-FGP is not expected to identify
those two components individually; instead, it should recover their shared subspace. This is exactly what
we observe. Figure 1b shows that the within-block energy converges to one across all three runs. The final
value is 0.999995 ± 0.000005, indicating almost perfect recovery of the repeated-kernel block.

Conclusion
We introduced LeJEPA-FGP, a structured extension of LeJEPA that replaces the pointwise isotropic
Gaussian embedding law with a factorized Gaussian-process law over indexed representations. The key
idea is to keep the target distribution Gaussian, thereby preserving the Hermite spectral mechanism
underlying LeJEPA’s linear recovery theory, while enriching the target with channel-wise covariance
structure. This changes the symmetry of the embedding law: instead of being invariant under arbitrary
orthogonal rotations, the factorized-GP target distinguishes latent channels through their covariance

First, FGP-SIGReg provides a direct distribution-matching regularizer for factorized GP embedding

theory. These results show that factorized Gaussian-process regularization provides a principled way to
inject structured temporal or spatial information into joint-embedding world models while retaining the
Gaussian foundation of LeJEPA.
