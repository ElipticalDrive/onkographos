What
Meow2/Math/Ray.h adds:
rayFromScreen: the ray under a pixel, for editor picking (M9).
Ray tests against planes, spheres, axis-aligned and oriented boxes, and triangles (with barycentrics and optional back-face culling).
pickClosest: the nearest of many boxes.
The test Math.Ray.PickClosestFromScreen covers M3's "a ray from a screen point picks the right AABB".
How it works
rayFromScreen: un-projects the pixel at depth 1 (the near plane) and at depth 0.5, using screenToWorld from [M3] Camera maths #122. The direction is the normalised difference, which works for perspective and orthographic cameras alike.
Sphere: solves t² + 2bt + c = 0 (unit direction). It exits early when the origin is outside (c > 0) and the ray points away (b > 0).
AABB (slabs): per axis, computes the entry and exit distances. A zero direction component is handled explicitly: inside the slab it continues, outside it misses. The ray hits when max(entries) ≤ min(exits).
Oriented box: moves the ray into the box's frame (XMVector3InverseRotate), then runs the slab test. Rotation preserves lengths, so the distance is unchanged.
Triangle (Möller–Trumbore): solves for t, u, v with two cross products and no precomputed plane. The determinant's sign tells front from back.
Why it's done this way
Concepts
Picking: the mouse is a 2D point, but the scene is 3D. The pixel corresponds to a whole line of 3D points, from the near plane into the distance. Un-projecting two depths gives that line. Testing it against each object's bounds and keeping the closest hit is how editors select.
The slab method: a box is the intersection of three "slabs" (the space between two parallel planes). The ray is inside each slab for an interval of t, and it's inside the box where all three intervals overlap. It needs no branches on which face is hit, and it's the standard in every ray tracer.
Möller–Trumbore: it writes the hit point both as origin + t·dir and as v0 + u·e1 + v·e2, then solves that 3×3 system with Cramer's rule using cross products. You get the distance and the barycentrics (for interpolating UVs and normals at the hit) in one go, without storing a plane per triangle.
Front faces: in left-handed coordinates with D3D's convention, front faces are clockwise as seen by the viewer. The determinant is positive exactly then, so cullBack is one comparison.
Decisions
Distances, not points: comparing hits needs the distance anyway, and pointAt gives the point when wanted.
Starting inside counts as a hit at 0. For picking and line of sight, "you're inside it" should win.
Grazing hits count (touching an edge or face): consistent with intersects in [M3] Bounding volumes and frustum culling #124. Picking prefers false positives on exact edges over gaps.
pickClosest takes boxes, the editor's per-entity bounds. Triangle-exact picking needs mesh data and a BVH, which come with the renderer.
Alternatives rejected
Option
Why not
DirectXCollision's Intersects(origin, dir, dist)
works, but its edge cases (inside, grazing) aren't documented; ours are tested and documented
GPU picking (render object IDs, read a pixel)
needs the renderer, and later can complement this for pixel-exact picking
Ray from the camera position instead of the near plane
wrong for orthographic cameras, and can hit things between the eye and the near plane that aren't drawn
References:
Möller & Trumbore, Fast, Minimum Storage Ray/Triangle Intersection
Williams et al., An Efficient and Robust Ray–Box Intersection Algorithm
Scratchapixel: ray-box intersection
