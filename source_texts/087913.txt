Phase 0 of taking this engine to four dimensions. Not the engine work:
the question that decides whether the engine work is worth starting,
answered in a day rather than discovered in week four.

You cannot draw four dimensions, so a 4D world reaches a 3D renderer by
slicing - render the hyperplane at the player's w, and moving along w
swaps which slice you see. That only means anything if adjacent slices
are related. If w and w+1 are unrelated worlds then the fourth dimension
is a seed change with extra steps, and no amount of engine work fixes it.

docs/4d.md has the three renders. One step along w keeps the world: same
ocean, same island chain, same mountain mass, with coastlines redrawn and
lakes opened and closed. Eight steps is somewhere else entirely, and it
got there continuously rather than by jumping. That is what a spatial
axis is supposed to do.

Getting there needed two things.

4D noise, because FastNoiseLite exposes GetNoise(x,y) and GetNoise(x,y,z)
and nothing else - it is the one part of a 4D terrain generator that
cannot be reused. src/world/noise4d.cpp is 4D gradient noise: 16
hypercube corners, 32 gradients, quintic fade, quadrilinear
interpolation. Perlin rather than simplex deliberately, because 4D
simplex needs coordinate ranking across five vertices and is much harder
to convince yourself is right, while Perlin in 4D is the 3D algorithm
with twice the corners and has an exact property to test against.

And a slice harness, src/bench/slice4d.cpp, which found two things:

  The fourth axis needs its own scale. Treating w like x and z changed
  4.6% of columns by at most one block - the world was frozen along w.
  Not a bug: the continent field's noise cell is 250 units wide, and a
  player who can walk 250 units along x cannot walk 250 along w. Swept
  the scale (1 -> 4.6% of columns, 5 -> 51%, 25 -> 77%, 60 -> 85%) and
  took 6. The max column jump stays in single digits at every scale,
  which is the row that matters: the field is continuous along w
  regardless, so this is a choice about feel, not correctness.

  The 3D height formula does not transfer. kSeaLevel + n*28 + 14 put
  every column between y=31 and y=46 - no water anywhere - because this
  fbm normalizes by the amplitude sum and FastNoiseLite's does not, so
  the composite measures p1..p99 of -0.17..0.21 instead of filling
  [-1,1]. Refitted to the measured distribution. Worth recording as the
  shape of what the rest of the 4D work looks like: the algorithms port,
  the constants do not.

32 checks in tests/test_noise4d.cpp, pinning properties rather than
outputs: exactly zero at integer lattice points, pure and seed-stable,
in range and using it, C1 and C2 across cell boundaries, isotropic, and
sliceable.

Fifteen faults injected, all fifteen caught. Three fail at compile time:
a gradient table with a zero, duplicated, or over-long row moves the
field's standard deviation by 1.6%, which is too small to gate on
without a brittle bound, so grad4_table_is_well_formed() asserts the
structure instead. Two needed a second round:

  Dropping w from the gradient dot survived every value, range and
  continuity check - the hash still varies with w and the interpolation
  still runs along it, so the field still responds to w and merely
  degrades to value noise on that axis. Isotropy catches it: the
  fastest/slowest axis ratio is 1.004-1.015 correct, 1.58-1.60 broken.

  Perlin's original cubic fade passed everything, because it is C1 - it
  is only the curvature that jumps. The C2 check separates them: 1.13-1.28
  against 11.3-12.1.

Nothing in the engine is 4D yet and nothing existing changed. --validate
still reports gpu_mesh_mb=10.99 and check_invariance.py still passes;
this commit is additive.

Turns the previous commit's noise into a terrain generator, and answers
the question that decides whether a 4D engine is buildable: what does
moving along w actually cost?

TerrainGen4D is the 3D generator's structure with w threaded through
every sample - domain-warped fBm for height, two intersecting
iso-surfaces for caves, altitude bands for surface material. The
frequencies port unchanged, because a frequency is about feature size in
world units and that does not depend on how many axes there are. The
amplitudes did not, for the reason docs/4d.md records.

29 checks in tests/test_terrain_gen4d.cpp. The two that matter are the
ones that decide whether w is a dimension at all: a slice has to be an
ordinary usable world, and adjacent slices have to be related. Both
failures are silent - a w that does nothing gives a perfectly correct
world with no fourth dimension in it, and a w that does too much gives
one that teleports.

Then the cost. A step along w swaps the whole visible slice, so every
mesh is invalid. src/bench/wcost.cpp measures the full rebuild and, more
usefully, how much of it is avoidable. Radius 6, 169 chunks,
single-threaded on an M4: 198 ms per step (gen 95 + mesh 103). Change
fractions, stable across radii 4-8 and three seeds:

    chunk   (today's mesher)       100%    198 ms   1.00x
    section (renderer's unit)    19-24%    117 ms   1.70x
    column  (theoretical floor)  92-95%    191 ms   1.03x

Chunk-granular incremental re-meshing is worth exactly nothing. That was
one of three optimisations this work set out to try, and it is dead:
every chunk in the window changes on every step, so there is nothing to
skip. Column granularity sounds finer and is worse than sections.

Both have one cause, and it is the finding: the fourth dimension mostly
moves the surface. A 32-block section deep underground is solid rock at
every w and one high above the terrain is empty at every w, so only the
few straddling the surface change. A column spans all 256 blocks, so
almost every column has something moving somewhere.

So a 4D engine should mesh at section granularity, not chunk
granularity. Today's mesher builds a chunk-wide mesh and buckets it into
sections afterwards, which makes that a redesign rather than a tuning
knob - but now a redesign with a number attached.

Nine faults injected. Seven caught. Two are documented as not catchable
rather than papered over, both for the same reason - this generator
composes several noise fields, so breaking one is masked by the others:

  One field losing its seed offset. continents_ carries 0.65 of the
  height and still differs everywhere, so pinning hills_ to a fixed seed
  changes nothing measurable. The test that matters still holds: the
  seed being dropped entirely is caught.

  One of the two cave fields losing w. Caves are an intersection, so
  freezing one still leaves passages opening and closing. Measured over
  five seeds the cave-motion ratio is 1.63-2.10 correct and 1.26-1.75
  broken; the ranges overlap, so any bound would be flaky. Both fields
  losing w - which would freeze the underground while the surface
  morphed - is caught.

Still additive: --validate reports gpu_mesh_mb=10.99, check_invariance
byte-identical, all five test suites green.

Coordinates are floats, so past about 1e7 world units two nearby
positions round to the same float and the noise returns one value for
both. The terrain does not error when that happens - it goes flat, which
is the quietest failure a world advertising no bounds could have.

Measured over 2000 samples half a unit apart: fully resolved from 1e2 to
1e6, 998/2000 distinct at 1e7, and 1/2000 at 1e8. So the usable range is
about a million world units, 62,500 chunks from the origin, and it is
now written down rather than left to be found later.

The test is known to be real rather than decorative because pushing its
magnitude list to 1e8 fails it. A single-axis truncation does not fail
it, and that is correct: real precision loss hits all four coordinates at
once, which is the case measured above.

Also pinned: nothing returns NaN or infinity at any magnitude or sign,
verified by injection. That one matters because a NaN would pass straight
through the height clamp - NaN compares false against both bounds - and
land in chunk contents.

Not specific to 4D: the 3D generator's FastNoiseLite coordinates are
float by default too, so the ceiling is shared.

The 4D Perlin math itself came back clean - the review validated sample()
against an independently written reference using the opposite corner
collapse order, 1.6M points, worst difference 7.2e-07. The defects were
all in the numbers around it.

1. The w-scale table was measured against constants that no longer
   existed, and the conclusion it supported was backwards.

   The original measurement was real: with w treated like x and z, a step
   changed 4.6% of columns by at most one block, so kWScale was set to 6.
   Then the height amplitude was refitted from 28 to 126 and the table was
   never re-measured. Both the header and docs/4d.md carried numbers
   describing a generator that had been replaced underneath them.

   Re-measured against shipped constants at one unit per slice:

       scale    columns changed    max jump
         1          51.2%              4
         6          88.1%             18
        60          96.3%             46

   Scale 1 was never frozen once the amplitude was right. The scaling was
   compensating for the amplitude, not for anything about the fourth
   dimension: the amplitude is 4.5x larger, and 4.5 x 1 block is the
   4-block jump scale 1 produces. kWScale is 1 now - the fourth axis is
   scaled exactly like the other three.

   Two smaller errors went with it. The old table's rows were not
   mutually comparable (the first row was measured at 0.35 units per
   slice, the rest at 1.0). And "the max jump stays in single digits at
   every scale, so the field is continuous along w regardless" was false
   in its own table, where scale 60 read 11.

   Root cause was duplication: slice4d.cpp carried its own kWScale,
   height formula and amplitude. A harness that measures a copy of the
   thing measures nothing. It calls world::TerrainGen4D now, so its
   SLICE4D line prints the table's top row by construction.

2. The documented command did not produce the committed images.

   slice4d defaulted to 0.35 units per slice, so w_01.png was w=0.35 and
   w_08.png was w=2.80 while the captions said one step and eight, and
   `./build/slice4d` produced different files than the ones committed.
   The default is one unit per slice, so the file name is the w value and
   the documented command reproduces all three byte for byte.

3. The [-1, 1] range was not a bound.

   The normalizer was fitted to a raw peak of 1.2244 found over 20M
   samples and 8 seeds. A 200M sweep across 200 seeds finds 1.2572 and
   produces a normalized sample at 1.018. The test had simply not met a
   violating sample - exactly the "coin flip on the next sample rather
   than a bound" the constant's own comment warned against.

   0.75 now, not the 0.7954 that would exactly fit the wider sweep:
   widening a sweep can only push the observed maximum up, so a constant
   fitted to the last maximum is guaranteed to be wrong eventually.
   noise4d_range sweeps 200M across 200 seeds. The height amplitude moved
   115 -> 126 to follow, since it is a function of the noise's scale.

4. The resolution test counted run lengths, not distinct values.

   `if (v != prev) ++distinct` counts consecutive differences, so a field

The 4D review's last contribution was an analysis of the limits of its
own strongest result, and it found a real hole.

sample() was validated against an independently written reference that
collapses the corners w->z->y->x rather than x->y->z->w, agreeing to
7.2e-07 over 1.6M points. Multilinear interpolation is order-independent
only if the bit-to-offset mapping is right, so that pins the corner bit
assignment, the sixteen distance vectors, the weight-to-stage mapping and
the nesting arithmetic. That is the part most likely to be subtly wrong,
and it is right.

It is also all it proves. Everything the reference shares is invisible to
it, and one shared thing was load-bearing:

Taking the distance vector as (corner - sample) instead of
(sample - corner) is an ordinary transcription slip, and since the dot
product is linear in it, the result is exactly a negated field. Injected,
ALL 67 checks across both 4D test files pass - a negated Perlin field is
still zero-mean, isotropic, C1, C2, in range, and zero at lattice points.
It is valid noise. The reference shared the convention, so the
differential check was blind to it as well.

Closed with a known-answer test. Deliberately a golden test in a file
that otherwise pins relationships, because a sign convention has no
relational anchor - the same reason test_crc_known_answer exists in
test_world.cpp. Four points and two seeds, values verified identical at
-O0 and -O2. Re-injecting the reversal now fails with
"expected 0.328125, got -0.328125".

Also recorded, in the code and in docs/4d.md:

  The gradient table's static_assert does far more than it appears to.
  There are exactly 32 permutations of (0, +/-1, +/-1, +/-1), and the
  assert demands 32 distinct rows each with three non-zero components in
  {-1,0,1} - thirty-two distinct vectors meeting that description are
  necessarily the complete set. The table is pinned entirely, with only
  row order free, and order does not matter. A retyped reference table
  would have agreed with itself and proved nothing.

  The normalizer was open by construction: the reference was handed the
  same constant. That is exactly what turned out to be broken, and an
  independent 200M sweep was what found it. The oracle covers algebra,
  not calibration.

  Gradient selection uniformity was verified once (164M lattice corners,
  four seeds, 0.194% spread, chi-square 28.1 on 31 dof against a p=0.001
  critical value of 61.1) and is NOT guarded. An attempt at a regression
  test was removed rather than tuned: the index is not observable through
  the public surface, and a histogram of output values is a poor proxy
  because the field is bell-shaped, so buckets at the extremes are
  legitimately empty. Fitting the bucketing to the field's own
  distribution would have made it pass for a reason unrelated to its
  claim, which is the failure this whole round of work has been about.

--4d generates the world from TerrainGen4D at a slice w, and , and .
step along it. The 3D engine is untouched and remains the default: with
no slice source set every path behaves exactly as it did, --validate
still reports gpu_mesh_mb=10.99, and check_invariance is byte-identical.

The design is the one docs/4d.md scoped. A 4D world reaches a 3D
renderer by slicing, so the mesher, the culler, the lighting and the
renderer never learn there is a fourth axis - they only ever see the
slice at the player's w. That keeps this a change to generation and
streaming rather than to everything, and it is why the whole thing is
one branch in the worker job plus a re-request path.

  World::set_slice_source(gen, w)  points the world at the 4D generator
  World::step_slice(delta, ...)    moves to an adjacent slice

A step re-requests every resident chunk, because the cost model measured
100% of them changing per step - there is nothing to skip. Chunks are
deliberately NOT cleared first, so the previous slice keeps drawing until
its replacement lands and the world morphs rather than blinking through
emptiness. The slice generator and w are captured by value into each job,
like the mesher kind already was: the player can step while jobs are in
flight, and a worker reading the member would generate a chunk for a
slice nobody asked for.

Measured at radius 6, 169 chunks: 51 ms per slice step across 9 workers.
Consistent with the 183 ms single-threaded figure wcost reports.

--verify-4d is the headless check, in audit.sh. Three claims: a step must
CHANGE the world, stepping back must return it to exactly what it was
(the determinism the design rests on - a slice is a pure function of seed
and w), and the GPU meshes must be valid at every stop, since a slice
change re-meshes every chunk in the window. Plus a rapid-step phase, six
steps with no settle between, for the case a player creates by holding
the key.

Two faults injected and caught: step_slice not moving w, and the worker
ignoring w and always generating slice 0. Both report changed=0 FAILED.

Three mesh sites had to move together, because the mesh encoding rides in
u_model and one uniform serves the whole terrain pass - a cube mesh drawn
with the prism model matrix draws crushed into a sixteenth of its chunk.
So every mesh built while prisms are on comes from the prism path:

  - streaming builds cells FIRST and rasterises the voxel grid from them.
    That ordering is what keeps what the player collides with equal to
    what they can see; voxels first and cells derived would put the two a
    rounding apart at every tilt.
  - a chunk restored from disk IS the authority on its own contents, so
    its tiling reads columns back out of it instead of regenerating them.
  - an edit re-tiles from the edited chunk, which costs a few milliseconds
    against the cube path's fraction of one. That is the price of the edit
    landing on the shape the player is actually looking at.

Light and AO are baked the way the cube mesher bakes them, sampled from
the cell OUTSIDE the face. The first render without this was pure white -
a hardcoded full-bright light level reads as a blown-out world, not as a
missing feature, which is exactly the kind of bug a screenshot catches and
a test does not.

AO is per face and derived from the tiling, not from a 3x3 voxel stencil.
The voxel formula asks which of eight cells around a corner are solid; a
cell here has three to six neighbours and no corners in common with a
grid, so there is nothing for it to index. Counting how many of a cell's
own neighbours are solid at the face's height is the same statement in the
tiling's terms and darkens a pit, a crevice and a cave mouth in the same
places.

--validate learned that a prism mesh is not a voxel mesh. "Every face is
backed by a solid voxel on the correct side" assumes faces lie on integer
planes and that what is behind one is a voxel, and neither is true of a
cross-section. It now asks the two questions that ARE true of it and that

This mesher's own comments said horizontal faces could not be merged:
"neighbouring cells present their own polygons at their own angles, so no
two cap faces are coplanar-and-adjacent in the way the greedy mesher
needs." That is true of the polygons and false of the conclusion, and it
cost a third of the geometry.

The preimage of a convex set under a linear map is convex. to_4d is
linear. A lattice box is convex. So a BOX of cells presents ONE convex
polygon to the slice - at any angle, however many cells it spans - which
fan-triangulates exactly like a single cell's and is still bounded by the
same six half-planes. Greedy meshing applies unchanged; it just has to
sweep its mask over (i, k) of the 4D lattice instead of (x, z) of the
chunk, where the faces are not axis-aligned and a rectangle sweep has
nothing to grip.

At a flat cut the lattice IS the voxel grid, so this reduces to the cube
mesher's greedy pass exactly.

    flat           2.47x     1.60x  greedy quad count

Writing box_polygon exposed a latent overflow in the kernel:
SlicePolygon::kMaxVerts was 8, which is the bound for the six lattice
half-planes alone. Clipping to the patch square adds four more, so ten is
the real ceiling, and the clipper TRUNCATES SILENTLY when it runs out -
it would have handed back a polygon that is simply the wrong shape. It
held for single cells because a unit cell can only reach one corner of a
16-wide patch, and it stops holding the moment a merged box spans enough
of the patch to touch two edges.

Block id, light and AO are all in the merge key rather than averaged, so
a merged face is exactly what the unmerged faces were. The cube mesher
can merge across differing light because it samples per corner and lets
the gradient interpolate; one flat polygon with one value cannot, so it
does not.

says where the time goes: terrain generation 4.15 ms/chunk against 1.12
for the mesh - 79% of a rebuild is regenerating noise.

Two changes, both measured.

FACING FIRST. The rebuild queue was ordered by distance from the player,
which rebuilds a chunk three behind them before one five ahead - work the
frame cannot show. A rotation invalidates the whole window at once, so the
queue is never short and the only question is what comes off it first.
Ordered by facing, then distance. A hemisphere rather than the frustum on
purpose: the frustum starves everything a turn of the head is about to
reveal.

--bench-4d gained an `ahead ms` column, the time until the half of the
window the camera faces goes clean, because that is the number a player
feels - the world behind them can stay stale another second and nobody can
tell. A/B, ordering on and off:

    motion         ahead ms          settle ms
    translate      2051 -> 1382      unchanged
    scroll 15/s    2109 -> 1545      unchanged
    scroll 60/s    1839 -> 1403      unchanged

The visible world catches up 24-33% sooner for identical total work.
Without the change ahead_ms equalled settle_ms to within 1%, which is the
proof there was no prioritisation happening at all.

CAVE SHORT-CIRCUIT. A cave needs BOTH iso-surfaces within kCaveIsoWidth,
and the first rejects ~90% of cells, but the second was evaluated
unconditionally. Exactly equivalent to reorder; 11% off terrain
generation, 1.866 ms/chunk against 2.097.

That measurement needed its own method. Sequential runs gave 2.994, 2.102
and 1.917 ms for code that differed in one branch - a 56% spread between
two runs of the SAME binary. Building both variants and interleaving them
gave three significant figures of agreement across six rounds. On a loaded
machine, alternate the binaries; do not trust consecutive timings.

This does not make it as smooth as 4D Miner and is not claimed to. The
structural fix is not to regenerate at all: 4D block data cached per chunk
would turn a slice change from a noise evaluation into a memory walk. That
is a storage redesign, not a knob.

The real symptom was the ubuntu build+bench job, which did not fail at

    100% tests passed, 0 tests failed out of 6

against a suite two tests short, and went green. ctest cannot notice a
test that was never registered - it runs what is in CTestTestfile.cmake,
and these were not in it. A green run on a suite whose size depends on
the platform is not evidence about the platform where it is smaller.

CI jobs compare the registered tests against it, in both directions:
missing fails, and an unlisted new test fails too, because a list that
maintains itself is not a claim about coverage. Validated by putting the
bug back - registering the two tests only when NOT APPLE - where the
guard names both missing tests and ctest alone reports "Total Tests: 6"
without complaint.

    prism_mesh.cpp:41: runtime error: -nan is outside the range of

tile_footprint snaps every corner to the grid the packed vertex can
address and drops the edges that collapse; a cell left with fewer than
three corners keeps its lattice identity but has count == 0, because it
can no longer put a pixel on the screen. build_prism_chunk_from_blocks
then re-derived each cell's voxel column by averaging its corners, and
for those cells averaged zero of them: 0/0, then a cast of NaN to int.

The correct answer was already in the cell. tile_footprint fills
cell.vx/vz from the polygon BEFORE the snap, so a cell whose corners
have since merged away still knows which voxel column it came from -
identical to the recomputed value for every cell that survived, and the
only defined value for the ones that did not. Use it.
