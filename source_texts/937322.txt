Summary
Adds SimplexNoise<T,N>, a sibling to PerlinNoise<T,N> that scales far better in high dimensions. Where Perlin interpolates the 2ᴺ corners of a hypercube, simplex noise sums the N+1 corners of the single lattice simplex containing the sample point — O(N²) per sample instead of O(2ᴺ). Client code can pick whichever is appropriate.

Design
Skew matrix. The unit cube is mapped to an equilateral parallelepiped by M = I − G·J (and inverse I + F·J), the standard simplex-noise skew. Exposed as static skew_matrix() / unskew_matrix(); the hot path applies the equivalent O(N) rank-1 form (using the dense matrix per corner would make eval O(N³)). A test confirms the two matrices are mutual inverses and agree with the rank-1 form to machine precision.
Freudenthal/Kuhn triangulation. Floor + argsort of the fractional coordinates yields the N+1 corners of the containing simplex.
Good gradients. Uniform-sphere gradients from a hashed table — the same high-quality gradients as PerlinNoise — not the small fixed gradient set common in simplex implementations.
Kernel. (r² − |d|²)⁴ · (g·d), with r² set to the minimum simplex height², derived from the lattice geometry rather than a magic constant. This is the inradius of a vertex's simplex star, so a corner's contribution vanishes in both value and gradient exactly as the sample leaves the containing simplex → the field is C¹. (A barycentric-interpolation scheme is only C⁰ — faceted — because the barycentric gradient differs across shared faces.) For N=2 this formula yields r²=0.5, matching Ken Perlin's classic 2D constant.
Analytic gradient. ∇ = Σ −8w³(g·d)·d + w⁴·g, validated against finite differences.
Validation
Analytic gradient vs finite differences (double, N=2–6): max error ~1.7e-8 (pure FD truncation); gradient().first equals eval() exactly.
C¹ continuity: along a dense line the gradient's rate of change stays bounded — a faceted (C⁰) field would spike by orders of magnitude at simplex boundaries.
Range ≈ [−1, 1], mean ≈ 0. Feature frequency is comparable to Perlin at the same input scale (zero-crossings/unit within ~15%).
Compiles and runs for N=1–8, float and double, under -Werror.
regression/simplex_noise.cpp mirrors perlin.cpp's FD gradient check and adds the continuity check.
Notes / possible follow-ups
Contrast falls with N (std ≈ 0.54→0.11 from N=2→6) — the known simplex "gaps" effect. r² is at the strict min-height for guaranteed C¹; enlarging it past that would improve coverage/contrast at the cost of a small controlled discontinuity.
The eval hot path still has easy wins available (e.g. the amplitude-normalization sampling in the constructor could be replaced with a precomputed per-N constant).

A sibling to PerlinNoise that sums the N+1 corners of the containing
lattice simplex instead of interpolating the 2^N corners of a hypercube,
making it O(N^2) per sample rather than O(2^N). The two are roughly equal
cost near N=4; simplex wins by ~4x at N=7 and the gap widens with N.

Design:
  - Skew/unskew via the matrix that maps the unit cube to an equilateral
    parallelepiped (M = I - G*J and its inverse I + F*J); exposed as static
    skew_matrix()/unskew_matrix() and applied in O(N) rank-1 form in the
    hot path.
  - Freudenthal/Kuhn triangulation (floor + argsort of fractionals) to find
    the containing simplex's corners.
  - Uniform-sphere gradients from a hashed table (the same high-quality
    gradients as PerlinNoise), rather than a small fixed gradient set.
  - Radial kernel (r^2 - |d|^2)^4 with r^2 = the minimum simplex height^2,
    derived from the lattice geometry. This makes each corner's contribution
    vanish (value and gradient) exactly as it leaves the containing simplex,
    giving a C1 field. For N=2 this reproduces Ken Perlin's classic 0.5.
  - Analytic spatial gradient, validated against finite differences.

Regression test mirrors perlin.cpp's finite-difference gradient check and
adds a continuity check (bounded gradient step => C1, no faceting).
