01
The problem
Render point clouds and volumes WITHOUT meshes or ray marching: points have no connectivity, and naive point rendering is full of holes and aliasing.
02
The core idea
Represent the data as anisotropic Gaussian kernels and SPLAT them: project each 3D Gaussian to a 2D screen-space Gaussian (the EWA — elliptical weighted average — filter), then composite them back-to-front. Closed-form projection, correct anti-aliasing, no rays.
03
What it left open
A forward renderer for scanned points and volume data — nobody optimized the Gaussians from photographs. Point-based graphics faded as GPU triangle pipelines won; after a few hardware-accelerated follow-ups (2002–2005) the technique stayed a niche for close to two decades, until differentiable rendering needed exactly this math. Its screen-space filter later drew a diagnosis of its own: Mip-Splatting found its size "chosen empirically", oversmoothing images when zooming out.

builds on Kajiya & Von Herzen 1984 Same emission-absorption model of participating media, evaluated by projecting kernels instead of marching rays — the rasterization answer to the ray-marching question (building on Westover's splatting and Heckbert's EWA filtering).
What stands on it 3
revives this work 3D Gaussian Splatting 2023 Brings EWA splatting back from two decades on the margins of point-based graphics, virtually unchanged as math — anisotropic Gaussians, screen-space projection, sorted alpha blending — but now the Gaussians are OPTIMIZED from photographs through the differentiable version of that very renderer.
fixes this work Mip-Splatting 2023 Repairs EWA's screen-space filter, whose size Mip-Splatting calls "chosen empirically": EWA advocates an identity covariance, "effectively occupying a 3x3 pixel region on the screen", which "leads to overly smooth results when zooming out". The 2D Mip filter swaps it for an approximation of one pixel's box filter, and beats 3DGS + EWA — the baseline that replaces 3DGS's dilation with the EWA filter — most clearly at lower resolutions.
builds on this work 2D Gaussian Splatting 2024 Completes the EWA revival: 3DGS brought back Zwicker's volume splatting; 2DGS brings back the other half — SURFACE splatting with oriented surfels — now recovered from photos alone, where earlier surfel methods, even differentiable ones, needed known geometry or depth.
